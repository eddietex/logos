# See the Land and Commission Joshua - Numbers 27 and Deuteronomy 3

## Passages involved
- [[Joshua Commissioned]] — Numbers 27:12–23.
- [[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]] — Deuteronomy 3:23–29.

## Analysis

Both accounts place a command to see the land beside provision for Joshua's leadership.
Numbers 27:12–14 names the mountain of Abarim, anticipates Moses' death, and recalls rebellion
at Meribah. Moses then requests a leader for the congregation (15–17). Yahweh identifies Joshua
and specifies a public commission; Moses performs it before Eleazar and the people (18–23).

Deuteronomy 3:23–29 reports a different petition: Moses asks to cross the Jordan himself.
Yahweh refuses, allows a view from Pisgah, and commands him to commission and strengthen Joshua.
Neither the ascent nor the commission is performed within this unit. Eleazar, the Urim, and
laying on of hands are not mentioned here; their absence is not a denial of Numbers' ceremony.

The prayers have different objects, and no exact chronology relating them is supplied in these
units. A combined reconstruction must not turn Numbers' prayer for the congregation into
Deuteronomy's refused request for personal entry. The different explanations of exclusion
remain explicit in [[Moses Excluded - Numbers 20 and Deuteronomy 1]].

Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:12–23 and Deuteronomy 3:23–29; paraphrases only.

## Significance

The leader can prepare a future he will not personally enter. Both accounts continue Israel's
inheritance through Joshua without reversing Moses' exclusion; Deuteronomy gives particular
weight to Moses' duty to encourage his successor after his own request is denied.
