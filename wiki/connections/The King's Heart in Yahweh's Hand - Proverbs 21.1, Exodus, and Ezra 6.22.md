# The King's Heart in Yahweh's Hand - Proverbs 21.1, Exodus, and Ezra 6.22

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 21:1** — *"The king's heart is in Yahweh's hand like the watercourses. He turns it
  wherever he desires."* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Proverbs 21:29** — *"A wicked man hardens his face; but as for the upright, he establishes his
  ways."* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Exodus 4:21** — *"I will harden his heart and he will not let the people go."* —
  [[Moses Returns to Egypt]]
- **Exodus 9:12** — *"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he didn't listen to them"* —
  [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]
- **Exodus 9:34** — Pharaoh *"sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his servants."* —
  [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]
- **Ezra 6:22** — Yahweh *"had turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them, to strengthen their
  hands in the work of God"* *(WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Proverbs states a principle; Exodus and Ezra narrate two opposite instances of it.** Proverbs
21:1 pictures the king's heart as irrigation water that Yahweh directs *"wherever he desires"*. The
image is about the most powerful will in the land being within reach of a greater one. It does not
say how the turning works, and it names no king.

Exodus shows a royal heart directed toward refusal. Yahweh announces before the plagues that he
will harden [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]]'s heart (4:21), and the narrator reports that he did
(9:12). Yet the same chapter says Pharaoh *"sinned yet more, and hardened his heart"* (9:34). Both
agents are named, and the text holds Pharaoh accountable for a heart it also says Yahweh hardened.
Ezra 6:22 turns a foreign king's heart the other way, toward favor for the returned community and
the work of the temple.

**The tension is kept, not solved.** Proverbs 21:1 could be read as removing the king's
responsibility; Exodus 9:34 refuses that reading by calling the hardening Pharaoh's sin. Proverbs
21:29 also places a hardened *face* on the wicked man as his own act. The wiki records divine
direction and human accountability side by side, as these texts do, without a theory of how they
fit. See [[Hardening of the Heart]] and [[Providence]].

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 21:1, 29 and Exodus 4:21; 9:12, 34. Ezra 6:22 quoted from the WEB at
bible-api.com (2026-10-26). No claim about Hebrew vocabulary is made; whether the verbs of Exodus
and Proverbs 21:29 coincide has not been checked.

## Significance

The saying gives the wise a reason not to fear kings absolutely and not to trust them absolutely:
their hearts are not the last word. See [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Man Plans, Yahweh Directs - Proverbs 16.1, 9 and 19.21]].
