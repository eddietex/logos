# The Replacement Tablets - Exodus 34 and Deuteronomy 10

## Passages involved

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:1–21, the assembly's address.
- [[The Blood of the Covenant]] — Exodus 24:3–8, 12, 18, Moses' writing and promised tablets.
- [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]] — Exodus 31:18, tablets given.
- [[The Golden Calf]] — Exodus 32:15–19, divine work broken.
- [[The Covenant Renewed]] — Exodus 34:1–4, 10–28, replacement and writing.
- Deuteronomy 10:1–5 — the replacement account retold; verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus 32:16 attributes both the tablets and their writing to God. In 34:1, 4 the replacement
stones are cut by Moses, while God promises to write the former words on them. The change in
who prepares the stones is explicit; the promise concerns continuity of the words.

**The closing writing notices are not identical.** Exodus 34:27 commands Moses to write the
words on which the covenant with him and Israel rests. Verse 28 reports the forty-day stay
without food and water and then says that he wrote the covenant words, the ten commandments,
on the tablets. The WEB leaves the last writer as a pronoun. If its subject is Moses, the
sentence stands in tension with God's promise in 34:1. If its subject is God, it resumes that
promise after describing Moses' stay. Distinguishing Moses' written record from God's tablet
writing is a coherent reading, but Exodus 34:27 does not name a separate book or explain that
distinction. No Hebrew claim is needed to state this question in the WEB.

**Deuteronomy clarifies its own account.** In 10:1–5 God orders Moses to cut stones and make
an ark, promises the former words, writes them, and gives Moses the tablets. Verse 4 identifies
the ten commandments with what Yahweh spoke from the fire at the assembly. Moses deposits
them in the ark. This is explicit evidence for divine writing and continuity in Deuteronomy;
it should not be used to pretend Exodus 34:27–28 contains equally explicit wording.

**The nearby law collection raises a related question.** Exodus 34:10–26 emphasizes exclusive
worship, feasts, firstborn redemption, Sabbath, and offerings. It is not a repetition in full
of Exodus 20:1–17. The phrase identifying ten commandments in 34:28 follows this different
collection, while 34:1 points back to the former tablet words. One reading distinguishes the
renewal stipulations from the commandments written again on the tablets. Another associates
the closing designation more directly with the nearby worship laws and sees differing legal
traditions. The text does not number ten individual laws here or explain a compositional
history. The wiki records the tension and these possible readings without deciding that history.

## Significance

The tablets connect [[Covenant]], [[Law and Obedience]], and [[Work]]: God's words survive
the breach, while Moses must prepare new stones and mediate the renewed relationship. The
renewal does not make the calf unreal, and the different writing notices deserve preservation.
See [[The Finger of God - Exodus 8.19, Exodus 31.18, and Luke 11.20]].
