# I Will Pour Out My Spirit - Proverbs 1.23, Joel 2.28, and Acts 2.17

## Passages involved

- [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]] — Proverbs 1:23: *"Turn at my reproof. Behold, I will pour out my
  spirit on you. I will make known my words to you."* Wisdom, to the simple, in the city gates.
- Joel 2:28 *(not yet ingested)* — *"It will happen afterward, that I will pour out my Spirit on all
  flesh; and your sons and your daughters will prophesy. Your old men will dream dreams. Your young
  men will see visions"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com). God, to Israel, after the locusts.
- Acts 2:17 *(not yet ingested)* — *"It will be in the last days, says God, that I will pour out my
  Spirit on all flesh …"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com). Peter at Pentecost, quoting Joel.

## Analysis

**In English the three promises are one sentence; in the original languages they are not.** The
WEB gives all three as *"I will pour out my spirit"*. The Hebrew and Greek, checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah* and the SBLGNT, show what is shared and what is not:

| | verb | object | recipients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proverbs 1:23 | *ʾabbîʿâ* (*nāḇaʿ*, to gush, bubble up) | *rûḥî*, my spirit | *lāḵem*, to you — the simple who turn |
| Joel 3:1 (Eng. 2:28) | *ʾešpôk* (*šāpaḵ*, to pour out) | *ʾet-rûḥî*, my spirit | *ʿal-kol-bāśār*, on all flesh |
| Acts 2:17 | *ekcheō* (to pour out) | *apo tou pneumatos mou*, *of* my Spirit | *epi pasan sarka*, on all flesh |

- **The verbs differ.** Joel's *šāpaḵ* is the ordinary verb for pouring a liquid out — and for
  shedding blood (Proverbs 1:16, *lišpok-dām*, two pericopes back on the same page of the book).
  Proverbs' *nāḇaʿ* is a spring's verb, the welling-up of water from below; Proverbs 18:4 uses it of
  *"a flowing brook"* (*naḥal nōḇēaʿ*), *"the fountain of wisdom"*. Joel pictures the Spirit poured
  down; Proverbs pictures it welling up.
- **The recipients differ.** Joel's promise is unconditional and universal — *all flesh*, sons and
  daughters, old and young, and *"also on the servants and on the handmaids"* (2:29, WEB, checked
  against bible-api.com). Wisdom's is conditional — *"Turn at my reproof"* —
  and addressed to those who do.
- ***Rûaḥ* may not mean the same thing.** In Proverbs 1:23 it is paralleled by *"my words"*, and the
  line can be read as Wisdom pouring out her *mind*: what is in her, spoken. Some translations are
  reported to render it so (not checked here). In Joel the *rûaḥ* produces prophecy, dreams, and
  visions — it is the prophetic Spirit of God, as in Moses' wish *"that all Yahweh's people were
  prophets, that Yahweh would put his Spirit on them!"* (Numbers 11:29, *not yet ingested*; WEB,
  checked against bible-api.com).
- **Acts follows the Septuagint's partitive** — *apo tou pneumatos mou*, *from* or *of my Spirit* —
  rather than the Hebrew's direct object. That the Septuagint of Joel has the same partitive is the
  usual account; it was not checked against a Septuagint edition here.

**So the connection is not a quotation.** Nothing suggests Joel is alluding to Proverbs, or Peter to
either Proverbs or Wisdom. The connection is conceptual and verbal in English, and partially verbal
in Hebrew (*rûḥî*, *my spirit*, with a verb of pouring). It is recorded because the same image is
used by Wisdom personified and by God, and because the two together raise the question the New
Testament answers — whose Spirit is Wisdom's?

## Significance

Proverbs 1:23 shows **Wisdom speaking in God's register**: she promises what in Joel only God
promises. That is one of the features of the Proverbs 1 speech that the New Testament develops when
it hears Jesus as Wisdom's voice (see
[[Wisdom Sends Prophets - Proverbs 1.20-33, Luke 11.49, and Matthew 23.34-37]]), and that later
theology develops when it associates the Spirit with wisdom — as Exodus already had, in the craftsmen
*"filled with the Spirit of God, in wisdom"* (Exodus 31:3; see
[[A Man in Whom Is the Spirit of God]]).

It also marks a difference worth keeping. Joel's outpouring comes *"afterward"* — after judgment, as
gift — and Acts places it *"in the last days"*. Wisdom's is offered *before* the judgment, as the
alternative to it: turn, and I will pour out my spirit; refuse, and *"calamity overtakes you like a
storm"* (1:27). The same image sits on either side of the disaster.
