## Analysis

### Joshua 6 now ingested

Joshua 6 is now ingested. Verses 17–25 distinguish destruction of the city and its inhabitants,
rescue of Rahab’s household, and metals reserved for Yahweh’s treasury. Verse 18 warns that
Israel’s camp can itself become accursed by taking devoted property. The command in Deuteronomy
20:16–18 and Rahab’s survival stand in tension: Joshua states the exception and its reason but
provides no general reconciliation. This addition rests on the stored WEB, not a new lexical claim.
Joshua 7 remains outside this completed ingest.


### Deuteronomy 20:16–18 now ingested

The law of war restates the *ḥērem* of 7:2 for the cities *"that Yahweh your God gives you for an
inheritance"*: *"you shall save alive nothing that breathes; but you shall utterly destroy them"* (16–17),
*kî-haḥărēm taḥărîmēm* (WLC 20:17), the same infinitive-plus-finite construction as 7:2. Two things are
new. The *ḥērem* is now set against a second category, the cities *"very far off"* (15), which are offered
peace and, if they fight, lose only their males (10–14); so the *ḥērem* is not Deuteronomy's rule for war
in general but for the land. And the reason is stated: *"that they not teach you to follow all their
abominations, which they have done to their gods"* (18). The list names six peoples; the Girgashite of 7:1
is missing, without explanation. Joshua 10:40 and 11:14 narrate the command carried out with the same
noun, *nəšāmâ*, *"all that breathed"* (WLC; WEB, bible-api.com), and Joshua 11:19–20 says that no city but
Gibeon *"made peace"*, which bears on whether 20:10's offer reached the land's cities at all. The text of
Deuteronomy 20 does not settle that; see [[Laws for Going to War]].

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 7:1–2; 20:10–18. Joshua 10:40; 11:14, 19–20 verified from
bible-api.com (WEB), 2026-11-08. WLC (morphhb), retrieved 2026-11-08.

### Deuteronomy 13:12–18 now ingested

*Ḥērem* is turned inward. A town of Israel drawn to other gods is to be struck *"destroying it
utterly"* (*haḥărēm*, WLC 13:16) with its inhabitants and livestock, after diligent inquiry (13:14).
Unlike Jericho's metal, which was *"holy to Yahweh"* and went *"into Yahweh's treasury"* (Joshua
6:19, WEB, verified from bible-api.com), this plunder goes nowhere: it is gathered into the square and
burned with the city *"to Yahweh your God"*, and the city is never rebuilt (13:16). *"Nothing of the
devoted thing"* (*haḥērem*, WLC 13:18) may cling to Israel's hand — the verb *dābaq*, the same one
used in 13:4 for clinging to Yahweh (WLC 13:5). Keeping it would bring the anger that the
destruction is meant to turn away (13:17); this is the rule that Joshua 7 narrates broken, cited
here by reference until Joshua is stored.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 13:12–18; WLC (morphhb), retrieved 2026-11-06.
Joshua 6:19 verified from bible-api.com (WEB), 2026-11-06.

### Deuteronomy 7:12–26 now ingested

Verse 26, left open by the previous section, is now ingested. Here *devoted thing* is said of the
silver and gold on the nations' images, not of people. The status spreads: whoever brings it into
the house will *become a devoted thing like it*. Coveting and taking it are forbidden together (25).
This prepares directly for Joshua 7, where Achan takes devoted things and the consequence falls on
Israel; that narrative is cited here by reference only until Joshua is stored. Deuteronomy 7:16,
*your eye shall not pity them*, keeps the severity of verse 2 toward people.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 7:12–26.

### Deuteronomy 7:1–11 now ingested

Deuteronomy 7:2 is now ingested. Destruction follows Yahweh’s delivery of the nations; treaty and
mercy are forbidden. The instruction is not narrated as Israel’s voluntary vow. Verse 5 separately
orders destruction of worship objects, so verse 2 cannot be reduced to those objects. Verse 26
remains outside this completed pericope.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 7:1–11; Exodus 34:12–16.

**Now ingested: [[Arad and Hormah]].** Numbers 21:1–3 begins with an attack and captives,
then reports Israel's conditional vow, Yahweh's hearing, and destruction of the people
and cities. Victory is attributed to Yahweh, but a divine command initiating the vow
is not reported. The earlier rout at Hormah is a narrative contrast, not evidence
that every detail of the two battles or their sites is identical.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Numbers 14:39–45; 21:1–3.

**One root, *ḥāram* and *ḥērem*, in all of these (WLC).** WEB's *devoted* and *utterly destroy* conceal
that. The Hebrew joins a priest's field to a conquered city.

| Text | What is *ḥērem* | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Leviticus 27:21, 28 | a field; property | the priest's; not sold or redeemed |
| Leviticus 27:29 | a human being | put to death |
| Exodus 22:20 | one who sacrifices to another god | death, as a sentence |
| Numbers 21:2 | enemy cities, vowed | destroyed; the place is named Hormah (21:3), already the site of the defeat of [[The Defeat at Hormah]] (14:45) |
| Deuteronomy 7:2 | the nations of the land | destruction, in war |
| Deuteronomy 20:16–17 | the cities of the six peoples of the land, not the cities *"very far off"* | *"nothing that breathes"* saved alive |
| Deuteronomy 13:16, 18 (WLC; WEB 13:15, 17) | an Israelite town gone after other gods | destroyed, plunder burned, never rebuilt |
| Joshua 6:17 | Jericho | all devoted except Rahab's house |
| 1 Samuel 15:3 | Amalek | Saul spares Agag and the best animals |
| Numbers 18:14; Ezekiel 44:29 | devoted things in Israel | priestly income |

**What the texts share is irreversibility.** The common element is not always death. It is that what
becomes *ḥērem* leaves human use and cannot return. Leviticus says so directly: not sold, not
redeemed. Joshua 7 and 1 Samuel 15 are then cases of people treating *ḥērem* as if it could be kept.
That framing is an interpretation that joins texts from different books, but each verse supports its
own part.

**Numbers 21:2 is a vow.** It uses *neder* with the verb *ḥāram*, so the two main words of Leviticus
27 meet there. Whether Leviticus 27:29 has war in view is not stated. Exodus 22:20 offers a judicial
setting, and neither can be shown to be the setting.

**This does not settle the moral problem.** The war texts, including 1 Samuel 15:3's infants, remain
among the hardest in Scripture. Noting that the word also covers fields explains its range. It does
not make those commands any easier, and this page does not try to.

**A contrast case: Numbers 31.** The war against Midian is total in its killing but is never called
*ḥērem*; no form of the root *ḥrm* occurs in the chapter (Miqra according to the Masorah, searched by
consonants). Its plunder is counted, halved, and levied for priest and Levites (31:25–47), where devoted
things go wholly to Yahweh (Leviticus 27:28). The war is named vengeance (31:2–3), not devotion.

## Significance

Leviticus supplies the rule behind later stories: Achan's theft and Saul's mercy are breaches of the
*not sold, not redeemed* clause, whether or not the narrators are citing it. Joshua 6 is now ingested; Joshua 7 and 1 Samuel 15 remain to be ingested. See [[War]], [[Holiness]], and
[[Amalek - From a Concubine's Son to Israel's First Enemy]].
