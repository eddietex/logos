# Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15

## Passages involved

- [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]] — Leviticus 27:21, 28–29.
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:20: *"He who sacrifices to any god, except to Yahweh
  only, shall be utterly destroyed."* (WLC 22:19.)
- Numbers 18:14 — *"Everything devoted in Israel shall be yours."* *(WEB, bible-api.com; not yet
  ingested)*
- Numbers 21:2 — *"Israel vowed a vow to Yahweh, and said, 'If you will indeed deliver this people
  into my hand, then I will utterly destroy their cities.'"* *(same)*
- Deuteronomy 7:2, 26 — *"you shall utterly destroy them"*; *"for it is a devoted thing."* *(same)*
- Joshua 6:17; 7:1 — *"The city shall be devoted"*; Achan *"took some of the devoted things."*
  *(same)*
- 1 Samuel 15:3, 9 — *"utterly destroy all that they have"*; Saul and the people *"were not willing
  to utterly destroy them."* *(same)*
- Ezekiel 44:29 — *"every devoted thing in Israel shall be theirs."* *(same)*

## Analysis

**One root, *ḥāram* and *ḥērem*, in all of these (WLC).** WEB's *devoted* and *utterly destroy* conceal
that. The Hebrew joins a priest's field to a conquered city.

| Text | What is *ḥērem* | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Leviticus 27:21, 28 | a field; property | the priest's; not sold or redeemed |
| Leviticus 27:29 | a human being | put to death |
| Exodus 22:20 | one who sacrifices to another god | death, as a sentence |
| Numbers 21:2 | enemy cities, vowed | destroyed; the place is named Hormah (21:3) |
| Deuteronomy 7:2 | the nations of the land | destruction, in war |
| Joshua 6:17 | Jericho | all devoted except Rahab's house |
| 1 Samuel 15:3 | Amalek | Saul spares Agag and the best animals |
| Numbers 18:14; Ezekiel 44:29 | devoted things in Israel | priestly income |

**What the texts share is irreversibility.** The common element is not always death. It is that what
becomes *ḥērem* leaves human use and cannot return. Leviticus says so directly: not sold, not
redeemed. Joshua 7 and 1 Samuel 15 are then cases of people treating *ḥērem* as if it could be kept.
That framing is an interpretation that joins texts from different books, but each verse supports its
own part.

**Numbers 21:2 is a vow.** It uses *neder* with the verb *ḥāram*, so the two main words of Leviticus
27 meet there. Whether Leviticus 27:29 has war in view is not stated. Exodus 22:20 offers a judicial
setting, and neither can be shown to be the setting.

**This does not settle the moral problem.** The war texts, including 1 Samuel 15:3's infants, remain
among the hardest in Scripture. Noting that the word also covers fields explains its range. It does
not make those commands any easier, and this page does not try to.

## Significance

Leviticus supplies the rule behind later stories: Achan's theft and Saul's mercy are breaches of the
*not sold, not redeemed* clause, whether or not the narrators are citing it. Joshua and 1 Samuel are
not yet ingested, and their pages will need to revisit this one. See [[War]], [[Holiness]], and
[[Amalek - From a Concubine's Son to Israel's First Enemy]].
