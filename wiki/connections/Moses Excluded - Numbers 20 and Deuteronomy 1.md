# Moses Excluded - Numbers 20 and Deuteronomy 1

## Passages involved
- [[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]] — Deuteronomy 3:23–29.
- [[The Sentence and the Presumptuous Ascent]] — Deuteronomy 1:37–38.
- [[The Waters of Meribah]] — Numbers 20:1–13.
- [[Joshua Commissioned]] — Numbers 27:12–23.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 3:26 repeats the explanation that Yahweh was angry with Moses for the people's
sakes. His plea to cross is denied, though he may look from Pisgah (3:23–27). He must strengthen
Joshua (3:28). This continues the tension described below: it does not replace Numbers'
account of the leaders' responsibility. See
[[See the Land and Commission Joshua - Numbers 27 and Deuteronomy 3]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:23–29.

Deuteronomy 1:37 recalls Yahweh’s anger at Moses for the people’s sakes, immediately after the
promise to Caleb and before the charge to encourage Joshua. Moses’ exclusion forms part of the
speech about the generation denied entry. No rock, water dispute, or action by Aaron is mentioned.

Numbers 20:12 explicitly attributes the exclusion of Moses and Aaron to their failure to believe
Yahweh and sanctify him before Israel. It follows Moses’ striking the rock twice. Numbers 27:12–14
again ties Moses’ sentence to rebellion at the waters in the wilderness of Zin; the commissioning
of Joshua follows (15–23).

**Tension retained.** Numbers emphasizes the leaders’ responsibility at Meribah; Deuteronomy 1
emphasizes Moses suffering exclusion for the people’s sakes in a speech about the earlier refusal.
The people’s provocation and Moses’ responsibility can coexist, but that interpretation does not
remove the different emphases or supply the unstated chronology. Taking Deuteronomy 1:37–38 as
an anticipatory aside is a possible reading, not an explanation supplied by these verses.

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 20:1–13 and 27:12–23. No original-language claim
is required for this comparison.

## Significance

The leader who warns the people is also excluded, yet must encourage the successor who will
bring them in. Both tellings join Moses’ limit to continued provision for Israel’s future.
The wiki preserves the differing explanations instead of silently replacing one with the other.
