# Enough for the Work - Manna and the Sanctuary Offering

## Passages involved

- [[Manna and Quail]] — Exodus 16:16–30; daily need, gathering, and Sabbath provision.
- [[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]] — Exodus 36:3–7; repeated gifts, sufficient supply, and the
  end of collection.

## Analysis

Exodus 16:16–18 sets gathering by household need. The measured manna leaves neither surplus nor
lack. Some keep it against Moses' instruction and it spoils (16:19–20); the permitted double
portion for Sabbath is preserved (16:22–26). The daily gift has a stated rhythm, and taking more
is not always better obedience.

Exodus 36:3–7 also describes a recurring supply: offerings arrive every morning. Here the workers
report a real excess, and Moses stops further giving. The narrator confirms that there is enough
and more. The limit is the sanctuary's material requirement, rather than a daily household ration
or the week's rest day. The surplus is neither condemned nor reported spoiled.

The comparison concerns sufficiency and the discipline of stopping collection. The scenes differ:
one is food Yahweh supplies in response to complaint; the other is a willing human contribution
for commanded work. Exodus 16 reports disobedient keeping and gathering as well as rest; Exodus
36 reports the successful restraint of further gifts. The comparison does not assert a direct
quotation, an identical source-language formula, or a single rule against all material reserves.

**Sources:** Exodus 16:16–30 and 36:3–7 read in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-24.
The comparison is paraphrased; no later book or source-language claim is needed.

## Significance

[[Wealth and Possessions]], [[Sacrifice and Offering]], and [[Law and Obedience]] meet in a
practical question: what is this supply for? In both scenes the purpose of gathering limits it.
The sanctuary narrative gives leaders a positive task in stopping a successful collection when
its specified work is supplied, without turning sufficiency into a prohibition on generosity.
