# Went to Sojourn from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17.8 and Ruth 1.1

## Passages involved

- **Judges 17:7–9** — [[Micah's Idol and Levite]]. *The man departed out of the city, out of Bethlehem
  Judah, to live where he could find a place, and he came to the hill country of Ephraim, to the house of
  Micah* (17:8, stored WEB).
- **Judges 19:1** — [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]]. A Levite *living*, *gār*, in Ephraim takes a
  concubine *out of Bethlehem Judah* (stored WEB; WLC).
- **Ruth 1:1** — [[Famine and Death in Moab]]. *A certain man of Bethlehem Judah went to live in the
  country of Moab, he, and his wife, and his two sons* (stored WEB).

## Analysis

The Hebrew of the two departures is nearly the same sentence (WLC):

| | Judges 17:8 | Ruth 1:1 |
|---|---|---|
| verb of going | *wayyēleḵ hāʾîš*, *the man went* | *wayyēleḵ ʾîš*, *a man went* |
| from | *mēhāʿîr mibbêt leḥem yəhûdâ*, *from the city, from Bethlehem Judah* | *mibbêt leḥem yəhûdâ*, *from Bethlehem Judah* |
| purpose | *lāgûr baʾăšer yimṣāʾ*, *to sojourn where he could find* | *lāgûr biśdê môʾāḇ*, *to sojourn in the fields of Moab* |
| who goes | one young Levite | a man, his wife, and two sons |
| why | not stated | *there was a famine in the land* |
| where it ends | an idol, a priesthood for hire, and Dan's shrine (Judges 18:30–31) | three graves, two widows, and later the line of David (Ruth 4:17–22) |

*Lāgûr*, the infinitive of *gûr*, *to sojourn*, occurs in the WLC of Judges at 17:8, 9 and in Ruth only at
1:1. The phrase *Bethlehem Judah* (*bêt leḥem yəhûdâ*) occurs in Judges at 17:7, 8, 9; 19:1, 2, 18, and in
Ruth at 1:1, 2. Each of the three stories that begins there in these two books begins with someone
leaving.

Ruth follows Judges in the English canon and dates itself *in the days when the judges judged* (1:1). A
reader coming straight from the Levite's journeys meets the same town and almost the same sentence again.
The first departure led to a stolen idol, the second to the outrage at Gibeah; the third leads, after
loss, to the family of David, whose father is *that Ephrathite of Bethlehem Judah* (1 Samuel 17:12, WEB
checked at bible-api.com).

**What is verified and what is not.** The Hebrew wording and the occurrence counts are from the WLC files
of Judges and Ruth. Whether the author of Ruth wrote with Judges 17:8 in view cannot be shown: the
sentence uses common words, and the shared elements may come from a stock way of saying someone left
home. The wiki records the parallel as verbal and leaves intent open.

## Significance

The town at the heart of Judges' worst stories is also the town of Ruth and of David. The two books
start their journeys out of Bethlehem with the same words and end them in opposite places: Judges in
*Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes* (21:25), Ruth in a genealogy that ends with a king's
name. See [[Two Levites from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17 and 19]], [[Bethlehem]],
[[Land and Sojourning]], and [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].
