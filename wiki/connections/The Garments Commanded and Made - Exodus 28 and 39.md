# The Garments Commanded and Made - Exodus 28 and 39

## Passages involved

- [[The Priestly Garments]] — Exodus 28:1–43; the design and explanations of use.
- [[The Priestly Garments Made]] — Exodus 39:1–31; manufacture and repeated obedience notices.
- [[The Ordination of the Priests]] — Exodus 29:4–9, 21, 29–30; dressing, sanctification,
  and succession prescribed separately.
- [[The Freewill Offering]] — Exodus 35:19–27; the clothing commissioned and materials brought.

## Analysis

The same main articles move from command to manufacture: the ephod (28:6–14; 39:2–7),
breastplate (28:15–30; 39:8–21), blue robe (28:31–35; 39:22–26), linen clothing
(28:39–43; 39:27–29), and inscribed plate (28:36–38; 39:30–31). Chapter 39 moves the plate
after the linen garments. Its opening verse describes clothing for ministry and holy garments
for Aaron; its concluding verse attaches the holy crown to the turban.

**The making account adds craft detail.** Exodus 39:3 describes beating gold into plates and
cutting wires to work into the textiles. Chapter 28 specifies gold among the ephod’s materials
without giving this process. In the stored WEB, 39:24 also names twined linen for the pomegranates,
and 39:29 lists the sash’s materials more fully than 28:39. The text asserts obedience without
presenting the report as a verbatim duplication of the instructions.

**The named stones preserve representation.** Exodus 39:6–7 keeps the engraved shoulder stones
and their memorial purpose; 39:14 keeps twelve stones for twelve tribes. The gem labels in
39:10–13 match the list in 28:17–20 in the stored WEB. Neither account assigns a specific tribal
name to each stone, and this English comparison does not identify ancient minerals or establish
source-language equivalence. See
[[Twelve Stones and Twelve Names - The Breastplate and the New Jerusalem]].

**Several explanations are not repeated.** The breastplate’s making does not mention the Urim
and Thummim (compare 28:30), the bell account does not repeat the death warning (28:35), and the
crown account does not restate Aaron’s bearing of iniquity (28:38). These omissions do not tell
us that the makers abandoned those provisions. In particular, 28:30 directs placement of the
Urim and Thummim without describing their manufacture. See
[[Urim and Thummim - The Breastplate of Judgment and Later Inquiry]].

**The stored English preserves a discrepancy in attachment.** Exodus 28:37 says the blue lace
is on the sash; 39:31 fastens the plate on the turban above. The forehead reference of 28:38
and crown-on-turban direction of 29:6 support the latter placement. The earlier passage’s notes
record a source-language resolution, but this comparison makes no new assertion about Hebrew.
Both stored WEB readings are retained; the raw source is not corrected silently.

**Manufacture, dressing, and consecration are separate events.** Aaron and his sons are the
beneficiaries of the making, not actors shown putting the garments on in chapter 39. Chapter 29
prescribes washing, dressing, anointing, and a seven-day ordination. Those directions are not
fulfilled merely by the objects’ existence. Likewise, the seven WEB obedience notices in
39:1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29, 31 do not supply a seven-day work schedule. See
[[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

## Significance

The clothing gives visible form to a priesthood that represents a people before Yahweh. Its
names, memorials, and holy inscription belong to commanded service. The correspondence between
chapters 28 and 39 is strong enough to track execution, while their differences protect the
reader from treating a making report as a full ritual manual. [[Law and Obedience]], [[Work]],
[[Priesthood]], and [[Holiness]] meet in prepared objects before their use.
