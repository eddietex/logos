# Leavened Bread with Thanksgiving - Leviticus 2 and 7

## Passages involved

- [[The Meal Offering]] — Leviticus 2:11–12.
- [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]] — Leviticus 6:16–17.
- [[Thanksgiving, Vows, and the Peace Offering]] — Leviticus 7:12–14.

## Analysis

Leviticus 2:11 excludes yeast from the meal offering and from altar burning; 2:12
permits the materials as first fruits without their rising on the altar. Leviticus
6:16–17 also requires the priestly meal-offering portion to be eaten and baked without
yeast. These are explicit restrictions, not merely a preference for one recipe.

Leviticus 7:12–14 requires unleavened preparations and leavened bread with thanksgiving,
then assigns one from each offering to the priest who sprinkles blood. It does not
instruct burning that leavened bread. **Apparent contradiction:** a general claim that
all bread brought to Yahweh must be unleavened conflicts with 7:13. The texts distinguish
the meal offering and altar use from thanksgiving accompaniments; neither prescription
should be silently rewritten to match the other.

Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 2; 6–7. No source-language assertion is made.

## Significance

The destination and offering type matter. Yeast cannot be treated as inherently sinful
on the strength of these laws, which themselves require it in one setting.
