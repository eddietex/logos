# The Word Is Near You - Deuteronomy 30.11-14 in Romans 10.6-8

## Passages involved

- [[The Word Is Near and the Choice Is Life]] — Deuteronomy 30:11–14; *"It is not in heaven, that
  you should say, 'Who will go up for us to heaven, and bring it to us …?'"* (12); *"Neither is it
  beyond the sea"* (13); *"But the word is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, that
  you may do it"* (14).
- **Romans 10:6–8** *(not yet ingested)* — *"But the righteousness which is of faith says this,
  'Don't say in your heart, "Who will ascend into heaven?" (that is, to bring Christ down); or, "Who
  will descend into the abyss?" (that is, to bring Christ up from the dead.)' But what does it say?
  'The word is near you, in your mouth, and in your heart'; that is, the word of faith, which we
  preach"*.
- **Deuteronomy 9:4** — *"Don't say in your heart"*, the warning against Israel claiming the land for
  its own righteousness — [[Not Because of Your Righteousness]]

Deuteronomy is quoted from `raw/bible/`; Romans 10:6–8 from the WEB at bible-api.com
(`?translation=web`), retrieved 2026-11-10. Romans is not stored in raw/.

## Analysis

**What Paul keeps.** The question about going up to heaven, and the closing affirmation that the word
is near, *in your mouth, and in your heart*. In the WEB of Deuteronomy the word is *very near … that
you may do it*; Paul's quotation stops at *in your heart* and does not include *that you may do it*.

**What Paul changes.** Deuteronomy's second question is horizontal: *Who will go over the sea for us*
(13). Paul's is vertical: *Who will descend into the abyss?* In the SBLGNT the verb is *katabainō*,
paired with *anabainō* for the ascent, and the place is *abyssos* (MorphGNT lemmas, checked). Where
Deuteronomy pairs height and distance, Paul pairs height and depth, and that lets him read the two
questions of the ascension and the resurrection. Whether Paul's wording follows an existing Greek or
Jewish interpretive form of Deuteronomy 30:13 has not been checked here; no claim is made about his
source.

**What Paul adds.** Each line is followed by *that is* (*tout' estin*), an explanation applying it to
Christ and then to *the word of faith, which we preach*. The speaker is *the righteousness which is of
faith*, personified. Paul also opens with *Don't say in your heart*, which is not in Deuteronomy 30
but is the phrase of Deuteronomy 9:4, and of 8:17 in
[[Remember the Wilderness and the Giver of Wealth]]. Whether that echo is intended is an inference, not
a claim the passage makes.

**What the two share.** Both deny that the thing needed is out of reach and must be fetched by some
heroic journey. In Deuteronomy it is the command, already spoken to Israel. In Romans it is *the word
of faith*, already preached. In both the word is in the mouth and the heart.

**The tension.** Deuteronomy 30:11–14 is about the command being doable. Romans 10:5, immediately
before, speaks of a righteousness based on the law (paraphrased, not quoted; Romans 10:5 was not
verified for this page), and verse 6 sets *the righteousness which is of faith* beside it. Readers
differ on whether Paul reads Deuteronomy 30 against its sense, or draws out what 30:6 — the
circumcised heart — already implied. The wiki notes both readings and settles neither. The Romans side will be revisited when Romans is ingested.

## Significance

Deuteronomy 30:11–14 is one of the most direct Old Testament texts on whether the law can be kept, and
its New Testament reuse makes it one of the most argued about. Read with the promise of 30:6 just
before it, the nearness of the word in the heart may already belong to what Yahweh promises to do;
read on its own, it says Israel already has what it needs. Paul's use sits on that line.
