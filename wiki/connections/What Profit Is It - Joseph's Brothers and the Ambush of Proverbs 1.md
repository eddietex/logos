# What Profit Is It - Joseph's Brothers and the Ambush of Proverbs 1

## Passages involved

- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:18–28; the brothers' plot at Dothan, the pit, and the
  sale for twenty pieces of silver.
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:21–22; *"We are certainly guilty concerning our
  brother … behold, his blood is required."*
- [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] — Proverbs 1:10–19; a gang's invitation to ambush the innocent, and
  the father's reply.

## Analysis

Proverbs 1:8–19 is not written about Joseph, and nothing in it alludes to Genesis by name. The
parallel is one of vocabulary and plot, and it is close enough to be worth setting out. All Hebrew
below was checked against Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*; English is the stored WEB.

| | Genesis 37; 42 | Proverbs 1 |
|---|---|---|
| the plot | *"they conspired against him to kill him"* (37:18) | *"Let's lay in wait for blood"* (1:11) |
| the invitation | *"Come"* — *ləkû* (37:20) | *"Come with us"* — *ləkâ ʾittānû* (1:11) |
| the pit | *"cast him into one of the pits"* — *habbōrôt* (37:20, 22, 24) | *"those who go down into the pit"* — *bôr* (1:12) |
| the blood | Reuben: *"Shed no blood"* — *ʾal-tišpəkû-dām* (37:22) | *"They hurry to shed blood"* — *lišpok-dām* (1:16) |
| the gain | Judah: *"What profit is it"* — *mah-beṣaʿ* (37:26) | *"everyone who is greedy for gain"* — *bōṣēaʿ bāṣaʿ* (1:19) |
| the purse | *"twenty pieces of silver"* (37:28) | *"We'll all have one purse"* (1:14) |
| the end | *"his blood is required"* (42:22) | *"these lay wait for their own blood"* (1:18) |

**The differences are as instructive as the likenesses.** The gang of Proverbs 1 is a gang of
strangers recruiting a young man into violence against an outsider; Joseph's brothers are a family
turning on one of their own. The gang's speech is unanimous; the brothers' is not — [[Reuben]] argues
against bloodshed and plans a rescue (37:21–22), and [[Judah]]'s *"What profit is it?"* turns them from
murder to sale (37:26–27). In Proverbs' terms, Judah's question is the right word asked for the wrong
reason: it sees that killing gains nothing, and finds gain in selling instead. **The brothers do not
consent to the whole of what the gang proposes, and they consent to enough.**

**And the reversal of Proverbs 1:18 arrives in Genesis by way of conscience, not ambush.** No trap
springs on the brothers. Some twenty years on, before a governor they do not recognise, they say to one
another *"We are certainly guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the distress of his soul,
when he begged us, and we wouldn't listen. Therefore this distress has come upon us"* (42:21), and
Reuben adds *"his blood is required"* (42:22). They read their own distress as the return of the
blood they lay in wait for. Genesis lets them say it and does not confirm their reading as a verdict
— the governor is the brother himself, and the distress is his test of them, not a penalty — which
is one more reason to treat Proverbs 1:18 as the shape a way of life takes rather than a mechanism.
See [[His Blood Is Required - Darash and the Reckoning for Blood]] and
[[Truly We Are Guilty - The Brothers' Confession and the Gap in Genesis 37]]; the price is followed on
[[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]].

## Significance

The pairing shows the lecture of Proverbs 1 is not a caricature. A father who wants his son to hear
how attractive the gang's invitation is (*"one purse"*, belonging, gain) could find the whole pattern
in Israel's own founding family, where the gang was Jacob's sons and the purse twenty pieces of
silver. It also shows the limits of the lecture's scheme: Genesis has a Reuben and a Judah,
partial refusals and a profit motive that saves a life while selling it, and an end that is
repentance rather than ruin. See [[Retribution]], [[Wealth and Possessions]], [[Blood]], and
[[Brotherhood]].
