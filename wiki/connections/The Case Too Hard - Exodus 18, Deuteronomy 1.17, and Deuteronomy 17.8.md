# The Case Too Hard - Exodus 18, Deuteronomy 1.17, and Deuteronomy 17.8

## Passages involved

- [[Jethro's Counsel]] — Exodus 18:22, 26; *"every great matter they shall bring to you, but every
  small matter they shall judge themselves"* (18:22); *"They brought the hard causes to Moses"*
  (18:26).
- [[Judges Appointed for the People]] — Deuteronomy 1:17; *"The case that is too hard for you, you
  shall bring to me, and I will hear it."*
- [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]] — Deuteronomy 17:8–13; *"If there arises a matter too
  hard for you in judgment … then you shall arise, and go up to the place which Yahweh your God
  chooses"* (17:8).
- [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]] — Genesis 18:14; *"Is anything too hard for
  Yahweh?"*
- Deuteronomy 30:11 — *"For this commandment which I command you today is not too hard for you or
  too distant."* Stored WEB; not yet ingested.
- 2 Chronicles 19:8–11 — Jehoshaphat's court at Jerusalem, of Levites, priests, and heads of
  fathers' houses, with the chief priest over matters of Yahweh. Not yet ingested and not quoted.

## Analysis

**Three tellings of one problem: what happens to the case the ordinary judges cannot settle.**

| | who judges ordinary cases | where the hard case goes | the word for *hard* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exodus 18:22, 26 | the rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties, tens | to Moses | *haddābār haggādōl*, *the great matter* (22); *haddābār haqqāšeh*, *the hard matter* (26) |
| Deuteronomy 1:17 | the tribal heads made judges | to Moses: *"bring to me, and I will hear it"* | *yiqšeh*, from *qāšâ* |
| Deuteronomy 17:8–9 | judges and officers in every town | up to the chosen place, to the Levitical priests and *"the judge who shall be in those days"* | *yippālēʾ*, from *pālāʾ* |

(Hebrew from WLC, morphhb, Exodus 18:22, 26; Deuteronomy 1:17; 17:8.)

**The first two send the case to a person, the third to a place.** Exodus 18 and Deuteronomy 1 are set
in the wilderness, with Moses alive and present. Deuteronomy 17 looks to the land, after Moses. The
role he held is now divided: a judge who changes with *"those days"*, and the priests who stand
*"to minister there before Yahweh"* (17:12). Nothing in the text says that the place's court replaces
Moses, but it does the work 1:17 gave him.

**The verdict gets the authority of the law.** Exodus 18 says nothing about refusal. Deuteronomy 17
makes the higher court's ruling binding, in the words used for the commandments: *"You shall not
turn away from the sentence which they shall show you, to the right hand, nor to the left"* (17:11;
compare 5:32). The man who refuses it *"shall die"* (17:12).

**The change of word.** Exodus 18:26 and Deuteronomy 1:17 call the case *qāšeh*, *hard*, the adjective of
Israel's *"hard service"* in Egypt (Exodus 1:14) and of the *"stiff-necked"* people (Exodus 32:9). Deuteronomy 17:8 uses the niphal of *pālāʾ*, the verb of
*wonders*. The form *yippālēʾ* with the noun *dābār* occurs in the Torah only at Deuteronomy 17:8 and
Genesis 18:14, *hăyippālēʾ mêYHWH dābār*, *"Is anything too hard for Yahweh?"* (WLC, morphhb, lemma
and form search across Genesis–Deuteronomy). Deuteronomy 30:11 uses the participle to say the
commandment is *not* too hard: *lōʾ-niplēʾt hiwʾ mimməkā*. So the root marks three limits. Nothing is
beyond Yahweh. Some cases are beyond the town court. The commandment is not beyond Israel. **What is
too hard for the town is taken to the place where Yahweh's name dwells.** That pattern is a reading of
the shared vocabulary; none of the texts cites another.

**Jehoshaphat** is commonly read as carrying out this law. 2 Chronicles 19 has him set judges in the
fortified cities and a court at Jerusalem of Levites, priests, and heads of families, with the chief
priest over Yahweh's matters and another officer over the king's. Chronicles is not yet
ingested, and the wiki records the parallel without quoting it or settling whether the Chronicler
had Deuteronomy 17 in view.

## Significance

Moses' complaint in Deuteronomy 1:9, *"I am not able to bear you myself alone"*, is answered twice.
In the wilderness, by sharing the load with judges and keeping the hard case. In the land, by giving
the hard case to the place. The law plans for the end of its mediator. Its answer is not another
Moses, though 18:15 will promise a prophet *"like me"*, but a court at the place, with priests
and a judge, whose verdict is to be kept like the law itself. See [[Judgment]],
[[Priesthood]], [[Law and Obedience]], [[Mediation and Advocacy]], and
[[The Judgment Is God's - Exodus 23, Leviticus 19.15, and Deuteronomy 1.17]].
