# Your Eyes Have Seen - Remembered Victory and Joshua’s Courage

## Passages involved
- [[The Spies and the Refusal to Go Up]] — Deuteronomy 1:19–33
- [[The Defeat of Og Recounted]] — Deuteronomy 3:1–11
- [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]] — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

## Analysis

At Kadesh, Moses argues from what Israel has seen in Egypt and the wilderness that Yahweh will
fight for them (1:29–31). The speech nevertheless records their failure to believe (1:32).
In 3:21–22 Moses appeals to Joshua’s sight of the two kings’ defeat as the precedent for the
kingdoms ahead. The reassurance given against Og had itself appealed to the defeat of Sihon
(3:2). Each remembered deliverance supplies grounds for facing the next threat.

This is a recurring argument within Moses’ retrospect, not a prediction independently fulfilled
by the wording of a later verse. Deuteronomy 3:21–22 records the exhortation, not Joshua’s spoken
answer or a description of his private emotions. The preceding call for the eastern warriors to
cross also prevents divine warfare from being read as exemption from human service (3:18–20).

## Significance

Remembered victory equips a successor, but seeing does not mechanically produce trust: Kadesh
remains the counterexample. See [[Faith]], [[Remembrance]], and [[War]].
