## Analysis

### The five kings at Makkedah — Joshua 10:16–27

Joshua now gives his commanders the encouragement he had received. In Deuteronomy 3:21–22,
Moses points him to the defeated kings as evidence of what Yahweh will do to kingdoms ahead.
In Joshua 10:24–25, the commanders put their feet on five defeated kings’ necks and Joshua
promises the same divine action against future enemies. The charge to be strong and courageous
also recalls Joshua 1:6–9. The receiver of encouragement has become its speaker.

This is a narrative and English-language comparison, not a claim of exact Hebrew identity.
The violent public subjection is part of the scene and should not disappear into a generic
lesson about confidence. Sources: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:21–22 and Joshua 1:6–9; 10:24–25.

See [[The Five Kings in the Cave at Makkedah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:16–27.


At Kadesh, Moses argues from what Israel has seen in Egypt and the wilderness that Yahweh will
fight for them (1:29–31). The speech nevertheless records their failure to believe (1:32).
In 3:21–22 Moses appeals to Joshua’s sight of the two kings’ defeat as the precedent for the
kingdoms ahead. The reassurance given against Og had itself appealed to the defeat of Sihon
(3:2). Each remembered deliverance supplies grounds for facing the next threat.

This is a recurring argument within Moses’ retrospect, not a prediction independently fulfilled
by the wording of a later verse. Deuteronomy 3:21–22 records the exhortation, not Joshua’s spoken
answer or a description of his private emotions. The preceding call for the eastern warriors to
cross also prevents divine warfare from being read as exemption from human service (3:18–20).

Deuteronomy 11:2–7 extends the argument from victories in war to the whole sequence: signs in
Egypt, the sea, the wilderness, and the earth opening under Dathan and Abiram. Moses insists he
speaks to those whose "eyes have seen all of Yahweh’s great work" (7), not to their children
(2). Here the seen deeds ground obedience and possession of the land (8), not only courage in
battle. Judgment on rebels inside Israel is counted among them, so what was seen is a warning
as well as a reassurance.

Sources: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:19–33 and 3:1–22. No source-language assertion is made.

Deuteronomy 31:4 explicitly names Sihon and Og as the precedent for the nations ahead.
The encouragement now reaches all Israel (6) before Moses addresses Joshua publicly (7–8).
Remembered victory and promised continuing presence support both congregation and successor.
Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:1–8.

## Significance

Remembered victory equips a successor, but seeing does not mechanically produce trust: Kadesh
remains the counterexample. See [[Faith]], [[Remembrance]], and [[War]].
