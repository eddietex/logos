# Shunned by His Neighbor - Proverbs 14.20 and Job 19.13-19

## Passages involved

- [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] — Proverbs 19:1, 4, 6–7, 17, 22.

- [[A Way That Seems Right]] — Proverbs 14:20–21.
- Proverbs 19:4, 7 — the same observation restated; now ingested in the passage linked above.
- [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] — Job 19:13–19.

## Analysis

Proverbs 14:20 reports a social fact:

> The poor person is shunned even by his own neighbor, but the rich person has many friends.

It neither recommends nor condemns. The next verse does the judging: *"He who despises his neighbor
sins, but blessed is he who has pity on the poor"* (14:21). Proverbs returns to the observation at
19:4 — *"Wealth adds many friends, but the poor is separated from his friend"* — and at 19:7, where
*"All the relatives of the poor shun him"* and he *"pursues them with pleas, but they are gone."*

**Job 19 is the observation told from inside.** Stripped of everything, Job lists who has left him:
brothers put far away, acquaintances estranged, relatives gone, familiar friends forgetful, his
household counting him *"a stranger"*, his servant not answering though he begs him with his mouth,
his breath offensive to his wife, children despising him, and *"All my familiar friends abhor me"*
(19:13–19). Proverbs 19:7's pleading poor man and Job's begging master are the same figure.

**Description is not endorsement.** Read alone, 14:20 could be taken as worldly advice about whom to
befriend. Placed beside 14:21 it is the premise of a judgment: the shunning is real, and it is sin.
Job 19 shows the cost of that sin to the one shunned. Neither text cites the other; the parallel is
thematic.

Sources: Proverbs 14:20–21; 19:4, 7; Job 19:13–19, checked in stored WEB.

### Proverbs 19 in context

The ingested chapter now places the abandonment in its own moral setting. The poor person has
integrity (19:1), pity for him counts as lending to Yahweh (17), and he ranks above a liar (22).
Gifts attract friends in verse 6, but Yahweh's promised reward in verse 17 gives a reason to aid the
person unable to purchase them. Job 19:13–21 is a thematic comparison, not a diagnosis that he has
earned rejection. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 19:1, 4, 6–7, 17, 22; Job 19:13–21.

## Significance

[[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] lose community along with goods, a loss [[Wealth and Possessions]]
does not repair once it has gone. The pairing also keeps Proverbs' observational sayings from being
read as moral approval of what they observe.
