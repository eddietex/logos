# The Two Arks - Noah's Ship and Moses' Basket

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 6:14–7:24** — *"Make a ship of gopher wood … and shall seal it inside and outside with
  pitch"* (6:14); the *tēbâ* carries Noah's household through the flood —
  [[Noah and the Flood]]
- **Exodus 2:3, 5** — *"she took a papyrus basket for him, and coated it with tar and with pitch"*;
  the *tēbâ* carries the infant Moses on the river of Pharaoh's decree — [[The Birth of Moses]]
- **Exodus 1:22** — *"You shall cast every son who is born into the river"* —
  [[The Oppression in Egypt]]
- **1 Corinthians 10:1–2** — the fathers *"baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea"* — see
  [[The Flood and Baptism]]

## Analysis

***Tēbâ* is used in the Hebrew Bible of two objects and no others**: Noah's vessel (Genesis 6–9,
the WEB's *ship*) and Moses' basket (Exodus 2:3, 5). The ordinary words for a boat (*ʾŏnîyâ*,
*sĕpînâ*) are never used of either, and the ark of the covenant is a different word altogether
(*ʾărôn*). **The narrator of Exodus had any number of ways to say *basket* and chose the one that
had only ever meant Noah's ark.**

| | Noah's *tēbâ* | Moses' *tēbâ* |
|---|---|---|
| made of | gopher wood (6:14) | papyrus, *gōmeʾ* (2:3) |
| sealed with | *kōper*, pitch (6:14) | *ḥēmār* and *zepet*, tar and pitch (2:3) |
| built by | Noah, at God's command and to God's measurements | a mother, with no word from God |
| carries | one righteous household and the animals | one infant |
| through | the flood God sends on all flesh | the river a king has made a grave |
| steered by | nothing — no rudder, no sail | nothing — laid in the reeds and left |
| comes to rest | *"on Ararat's mountains"* (8:4) | in the hands of Pharaoh's daughter (2:5) |
| opens onto | a new world and a covenant (9:8–17) | a deliverer and, eventually, the exodus |

**In both cases the water is judgment and the ark is how one life passes through it.** Neither
vessel is steered; both are at the mercy of what carries them, and in both the narrator lets the
reader see that the one who placed the passenger inside does not control the outcome. **God shuts
Noah in (7:16); the mother lays her son in the reeds and her daughter watches from far off.**

**The difference is God's visibility.** Genesis 6 is God's plan in God's words: he decides, he
specifies cubits and decks and a door, he shuts the door. Exodus 2 has no word from God at all. **The
second ark is the first one built from below** — by a mother improvising with reeds and tar,
obeying the letter of a king's law. The narrator trusts the shared word to carry the theology he
does not state.

**The sealant is a different word, and the difference points elsewhere.** The wiki's earlier pages
say both arks are *"sealed with pitch"* and *"waterproofed with bitumen"*, which is true in substance
and in the WEB's English. **The Hebrew is not the same**: Noah's is *kōper* (6:14, and the verb
*kāpar*, the root later used of atonement); the basket's is *ḥēmār* and *zepet*. *Ḥēmār* is Babel's
*"tar for mortar"* (Genesis 11:3) and the *"tar pits"* of Siddim (14:10). **So the basket is Noah's
ark in name and Babel's tower in material** — the rescue vessel made of what the proud city was made
of. See [[Come, Let Us - Babel's Bricks and Pharaoh's]].

**Both arks precede an exodus through water.** Noah's household comes out of the ark into a world
washed clean; Moses comes out of the river to lead Israel through the *sûp* — the *"reeds"* where
his basket lay (2:3) are the *yam sûp*, the WEB's *Red Sea* (first named at 10:19; then 13:18; 15:4; see [[The Red Sea]]). **Paul calls that crossing
a baptism** (1 Corinthians 10:2), and 1 Peter calls the flood one (1 Peter 3:20–21), so the New
Testament baptizes both ark stories, one directly and one through what it leads to. See
[[The Flood and Baptism]].

## Significance

The shared word makes Moses a second Noah: a single life preserved through a judgment by water so
that, through him, a people survives. It also states, without a single divine verb, that the God of
the flood is at work in a basket on the Nile — the reader who knows Genesis supplies what Exodus
leaves out. See [[Providence]], [[Life and Death]], and [[Judgment]].
