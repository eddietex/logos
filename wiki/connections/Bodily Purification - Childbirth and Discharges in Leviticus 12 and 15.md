# Bodily Purification - Childbirth and Discharges in Leviticus 12 and 15

## Passages involved

- [[Purification After Childbirth]] — Leviticus 12:1–8.
- [[Bodily Discharges and Cleansing]] — Leviticus 15:1–33.


## Analysis

Leviticus 12 compares the mother’s initial impurity to menstruation (2, 5), whose
contact rules are detailed in 15:19–24. Both chapters connect bodily purification
with approach at the Tent of Meeting and priestly offerings, without specifying a
personal offense as the cause of the condition.

The schedules differ. Childbirth has seven plus thirty-three days for a son and
fourteen plus sixty-six for a daughter (12:2–5). Chapter 15 distinguishes evening
limits, seven days of menstruation, and seven days counted after prolonged discharges
cease. Childbirth normally requires a lamb and a bird, with two birds for poverty
(12:6–8); the discharge cases prescribe two birds without that means condition
(15:14–15, 29–30). Ordinary menstruation is not given those offerings in 15:19–24.

A sin offering therefore does not by itself establish wrongdoing. The shared concern
is cleansing and access, but the respective legal procedures are not interchangeable.

## Significance

The bodily laws give a shared framework without a single universal timetable.
Comparing them guards against importing childbirth’s offerings into every period.
