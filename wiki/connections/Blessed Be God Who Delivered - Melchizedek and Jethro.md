# Blessed Be God Who Delivered - Melchizedek and Jethro

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 14:18–20** — *"Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine: and he was priest of God Most
  High. He blessed him, and said, 'Blessed be Abram of God Most High, possessor of heaven and earth: and blessed be
  God Most High, who has delivered your enemies into your hand.' Abram gave him a tenth of all."* —
  [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]
- **Exodus 18:1, 9–12** — *"Jethro, the priest of Midian"*; *"Blessed be Yahweh, who has delivered you out of the
  hand of the Egyptians … Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"*; a burnt offering, and Aaron and the
  elders *"eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God"* — [[Jethro's Counsel]]

## Analysis

**Twice before Sinai a priest from outside the chosen line meets the chosen one after a victory, blesses God for the
deliverance, and shares bread.** In Genesis and Exodus the word *kōhēn* is used of only three non-Israelite
priesthoods — Melchizedek's, Egypt's (Potiphera, *"priest of On"*, and Pharaoh's priests), and Jethro's (WLC lemma
search, Genesis–Exodus) — **and the two that bless God are these two.**

| | Melchizedek (Genesis 14) | Jethro (Exodus 18) |
|---|---|---|
| title | *kōhēn ləʾēl ʿelyôn*, *"priest of God Most High"* (14:18) | *kōhēn midyān*, *"the priest of Midian"* (18:1) |
| occasion | Abram returning from the defeat of the four kings | Israel delivered from Egypt and from Amalek |
| the meeting | comes out to Abram | comes to Moses at the Mountain of God |
| bread | *"brought out bread and wine"* (14:18) | *"to eat bread … before God"* (18:12) |
| the blessing of God | ***ûbārûk ʾēl ʿelyôn***, *"blessed be God Most High"* (14:20) | ***bārûk YHWH***, *"Blessed be Yahweh"* (18:10) |
| the reason | *"who has delivered (miggēn) your enemies into your hand"* | *"who has delivered (hiṣṣîl) you out of the hand of the Egyptians"* |
| the hand | the enemies *into* Abram's hand (*bəyādekā*) | Israel *out of* Egypt's hand (*miyyad*), three times |
| the name of God | *ʾĒl ʿElyôn*, which Abram then names as *"Yahweh, God Most High"* (14:22) | *Yahweh*, by name, *"greater than all gods"* |
| what passes between | Abram gives him a tenth | Jethro gives Moses counsel |

(Hebrew from WLC.) **The verbs of deliverance differ** — *miggēn*, *handed over*, and *hiṣṣîl*, *snatched out* —
**and so does the direction of the hand**, but the shape is the same: **a blessing of God spoken by an outsider,
with *bārûk* and *who has delivered*, over a meal.**

**The differences are as telling.** Melchizedek blesses Abram as well as God, and receives a tithe — **he is above
Abram**, as Hebrews will argue (Hebrews 7:4–7, by reference). **Jethro blesses only God**, confesses that he has
learned something (*"Now I know"*), and then, the next day, **serves Moses with advice**; he receives nothing, and
goes home. **Melchizedek's god is *ʾĒl ʿElyôn*, which Abram identifies with Yahweh; Jethro names Yahweh himself**,
learned from Moses' telling (18:8). **One is a priest whose knowledge of God precedes Abram's story; the other is a
priest who learns from Israel's.**

**Neither text cross-refers the other**, and nothing in Exodus 18 alludes to Genesis 14 in particular — *bārûk
YHWH* is also Noah's (Genesis 9:26) and Abraham's servant's (24:27), and *"Now I know"* is the angel's at Moriah
(22:12) (WLC). **The likeness is a pattern of scene, not a quotation.** Later tradition built far more on
Melchizedek (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 5–7) and on Jethro (as the model convert, in rabbinic sources not checked here)
than either text says.

## Significance

- **Blessing flows toward Israel's God from outside before Sinai.** The nations are promised blessing *through* Abram
  (Genesis 12:3); these two priests bless *Abram's God*, at the two points where the line is most visibly delivered.
  See [[The Nations]] and [[Blessing]].
- **Priesthood exists before Aaron's, and the text does not treat it as illegitimate.** Both scenes have a priest and
  no altar named; Jethro's offering is the first burnt offering actually made in Exodus. See
  [[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]] and [[Priesthood]].
- **Confession can come from the outsider first.** Pharaoh's *"I don't know Yahweh"* (Exodus 5:2) is answered not
  by Pharaoh but by a Midianite's *"Now I know"*, and Israel's own covenant confession at Sinai comes after it.
