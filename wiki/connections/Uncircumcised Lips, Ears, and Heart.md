# Uncircumcised Lips, Ears, and Heart

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 17:14** — *"The uncircumcised male who is not circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin,
  that soul shall be cut off from his people. He has broken my covenant."* The literal sense, and the
  first occurrence of *ʿārēl* — [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]]
- **Exodus 4:24–26** — Zipporah circumcises her son with a flint at the lodging place —
  [[Moses Returns to Egypt]]
- **Exodus 6:12, 30** — *"How then shall Pharaoh listen to me, who am of uncircumcised lips?"*;
  *"Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh listen to me?"* The first figurative
  use — [[I Am Yahweh]]; the second is answered by Aaron as Moses' prophet (7:1) —
  [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]]
- **Exodus 12:48** — *"no uncircumcised person shall eat of it"*: the Passover closed to the
  uncircumcised — [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]]. **Spoken to Moses and Aaron (12:43)
  — so the man who called his own lips *ʿăral* is the one who delivers the rule** (WLC); the stranger
  who wants to eat must first have *"all his males"* circumcised, in the words of Genesis 17:10. See
  [[One Law for the Native and the Stranger]]
- **Leviticus 26:41** — *"if then their uncircumcised heart is humbled"*, in exile — [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]]
- **Deuteronomy 10:16; 30:6** — Israel told to circumcise the foreskin of its heart; then God
  promising to circumcise it himself *(not yet ingested; paraphrased)*
- **Jeremiah 4:4; 6:10; 9:25–26** — circumcise yourselves to Yahweh; an ear that is uncircumcised and
  cannot listen; nations and Israel alike uncircumcised in heart *(not yet ingested; paraphrased)*
- **Acts 7:51; Romans 2:28–29** — Stephen's *"uncircumcised in heart and ears"*; Paul's circumcision
  of the heart, by the Spirit *(not yet ingested; paraphrased)*

## Analysis

**Moses is the first person in Scripture to call a part of the body other than the flesh
*uncircumcised*.** In Genesis, Exodus, and Job the adjective *ʿārēl* occurs only four times (WLC
lemma search): Genesis 17:14, Exodus 6:12, 6:30, and 12:48. The first and last are law, and both
are about exclusion. The uncircumcised male is *cut off* from his people; the uncircumcised person
may not eat the Passover. **Between those two laws Moses uses the word of himself.** He means that his
lips are closed, unfit, and cannot do what they are for. The objection matches 4:10 (*"slow of
speech"*, literally *heavy of mouth*), but the image is darker. **He describes himself as a mouth
outside the covenant.**

**The timing is pointed.** Only one chapter earlier Moses' own son was circumcised in a crisis, and
by his wife rather than by him (4:24–26). The narrator does not connect the two passages. A reader of
Exodus in order meets them a page apart.

**The image then moves from the lips to the ear and the heart.** The later uses (paraphrased here;
not yet ingested) apply it to the organs of *listening* and *willing*, which is where Exodus 6 already
puts the failure. Moses blames his lips. The narrator, though, says it was the people who *"didn't
listen"* (6:9). Jeremiah later speaks of an ear that is *uncircumcised* and so cannot listen.
**Exodus 6 holds both failures: an uncircumcised mouth, as Moses fears, and ears that cannot hear,
as Israel shows.** Deuteronomy turns the image into a command (circumcise your heart) and then into
a promise (God will do it). Paul and Stephen take it into the New Testament.

## Significance

- **It makes circumcision a matter of fitness for God's purpose, not only of membership.** The first
  figurative use concerns speaking for God, not belonging to his people.
- **God does not accept the objection and does not correct it.** At 6:13 he repeats the command. At
  7:1 he answers the repeated objection of 6:30 by sending Aaron as Moses' prophet. **The
  uncircumcised lips are never healed. They are given a partner.**
