# The Staff of Bread - Leviticus 26.26-29, Deuteronomy 28, and Ezekiel 4-5

## Passages involved

- [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] — Leviticus 26:22, 25–26, 29, 33, 39; *"When I break your staff of bread, ten women shall bake
  your bread in one oven, and they shall deliver your bread again by weight."*
- **Deuteronomy 28:53** — *"You will eat the fruit of your own body, the flesh of your sons and of
  your daughters"* (WEB).
- **Ezekiel 4:16–17** — *"I will break the staff of bread in Jerusalem. They will eat bread by weight,
  and with fearfulness … and pine away in their iniquity."* (WEB)
- **Ezekiel 5:10, 16–17** — fathers and sons eating each other; famine, evil animals, pestilence, and
  sword (WEB).
- **Ezekiel 14:13, 21** — the staff of bread again, and *"my four severe judgments"* (WEB).
- **Psalm 105:16** — the phrase of Joseph's famine; WEB has *"He destroyed the food supplies"*.
- **Jeremiah 19:9** — *"the flesh of their sons and the flesh of their daughters"* (WEB).
- **2 Kings 6:28–29; Lamentations 4:10** — the thing narrated: at a siege, and after one (WEB).

## Analysis

**The phrase.** *Maṭṭēh leḥem* with *šābar*, *break*, occurs in five verses: Leviticus 26:26; Psalm
105:16; Ezekiel 4:16; 5:16; 14:13 (WLC lemma search). The image is usually explained as the staff or
rod on which ring-loaves were carried, or as bread as the staff of life; the texts do not say.

**Ezekiel's vocabulary is close to Leviticus 26 beyond that one phrase** (WLC):

| Leviticus 26 | Ezekiel | Shared |
|---|---|---|
| 26:26 *wəhēšîbû laḥməkem bammišqāl* | 4:16 *wəʾākəlû leḥem bəmišqāl* | bread by weight |
| 26:39 *yimmaqqû baʿăwōnām* | 4:17 *wənāmaqqû baʿăwōnām* | pine away in their iniquity |
| 26:22 *ḥayyat haśśādeh … wəšikkəlâ* | 5:17 *ḥayyâ rāʿâ wəšikkəlukh* | animals and bereavement (compare 26:6 for *ḥayyâ rāʿâ*) |
| 26:33 *ʾezāreh … wahărîqōtî ʾaḥărêkem ḥāreb* | 5:12 *ʾezāreh wəḥereb ʾārîq ʾaḥărêhem* | scatter, and draw the sword after |
| 26:25 sword, pestilence, enemy | 5:17; 14:21 sword, famine, animals, pestilence | the list |

The Ezekiel 5:12 clause is the tightest match: the same verbs in the same order with the same object.
This page reports the shared wording. It does not argue which text depends on which.

**Eating the children.** Leviticus 26:29 is two clauses with no siege named, though verse 25 has
gathered Israel into cities. Deuteronomy 28:53 and Jeremiah 19:9 name the siege and repeat *the flesh
of sons and daughters*. Ezekiel 5:10 adds what the others lack: sons eating fathers. In 2 Kings 6:28–29
it is told as a case brought to a king, and in Lamentations 4:10 it is a line of grief. **The warning
appears as law, then as prophecy, then as a report. None softens it.**

**Sky and earth reversed.** A smaller parallel in the same families of text: Leviticus 26:19 has a
sky of iron and earth of brass; Deuteronomy 28:23 has a sky of brass and earth of iron (WLC; WEB).
The metals swap and the sense does not change.

## Significance

- It shows the sanctions of [[Curse]] and [[Covenant]] supplying later prophecy with its stock of
  phrases.
- It widens [[Famine and Plenty]]: in Genesis famine is unexplained, while here it is announced as
  a covenant sanction. Psalm 105:16 places the same phrase on the famine of Joseph's day, which
  brought no charge against anyone.
- It keeps [[Judgment]] from being abstract. These texts name the cost to children, and do not excuse
  it. Compare [[Retribution]] and its limits in Job.
- Compare [[The Bars of Their Yoke - Leviticus 26.4-13 and Ezekiel 34.25-28]], where Ezekiel takes up
  the blessing half of the same chapter.
