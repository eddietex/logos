# A Man in Whom Is the Spirit of God

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 1:2** — *"God's Spirit was hovering over the surface of the waters"* — ***rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm***,
  over an unformed world — [[The Creation of the World]]
- **Genesis 41:38** — Pharaoh: *"Can we find such a one as this, a man in whom is the Spirit of God?"* —
  [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Exodus 31:1–5; 35:30–35** — Bezalel, *"filled with the Spirit of God, in wisdom, and in
  understanding, and in knowledge, and in all kinds of workmanship"*
- **Numbers 24:2** — *"the Spirit of God came on him"*, of Balaam, a foreign diviner
- **Numbers 27:18** — Joshua, *"a man in whom is the Spirit"*
- **Judges 3:10; 6:34; 11:29; 14:6** — the Spirit coming on the judges, for a task and then leaving
- **1 Samuel 16:13–14** — the Spirit comes on David and departs from Saul in one verse
- **Daniel 4:8–9, 18; 5:11, 14** — Nebuchadnezzar and Belshazzar on Daniel: *"in whom is the spirit of
  the holy gods"*
- **Isaiah 11:2; 42:1; 61:1** — the Spirit resting on a coming figure, permanently
- **Luke 4:18–21; John 3:34; Acts 10:38** — that promise applied

## Analysis

**Genesis uses the phrase *rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* exactly twice, and the distance between them is the whole
book.**

**The first time it is over the deep** — *"the earth was formless and empty. Darkness was on the surface
of the deep and God's Spirit was hovering over the surface of the waters"* (1:2). **No person is
involved.** The second time it is **a thirty-year-old Hebrew convict who has been in the room for
roughly twenty verses** — and **the man who says it is the king of Egypt.**

### Three things about who says it

**1. Joseph does not claim it.** **His own account of himself is a denial**: *"It isn't in me"*
(41:16) — ***bilʿāday***, *apart from me*. **The chapter's theological verdict on Joseph is delivered by
somebody else**, and Joseph never mentions the Spirit at all, here or anywhere.

**2. A pagan king says it.** Genesis has a habit of putting its clearest confessions in foreign mouths —
[[Melchizedek]] blessing Abram *"of God Most High, possessor of heaven and earth"* (14:19),
[[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]] pleading *"in the integrity of my heart"* to God directly (20:5),
[[Laban]] admitting *"Yahweh has blessed me for your sake"* (30:27). **This is the strongest of them**,
and **the speaker is the head of the religion of [[On]].**

**3. It is a question, not a proclamation.** ***Hănimṣāʾ kāzeh** — "can we find such a one as this?"* —
**addressed to his own courtiers**, and the implied answer is no. **Pharaoh's grounds are empirical**: a
guild of professionals failed, a prisoner succeeded, **and the prisoner said in advance where the answer
was coming from.**

### What the phrase comes to mean

**In the Torah the Spirit of God on a person is almost always for a job.**

| who | for what | how long |
|---|---|---|
| **Joseph** (Genesis 41:38) | reading a dream, then running a famine | not said |
| **Bezalel** (Exodus 31:3) | **craftsmanship** — the tabernacle | the build |
| **the seventy elders** (Numbers 11:25) | bearing the burden of the people | *"they didn't do so again"* |
| **Balaam** (Numbers 24:2) | an oracle he was hired to reverse | three oracles |
| **Joshua** (Numbers 27:18) | succeeding Moses | his life |
| **the judges** | a deliverance, one at a time | the emergency |
| **Saul** (1 Samuel 16:14) | kingship | **withdrawn** |

**Bezalel is the closest formal parallel to Joseph** — **the pair *ḥokmâ* and *təbûnâ*, wisdom and
understanding, is used of both** (Genesis 41:33, 39; Exodus 31:3) — **and both gifts are practical.**
**Nothing in either text is about ecstasy or prophecy in the narrow sense.** What the Spirit confers in
these passages is **competence that exceeds the trained professional's**, which is precisely the claim
Genesis 41:8 sets up by summoning the guild and reporting a blank. See [[Wisdom]].

### The Daniel repetition

**Daniel 5:11 is Genesis 41:38 with the nouns changed**, and the Aramaic is
***dî rûaḥ ʾĕlāhîn qaddîšîn bēh*** — *in whom is the spirit of the holy gods.* **The plural is a pagan
speaking as a pagan**, exactly as Pharaoh's *ʾĕlōhîm* is, **and the setting is identical**: a king
troubled by something nobody in his establishment can read, a Hebrew exile brought in from outside the
system, a disclaimer, an interpretation, and an investiture in gold.

**Whether Daniel is written with Genesis 41 open is not decidable from the wording alone** — **the
vocabulary is what any Aramaic author would use** — **but the scene is the same scene.** See
[[Do Not Interpretations Belong to God - Joseph and Daniel]] and
[[The Signet Ring and the Second Chariot - Joseph, Mordecai, and Daniel]].

### Where it goes

**The prophets take the temporary, task-bound Spirit of the narratives and make it permanent.**
Isaiah 11:2 has it *"rest on him"* — *wənāḥâ ʿālāyw* — **a verb of settling rather than of rushing**; and
Isaiah 61:1's *"The Spirit of the Lord Yahweh is on me"* is the text Luke 4:18 has read in a synagogue and
declared fulfilled. **John 3:34 states the contrast outright**: *"for he gives the Spirit without
measure."*

**The line from Genesis 41 to there is not a prediction and this page does not claim one.** What it is
**is a vocabulary**: the Bible's way of saying that a person is doing something they could not have been
trained to do **starts with an Egyptian king trying to explain what just happened in his throne room.**

## Significance

**Genesis's highest estimate of a human being is spoken by a foreigner, about a slave, in a sentence the
slave would not have said about himself.** The phrase's first application is not to a patriarch, a
priest, or a prophet — **it is to an administrator** — and what it certifies is **that he read something
correctly and told the truth about where the reading came from.**

**For the wiki's purposes the practical point is the pattern**: the Spirit of God in the Hebrew Bible
lands most often on people doing concrete work under pressure, **and Genesis 41:38 is where the pattern
begins.**
