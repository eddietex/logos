# Profit and Remembrance - Proverbs and Ecclesiastes 1

## Passages involved

- [[Vanity of Vanities]] — Ecclesiastes 1:3, 11.
- [[A Way That Seems Right]] — Proverbs 14:23.
- [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]] — Proverbs 10:7.
- [[The Worthy Woman]] — Proverbs 31:31.

## Analysis

**Profit asserted and questioned.** Proverbs 14:23 affirms that hard work produces profit,
contrasting it with impoverishing talk. Ecclesiastes 1:3 asks what people gain from all their
labor under the sun, and places the question within a poem of recurrence and passing
generations. Proverbs 31:31 adds an obligation: others should give the worker the fruit of
her hands and recognize her works. The opening of Ecclesiastes does not cancel that duty.

**Memory blessed and denied.** Proverbs 10:7 distinguishes the blessed memory of the righteous
from the decay of the wicked person's name. Ecclesiastes 1:11 announces forgetting for both
predecessors and successors without distinguishing their moral character. This is an explicit
tension between the texts, not a reason to silently change either page's claim.

**A possible distinction has limits.** Proverbs can be read as describing real benefits and
community obligations within life, while Ecclesiastes asks what endures across generations.
That distinction helps explain how both can speak truthfully, but neither verse supplies a
formal division between short-term and ultimate profit. Nor does Ecclesiastes 1:11 promise an
exception for righteous people. This connection retains the difference instead of presenting
a harmonization as the text's own explanation.

Sources: all four passages checked in stored WEB on 2026-10-27. This comparison makes no claim
about shared Hebrew vocabulary or direct literary dependence.

## Significance

[[Work]] and [[Remembrance]] acquire a limit without losing their ethical claims. The reader
must both acknowledge good labor and face the insecurity of its lasting recognition.
[[Vanity]] brings that insecurity into the center of wisdom's inquiry.
