# The Signet Ring and the Second Chariot - Joseph, Mordecai, and Daniel

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 41:42–43** — the signet ring, fine linen, a gold chain, the second chariot, and a herald —
  [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Esther 3:10; 8:2** — Ahasuerus takes his signet ring off and gives it to Haman, **and then takes it
  back and gives it to Mordecai**
- **Esther 6:6–11** — the royal robe, the king's horse, and a herald crying before him in the city square
- **Esther 8:15; 10:3** — Mordecai in blue and white, a great crown of gold, and fine linen and purple;
  *"second to king Ahasuerus"* — ***mišneh***
- **Daniel 5:7, 16, 29** — scarlet, a chain of gold about the neck, and *"the third ruler in the
  kingdom"*
- **Daniel 2:48** — Daniel made *"ruler over the whole province of Babylon"* after reading a dream
- **Genesis 44:1–13** — Joseph's own *gābîaʿ*, the cup planted in Benjamin's sack *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Three Jews are raised to the second place in three pagan empires, and all three scenes use the same
props.**

| | **Joseph** (Genesis 41) | **Mordecai** (Esther) | **Daniel** (Daniel 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **what earns it** | reading a dream nobody else could | exposing a plot, recorded and forgotten | reading a wall nobody else could |
| **signet ring** | 41:42 — *"took off his signet ring … and put it on Joseph's hand"* | 8:2 — the identical gesture, the same ring Haman had | — |
| **robes** | 41:42 — *"robes of fine linen"* | 6:8; 8:15 — royal apparel, *"fine linen and purple"* | 5:29 — *"clothed with purple"* |
| **gold at the neck** | 41:42 — *"a gold chain about his neck"* | 8:15 — *"a great crown of gold"* | 5:29 — *"a chain of gold about his neck"* |
| **the vehicle** | 41:43 — *"the second chariot which he had"* | 6:8–9 — *"the horse that the king rides on"* | — |
| **the herald** | 41:43 — *"They cried before him, 'Bow the knee!'"* | 6:9, 11 — *"Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delights to honor"* | 5:29 — proclamation |
| **the rank** | 41:40 — *"only in the throne I will be greater than you"* | 10:3 — *"second to king Ahasuerus"* | 5:29 — *"the third ruler"* |
| **the interval first** | **two years forgotten** (40:23; 41:1) | **a night's insomnia, years after the deed** (6:1–3) | a career in and out of favour |

### The signet is the one that matters

**The robe is honour and the chain is wealth; the ring is the state.** ***Ṭabbaʿat*** is the seal that
authenticates a document — **whoever holds it can issue orders in the king's name and nobody can tell the
difference.** Esther makes the point brutally: **the same ring goes to Haman to authorise a genocide
(3:10) and then to Mordecai to authorise the answer to it (8:2)**, and **the book uses the identical
formula both times.** **The instrument is neutral; everything depends on whose hand it is on.**

**Genesis has no such irony available** — there is only one transfer — **but it is the same constitutional
act**: 41:44, *"without you shall no man lift up his hand or his foot in all the land of Egypt."*

### What is shared and what is not

**The shared shape is a genre**, and it has a name in the scholarship: **the court tale of the wise
courtier** — a Jew at a foreign court, an emergency the professionals cannot handle, a disclaimer of
personal credit, a correct reading, and an investiture. **Genesis 41 is the earliest instance and the
others are visibly cut from it.**

**But the differences are as sharp as the parallels, and the wiki records them.**

- **Joseph's rise is not a rescue of his people.** Mordecai's elevation saves the Jews of the empire and
  Daniel's is set inside a judgment on Babylon. **Joseph's saves Egypt** — **and only later, and
  incidentally as far as the chapter knows, his family** (45:7).
- **Joseph is not in exile.** He was sold; there is no community around him, no dietary scruple, no
  refusal to bow, and **no Israelite audience anywhere in Egypt.** **He is entirely alone**, which is
  what makes 41:51's *"all my father's house"* land as it does.
- **Joseph never uses his position against the court that gave it.** Mordecai writes counter-decrees;
  Daniel reads a sentence of doom to the king who is decorating him. **Joseph administers a famine for
  Pharaoh, competently, and 47:20 ends with Pharaoh owning all the land of Egypt.** See
  [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]].
- **The disclaimer is Joseph's invention and the others inherit it.** *"It isn't in me. God will give
  Pharaoh an answer of peace"* (41:16) becomes *"there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets"*
  (Daniel 2:28) — see
  [[Do Not Interpretations Belong to God - Joseph and Daniel]].

### One prop that runs the other way

***Mišneh***, *second*, is the word in Joseph's *"second chariot"* (41:43) **and in Mordecai's title at
Esther 10:3.** **It is also, in Genesis, what Joseph's own brothers are made to carry** — *"double
money"*, *kesep mišneh*, at 43:12, 15. **And the props come back as evidence**: **the silver cup planted
in Benjamin's sack** (44:2) is the vizier's own tableware used to manufacture a crime, **which is the
third time in Joseph's life that an object is produced against someone**, after the coat and the cloak.
See [[A Garment Used as Evidence - The Coat and the Cloak]].

## Significance

**The Bible has a standard picture of a Jew at the top of a gentile government, and Genesis 41 drew it.**
Every later version reuses the ring, the robe, the gold and the herald, **and every later version has an
Israelite community for the hero to save.**

**Joseph has none**, which is the feature of the original that the copies do not reproduce: **the man in
the second chariot has no people in that country at all**, and the seven years in which he is the most
powerful foreigner in the world are the seven in which **his father believes he is dead.**
