# Diviners, Mediums, and the Dead - Deuteronomy 18.10-11 and the Torah's Forbidden Arts

## Passages involved

- [[A Prophet Like Moses]] — Deuteronomy 18:9–14; the nine practices that *"shall not be found with
  you"*, and the nations who *"listen to those who practice sorcery and to diviners"* (14).
- [[Jacob's Flocks]] — Genesis 30:27; Laban: *"I have divined"* (*nāḥaš*).
- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:5, 15; Joseph's cup, *"by which he indeed divines"*.
- [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] — Exodus 7:11; *"the wise men and the sorcerers"* (*məkaššəpîm*).
- Exodus 22:18 — *"You shall not allow a sorceress to live"* (stored WEB).
- [[Holy Living in Field and Community]] — Leviticus 19:26, 31; enchantments, sorcery, mediums, and
  wizards.
- [[Penalties and a People Set Apart]] — Leviticus 20:6, 27; turning to mediums, and the medium put to
  death.
- [[Balak Sends for Balaam]] — Numbers 22:7; *"the rewards of divination"*.
- [[Balaam's First Two Oracles]] — Numbers 23:23; *"Surely there is no enchantment with Jacob; Neither is
  there any divination with Israel."*
- 1 Samuel 28:3 — Saul had sent away *"those who had familiar spirits and the wizards out of the land"*
  (WEB, checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested).
- 2 Kings 21:6 — Manasseh *"made his son to pass through the fire, practiced sorcery, used enchantments,
  and dealt with those who had familiar spirits, and with wizards"* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com; not
  yet ingested).

## Analysis

**Deuteronomy gathers what the earlier books name one or two at a time.** By the WLC lemma tags
(morphhb), every term in 18:10–11 except two has an earlier Torah occurrence:

| Term (WLC) | WEB in Deuteronomy 18 | Earlier in the Torah |
|---|---|---|
| *qōsēm qəsāmîm* | *uses divination* | noun at Numbers 22:7 (*"rewards of divination"*), 23:23 |
| *məʿônēn* | *practices sorcery* | Leviticus 19:26 (*"practice sorcery"*) |
| *mənaḥēš* | *an enchanter* | Genesis 30:27; 44:5, 15; Leviticus 19:26 (*"use enchantments"*); noun Numbers 23:23; 24:1 |
| *məkaššēp* | *a sorcerer* | Exodus 7:11; 22:18 |
| *ḥōbēr ḥāber* | *a charmer* | — (noun only here in the Torah) |
| *šōʾēl ʾôb* | *a consulter with a familiar spirit* | Leviticus 19:31; 20:6, 27 (*"mediums"*) |
| *yiddəʿōnî* | *a wizard* | Leviticus 19:31; 20:6, 27 (*"wizards"*) |
| *dōrēš ʾel-hammētîm* | *a necromancer* | — (phrase only here) |

The ninth item, the son or daughter passed through the fire, heads the list and is traced separately on
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]].

**The law hardens across the books.** Genesis narrates divination without comment: Laban divines and
gets a true answer (30:27), and Joseph's steward speaks of a divining cup (44:5) — see
[[Divination in Genesis - Laban's Nahash and Joseph's Cup]]. Exodus 22:18 names one death penalty, for the
sorceress. Leviticus 19 forbids enchantments and sorcery and turning to mediums, which *defiles* (19:31);
Leviticus 20 punishes the one who turns to them by Yahweh's own cutting off (20:6), and the medium or wizard
by stoning (20:27). Deuteronomy 18 brings all of them into one list and calls the whole list
*"an abomination to Yahweh"* (12) and the reason the nations are driven out.

**What Deuteronomy adds is the replacement.** Leviticus forbids; Deuteronomy forbids and then says what
Israel will have instead: *"a prophet from among you"* (18:15). The arts in 10–11 all seek knowledge from
below — omens, spirits, the dead. The prophet receives it from Yahweh, who puts his words in his mouth
(18:18). Balaam's line in Numbers 23:23 had said as much from outside: there is no enchantment with Jacob,
because of what God does for Israel.

**The English words drift.** WEB's renderings are not consistent across books. *ʾôb* is *"mediums"* in
Leviticus 19:31 and *"a familiar spirit"* in Deuteronomy 18:11 and 1 Samuel 28:3; *məʿônēn*'s verb is
*"practice sorcery"* in Leviticus 19:26, while *"sorcerer"* in Deuteronomy 18:10 is a different word,
*məkaššēp*. A reader comparing the English alone would miss that Leviticus 19:31 and Deuteronomy 18:11 name
the same pair.

**What the terms meant is mostly not recoverable from the Torah.** None of these passages describes a
practice. Meanings offered in the commentaries rest on etymology (for example *məʿônēn* with *ʿānān*,
*cloud*; *ʾôb* with a pit or a skin bottle) and on comparison with other ancient Near Eastern practice. This
page records the Hebrew terms and their distribution only; it does not adopt a definition for any of them,
and no lexicon was consulted.

## Significance

The thread runs on into the histories: Saul expels the mediums and wizards and then consults one
(1 Samuel 28:3, 7), and Manasseh does almost the whole of Deuteronomy 18:10–11 in one verse (2 Kings
21:6). Both will need this page when they are ingested. See [[Idolatry]], [[The Nations]], and
[[Prophecy and Vision]].
