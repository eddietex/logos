# Sweet Food and Gravel - Proverbs 20.17 and Job 20

## Passages involved

- [[Wine Is a Mocker]] — Proverbs 20:14, 17, 21.
- [[The Two Feasts]] — Proverbs 9:17–18.
- [[Zophar's Second Speech]] — Job 20:12–15.
- [[Thirty Excellent Things]] — Proverbs 23:1–3, 6–8.

## Analysis

Proverbs 20:17 turns an initially sweet fraudulent meal into a mouthful of gravel. Its
immediate setting includes a buyer who disparages goods and then boasts (14), and its later
warning denies final blessing to a quickly gained inheritance (21). The apparent gain is
evaluated by its end. The food is an image of dishonest advantage, not a rule about
contaminated meals.

Proverbs 9:17–18 has already paired the attraction of illicit food and drink with a concealed
end in death. Proverbs 20 compresses that reversal into a single saying without retelling the
foolish woman's invitation. Shared imagery does not establish direct quotation.

Zophar develops a closely related reversal in Job 20:12–15: wickedness savored in the mouth
becomes venom inside, and swallowed wealth is vomited up. Proverbs has gravel where Zophar has
poison. **The moral warning and its application must be distinguished:** Zophar speaks in a
dispute about a sufferer whom God has affirmed as holding to integrity (Job 2:3). A warning
against enjoying fraud does not prove that someone who suffers has committed fraud.

### Proverbs 23 in context

Two tables in [[Thirty Excellent Things]] carry the same reversal. The ruler's dainties are *"deceitful
food"* (23:3), and at the stingy host's *"The morsel which you have eaten you shall vomit up, and lose
your good words"* (23:8) — Zophar's verb for riches swallowed and cast out (Job 20:15), here applied to a
meal whose welcome was false. See also
[[Deceitful Food - The Savory Food of Genesis 27 and the Ruler's Delicacies]]. Source: stored WEB
Proverbs 23:3, 8; Job 20:15. Shared imagery does not establish quotation.

## Significance

The mouth links [[Deception]], [[Wealth and Possessions]], and [[Retribution]]: gain can feel pleasant before its
consequences appear. Job prevents reversing that warning into an accusation against every
person whose circumstances become bitter.
