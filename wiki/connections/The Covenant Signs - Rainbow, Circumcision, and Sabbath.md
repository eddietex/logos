# The Covenant Signs - Rainbow, Circumcision, and Sabbath

## Passages involved

- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — Genesis 9:8–17; the rainbow.
- [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]] — Genesis 17:7–14; the embodied covenant token.
- [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]] — Exodus 31:12–17; Sabbath as the sign between Yahweh
  and Israel.
- Ezekiel 20:12, 20 — Sabbaths as a sign of knowledge of Yahweh; verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

The three passages join a covenant to a sign and language of continuing generations. Their
differences matter as much as the resemblance.

In Genesis 9 the covenant includes Noah, his offspring, every living creature, and the earth. God
sets the rainbow and says he will look at it and remember his promise not to destroy all flesh by a
flood (9:12–17). The passage does not instruct Noah to manufacture or perform the sign.

In Genesis 17 the covenant token is circumcision. It must be performed on males in Abraham's
household and descendants, including those bought with money (17:10–13). Failure carries the
sanction of being cut off for breaking the covenant (17:14). The sign now requires an act of
obedience; it is not simply something God places before the people.

Exodus 31 gives Israel a recurring practice rather than a mark on the body. Sabbath is a sign
between Yahweh and the children of Israel (31:13, 17), observed through their generations as a
perpetual covenant (31:16). It signifies knowledge that Yahweh sanctifies them. Profanation or work
brings death, and being cut off is also stated (31:14–15). The creation rationale reaches back
before Israel without changing the named parties to this particular covenant sign.

The similarities are textual: signs, a relationship between God and specified parties, continuing
generations, and—in Genesis 17 and Exodus 31—a demanded practice and sanction. **These passages do
not announce a sequence in which each sign replaces the previous one.** Nor does their shared
language establish that the scope of each covenant is identical.

### The Sabbath sign in Ezekiel

Ezekiel 20:12 recounts God giving Sabbaths as a sign so the people would know him as the one who
sanctifies them. Verse 20 again joins holy Sabbaths, the sign, and knowledge of Yahweh as their God.
This closely matches Exodus 31:13, 17 in the WEB. The connection is stronger than a generic
resemblance between rest days: both explicitly state the sign's parties and purpose. Ezekiel's
surrounding historical indictment is not ingested here, and no claim of exact Hebrew identity is
made.

## Significance

[[Covenant]] signs differ in audience, practice, and sanction. Reading the rainbow,
[[Circumcision]], and [[Sabbath]] together makes those differences visible while tracing a common
function: an enduring relationship given a recognizable sign. See [[Signs and Wonders]] and
[[Holiness]].
