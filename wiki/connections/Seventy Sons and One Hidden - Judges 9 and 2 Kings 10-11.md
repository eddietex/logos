## Analysis

Three times in Israel's history a whole house of a ruler's sons is killed. In the first and the last
of these scenes one son is hidden and lives.

| | Judges 9 | 2 Kings 10 | 2 Kings 11 |
|---|---|---|---|
| the house | Jerubbaal's seventy sons | Ahab's seventy sons | the royal offspring of Judah |
| who orders it | Abimelech, their half-brother | Jehu, by letter | Athaliah, their grandmother |
| who does it | Abimelech, with hired men | the great men of Samaria | Athaliah, *she arose and destroyed* |
| the survivor | Jotham, *for he hid himself* | none | Joash, hidden by his aunt |

The number is the first link. Judges 9:5 and 2 Kings 10:6–7 both have *šibʿîm ʾîš*, *seventy men*
(WLC); in Genesis–Judges that pair otherwise counts only the seventy elders of Numbers 11. The second
link is the hiding. Jotham hides himself (*ḥābāʾ*); Joash is hidden by others (*sātar*, WLC). The two
verbs are different, so the parallel is in the event, not the wording.

The differences matter. Abimelech kills his own brothers to become king. Jehu kills another king's sons
under a prophetic commission, *You must strike your master Ahab's house* (2 Kings 9:7, WEB checked at
bible-api.com; not yet ingested). Athaliah kills her own grandchildren to rule.
Judges 9 is closest to 2 Kings 11 in its shape: a usurper, a massacre of the heirs, one boy saved. It
is closest to 2 Kings 10 in its number.

In Judges, the survivor does not become king. Jotham speaks, curses, and flees (9:7–21). Joash is
hidden *in Yahweh's house six years* and then crowned, *they made him king, and anointed him* (2 Kings
11:3, 12, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested). The book of Judges gives the last word to
God, who *repaid the wickedness of Abimelech* (9:56).

## Significance

- **A house of seventy is a dynasty in miniature.** Gideon's seventy sons echo the seventy of Jacob's
  house (Exodus 1:5; see [[Seventy Souls - The Count of Jacob's House in Genesis, Exodus and Acts]]).
  Killing them ends a house.
- **One survivor keeps the account open.** Jotham's curse (9:57) and Joash's crowning both come from
  the one who was not killed.
- **The wiki records the parallel without claiming dependence.** Whether Kings is shaped by Judges 9 is
  not decidable from the texts. The Hebrew of 2 Kings was read only for 10:6–7 and 11:2.
