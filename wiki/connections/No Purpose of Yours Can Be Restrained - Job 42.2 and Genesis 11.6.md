# No Purpose of Yours Can Be Restrained - Job 42.2 and Genesis 11.6

## Passages involved

- **Job 42:2** — Job's last speech: *"I know that you can do all things, and that no purpose of yours can be
  restrained."* — *wəlōʾ-yibbāṣēr mimməkā məzimmâ* — [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]]
- **Genesis 11:6** — Yahweh at Babel: *"Behold, they are one people, and they have all one language, and
  this is what they begin to do. Now nothing will be withheld from them, which they intend to do."* —
  *lōʾ-yibbāṣēr mēhem kōl ʾăšer yāzəmû laʿăśôt* — [[The Tower of Babel]]
- **Job 40:9** — *"Or do you have an arm like God? Can you thunder with a voice like him?"* — the question
  42:2 answers — [[Behemoth and Leviathan]]
- **Genesis 18:14** — *"Is anything too hard for Yahweh?"* — [[Is Anything Too Hard for Yahweh]]

## Analysis

**Two sentences, one about human beings and one about God, built from the same two words.** The verb
is *bṣr* in the passive (*niphal*), *to be cut off, withheld, made inaccessible* — the root of the
*fortified* city (*bāṣûr*), the wall that stops a besieger. The thing that is not withheld is in both
places a word from *zmm*, *to plan, to purpose*: *yāzəmû*, *they intend*, at Babel; *məzimmâ*, *purpose*,
in Job. The phrase in each case is *lōʾ-yibbāṣēr min-*, *it will not be withheld from …*.

At **Babel**, God says it of humanity. The people have one language and a single project, and *nothing
they plan will be beyond them*. The sentence is a judgment of danger, and God answers it by confusing the
language and scattering them (11:7–8). **Unrestrainable purpose is the thing the story will not allow a
human community to have.**

In **Job**, a man says it of God. *No purpose of yours can be restrained* — nothing God plans is beyond
him. It comes immediately after the second whirlwind speech offered Job the divine role (*"an arm like
God"*, 40:9; *"Array yourself with honor and majesty"*, 40:10) and showed him the creatures he could not
touch. Job does not take the offer. He hands the sentence of Babel back to the one it belongs to.

**The wiki reads the echo as real but does not claim the author of Job intended Genesis 11.** The
pairing of *bṣr* and *zmm* in this construction is rare, and the two sentences make the same claim about
different subjects. Whether that is an allusion, a shared idiom, or a coincidence of two writers saying
*nothing is impossible for X* in the natural Hebrew for it, the text does not settle. What the pairing
does is useful either way: it shows what Job's confession is *not*. Job is not saying God is strong. He is
saying that the limitless purpose Babel reached for, and was stopped from having, is God's.

## Significance

- **It gives Job 42:2 a contrast in Genesis.** Babel is human self-exaltation — *"let's make a name for
  ourselves"* (Genesis 11:4) — and the second whirlwind speech is about pride (40:11–12; 41:34). Job's first
  sentence after it gives up the claim the builders made. See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].
- **It sits beside [[Is Anything Too Hard for Yahweh]].** Genesis 18:14 asks the question; Job 42:2–3
  answers it in the first person, with *niplāʾôt*, *too wonderful*, one verse later.
- **It is the end of a lawsuit, not of an argument.** Job does not say that God's purposes are just, or
  explained; he says they cannot be stopped. What he says next (42:3) is that he spoke without knowledge.
  See [[Providence]].
