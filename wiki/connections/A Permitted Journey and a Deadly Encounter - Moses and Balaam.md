# A Permitted Journey and a Deadly Encounter - Moses and Balaam

## Passages involved

- [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] — Exodus 4:18–31, especially 19–26.
- [[Balak Sends for Balaam]] — Numbers 22:20–21.
- [[Balaam's Donkey]] — Numbers 22:22–35.

## Analysis

In the stored WEB Exodus 4:19, Yahweh tells Moses to return to Egypt. On that journey Yahweh
meets him and wants to kill him (24); Zipporah’s circumcision of her son precedes his release
(25–26). In Numbers 22:20 God permits Balaam to travel, yet anger and an armed angel interrupt
his journey (22–23). The donkey’s evasions save him before he understands the threat (33).

The shared pattern is a permitted journey interrupted by mortal divine opposition, with another
actor’s intervention preserving the traveler. This is a literary comparison, not an explicit
quotation or a claim that either episode explains the other. Moses is returning for Israel’s
deliverance; Balaam is responding to a king who seeks Israel’s curse. Circumcision has no stated
role in Balaam’s scene, and the donkey is not a ritual substitute for him.

Numbers supplies a rebuke of the traveler’s way (32) and renews the restriction on speech (35).
That still does not fully explain why permission in 20 is followed by anger in 22. Exodus does
not supply a narrated reason for the attack in 24. Each text’s unresolved questions remain its
own; this parallel must not make divine permission equivalent to unconditional approval.

Sources: stored WEB Exodus 4:18–31 and Numbers 22:20–35, paraphrased. No source-language claim
or quotation from an unstored book is used.

## Significance

Both journeys expose the vulnerability of a commissioned or permitted traveler. Rescue comes
through an actor whose action the traveler does not initiate; onward travel remains dependent
on God. The resemblance sharpens the questions without supplying an invented common offense.
