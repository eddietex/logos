# Counsel and Might - Job 12.13, Proverbs 8.14, and Isaiah 11.2

## Passages involved

- **Job 12:13** — *"With God is wisdom and might. He has counsel and understanding."* Job's hymn of
  God's overthrowing power — [[Though He Slay Me]].
- **Proverbs 8:14** — *"Counsel and sound knowledge are mine. I have understanding and power."*
  Wisdom speaking — [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]].
- **Proverbs 8:15–16** — *"By me kings reign, and princes decree justice. By me princes rule"* — the
  next verses, which make the four qualities the means of rule.
- **Isaiah 11:2** — *"Yahweh's Spirit will rest on him: the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the
  spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of Yahweh."* The shoot from
  the stock of Jesse. Not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-10-25.
- **Proverbs 8:13** — *"The fear of Yahweh is to hate evil"*, the verse before 8:14, as Isaiah 11:2
  ends on *"the fear of Yahweh"*.

## Analysis

**Four words, three owners.** The Hebrew, read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*:

| | wisdom | might | counsel | understanding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Job 12:13 (God) | *ḥoḵmâ* | *ḡəḇûrâ* | *ʿēṣâ* | *təḇûnâ* |
| Proverbs 8:14 (Wisdom) | (the speaker, *ʾănî-ḥoḵmâ*, 8:12) | *ḡəḇûrâ* | *ʿēṣâ* | *bînâ* |
| Isaiah 11:2 (the king) | *ḥoḵmâ* | *ḡəḇûrâ* | *ʿēṣâ* | *bînâ* |

Job says the set belongs to God: *ʿimmô*, *with him*, and *lô*, *his*. Proverbs 8:14 says it in the
first person, with the same possessive *lî*, *mine* — *lî-ʿēṣâ … lî ḡəḇûrâ* — so Wisdom claims in
God's grammar what Job gave to God. Isaiah 11:2 says the set *rests on* a coming son of Jesse, as
*rûaḥ*, spirit, given by Yahweh. *ʿĒṣâ ûḡəḇûrâ*, *counsel and might*, stands as a pair in both Job
12:13 and Isaiah 11:2; in Proverbs 8:14 the two words are in the verse, in separate halves.

**Job uses the set to describe overthrow; Proverbs and Isaiah use it to describe rule.** Job 12:13
heads a hymn about what God does to counsellors, judges, kings, and priests: he *"leads counselors
away stripped"*, *"makes judges fools"*, *"loosens the bond of kings"* (Job 12:17–18, stored WEB).
Proverbs 8:15–16 says the opposite thing about the same people: *"By me kings reign … nobles, and all
the righteous rulers"*. Job sees the wisdom and might of God unmaking the wise and the mighty;
Proverbs sees wisdom making them. **The wiki records this as a difference of angle, not a
contradiction stated by either book**: both put the qualities first in God or in God's wisdom and only
derivatively in rulers, and Job's speech is about rulers deprived of what Proverbs says they rule by.

**Isaiah 11 adds the fear of Yahweh, which Proverbs 8 has in the previous verse.** Isaiah's list ends
*"the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of Yahweh"*; Proverbs 8:13 has just said *"The fear of
Yahweh is to hate evil"*, and 8:12 has *knowledge* (*daʿaṯ*). Proverbs 8:12–15 and Isaiah 11:2–4
thus share knowledge, the fear of Yahweh, counsel, might, understanding, and righteous judgment by a
ruler (Isaiah 11:4, *"with righteousness he will judge the poor"*, WEB at bible-api.com, not stored).
The wiki does not claim that either text depends on the other; the overlap is a shared vocabulary of
royal wisdom.

**Later reading.** Christian interpretation reads Isaiah 11:1–2 of Christ, and the New Testament calls
Christ *"the power of God and the wisdom of God"* (1 Corinthians 1:24, WEB at bible-api.com, not stored):
*might* and *wisdom*, the first pair of Job 12:13, in one person. The wiki records the line of reading
without claiming Paul had these verses in view.

## Significance

The thread shows the wisdom tradition placing one set of qualities in three places — in God, in
Wisdom personified, and in the anointed king — and so explains why Proverbs can make Wisdom speak in
God's register without calling her God, and why its book is attached to a king. See [[Wisdom]],
[[Kingship and Empire]], [[The Fear of God]], and
[[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]].
