# Through the Window - Proverbs 7.6, Genesis 26.8, and Judges 5.28

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 7:6–7** — [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]: *"For at the window of my house, I
  looked out through my lattice. I saw among the simple ones. I discerned among the youths a young
  man void of understanding"* (*bəḥallôn bêṯî bəʿaḏ ʾešnabbî nišqāp̄tî*)
- **Genesis 26:8** — [[Isaac in Gerar]]: *"Abimelech king of the Philistines looked out at a window,
  and saw, and, behold, Isaac was caressing Rebekah, his wife"* (*wayyašqēp̄ … bəʿaḏ haḥallôn*)
- **Judges 5:28** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"Through the window she looked out, and
  cried: Sisera's mother looked through the lattice"* (*bəʿaḏ haḥallôn nišqəp̄â … bəʿaḏ hāʾešnāḇ*)

- Compare **Song of Solomon 2:9** — [[Rise Up, My Love]]: *"Behold, he stands behind our wall! He
  looks in at the windows. He glances through the lattice."* (*mašgîaḥ min-haḥallōnôt mēṣîṣ
  min-haḥărakkîm*)

## Analysis

**The same verb, the same preposition, the same nouns.** All three use *šāqap̄*, *to look down* or
*out*, with *bəʿaḏ*, *through*, and a window. Genesis has *ḥallôn*; Proverbs has *ḥallôn* and
*ʾešnāḇ*, *lattice*; Judges has both nouns and the same stem as Proverbs (*nišqāp̄tî*,
*nišqəp̄â*). The Hebrew of all three is read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*. The
wiki records this as a literary parallel: a stock scene with stock vocabulary, not one text
borrowing from another.

**Each watcher sees something the people below do not know is seen.**

1. **Abimelech** sees a marriage that had been hidden. Isaac had called Rebekah his sister; one look
   through the window, and the king knows she is his wife, and that his people were in danger of
   taking her (Genesis 26:9–11). The window exposes a lie and protects a marriage.
2. **Sisera's mother** watches for a son who will not come. The reader already knows he is dead in
   Jael's tent (Judges 5:24–27, by reference); she does not, and her ladies' answer — that he is
   dividing the spoil — is the last irony of Deborah's song.
3. **The father in Proverbs** sees a young man who does not know he is watched, walking toward a
   house he does not know is a grave (7:27). Unlike the other two, he looks *in order that someone
   else may see*: the scene is narrated for the son.

**Two marriages and a son.** In Genesis the window protects a marriage from adultery; in Proverbs
it watches a marriage betrayed. In Judges a mother waits for a son; in Proverbs a father watches
one that is not his, so that his own will not end the same way. The wiki does not claim the
writers had these pairings in mind. It records that the window, in Hebrew narrative, is where a
person stands to see what the street does not show.

**A watcher outside, looking in.** Song of Solomon 2:9 has a window and a lattice, but not this
scene's vocabulary. The window noun is the same, *ḥallôn*, but the lattice is *ḥărakkîm*, not
*ʾešnāḇ*, and the verbs are *mašgîaḥ* and *mēṣîṣ*, not *šāqap̄* (Sefaria, *Miqra according to the
Masorah*, 2026-10-28). The direction is reversed: the beloved stands outside the wall and looks in,
and what he wants is the woman to come out (2:10). The window there is a place of approach, not of
surveillance, and is listed as a contrast, not a fourth member of the stock scene.

## Significance

The scene gives [[Seeing and Being Seen]] a human watcher in place of God. Proverbs 5:21 had said
*"the ways of man are before Yahweh's eyes"*; in chapter 7 a father's eyes do the same work, and
the son is invited to stand at the window with him.
