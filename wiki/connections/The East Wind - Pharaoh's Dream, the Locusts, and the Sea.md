# The East Wind - Pharaoh's Dream, the Locusts, and the Sea

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 41:6, 23, 27** — *"seven heads of grain, thin and blasted with the east wind"*; Joseph:
  *"the seven empty heads of grain blasted with the east wind; they will be seven years of famine"* —
  [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Exodus 10:13** — *"Yahweh brought an east wind on the land all that day, and all night; and when
  it was morning, the east wind brought the locusts"* — [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- **Exodus 10:19** — *"Yahweh turned an exceeding strong west wind, which took up the locusts, and
  drove them into the Red Sea"* — [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- **Exodus 14:21** — *"Yahweh caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all night, and made the
  sea dry land"* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**In the Torah the *rûaḥ qādîm*, the east wind, blows in three places, and all three are Egypt.**
*Qādîm* occurs at Genesis 41:6, 23, 27, at Exodus 10:13 (twice), and at Exodus 14:21, and nowhere else
from Genesis to Deuteronomy (WLC lemma search). **Each time it is in God's hand, and each time a
Pharaoh is on the receiving end.**

| | Genesis 41 | Exodus 10 | Exodus 14 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **what the wind does** | withers the grain in a dream | brings the locusts | drives back the sea |
| **how long** | — | *"all that day, and all night"* | *"all night"* |
| **whose Pharaoh** | [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] | [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] | the same |
| **result for Egypt** | warned of famine, and saved by Joseph | famine made real: *"nothing green"* | its army drowned |
| **result for Israel** | Jacob's family fed and settled | spared, unstated | a dry road out |

**In Genesis the east wind is a warning, given to a Pharaoh who listens.** The thin ears are *"blasted
with the east wind"*, *šədûpōt qādîm*, and Joseph says the dream means seven years of famine (41:27).
Pharaoh acts on it, and Egypt becomes the granary of the world. **In Exodus the same wind does to the
real fields what it did to the dream's ears**: the locusts it carries eat *"every herb of the land, and
all the fruit of the trees which the hail had left"*, until *"There remained nothing green"* (10:15).
**This Pharaoh is warned too, and does not listen.**

**Then the east wind opens the sea.** The duration matches: *"all that day, and all night"* for the
locusts (10:13), *"all night"* for the sea (14:21). **The wind that brought the plague is the wind that
makes the road.**

**Between them is the west wind.** At 10:19 God *"turned"* (*wayyahăpōk*) the wind, a *rûaḥ-yām*,
literally *a sea wind*, and it carried the locusts into the *yam sûp*. **Wind and sea swap roles across
the two scenes**: in chapter 10 a sea-wind throws the plague into the sea; in chapter 14 an east wind
pushes the sea back, and the sea then covers the army. The *"not one"* is the same both times: *"There
remained not one locust"* (10:19), *"There remained not so much as one of them"* (14:28). See
[[The Red Sea]].

**Readers have long asked whether these are natural events used by God or wholly outside nature.** A
hot desert wind withering grain, a wind bearing locust swarms, and a wind setting down shallow water are
all known phenomena. **The text does not raise the question.** It says Yahweh *"brought"* the wind
(*nihag*, 10:13) and *"caused the sea to go back"* (14:21), and is interested in the timing — *"when it
was morning"* — not the mechanism. The wiki records the question and does not answer it.

## Significance

- **It ties the Joseph story to the Exodus through one image.** Genesis 41 is where Egypt is saved
  through a Hebrew's reading of God's warning; Exodus 10 and 14 are where Egypt is ruined for refusing
  one. See [[Do Not Interpretations Belong to God - Joseph and Daniel]] and [[Providence]].
- **It makes the wind an instrument, not a scenery detail.** Every east wind in the Torah is said to be
  God's, and each comes at a turn in Egypt's relation to Israel. See [[Signs and Wonders]] and
  [[Judgment]].
- **It links the eighth plague to the sea**, and so the plagues to the crossing: the wind and the *yam
  sûp* both appear first at 10:13–19.
- **Later Scripture keeps the east wind as God's tool of ruin** — the [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
  page notes Hosea 13:15 and Jonah 4:8 — but those passages are not yet ingested and are not quoted
  here.
