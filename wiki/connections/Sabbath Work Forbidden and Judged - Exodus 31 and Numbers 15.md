# Sabbath Work Forbidden and Judged - Exodus 31 and Numbers 15

## Passages involved
- [[Manna and Quail]] — Exodus 16:22–30.
- [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]] — Exodus 31:12–17.
- [[The Freewill Offering]] — Exodus 35:1–3.
- [[The Sabbath Breaker]] — Numbers 15:32–36.

## Analysis

Exodus 31:14–15 prescribes death for Sabbath profanation and work; Exodus 35:2 repeats the
sanction and 35:3 separately forbids kindling fire. Numbers 15 narrates a man gathering sticks,
his detention, a ruling addressed to Moses, and communal stoning outside the camp. The law
has become a concrete case, with procedure specified by Yahweh.

**The unresolved detail matters.** Numbers 15:34 says it had not been declared what should be
done to him, despite the earlier death sanction. An uncertainty about method or application
would fit the sequence, but the text does not identify which. Nor does gathering sticks prove
that a fire was lit. Both limitations belong in any harmonization.

**The manna case has a different outcome.** Exodus 16:27–30 reports attempted Sabbath
gathering, rebuke, and eventual rest, with no execution. It precedes the explicit death laws
in the narrative. That sequence helps distinguish the cases, but an explanation in terms of
more culpable intent in Numbers goes beyond what the two stories say.

## Significance

The Sabbath is a protected covenant obligation, not merely a recommendation to stop working.
At the same time, this comparison resists treating every violation as though the text narrated
the same penalty. See [[Sabbath]], [[Law and Obedience]], and [[Judgment]].
