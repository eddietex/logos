# On This Mountain - The Sign at the Bush and the Assembly at Sinai

## Passages involved

- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:1–12, especially the promised return in 3:12.
- [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] — Exodus 19:1–25, especially the assembly, God's speech, and Moses' role.

## Analysis

At the bush Moses asks who he is to bring Israel out of Egypt (Exodus 3:11). God promises his
presence and gives a token whose fulfillment lies beyond the mission's departure:

> **Exodus 3:12** “Certainly I will be with you. This will be the token to you, that I have sent you:
> when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain.”

Exodus 19 supplies the return with the people: they camp before the mountain, Moses leads them
out to meet God, and God speaks in their hearing (19:1–2, 9, 17–19). This realizes the promised
setting of service; the law and covenant worship will give that service further shape. The
recognition runs backward: the assembly at the mountain confirms the commission given before
Moses had confronted Pharaoh.

The pattern of the encounter also expands. At the bush Moses is told not to come close and the
ground is holy (3:5); at Sinai a whole mountain is bounded, the people are sanctified, and only
those summoned may ascend (19:10–24). At the bush Moses hears alone; at Sinai the people's hearing
is expressly intended to establish trust in him (19:9).

The wiki files both scenes on [[Horeb]], whose alternative name is Sinai. This is a narrative
identification, not a claim to have located the mountain on a modern map. WEB quotations and
comparisons were checked against `raw/bible/Exodus.md`.

## Significance

The exodus reaches a destination defined as meeting and serving God. Moses' authority rests on
the one who sent him, and the sign first given privately becomes a public assembly.
