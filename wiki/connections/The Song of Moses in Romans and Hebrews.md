# The Song of Moses in Romans and Hebrews

## Passages involved
- [[The Song of Moses]] — Deuteronomy 32:17, 21, 35–36, 43.
- Romans 10:19; 12:19; 15:10.
- Hebrews 10:30.
- 1 Corinthians 10:20 — related sacrificial warning.

## Analysis

The same song supplies several distinct arguments. Romans 10:19 cites Moses on provoking
Israel through a nation without understanding, matching Deuteronomy 32:21. Romans 15:10
uses the invitation to the nations from 32:43 within praise shared with God’s people.
Provocation and shared rejoicing are both present in the source song; they are not competing
summaries that require one to be removed.

Romans 12:19 applies Deuteronomy 32:35’s divine recompense to the prohibition of personal
revenge. Hebrews 10:30 brings together vengeance and judgment of God’s people from 32:35–36.
The latter is a warning; it should not erase the compassion toward powerless servants that
accompanies judgment in Deuteronomy 32:36. Each later writer selects words for a particular
exhortation rather than retelling the entire song.

1 Corinthians 10:20 also echoes the opposition between sacrifice to demons and sacrifice to
God in Deuteronomy 32:17. It warns its readers about participation in such worship. The
comparison is visible in the verified English texts; no claim of exact Greek identity is
needed here.

## Significance

[[The Nations]], [[Vengeance]], and [[Idolatry]] show the range of the song’s later reception.
God’s judgment of his own people continues to warn; his ownership of recompense restrains
private retaliation, and the nations’ rejoicing broadens the horizon beyond Israel alone.
