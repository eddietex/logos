# God Make You as Ephraim and as Manasseh - The Blessing Formula

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 48:20** — *"In you will Israel bless, saying, 'God make you as Ephraim and as Manasseh'"* —
  [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]]
- **Ruth 4:11–12** — *"Yahweh make you like [[Rachel]] and like [[Leah]], which two built the house of
  Israel … let your house be like the house of [[Perez]], whom Tamar bore to Judah"*
- **Jeremiah 29:22** — *"Yahweh make you like Zedekiah and like Ahab, whom the king of Babylon roasted
  in the fire"* — **the same construction, as a curse**
- Compare **Genesis 12:2** — *"be a blessing"*, and **Genesis 22:18; 26:4** — *"in your offspring will
  all the nations of the earth be blessed"* — [[The Call of Abram]]
- Compare **Numbers 6:24–26** — the priestly benediction, a blessing formula with no exemplar in it
- Compare **Isaiah 65:16** — swearing *"by the God of truth"*, and **Zechariah 8:13** — *"you shall be a
  blessing"*

## Analysis

**Three times in the Hebrew Bible somebody wishes a fate on a person by naming two people who already
had it. Twice it is a blessing and once it is a curse, and the first of the three is Genesis 48:20.**

| text | *God / Yahweh make you like…* | what the pair stands for |
|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 48:20** | *Ephraim and Manasseh* | **multiplied offspring, and greatness out of birth order** |
| **Ruth 4:11–12** | *Rachel and Leah*; then *the house of Perez* | **fruitful wives, and a line that survives an irregular start** |
| **Jeremiah 29:22** | *Zedekiah and Ahab* | **two men burned alive by Babylon** |

**All three name a pair rather than an individual**, and in all three the pair is drawn from Genesis
or its immediate descendants: **[[Ephraim]] and [[Manasseh]]; [[Rachel]] and [[Leah]]; and, in Ruth's
second clause, [[Perez]] son of [[Tamar]].**

### What the formula does in Genesis 48

**It is the only thing in the chapter about the future of the blessing rather than the future of the
boys.** *"**In you** will Israel bless"* — **the pronoun is singular** and is usually read as addressed
to [[Joseph]], or to the two boys taken as one unit; **the wiki notes the ambiguity and does not resolve
it.** ***Israel*** **here is the nation, not the man**, which makes 48:20 the one verse in the chapter
where the name has stopped referring to the person speaking. See
[[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

**And the clause is a prediction that came true.** **The Sabbath-eve blessing of sons in Jewish practice
is Genesis 48:20 recited verbatim** — *yəsîməkā ʾĕlōhîm kəʾEprayim wəkiMnaššeh* — **with a counterpart
over daughters naming [[Sarah]], [[Rebekah]], [[Rachel]] and [[Leah]]**, itself assembled from Ruth
4:11. **The wiki records the liturgical afterlife as a matter of fact about how the verse has been used**
and not as an interpretation of it.

### Why these two and not [[Joseph]]

**The obvious exemplar for a blessing over a boy is the man who ran Egypt.** **Genesis 48:20 names his
sons instead**, and two things follow.

1. **The pair is chosen for what happened to them, not for what they did.** Neither boy speaks or acts
   anywhere in Scripture. **What they are famous for is having been adopted, blessed, and reordered** —
   **so the formula wishes on a child a thing that is entirely done to him.** Compare
   [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].
2. **The order is preserved in the formula.** ***"As Ephraim and as Manasseh"*** — **younger first**,
   and the narrator's next clause is *"He set Ephraim before Manasseh."* **Israel's standing blessing
   over its sons embeds the reversal**, which is the chapter making sure the reversal is not lost. See
   [[Two Blind Fathers - Genesis 27 and Genesis 48]].

### Ruth 4 and Jeremiah 29

**Ruth 4:11–12 is the closest formal parallel in Scripture**, and it is spoken by *"all the people who
were in the gate, and the elders"* — **a crowd, not a patriarch** — over Boaz and a Moabite widow. **Its
first pair, [[Rachel]] and [[Leah]], is the pair Genesis 48:7 is about**, and its second exemplar,
[[Perez]], **is the other Genesis younger-son reversal** (38:27–30). See
[[Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel]] and
[[Two Sets of Twins - Jacob and Esau, Perez and Zerah]].

**Jeremiah 29:22 turns the construction inside out.** *"Of them shall be taken up a curse by all the
captives of Judah who are in Babylon, saying, 'Yahweh make you like Zedekiah and like Ahab.'"*
**Jeremiah says explicitly what Genesis 48:20 leaves implied — that a pair of names can become a
proverb** — **and he says it of two men whose names became one because of how they died.** **The
grammar of blessing and the grammar of cursing are identical in Hebrew**, and these two verses are the
proof. See [[Curse]] and [[Language and Speech]].

## Significance

**A blessing that names exemplars is doing something different from a blessing that describes a
future.** **Numbers 6:24–26 asks God to act; Genesis 12:2–3 makes a man into a standard of blessing for
others; Genesis 48:20 makes two children into the standard.** **It is the earliest verse in Scripture
that turns named persons into a measure**, and it does so before either of them has done anything.

**And it is the mechanism by which the two most Egyptian members of the family became the words Israel
says over its sons.** [[Ephraim]] and [[Manasseh]] were born in [[Egypt]] to the daughter of a priest of
[[On]] (41:45, 50); **they are the only tribes of Israel with a foreign mother and a foreign
birthplace**; **and Genesis 48:20 puts their names in the mouth of every Israelite parent.** See
[[Adoption]] and [[The Nations]].
