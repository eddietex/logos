# Remove the Evil from Among You - Deuteronomy 13.5 and 1 Corinthians 5.13

## Passages involved

- [[Do Not Follow Other Gods]] — Deuteronomy 13:5; the formula's first use, for the prophet who
  leads to other gods.
- [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]] — Deuteronomy 17:7, 12; the worshipper of other gods put to death on two or three
  witnesses, and the man who refuses the verdict of the court at the place.
- [[Refuge for the Unwitting Killer]] — Deuteronomy 19:13; the same verb with *"the innocent blood"* as its object.
- [[Landmarks, Witnesses, and False Testimony]] — Deuteronomy 19:19; the false witness.
- [[An Unsolved Killing and the Broken-Necked Heifer]] — Deuteronomy 21:9; *"So you shall put away the innocent blood from among you"*, the
  verb *bāʿar* (WLC *tǝbaʿēr*) with the object of 19:13, after a rite in which no one is put to death.
- Deuteronomy 21:21, 22:21, 22:22, 22:24, 24:7 — the formula's later uses in Deuteronomy's
  laws (stored WEB, not yet ingested).
- 1 Corinthians 5:13 — *"But those who are outside, God judges. 'Put away the wicked man from among
  yourselves.'"* (WEB, verified from bible-api.com).

## Analysis

**The formula closes a sentence, usually of death.** In the stored WEB of Deuteronomy, *"So you
shall remove the evil from among you"* follows each of these cases (searched by English phrase). At
17:12 and 22:22 the WEB reads *"from Israel"* instead of *"from among you"*, and at 17:12 the verb is
*put away*:

| Deuteronomy | Case |
|---|---|
| 13:5 | a prophet or dreamer who says *"Let us go after other gods"* |
| 17:7 | a man or woman who serves other gods, on two or three witnesses |
| 17:12 | the man who presumptuously refuses the priest or judge |
| 19:19 | the false witness, who receives what he planned for his brother — not necessarily death |
| 21:21 | the stubborn and rebellious son |
| 22:21, 22, 24 | sexual offences: a bride found not to be a virgin, adultery, and a betrothed woman in a town |
| 24:7 | the man who kidnaps and sells an Israelite |

In 13:5 the verb is *bāʿar* (WLC 13:6, morphhb), whose range includes *to burn* and *to purge* or
*sweep away*. Whether the same Hebrew wording is used in every later occurrence is not checked
here; the list above is by the WEB's English.

**The evil is spoken of as something in Israel's midst, not only in the offender.** *"From among
you"* makes the community the place that has to be cleared. 13:11 states the result for the
community: *"All Israel shall hear, and fear, and shall not do any more wickedness like this among
you."*

**Paul quotes the formula and changes the penalty.** In 1 Corinthians 5 *"one has his father's
wife"* (5:1). Paul faults the church for not mourning, *"that he who had done this deed might be
removed from among you"* (5:2), and tells them not to associate with or *"even eat with"* a
so-called brother who lives this way (5:11). He ends with the Deuteronomy formula: *"Put away the
wicked man from among yourselves"* (5:13). The quoted words keep Deuteronomy's *"from among"*; the
action is exclusion from the community, not execution. Paul also limits it to those inside: *"those
who are outside, God judges"* (5:13).

Which Deuteronomy verse Paul is quoting is not stated in 1 Corinthians. Because the formula occurs
in several laws, including the sexual offences of 22:21–24, the wiki does not tie the quotation to
13:5 alone. It is filed here because 13:5 is the formula's first occurrence.

### Deuteronomy 17:7, 12 now ingested

In [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]] both uses close a death sentence. At 17:7 the witnesses'
hands are first, then all the people's, *"So you shall remove the evil from among you"*. At 17:12 the
evil removed is the presumption of a man who will not listen *"to the priest … or to the judge"*.
Both verses use *ûbiʿartā hāraʿ* (WLC, morphhb). The second is the first case in which the offence is
refusing a court, not worship or violence.

### Deuteronomy 19:13 now ingested

*"You shall purge the innocent blood from Israel"* is not in the English-phrase list above, but its verb
is: *ûbiʿartā dam-hannāqî miyyiśrāʾēl* (WLC, morphhb). What is removed is not *"the evil"* but the
murdered man's blood, by handing his murderer to the avenger (19:12), and the WEB's *"from Israel"*
matches the variant at 17:12 and 22:22. The same verb with the same object returns at 21:9, WEB *"put
away the innocent blood"*. A morphhb lemma search for *bāʿar* (1197 a) in Deuteronomy finds it at 13:6
(13:5 English); 17:7, 12; 19:13, 19; 21:9, 21; 22:21, 22, 24; 24:7, besides the sense *burn* at 4:11;
5:23; 9:15 and *remove* at 26:13–14. See [[Refuge for the Unwitting Killer]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 19:13; 21:9; Hebrew
from WLC (morphhb), retrieved 2026-11-07.

### Deuteronomy 19:19 now ingested

*"So you shall remove the evil from among you"* (19:19) is *ûbiʿartā hāraʿ miqqirbekā* (WLC), the 13:5
form. What is removed is the false witness, and he receives what he *"had thought to do to his brother"*,
so the penalty depends on the charge he made, as the table above says. 19:20 then gives the formula's
usual companion, *"Those who remain shall hear, and fear"*, as at 13:11 and 17:13. See [[Landmarks, Witnesses, and False Testimony]].

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 13:11; 17:13; 19:19–20. Hebrew from WLC (morphhb), retrieved 2026-11-07.

## Significance

The formula links Deuteronomy's laws about idolatry, perjury, rebellion, sexual sin, and kidnapping
as offences that threaten the community, not only the victim. Its New Testament use shows the
idea carried into church discipline with a different penalty. The later Deuteronomy pericopes that
use it will need to add themselves to this page as they are ingested. See [[Judgment]] and
[[Holiness]].
