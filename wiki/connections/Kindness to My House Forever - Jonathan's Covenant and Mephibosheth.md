# Kindness to My House Forever - Jonathan's Covenant and Mephibosheth

## Passages involved

- **1 Samuel 18:3–4** — [[Jonathan Loves David]] — *Then Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as
  his own soul*; Jonathan gives David his robe, clothing, sword, bow, and sash (stored WEB).
- **1 Samuel 20:8, 14–17, 23, 42** — [[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]] — *a covenant of Yahweh*; *show me the
  loving kindness of Yahweh, that I not die; but you shall also not cut off your kindness from my house forever; no,
  not when Yahweh has cut off every one of the enemies of David from the surface of the earth*; *Yahweh is between me
  and you, and between my offspring and your offspring, forever* (stored WEB).
- **1 Samuel 23:16–18** — Jonathan at Horesh: *you will be king over Israel, and I will be next to you*; *They both
  made a covenant before Yahweh* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **1 Samuel 24:20–22** — Saul at En Gedi: *Swear now therefore to me by Yahweh, that you will not cut off my offspring
  after me, and that you will not destroy my name out of my father's house.* *David swore to Saul* (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested).
- **2 Samuel 9:1–13** — David asks whether anyone is left of Saul's house so that he may show *the kindness of God*
  for Jonathan's sake, finds Jonathan's lame son Mephibosheth, restores Saul's land to him, and brings him to eat
  continually at the king's table (recalled, not checked; no 2 Samuel WEB stored).
- **2 Samuel 21:7** — When seven of Saul's sons are handed over to the Gibeonites during the famine, *the king spared
  Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan the son of Saul*, because of Yahweh's oath between David and Jonathan (recalled,
  not checked; no 2 Samuel WEB stored).

## Analysis

**A friendship covenant becomes a dynastic treaty.** When [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]] and [[David]] first make a
covenant at 18:3, no terms are recorded and God is not named; the narrator gives only love as the motive, and the act
that follows is Jonathan handing over his robe and weapons (18:4). Two chapters later, under the threat of Saul's
murderous anger, the covenant acquires both its divine guarantor and its multi-generational terms. David appeals to it
as *a covenant of Yahweh* (20:8). Jonathan then turns the covenant toward the future he already sees coming: *May
Yahweh be with you, as he has been with my father* (20:13). Knowing that a new dynasty ordinarily wipes out the old
king's house, the crown prince asks the fugitive to show him *the loving kindness of Yahweh* while he lives and *not
cut off your kindness from my house forever; no, not when Yahweh has cut off every one of the enemies of David from the
surface of the earth* (20:14–15). *So Jonathan made a covenant with David's house* (20:16), sealed by a repeated oath
(17) and placed under Yahweh's watch *between my offspring and your offspring, forever* (42).

| Stage | Passage | Parties and scope | What is sworn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First covenant** | 1 Samuel 18:3–4 | Jonathan and David | No spoken terms; grounded in love; robe and weapons given |
| **Field covenant** | 1 Samuel 20:8, 14–17, 42 | Jonathan and David's house; *my offspring and your offspring, forever* | *Loving kindness of Yahweh* while Jonathan lives; *kindness* never cut off from Jonathan's house |
| **Third covenant** | 1 Samuel 23:16–18 | Jonathan and David *before Yahweh* | Jonathan confesses *you will be king over Israel, and I will be next to you* |
| **Saul's parallel oath** | 1 Samuel 24:20–22 | Saul and David *by Yahweh* | David swears not to *cut off my offspring after me* or destroy Saul's name |
| **Kept at the table** | 2 Samuel 9:1–13 *(recalled)* | David and Mephibosheth (son of Jonathan) | David shows *the kindness of God* for Jonathan's sake; Mephibosheth eats at David's table |
| **Kept in judgment** | 2 Samuel 21:7 *(recalled)* | David spares Mephibosheth | Spared explicitly because of Yahweh's oath between David and Jonathan |

**Both father and son ask the same verb.** Jonathan asks David *not cut off your kindness from my house forever*
(20:15); four chapters later, after David spares him in the cave at En Gedi, [[Saul]] himself confesses *I know that
you will surely be king* and asks David to swear *that you will not cut off my offspring after me* (24:20–21, stored
WEB). The crown prince sees the transfer of the kingdom in chapter 20; the king himself concedes it in chapter 24, and
reaches for the same covenant protection his son had already secured.

**The empty seat and the restored seat.** In 1 Samuel 20:25–27, David's place at Saul's table stands empty on both days
of the new moon, and Jonathan rises from his father's table in fierce anger and eats nothing (20:34). In 2 Samuel 9
(recalled, not checked), David remembers his oath to Jonathan and seats Jonathan's son Mephibosheth permanently at the
king's table. The covenant made when David was driven from Saul's table is kept by bringing Jonathan's son to David's
table.

**Tension recorded for 2 Samuel 21.** When the Gibeonite blood-guilt crisis arrives in 2 Samuel 21 (recalled, not
checked), David hands over seven of Saul's descendants to be killed while sparing Mephibosheth specifically *because of
Yahweh's oath that was between them, between David and Jonathan* (21:7). That keeps the oath of 1 Samuel 20:15, 42 to
Jonathan's house, while standing in sharp tension with David's oath to Saul at 1 Samuel 24:21–22 not to cut off Saul's
offspring. The wiki notes that tension here ahead of 2 Samuel's ingestion and does not smooth it over.

## Significance

In the narratives of Samuel, Jonathan's covenant in chapter 20 is the hinge between two dynasties. It turns what would
ordinarily be a war of succession between rival royal houses into a sworn obligation of *loving kindness* across
generations — modeled on *the loving kindness of Yahweh* himself (20:14) and enforced by Yahweh as witness *between me
and you, and between my offspring and your offspring, forever* (20:42). See [[Covenant]], [[Love]],
[[Oaths and Vows]], [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]], [[David]], and [[Saul]].
