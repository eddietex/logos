# When You Walk and When You Lie Down - Deuteronomy 6.6-9 and Proverbs 6.20-23

## Passages involved

- **Deuteronomy 6:6–9** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"These words, which I command you
  today, shall be on your heart; and you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall
  talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and
  when you rise up. You shall bind them for a sign on your hand, and they shall be for frontlets
  between your eyes. You shall write them on the door posts of your house, and on your gates."*
- **Exodus 13:9** — the feast of unleavened bread *"shall be for a sign to you on your hand, and for
  a memorial between your eyes, that Yahweh's law may be in your mouth"* —
  [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]]
- **Proverbs 6:20–23** — [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]: *"My son, keep your father's commandment,
  and don't forsake your mother's teaching. Bind them continually on your heart. Tie them around
  your neck. When you walk, it will lead you. When you sleep, it will watch over you. When you
  awake, it will talk with you. For the commandment is a lamp, and the law is light."*
- **Proverbs 3:3** — *"Bind them around your neck. Write them on the tablet of your heart."* —
  [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]]
- **Proverbs 7:3** — *"Bind them on your fingers. Write them on the tablet of your heart."* —
  [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]
- **Proverbs 1:8–9** — *"don't forsake your mother's teaching: for they will be a garland to grace
  your head, and chains around your neck."* — [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]]

## Analysis

**Three shared elements, in one place.** Proverbs 6:20–22 shares with Deuteronomy 6:6–8 the
heart as the place of the words (*ʿal-lᵊḇāḇeḵā*, Deuteronomy 6:6; *ʿal-libbəḵā*, Proverbs 6:21), the
verb for binding them on the body (*ûqšartām*, Deuteronomy 6:8; *qošrēm*, Proverbs 6:21), and the
daily round of walking, lying down, and rising (Deuteronomy 6:7; Proverbs 6:22). One word is
identical in form: *bəšoḵbəḵā*, *when you lie down*. Deuteronomy's walking is *bəleḵtəḵā*, Proverbs'
is *bəhiṯhallekḵā* (the reflexive stem, *walking about*); Deuteronomy's rising is *bəqûmeḵā*,
Proverbs' waking is *wahăqîṣôṯā*. The Hebrew is read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the
Masorah*. With all three together, **the wiki reads Proverbs 6:20–22 as an allusion to
Deuteronomy 6**, not a coincidence of common words — while noting that Proverbs never names Moses
or the covenant, and that the direction of influence is the usual reading rather than something
the texts state.

**Who speaks the words changes.** In Deuteronomy, Israel is to *talk of them* (*wəḏibbartā bām*) at
every point of the day: the person carries and speaks the commandments. In Proverbs, the teaching
itself acts — *it will lead you*, *it will watch over you*, *it will talk with you* (*tanḥeh*,
*tišmōr*, *ṯəśîḥeḵā*). **The commandment that Deuteronomy tells Israel to keep, Proverbs promises
will keep the son.** It is the same reversal Proverbs makes with *šāmar* throughout chapters 2–7:
the son keeps the words, and the words keep him (2:11; 4:6; 6:24; 7:5).

**From parents to parents.** Deuteronomy 6:7 commands the fathers of Israel to teach the words to
their children. Proverbs 6:20 is that teaching heard from the child's side: *your father's
commandment* and *your mother's teaching*. The mother's *tôrâ* stands beside the father's *miṣwâ*,
as at 1:8; in this lecture both terms are then generalized without the possessives (6:23), the
pair that Exodus 24:12 used for what was written on the stone tablets (*wəhattôrâ wəhammiṣwâ*).

**The body changes; the claim does not.** Deuteronomy binds the words on the hand and between the
eyes; Exodus 13:9 had already made the feast a sign on the hand and a memorial between the eyes.
Proverbs binds them on the heart and the neck (6:21; 3:3) and on the fingers (7:3). Whether
Deuteronomy's sign was meant literally — the later practice of *tefillin* and *mezuzot* takes it so —
or figuratively is outside what these texts settle, and the wiki does not decide it. Proverbs
plainly uses the image figuratively: the words are worn like a necklace (1:9) and written on the
heart (3:3).

## Significance

Proverbs is sometimes read as wisdom without covenant, instruction drawn from observation and
household tradition. This passage is the clearest place so far where a lecture of Proverbs takes
Deuteronomy's words for the covenant law — heart, binding, the walking and lying down of every
day — and gives them to a father and mother teaching their son. It does not make the father's
teaching the Sinai law, but it makes the household the place where that law is carried. See
[[Law and Obedience]], [[Walking with God]],
[[The Tablet of the Heart - Proverbs 3.3, Jeremiah 31.33, and 2 Corinthians 3.3]], and
[[A Lamp and a Light - Proverbs 6.23 and Psalm 119.105]]. For the household as the place where
instruction is handed on, see also [[Wisdom Handed On - Proverbs 4 and Exodus 13]].
