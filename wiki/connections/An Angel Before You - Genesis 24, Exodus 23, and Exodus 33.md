## Analysis

**The sentence is first spoken by Abraham about a household errand, and then by God about a nation's
march.** In Hebrew, Genesis 24:7 has *yišlaḥ malʾākô ləpānêkā*, *he will send his angel before you*.
Exodus 23:20 has *šōlēaḥ malʾāk ləpānêkā*, *sending an angel before you*, and 33:2 has *wəšālaḥtî
ləpānêkā malʾāk* (all MAM). The verb, the noun, and *before you* are the same each time. In Genesis
24 the narrator never reports the angel at all. In Exodus 14:19 an angel *"went before the camp"*. In
Exodus 23 the promise is made to the whole people.

**What changes between chapter 23 and chapter 33 is the word *qereb*, *midst*.** The thread runs
through four verses (all MAM):

| Verse | Hebrew | WEB |
|---|---|---|
| 17:7 | *hăyēš YHWH bəqirbēnû* | *"Is Yahweh among us, or not?"* |
| 23:21 | *kî šəmî bəqirbô* | *"for my name is in him"* |
| 33:3 | *kî lōʾ ʾeʿĕleh bəqirbəkā* | *"for I will not go up among you"* |
| 34:9 | *yēlek-nāʾ ʾădōnāy bəqirbēnû* | *"please let the Lord go among us"* |

At Massah the people ask whether Yahweh is *in their midst*. At Sinai God answers with an angel who
has God's name *in his midst*. After the calf, God offers the angel *without* himself in their midst,
and Moses refuses that offer and asks for God to go *in our midst*. Exodus 33:3 gives a reason: God's
presence among a stiff-necked people would consume them. Moses' plea at 34:9 uses the same reason,
*"although this is a stiff-necked people"*, the other way round. The WEB's different renderings (*in
him*, *among you*, *among us*) hide that it is the same word.

**Whether the angel of 23:20 and the angel of 33:2 are the same.** Read on their own, the two promises
sound alike. In context they are not equal: at 23:21 the angel carries God's name; at 33:2–3 the
angel is offered *instead of* God going up. Readers take this either as a demotion after the calf, or
as two different messengers. The text says neither, and the wiki does not decide. Exodus 32:34 sits
between them and adds a delayed punishment to the promise. See [[The Angel of Yahweh]].

### Exodus 32 now ingested

Exodus 32:34 now belongs to [[The Golden Calf]]. The promise of guidance survives the calf, but it
is immediately joined to reserved punishment. The verse supplies neither a declaration of full
pardon nor an explicit identification of the angel. The negotiation of presence in chapters 33–34
remains ahead.

## Significance

- **The angel thread becomes the presence thread.** The question at the center of Exodus 32–34 is
  not whether Israel will reach the land but whether God himself will go with them. This pericope
  supplies the vocabulary for that question before the crisis comes. See
  [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].
- **Genesis 24 is a quiet first instance.** A promise that the narrator never confirms happening
  becomes, in Exodus, a promise the whole book has to argue over.
- **The point is not settled here.** The fuller treatment belongs to the ingest of Exodus 32–34.
  The verses from those chapters are quoted from `raw/bible/Exodus.md` and their Hebrew checked in
  MAM; chapter 32 now has a passage page, while chapters 33–34 remain ahead.
