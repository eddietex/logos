# Abraham's Other Sons in Isaiah 60

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 25:1–4** — Keturah's line: Midian, and Midian's son **Ephah**; Jokshan's son **Sheba**
- **Genesis 25:12–16** — Ishmael's twelve princes, of whom the first two listed are **Nebaioth** and
  **Kedar**
- **Genesis 25:6** — the sons of the concubines given gifts and sent *"eastward, to the east country"*
- **Isaiah 60:6–7** — *"The multitude of camels will cover you, the dromedaries of Midian and Ephah.
  All from Sheba will come. They will bring gold and frankincense, and will proclaim the praises of
  Yahweh. All the flocks of Kedar will be gathered together to you. The rams of Nebaioth will serve
  you."*
- Genesis 12:3 — *"in you all the families of the earth will be blessed"*
- Isaiah 21:11, 14; Jeremiah 49:28–33; Ezekiel 27:20–21 — the same peoples as ordinary nations in the
  oracles
- Matthew 2:11 — gold and frankincense, from the east

## Analysis

**Isaiah 60 names five peoples in two verses, and every one of them is a descendant of Abraham
listed in Genesis 25.**

| Isaiah 60 | Genesis 25 | line |
|---|---|---|
| *the dromedaries of **Midian*** | 25:2, a son of Keturah | Keturah |
| *and **Ephah*** | 25:4, Midian's firstborn | Keturah |
| *all from **Sheba*** | 25:3, Jokshan's son | Keturah |
| *the flocks of **Kedar*** | 25:13, Ishmael's second son | Ishmael, through Hagar |
| *the rams of **Nebaioth*** | 25:13, Ishmael's firstborn | Ishmael, through Hagar |

**Three from the wife who is called a concubine, two from the slave who is called a wife.** The two
branches Genesis 25 exists to close out are the two branches Isaiah 60 brings back — and it brings
them back not as tribute-paying subjects but with a verb: *"they will proclaim the praises of
Yahweh"* (60:6).

### What Genesis did with them, and what Isaiah does

**Genesis paid them off and pointed them east.**

> **25:6** but to the sons of Abraham's concubines, Abraham gave gifts. He sent them away from Isaac
> his son, while he yet lived, eastward, to the east country.

That is the last the book has to say about the Keturah line, and 25:18 is the last it has to say about
Ishmael's. **They are given property and a direction, and closed.** Isaiah reverses both terms: the
direction becomes *toward* — *"they all gather themselves together, they come to you"* (60:4) — and
the property comes with them, *"gold and frankincense"*, which is what a camel caravan out of Midian
and Sheba was actually carrying on that road. See
[[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]] and [[Keturah]].

**The trade route is not incidental.** [[Havilah]] is introduced at Genesis 2:11 as the land *"where
there is gold"*; the Ishmaelites of 37:25 come up out of Gilead *"with their camels bearing spices and
balm and myrrh, going to carry it down to Egypt"*. **Isaiah takes the incense caravan of the northern
Arabian desert — a real thing, on a real road, run by these exact peoples — and has it arrive at Zion
carrying the same goods for a different purpose.**

### This is Genesis 12:3, and Isaiah does not say so

*"In you all the families of the earth will be blessed."* The promise is the reason the cycle exists,
it is restated four times to Abraham, and **it is the one clause of the covenant that is still
completely outstanding when he is buried at 25:9.** See
[[All the Families of the Earth - Genesis 12.3 in the New Testament]].

**Isaiah 60 supplies an instance of it that is easy to miss, because he names peoples rather than
ancestors.** A reader who does not have Genesis 25 in front of them hears a list of Arabian tribes; a
reader who does hears Abraham's own disinherited sons. **Neither Genesis nor Isaiah connects them, and
the wiki records the observation as an observation rather than as an argument about what Isaiah
intended.** What is checkable is the overlap: five names, two verses, one genealogy.

And it sharpens the pattern the book has been running since chapter 10 — **the covenant's neighbours
are its relatives.** Moab and Ammon are Lot's (19:37–38); the Arameans are Nahor's (22:20–24); the
Edomites are Esau's; the Midianites and Ishmaelites are Abraham's. **Israel spends the historical
books fighting its own cousins**, and the prophets keep bringing them back in. See [[The Nations]] and
[[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].

### The Matthew question, kept at arm's length

Matthew 2:11 has visitors from the east opening treasures of *"gold, frankincense, and myrrh"* — two
of the three goods Isaiah 60:6 names, arriving from the same direction. The identification of the magi
with Isaiah's caravan is ancient, it is why the Christian tradition made them kings, and **Matthew
himself does not quote Isaiah 60 or make the link.** It is recorded here as reception history and is
not the point of this page; the Genesis-to-Isaiah overlap stands on its own without it.

## Significance

**The Abraham cycle ends by disinheriting seven sons, and the prophets bring five of their peoples
back with gifts.**

Genesis is scrupulous about the difference between blessing and covenant — Ishmael is fruitful,
multiplied, and princely, and the covenant is Isaac's (17:20–21) — and it never suggests that the
sons sent east are outside God's interest. **It simply stops following them.** Isaiah 60 is the
Hebrew Bible picking the thread back up six centuries later and having the camels come the other way.

**Whether that is a fulfilment of 12:3 or a coincidence of tribal geography is not settled by either
text.** What both texts do supply is that the promise was always stated in terms of *all the families
of the earth*, and that the families in question turned out, in the end, to include the ones Abraham
paid off. See [[Blessing]], [[Call and Election]], and [[Hope]].
