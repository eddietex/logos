# Korah Died but His Sons Lived - Numbers 16 and 26

## Passages involved
- [[The Second Census]] — Numbers 26.
- [[The Rebellion of Korah]]
- [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]]

## Analysis

Numbers 16:27–33 includes households and children in the swallowing scene and speaks broadly
of Korah's men and possessions. Numbers 26:10 explicitly places Korah in the swallowing;
26:11 explicitly exempts his sons from death. The later statement qualifies an indiscriminate
reading of the earlier one. It supplies no narrative of how or why those sons escaped.

Exodus 6:24 already names Korah's sons and the Korahite families; Numbers 26:58 still lists
the Korahites among Levi's families. Genealogical continuity and the father's judgment
therefore coexist. Neither a claim that every child of every rebel died nor a general
rule that children never suffer with households fits both accounts.

Sources: stored WEB in Numbers and Exodus; no source-language claim is made.

## Significance

The later census preserves a precise exception without explaining away the troubling household
deaths in the rebellion narrative.
