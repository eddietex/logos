# Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1

## Analysis

**The place of the fear becomes the inheritance.** The men at Hebron are the ones who stopped Israel
at Numbers 13:22, 28, 33. Forty-five years later, Joshua 14:12 has Caleb ask for that same country:
*for you heard in that day how the Anakim were there, and great and fortified cities. It may be that
Yahweh will be with me, and I shall drive them out*. He repeats the wording of the report, *great
and fortified*, and adds the name the report left out.

**Where he walked.** Numbers 14:24 promises him *the land into which he went*, Deuteronomy 1:36 *the
land that he has trodden on*, and Joshua 14:9 *the land where you walked*. The singular *wayyābōʾ*
at Numbers 13:22, *he came to Hebron* (WLC), has been read as Caleb going there alone. That reading
is recalled, and the singular could be collective. It would explain why a promise to *him* lands on
Hebron.

**Three names survive the forty-five years.** Ahiman, Sheshai, and Talmai are in Numbers 13:22,
Joshua 15:14, and Judges 1:10, the same three. So either they are clan names or the story is
remarkably compressed. The wiki leaves it there.

**Who removed them** is told three ways, and the wiki keeps all three:

- Joshua 11:21 — *Joshua came at that time, and cut off the Anakim from the hill country, from
  Hebron*
- Joshua 15:14 and Judges 1:20 — Caleb drove them out
- Judges 1:10 — *Judah went against the Canaanites who lived in Hebron … They struck Sheshai,
  Ahiman, and Talmai*

Calling the people of Hebron *Canaanites* in Judges 1:10 is a further difference. Joshua 11:22
leaves a remnant at Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod. See [[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]].

## Significance

- Caleb's one sentence in Numbers 13:30 is answered by Joshua 14–15 in fact. He was able.
- Hebron is already the wiki's place of the promise, through [[Abram and Lot Separate]] and
  [[Machpelah]], so the one man who wanted to go up received the town where the fathers were buried.
