# Outside the Camp - The Sin Offering and Hebrews 13

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 29:10–14** — the bull of the priests' ordination: blood on the altar, fat burned on it, and
  *"the meat of the bull, and its skin, and its dung, you shall burn with fire outside of the camp:
  it is a sin offering"* — [[The Ordination of the Priests]].
- **Leviticus 8:14–17** — the same disposal performed *(verified WEB; not yet ingested)*.
- **Hebrews 13:10–13** — the bodies of animals whose blood is brought into the holy place are burned
  *"outside of the camp"*, and Jesus *"suffered outside of the gate"* *(verified WEB; not yet
  ingested)*.
- **Hebrews 7:27** — a high priest who does not need *"to offer up sacrifices daily, first for his
  own sins, and then for the sins of the people"* *(verified WEB; not yet ingested)*.

## Analysis

**Exodus 29:14 is the first sin offering in Exodus burned outside the camp.** The bull's blood and
fat belong to the altar. Its meat, skin, and dung are taken away and burned. Exodus does not give the
reason. It does show that this offering is made for the priests themselves, who lay their hands on
it before they have served (29:10).

**Hebrews reads the location as a pattern.** Hebrews 13:11 speaks of animals *"whose blood is brought
into the holy place by the high priest as an offering for sin"*, whose bodies are burned *"outside of
the camp"*. Hebrews 13:12 continues: *"Therefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people
through his own blood, suffered outside of the gate."* Then 13:13 calls readers to *"go out to him
outside of the camp, bearing his reproach"*.

**The closest Torah analogue for Hebrews' wording is not Exodus 29.** The ordination bull's blood is
put on the altar's horns and at its base (Exodus 29:12; Leviticus 8:15), not brought into the holy
place. Hebrews' description fits offerings whose blood enters the sanctuary, which Exodus has not yet
narrated. Exodus 29 shares the disposal outside the camp and the category of sin offering. It is
not the specific rite Hebrews describes, and this page does not claim that it is.

**Hebrews 7:27 contrasts priests who need offerings for themselves.** Exodus 29 begins the priests'
service with a sin offering on their behalf and then establishes daily offerings (29:38–42). Hebrews
contrasts that with one who *"did this once for all, when he offered up himself"* (7:27).

## Significance

**The typology rests on a place and a category, not on a single verse.** Exodus 29 is where the sin
offering and its disposal outside the camp first appear together in Exodus. Hebrews applies that
pattern to Jesus' death outside the city and to his followers' leaving of the camp. Readers can trace
the Exodus contribution without collapsing the differences between ordination, later sin offerings,
and Hebrews' argument. See [[Sin]], [[Sacrifice and Offering]], [[Blood]], and [[Priesthood]].
