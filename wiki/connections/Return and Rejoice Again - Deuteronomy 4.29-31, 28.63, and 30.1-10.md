# Return and Rejoice Again - Deuteronomy 4.29-31, 28.63, and 30.1-10

## Passages involved
- [[No Form Seen and No Image Made]] — Deuteronomy 4:25–31, especially 29–31.
- [[Curses for Disobedience]] — Deuteronomy 28:63–64.
- [[Return Restoration and the Circumcised Heart]] — Deuteronomy 30:1–10.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy gives the same sequence twice, once at the end of the first address and once after the
covenant curses: scattering, return to Yahweh *with all your heart and with all your soul*, and a
God who does not finish with Israel at the exile.

| | Deuteronomy 4:29–31 | Deuteronomy 30:1–10 |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | *When you are in oppression, and all these things have come on you, in the latter days* (4:30) | *when all these things have come on you, the blessing and the curse* (30:1) |
| Israel | *seek … find … search after him with all your heart and with all your soul*; *return … and listen to his voice* (4:29–30) | *call them to mind … return … obey his voice … with all your heart, and with all your soul* (30:1–2) |
| Yahweh | *a merciful God. He will not fail you, neither destroy you, nor forget the covenant* (4:31) | *have compassion on you … gather you … circumcise your heart* (30:3–6) |
| Land | not mentioned | *bring you into the land which your fathers possessed* (30:5) |

Chapter 30 adds two things chapter 4 lacks: the return to the land, and the promise that Yahweh will
circumcise the heart. The second answers 29:4, *Yahweh has not given you a heart to know*.

Between the two comes 28:63. The WEB reads: *as Yahweh rejoiced over you to do you good, and to
multiply you, so Yahweh will rejoice over you to cause you to perish, and to destroy you*, followed in
28:64 by *Yahweh will scatter you among all peoples*. Deuteronomy 30:9 takes up the first half of that
sentence again: *Yahweh will again rejoice over you for good, as he rejoiced over your fathers*. The
same Hebrew verb, *śûś* (Strong's 7797), is tagged in 28:63 and 30:9 in the Open Scriptures WLC
(morphhb). Chapter 30 also reverses the scattering: *gather you from all the peoples where Yahweh your
God has scattered you* (30:3).

The order of agents differs between the two texts. In 4:29–31 Israel seeks and returns, and the reason
given for hope is Yahweh's mercy. In 30:1–10 Israel returns (2), Yahweh restores (3–5), Yahweh changes
the heart (6), and Israel returns and obeys (8), with a closing *if* (10). Neither text explains
whether the return is the condition or the result of divine action; both keep the human turning and
the divine promise together.

Sources: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:29–31, 28:63–64, 29:4, 30:1–10. Hebrew lemma checked in the Open
Scriptures WLC (morphhb, GitHub), Deuteronomy 28:63 and 30:9, retrieved 2026-11-10.

## Significance

The threatened end of [[Curses for Disobedience]] is not the end of Deuteronomy's account. The
[[Covenant]] sanctions run their course, and then [[Compassion and Mercy]], [[Remembrance]], and the
promised heart change open a way back. The disturbing statement that Yahweh would rejoice over
Israel's destruction is not erased; 30:9 says he will rejoice *again* for good, which keeps
[[Joy and Enjoyment]] and [[Judgment]] in the same frame. [[Hope]] after exile begins inside the
Torah, before the land has been entered.
