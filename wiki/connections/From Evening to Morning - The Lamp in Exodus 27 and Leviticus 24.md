# From Evening to Morning - The Lamp in Exodus 27 and Leviticus 24

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 27:20–21** — Israel supplies oil; Aaron and his sons tend the lamp outside the veil —
  [[The Altar and the Court]].
- **Leviticus 24:1–4** — the rule repeated with Aaron named, and the lamps on the pure gold
  lampstand specified *(verified WEB, not yet ingested)*.
- **Exodus 25:31–40; 26:35** — the lampstand made and assigned its location —
  [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] and [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].
- **Exodus 30:7–8** — morning tending and evening lighting alongside incense service
  *(local WEB read; not yet ingested)*.

## Analysis

**The later instruction closely repeats the earlier one.** Both passages direct Moses to command
Israel to provide pure beaten olive oil for the light. Both locate the service outside the veil
in the Tent of Meeting, before Yahweh, from evening to morning; both make it a lasting statute
through the generations. The continuity concerns the supplied material, the place, the schedule,
and the responsibility for the light, rather than a symbolic explanation of oil.

**The formulations are not identical.** Exodus 27:21 names Aaron and his sons; Leviticus 24:3
names Aaron. Leviticus 24:4 then expressly identifies the lamps on the pure gold lampstand.
This makes the furnishing explicit where Exodus 27 assumes the instructions of chapter 25.
Leviticus' naming of Aaron alone is not an express revocation of his sons' role.

**Continual service has a stated nightly period.** Both WEB passages combine continual light
with evening-to-morning attendance. That establishes recurring night service, but does not by
itself resolve every question about daytime burning. Exodus 30:7–8 distinguishes morning tending
from evening lighting. No Hebrew lexical conclusion is needed or asserted for this comparison.

**The command anticipates the office.** Exodus places the rule immediately before the explicit
appointment of Aaron and his sons in 28:1. The family is assigned a duty here; their garments
and consecration are still to be prescribed. See [[Priesthood]], [[Aaron]], and [[Moses]].

## Significance

The lamp connects the people who provide its oil to the family who tends it on their behalf.
It also connects the making of sanctuary furniture to the repeated work of its use. See
[[Light and Darkness]], [[Work]], [[Mediation and Advocacy]], and [[The Dwelling of God]].

The later symbolic lampstand images belong to their own settings; Exodus and Leviticus do not
identify the oil as the Spirit. See [[Seven Lamps - The Lampstand in Zechariah 4 and Revelation 1]].
