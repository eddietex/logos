# Rash Words of the Lips - Leviticus 5.4, Numbers 30, and Psalm 106.33

## Passages involved

- [[Confession and Offerings According to Means]] — Leviticus 5:4: *"Or if anyone swears rashly with his
  lips to do evil, or to do good, whatever it is that a man might utter rashly with an oath, and it is
  hidden from him; when he knows of it, then he shall be guilty of one of these."* *(stored WEB)*
- [[Vows of Men and Women]] — Numbers 30:6, 8: a husband may void *"her vow which is on her, and the rash
  utterance of her lips, with which she has bound her soul. Yahweh will forgive her."* *(stored WEB)*
- **Psalm 106:32–33** — *"They angered him also at the waters of Meribah, so that Moses was troubled for
  their sakes; because they were rebellious against his spirit, he spoke rashly with his lips."* *(WEB
  verified at bible-api.com, 2026-11-05; not yet ingested)* — compare [[The Waters of Meribah]].

## Analysis

**One verb, with the lips.** All three texts use the verb *biṭṭēʾ*, to speak rashly, with *lips*:
*ləbaṭṭēʾ biśpātayim* (Leviticus 5:4), *mibṭāʾ śəpāteyhā*, the noun from the same root (Numbers 30:6, 8), and
*wayəbaṭṭēʾ biśpātāyw* (Psalm 106:33; all Miqra according to the Masorah via Sefaria). WEB renders all three
with *rash*.

**Three outcomes.** In Leviticus the rash oath, once known, makes the speaker guilty, and he confesses and
brings a female lamb or goat for a sin offering (5:5–6). In Numbers a woman's rash utterance can be voided by a
father or husband, and *Yahweh will forgive her*, with no offering named. In Psalm 106 the rash speech is
Moses' own, at Meribah, and the psalm places it inside Israel's provocation: *they were rebellious against his
spirit*.

**Moses' words at Meribah.** Numbers 20:10 gives Moses' speech to the assembly, and 20:12 the sentence that he
will not bring the assembly into the land. Psalm 106:33 is the only text quoted here that names the fault as
rash speech; Numbers 20:12 names it as not believing in Yahweh and not sanctifying him. Both are recorded; the
wiki does not harmonize them. See [[Meribah's Sentence and Aaron's Death]].

**Whose *spirit*.** *His spirit* in Psalm 106:33 is ambiguous in Hebrew (*rûḥô*): the rebellion may be against
God's spirit or against Moses'. WEB's lower-case *his* does not decide it, and neither does the wiki.

## Significance

Leviticus treats a rash oath as guilt to be confessed and atoned. Numbers lets a household authority cancel a
woman's rash word and says Yahweh forgives her. Psalm 106 uses the same verb for the act that cost Moses the land.
See [[Language and Speech]], [[Oaths and Vows]], and
[[In the Multitude of Words - Proverbs 10.19, 17.27-28, and Ecclesiastes 5.2-7]].
