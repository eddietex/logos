## Analysis

**The Hebrew phrase.** *ʾÎš kilḇāḇô* (1 Samuel 13:14, WLC): *a man according to his heart*. The preposition is
*kə-*, *like, according to*. The phrase is read two ways. One takes it as a description of the man, whose heart is
like Yahweh's. The other takes *heart* as *will* or *choice*, so that the man is the one Yahweh has chosen,
*according to his wish*; this reading has scholarly defenders (recalled, not checked). The wiki records both.

**The heart at Bethlehem.** Three chapters later, when Samuel looks at Jesse's eldest, Yahweh says he does not see
*as man sees*: *Yahweh looks at the heart*, *YHWH yirʾeh lallēḇāḇ* (16:7, stored WEB; WLC). *Lēḇāḇ* is the same
noun as *kilḇāḇô*. Saul had been chosen with attention to his height (9:2; 10:23); the next choice is made by
what Yahweh sees in the heart. Whether 16:7 interprets 13:14 is not said, but the noun links them.

**The man unnamed.** 1 Samuel 13 does not name him, and 15:28 calls him only *a neighbor of yours who is better
than you*. The name comes at the anointing (16:13, stored WEB; not yet ingested). Samuel speaks of him at Gilgal in
completed verbs, *Yahweh has sought … and Yahweh has appointed him* (13:14, WLC *biqqēš … wayṣawwēhû*), before the
narrative has introduced him.

**Paul's use.** In Acts 13:22 Paul, at Pisidian Antioch, gives David's choice as a divine testimony, *I have found
David the son of Jesse, a man after my heart, who will do all my will* (WEB checked). The sentence is commonly
described as a composite of 1 Samuel 13:14 with Psalm 89:20, *I have found David my servant*, and perhaps Isaiah
44:28, *he will perform all my pleasure* (the identification is recalled, not checked; neither Psalm nor Isaiah is
stored). The clause *who will do all my will* reads the phrase as a statement about obedience, the opposite of
Saul's failure *to keep* what Yahweh commanded. The same speech gives Saul *forty years* (13:21), a figure 1 Samuel
13:1's Hebrew does not supply.

## Significance

The phrase names David before the story does, and it is the reason the story gives for the change of king. The two
readings of *kilḇāḇô* lead to two emphases: on David's character, which the books go on to show in both its devotion
and its sins; or on Yahweh's freedom to choose, which matches 16:7. Acts takes it in the first sense, as obedience.

### 1 Samuel 16:7 now ingested — the heart and the height

16:7 is now in [[Samuel Anoints David]]. In context, the sentence answers Samuel's mistake about Eliab, *Surely
Yahweh's anointed is before him* (16:6), and names what Samuel was looking at: *his face* and *the height of his
stature*, *gəḇōah qômāṯô* (16:7, WLC). Saul had been *taller than any of the people* (9:2) and Samuel had shown him to
the people on that basis (10:23–24). The verse is about the error of the one choosing, not about a virtue in the one
chosen: Yahweh gives a reason for rejecting Eliab and none for choosing David. The narrator then describes David
by his looks (16:12), not his heart. This favours neither reading of *kilḇāḇô* decisively. It does show that, in 1
Samuel, the heart is first of all what Yahweh *sees*, *yirʾeh … lallēḇāḇ*. Eliab later claims to see David's heart,
*the naughtiness of your heart*, *rōaʿ ləḇāḇeḵā* (17:28, stored WEB; not yet ingested). Sources: stored WEB 1 Samuel
9:2; 10:23–24; 16:6–12; 17:28. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.
