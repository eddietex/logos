# Spoken of Me the Thing That Is Right - The Verdict of Job 42.7

## Passages involved

- **Job 42:7–8** — *"My wrath is kindled against you, and against your two friends; for you have not spoken
  of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job has."* Said twice, the second time at the end of 42:8 —
  [[The Restoration of Job]]
- **Job 38:2** — *"Who is this who darkens counsel by words without knowledge?"* — God's first words to Job
  — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Job 40:8** — *"Will you even annul my judgment? Will you condemn me, that you may be justified?"* —
  [[Behemoth and Leviathan]]
- **Job 42:3** — Job: *"I have uttered that which I did not understand"* — [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]]
- **Job 13:7–10** — Job to the friends: *"Will you speak unrighteously for God, and talk deceitfully for
  him? Will you show partiality to him? Will you contend for God? … He will surely reprove you, if you
  secretly show partiality."* — [[Though He Slay Me]]
- **Job 7:7** — Job's first words to God: the turn none of the friends makes — [[The Arrows of the Almighty]]
- **Job 32:2–3** — Elihu angry with the friends *"because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned
  Job"* — [[Elihu Speaks in Anger]]
- **Job 22:6–9** — the crimes Eliphaz invented, which God's verdict does not mention —
  [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]]
- **Job 1:8; 2:3** — *"Have you considered my servant Job?"* — the title God reissues four times at 42:7–8

## Analysis

**The verdict has two halves, and both are surprising.** That the friends are rebuked is not, by the end of
the book, a surprise to the reader; that Job is commended, by the God who opened his answer with *words
without knowledge*, is.

### What the friends did wrong

The charge is precise. It is not that they were cruel to Job, though they were; and not that they lied
about him, though Eliphaz did (22:6–9). It is that they did not speak *of God* — or *to* God — *the thing
that is right*.

Job had warned them in exactly these terms at 13:7–10. *"Will you speak unrighteously for God, and talk
deceitfully for him?"* — a defence of God built on a falsehood about Job is not a service to God. *"Will
you show partiality to him?"* — *tiśśāʾûn pānāyw*, lift his face, the bribe of a witness who favours the
powerful party. *"He will surely reprove you."* **At 42:7–8 he does.** And he turns their idiom round: the
friends lifted God's face; God says of Job *pānāyw ʾeśśāʾ*, *I will lift his face* (42:8), and 42:9 reports
that he did.

The friends' theology was orthodox sentence by sentence. Eliphaz's *"Happy is the man whom God
corrects"* (5:17) has its counterpart in Proverbs 3:11–12, which Hebrews 12 quotes (see
[[Do Not Despise the Chastening - Job 5.17, Proverbs 3.11-12, and Hebrews 12]]); Paul quotes him at 1
Corinthians 3:19 (see [[He Takes the Wise in Their Craftiness - Job 5.13 in 1 Corinthians 3.19]]). The
verdict does not withdraw those sentences. **What it condemns is their use** — true statements about God
applied to a case they did not fit, so that the whole was false while the parts were true. *Nəkônâ*, from
*kûn*, is what is *firm*, *established*, *set right*: speech that corresponds to the case.

### What Job did right

Three answers have been given, and the text supports each without settling it.

1. **He told the truth about his case.** He would not confess sins he had not committed (27:5–6), and he
   would not say that what was happening to him was just. That is what *nəkônâ* means at its plainest.
2. **He spoke to God.** The Hebrew *ʾēlay* is most naturally *to me*; the WEB's *of me* is a recognised sense
   after a verb of speaking. If *to me* is pressed, the contrast is the direction of speech: Job addressed God
   from 7:7 on, and the friends never addressed him once. **They talked about God in his presence and never
   to him.** The wiki records this reading and does not adopt it over the WEB's.
3. **His last words.** Some read *as my servant Job has* as referring to 42:1–6 alone. The wiki notes that the
   verdict is addressed to the friends about a dialogue they shared, which tells against restricting it.

### The tension with 38:2

God said Job *darkened counsel by words without knowledge* (38:2), and asked whether he would *condemn* God to
justify himself (40:8). Job agreed: *"I have uttered that which I did not understand"* (42:3). Then God says
he spoke *the thing that is right*. **Both are in the text, and the book does not reconcile them.** The most
economical reading is that they are judgments on different things — 38:2 on Job's *knowledge*, which was not
enough to judge God's ordering of the world; 42:7 on his *truthfulness*, which never failed. A man can be
wrong in his reach and right in his report. The wiki records that reading as the most natural, and leaves
the tension standing. See [[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]].

### Who is left out

The verdict names Eliphaz and *"your two friends"*. **Elihu, who spoke six chapters, is in neither half.** He
had been angry with the friends for condemning Job without an answer (32:3) and with Job for justifying himself
rather than God (32:2) — that is, he had pronounced the book's verdict early, on both sides, and got half of it
wrong. God does not mention him. See [[Elihu the Buzite]].

## Significance

- It is the book's interpretive key, given at the end on purpose: forty chapters of argument are to be read
  knowing that the man who accused God is the one God approves. See [[Job]].
- It separates *speaking for God* from *speaking rightly of God*, and puts the friends on the wrong side of the
  difference. That is the warning of 13:7–10, kept.
- It makes honesty before God a form of righteousness. The psalms of lament, which accuse and protest in God's
  hearing, sit on the same side of this line as Job; see [[Mourning and Lament]] and [[Righteousness]].
