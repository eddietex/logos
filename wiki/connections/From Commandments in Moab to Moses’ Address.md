# From Commandments in Moab to Moses’ Address

## Passages involved
- [[The Setting of the Covenant Instruction]] — Deuteronomy 4:44–49
- [[The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad]] — Numbers 36:13
- [[Sihon and Og]] — Numbers 21:21–35
- [[The Words of Moses Beyond the Jordan]] — Deuteronomy 1:1–5

## Analysis

Numbers ends by locating Yahweh’s commandments through Moses in the plains of Moab beside
the Jordan (36:13). Deuteronomy opens with Moses addressing Israel beyond that river, explicitly
in Moab, according to Yahweh’s command (1:1–5). The literary transition continues the setting
and mediator while changing from the close of a legal narrative to the introduction of an
extended address.

The notice of Sihon and Og’s defeat in Deuteronomy 1:4 reaches back to Numbers 21:21–35.
Heshbon belongs to Sihon’s identification in both; Numbers 21:33 specifies the battle at Edrei.
Deuteronomy’s additional naming of Ashtaroth concerns Og’s residence. The notices have
different levels of detail and do not require two separate campaigns.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers 21:21–35; 36:13; Deuteronomy 1:1–5. These are paraphrases.

### The renewed setting

Deuteronomy 4:44–49 renews the narrative heading before the next address. It again names
Moses, Israel, the law, the eastern Jordan setting, and the conquered kingdoms of Sihon and Og,
now specifying the valley opposite Beth Peor and the extent from Aroer to Hermon. The setting
continues 3:29. Its compressed reference to coming out of Egypt does not replace the fortieth-year
date in 1:3–4. Instruction is framed by the journey and possession, without narrating another
campaign. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:1–5; 3:29; 4:44–49.

## Significance

The law is presented to a people with a remembered journey and recent victories, before the
western crossing. Continuity of setting does not make Deuteronomy a verbatim repetition of
Numbers, nor does this opening establish the authorship of every narrative frame.
