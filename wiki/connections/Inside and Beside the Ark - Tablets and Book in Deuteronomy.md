# Inside and Beside the Ark - Tablets and Book in Deuteronomy

## Passages involved
- [[New Tablets and the Journey Onward]] — Deuteronomy 10:1–11.
- [[Read the Law to All Israel]] — Deuteronomy 31:9–13.
- [[The Book Beside the Ark]] — Deuteronomy 31:24–29.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 10:2–5 distinguishes the stone tablets, written by Yahweh with the ten
commandments and deposited by Moses inside the ark. Deuteronomy 31:24–26 describes Moses
finishing the law book and commanding the Levites to place it beside the ark. The different
locations should not be flattened into a claim that this book is another object inside it.

The bearers have already been identified with Levi in 10:8 and as Levitical priests in 31:9.
The law is to be publicly read for learning and obedience (31:10–13), yet also preserved as
an accusing witness (31:26). Neither its sacred location nor its circulation guarantees
obedience. The text does not delimit the completed book’s contents in this paragraph.

## Significance

[[Covenant]], [[Priesthood]], and [[Law and Obedience]] meet in the preservation of testimony.
The difference between divine tablet-writing and Moses’ book-writing is explicit here; it
does not settle every compositional question about the final form of Deuteronomy.
