# Let Your Face Shine - Numbers 6 in Psalms 4, 67, 80, and 119

## Passages involved

- [[Yahweh Bless You and Keep You]] — Numbers 6:24–26.
- Psalm 67:1 — *"May God be merciful to us, bless us, and cause his face to shine on us."* *(WEB,
  bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- Psalm 4:6 — *"Yahweh, let the light of your face shine on us."* *(same)*
- Psalm 31:16 — *"Make your face to shine on your servant."* *(same)*
- Psalm 80:3 — *"Cause your face to shine, and we will be saved."* The refrain recurs at 80:7, 19. *(same;
  verse 3 quoted, the Hebrew of all three checked)*
- Psalm 119:135 — *"Make your face shine on your servant. Teach me your statutes."* *(same)*
- Daniel 9:17 — *"cause your face to shine on your sanctuary that is desolate."* *(same)*
- Psalm 121:7–8; 134:3 — *keep* and *bless* in the singular. *(same)*
- 2 Corinthians 4:6 — *"the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ."*
  *(same)*

## Analysis

By Strong's tags (WLC, morphhb), the verb *ʾôr* with God's *pānîm* occurs at Numbers 6:25, Psalms 31:17,
67:2, 80:4, 8, 20, 119:135, and Daniel 9:17. Psalm numbers there are WLC's; WEB's run one lower in
Psalms 31, 67, and 80. The other verses that the tags join are not about God's face: the lamps of
Exodus 25:37 and Numbers 8:2, and a person's face at Ecclesiastes 8:1. Every use with God's face after
Numbers is a prayer or a wish, and none is a priest speaking. The blessing's request has moved into the
mouth of the people.

**Psalm 67 is the closest.** In the space of one verse it has *be gracious*, *bless*, and *his face shine*,
the verbs of Numbers 6:24–25, in a different order and with *ʾĔlōhîm* in place of *YHWH* (WLC). By tags,
*ḥānan* and *ʾôr* join in one verse only here and at Numbers 6:25, apart from Isaiah 27:11, where the
words are used in other senses. The persons have changed as well. The priest said *you*; the psalm says
*us*. It then gives a purpose that Numbers does not, *that your way may be known on earth* (67:2). A
blessing on Israel becomes a means of reaching *all nations*. The psalm does not cite Numbers, so this
dependence is the wiki's inference, though a strong one.

**Psalm 4** joins two lines. *Neśâ ʿālênû ʾôr pānêkā* (4:7, WLC) has the lifting of 6:26 and the light of
6:25, with *lift up* tagged to the same verb though spelled unusually. WEB's *let ... shine* smooths it.

**Psalm 80** is what the blessing sounds like from outside it. The refrain, *Turn us again*, comes from a
people who no longer have what Numbers 6 asks for. Compare
[[The Lifted Face and the Hidden Face - Numbers 6 and Deuteronomy 31]].

**Psalm 119:135** ties the shining face to *your statutes*, a link Numbers does not make. **Daniel 9:17**
asks for the face to shine on a sanctuary and not on a person.

**Keep and bless.** Psalm 121:7–8 has *šāmar* with a singular *you* (WLC) and Yahweh as subject, and
Psalm 134:3 opens with the same word as Numbers 6:24, *yəbārekkā YHWH*, with *from Zion* added. Ruth 2:4 has
the same two words as the reapers' greeting to Boaz, so the phrase alone does not prove dependence on
Numbers.

**2 Corinthians 4:6** speaks of God's glory *in the face of Jesus Christ*. It stands inside Paul's reading
of Exodus 34, and the verse itself cites Genesis 1. It does not cite Numbers 6, and the wiki records it
as a possible further step, not a demonstrated one. See
[[The Shining Face and the Veil - Exodus 34 and 2 Corinthians 3]].

## Significance

Numbers gives the priest words to say over the people. The Psalms show those words being prayed by the
people themselves. Hebrew from WLC (morphhb) by Strong's tags; Greek not checked; no commentary
consulted.
