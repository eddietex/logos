# The Forgotten Sheaf - Gleaning in Leviticus 19 and 23, Deuteronomy 24, and Ruth 2

## Passages involved

- [[Be Holy and Love Your Neighbor]] — Leviticus 19:9–10; *"you shall not wholly reap the corners of
  your field, neither shall you gather the gleanings of your harvest. You shall not glean your vineyard,
  neither shall you gather the fallen grapes of your vineyard. You shall leave them for the poor and for
  the foreigner."*
- [[First Fruits and the Feast of Weeks]] — Leviticus 23:22; the same law repeated after the feast of
  weeks: *"You must leave them for the poor, and for the foreigner."*
- [[Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning]] — Deuteronomy 24:19–22; the forgotten sheaf, the olives,
  and the vineyard, each *"for the foreigner, for the fatherless, and for the widow"*.
- [[Fugitives, Interest, Vows, and a Neighbor's Produce]] — Deuteronomy 23:24–25; the passer-by who
  eats from a neighbor's field and vineyard.
- Ruth 2:2, 15–16 *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — Ruth asks, *"Let me now go to the
  field, and glean among the ears of grain after him in whose sight I find favor"*; Boaz commands his
  young men, *"Let her glean even among the sheaves … Also pull out some for her from the bundles, and
  leave it."*

## Analysis

**Two laws, not one.** Leviticus and Deuteronomy both leave part of the harvest, but they leave different
things to different people.

| | Leviticus 19:9–10; 23:22 | Deuteronomy 24:19–21 |
|---|---|---|
| Grain | the corners not reaped; the gleanings not gathered | the forgotten sheaf not fetched |
| Olives | — | the boughs not gone over again |
| Grapes | the vineyard not gleaned; fallen grapes not gathered | the vineyard not gleaned |
| For whom | *"the poor and … the foreigner"* | *"the foreigner, … the fatherless, and … the widow"* |
| Reason | *"I am Yahweh your God"* | blessing on *"all the work of your hands"*; *"you were a slave in the land of Egypt"* |

The Hebrew for *"poor"* in Leviticus is *ʿānî*; Deuteronomy 24:19–21 does not use it, and names the
widow and the fatherless instead (WLC). The verb for gleaning the vineyard is the same in both, the polel
of *ʿālal*, and in the Torah occurs only at Leviticus 19:10 and Deuteronomy 24:21 (WLC lemma search).

**What is left, and how.** Leviticus leaves a part of the field by design: the corners are never reaped.
Deuteronomy leaves what the harvest misses: the sheaf that was *"forgotten"* (*šākaḥ*), the olives after
the first beating, the grapes after the first picking. In Deuteronomy *šākaḥ* is otherwise the book's
warning word, *"Beware lest you forget Yahweh"* (6:12). The sheaf is the one thing Israel is told to leave
forgotten.

**Ruth.** Ruth 2 shows a gleaner at work. Ruth is a Moabite and a widow, so she belongs to two of
Deuteronomy's three classes; she asks to glean *"after him in whose sight I find favor"* (2:2), which
suggests gleaning depended on the owner's goodwill in practice (an inference from her words, not a
statement of law). Boaz goes past both laws. He lets her glean *"even among the sheaves"* and has his
men *"pull out some for her from the bundles, and leave it"* (2:15–16): grain left on purpose, where
Deuteronomy required only what was forgotten. Ruth does not cite Leviticus or Deuteronomy. The book has
not been ingested; the rest of Ruth 2 was not checked.

**The passer-by.** Deuteronomy 23:24–25 gives a different right: anyone may eat grapes and pluck ears in a
neighbor's field, but not carry them away. The gleaning laws give the poor what they can gather after the
harvest; 23:24–25 gives anyone what they can eat before it. See
[[Plucking a Neighbor's Grain - Deuteronomy 23.24-25 and the Sabbath Grain Fields]].

## Significance

The harvest laws make the poor a part of every harvest, not a recipient of occasional charity. Leviticus
grounds it in Yahweh's name; Deuteronomy in Israel's memory of slavery and the promise of blessing on the
work of the farmer's hands. Ruth is the one narrative in the canon built around a gleaner, and her story
ends in the line of David (Ruth 4:17–22, reported by reference and not checked). See
[[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], [[Within Your Gates - The Levite, the Foreigner, the Fatherless, and the Widow]],
and [[You Were a Slave in Egypt - The Remembrance Refrain of Deuteronomy]].
