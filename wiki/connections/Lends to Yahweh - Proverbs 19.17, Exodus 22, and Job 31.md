# Lends to Yahweh - Proverbs 19.17, Exodus 22, and Job 31

## Passages involved

- [[A Good Name]] — Proverbs 22:9, 16.

- [[The King's Heart]] — Proverbs 21:13, 26.

- [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] — Proverbs 19:1, 4, 7, 17, 22.
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:21–27.
- [[Job's Oath of Innocence]] — Job 31:16–23.

## Analysis

Proverbs 19:17 gives generosity an unexpected creditor and debtor:

> He who has pity on the poor lends to Yahweh; he will reward him.

The person helped is poor; the one who undertakes the reward is Yahweh. Verses 4 and 7 have already
described friends withdrawing when there is no wealth to attract them. Verse 17 redirects action
toward the person without social or financial leverage. It is a metaphor of divine commitment, not a
contract specifying interest, a timetable, or an increase in the giver's bank balance.

**Exodus gives concrete limits to the lending relationship.** Exodus 22:25 forbids interest on a
loan to the poor person among God's people. Verses 26–27 require the return of a pledged garment
before sunset, because it is the person's covering; Yahweh promises to hear the cry. There the
lending is literal and its terms are regulated. In Proverbs 19:17 the loan is figuratively to
Yahweh. The texts should not be collapsed into the same transaction, but both make treatment of the
vulnerable accountable to God.

**Job gives an account of the conduct.** Job 31:16–23 invokes judgment if he has withheld food,
clothing, or protection from the poor and fatherless. He says divine calamity and majesty restrain
him (23). His self-defense exemplifies the kind of help Proverbs commends, without using its lending
metaphor. His present suffering also prevents treating generosity as a guarantee of uninterrupted
prosperity.

The parallel is thematic; neither a direct quotation nor shared Hebrew terminology is asserted.

### Proverbs 21 in context

Proverbs 21 states the negative case — stopped ears to the poor bring an unheard cry (13) — and the
positive: *"the righteous give and don't withhold"* (26).

### Proverbs 22 in context

Proverbs 22:9 blesses *"He who has a generous eye"*, *"for he shares his food with the poor"*, and
22:16 sends both the oppressor of the poor and the giver to the rich to poverty. The pair inverts
the expected economics in the same way 19:17 does: giving to the poor is the gain, taking from them
the loss.

## Significance

[[Compassion and Mercy]] is accountable to [[God]] even when the recipient cannot repay.
[[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] are not excluded from obligation by their inability to attract
friends, and [[Wealth and Possessions]] becomes a means of care rather than a measure of their
worth.
