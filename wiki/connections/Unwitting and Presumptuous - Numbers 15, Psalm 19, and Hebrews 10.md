# Unwitting and Presumptuous - Numbers 15, Psalm 19, and Hebrews 10

## Passages involved

- **Numbers 15:27–31** — error forgiven, the high hand cut off —
  [[Offerings in the Land and Sins of Ignorance]]
- **Deuteronomy 17:12** — *"The man who does presumptuously"*, by *zādôn*, shall die
- **Psalm 19:12–13** — *"Who can discern his errors? Forgive me from hidden errors. Keep back your
  servant also from presumptuous sins"*
- **Luke 23:34; Acts 3:17; 1 Timothy 1:13** — *"they don't know what they are doing"*, *"you did this in
  ignorance"*, and *"I did it ignorantly in unbelief"*
- **Hebrews 5:2; 9:7** — the high priest with *"those who are ignorant and going astray"*, and blood for
  *"the errors of the people"*
- **Hebrews 10:26–29** — *"if we sin willfully after we have received the knowledge of the truth, there
  remains no more a sacrifice for sins"*

(WEB, checked at bible-api.com, `translation=web`, 2026-11-02, apart from Numbers in `raw/bible/`.)

## Analysis

Numbers sets two categories side by side, *bišgāgâ* and *bəyād rāmâ*. Psalm 19 prays over the same
pair in other words: *šəgîʾôt*, which by the tags occurs only there, and *zēdîm*, from the family used
of Hormah at Deuteronomy 1:43 and of the sin at Deuteronomy 17:12–13 (Psalm 19:13–14 in WLC). The
psalm adds what the law does not. A person cannot fully know his own errors, and the presumptuous kind
can *have dominion*, so he asks to be kept from it.

Hebrews has the same line in Greek, *ἀγνοημάτων* at 9:7 and *ἑκουσίως* at 10:26 (SBLGNT). Rahlfs's
Numbers 15 uses *ἀκουσίως* for the error. Hebrews does not cite Numbers. At 10:28 it mentions a man who
sets aside Moses' law, and its *two or three witnesses* is the wording of Deuteronomy 17:6, not
Numbers 15. Like Numbers, its sentence has no sacrifice left. The difference is the object: *trodden
under foot the Son of God* (10:29).

Luke, Acts, and 1 Timothy use ignorance as ground for mercy in a way that parallels the *šəgāgâ*
category. In 1 Timothy 1:13 the speaker calls himself a *blasphemer* and also says he acted ignorantly.
Numbers 15 keeps those two words apart. Whether the writer has Numbers in mind is not shown.

## Significance

The line drawn in Numbers is not one the Bible simply repeats. Numbers leaves the high-handed with no
offering. Leviticus 6:1–7 gives one for deliberate fraud, 2 Samuel 12:13 reports forgiveness, and
Hebrews 10 keeps the warning. The wiki notes all of them and resolves none of them.
