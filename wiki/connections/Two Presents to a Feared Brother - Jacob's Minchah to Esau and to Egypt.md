# Two Presents to a Feared Brother — Jacob's Minchah to Esau and to Egypt

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 32:13–21** — [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]]; the droves sent ahead to [[Esau]]
- **Genesis 33:8–11** — [[The Reconciliation with Esau]]; the present pressed on him and accepted
- **Genesis 43:11–15, 25–26** — [[The Second Journey]]; the present carried down to [[Joseph]]
- **Genesis 4:3–5** — the word's first use, of [[Cain]]'s and [[Abel]]'s offerings

## Analysis

### The same noun, the same tactic, twenty years apart

***Minḥâ*** is a gift given upward: **tribute to a superior, a placating present to someone with the
power to hurt you.** It becomes the grain offering of Leviticus 2; **its first appearance in
Scripture is Genesis 4:3–5**, where [[Cain]] and [[Abel]] each bring one.

**[[Jacob]] sends one twice, and both times to a brother he expects to be killed by.**

| | **Genesis 32:13–21** | **Genesis 43:11–15** |
|---|---|---|
| **the recipient** | [[Esau]], coming with four hundred men | *"the man"*, lord of Egypt |
| **what Jacob knows** | that it is his brother | **nothing** |
| **the goods** | 550 head of livestock, in graded droves | balm, honey, spices, myrrh, nuts, almonds |
| **who carries it** | servants, *"every herd by itself"* | his sons |
| **Jacob's position** | *"he himself stayed that night in the camp"* (32:21) | in Canaan; he does not go |
| **the prayer before** | 32:9–12, arguing from the covenant | 43:14, ***ʾĒl Šadday*** and ***raḥămîm*** |
| **the stated aim** | *"I will appease him with the present that goes before me … Perhaps he will accept me"* (32:20) | *"May God Almighty give you mercy before the man"* (43:14) |
| **outcome** | Esau runs, embraces, weeps, and has to be persuaded to take it (33:4, 11) | **never mentioned again after 43:26** |

**The method is identical and Jacob has not revised it in twenty years.** Faced with a powerful man
who may destroy his family, **he sends goods ahead and stays behind.** At the Jabbok he sends the
present, then his wives and children, and is left alone (32:22–24). Here he sends the present, then
[[Benjamin]], and is left in Canaan. **[[Wealth and Possessions]] is how this man negotiates with
danger**, and the book has shown it from the lentils onward.

### What the second one does not know

**The parallel's whole force is in the column that says *nothing*.**

At 32:20 Jacob names his aim precisely: ***ʾăkappərâ pānāyw*** — *I will cover his face*, a verb that
will become the vocabulary of atonement — *"and afterward I will see his face. Perhaps he will accept
me."* **He is managing a brother whose grievance he can itemise**, and he has had two decades to
prepare.

At 43:11 he is managing a stranger. **He does not know the man has a grievance, does not know what
the grievance is, and does not know he is the reason for it.** *"Take from the choice fruits of the
land"* is a guess. **And the guess is right about the wrong thing**: the goods he picks are three of
the commodities that carried Joseph to Egypt in the first place (37:25), which he also does not know.
See [[Balm, Spices, and Myrrh - The Caravan's Cargo and Jacob's Present]].

### The two receptions

**Esau does not want it.** *"What do you mean by all this company which I met?"* … *"I have enough,
my brother; let that which you have be yours"* (33:8–9), and Jacob has to press: *"Please take the
gift that I brought to you, because God has dealt graciously with me"* (33:11). **The present is
refused, argued over, and accepted as a formality.** Its purpose had already been served by something
else entirely — **Esau ran before he saw a single goat.**

**Joseph never acknowledges his.** It is prepared at 43:25, presented at 43:26 with the bowing, and
**Genesis does not mention it again.** He asks about their father instead. **The tribute that was
supposed to buy *raḥămîm* is not what produces it**; what produces it is the sight of Benjamin
(43:29–30). **Twice, in two chapters twenty years apart, the goods are irrelevant and the brother is
already moved.**

### The verb underneath both

*"I will appease him with the present"* (32:20) is ***kāpar***, *cover*. *"May God Almighty give you
mercy"* (43:14) is ***raḥămîm***. **The first is something Jacob does to a face; the second is
something he asks God to do inside one.** The distance between them is the distance between the man
at the Jabbok and the man at 43:14 — **he has stopped believing that the arrangement of goods
determines the outcome, without quite stopping doing it.** See [[Compassion and Mercy]] and
[[Prayer]].

## Significance

**It is the clearest measure Genesis gives of how little [[Jacob]] changes.** He is renamed at 32:28
and the book goes on calling him Jacob; **he is given a covenant name and he goes on packing
livestock.** See [[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]]. The second present
is the first one repeated by a man who is older, poorer, and no less frightened.

**It also puts the two reconciliations of Genesis in the same frame.** [[Esau]] and [[Joseph]] are
the book's two wronged brothers, and both are approached the same way by the same man; **in both
cases the mercy arrives ahead of the tribute and owes it nothing.** The pattern the cycle finally
states at 50:20 — that what the family arranges and what actually happens are two different
stories — **is already visible in a herd of goats and a bag of almonds.** See [[Providence]] and
[[Strife and Peacemaking]].
