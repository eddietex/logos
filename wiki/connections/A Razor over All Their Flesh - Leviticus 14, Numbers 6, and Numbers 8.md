# A Razor over All Their Flesh - Leviticus 14, Numbers 6, and Numbers 8

## Passages involved

- [[The Cleansing of the Leper]] — Leviticus 14:8–9; all the hair, twice
- [[The Nazirite Vow]] — Numbers 6:5, 9, 18; no razor, and the head shaved at the end
- [[The Lamps and the Levites Cleansed]] — Numbers 8:7; a razor over the whole body
- Leviticus 21:5 — priests forbidden to shave their heads *(stored WEB)*
- Ezekiel 5:1; Isaiah 7:20 — the razor as an image of judgment *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*

## Analysis

| Text | Who | How much | Purpose stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leviticus 14:8–9 | one healed of skin disease | *all his hair*, then head, beard, and eyebrows | to be clean |
| Numbers 6:5 | Nazirite | no razor on the head | he *shall be holy* |
| Numbers 6:18 | Nazirite | head, at the Tent door, at the end | hair goes on the fire |
| Numbers 8:7 | Levites | *all their flesh* | to be cleansed |

**Of these, only the leper and the Levite shave the whole body.** The Levites are in the company of the person
returning from outside the camp. In both cases the shaving is a step toward being *clean*, is joined with
washing clothes, and comes before offerings. The difference is that the leper has had a disease and the
Levites have not. Numbers 8 does not say what uncleanness the razor deals with. Reading the Levites as
being re-entered into Israel's life, as someone coming back from the outside would, is the wiki's
suggestion, and it fits poorly with their being taken *from among* Israel (8:6).

**Different words.** Leviticus 14 and the shaving in Numbers 6:9, 18 use *gālaḥ*. Numbers 8:7 instead says
*wəheʿĕbîrû taʿar ʿal kol bəśārām*, *they shall pass a razor over all their flesh* (WLC). In the Torah the
tags give *taʿar* only at Numbers 6:5 and 8:7, and both are close together. So the Nazirite and the Levite
are linked by the word, and the Levite and the leper by the act. The same pairing of razor and verb comes
at Ezekiel 5:1, where the prophet's shaved head is a sign of Jerusalem's judgment. Isaiah 7:20 uses the
razor for Assyria. Numbers 8 has no such sense.

**The same tool, opposite signs.** The Nazirite is holy while uncut, and the Levite is made fit by
being cut. Priests may not shave their heads at all (Leviticus 21:5). The hair of the three groups therefore
marks no single meaning. Each rite gives the razor its own part.

## Significance

The razor is one of the things that shows what the Levites' rite is like: it borrows from the return of
the unclean and not from the anointing of the priest. See [[Clean and Unclean]],
[[Cleansed, Not Anointed - The Levites of Numbers 8 and the Priests of Leviticus 8]], and
[[Shaved Heads and Fulfilled Days - Numbers 6 and Acts 18 and 21]].
