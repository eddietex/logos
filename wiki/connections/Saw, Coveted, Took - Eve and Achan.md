# Saw, Coveted, Took - Eve and Achan

## Passages involved

- [[The Fall]] — Genesis 3:6: *"When the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a
  delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one wise, she took some of its
  fruit, and ate"* (stored WEB).
- [[The Sin of Achan]] — Joshua 7:21: *"When I saw among the plunder a beautiful Babylonian robe, two
  hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold weighing fifty shekels, then I coveted them and took
  them."* (stored WEB)
- [[Blessing and the Removal of the Nations]] — Deuteronomy 7:25: *"You shall not covet the silver or
  the gold that is on them, nor take it for yourself, lest you be snared in it"* (stored WEB).
- Exodus 20:17 — *"You shall not covet your neighbor's house …"* (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**Three roots in the same order.** Genesis 3:6 reads *wattēreʾ … neḥmāḏ … wattiqqaḥ* — *saw*,
*desirable*, *took* — from *rāʾâ*, *ḥāmaḏ*, *lāqaḥ* (WLC). Achan's confession reads *wāʾēreʾ …
wāʾeḥmǝḏēm wāʾeqqāḥēm* — *I saw … I coveted them … I took them* — from the same three roots (WLC
Joshua 7:21). Both also call the object *good* (*ṭôḇ* in Genesis 3:6; *ṭôḇâ* of the robe in Joshua
7:21). In Genesis *ḥāmaḏ* is a passive participle describing the tree; in Joshua it is Achan's own
verb, a confession of desire.

**Deuteronomy 7:25 forbids the pair.** *Covet* and *take* stand together in the law about the
nations' gold: the same two verbs Achan uses in sequence. The tenth commandment uses *ḥāmaḏ* too
(Exodus 20:17); that identification is commonly made and is consistent with English *covet* in both,
but the Exodus Hebrew is not re-checked on this page.

**Two firsts.** Each text is the first transgression after entry into a gift: the garden, and the
land. In each, what is taken was forbidden by a specific word, the taking is hidden (Genesis 3:8,
Joshua 7:21), and the consequence reaches beyond the one who took. That structural parallel is the
wiki's reading; Joshua does not cite Genesis.

## Significance

The repetition gives a narrative anatomy of covetous sin — seeing, desiring, taking — that recurs in
the Bible's account of first failures. It ties the Tenth Commandment's interior *covet* to an act,
which is how Deuteronomy 7:25 had framed it. See [[Envy]], [[Sin]], [[Achan]], and [[Eve]].
