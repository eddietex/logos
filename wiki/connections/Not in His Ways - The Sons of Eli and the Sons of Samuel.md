# Not in His Ways - The Sons of Eli and the Sons of Samuel

## Passages involved

- [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] — 1 Samuel 2:12–17, 22–25 — *Now the sons of Eli were wicked men. They didn't
  know Yahweh* (2:12); *Now Eli was very old; and he heard all that his sons did to all Israel* (2:22).
- [[A Man of God Against the House of Eli]] — 1 Samuel 2:27–36 — the house of Eli rejected.
- [[Yahweh Calls Samuel]] — 1 Samuel 3:13 — *his sons brought a curse on themselves, and he didn't restrain
  them*.
- [[Israel Asks for a King]] — 1 Samuel 8:1–5 — *When Samuel was old, he made his sons judges over Israel …
  His sons didn't walk in his ways, but turned away after dishonest gain, took bribes, and perverted
  justice* (8:1, 3).
- 1 Samuel 12:2–5 *(stored WEB; not yet ingested)* — *I am old and gray-headed. Behold, my sons are with you.*

## Analysis

**Two old judges, two pairs of sons.** 1 Samuel introduces both of its judges' sons in the same frame: the
father old, the sons corrupt, the people affected. Eli *was very old*, *zāqēn məʾōḏ* (2:22, WLC), when he heard
what his sons did; *Samuel was old*, *zāqēn* (8:1, WLC), when he made his sons judges, and the elders repeat
*you are old* to him (8:5).

| | **Eli's sons** | **Samuel's sons** |
|---|---|---|
| Names | Hophni and Phinehas (1:3) | Joel and Abijah (8:2) |
| Office | priests at Shiloh | judges at Beersheba |
| What they take | the sacrificial meat, by fork and by force (2:13–16) | bribes, *šōḥaḏ* (8:3) |
| The word *mišpāṭ* | *the custom of the priests*, *mišpaṭ hakkōhănîm* (2:13) | *perverted justice*, *wayyaṭṭû mišpāṭ* (8:3) |
| The father | rebukes them, and does not restrain them (2:23–25; 3:13) | not said to rebuke them |
| The result | the house rejected; both sons die in one day (2:34; 4:11) | the people ask for a king (8:5) |

Hebrew from the WLC; English from the stored WEB.

**The shared word.** In 1 Samuel the noun *mišpāṭ* occurs at 2:13; 8:3, 9, 11; 10:25; 27:11; and 30:25 (WLC
lemma search). Its first two uses are the two sets of sons: the priests' *custom* of taking, and the judges'
*justice* turned aside. Its next two are *the way of the king* (8:9, 11), which Samuel describes as another
custom of taking: *he will take*, *yiqqāḥ*, four times (8:11–16). The wiki notes the sequence; it does not
claim the narrator built the chapter on it.

**What differs.** Eli's sons sin at the altar and against Yahweh's offering (2:17); Samuel's sons sin in the
court, against the law of Deuteronomy 16:19, *You shall not pervert justice … You shall not take a bribe*
(stored WEB; *nāṭâ mišpāṭ* and *lāqaḥ šōḥaḏ* in both, WLC). Eli is judged for not restraining his sons
(3:13); Samuel is not judged at all, and the narrator neither blames nor excuses him. Eli's house is ended by
an oracle; Samuel's sons are simply never mentioned again in 1 Samuel. In his farewell Samuel says only *my
sons are with you* (12:2) and asks the people to witness his own hands clean of what his sons did: *Of whose
hand have I taken a ransom to make me blind my eyes?* (12:3, stored WEB; not yet ingested). *Blind* echoes the
law's reason for refusing bribes, *a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise* (Deuteronomy 16:19); the noun in 12:3
is *kōp̄er*, not *šōḥaḏ* (WLC).

**The succession that fails.** Both times, the hereditary succession of a sanctuary or a judgeship breaks,
and something new comes from outside it. After Eli, Yahweh calls a boy from Ramah who is not of his house;
after Samuel, the people ask for a king. The pattern is reported, not offered as the book's thesis.

## Significance

The comparison puts the request of 8:5 in context. The elders' reason is true, and the narrator has already
shown it to be true once: an old leader's sons abusing an office given to them by birth or by appointment. What
the elders ask for is another hereditary office. See [[Kingship and Empire]], [[Priesthood]], and
[[A Bribe Blinds - Proverbs 17.23, Exodus 23.8, and Deuteronomy 16.19]]. Related people: [[Eli]], [[Hophni]],
[[Phinehas (son of Eli)]], [[Samuel]], [[Joel (son of Samuel)]], [[Abijah (son of Samuel)]].
