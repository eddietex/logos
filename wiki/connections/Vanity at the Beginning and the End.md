# Vanity at the Beginning and the End

## Passages involved

- [[The End of the Matter]] — Ecclesiastes 12:9–14; assessment and charge after the refrain.

- [[Vanity of Vanities]] — Ecclesiastes 1:1–11, especially 1:2.
- [[Remember Your Creator]] — Ecclesiastes 11:7–12:8, especially 12:8.

## Analysis

Ecclesiastes 1:2 gives the Preacher's comprehensive verdict before asking what a person
gains from labor. Ecclesiastes 12:8 returns to that verdict after the dust and spirit
return to their destinations. Both name the Preacher as speaker. The WEB wording is:

> **1:2** “Vanity of vanities,” says the Preacher; “Vanity of vanities, all is vanity.”

> **12:8** “Vanity of vanities,” says the Preacher. “All is vanity!”

The verbal return frames the inquiry without proving how or when the book was composed.
At the opening, passing generations stand beside a continuing world; at the close,
the young addressee is directed to remember the Creator before decline and death.
The final refrain has also been prepared by the vanity of what comes (11:8) and of
youth itself (11:10). Its immediate company is a command to enjoy a passing life,
not a prohibition of all enjoyment.

**A frame does not settle every tension.** The closing verdict does not delete the
counsel to rejoice (11:9), God's judgment (11:9), or the Creator (12:1). Nor does it
provide a formula for reconciling all the book's competing evaluations. Transience
and frustrated gain are contextual readings of vanity here, not a source-language
lexical definition. The following assessment of the Preacher and final charge in
12:9–14 form the distinct unit [[The End of the Matter]], now ingested. The final
charge retains responsibility for good and evil without rescinding the refrain or
explaining every observed injustice.

Sources: both quotations copied from immutable stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:2 and 12:8;
comparisons read in Ecclesiastes 1:1–11; 11:7–12:14. No Hebrew wording or authorship
reconstruction is asserted.

## Significance

[[Vanity]] frames the inquiry while [[Joy and Enjoyment]], [[Judgment]], [[Remembrance]],
[[Creation]], and [[Life and Death]] remain inside it. The ending repeats the opening
judgment after the reader has encountered both life's goods and their limits.
