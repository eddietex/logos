# The Second Month - Numbers 9 and Hezekiah's Passover

## Passages involved

- [[The Passover in the Second Year]] — Numbers 9:10–13; the second-month date
- 2 Chronicles 30:1–20 — Hezekiah's Passover *(WEB checked at bible-api.com, not yet ingested)*
- Ezra 6:19–21 — the Passover after the return *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- [[Hezekiah]]

## Analysis

The only second-month Passover narrated in the Bible is Hezekiah's: *Then they killed the Passover on the
fourteenth day of the second month* (2 Chronicles 30:15, WEB). Its date is the date of Numbers 9:11. The
Chronicler does not cite Numbers 9, so the link is an inference from the date, though a strong one.

The reason differs. Numbers 9 gives the later date to individuals, unclean or far away. Chronicles moves it
for the nation: *they could not keep it at that time, because the priests had not sanctified themselves in
sufficient number, and the people had not gathered themselves together to Jerusalem* (30:3, WEB). The verb
phrase *lōʾ yāḵəlû* with *ʿāśâ*, *they could not do*, stands at both Numbers 9:6 and 2 Chronicles 30:3 (WLC).
The decision is also made differently. Numbers 9 waits for Yahweh's word, and Hezekiah *had taken counsel with
his princes and all the assembly* (30:2, WEB).

Then the Chronicler goes past Numbers 9. Even in the second month many had not cleansed themselves, *yet they
ate the Passover other than the way it is written* (30:18, WEB). The law had given a month so the unclean
could eat clean. These ate unclean. Hezekiah prays, *May the good Yahweh pardon everyone who sets his heart to
seek God ... even if they aren't clean according to the purification of the sanctuary* (30:18–19, WEB), and
*Yahweh listened to Hezekiah, and healed the people* (30:20, WEB). The Levites also take on the killing for
those not clean (30:17). Numbers 9:6–7 had men kept back by uncleanness. Here the uncleaned are not kept back,
and it is called a departure from what is written.

Ezra 6:20–21 gives the other picture: the priests and Levites *had purified themselves together, all of them
were pure*, the Passover is in the first month, and those who ate include people who had separated themselves
to join them.

## Significance

Both texts care about the same two things, that the people should keep the Passover and that they should be
clean. Numbers 9 holds both by moving the date. In Chronicles the date is moved and the second still fails, and
prayer covers the gap. The text does not treat this as a new rule. It does not say what healing means at 30:20.

Whether Numbers 9's provision for individuals was meant to reach a national delay is not stated; the Chronicler
does not defend the extension. See [[Festivals and First Fruits]], [[Clean and Unclean]], and
[[Mediation and Advocacy]].
