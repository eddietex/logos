## Analysis

**Proverbs 18:10 and 18:11 are a pair, and the second is built from the words of the first.** The
righteous man's tower is *migdal-ʿōz*, a *tower of strength*, and in it he is *niśgāḇ*, *set on high*.
The rich man's city is *qiryat ʿuzzô*, *the city of his strength*, and his wall is *niśgāḇâ*, *set on
high* — but *bəmaśkîtô*, *in his imagination*. The two verses use the same strength word and the same
height word (Hebrew from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25), and the only
difference is where the safety is: in a name, or in a mind. The rich man's line is not new.
Proverbs 10:15 had the same first half, *hôn ʿāšîr qiryat ʿuzzô*, as a neutral statement that
wealth protects. Placed after 18:10, the collection adds the judgment that the protection is imagined.

**The strong tower is a real building elsewhere.** In Judges, *"a strong tower within the city"*
(Judges 9:51, *migdal-ʿōz*) was where *"all the men and women of the city fled"* and *"shut themselves
in"*. Proverbs takes the picture of a whole town running into its citadel and makes Yahweh's name the
citadel. Psalm 61:3 does the same in prayer: *"you have been a refuge for me, a strong tower from the
enemy"*, with the same two words (*migdal-ʿōz*, Psalm 61:4 in Hebrew numbering).

**The height belongs to the Psalms too.** *Nišgāḇ* is the root *śgb*, *to be high and so
inaccessible*. Psalm 18:2 ends its list of names for God with *miśgabbî*, *"my high tower"*, and Psalm
20:1 joins the root to the name directly: *yəśaggeḇkā šēm ʾĕlōhê yaʿăqōḇ*, *"May the name of the God
of Jacob set you up on high"* (Psalm 18:3 and 20:2 in Hebrew numbering; Hebrew from Sefaria). Treating
*śgb* as one root across these verses is a standard lexical judgement that this page has not checked
against a lexicon.

**Babel is the tower built for a name.** The builders of Genesis 11:4 want *"a city, and a tower"*
(*ʿîr ûmiḡdāl*) so as to *"make a name for ourselves"* (*naʿăśeh-lānû šēm*), and their purpose is
defensive: *"lest we be scattered"*. Proverbs 18:10–11 has the same three things — a name, a tower,
and a city — arranged the other way round. The righteous do not build anything; the name already is
the tower, and they run into it. The rich man does build a strong city, and it exists only in his
imagination, which is where Babel's ended too. Proverbs does not cite Genesis, and the shared words
are common nouns; the comparison is the wiki's, not a claim of literary dependence. See
[[A Name for Ourselves and a Great Name]].

**The third fortified city is a person.** Proverbs 18:19 uses the phrase again — *qiryat-ʿōz*, *a
fortified city* — for an offended brother, and adds the *bars of a fortress*. In the chapter's terms
the hardest wall to get through is not wealth or stone but a brother who has been wronged.

**Job is the counter-test.** Job said *"Blessed be Yahweh's name"* (Job 1:21) on the day he lost
everything, and he had refused to call gold his confidence (31:24). He was righteous, he ran to the
name, and he was not kept from harm. Proverbs 18:10 promises safety, not exemption, and the wiki reads
it as the Proverbs pages read every retribution saying: a general truth, not a verdict on any one
case.

## Significance

The verse gives Proverbs' only statement about *"Yahweh's name"* (searching the stored WEB), and it is
about refuge, not reputation. Set next to Babel and next to its own neighbour verse, it says that
security is received rather than built, and that the security people build for themselves is
largely imagined. See [[The Names of God]], [[Wealth and Possessions]],
[[Pride and Self-Exaltation]], [[City and Civilization]], and
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].
