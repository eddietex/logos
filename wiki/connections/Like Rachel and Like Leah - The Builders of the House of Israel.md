# Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 29:16–30** — the two sisters, married a week apart, one loved more than the other —
  [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]]
- **Genesis 29:31–30:24; 35:16–18** — the twelve sons and Dinah, out of two wives and two slaves
- **Ruth 4:11–12** — *"All the people who were in the gate, and the elders, said, 'We are witnesses.
  May Yahweh make the woman who has come into your house like Rachel and like Leah, which two built
  the house of Israel; and treat you worthily in Ephrathah, and be famous in Bethlehem. Let your house
  be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah.'"*
- **Genesis 35:19; 48:7** — Rachel buried *"on the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem)"*
- **Jeremiah 31:15; Matthew 2:18** — Rachel weeping at Ramah for children who *"are no more"*
- **Ruth 4:17–22; Matthew 1:3–6** — the line from Perez to David, and past him

## Analysis

**The one blessing in the Bible that invokes these two women puts them side by side, gives them a
verb, and names the younger first.**

The elders at the gate of Bethlehem bless Ruth as a bride, and the formula they reach for is Genesis
29–30: ***"like Rachel and like Leah, which two built the house of Israel."*** **Three things about
that sentence are worth stating exactly, because each of them cuts against something in the Genesis
chapter it quotes.**

**First, the verb is *built*.** ***Bānû*** — the same root as ***bānîm***, sons, and the verb Sarai
uses at 16:2 (*"I may obtain children by her"*, literally *be built up from her*). **Genesis 29–30
never calls what the sisters are doing building.** It calls it rivalry, and it reports it as a bidding
war. **Ruth 4:11 renames the whole business** and hands the result to both of them jointly.

**Second, Rachel is named first**, though [[Leah]] is the elder, bore six of the twelve, and — this is
the point — **is the ancestor of the people saying the blessing.** Bethlehem is in Judah. Boaz is
Judah's descendant; so is the child they are blessing him toward; so is David. **The elders of
Leah's own town name Rachel ahead of her**, and neither Ruth nor any commentary in the Bible explains
why. The standing readings are that Rachel was the loved wife and the order is honorific, or that the
order is simply the order Jacob met them in. **The wiki records the fact and neither reading is
adopted.**

**Third, the very next verse corrects the correction.** *"Let your house be like the house of Perez,
whom Tamar bore to Judah"* (4:12) — **and Perez is Leah's grandson, by Judah, out of the incident at
Genesis 38.** So the blessing invokes Rachel first for the household and then, one sentence later,
routes the actual line through Leah's son and a Canaanite widow who had to trick her father-in-law to
get it. **Ruth 4:11–12 is the Bible's own summary of what Genesis 29 produced**, and it declines to
tidy any of it.

### The two graves

**The chapter that marries them a week apart ends up burying them four days' journey apart**, and the
Bible keeps the distinction:

| | **Leah** | **Rachel** |
|---|---|---|
| **buried** | Machpelah, with Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebekah (49:31) | on the road to Ephrath, under a pillar (35:19–20) |
| **named in the burial** | by Jacob, from his deathbed | by Jacob, who *"set up a pillar on her grave"* |
| **her tribes** | Reuben, Simeon, **Levi**, **Judah**, Issachar, Zebulun | **Joseph**, Benjamin |
| **what comes of it** | the priesthood and the kingship | the double portion, and two tribes out of one son |
| **her afterlife in Scripture** | one clause at 49:31, and Ruth 4:11 | Ruth 4:11, and Jeremiah 31:15 |

**Rachel gets the road and the prophet.** Jeremiah 31:15 puts her weeping at Ramah *"for her
children, because they are no more"* — the deportation route north — and God's answer to her is the
promise of return two verses later. **Matthew 2:18 moves the quotation to Bethlehem**, which is where
Genesis said her grave was, and applies it to the killing of the infants. **The matriarch who died on
the road becomes the Bible's standing image of a mother mourning a nation's children**, and the two
texts that use her that way are the two places the road matters.

**Leah gets the tomb and the line.** She lies in the cave Abraham bought, the only patriarchal
property in Canaan, and the tribes that carry Israel's institutions are hers. **Genesis gives her one
sentence about it and Jacob is the one who says it**: *"there I buried Leah"* (49:31).

## Significance

- **It is the Bible's own verdict on Genesis 29, and it is a blessing.** A chapter in which one woman
  is substituted for another, both are given away by a father who names neither in the agreement, and
  the narrator ends by ranking them, is remembered four books later as **two women who built a
  house.** The wiki notes the distance between the two descriptions without collapsing it.
- **It puts the messianic line through the un-preferred wife.** Ruth 4 runs Perez → Boaz → Obed →
  Jesse → David, and Matthew 1 runs it on to Jesus. **Every name in that list descends from Leah**,
  the sister Jacob did not choose and the one God is first said to see. See
  [[Loved and Hated - Genesis 29 and Deuteronomy 21.15-17]] and [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].
- **Ruth is the book that goes back to Genesis for its vocabulary**, and this is its densest
  instance: the betrothal type-scene redistributed at a threshing floor, the levirate obligation of
  Genesis 38 discharged properly rather than by a trick, and here the two matriarchs of Genesis 29
  invoked by name in the town where the story is going. See
  [[The Betrothal at the Well]].
