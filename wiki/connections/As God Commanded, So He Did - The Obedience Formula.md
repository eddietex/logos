## Analysis

**The formula is *kəkōl ʾăšer ṣiwwâ … kēn ʿāśâ*, *"according to all that he commanded … so he
did"*.** It has two halves, and the second repeats the first: *did* at the start, *so he did* at the
end. **It is the narrator's way of saying that nothing was added and nothing left out.**

**Genesis uses the full form once, of Noah** (6:22, *kēn ʿāśâ*). Genesis 7:5 repeats the thought
with *Yahweh* and without the closing *kēn ʿāśâ*. **The obedience is the building of the ark** —
a structure built to a divine specification, with measurements.

**Exodus uses it first of Moses and Aaron, at 7:6**, after five objections at the bush, a complaint
at 5:22–23, and *"uncircumcised lips"* twice. **Its first use of Moses comes the moment he stops
objecting.** Then it is used of the whole people at the first Passover (12:28, 50), and finally of
the tabernacle (39:32, 42–43; 40:16), **where it is repeated until it becomes the structure of the
closing chapters**. (WLC search of Genesis and Exodus for *ṣiwwâ* with *kēn ʿāśâ*; Genesis 42:25
and 50:12 use the same verbs for obedience to Joseph and to Jacob, not to God.)

**The tabernacle instructions open with the formula's own words, as a command.** *Kəkōl ʾăšer ʾănî marʾeh
ʾôtəkā … wəkēn taʿăśû*, *"according to all that I show you … even so you shall make it"* (25:9; MAM).
The *kəkōl … kēn* frame of 39:32 and 40:16 is already there; what changes at the end is the tense.
**The book sets the standard in the instruction and reports it met in the completion.**

**The pattern is ark → plagues → Passover → tabernacle.** The formula marks the moments when a
human being does exactly what God specified — **and two of the four are buildings to a pattern**:
the ark and the tabernacle.

**The enclosure provides a detailed command-and-execution comparison.** Exodus 26:1–37 specifies
curtains, coverings, boards, bars, veil, and screen; 36:8–38 reports their making in that order.
The repeated dimensions and materials make the correspondence visible. This is not a verbatim
repetition: 36 does not restate the folding and overhang directions of 26:9, 12–13. The erection
and placement commanded in 26:30, 33–35 are reported in 40:18–28. Both accounts were read in
local WEB raw text; 36:8–38 is now ingested in [[The Tabernacle Built]], and 40:18–28 is now in
[[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]]. This extends the obedience motif without
claiming the full formula occurs in chapter 26.

**Altar and court also move from instruction to making and placement.** Exodus 27:1–19
prescribes them; 38:1–7, 9–20 reports their making; 40:6–8 commands their positions and
40:24–33 describes the light, offerings, and completed enclosure. Chapter 38 also adds the
basin (38:8) between the altar and court accounts, so it is not a verbatim duplicate of
chapter 27. Chapter 27's lamp rule (27:20–21) concerns continued service, not merely a
construction completed once. See [[The Altar and the Court]]. Chapter 38 is now ingested in
[[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]]; chapter 40 is now ingested in [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]].
Both were compared from the local WEB text.

### The offering now brought — Exodus 35:1–29

[[The Freewill Offering]] makes command and response visible before the completion formulas:
Moses relays what Yahweh commands, calls for everything commanded to be made, and the willing
bring gifts for the work commanded through him (35:1, 4, 10, 29). Actual spinning is reported
in 35:25–26; completion of all components is not. This comparison concerns the WEB narrative
pattern, not an assertion that 35:29 contains the full Hebrew formula discussed above.

### The command governs the work's supply — Exodus 36:1–7

In [[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]], the workers are to make everything according to
Yahweh's command (36:1), and their report of surplus measures the contributions against the
work Yahweh commanded (36:5). Moses stops further gifts (36:6). This extends the narrative
movement from instruction to response while leaving the completion reports of chapters 39–40
ahead. It does not assert that this unit contains the full Hebrew formula analyzed above.

Source: Exodus 36:1–7 checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The enclosure made — Exodus 36:8–38

The detailed making is now ingested in [[The Tabernacle Built]]. Matching counts, dimensions,
materials, and joins demonstrate the correspondence with Exodus 26 without requiring an
identical report. The entrance pillars retain a difference of description: 26:37 commands
gold overlay on the pillars, whereas 36:38 names their capitals and fillets. The report does
not expressly exclude further overlay, but neither repeats it in full. This is a limit on an
exact reconstruction, not evidence sufficient to accuse the makers of disobedience. The unit
does not itself supply the completed-work declaration of 39:32, 42–43.

See [[The Tabernacle Built]]. Source: Exodus 26 and 36:8–38 checked in local raw WEB,
2026-10-24; later Exodus references checked in the same source.

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The ark, mercy seat, table, and lampstand of 37:1–24 closely follow 25:10–39; the incense
altar in 37:25–28 follows 30:1–5. Verse 37:29 reports the oil and incense in one sentence
rather than repeating the recipes. Detailed manufacture supplies evidence of correspondence,
without a completed-work declaration in this chapter. Directions about use are not revoked
when omitted: the testimony, bread, and lamp lighting are narrated in 40:20–25.

The pole descriptions retain a question of sequence. Exodus 37:5 reports inserting them,
25:15 forbids removal, and 40:20 describes setting them on the ark. The last passage does
not explicitly narrate a removal. A recap or adjustment is possible, but a precise sequence
cannot be established from these WEB statements alone. This comparison extends the narrative
motif without asserting the presence of a particular Hebrew formula in chapter 37.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38

Exodus 38:22 credits Bezalel with making all that Yahweh commanded Moses. This extends the obedience
motif through an explicit WEB statement, without claiming here that its Hebrew wording is identical
to the formula analyzed above. The altar and court’s correspondence to chapter 27 supplies detail,
while the basin’s mirror source in 38:8 supplies new information. The summary credit does not turn
the inventory into the final erection or the blessing of the finished work in chapters 39–40.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]]. Source: the cited Exodus passages
checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The three notices in 39:32, 42, and 43 now belong to an ingested passage. They move
from the narrator’s completion statement to a repeated collective attribution and then to Moses’
inspection. The inventory between them gives concrete content to the claim: enclosure, furniture,
supplies, court, and priestly garments. Israel’s collective credit does not deny the named makers
of 38:22–23. This extension compares the WEB narrative without making a new claim about exact
Hebrew formulae. Chapter 40:16 is now ingested in [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]].

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40

Exodus 40:16 gives the general obedience statement. The detailed erection report
contains seven instances of the stored WEB wording “as Yahweh commanded Moses”
(40:19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 29, 32). The clauses accompany covering, screening the ark,
bread, lamps, incense, offerings, and washing. Verse 33 then reports finished work.
This count concerns the verified English wording and does not independently verify
the Hebrew formula analyzed earlier. The summary in 40:16 is broader than the selected
acts retold in 40:17–33; omitted ritual details should not become a denial of obedience.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in stored WEB,
2026-10-25.

### The consecration now ingested — Leviticus 8

Leviticus 8:4 affirms Moses’ response; 8:9, 13, 17, 21, and 29 repeat the WEB wording
“as Yahweh commanded Moses.” Verse 36 ends with Aaron and his sons doing what Yahweh
commanded through Moses. The repeated notices connect the preparation of people with
the earlier making and raising of the sanctuary. This addition compares verified English
wording, without asserting identity with the exact Hebrew formula discussed above.

Obedience notices do not make the accounts verbatim duplicates: Exodus 29:21 and
Leviticus 8:30 report the oil-and-blood sprinkling at different points in the sequence.
See [[Ordination Commanded and Performed - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 8]].

Source for this addition: stored WEB Exodus 28–29 and Leviticus 6–8, checked 2026-10-29.

### The inaugural service — Leviticus 9

Moses announces that the commanded action precedes the appearing glory (9:6).
The account then marks Aaron's service by command and ordinance (7, 10, 16, 21).
The narrative reaches a visible response in verses 23–24. This is a particular promised
manifestation, not a rule that every act of obedience receives visible fire.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Leviticus 4:1–12; 7:28–38; 8:33–36; 9:1–24
and Exodus 40:34–38, checked 2026-10-29.

### The uncommanded act and restored obedience — Leviticus 10

The WEB specifies that Yahweh had not commanded the brothers’ offering (10:1).
That negative statement interrupts the commanded service of chapters 8–9.
The removal of the bodies follows Moses’ speech (5), and the survivors do according to
his word (7). Teaching what Yahweh spoke through Moses becomes a continuing duty (11).
These English observations do not claim an independently verified Hebrew formula.

See [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 9–10.

### The census — Numbers 1:19, 54

Numbers opens with the formula twice: *As Yahweh commanded Moses, so he counted them* (19) and
*According to all that Yahweh commanded Moses, so they did* (54). See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]].

## Significance

**The formula connects Noah and Moses as builders of the things that carry God's people through
judgment and keep God among them.** The text draws the verbal link; the wiki notes the shared
setting — a structure built to God's dimensions — as an observation, not a claim the text makes.
See [[The Flood and Baptism]] for the ark's other afterlife.
