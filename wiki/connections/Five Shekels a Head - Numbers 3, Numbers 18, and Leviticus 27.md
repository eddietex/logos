# Five Shekels a Head - Numbers 3, Numbers 18, and Leviticus 27

## Passages involved

- [[The Levites and the Firstborn Counted]] — Numbers 3:46–51; five shekels for each of the 273
- [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]] — Leviticus 27:3, 6, 25; a male of a month to five years
  valued at five shekels
- Numbers 18:15–16 — the firstborn redeemed from a month old at five shekels *(not yet ingested;
  stored WEB)*
- [[The Half Shekel - Census, Ransom, and the Temple Tax]] — Exodus 30:13; another price by the same
  shekel
- Ezekiel 45:12 — *The shekel shall be twenty gerahs* (WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet
  ingested)

## Analysis

**One price, one exchange, one rule.** Numbers 3:47 uses five shekels once, for the men left over.
Numbers 18:16 makes it the fixed price of redeeming a firstborn, *from a month old*. So a figure that
first settles a shortfall at Sinai returns as a standing law. Numbers does not connect the two.

**The same sum in the vow table.** Leviticus 27:6 values a male from a month to five years at five
shekels, and a man from twenty to sixty at fifty (27:3). The firstborn price matches the youngest
bracket, which would fit a child redeemed at a month old. But Numbers 3 charges it for every one of the
273, with no regard to age. That is the wiki's comparison, and no text draws it.

**The shekel of the sanctuary.** The gloss *twenty gerahs* stands at Exodus 30:13, Leviticus 27:25,
Numbers 3:47, 18:16, and Ezekiel 45:12, and nowhere else (WLC, by Strong's tag on *gērâ*). Each is a
sacred payment or a measure for one. The gloss fixes the unit. It does not tell us a modern weight, and
the wiki gives none.

**To whom it goes.** The half-shekel of Exodus 30:16 goes to the service of the Tent. The five shekels
go to Aaron and his sons (3:48, 51). Numbers 18 sets the redemption money among the priests' dues.

**Half a shekel and five.** Every counted man paid half a shekel, and every unmatched firstborn paid
ten times that. Both payments are *laggulgōlet*, by the skull (Exodus 38:26; Numbers 3:47, WLC). The
half-shekel is called a ransom for the soul, *kōper* (Exodus 30:12). The five shekels are *pidyôm*, from
*pādâ*. The two words overlap in sense and stand together nowhere in these passages.

## Significance

- Silver fills the place where a person cannot be matched, which is the plain meaning of *redemption
