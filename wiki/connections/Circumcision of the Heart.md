# Circumcision of the Heart

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 17:10–14** — the rite itself: *"You shall be circumcised in the flesh of your foreskin.
  It will be a token of the covenant between me and you"*, with a sanction for the man who is not
  cut. See [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]].
- **Leviticus 26:41** — the metaphor's earliest form, in the covenant curses: *"their uncircumcised
  heart is humbled"*.
- **Deuteronomy 10:16** — *"Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more
  stiff-necked."* A command.
- **Deuteronomy 30:6** — *"Yahweh your God will circumcise your heart, and the heart of your
  offspring, to love Yahweh your God with all your heart."* The same thing, as a promise, with God as
  the surgeon.
- **Jeremiah 4:4** — *"Circumcise yourselves to Yahweh, and take away the foreskins of your heart"*,
  with fire as the alternative.
- **Jeremiah 6:10; 9:25–26** — uncircumcised *ears*; and a list that groups Judah with Egypt, Edom,
  Ammon, Moab, and the desert peoples as *circumcised in the flesh* while *"all the house of Israel
  are uncircumcised in heart."*
- **Ezekiel 44:7, 9** — foreigners *"uncircumcised in heart and uncircumcised in flesh"* barred from
  the sanctuary: the two kinds named side by side as separate requirements.
- **Acts 7:51** — Stephen, at the end of his defence: *"You stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart
  and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit!"* — Deuteronomy 10:16 and Jeremiah 6:10 in one clause.
- **Romans 2:25–29** — *"he is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is that of the heart, in
  the spirit, not in the letter."*
- **Colossians 2:11–12** — *"a circumcision not made with hands"*, set beside burial and raising in
  baptism.

## Analysis

**The metaphor is not a New Testament invention and it is not a rejection of the rite.** It begins in
the Torah, in the same books that command circumcision in the flesh, and it works by taking the
physical sign seriously enough to ask what it was for.

The logic runs: a foreskin is something that covers and dulls; to circumcise is to remove what gets
in the way. So an *uncircumcised heart* is a heart with something in the way — the Hebrew idiom does
the same job with lips (Exodus 6:12, *"uncircumcised lips"*, of Moses' speech) and with ears
(Jeremiah 6:10) and with fruit trees (Leviticus 19:23, whose first three years' fruit is
*uncircumcised*, meaning off-limits). Applied to the heart it means obstructed, unreceptive, closed.

### The move that Deuteronomy makes twice

Deuteronomy 10:16 and 30:6 are the same sentence with the subject changed, and twenty chapters apart:

| | Deuteronomy 10:16 | Deuteronomy 30:6 |
|---|---|---|
| Who acts | *you* — *circumcise the foreskin of your heart* | *Yahweh your God will circumcise your heart* |
| Mood | imperative | promise |
| Scope | the generation addressed | *"and the heart of your offspring"* |
| Result | *"be no more stiff-necked"* | *"to love Yahweh your God with all your heart"* |

That shift — a command that the same book later says God will perform himself — is the Old
Testament's own admission that the first version did not work, and it is the seed of Jeremiah's new
covenant *"written on their hearts"* (31:33) and Ezekiel's *"heart of flesh"* (36:26).

### Jeremiah's list, which is the sharpest thing in the thread

Jeremiah 9:25–26 says God will punish *"all those who are circumcised in their uncircumcision"* —
Egypt, Judah, Edom, Ammon, Moab, and the desert-dwellers — *"for all the nations are uncircumcised,
and all the house of Israel are uncircumcised in heart."*

Two facts are stacked in it. **The first is historical**: Judah's neighbours practised circumcision
too, so the mark did not in fact distinguish Israel from the region — which the archaeology confirms
and which the Philistines' famous exception makes visible from the other side. **The second is
theological**: a mark that everyone has cannot be the thing that matters, and Jeremiah puts Judah on
the list rather than above it.

### What Paul does and does not do with it

Romans 2:25–29 is standardly read as replacing the rite with an inward disposition, and it is more
precise than that. Paul's claim is that circumcision *"profits if you are a doer of the law"* and
becomes *"uncircumcision"* if you are not — which is Jeremiah's argument, made to a Jewish
interlocutor in Jeremiah's terms, and neither the abolition of the sign nor the invention of the
metaphor. The abolition question is settled elsewhere and on different grounds: at Acts 15 by a
council, and in Galatians by an argument about what adding the rite to faith would mean for Gentiles.

Colossians 2:11–12 then makes the one move the Old Testament does not: it attaches the
*"circumcision not made with hands"* to a person and an event — *"the circumcision of Christ"* — and
sets it beside baptism. Whether the verse licenses reading baptism as the covenant sign's successor
is the hinge of a four-hundred-year argument between paedobaptist and credobaptist traditions, and
the wiki records the verse and the dispute without settling either.

## Significance

- **The Old Testament criticizes its own central rite, from inside the Torah.** The command to
  circumcise the heart stands in the same canon, and often the same book, as the command to
  circumcise the flesh. Any account of the Bible in which the inward reading arrives with
  Christianity has to explain Deuteronomy 10:16.
- **It names the failure mode of every sign.** Genesis 17 gives a mark that can be perfectly kept by
  a man who has done nothing else; the thread from Leviticus 26 to Acts 7 is Scripture noticing.
- **And it turns a command into a promise.** Deuteronomy 30:6 is where the Old Testament first says
  that the thing required of Israel will have to be done to Israel — the sentence Jeremiah 31 and
  Ezekiel 36 are elaborations of, and the reason the new covenant is described as an operation rather
  than an agreement.
