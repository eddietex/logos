# The Unbetrothed Virgin - Exodus 22.16-17 and Deuteronomy 22.28-29

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 22:16–17** — [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]]: *"If a man entices a virgin who isn't pledged
  to be married, and lies with her, he shall surely pay a dowry for her to be his wife. If her father
  utterly refuses to give her to him, he shall pay money according to the dowry of virgins."*
- **Deuteronomy 22:28–29** — [[Marriage Accusations and Sexual Offenses]]: *"If a man finds a lady who is a
  virgin, who is not pledged to be married, grabs her, and lies with her, and they are found; then the man
  who lay with her shall give to the lady's father fifty shekels of silver. She shall be his wife, because
  he has humbled her. He may not put her away all his days."*
- **Genesis 34:12** — [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]]: Shechem's offer, *"Ask me a great amount for a
  dowry, and I will give whatever you ask of me, but give me the young lady as a wife."*
- **Deuteronomy 22:19** — the slanderous husband's fine, *"one hundred shekels of silver"*, and the same
  *"He may not put her away all his days."*

All quotations from stored WEB.

## Analysis

**The same case, told twice.** Both laws concern a virgin *"not pledged to be married"* — the same
expression, *lōʾ ʾōrāśâ*, in both (Exodus 22:15 WLC; Deuteronomy 22:28) — and a man who *"lies with her"*
(*wǝšākab ʿimmāh*, both, WLC). Both end in marriage and a payment. In the Torah the verb *ʾāraś*, *to
pledge in marriage*, occurs only in these two laws, the pledged woman of Deuteronomy 22:23–27, the
betrothed soldier of 20:7, and the curse of 28:30 (WLC lemma 781).

**What differs.**

| | **Exodus 22:16–17** | **Deuteronomy 22:28–29** |
|---|---|---|
| the act | *"entices"* — *yǝpatteh*, from *pātâ* | *"grabs"* — *ûtǝpāśāh*, from *tāpaś* |
| discovery | not mentioned | *"and they are found"* |
| payment | *mōhar*, *"a dowry"*, or *"money according to the dowry of virgins"* | *"fifty shekels of silver"*, to the father |
| the marriage | the man must pay *"for her to be his wife"* | *"She shall be his wife"* |
| the father | may *"utterly refuse"* | not given a refusal |
| divorce | not mentioned | *"He may not put her away all his days"* |
| the reason | none given | *"because he has humbled her"* (*ʿinnāh*) |

Hebrew from WLC (Exodus 22:15–16, Deuteronomy 22:28–29). *Pātâ* in the Torah is used elsewhere only of
the heart that may be *"deceived"* at Deuteronomy 11:16 (WLC lemma 6601; Genesis 9:27's *yapt* is listed
under the same lemma and is a wordplay on Japheth's name). *Mōhar* occurs in the Torah only at Exodus 22:16
(WLC; WEB 22:17) and Genesis 34:12 (WLC lemma 4119).

**Three ways to read the pair, none adopted.**

1. **Two different cases.** Exodus is seduction, *entice*; Deuteronomy is force, *grab*, and the stricter
   terms — no refusal, no divorce — answer the greater wrong. On this reading Deuteronomy supplements
   Exodus and does not replace it.
2. **One case, revised.** Deuteronomy restates the Exodus law, as it restated the law of the enemy's ox
   with *"your brother"* (Exodus 23:4–5; Deuteronomy 22:1–4), fixing the payment and removing the power
   of divorce. On this reading the father's refusal is either assumed or deliberately dropped.
3. **Different interests protected.** Exodus protects the father's right to give his daughter; Deuteronomy
   protects her against being taken and then discarded. The two are not in conflict, but they are not
   framed around the same person.

The texts do not decide between these. **The wiki flags one point as a real difference rather than an
omission:** Exodus lets the father refuse the marriage; Deuteronomy, read on its own, does not. Whether
Deuteronomy assumes the Exodus clause is not stated.

**The narrative cases.** Shechem, who *"took her, lay with her, and humbled her"* (Genesis 34:2), offers
exactly what these laws require: a *mōhar* of any size and marriage (34:12). Jacob's sons answer with
deceit and a massacre. Amnon, in 2 Samuel 13, refuses even that and sends Tamar away; that chapter is
recorded on [[Dinah and Tamar - Two Rapes and Two Avenging Brothers]] and was not rechecked here. Neither
narrative cites either law, and the wiki does not read them as cases under it. See also
[[Took Her and Humbled Her - Genesis 34 and Deuteronomy 21.10-14]].

**Fifty and a hundred.** Deuteronomy's fifty shekels is half the fine of the man who falsely accuses his
bride (22:19), and both men are told *"He may not put her away all his days"*. The law treats the man who
seizes an unpledged virgin and the man who slanders his wife alike in one respect: neither may later send
her out (see the *šillaḥ* note on [[Marriage Accusations and Sexual Offenses]]). Whether fifty shekels was
the ordinary bride-price is often inferred; neither law says so.

## Significance

The pair shows Deuteronomy's handling of an earlier law at its sharpest: the subject and the remedy are
the same, and the verb, the payment, and the father's role all change. It also shows a law whose
protection and whose cost fall on the same woman — she cannot be discarded, and she is married to the man
who took her. The wiki records both. See [[Marriage]], [[Sexual Sin]], and [[Law and Obedience]].
