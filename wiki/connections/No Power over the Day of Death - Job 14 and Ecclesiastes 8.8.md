# No Power over the Day of Death - Job 14 and Ecclesiastes 8.8

## Passages involved

- [[Though He Slay Me]] — Job 14:1–14, especially 5, 10, and 14.
- [[The King and the Limits of Power]] — Ecclesiastes 8:8, within 8:1–9.
- [[The Wise and the Fool Share One End]] — Ecclesiastes 2:13–16.

## Analysis

Job addresses God about a life whose bounds the person cannot pass (14:5). He asks for
relief during its short span (6), contrasts a tree's renewal with human death (7–12), and
wishes to be hidden until God's wrath passes (13). His question about living again leads
to an image of waiting: in the stored WEB of 14:14, “All the days of my warfare would I wait,
until my release should come.” This is Job's speech of longing, not a narrated resurrection.

Ecclesiastes 8:8 states, “neither does he have power over the day of death.” It places this
beside inability to contain the spirit and the absence of discharge in war. Unlike Job 14,
it does not address God with a request for hiding or release. Read after the king's apparent
freedom in 8:3–4, the statement about no man having such power exposes the boundary around
human authority, including royal authority. This contextual application is a reading of
the sequence; verse 8 is phrased generally rather than as a king's death notice.

The military language makes the contrast suggestive: Job imagines waiting for release,
while Ecclesiastes speaks of no discharge in war. The latter may illustrate death's
inescapability, but it does not explicitly identify the war with death. This is a comparison
of verified English wording and themes, not a claim of a shared Hebrew term or literary
borrowing. The two clauses should not be merged into a single assertion about an afterlife.

**Tension retained.** Ecclesiastes 8:5 commends the safety of keeping the commandment; 8:8
denies mastery of death. As in 2:13–16, where wisdom excels folly but both die, a practical
advantage does not establish exemption from mortality. Job's plea likewise does not deny
the bounds he has just named.

## Significance

[[Life and Death]] limits [[Kingship and Empire]] without making political power harmless.
[[Wisdom]] can guide conduct within those limits. [[War]] supplies language of service and
release, whose different uses here must remain visible. For the related image of brevity,
see [[Life Like a Shadow - Job 14.1-2 and Ecclesiastes 6.12]].
