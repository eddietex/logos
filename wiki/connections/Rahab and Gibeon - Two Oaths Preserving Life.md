# Rahab and Gibeon - Two Oaths Preserving Life

## Passages involved
- [[Rahab and the Spies]]
- [[The Fall of Jericho]]
- [[The Gibeonite Deception]]
- Source comparisons: stored WEB Joshua 2, 6, 9; Deuteronomy 7:1–2; 20:10–18.

## Analysis

Rahab and the Gibeonite envoys both report knowledge of Yahweh’s victories over Sihon and Og (Joshua
2:10; 9:9–10), fear destruction, and seek a sworn guarantee of survival. Joshua 6:22–25 fulfills the
spies’ pledge to Rahab; Joshua 9:18–27 preserves the Gibeonites because of the princes’ oath.

The differences matter. Rahab deceives Jericho’s agents to protect Israel’s spies (2:4–6), while
Gibeon deceives Israel about its own origin (9:6–13). Rahab’s household is brought out and she lives
in Israel (6:25); the Gibeonites receive a curse of enduring labor (9:23). Neither passage licenses
a general claim that deception itself wins divine approval.

## Significance

The conquest contains particular stories of survival. Shared fear and oaths link these cases, while
the direction of deception and resulting status keep them distinct.
