# Their Feet Run to Evil - Proverbs 1.16, Isaiah 59.7, and Romans 3.15

## Passages involved

- [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] — Proverbs 1:16: *"for their feet run to evil. They hurry to shed
  blood."* A father's description of a gang.
- Isaiah 59:7–8 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Their feet run to evil, and they hurry to shed innocent blood.
  Their thoughts are thoughts of iniquity. desolation and destruction are in their paths. They don't
  know the way of peace"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com). A prophet's description of his people.
- Romans 3:15–17 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Their feet are swift to shed blood. Destruction and misery
  are in their ways. The way of peace, they haven't known"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com).
  Paul's description of everyone, in the catena of 3:10–18.

## Analysis

**Proverbs and Isaiah share a line almost word for word.** In the Masoretic text (Sefaria, *Miqra
according to the Masorah*) Proverbs 1:16 reads *raglêhem lāraʿ yārûṣû wîmahărû lišpok-dām* and
Isaiah 59:7 reads *raglêhem lāraʿ yārūṣû wîmahărû lišpōk dām nāqî* — the same six words, differing
only in spelling (Proverbs writes *yārûṣû* with a *waw*, Isaiah without) and in Proverbs' hyphen
(*maqqef*) joining the last two, with Isaiah adding *nāqî*, *innocent*. Proverbs has
the *innocent* too, but four verses earlier: the gang lies in wait *lənāqî ḥinnām*, *for the innocent
without cause* (1:11). Which text is earlier, or whether both draw on a common saying, is not
recoverable from the texts, and the wiki makes no claim about direction.

**Romans 3:15–17 follows Isaiah, not Proverbs.** The decisive evidence is in the English alone:
Paul's next two lines — *"Destruction and misery are in their ways. The way of peace, they haven't
known"* — correspond to Isaiah 59:7b–8 (*"desolation and destruction are in their paths. They don't
know the way of peace"*), and Proverbs 1 has nothing matching them. Paul's quotation is shortened
(*"swift to shed blood"* for the two clauses of the Hebrew), and the claims here are about the WEB
English; the Greek of Romans 3 and of the Septuagint were not checked in this pass. It is often
reported that Proverbs 1:16 is absent from the major Septuagint codices Vaticanus and Sinaiticus;
that was not verified here, and the argument above does not depend on it.

**The same sentence describes three widening circles.** In Proverbs it is a gang — a particular set
of young men, and the son is told not to walk with them. In Isaiah it is the nation: Isaiah 59
begins by telling Israel that its iniquities have separated it from its God, and the *"feet"* are
the people's own. In Romans it is humanity: the catena is introduced by *"There is no one
righteous; no, not one"* (3:10) and closes on *"There is no fear of God before their eyes"* (3:18).
**What Proverbs warns the son against joining, Isaiah says his people have joined, and Paul says
everyone has.**

## Significance

The movement matters for how Proverbs 1 is read. The father's lecture assumes a clean line between
the son and the sinners: they run to evil, he keeps his foot from their path. The canon keeps the
sentence and moves the line. By Romans 3 the *"feet … swift to shed blood"* belong to the whole
human race, the son included — and the catena ends on the absence of *"the fear of God"*, which is
exactly what [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]] put at the beginning of knowledge (1:7). The wisdom
book's road-map is not cancelled by this; it is relocated, from a warning about bad company to a
diagnosis of a common condition. See [[Sin]], [[Blood]], [[Walking with God]], and
[[What Is Man That He Should Be Clean]], which follows Romans 3:10–23 back through Job.
