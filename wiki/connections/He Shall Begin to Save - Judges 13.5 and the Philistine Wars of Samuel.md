# He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel

## Passages involved

- [[Shamgar and the Ox Goad]] — Judges 3:31; *who struck six hundred men of the Philistines with an ox
  goad. He also saved Israel.*
- [[The Announcement of Samson's Birth]] — Judges 13:5; *He shall begin to save Israel out of the hand
  of the Philistines.* And 13:25, *Yahweh's Spirit began to move him.*
- [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]] — Judges 15:18, 20; *this great deliverance*, and *He judged Israel
  twenty years in the days of the Philistines*. Judges 16:31 repeats the twenty years (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested).
- 1 Samuel 7:13 — *So the Philistines were subdued, and they stopped coming within the border of
  Israel. Yahweh's hand was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel.* (WEB at bible-api.com; not
  stored)
- 1 Samuel 9:16 — of Saul: *He will save my people out of the hand of the Philistines* (same)
- 2 Samuel 8:1 — *After this, David struck the Philistines and subdued them* (same)
- [[Samson's Riddle]] — Judges 14:4, 19; *he sought an occasion against the Philistines*, and thirty struck at Ashkelon.

## Analysis

**The promise is of a start.** *Wəhûʾ yāḥēl ləhôšîaʿ ʾet-yiśrāʾēl miyyad pəlištîm* (Judges 13:5, WLC):
*he shall begin to save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines*. Every earlier deliverance in Judges
ended with the enemy *subdued*, *kānaʿ* (WLC lemma 3665): Moab (3:30), Jabin (4:23), Midian (8:28), and
Ammon (11:33). In the WLC of Judges the verb is never used of the Philistines. Samson's notice will say
only that he *judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines* (15:20), that is, while they
still ruled.

**The book marks the beginning twice.** The verb *ḥālal*, *begin*, appears in the promise (13:5) and
again in the last verse of the chapter, *Yahweh's Spirit began to move him* (13:25, *wattāḥel*). It
returns at Samson's end, when Delilah *began to afflict him* (16:19, [[Samson and Delilah]]) and his hair *began to grow again*
(16:22), all WLC. The chapter ends on a beginning, and his story ends on another.

**Samuel's books finish the sentence.** The exact phrase of 13:5, *save … out of the hand of the
Philistines*, *wəhôšîaʿ … miyyad pəlištîm*, is Yahweh's word to Samuel about Saul (1 Samuel 9:16, WLC),
with *my people* for *Israel*. The verb Judges withheld from the Philistines arrives in Samuel: *the
Philistines were subdued*, *wayyikkānəʿû* (1 Samuel 7:13, WLC), *all the days of Samuel*; and *David
struck the Philistines and subdued them*, *wayyaknîʿēm* (2 Samuel 8:1, WLC). The two verses overlap
rather than succeed each other, since Philistine wars continue under Saul between them. How the 1
Samuel 7:13 summary relates to those later wars is a question for the ingest of 1 Samuel.

**Shamgar stands before both.** *He also saved Israel* (3:31; *wayyōšaʿ gam-hûʾ*, WLC) is said of the
first man in Judges to fight the Philistines, in one verse with no cycle around it. So the book begins
and ends its Philistine material with a single man's deed. See
[[One Man Against the Philistines - Shamgar's Ox Goad and Samson's Jawbone]].

### What the thread does not claim

It does not claim that the narrator of Judges wrote *begin* with Samuel and David in view. The verbal
links are in the Hebrew, but the books of Samuel have not yet been ingested here, and their own
account of the Philistine wars should be read in place before this page says more. The thread records
that *begin to save* is completed, in the same words, outside Judges.

## Significance

- **It explains the shape of the Samson story before it starts.** A deliverer promised only a
  beginning will not bring rest, and the Samson chapters never say the land had rest.
- **It ties the end of the judges to the rise of the kings.** The book's refrain, *In those days there
  was no king in Israel* (17:6; 21:25), sits between a judge who only began to save and a king who is
  anointed to save from the same hand (1 Samuel 9:16). See [[Kingship and Empire]].
- **It is one of the threads that make Judges unfinished on purpose.** Dan, Samson's tribe, is also not
  settled at the end of his story; its migration follows in chapter 18.

### Judges 14 — the first blow

The beginning promised at 13:5 starts at 14:4: Yahweh *sought an occasion against the Philistines. Now at
that time the Philistines ruled over Israel*. The occasion is a marriage, and the first blow is thirty men
of Ashkelon struck to pay a wager (14:19). The word *save*, *yāšaʿ*, does not occur in the chapter. The
Spirit verb of 14:6, 19, *ṣālaḥ*, is the verb later used of Saul, whom 1 Samuel 9:16 promises will *save
my people out of the hand of the Philistines*; see
[[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]]. See [[Samson's Riddle]]. Sources: stored WEB Judges 13:5; 14:4,
19; 1 Samuel 9:16 as cited above. Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-16.

### Judges 15 — a great deliverance, in the days of the Philistines

Chapter 15 has the first saving-word since 13:5, and Samson speaks it: *You have given this great
deliverance*, *təšûʿâ* (15:18, WLC), after a thousand dead at Lehi. The narrator then gives his twenty
years *in the days of the Philistines* (15:20), *bîmê pəlištîm* (WLC): the judgeship is dated by the
enemy's rule, which it does not end. The phrase *təšûʿâ gədôlâ* recurs in 1 Samuel 19:5 and 2 Samuel
23:10, 12, of David and his men against the Philistines (WEB at bible-api.com; WLC). The beginning has a
great deliverance in it, and the Philistines are still there. See [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]]. Sources:
stored WEB Judges 13:5; 15:18, 20; Hebrew WLC (`Judg.xml`, `1Sam.xml`, `2Sam.xml`), 2026-11-16.
