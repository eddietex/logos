# Christ Our Passover - Exodus 12 in the New Testament

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 12:1–28** — the lamb *"without defect"*, the blood on the doorposts, *"when I see the blood,
  I will pass over you"*, the seven days of unleavened bread, and *"the destroyer"*. See
  [[The Passover]].
- **1 Corinthians 5:7–8** — *"Purge out the old yeast, that you may be a new lump, even as you are
  unleavened. For indeed Christ, our Passover, has been sacrificed in our place. Therefore let us keep
  the feast, not with old yeast, neither with the yeast of malice and wickedness, but with the
  unleavened bread of sincerity and truth"* (WEB)
- **John 1:29** — *"Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!"* (WEB)
- **1 Peter 1:18–19** — *"knowing that you were redeemed, not with corruptible things, with silver or
  gold, from the useless way of life handed down from your fathers, but with precious blood, as of a
  lamb without blemish or spot, the blood of Christ"* (WEB)
- **Hebrews 11:28** — *"By faith, he kept the Passover, and the sprinkling of the blood, that the
  destroyer of the firstborn should not touch them"* (WEB)
- **Luke 22:15** — *"I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer"* (WEB)
- **John 19:14, 36** — *"the Preparation Day of the Passover"*; *"A bone of him will not be broken"*
  (WEB), with Exodus 12:46 — *"Do not break any of its bones"*, at
  [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]] — Numbers 9:12, and Psalm 34:20

## Analysis

**The New Testament does not quote Exodus 12 so much as use it as a vocabulary.** No New Testament
writer cites a sentence of 12:1–28 with *"as it is written"*. What they take is its nouns — the lamb,
the blood, the leaven, the destroyer, the Passover itself — and each writer takes a different one for a
different purpose.

### Paul: the leaven, and the Passover already killed

**1 Corinthians 5 is about a moral case in the church, not about the Passover.** Paul has just told the
Corinthians to expel a man living with his father's wife, and he reaches for the festival to say why:
*"Don't you know that a little yeast leavens the whole lump?"* (5:6). **The argument runs on the order
of Exodus 12:15** — on the first day *"you shall put away yeast out of your houses"*. The lamb has been
killed, so the week of unleavened bread has begun, so the leaven must already be out.

The Greek is shorter than the WEB: *καὶ γὰρ τὸ πάσχα ἡμῶν ἐτύθη Χριστός*, *for also our Passover was
sacrificed, Christ* (SBLGNT). **The WEB's *"in our place"* follows a reading (*ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν*) that the
SBLGNT does not print** and marks with a variant sign. Paul's sentence, in the text the SBLGNT
reconstructs, identifies Christ with the Passover victim and says nothing about substitution. Whether
the Passover was understood as a substitution at all is itself open: Exodus 12 calls it a *zebaḥ* (12:27),
a sacrifice eaten by the household, and says the blood is a *sign* God sees (12:13) — not that the lamb
dies instead of the firstborn. See [[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]].

**Paul also does what Exodus 12:17 does — speaks of the festival from after the event.** *"Even as you
are unleavened"*: the Corinthians already are what the festival asks them to become.

### John: the Lamb, and the timing

*"Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world"* — *ὁ ἀμνὸς τοῦ θεοῦ* (SBLGNT). **The
saying does not name the Passover**, and *ἀμνός* is not obviously the Passover's word: the Hebrew of
Exodus 12:3–5 has *śeh*, a head of small cattle, and the Greek of the Septuagint there is not checked in
this pass. *"Takes away the sin"* fits the Passover poorly, since Exodus 12 says nothing about sin.
**Readers have heard three lambs in it** — the Passover lamb, the lamb of Isaiah 53:7 led to the
slaughter, and the lamb Abraham said God would provide (Genesis 22:8) — and the Gospel does not choose.

**John's Passion makes the Passover link that 1:29 leaves open.** In John, Jesus is condemned when *"it
was the Preparation Day of the Passover"* (19:14, WEB) — the day the lambs were killed — and his legs are
not broken, *"that the Scripture might be fulfilled, 'A bone of him will not be broken'"* (19:36, WEB).
John does not say which Scripture. The Passover law forbids breaking the lamb's bones (Exodus 12:46,
*wəʿeṣem lōʾ tišbərû-bô*; Numbers 9:12, *"nor break a bone of it"*, WEB), and Psalm 34:20 says of the
righteous, *"He protects all of his bones. Not one of them is broken"* (WEB); readers have heard either or
both. **John's *Ὀστοῦν οὐ συντριβήσεται αὐτοῦ*** (SBLGNT) **is passive, like the Psalm's niphal
*nišbārâ*, *is broken*** (Psalm 34:21 in Hebrew; WLC), **where both Passover laws are active prohibitions
in the plural** — *you shall not break*, *they shall not break* (WLC). On form, the
citation is closer to the Psalm; on setting — the Preparation Day, the lambs — it is closer to the
Passover. Whether the Septuagint of either verse matches John's wording is not checked in this pass.
**Exodus 12:46 gives no reason for the rule**, so the law supplies John no interpretation of its own. See
[[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]].

### 1 Peter: without blemish, and redeemed

*"As of a lamb without blemish or spot"* — *ὡς ἀμνοῦ ἀμώμου καὶ ἀσπίλου* (SBLGNT). **This is the
requirement of Exodus 12:5, *tāmîm*, *"without defect"***, which Genesis used of Noah and Abram (6:9;
17:1) before Exodus used it of an animal. **And Peter puts it beside *"you were redeemed … not with …
silver or gold"*** — the silver and gold Israel carried out of Egypt (11:2; 12:35) — **so that both
halves of the exodus night stand in one sentence**: the plunder that was taken, and the blood that was
not paid with it. Whether Peter intends the plunder echo is not stated. See
[[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]].

### Hebrews: the destroyer

*"By faith, he kept the Passover, and the sprinkling of the blood, that the destroyer of the firstborn
should not touch them"* — *ἵνα μὴ ὁ ὀλοθρεύων τὰ πρωτότοκα θίγῃ αὐτῶν* (SBLGNT). **Hebrews is the only
New Testament text that takes up the destroyer of Exodus 12:23**, and the only one that makes Moses'
keeping of the Passover an act of faith. **It does not identify the destroyer either.** Hebrews says
Moses *πεποίηκεν*, *has kept* (perfect), the Passover — the rite as something done once in Egypt whose
effect stands. Whether *ὁ ὀλοθρεύων* reproduces the Septuagint's word at Exodus 12:23 is not checked in
this pass.

### Luke: the meal

At the Last Supper Jesus says *"I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I
suffer"*. **In the Synoptic Gospels the Last Supper is a Passover meal; in John the crucifixion falls as
the Passover lambs are killed.** The two chronologies are a long-standing problem in Gospel
scholarship; the wiki records the difference and does not resolve it.

## Significance

- **Each writer takes a different element**: Paul the leaven, John the lamb (and the timing), Peter the
  *tāmîm*, Hebrews the destroyer, the Synoptics the meal. **None reads the whole rite**, and none of
  them quotes Exodus 12's own explanation — *"when I see the blood, I will pass over you"*.
- **Substitution is read into the Passover more often than it is found there.** Exodus 12 calls the
  blood a sign and the lamb a meal-sacrifice. The substitution language attached to the Passover comes
  from the New Testament, and in 1 Corinthians 5:7 it depends on a textual variant.
- **The leaven is the most fully worked out.** Paul's argument uses the Exodus 12 sequence — the lamb
  killed, then seven days without leaven — and the order matters to it.
