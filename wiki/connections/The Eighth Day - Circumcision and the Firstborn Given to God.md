## Analysis

### The mother and the son — Leviticus 12

[[Purification After Childbirth]] now supplies the full setting: circumcision on day eight does
not complete the mother’s further purification period. The text never explicitly commands
the boy to stay with his mother for seven days; the earlier assertion of an identical
mother-and-newborn rule overstated the parallel and is corrected here.


**The Torah uses one interval, seven days and then the eighth, for a newborn boy and a newborn
animal alike.** A son is circumcised at eight days old — *ben-šəmōnat yāmîm* (Genesis 17:12; 21:4,
MAM) — and a firstborn calf or lamb is left *"seven days … with its mother"* and given to God *"on
the eighth day"*, *bayyôm haššəmînî* (Exodus 22:30; MAM 22:29). Leviticus 22:27 repeats the animal
rule for every newborn offered, with the same *seven days … with its mother* and *from the eighth
day* (MAM), and Leviticus 12:3 repeats the boy's.

**In Exodus 22:29–30 the two lines meet in one sentence.** *"You shall give the firstborn of your
sons to me. You shall do likewise with your cattle and with your sheep"*: the son and the animal are
named together, and then the eighth day is attached to the animal. **The text does not say that the
firstborn son is given on the eighth day**, and nothing in Exodus connects the firstborn law with
circumcision; the connection drawn here is the shared interval, which the reader can see and the
text does not explain.

**The seven-day periods have different stated subjects.** The animal is expressly to remain
with its mother. For the boy the adjacent rule concerns the mother's uncleanness: *"If a woman conceives, and bears a
male child, then she shall be unclean seven days"* (Leviticus 12:2), and the next
verse puts the circumcision on the eighth. Readings
that the seven days complete a creation-week before the child or animal is claimed are
interpretations and are not stated in these texts.

**Luke records both claims made over the same child.** Jesus is circumcised and named *"when eight
days were fulfilled"* (Luke 2:21), and then presented as a firstborn, *"holy to the Lord"* (2:23).
The second is traced at
[[Holy to the Lord - The Firstborn Law and the Presentation of Jesus]].

### The inaugural service — Leviticus 9

Leviticus 8:33–36 prescribes seven days at the tent door; 9:1 opens the eighth day
with Aaron's service, culminating in glory (9:23–24). This now-checked interval extends
the page's motif beyond newborn children and animals to installed priests. The shared
timing does not make ordination a circumcision rite or explain all eighth-day laws.
The preceding days and this inauguration are now ingested.
