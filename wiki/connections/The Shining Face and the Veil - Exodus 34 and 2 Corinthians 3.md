# The Shining Face and the Veil - Exodus 34 and 2 Corinthians 3

## Passages involved

- [[The Shining Face of Moses]] — Exodus 34:29–35; radiance, approach, instruction, and veiling.
- [[The Covenant Renewed]] — Exodus 34:1–28; the replacement tablets and renewed covenant.
- 2 Corinthians 3:1–18 — Paul's comparison of covenant ministries; verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Paul's argument starts with a community described as a letter of Christ, written by the
Spirit on hearts rather than stone (3:1–6). Moses' shining face then supplies evidence that
even the ministry Paul characterizes as death and condemnation came with glory (3:7–9).
His argument depends on that glory being real: the ministry of the Spirit and righteousness
surpasses it, and what remains exceeds what passes away (3:8–11).

**The accounts differ in emphasis and explanation.** Exodus reports that the Israelites are
afraid to approach, then that they approach, hear, and see Moses' face (34:30–35). Paul says
they could not look steadfastly at it because of its glory (3:7). Seeing and sustaining a gaze
are not identical claims in the WEB, so these need not directly contradict one another.
Nevertheless, Paul's statement should not replace Exodus's actual sequence.

Exodus puts the veil on after Moses speaks (34:33) and does not narrate the radiance fading.
Paul explicitly associates the veil with preventing a steadfast look at the end of what was
passing away (3:13). That purpose and the passing character of the glory belong to Paul's
interpretation; they are not an explanation Exodus itself supplies. The tension is preserved
without inventing an unreported scene of fading under the veil.

Paul moves from the covering on Moses' face to a veil over understanding when Moses is read
(3:14–15), then to its removal in turning to the Lord (3:16). The removal recalls Moses' own
unveiling before Yahweh in Exodus 34:34. Paul's ending extends unveiled encounter and
transformation to the whole community he addresses (3:17–18), rather than keeping the image
confined to its exceptional mediator.

These are Paul's theological claims within his argument about ministry, not a license to
infer that every individual Jewish reader lacks understanding. Nor does this page decide
questions about the Greek wording, the precise referent of the Lord in every clause, or the
range of the expression about what is passing away. The comparison above follows the WEB.

## Significance

The connection joins [[Covenant]], [[The Glory of God]], [[Seeing and Being Seen]], and
[[Mediation and Advocacy]]. Exodus narrates renewed access to instruction; Paul uses that
scene to explain the surpassing glory and shared transformation of the ministry he serves.
The face veil is distinct from the architectural veil discussed in
[[The Veil - Separation in Exodus and Access in Hebrews]].
