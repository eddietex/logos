# Higher Than Heaven, Deeper Than Sheol - The Measure of the Unsearchable

## Passages involved

- **Job 11:7–9** — Zophar: *"Can you fathom the mystery of God? Or can you probe the limits of the
  Almighty? They are high as heaven. What can you do? They are deeper than Sheol. What can you know?
  Its measure is longer than the earth, and broader than the sea."* — [[Zophar's First Speech]]
- **Job 5:9; 9:10** — *"who does great things that can't be fathomed"* (*ʾên ḥēqer*), said by
  Eliphaz and repeated word for word by Job — [[Eliphaz's First Speech]],
  [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]]
- **Job 26:5–6, 14** — *"Sheol is naked before God, and Abaddon has no covering"*; *"Behold, these
  are but the outskirts of his ways. How small a whisper do we hear of him!"* —
  [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]
- **Job 28:3, 12–14, 21–23, 27** — the miner who *"searches out, to the furthest bound"* (*taklît*),
  wisdom that the deep and the sea say is not in them, and God who *"searched it out"* —
  [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]]
- **Job 36:26; 37:23** — Elihu: *"Behold, God is great, and we don't know him. The number of his years
  is unsearchable"* (*lōʾ-ḥēqer*); *"We can't reach the Almighty"* (*lōʾ məṣāʾnūhû*, *we have not found
  him*) — [[Elihu on God's Greatness]]
- **Job 38:16–18** — God: *"Have you walked in the recesses (ḥēqer) of the deep? Have the gates of
  death been revealed to you? … Have you comprehended the earth in its width?"*
  — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Psalm 139:7–10** — *"If I ascend up into heaven, you are there. If I make my bed in Sheol, behold,
  you are there!"*
- **Amos 9:2–3** — *"Though they dig into Sheol, there my hand will take them; and though they climb
  up to heaven, there will I bring them down."*
- **Psalm 145:3; Isaiah 40:28** — *"his greatness is unsearchable"*; *"his understanding is
  unsearchable"* — *ʾên ḥēqer* again
- **Romans 11:33** — *"Oh the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and the knowledge of God! How
  unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past tracing out!"*
- **Ephesians 3:18–19** — *"to comprehend with all the saints what is the width and length and height
  and depth, and to know Christ's love which surpasses knowledge"*

## Analysis

**Zophar's four measures are the cosmos.** Height, depth, length, breadth, each set against a
region: heaven above, [[Sheol]] below, earth and sea across. The Hebrew world-picture had no larger
frame, so the claim is total — God exceeds the whole of it in every direction, and there is nowhere
for the mind to stand outside him and take his measure. It is the first time Scripture puts divine
incomprehensibility in the form of **dimensions**, and it does not stay in Zophar's mouth.

**The same map is used three ways.**

| Text | Heaven and Sheol | What follows from it |
|---|---|---|
| **Job 11:7–9** | God's limits are higher than one, deeper than the other | *"What can you do? … What can you know?"* — so be silent, and repent |
| **Psalm 139:8** | God is present in both | *"you are there"* — so there is nowhere to flee, and nowhere to be lost |
| **Amos 9:2** | God's hand reaches into both | so there is nowhere to hide from judgment |

Zophar's is the epistemic version: the cosmos measures what we cannot know. The psalm's is the
version of presence, and is comfort; Amos's is the same presence turned to threat. Job, in the
dialogue, will hold all three at once — a God he cannot know (9:11), cannot escape (7:19–20), and
cannot find (23:8–9).

**The word that runs through Job is *ḥēqer*.** It means the result of searching — the place reached
by digging, the bottom of a thing. Eliphaz says God does things *ʾên ḥēqer*, without bottom (5:9);
Job takes the line into his own hymn (9:10); Zophar asks whether Job can find God's *ḥēqer* (11:7).
Elihu, the last human speaker, says it of God's years — *"The number of his years is unsearchable"*
(36:26) — and answers Zophar's question with Zophar's verb: *"We can't reach the Almighty"* (37:23),
literally *we have not found him*. And then God, who never quotes the friends, uses the word once: ***"Have you walked in the
recesses (ḥēqer) of the deep?"*** (38:16), in a sequence that goes on to the gates of death and the
breadth of the earth (38:17–18) — **Zophar's depth, Sheol, and breadth, in Zophar's order.** The
friends' best theology turns out to be God's own topic.

**What God does with it is the opposite of what Zophar does.** Zophar's questions — *"Can you
fathom…? Can you probe…?"* — are rhetorical, and the expected answer closes the matter: no, so stop.
God's questions in chapters 38–41 are the same kind, and they go on for four chapters. They do not
end the conversation; they are the conversation. The questions show Job a world rather than proving
him wrong, and Job's response (42:5) is not *I see I was guilty* but *"now my eye sees you"*. **The
same premise in Zophar's mouth is a gag, and in God's an invitation.**

**Chapter 28 sits between them.** The hymn to wisdom searches every dimension Zophar named — the
miner going down to *taklît*, the farthest bound (28:3; Zophar's *"the limits (taklît) of the
Almighty"*, 11:7), the deep and the sea saying *"it isn't in me"* (28:14), Abaddon and Death having
only heard a rumour of it (28:22) — and concludes that *"God understands its way"* (28:23). That is
11:7–9 without 11:6: unsearchable, and without the accusation. **The verb of the search is the noun of
Zophar's question**: the miner *ḥôqēr*, *searches out* (28:3), and God, at the founding of the world,
*ḥăqārāh*, *searched it out* (28:27) — the root of *ḥēqer*, *"Can you fathom (ḥēqer) the mystery of
God?"* (11:7). The hymn grants both searches and lets only one of them reach wisdom.

**Paul quotes Job and borrows Zophar.** Romans 11:33–35 is a doxology on God's *unsearchable*
judgments, and 11:35 is a quotation of Job 41:11 (*"Who has first given to him?"*). Ephesians 3:18's
four dimensions — *width and length and height and depth* — are Zophar's four, in a different order,
and they measure something Zophar did not name: *"Christ's love which surpasses knowledge."* The
frame is the same — a measure beyond measuring — and it is turned, again, from a reason for silence
to a reason for prayer (Ephesians 3:14).
**Job 26 takes Zophar's measure and turns it over.** Zophar had said God's limits are *"deeper than
Sheol. What can you know?"* (11:8) — Sheol as the image of what man cannot reach. Job's hymn begins
at the same depth and says the opposite thing about it: *"Sheol is naked before God"* (26:6). The
deep is unsearchable to man and transparent to God, and Job's conclusion — that all of it is only
*"the outskirts of his ways"* (26:14) — accepts Zophar's premise and denies Zophar the use he made of
it, since on Job's terms the friends do not know enough about God to convict anyone. See
[[Abaddon Has No Covering - Job 26.6, Proverbs 15.11, and Revelation 9.11]].

## Significance

- It is the clearest case in Job of the friends being right about God and wrong about Job, and the
  vocabulary shows it: what Zophar says at 11:7–9, God says at 38:16–18.
- It gives the theme of [[Wisdom]] its spatial grammar for the rest of the book. Chapter 28 and the
  whirlwind both work in Zophar's dimensions.
- It sets out one map with three readings — unknowable (Job 11), inescapable in mercy (Psalm 139),
  inescapable in judgment (Amos 9) — that Scripture keeps returning to, and that the New Testament
  finally turns toward love (Ephesians 3).
