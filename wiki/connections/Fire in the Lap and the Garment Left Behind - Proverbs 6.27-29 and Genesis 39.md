# Fire in the Lap and the Garment Left Behind - Proverbs 6.27-29 and Genesis 39

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 6:25, 27–29** — [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]: *"Don't lust after her beauty in your
  heart, neither let her captivate you with her eyelids … Can a man scoop fire into his lap, and his
  clothes not be burned? Or can one walk on hot coals, and his feet not be scorched? So is he who
  goes in to his neighbor's wife. Whoever touches her will not be unpunished."*
- **Genesis 39:6–12** — [[Joseph in Potiphar's House]]: *"Joseph was well-built and handsome. After
  these things, his master's wife set her eyes on Joseph; and she said, 'Lie with me.'"* … *"How then
  can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?"* … *"She caught him by his garment, saying,
  'Lie with me!' He left his garment in her hand, and ran outside."*
- **Genesis 20:6** — [[Abraham and Abimelech]]: *"I also withheld you from sinning against me.
  Therefore I didn't allow you to touch her."*
- **Genesis 26:11** — [[Isaac in Gerar]]: *"He who touches this man or his wife will surely be put to
  death."*
- **Job 31:9, 12** — [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]: *"If my heart has been enticed to a woman, and I have
  laid wait at my neighbor's door … For it is a fire that consumes to destruction"*

## Analysis

**The same scene, from the other side.** Proverbs 6 is a warning to the son, and Genesis 39 is a
story about a son who needed none. The elements line up closely. **Beauty**: Proverbs warns against
coveting *her* beauty (*yopyāh*, 6:25); Genesis has *his* — Joseph is *yəp̄ēh-ṯōʾar wîp̄ēh marʾeh*,
*"well-built and handsome"* (39:6), the verse before the approach. **Eyes**: in Proverbs she
captivates *"with her eyelids"* (6:25); in Genesis *"his master's wife set her eyes on Joseph"*
(39:7). **Another man's wife**: Proverbs' *ʾēšeṯ rēʿēhû*, *his neighbour's wife* (6:29); Joseph's
own reason for refusing, *"because you are his wife"* (39:9). **Clothes**: the man who carries fire
in his lap cannot keep his clothes from burning, *ûḇəḡāḏāyw* (6:27); Joseph *"left his garment
(biḡdô) in her hand, and ran outside"* (39:12). The Hebrew is read from Sefaria's *Miqra according
to the Masorah*.

The last element is where the two part. **The man of Proverbs keeps the fire and loses his clothes
to it; Joseph lets the clothes go and keeps away from the fire.** The garment left in her hand
cost him his place and put him in prison (39:20), but the reproach that Proverbs says *"will not be
wiped away"* (6:33) was not his; the charge against him was false. The wiki does not claim that
Proverbs alludes to Genesis — no name, no phrase is borrowed — and records the correspondence as a
shared scene: the warning and the case.

**Touch.** Proverbs' *"Whoever touches her"* (*kol-hannōḡēaʿ bāh*, 6:29) uses the verb Genesis
twice uses for the same boundary. God tells Abimelech, *"I didn't allow you to touch her"*
(*lingōaʿ ʾēlêhā*, 20:6); the later Abimelech decrees death for *"He who touches (hannōḡēaʿ) this
man or his wife"* (26:11). In all three, *touching* another man's wife is the line, and crossing it
brings judgment. Genesis 20:6 adds what Proverbs leaves unsaid: that God himself may hold a man
back from it.

**Fire.** Job 31:12 called adultery *"a fire"* (*ʾēš hîʾ*) that consumes to *ʾăḇaddôn*,
*destruction*; Proverbs 6:27 turns the statement into a question with an obvious answer. Later in the
same lecture the danger becomes the husband's jealousy (6:34) — the passion whose *"flashes are
flashes of fire"* in Song of Solomon 8:6. See
[[No Ransom for the Adulterer - Proverbs 6.29-35, Leviticus 20.10, and Deuteronomy 22.22]].

## Significance

Genesis gives the wisdom tradition its model case: a young man alone, his master's wife, daily
pressure (*"day by day"*, 39:10), and a refusal grounded in the master's trust and in God. Proverbs
gives the same scene the form of instruction, without the hero. Read together, the warning shows
what Joseph refused, and Joseph shows that the warning can be kept, at a cost. See
[[Sexual Sin]], [[Integrity]], and [[Joseph]].
