# The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 19:1–29** — Lot takes in the two angels, *"baked unleavened bread"* (19:3), goes out *"to
  the door"* to the mob and is pulled back inside, the men *"at the door of the house"* are struck
  blind, the angels say *"we will destroy this place"* (19:13), and at dawn they bring Lot out. See
  [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]].
- **Exodus 12:1–28** — unleavened bread, blood on the door, *"none of you shall go out of the door of his
  house until the morning"* (12:22), and Yahweh *"will not allow the destroyer to come in"* (12:23). See
  [[The Passover]].
- **Exodus 12:29–39** — *"Rise up, get out"* (12:31), the dough unleavened because they *"couldn't wait"*
  (12:39), and the narrator's *"Yahweh struck"* (12:29). See
  [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]].

## Analysis

**Two nights on which a city or a land is destroyed and one household — or one people — is kept safe
inside a door.** The stories share a cluster of words that is rare elsewhere in Genesis–Exodus, and the
wiki records the overlap here because each word, taken alone, is ordinary.

| | Genesis 19 | Exodus 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Unleavened bread, *maṣṣôt* | Lot bakes it for the guests (19:3) | eaten with the lamb (12:8), and for seven days (12:15–20) |
| The door, *petaḥ* | Lot goes out to the door (19:6); the men at the door are struck (19:11) | blood on it; no one goes out of it until morning (12:22–23) |
| *Strike*, *nākâ* (hiphil) | the angels *"struck the men who were at the door"* with blindness (19:11) | *"I will … strike all the firstborn"* (12:12; 12:13) |
| Destroy, *šāḥat* | *"we will destroy (mašḥitîm) this place"* (19:13); *"Yahweh will destroy (mašḥît) the city"* (19:14) | *"no plague … to destroy you"* (*ləmašḥît*, the noun, 12:13); *"the destroyer (hammašḥît)"* (12:23) |
| Morning | *"When the morning came, then the angels hurried Lot"* (19:15) | *"until the morning"* (12:22) |
| Brought out, *yāṣāʾ* (hiphil) | *"they took him out"* (*wayyōṣiʾuhû*, 19:16) | *"I have brought (hôṣēʾtî) your armies out"* (12:17) |
| Haste | the angels *"hurried"* Lot (*wayyāʾîṣû*, 19:15); he *"lingered"* (19:16) | *"you shall eat it in haste"* (*bəḥippāzôn*, 12:11) |

(WLC throughout. ***Maṣṣôt* occurs once in Genesis, at 19:3**, and next at Exodus 12:8. The hiphil
participle of *šāḥat* occurs in Genesis–Exodus only at Genesis 6:13 (*"I will destroy them"*), 19:13,
14, and Exodus 12:23 — WLC lemma search of *šāḥat*, read by form. The *ləmašḥît* of 12:13 is the noun
*mašḥît*, *destruction*, from the same root; it occurs nowhere else in the Torah.)

### What is the same

**In both stories the safe place is a house, and the danger is outside its door.** Lot's mistake is to
go out to the mob (19:6); the angels *"brought Lot into the house to them, and shut the door"* (19:10).
Exodus 12 makes the same thing a rule: *"none of you shall go out of the door of his house until the
morning"*. **And in both the destroying is done by agents of God who are not simply God** — the two
*"men"* who are angels, sent by Yahweh to destroy (19:13), and *"the destroyer"* whom Yahweh will not
let in (12:23) — while the narrator also says God did it (19:24, 29; 12:12, 29).

**In both, the household eats unleavened bread on the night.** In Genesis it is hospitality and no
reason is given; in Exodus it is commanded, and at 12:39 the reason is haste.

### What is different

**Lot is saved without a sign.** Nothing marks his house; the angels are inside it, and he is saved
*"Yahweh being merciful to him"* (19:16) and because *"God remembered Abraham"* (19:29). **Israel's
houses are marked**, and God says what he will look for: *"when I see the blood, I will pass over
you"* (12:13).

**Lot lingers and is dragged out; Israel is dressed to leave.** *"He lingered; and the men grabbed his
hand"* (19:16) — set against the belt, the shoes, and the staff of 12:11. **Genesis 19:29 says God
*"sent Lot out"* (*wayəšallaḥ*)**, the verb of *"Let my people go"* throughout Exodus 5–11 (WLC).

**Sodom is destroyed for its outcry; Egypt is struck for the outcry it caused.** The *ṣəʿāqâ* that rose
from Sodom (Genesis 18:20–21; 19:13) rose from Israel in Egypt (Exodus 3:7, 9), and will rise from
Egypt itself (11:6; 12:30). See [[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]].
**Added with the ingest of Exodus 12:29–51 (2026-10-22): two more shared words, both in the
departure.** (WLC.)

| | Genesis 19 | Exodus 12 |
|---|---|---|
| *"Get up! Get out"*, *qûmû ṣəʾû* | Lot to his sons-in-law: *"Get up! Get out of this place, for Yahweh will destroy the city"* (19:14) | Pharaoh to Moses and Aaron: *"Rise up, get out from among my people"* (12:31) |
| Linger, *māhah* (hitpalpel) | *"But he lingered"* (19:16) | *"they … couldn't wait"* (*ləhitmahmēah*, 12:39) |

**The same two imperatives are spoken on both nights, from opposite sides.** In Sodom the warning comes
from the one household being saved and is taken as a joke (19:14); in Egypt it comes from the king of the
land being struck and is obeyed at once. ***Māhah*** occurs in Genesis–Deuteronomy only at Genesis 19:16;
43:10; and Exodus 12:39 (WLC lemma search): **Lot lingered and had to be taken by the hand; Israel could
not linger, and the unleavened bread is the proof.** That closes the gap the table above left open —
Lot's bread had no reason given, Israel's is given one.

**And the narrator again gives the act to God.** *"At midnight, Yahweh struck all the firstborn"* (12:29)
mentions no destroyer, as Genesis 19:24–25, 29 says *"Yahweh rained … sulfur and fire"* and *"God
destroyed the cities"* after the angels said *"we will destroy"*. **Both narratives let the agent and God
stand side by side without explaining how they relate.**

## Significance

- **The overlap is in vocabulary and setting, and Exodus never cites Genesis 19.** The wiki records it
  as a resonance, not as an allusion the writer is shown to have intended — the same judgment
  [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]] made of the unleavened bread when Genesis 19 was ingested.
- **What Exodus adds is the sign.** Both nights distinguish a household from the doomed around it;
  only the second gives the household something to do and God something to see.
- **Later Scripture joins the two.** Sodom and the Exodus are often paired as the paradigm judgments;
  the wiki will add those passages as ingestion reaches them, rather than citing them from memory now.
