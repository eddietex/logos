# Distinguishing and Teaching - Leviticus 10 and 11

## Passages involved

- [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]] — Leviticus 10:1–11.
- [[Clean and Unclean Animals]] — Leviticus 11:1–47.

## Analysis

After the deaths of Nadab and Abihu, Yahweh charges Aaron to distinguish holy from common
and unclean from clean, and to teach Israel the statutes mediated by Moses (10:10–11).
The next chapter opens with Yahweh addressing Moses and Aaron together (11:1–2), and
ends with the purpose of distinguishing unclean from clean and edible from inedible (46–47).

The correspondence gives concrete content to the teaching office. Chapter 11 does not
depict Aaron examining each meal or prescribe an offering for each instance of contact.
It communicates distinctions to all Israel, including rules households can apply to
vessels, water, seed, and dead animals. The priestly commission serves communal practice.

## Significance

The transition from priestly crisis to purity instruction is coherent: those charged
to distinguish must communicate what the distinctions require.
