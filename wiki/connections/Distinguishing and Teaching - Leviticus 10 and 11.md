# Distinguishing and Teaching - Leviticus 10 and 11

## Passages involved

- [[Mildew in a House]] — Leviticus 14:33–57.

- [[Mildew in Garments]] — Leviticus 13:47–59; material objects.

- [[Examining Diseases of the Skin]] — Leviticus 13:1–46; further application.

- [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]] — Leviticus 10:1–11.
- [[Clean and Unclean Animals]] — Leviticus 11:1–47.

## Analysis

After the deaths of Nadab and Abihu, Yahweh charges Aaron to distinguish holy from common
and unclean from clean, and to teach Israel the statutes mediated by Moses (10:10–11).
The next chapter opens with Yahweh addressing Moses and Aaron together (11:1–2), and
ends with the purpose of distinguishing unclean from clean and edible from inedible (46–47).

The correspondence gives concrete content to the teaching office. Chapter 11 does not
depict Aaron examining each meal or prescribe an offering for each instance of contact.
It communicates distinctions to all Israel, including rules households can apply to
vessels, water, seed, and dead animals. The priestly commission serves communal practice.

### Further application — Leviticus 13:1–46

[[Examining Diseases of the Skin]] makes the priest’s discriminating role explicit for individual cases.
Aaron or a priestly son examines signs, orders observation where needed, and pronounces
clean or unclean. Unlike chapter 11’s household instructions, these decisions require
priestly inspection. Pronouncement is not a described cure; changed signs can require a
new examination. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 13:1–46.

### Garments and skins — Leviticus 13:47–59

[[Mildew in Garments]] extends priestly inspection to wool, linen, and skins. The concluding
formula (59) explicitly gives clean and unclean pronouncement as the law’s purpose.
Unlike carcass-contact washing and waiting until evening in 11:32, the garment-plague
case requires inspection and can end in burning. Source: stored WEB passages cited.

### Houses and the closing teaching formula — Leviticus 14:33–57

[[Mildew in a House]] again opens with Moses and Aaron addressed together (33).
The owner reports what appears to be a plague; priestly inspection supplies the ruling
and directs repair or demolition. Verses 54–57 gather skin, garments, and houses into
a teaching purpose: knowing when something is clean or unclean. This returns the
individual procedures to the broader charge in 10:10–11. Source: stored WEB passages.

## Significance

The transition from priestly crisis to purity instruction is coherent: those charged
to distinguish must communicate what the distinctions require.
