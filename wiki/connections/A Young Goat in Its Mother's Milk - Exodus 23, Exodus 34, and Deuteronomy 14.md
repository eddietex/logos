# A Young Goat in Its Mother's Milk - Exodus 23, Exodus 34, and Deuteronomy 14

## Passages involved

- [[The Covenant Renewed]] — Exodus 34:1–28.

- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:19.
- Exodus 34:26 — checked in raw WEB.
- Deuteronomy 14:21 — verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

The raw WEB gives the same sentence at Exodus 23:19 and 34:26: “You shall not boil a young goat in
its mother’s milk.” In both places it follows the instruction to bring the first of the first fruits
to Yahweh's house. Neither passage explains the juxtaposition or names a prohibited foreign
ceremony.

Deuteronomy 14:21 repeats the rule after an instruction concerning an animal that dies of itself and
a declaration that Israel is a holy people. The literary setting therefore changes: the Exodus
repetitions stand with offerings, while Deuteronomy places it amid food restrictions. That change
supplies context, not an explicit reason for the goat rule.

**The text does not state a general ban on every combination of meat and dairy.** Nor do these
verses identify a fertility rite as the reason for the prohibition. Broader interpretations require
their own sources; none is adopted here. The observation is limited to a repeated instruction, the
young animal and its mother's milk, and the different neighboring laws.

### The renewal in its narrative setting — Exodus 34

In [[The Covenant Renewed]], the rule remains paired with first fruits amid the restored worship requirements
(34:26). Renewal after the calf changes its narrative setting without adding a reason for the
prohibition. Its repetition is evidence of continuity with 23:19, not independent evidence for a
reconstructed foreign rite.

Exodus 34:1–28 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-24.

## Significance

The repeated rule joins [[Sacrifice and Offering]] to the subject of [[Clean and Unclean]] without
explaining their relation. Preserving that silence prevents a plausible theory from becoming the
verse's stated meaning.
