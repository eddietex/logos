# Babel and the Gate of Heaven

## Passages involved

- Genesis 11:1–9 — the tower *"whose top reaches to the sky"*, the name, and the scattering
- Genesis 28:10–22 — the stairway whose *"top reached to heaven"*, and *"the gate of heaven"*
- Genesis 12:2–3 — *"I will … make your name great"*
- Genesis 13:16; 28:14 — the dust, and spreading abroad in four directions

## Analysis

**Two things in Genesis have their top in the sky, and they are opposites at every point.**

The idiom is the hinge. Genesis 11:4 has ***wərōʾšô baššāmayim*** — *and its top in the sky* — and
Genesis 28:12 has ***wərōʾšô maggîaʿ haššāmāymâ*** — *and its top reaching to heaven*. **These are the
only two places in the book where something's *head* is said to reach the sky.** One is built; one is
seen.

| | **Babel — Genesis 11** | **Bethel — Genesis 28** |
|---|---|---|
| **the structure** | a city and a tower, *"let's build ourselves"* | a *sullām*, *set upon the earth*; **no builder is named** |
| **the material** | brick for stone, tar for mortar — manufactured | one stone, *of the place*, picked up in a field |
| **who is awake** | everyone, working | nobody; it is a dream |
| **the top** | *in the sky* | *reaching to heaven* |
| **the traffic** | none; **God has to come down to see it** (11:5) | angels **ascending** and descending, continuously |
| **the name** | *"let's make a name for ourselves"* | God names himself; Jacob names the place |
| **the scattering** | feared: *"lest we be scattered abroad"* | promised: *"you will spread abroad"* to four points |
| **the gate** | *Babel*, popularly **gate of god** | *"this is the gate of heaven"* |
| **the outcome** | the building stops; the languages divide | the promise is given; the man walks on |

### The two ironies the wiki will vouch for

**1. God comes down to see the tower.** *"Yahweh came down to see the city and the tower, which the
children of men built"* (11:5). **The structure whose whole point was height does not reach far
enough for God to see it from where he is** — Genesis says so with a straight face. At Bethel there
is nothing to come down for: the stairway is already occupied, and the traffic on it starts from the
bottom.

**2. Babel's name is a joke about a gate.** The Akkadian ***bāb-ili*** was understood in Babylon
itself as **gate of god**, and Genesis 11:9 refuses the etymology, deriving the name instead from
***bālal***, *to confuse*: *"because Yahweh confused the language of all the earth there."* **Genesis
denies the city the title, and then hands it, seventeen chapters later, to a man asleep in a field.**
*Šaʿar haššāmayim* — the gate of heaven — is at Luz, and it has one stone in it.

### What the wiki does not claim

**It does not claim Genesis 28 was composed as a reply to Genesis 11.** The claim made is the narrow,
checkable one: the two passages share the *top reaching heaven* idiom exclusively, the gate motif,
and the scattering motif, and stand at opposite ends of all three.

**And the scattering words are different roots**, which matters and is easy to lose in English. Babel
fears ***pûṣ***, *to be dispersed*; Jacob is promised ***pāraṣ***, *to burst out, break through*.
**They are not the same verb and this page does not make a pun of them.** What is the same is the
direction of travel and the valuation: **the thing the builders organised a civilisation to prevent
is given to Jacob as a blessing**, in four directions, in the same sentence as the dust of the earth.

### And the name

**Babel's stated purpose is *"let's make a name for ourselves"* (11:4).** The book's answer to that
line has always been Genesis 12:2, *"I will … make your name great"* — a name received rather than
built, which is the subject of a page of its own. **Genesis 28 adds the other half.** Before Jacob
names anything, God names **himself**: *"I am Yahweh, the God of Abraham your father, and the God of
Isaac."* Jacob's own naming is of the ground, not of himself, and the name he gives it is **house of
God** — the reverse of the one Babel wanted, which was a house with their own name on it.

## Significance

**The pair frames the whole distance from Genesis 11 to Genesis 28: the difference between reaching
heaven and being reached.**

Both scenes are about access. The builders are not condemned for wanting it; they are stopped, and
what stops them is that the project was self-authored — their bricks, their name, their refusal to be
scattered. **Bethel gives the same thing away for nothing** to a man who was not looking for it, could
not have built it, and did not even know where he was.

**And it costs him the thing Babel was protecting.** The promise attached to the gate of heaven is
that Jacob **will** spread abroad — west, east, north, and south — which is precisely the fate the
tower was raised to prevent. In Genesis 11 dispersal is the judgment. **In Genesis 28 it is the
blessing**, and the only thing that changed is who is doing the sending.
