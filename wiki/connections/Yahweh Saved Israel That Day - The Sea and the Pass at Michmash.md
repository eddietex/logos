# Yahweh Saved Israel That Day - The Sea and the Pass at Michmash

## Passages involved

- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — **Exodus 14:30** — *Thus Yahweh saved Israel that day out of the hand of the
  Egyptians; and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore.* (stored WEB)
- [[The Song of the Sea]] — **Exodus 15:14–15** — *The peoples have heard. They tremble. Pangs have taken hold on the
  inhabitants of Philistia. … All the inhabitants of Canaan have melted away.* (stored WEB)
- [[Rahab and the Spies]] — **Joshua 2:9, 24** — *all the inhabitants of the land melt away before you*; *all the
  inhabitants of the land melt away before us* (stored WEB)
- [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]] — **1 Samuel 14:15–16, 23** — *the earth quaked, so there was an exceedingly
  great trembling*; *the multitude melted away and scattered*; *So Yahweh saved Israel that day* (stored WEB)

## Analysis

**The closing sentence.** 1 Samuel 14:23 reads *wayyôšaʿ YHWH bayyôm hahûʾ ʾeṯ-yiśrāʾēl*. Exodus 14:30 begins with
the same five words in the same order, *wayyôšaʿ YHWH bayyôm hahûʾ ʾeṯ-yiśrāʾēl*, and goes on *miyyaḏ miṣrāyim*,
*out of the hand of the Egyptians* (WLC, compared word by word). The WEB keeps the match in English: *Thus Yahweh
saved Israel that day* and *So Yahweh saved Israel that day*. Both sentences close a battle that Israel did not fight
for itself: at the sea Israel *stood still* (Exodus 14:13, stored WEB), and at Michmash two men began it and the
Philistines' own swords finished it (1 Samuel 14:13, 20).

**What Yahweh will work.** At the sea Moses says *see the salvation of Yahweh, which he will work for you today*,
*yəšûʿaṯ YHWH ʾăšer-yaʿăśeh lāḵem hayyôm* (Exodus 14:13, WLC). Jonathan's hope before the climb uses the same verb
with the same kind of object: *It may be that Yahweh will work for us*, *ʾûlay yaʿăśeh YHWH lānû* (1 Samuel 14:6,
WLC). Moses promises; Jonathan says *It may be*. And afterwards the people say Jonathan *has worked this great
salvation*, *ʿāśâ hayšûʿâ haggəḏôlâ* (14:45, WLC; stored WEB; [[Saul's Rash Oath]]), the noun of Exodus 14:13 with the
verb of both. Here the words are matched by lemma (*ʿāśâ*, 6213; *yəšûʿâ*, 3444), and *ʿāśâ* is one of the commonest
verbs in Hebrew, so the match is suggestive, not conclusive.

**The trembling and the melting.** The Song of the Sea says what the peoples will do when they hear: *They tremble*,
*yirgāzûn*, and *All the inhabitants of Canaan have melted away*, *nāmōḡû* (Exodus 15:14–15, WLC). The first people
named after *the peoples* is Philistia. At Michmash the narrator uses both verbs: *the earth quaked*, *wattirgaz
hāʾāreṣ* (14:15), and *the multitude melted away*, *hehāmôn nāmôḡ* (14:16) (WLC). In the WLC files of Genesis–2 Samuel
the verb *mûḡ* (morphhb lemma 4127) occurs only at Exodus 15:15, at Joshua 2:9 and 24, where Rahab and then the spies
report the Song's prediction come true in Canaan, and at 1 Samuel 14:16. The verb *rāḡaz* (lemma 7264) is
commoner (eight occurrences in those files), so the weight of the parallel falls on *mûḡ* and on the closing
sentence.

**The Philistines.** Exodus 15:14 names *the inhabitants of Philistia* among the peoples seized by pangs. At
Michmash it is the Philistines who tremble and melt. The Song looked ahead to the conquest; 1 Samuel 14 shows the same
terror falling on the people the Song named first, generations later, under the first king.

**What the parallel does not claim.** The text of 1 Samuel 14 does not cite Exodus, and *Yahweh saved* is common
enough as a verb. The match is in the full sentence and in the cluster: the same closing words, with the two verbs
of the Song in the verses before it. Where the narrator could have written *Jonathan saved Israel*, the people later
say exactly that, *Shall Jonathan die, who has worked this great salvation in Israel?* (14:45, stored WEB;
[[Saul's Rash Oath]]), and add at once *he has worked with God today*. The narrator's sentence at 14:23 has already decided whose
salvation it was.

## Significance

The narrator tells the first great victory of Saul's reign in the language of the sea, and gives it to Yahweh
through Saul's son rather than through Saul. The kingdom Israel asked for so that a king would *go out before us, and
fight our battles* (8:20, stored WEB) wins its first Philistine battle the way Israel won at the sea: by Yahweh's
action, with the enemy's own terror and confusion doing the work. See [[War]], [[Faith]], and
[[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].
