# Offerings According to Means - Leviticus 5 and 12

## Passages involved

- [[Confession and Offerings According to Means]] — Leviticus 5:1–13.
- [[Purification After Childbirth]] — Leviticus 12:1–8.

## Analysis

Both laws accommodate inability to afford a lamb by prescribing two turtledoves or two young
pigeons, one for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering (5:7; 12:8). The parallel is
explicit in the material and the affordability condition, but the surrounding cases differ.

Leviticus 5 addresses specified offenses, requires confession, and promises forgiveness.
It also provides fine flour if the person cannot afford the birds (5:11–13). Leviticus 12
addresses the mother's purification after childbirth, identifies no particular offense,
and promises cleansing after atonement. It supplies no flour alternative. Neither chapter's
provisions should be expanded by silently borrowing the other's.

In the ordinary childbirth provision the lamb is the burnt offering and the bird the sin
offering (12:6); the flock animal in 5:6 is itself the sin offering. Even the lamb stage is
therefore not an identical rite. Chapter 5 specifies offering the sin-offering bird first
(5:8–10); chapter 12 lists the burnt offering first without giving that procedural sequence.

## Significance

Affordability changes the required gift while preserving the stated remedy. The parallel
supports [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], but also qualifies blanket claims about
[[Sin]] and [[Atonement]]: the same offering category can serve differently described cases.
See [[Sacrifice and Offering]] and [[Clean and Unclean]].
