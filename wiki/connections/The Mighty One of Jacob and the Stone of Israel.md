# The Mighty One of Jacob and the Stone of Israel

## Passages involved

- Genesis 49:24 — *"by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob, (from there is the shepherd, the stone
  of Israel)"* — **three divine titles in one verse, and all three are firsts**
- Genesis 48:15 — *"the God who has fed me all my life long"*, the shepherd as a participle
- Isaiah 49:26; 60:16 — *"I, Yahweh, am your Saviour, your Redeemer, the Mighty One of Jacob"*
- Psalm 132:2, 5 — David's vow *"to the Mighty One of Jacob"*
- Isaiah 1:24 — *"the Mighty One of Israel"*, the one variant of the title
- Psalm 118:22 — *"the stone which the builders rejected has become the cornerstone"*
- Isaiah 8:14; 28:16 — a stone of stumbling; *"a precious cornerstone of a sure foundation"*
- Daniel 2:34–35, 44–45 — the stone cut out without hands
- Matthew 21:42; Acts 4:11; Romans 9:33; Ephesians 2:20; 1 Peter 2:4–8 — the New Testament's
  collection of the stone texts

## Analysis

**Genesis 49:24 is the densest verse of divine naming in the book, and it is buried in a
parenthesis.**

***ʾĂbîr yaʿăqōb*** — ***the Mighty One of Jacob*** — **occurs five times in the Hebrew Bible and
this is the first.** The root *ʾābar* is strength, and *ʾabbîr* elsewhere means a *bull* or a
*stallion*; **the pointing distinguishes the divine title from the animal, and the association of
power is deliberate.** **What is remarkable is the genitive.** [[Jacob]] is not describing the God
of his fathers here — he has already done that, twice, at 48:15 — **he is attaching God's title to
his own name.** **A man coins a name for God out of himself, on his deathbed, and Isaiah is still
using it seven centuries later** (49:26; 60:16), **as is the psalm about David bringing the ark to
Zion** (132:2, 5).

***Hārōʿeh*** — ***the shepherd*** — **completes what the previous pericope began.** At 48:15 God
was *"the God who has fed me"*, ***hārōʿeh ʾōtî***, **a participle with an object: *the one
shepherding me*.** **Here the participle has become a bare noun**: not *the one who shepherds me*
but ***the shepherd***. **Jacob spent twenty years doing the job** (31:38–40) **and gives the title
away in the last thing he says about God.** Filed at length on
[[The God Who Has Fed Me - The First Time God Is a Shepherd]].

***ʾEben yiśrāʾēl*** — ***the stone of Israel*** — **occurs nowhere else in the Bible.** **The
construction is unique and the referent is not explained.** Read as a title it makes God the stone;
read as an apposition to *shepherd* it does the same; **read as a reference to the people it would
mean something like *the rock from which Israel is hewn*, which is Isaiah 51:1's idea in different
words.** **The wiki takes it as a divine title, with the shepherd, because both stand inside the
same parenthesis and both follow *"by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob"*.**

**Stone is a live word in Jacob's story in a way it is in no one else's.** **He slept on one at
Bethel and set it up as a pillar** (28:11, 18, 22); **he rolled one off a well for Rachel** (29:2–3,
8, 10); **he set one up at Galeed as a witness** (31:45–46) **and another at Bethel again** (35:14)
**and a third on Rachel's grave** (35:20). **The man with more stones in his narrative than any
other figure in Genesis calls God a stone once, at the end.** See
[[The Pillar Jacob Raised and the Pillars the Law Forbids]].

## Significance

**None of the three titles is picked up inside Genesis, and all three have long afterlives.**

**The *Mighty One of Jacob* becomes an Isaianic name for the redeemer God** in exactly the contexts
where Israel is being brought home from exile — *"all flesh shall know that I, Yahweh, am your
Saviour, and your Redeemer, the Mighty One of Jacob"* (49:26). **Isaiah 1:24 substitutes *Israel*
for *Jacob*, the only variation on record.**

**The *shepherd* becomes the Old Testament's most used picture of God** — Psalm 23:1, Psalm 80:1,
Ezekiel 34, Isaiah 40:11 — **and the New Testament's for Jesus** (John 10:11; Hebrews 13:20;
1 Peter 5:4).

**The *stone* becomes a whole exegetical tradition.** Psalm 118:22's rejected cornerstone,
Isaiah 8:14's stone of stumbling and 28:16's tried foundation, and Daniel 2's stone cut without
hands are collected in the New Testament as a set: **Jesus quotes Psalm 118 against the chief priests
(Matthew 21:42), Peter quotes it in Acts 4:11, Paul fuses Isaiah 8 and 28 in Romans 9:33, and 1 Peter
2:4–8 puts all three texts in four verses.** **Genesis 49:24 is not among the verses they cite** —
**it is the first time the word is used of God and it stands outside the tradition that grew from
the others.** The wiki records the priority and does not claim an influence.

**Four titles for God stand in Genesis 49:24–25 and every one of them is in [[Joseph]]'s saying.**
The Mighty One of Jacob, the shepherd, the stone of Israel, and *El Shaddai* — **the fifth and last
occurrence of that name in Genesis** (49:25). **The eleven other sons are given animals, land and
verdicts; the son who was sold gets the names of God.**
