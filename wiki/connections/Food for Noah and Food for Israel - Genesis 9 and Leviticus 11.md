# Food for Noah and Food for Israel - Genesis 9 and Leviticus 11

## Passages involved

- [[Noah and the Flood]] — Genesis 7:1–9.
- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — Genesis 9:1–7.
- [[Clean and Unclean Animals]] — Leviticus 11:1–47.

## Analysis

Genesis 7 distinguishes clean and unclean animals without giving Noah criteria. Genesis 9:3
then permits every moving living thing as food, while 9:4 withholds flesh with its blood.
Leviticus 11 addresses Israel through Moses and Aaron and restricts edible creatures by
criteria and lists. Its rules cannot simply be read back into Genesis 9 as an expressed
species restriction there.

**Tension retained:** the broad grant to Noah and the restricted grant to Israel differ.
A reading in terms of distinct recipients and covenant obligations can account for that
difference, but neither unit explicitly offers that reconciliation. Leviticus also adds
carcass rules, including impurity from an otherwise edible animal (11:39–40).
The food grant and the condition of a dead animal are therefore separate questions.

## Significance

The clean/unclean distinction predates Sinai in the narrative, but its dietary criteria
are supplied here for Israel. Earlier permission and later restriction must both remain visible.
