# They Didn't Know Yahweh - Pharaoh, the Generation After Joshua, and Eli's Sons

## Passages involved

- [[Bricks Without Straw]] — Exodus 5:2: *Pharaoh said, "Who is Yahweh, that I should listen to his
  voice to let Israel go? I don't know Yahweh, and moreover I will not let Israel go."*
- [[Another Generation Arose]] — Judges 2:10: *another generation arose after them, who didn't know
  Yahweh, nor the work which he had done for Israel.*
- [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] — 1 Samuel 2:12: *Now the sons of Eli were wicked men. They didn't know
  Yahweh.*
- [[Yahweh Calls Samuel]] — 1 Samuel 3:7: *Now Samuel didn't yet know Yahweh, neither was Yahweh's word
  yet revealed to him.*

## Analysis

**Three verses, one sequence.** The Hebrew sequence *lōʾ* + *yāḏaʿ* + *ʾeṯ-YHWH*, *not know Yahweh*,
occurs in the Westminster Leningrad Codex files of Genesis through Ruth and 1 Samuel exactly three times:
*lōʾ yāḏaʿtî ʾeṯ-YHWH* (Exodus 5:2), *ʾăšer lōʾ-yāḏəʿû ʾeṯ-YHWH* (Judges 2:10), and *lōʾ yāḏəʿû
ʾeṯ-YHWH* (1 Samuel 2:12). (Lemma-sequence search over the OpenScriptures morphhb XML; books outside that
range were not searched.)

The three subjects descend in distance from the sanctuary. Pharaoh is a foreign king who has never been
told who Yahweh is and refuses to listen to his voice. The generation after Joshua is Israel, born in
the land, which did not know Yahweh *nor the work which he had done*. Eli's sons are priests, at his
altar, eating his offerings. The same three words describe all three.

In Exodus, Pharaoh's not-knowing is answered by the plagues, and the narrative repeatedly frames them as
so that Egypt and Pharaoh may *know* (for example Exodus 11:7, *tēḏəʿûn*, WLC; WEB not quoted here). In
Judges, the not-knowing generation begins the cycle of apostasy and oppression (2:11–19). In 1 Samuel,
the not-knowing priests are answered by a sentence of death on their house (2:27–36; 4:11).

**Not yet.** 1 Samuel sets a different phrase beside them within a chapter: *ûšəmûʾēl ṭerem yāḏaʿ
ʾeṯ-YHWH*, *Samuel didn't yet know Yahweh* (3:7, WLC). *Ṭerem*, *not yet*, replaces *lōʾ*, *not*. The
sons do not know Yahweh; the boy does not know him *yet*, and the next verses are his call. The contrast
is the narrative's own vocabulary; that it is deliberate is a reader's judgment.

**What *know* means here.** None of the three verses defines it. In Pharaoh's mouth it is refusal of
recognition (*Who is Yahweh?*); in Judges it is tied to remembering Yahweh's *work*; in 1 Samuel 2 it is
shown by conduct, in the sacrificial abuse of 2:13–17. Whether the sons lacked information or
acknowledgement the text does not separate, and the wiki does not decide it.

## Significance

- It places the priests of Shiloh on the same line as the oppressor of Exodus and the apostate generation
  of Judges, in the opening verse that describes them.
- It frames Samuel's call (1 Samuel 3) as the first step out of that line, with *not yet* in place of
  *not*.
