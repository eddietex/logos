# Draw Your Sword - The Deaths of Abimelech and Saul

## Passages involved

- [[The Fall of Abimelech]] — **Judges 9:53–54** — *A certain woman cast an upper millstone on
  Abimelech's head, and broke his skull. Then he called hastily to the young man his armor bearer, and
  said to him, "Draw your sword, and kill me, that men not say of me, 'A woman killed him.' His young man
  thrust him through, and he died."* (stored WEB)
- **1 Samuel 31:4** — *Then Saul said to his armor bearer, "Draw your sword, and thrust me through with
  it, lest these uncircumcised come and thrust me through, and abuse me!" But his armor bearer would
  not; for he was terrified. Therefore Saul took his sword, and fell on it.* (WEB checked at
  bible-api.com, 2026-11-15; not yet ingested)

## Analysis

Two men who were made king, or nearly, ask their armor bearers to kill them as they die. The orders
begin with the same two words, *šəlōp ḥarbəkā*, *draw your sword* (WLC `Judg.xml`, `1Sam.xml`). Both
use *dāqar*, *thrust through*: Abimelech's young man *wayyidqərēhû*, *thrust him through*; Saul's
*wədoqrēnî*, *and thrust me through*. Saul's attendant is *nōśēʾ kēlāyw*, *his armor bearer*, the phrase
of Judges 9:54 (WLC).

| | Abimelech (Judges 9) | Saul (1 Samuel 31) |
|---|---|---|
| the wound | a millstone from a woman | wounded by archers (31:3, not checked) |
| the order | *Draw your sword, and kill me* | *Draw your sword, and thrust me through with it* |
| the fear | *that men not say of me, "A woman killed him"* | *lest these uncircumcised come … and abuse me* |
| the armor bearer | obeys | *would not; for he was terrified* |
| the end | *His young man thrust him through* | *Saul took his sword, and fell on it* |

The differences matter as much as the words. Abimelech fears what men will *say*; Saul fears what his
enemies will *do*. Abimelech's attendant does what he is told. Saul's refuses, and Saul does it himself.
Abimelech's fear of shame fails: Joab still says *Didn't a woman cast an upper millstone on him from the
wall?* (2 Samuel 11:21, WEB checked at bible-api.com).

Abimelech's armor bearer is *hannaʿar*, *the young man*. Gideon's firstborn Jether was called *a youth*,
*naʿar*, when he would not draw his sword (Judges 8:20), and Gideon's servant [[Purah]] is *naʿar* (7:10)
(WLC). See [[Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel]].

## Significance

Abimelech's three years are the Bible's first attempt at kingship in Israel, and Saul is Israel's first
anointed king. Both end with the same request to the same kind of attendant. Whether 1 Samuel 31 was
written with Judges 9 in mind the text does not say; the shared words are *draw your sword* and
*thrust through*, which are ordinary words for this act. The wiki records the parallel in event and
wording and does not claim more.
