# Smoke from His Nostrils - Leviathan and the Theophany of Psalm 18

## Passages involved

- **Job 41:18–21** — of Leviathan: *"His sneezing flashes out light. His eyes are like the eyelids of the
  morning. Out of his mouth go burning torches. Sparks of fire leap out. Out of his nostrils a smoke goes, as
  of a boiling pot over a fire of reeds. His breath kindles coals. A flame goes out of his mouth."* —
  [[Behemoth and Leviathan]]
- **Psalm 18:7–8** — of Yahweh coming to rescue the psalmist: *"Then the earth shook and trembled. The
  foundations also of the mountains quaked and were shaken, because he was angry. Smoke went out of his
  nostrils. Consuming fire came out of his mouth. Coals were kindled by it."*
- **Job 41:10** — *"None is so fierce that he dare stir him up. Who then is he who can stand before me?"*
- **Job 3:9** — *"neither let it see the eyelids of the morning"* — [[Let the Day Perish]]
- **Job 37:2–5; 40:9** — the thunder as God's voice, and *"Can you thunder with a voice like him?"* —
  [[The Voice of His Majesty - Job 37.2-5 and Psalm 29]]

## Analysis

**Three details of Leviathan's portrait are the three details of God's own arrival in Psalm 18:8**: smoke
from the nostrils, fire from the mouth, coals kindled — not in the same order, but all three within two verses. Job 41:20–21 has *"Out of his
nostrils a smoke goes … His breath kindles coals. A flame goes out of his mouth."* Psalm 18:8 has *"Smoke went
out of his nostrils. Consuming fire came out of his mouth. Coals were kindled by it."* The vocabulary of the
two verses shares *smoke* (*ʿāšān*), *mouth* (*peh*) and *coals* (*geḥālîm*); the *nostrils* and the verb of
kindling are different words in the two (*ʾap* and *nəḥîrayim*; *bāʿar* and *lāhaṭ*). The English of both is
checked against the WEB; the Hebrew is offered as the wiki's reading of the shared nouns, and the parallel is
one of image, not of quotation.

**What it does in Job.** The speech has just made its argument out loud: *"None is so fierce that he dare stir
him up. Who then is he who can stand before me?"* (41:10). Leviathan is the creature no one can stand before,
in order to show that no one can stand before his maker. **The portrait then borrows the theophany's
features**, so that the creature looks, for four verses, like God arriving in anger. The terror Leviathan
produces (*"Terror dances before him"*, 41:22; *"the mighty are afraid"*, 41:25) is a scaled-down image of the
terror of the one who made him. That is the argument of 41:10 carried out in the description.

**What it does not do.** It does not make Leviathan divine, and it does not make him God's rival. Psalm 18
describes God coming *to save* the one who called on him; Job 41 describes a creature whose fire is
ornament. The borrowing magnifies the creature for the sake of the creator, which is the direction of every
portrait in both speeches.

**And the eyelids of the morning (41:18)**, in the verse before, are Job's own words (3:9): the dawn he wished
his birth-night never to see. God gives that dawn to the monster's eyes. See
[[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]].

## Significance

- **It shows the speeches reusing the language of God's appearing** at the moment God has appeared. Job
  asked for God to come (31:35); he has come in a storm (38:1; 40:6); and in the last chapter of his speech
  he describes a creature in the terms that the psalms use for his coming.
- **It answers the natural-history reading without refuting it.** A crocodile may stand behind 41:1–17; the
  fire of 41:18–21 is not a field observation but theophanic language, and that is deliberate. See the
  reading notes on [[Behemoth and Leviathan]].
