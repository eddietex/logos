# Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:8** — Joseph's brothers: *"Will you indeed reign over us? Or will you indeed have
  dominion over us?"* — [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]
- **Exodus 2:13–15** — a Hebrew to Moses: *"Who made you a prince and a judge over us? Do you plan to
  kill me, as you killed the Egyptian?"*; and Moses flees — [[Moses Flees to Midian]]
- **Exodus 4:29–31** — the return, sent: *"The people believed … they bowed their heads and
  worshiped"* — [[Moses Returns to Egypt]]
- **Exodus 5:20–21** — the beaten officers to Moses and Aaron: *"May Yahweh look at you, and judge"* —
  [[Bricks Without Straw]]
- **Exodus 18:13** — *"Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood around Moses from the
  morning to the evening"*
- **Acts 7:23–29, 35** — Stephen: *"He supposed that his brothers understood that God, by his hand,
  was giving them deliverance; but they didn't understand"* (7:25); *"This Moses, whom they refused,
  saying, 'Who made you a ruler and a judge?'—God has sent him as both a ruler and a deliverer"* (7:35)
- **Luke 12:14** — Jesus: *"Man, who made me a judge or an arbitrator over you?"*

## Analysis

**The question is a refusal of office, put to a man who has just acted as if he held one.** Moses'
first words in Scripture are a judge's: he addresses *"him who did the wrong"* — *lārāšāʿ*, *the
guilty party* — and asks why he strikes his fellow (2:13). The man does not dispute the verdict. He
disputes the jurisdiction: ***mî śāmkā ləʾîš śar wəšōpēṭ ʿālênû***, *who set you as a man, a prince
and a judge, over us?* And he adds what makes the question unanswerable — the killing of the Egyptian,
done in secret, is known.

**Genesis has already told this story about Joseph.** The brother who dreams of sheaves bowing is met
with *"Will you indeed reign over us?"* (37:8) — *hămālōk timlōk ʿālênû*, the same *over us*,
*ʿālênû*. **In both cases**:

| | Joseph | Moses |
|---|---|---|
| the claim | dreams of rule (37:5–9) | acts as judge (2:13) |
| the refusal | *"Will you indeed reign over us?"* | *"Who made you a prince and a judge over us?"* |
| the result | sold out of the land | flees out of the land |
| the exile | Egypt; a foreign wife, a priest's daughter (41:45) | Midian; a foreign wife, a priest's daughter (2:21) |
| sons named for the exile | Manasseh, Ephraim (41:51–52) | Gershom (2:22) |
| the return | the brothers come to him, and bow | he is sent back to them, and they believe (4:31) |
| the office | *"lord of all Egypt"* (45:9) | *"sat to judge the people"* (18:13) |

**The parallel is structural, and Exodus does not draw it.** No verse in Exodus 2 looks back at
Joseph. What the two share is the shape — rejected by his brothers, exiled, married to a foreign
priest's daughter, named sons for it, and the means of his brothers' deliverance after all.

**Stephen draws it, and draws both.** His speech (Acts 7) tells Joseph's story — *"The patriarchs,
moved with jealousy against Joseph, sold him into Egypt. God was with him"* (7:9) — and then Moses'
in the same pattern, and he quotes the question twice. The second quotation turns it into an
argument: *"This Moses, whom they refused … God has sent him as both a ruler and a deliverer"*
(7:35). **The speech is building to its charge**: *"Which of the prophets didn't your fathers
persecute?"* (7:52). Joseph and Moses are Stephen's precedents for a deliverer whom Israel refused
before God vindicated him, and the hearers are meant to supply the third name.

**Exodus itself answers the question in chapter 18.** The man refused as *judge* at 2:14 sits *"to
judge the people"* from morning to evening (18:13), and it is his Midianite father-in-law — the
man who took him in when he fled — who tells him he cannot do it alone. **The question *who made
you a judge?* is answered by the burning bush, and the office is then shared at Jethro's advice.**
See [[Jethro]].

**Jesus' saying is the question in reverse.** Asked to divide an inheritance, he declines: *"Man,
who made me a judge or an arbitrator over you?"* (Luke 12:14). **The wording is close to Exodus 2:14
in the Greek** — *tis me katestēsen kritēn*, against the Septuagint's *tis se katestēsen archonta kai
dikastēn* (as quoted at Acts 7:27) — and the effect is the opposite: **Moses claimed an office no one
gave him and was rebuked; Jesus refuses one he is offered.** Whether Luke means the echo is not
demonstrable; the wiki notes the verbal closeness and does not press it.

### The second refusal — Exodus 5:21

**The belief of 4:31 lasts one audience.** When the straw is withdrawn and the foremen are beaten,
they meet Moses and Aaron outside Pharaoh's presence and say: ***yēreʾ YHWH ʿălêkem wəyišpōṭ***, *"May
Yahweh look at you, and judge"* (5:21). **The verb is *šāpaṭ*, the *šōpēṭ* of 2:14.** The first Hebrew
asked who had made Moses a *judge*; these ones hand him to the Judge. **The refusal is now in God's
name** — and the God invoked is the one who sent him. Stephen's summary, *"they didn't understand"*
(Acts 7:25), holds for the sent Moses as for the self-appointed one. Moses' answer is not to the
officers but to God: *"Why is it that you have sent me?"* (5:22).

## Significance

- **It makes the rejected deliverer a pattern before it is a prophecy.** Genesis and Exodus both
  tell of a son of Israel refused by his brothers and sent away, who turns out to be the instrument
  of their rescue. Neither text calls it a type; Stephen is the first to read the two together, and
  he reads them toward Jesus.
- **It keeps Moses' failure in the record.** Exodus does not tell the killing as a heroic act and
  does not tell the rebuke as ingratitude only. The man who asked *"Who made you a judge?"* was asking
  a fair question, and the answer — *God did, later* — is the subject of Exodus 3. In
  [[The Burning Bush]] Moses puts the question to himself — *"Who am I, that I should go to
  Pharaoh?"* (3:11) — and God's reply to his fourth objection begins with the same two words as the
  Hebrew's taunt: *mî śāmkā*, *who made you* (2:14), and *mî śām peh lāʾādām*, *"Who made man's
  mouth?"* (4:11). **The one who sets the mouth in the man sets the man in the office.** Stephen calls
  the sending the answer to the refusal: *"This Moses, whom they refused … God has sent him as both a
  ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel who appeared to him in the bush"* (Acts 7:35). See
  [[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]].
- **It sets the human initiative and the divine one side by side.** Moses' attempt (2:11–14) and
  God's turning (2:23–25) frame the same chapter; see [[Moses Flees to Midian]].
