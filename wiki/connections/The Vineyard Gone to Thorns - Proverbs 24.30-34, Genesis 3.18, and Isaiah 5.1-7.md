# The Vineyard Gone to Thorns - Proverbs 24.30-34, Genesis 3.18, and Isaiah 5.1-7

## Passages involved

- [[The Field of the Sluggard]] — **Proverbs 24:30–31** — *"I went by the field of the sluggard, by
  the vineyard of the man void of understanding; Behold, it was all grown over with thorns. Its
  surface was covered with nettles, and its stone wall was broken down."*
- [[The Fall]] — **Genesis 3:17–18** — *"the ground is cursed for your sake. You will eat from it
  with much labor all the days of your life. It will yield thorns and thistles to you"*
- [[A Gentle Answer]] — **Proverbs 15:19** — *"The way of the sluggard is like a thorn patch, but the
  path of the upright is a highway."*
- [[Job's Oath of Innocence]] — **Job 31:40** — *"let briers grow instead of wheat, and stinkweed
  instead of barley."*
- **Isaiah 5:5–6** *(not yet ingested)* — *"I will take away its hedge, and it will be eaten up. I
  will break down its wall, and it will be trampled down. I will lay it a wasteland. It won't be
  pruned nor hoed, but it will grow briers and thorns."* (WEB verified at bible-api.com; Isaiah not
  yet in `raw/`)
- **Hebrews 6:7–8** *(not yet ingested)* — *"For the land which has drunk the rain that comes often
  on it, and produces a crop suitable for them for whose sake it is also tilled, receives blessing
  from God; but if it bears thorns and thistles, it is rejected and near being cursed, whose end is to
  be burned."* (WEB verified at bible-api.com)
- Compare [[Dark and Lovely]] — **Song of Solomon 1:6** — *"They made me keeper of the vineyards.
  I haven't kept my own vineyard."* An unkept vineyard with no thorns and no sluggard.
- Compare [[Rise Up, My Love]] — **Song of Solomon 2:15** — *"Catch for us the foxes, the little
  foxes that plunder the vineyards; for our vineyards are in blossom."* A vineyard guarded, not
  neglected.

- [[Our Sister and My Own Vineyard]] — Song of Solomon 8:8–12.

## Analysis

**Thorns as what the ground does when left to itself.** Genesis 3:18 makes thorns the ground's
answer to the curse: the field the man must work will *"yield thorns and thistles"*. Proverbs
24:30–31 shows a field where no one did the work, and the result is the same picture. Proverbs
does not cite Genesis, and the Hebrew words differ — Genesis has *qôṣ wəḏardar*, Proverbs
*qimmaśōnîm* and *ḥărullîm* — so the wiki claims no allusion. What the two share is a view of land:
cultivated ground is held against a tendency, and the tendency is thorns. Job 31:40 invokes the same
outcome as a self-curse — briers instead of wheat if he has wronged his land — and Proverbs 15:19
turns it into a figure for the sluggard's whole way of life. (Hebrew of Genesis 3:18 and Proverbs
15:19; 24:31 read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-26.)

**The broken wall.** Proverbs 24:31 ends on *wəḡeḏer ʾăḇānāyw nehĕrāsāh*, *its wall of stones was
torn down*. Isaiah's song of the vineyard uses the same noun, *gāḏēr*, with a different verb:
Yahweh says *pārōṣ gəḏērô*, *break down its wall*, and the vineyard will grow *šāmîr wāšāyiṯ*,
*briers and thorns* (Isaiah 5:5–6, Hebrew read from Sefaria). The picture is the same — a walled
vineyard, the wall down, thorns in — but the cause is opposite. In Proverbs no one acted, and the
wall fell; in Isaiah Yahweh acts, after his vineyard *"yielded wild grapes"* (5:2, 4), and the
vineyard is *"the house of Israel"* (5:7). Proverbs observes neglect; Isaiah pronounces judgment
on a people who had everything done for them. Isaiah's *hedge*, *məśûkkāṯô*, is written with the same consonants as the
*məśuḵaṯ* of Proverbs 15:19, where the sluggard's way is *like a hedge of thorns* (WEB *"thorn
patch"*). The wiki notes that the two vineyards are built from
the same agricultural material and does not claim either text knows the other.

**Fruit and thorns.** Hebrews 6:7–8 uses the same contrast — land that bears a useful crop *"receives
blessing"*, land that bears *"thorns and thistles"* is *"near being cursed"* — as a warning about
the fruit of a life. Its phrase *thorns and thistles* corresponds to Genesis 3:18's pair in the WEB;
whether the Greek follows the Septuagint of Genesis 3:18 was not checked in this pass.

**A neglected vineyard that is not a moral lesson.** Song of Solomon 1:6 also has a vineyard that
was not kept, and it reverses the Proverbs reading. The woman's own vineyard went untended because
her brothers *"made me keeper of the vineyards"*, meaning other people's. The cause is assigned
labor, not sloth, and no thorns are mentioned. The comparison is a caution against reading every
unkept field in Scripture as the sluggard's. Whether *"my own vineyard"* is also a figure for the
woman herself is debated and is not settled in that unit. See [[Work]].

**A vineyard in blossom, and something small getting in.** Song 2:15 adds a different threat. The
danger is not thorns from neglect or a wall broken in judgment, but *"the little foxes"* that spoil
vineyards in blossom, so someone is asked to catch them. The vineyards there are *"our
vineyards"*, worth protecting because they are flowering. Isaiah's *"It won't be pruned"* (5:6) uses
*yizzāmēr*, from the *z-m-r* root that may also stand behind the Song's *"time of the singing"*
(2:12; Hebrew read from Sefaria). That shared root is a lexical note, not a claim of allusion.

In [[Our Sister and My Own Vineyard]], the own vineyard returns beside Solomon’s leased
estate (8:11–12). Unlike the complaint in 1:6, the first-person voice asserts its
presence before her. This extends the personal-agency reading, without explaining
what happened to the previously neglected vineyard or attributing her neglect to sloth.
Source for this addition: stored WEB Song 1:6; 4:12–16; 8:7–12.

## Significance

Across these passages, thorns are what the ground produces when no fruit is produced: the curse's
default in Genesis, the sluggard's harvest in Proverbs, Job's imagined penalty, the judged vineyard
in Isaiah, the warning in Hebrews. Proverbs 24 is the plainest of them, and the most domestic: no
curse is pronounced, no prophet speaks, and the passer-by simply learns from what he sees. That
plainness is the point of the wisdom tradition — the same truth Isaiah applies to a nation can be
read off a neighbour's field. See [[Work]], [[Curse]], and
[[Go to the Ant - Proverbs 6.6-11, 24.30-34, and 30.25]].
