# Mouth to Mouth - Numbers 12.6-8, Exodus 33, and Deuteronomy 34

## Passages involved

- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:6–8; a vision and a dream for a prophet, *mouth to mouth* for Moses, and *he shall see Yahweh's form*
- [[Show Me Your Glory]] — Exodus 33:11, 20, 23; *face to face*, then *You cannot see my face*
- [[Hear the Statutes and Remember Horeb]] and [[No Form Seen and No Image Made]] — Deuteronomy 4:12, 15; *you saw no form* (WEB)
- [[A Prophet Like Moses]] — Deuteronomy 18:15, 18; *a prophet from among you, of your brothers, like me*; *like you*. The likeness named is the words in his mouth (18). See also [[A Prophet Like Me - Deuteronomy 18.15-19 in John, Acts, and the Transfiguration]]
- [[The Death of Moses]] — Deuteronomy 34:10; *there has not arisen a prophet in Israel like Moses, whom Yahweh knew face to face* (stored WEB). Now ingested; the MAM text at Sefaria confirms *pānîm ʾel-pānîm* in this verse.

## Analysis

**Four statements of how Moses meets God.**

| Text | Wording | What is said |
|---|---|---|
| Exodus 33:11 | *pānîm ʾel-pānîm* | Yahweh spoke to him, *as a man speaks to his friend* |
| Exodus 33:20, 23 | *lōʾ tûkal lirʾōt ʾet-pānāy* (33:20) | He cannot see the face; he will see the back |
| Numbers 12:8 | *peh ʾel-peh*, *ûtəmunat yhwh yabbîṭ* | Mouth to mouth, and he looks on the form |
| Deuteronomy 34:10 | *pānîm ʾel-pānîm* | Yahweh knew him so, and no later prophet is like him |

**The tension is real, but it is narrower than it looks.** Exodus 33 itself holds *face to face* and *You cannot see my face* in one chapter, so the phrase may describe directness and not sight. Numbers 12 points the same way, since *mouth to mouth* is said of speech.
But it adds a clause about seeing, and *təmûnâ* is the word Deuteronomy 4:12, 15 says Israel did not see. Rahlfs renders Numbers 12:8 with *τὴν δόξαν κυρίου*, which moves toward Exodus 33:18's request and keeps within what that chapter allows. The Hebrew does not ease the point that way.

**The phrase elsewhere.** By the WLC tags, *pānîm ʾel-pānîm* is used at Genesis 32:30 by Jacob, at Exodus 33:11, at Deuteronomy 34:10, at Judges 6:22 by Gideon, and at Ezekiel 20:35 of judgment. So it is not exclusive to Moses, and Jacob and Gideon both speak of it as something that could have killed them. *Peh ʾel-peh* for speech is, by the tags, only Numbers 12:8.

**Like, and not like.** Numbers 12 says prophets are not like Moses. Deuteronomy 18 says that a prophet like him will come. Deuteronomy 34 says that none has yet. The three fit if the promise is still open at the end of Deuteronomy, and the last chapter's *Since then* may imply exactly that, but no text states how they fit together.

## Significance

Numbers 12 makes the claim the rest of the Torah leans on: the law's mediator has a different access to God from every other prophet. It does this without resolving what he saw.
