# From the Filled Tent to the Speaking Tent - Exodus 40 and Leviticus 1

## Passages involved

- [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]] — Exodus 40:34–35.
- [[The Burnt Offering]] — Leviticus 1:1–5.
- [[The Ordination of the Priests]] — Exodus 29:42–46, the promise of meeting and speech.

## Analysis

Exodus 40:35 says Moses cannot enter the Tent of Meeting because the cloud stays on it
and Yahweh’s glory fills it. Leviticus 1:1 then says Yahweh calls Moses and speaks from
the tent. The narrative moves from a dwelling filled with presence to instruction given
from that dwelling. Exodus 29:42 had already connected the tent door, the continual burnt
offering, and divine meeting and speech.

The reports must retain their limits. Leviticus does not say that Moses has entered,
that the cloud has lifted, or that a sacrifice has removed the obstacle of Exodus 40.
The cattle offerer is brought to the door for acceptance (1:3–4), and the altar stands
there (5). Accepted worship is described without unrestricted access to the interior.
No contradiction requires inventing an intervening event: inability to enter at one
moment and hearing speech from the tent are different claims.

## Significance

The two books belong to one sanctuary narrative. Construction and filling lead into
instruction about approach; the dwelling is neither silent nor made freely accessible
merely by its presence. The connection preserves both nearness and limits.
