# The Nazirite and the Priest - Numbers 6 and Leviticus 8, 10, and 21

## Passages involved

- [[The Nazirite Vow]] — Numbers 6:1–21.
- [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]] — Leviticus 10:8–11, no wine at the Tent.
- [[The Holiness of the Priests]] — Leviticus 21:1–12, the dead, and the crown.
- [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]] — Leviticus 8:9, 25–29, the crown and the hands.
- Ezekiel 44:20–21 — priests, locks, and wine *(WEB, bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

The Nazirite is a lay person, and this page does not make the Nazirite a priest. There is no
anointing, no vestment, and no duty at the altar. But four features of the law run along the lines of
priestly law.

| | Ordinary priest | High priest | Nazirite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wine | not when going into the Tent (Leviticus 10:9) | same | none for the whole term, and nothing from the vine (Numbers 6:3–4) |
| The dead | may defile himself for near kin (21:2–3) | not even father or mother (21:11) | not for father, mother, brother, or sister (6:7) |
| On the head | — | *nēzer*, the crown (Exodus 29:6; Leviticus 21:12) | *nēzer*, WEB *separation* (6:7) |
| Hair | not to shave the head (21:5) | not to let it hang loose (21:10) | no razor, and locks grow long (6:5) |

**Wine.** The priest's rule depends on being on duty. The Nazirite's depends on the calendar of the vow.

**The dead.** The wording is close: *ʿal-nepeš mēt lōʾ yābōʾ* (Numbers 6:6) and *ʿal kol-napšōt mēt lōʾ
yābōʾ* (Leviticus 21:11; both WLC). The high priest and the Nazirite stand together here, and the reason
given is alike: for one, the crown of anointing oil *is upon him* (21:12); for the other, the *nēzer* of
his God *is on his head* (6:7).

**The crown.** All four uses of *nēzer* in the priestly law are the high priest's, and the thirteen in
Numbers 6 are the Nazirite's condition. WEB rightly translates by context, and that hides the link. This
page does not claim that *nēzer* in Numbers 6 means *crown*, only that the same noun is used and that
the shared phrase *on him* gives a reader reason to hear it.

**The hands.** Numbers 6:19 and Leviticus 8:27 (with Exodus 29:24) use *kappayim* of bread and meat
placed on palms for a wave offering; by Strong's tags (WLC) they are the only verses that join the two
words. In Leviticus 8 this installs people in an office, and it is burned. In Numbers 6 it ends a term,
and the priest receives it. The likeness in the gesture is real, and the meaning of the rite differs.

**Hair runs the other way.** Priests may neither shave nor let their locks grow (Ezekiel 44:20, with
*peraʿ*, tagged only there and in Numbers 6:5; Leviticus 21:5). The Nazirite's long hair is what the
priest may not have. So this is not a simple copy of priestly law.

## Significance

Israel was called *a kingdom of priests* at Exodus 19:6, while only one family served at the altar. The
Nazirite law shows one way an ordinary Israelite could take on, for a time, a holiness as strict as the
high priest's in some respects, with no access to anything priests alone may do. That reading is the
wiki's; the law itself does not cite Leviticus or Exodus 19. See
[[A Kingdom of Priests - Exodus 19 and First Peter 2]], [[Priesthood]], and [[Holiness]].
