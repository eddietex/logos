# Not Good to Be Alone - Genesis 2.18 and Ecclesiastes 4.8-12

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 2:18** — *"Yahweh God said, 'It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make him a
  helper comparable to him.'"* — [[The Garden of Eden]]
- **Ecclesiastes 4:8** — *"There is one who is alone, and he has neither son nor brother."* —
  [[Two Are Better Than One]]
- **Ecclesiastes 4:9–12** — *"Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their
  labor."* (9); *"woe to him who is alone when he falls"* (10); *"a threefold cord is not quickly
  broken"* (12) — [[Two Are Better Than One]]

## Analysis

**The same judgment, reached by different routes.** Genesis 2:18 is God's verdict, spoken before any
human being has been lonely or failed: the first thing in creation called *not good*. Ecclesiastes 4
reaches a comparable judgment from below, by observation — a man who labors endlessly with no one to
labor for (8), and three practical advantages of two over one (10–12). One is a divine declaration at
the beginning; the other is a wise man's finding under the sun.

**Different remedies.** Genesis answers solitude with the making of the woman, a *"helper comparable to
him"* (2:18, 20), and the passage closes with the man and his wife as one flesh (2:24). Ecclesiastes
names son and brother as what the solitary man lacks (4:8) and speaks of a *fellow* who lifts the one
who falls (4:10). It does not mention a wife. Verse 11's lying together for warmth is frequently
applied to marriage, but the text does not make that application, and verse 12's defence against an
attacker suits companions generally. Reading Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 as a marriage text imports Genesis 2
into it; reading Genesis 2:18 as about friendship in general drops the specific answer God gives there.
The wiki keeps both distinct.

**No claim of shared vocabulary.** Ecclesiastes 4 does not cite Genesis 2. The Hebrew of Ecclesiastes
4:8, 10 speaks of *ʾeḥāḏ*, *one*, without a *šēnî*, *second* (Mechon-Mamre's Masoretic text,
Ecclesiastes 4, read 2026-10-27). The Hebrew of Genesis 2:18 was not checked for this page, and no
claim is made that the two passages share their word for *alone*. The link is thematic.

**Labor as the shared setting.** Both passages place solitude beside work. The man of Genesis 2:15 is
put in the garden *"to cultivate and keep it"* before the verdict of 2:18; the man of Ecclesiastes 4:8
has *"no end to all of his labor"*. Ecclesiastes 4:9 gives the advantage of two in terms of labor's
reward. Neither passage treats companionship only as a feeling; both tie it to shared work and help.

## Significance

The comparison shows the Preacher affirming, from experience, a good that the creation narrative
states as God's judgment, while marking that Ecclesiastes widens the circle from the first couple to
any companion. It connects [[Brotherhood]], [[Marriage]], [[Work]], and [[Creation]].
