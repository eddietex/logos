# The Contentious Woman - Proverbs 19.13, 21.9, 19, 25.24, and 27.15

## Analysis

**A repeated formula.** Proverbs 21:9 is repeated word for word in the WEB at 25:24, inside the
collection copied by Hezekiah's men. The book's editors kept the duplicate, so the saying belonged
to more than one collection. Two images recur: being driven out of the house — to a housetop corner
(21:9; 25:24) or a desert (21:19) — and a dripping that never stops (19:13; 27:15).

**From whose side, and what it does not say.** The sayings speak from a husband's viewpoint and
describe a household made unlivable by constant quarrel. They are *better-than* sayings (see
[[Better Is Little - The Better-Than Sayings of Proverbs]]): they rank lonely discomfort above
domestic strife, not women below men. Proverbs also names a *contentious man* who kindles strife
(26:21), calls a prudent wife Yahweh's gift (19:14), and ends with the worthy woman of chapter 31.
The book's warnings against quarrelling apply to everyone; these sayings apply them to one setting.

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 19:13–14; 21:9, 19; 25:24; 26:21; 27:15. 25:24 and 27:15 were also
checked at bible-api.com (2026-10-26). No claim about Hebrew vocabulary is made, including whether
the words rendered *contentious* are the same in every verse.

## Significance

The repetition shows how the sentence collections were assembled: a saying could circulate and be
recorded twice. See [[Marriage]] and [[Strife and Peacemaking]].
