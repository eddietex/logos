# The Contentious Woman - Proverbs 19.13, 21.9, 19, 25.24, and 27.15

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 19:13** — *"A foolish son is the calamity of his father. A wife's quarrels are a
  continual dripping."* — [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]]
- **Proverbs 21:9** — *"It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop, than to share a house
  with a contentious woman."* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Proverbs 21:19** — *"It is better to dwell in a desert land, than with a contentious and
  fretful woman."* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Proverbs 25:24** — *"It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop, than to share a house
  with a contentious woman."* — [[The Glory of Kings]]
- **Proverbs 27:15** — *"A continual dropping on a rainy day and a contentious wife are alike"*
  *(stored WEB, verified also at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- **Proverbs 26:21** — *"As coals are to hot embers, and wood to fire, so is a contentious man to
  kindling strife."* — [[Answer a Fool]]
- **Proverbs 19:14** — *"a prudent wife is from Yahweh."* —
  [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]]

## Analysis

**A repeated formula.** Proverbs 21:9 is repeated word for word in the WEB at 25:24, inside the
collection copied by Hezekiah's men. The book's editors kept the duplicate, so the saying belonged
to more than one collection. Two images recur: being driven out of the house — to a housetop corner
(21:9; 25:24) or a desert (21:19) — and a dripping that never stops (19:13; 27:15).

**From whose side, and what it does not say.** The sayings speak from a husband's viewpoint and
describe a household made unlivable by constant quarrel. They are *better-than* sayings (see
[[Better Is Little - The Better-Than Sayings of Proverbs]]): they rank lonely discomfort above
domestic strife, not women below men. Proverbs also names a *contentious man* who kindles strife
(26:21), calls a prudent wife Yahweh's gift (19:14), and ends with the worthy woman of chapter 31.
The book's warnings against quarrelling apply to everyone; these sayings apply them to one setting.

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 19:13–14; 21:9, 19; 25:24; 26:21; 27:15. 25:24 and 27:15 were also
checked at bible-api.com (2026-10-26). No claim about Hebrew vocabulary is made, including whether
the words rendered *contentious* are the same in every verse.

### The duplicate in Hebrew — Proverbs 25:24

With 25:24 ingested, the Hebrew of the two sayings was compared in Sefaria's *Miqra according to the
Masorah* (read 2026-10-26). They differ by a single letter: 21:9 has *ṭôḇ lāšeḇeṯ*, *better to
dwell*, and 25:24 *ṭôḇ šeḇeṯ*, *better dwelling*; the rest — *ʿal-pinnaṯ-gāḡ mēʾēšeṯ midyānîm ûḇêṯ
ḥāḇer* — is the same, including the noun the WEB renders *contentious*. (25:24 is written *mdwnym* and
read *midyānîm*.) The earlier caveat about the other verses' vocabulary still stands. In chapter 25
the saying follows *"a backbiting tongue brings an angry face"* (25:23), so it sits in a run on speech
that sours a household and a neighbourhood. See [[The Glory of Kings]].

### The contentious man — Proverbs 26:21

With chapter 26 ingested, its *contentious man* was read in the same Hebrew edition (2026-10-26):
*wəʾîš midyānîm ləḥarḥar-rîḇ*, written *mdwnym* and read *midyānîm*, exactly as at 25:24. The
*contentious man* of 26:21 and the *contentious woman* of 25:24 thus share the noun and the same
written-and-read variant, three verses' distance apart in the collection of Hezekiah's men. The image
around him is fire — coals to embers, wood to fire — following *"For lack of wood a fire goes out.
Without gossip, a quarrel dies down"* (26:20). What the woman's sayings put as a household made
unlivable, the man's puts as a fire kept burning. The caveat about the vocabulary of 19:13, 21:19, and
27:15 still stands. See [[Answer a Fool]].

## Significance

The repetition shows how the sentence collections were assembled: a saying could circulate and be
recorded twice. See [[Marriage]] and [[Strife and Peacemaking]].
