# Bound to Be Handed Over - The Men of Judah and Delilah

## Passages involved

- [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]] — **Judges 15:10–14** — *We have come up to bind Samson*
  (10); *We have come down to bind you, that we may deliver you into the hand of the Philistines* (12);
  *They bound him with two new ropes* (13); *the ropes that were on his arms became as flax that was
  burned with fire* (14). Stored WEB.
- [[Samson and Delilah]] — **Judges 16:5–13** — *that we may bind him to afflict him* (16:5); *If they only bind me with new ropes
  with which no work has been done, then shall I become weak* (16:11); *So Delilah took new ropes, and
  bound him therewith … He broke them off his arms like a thread* (16:12). Stored WEB.
- [[Samson and Delilah]] — **Judges 16:21** — *The Philistines laid hold on him, and put out his eyes; and they brought him down to
  Gaza, and bound him with fetters of brass*. Stored WEB.

## Analysis

**One verb, two chapters.** *ʾĀsar*, *bind* (lemma 631), occurs in Judges only in chapters 15 and 16 (WLC):
five times in the men of Judah's scene (15:10, 12, and three times in 13), and twelve times in chapter
16 (16:5–13, twice in 21, and a participle in 25, where the WEB has *the prison*). Samson's last two
contests with the Philistines are told as a series of bindings.

**The same new ropes.** *ʿĂbōtîm ḥădāšîm*, *new ropes* (15:13; 16:11, 12, WLC, lemma 5688), occurs in
Judges only in these verses. In both scenes the ropes are on his arms, *zərôʿōtāyw* (15:14; 16:12, WLC),
and in both they fail: *as flax that was burned with fire* (15:14), *like a thread* (16:12).

| | Judges 15 | Judges 16 |
|---|---|---|
| who binds | three thousand men of Judah | Delilah, for the lords of the Philistines |
| why | *that we may deliver you into the hand of the Philistines* (15:12) | *that we may bind him to afflict him*, for silver (16:5) |
| Samson's part | asks only for an oath not to be killed (15:12) | tells her what will bind or weaken him (16:7, 11, 13, 17) |
| the ropes fail | *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him* (15:14) | no Spirit is named; *He broke them* (16:12) |
| the last binding | — | *fetters of brass*, after *Yahweh had departed from him* (16:20–21) |

**Who hands him over.** In chapter 15 his own people do it, openly, after asking *Don't you know that the
Philistines are rulers over us?* (15:11). In chapter 16 a woman he loves does it, secretly, for money.
Both deliver him *into the hand of the Philistines*, and both times the binding is the Philistines' plan
carried out by someone else's hands. The first time it ends in a thousand Philistine dead; the last time
in his blinding.

**The difference is the Spirit.** At Lehi the ropes melt when *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him*. In
chapter 16 the ropes break with no mention of the Spirit, and when the hair is gone *he didn't know that
Yahweh had departed from him* (16:20). Then the binding holds.

## Significance

- **Israel's part in Samson's capture.** The Philistines never take Samson by force. Judah binds him, and
  Delilah binds him. The book that began with Judah going up first against the Canaanites (1:1–2) shows
  Judah binding its own deliverer for the Philistines. See [[Judah]] and
  [[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].
- **A rehearsal.** Chapter 15 is the binding that fails; chapter 16 repeats it, with the same new ropes,
  until it does not fail. The wiki notes the pattern; Judges does not comment on it.
