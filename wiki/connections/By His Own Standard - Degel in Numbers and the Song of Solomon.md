# By His Own Standard - Degel in Numbers and the Song of Solomon

## Passages involved

- [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]] — Numbers 1:52, *every man by his own standard*
- [[The Camp Around the Tent]] — Numbers 2:2–34, eight of the occurrences
- Numbers 10:14, 18, 22, 25 — the four standards on the march *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)*
- [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]] — Song of Solomon 2:4, *His banner over me is love*
- [[Altogether Lovely]] — Song of Solomon 5:10, the verb
- [[My Dove Is Unique]] — Song of Solomon 6:4, 10, *awesome as an army with banners*
- Psalm 20:5 (WLC 20:6) — *we will set up our banners* *(not yet ingested; WEB verified at bible-api.com)*

## Analysis

**The noun is almost confined to one book.** By the Strong's tags of the WLC, across all thirty-nine
books, *degel* occurs fourteen times: thirteen in Numbers, and once at Song of Solomon 2:4. The verb
tagged with it, *dāgal*, occurs four times: Psalm 20:6 (WEB 20:5), Song of Solomon 5:10, 6:4, and
6:10. WEB has *standard* in Numbers and *banner* in the Song.

**So the love poem is the only other place the noun appears.** Wherever the reader has met it before
Song of Solomon 2:4, in canonical order, it was in this camp. *His banner over me* may then have a
military color, and the army of Song of Solomon 6:4 and 10 uses the verb. This does not settle what
the Song's line pictures. One noun does not make an allusion, the poem names no camp, and the verb's
sense in the Song is debated in ways not checked here: WEB's *army with banners* is an interpretive
rendering of a participle, not a noun meaning *army*.

**In Numbers the word is about belonging.** *Every man by his own standard* (1:52; 2:2) makes it a
place to stand. In the Song it is what is over a person. The comparison is only worth this much: both
uses have someone located under something that marks them.

## Significance

A small word study that keeps the Song's *banner* in view for readers of Numbers, and the reverse,
without claiming that either passage is quoting the other. See [[War]] and [[Love]].
