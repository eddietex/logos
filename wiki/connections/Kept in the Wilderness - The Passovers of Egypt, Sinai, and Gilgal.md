# Kept in the Wilderness - The Passovers of Egypt, Sinai, and Gilgal

## Passages involved
- [[The Second Circumcision at Gilgal]] — Joshua 5:1–9.

- [[The Passover]] — Exodus 12:1–28; the first, and the phrase *when you have come to the land* (25)
- [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] — Exodus 13:5; *when Yahweh shall bring you into the land*
- [[The Passover in the Second Year]] — Numbers 9:1–5; kept at Sinai
- [[The Stages of the Journey]] — Numbers 33:3; the departure after Passover.
- [[Passover at Gilgal and the End of the Manna]] — Joshua 5:10–12; Passover and the end of manna.

## Analysis

The Torah and Joshua narrate three Passovers before the kings: in Egypt, at Sinai, and at Gilgal. In the
tags, *pesaḥ* appears between Numbers 9 and Joshua 5 only in law or memory (Numbers 28:16; 33:3; Deuteronomy
16). A rite not narrated is not proved unkept. But the gap is about the length of the wilderness.

Exodus twice ties the service to the land (12:25; 13:5). Numbers 9:2 orders it in the wilderness, with no
comment on those verses. Either the land sentences set a permanent start without barring an earlier keeping,
or Sinai is an exception. The text does not choose.

Joshua 5 gives a reason to think about the gap. *All the people who were born in the wilderness along the way
... had not been circumcised* (5:5, WEB). They are circumcised at Gilgal, and then *They kept the Passover on
the fourteenth day of the month at evening* (5:10, WEB). Since Exodus 12:48 says *no uncircumcised person
shall eat of it*, the order in Joshua makes sense. If the generation born in the wilderness was uncircumcised
until then, they could not have kept it under that rule. Joshua 5 does not draw this conclusion, so it stays
an inference. The Sinai Passover falls before that generation mattered, in the second year, when those who
came out were circumcised (Joshua 5:5).

### Circumcision at Gilgal

Joshua 5:1–9 is now ingested: the children are circumcised and the camp waits for healing.
This supplies the immediate setting for the Passover in 5:10, now ingested with 5:11–12. The wilderness omission is stated; its cause and any complete history of Passover
observance during those years are not supplied.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 5:1–10.

### Food in the land

[[Passover at Gilgal and the End of the Manna]] completes the sequence: Passover, eating produce, and
then the cessation of manna. Exodus 12:25’s landward expectation now has an explicit
narrative setting. The feast persists while the food supply changes; see
[[From Manna to the Produce of Canaan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 5:10–12 and Exodus 12:25.

## Significance

The three Passovers mark the start, the camp, and the land. Each sits at a threshold: leaving Egypt, leaving
Sinai, entering Canaan. That pattern is the wiki's reading. See [[Festivals and First Fruits]],
[[The Wilderness]], [[Circumcision]], and [[Land and Sojourning]].
