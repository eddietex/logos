# The Same Judgment on Israel - Leviticus 18 and Numbers 33

## Passages involved

- [[Forbidden Unions and the Defiled Land]] — Leviticus 18:24–30.
- [[Drive Out the Inhabitants]] — Numbers 33:50–56.

## Analysis

Leviticus 18 attributes the inhabitants’ expulsion to the land’s defilement and warns Israel
that the land can expel it also (24–28). Numbers 33 commands removal of inhabitants and their
cult objects, then warns that failure will bring harassment and the reversal of Israel’s
position (52–56). In each text the people receiving the land stand under judgment as well.

The grounds and images differ. Leviticus closes a set of prohibited practices with the image
of a land vomiting out its inhabitants. Numbers ends an entry-and-allotment command with
thorns, harassment, and Yahweh’s first-person threat. These are parallel warnings, not the
same speech or proof of direct literary borrowing. Neither passage narrates the later exile.

The tension is between the land given (Numbers 33:53) and the recipients themselves threatened
(33:56). The gift is real within the narrative; immunity from the giver’s judgment is not
promised. Reading the gift as permanent security regardless of conduct contradicts the warning.

Sources: `raw/bible/Leviticus.md`, chapter 18, and `raw/bible/Numbers.md`, chapter 33 (WEB).

## Significance

The conquest warning also addresses Israel. These texts do not divide humanity into a people
capable only of receiving judgment and a people incapable of receiving it. Their different
accounts of disobedience converge on the possibility of losing the land.
