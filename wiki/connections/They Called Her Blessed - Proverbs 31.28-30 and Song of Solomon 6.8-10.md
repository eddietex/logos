# They Called Her Blessed - Proverbs 31.28-30 and Song of Solomon 6.8-10

## Passages involved

- [[The Worthy Woman]] — Proverbs 31:28–31; children call her blessed, her husband praises
  her above many women, and fear of Yahweh supplies the concluding criterion.
- [[My Dove Is Unique]] — Song of Solomon 6:8–10; the unique beloved among many women is called blessed
  and praised by daughters, queens, and concubines.

## Analysis

Both poems move from a woman's distinction among others to acknowledgment spoken about her.
In the stored WEB, Proverbs 31:28 says her children call her blessed and her husband praises
her; verse 29 ranks her above many women. Song 6:8 counts queens and concubines before 6:9
singles out the beloved and reports other women's praise. The correspondence is in the
English text's pattern of many women, one exceptional woman, and spoken recognition.
This page makes no claim of identical Hebrew wording, literary dependence, or direct quotation.

The speakers and grounds differ. Proverbs closes a portrait of work, care, wise speech,
and provision with praise from children and husband and recognition at the gates. Song
presents the lover's bodily praise and the admiration of other women. The mother in Song
6:9 supplies the beloved's family relation; she is not said to utter the public praise.
The daughters are not called the beloved's own children. Those distinctions prevent the
two scenes from becoming the same household story.

**Tension in the valuation of beauty.** Proverbs 31:30 explicitly calls charm deceitful and
beauty vain, then praises the woman who fears Yahweh. Song 6:4–10 celebrates beauty in city,
animal, fruit, and celestial images, without describing the woman's religious conduct.
The Song is not silently rewritten as praise of virtue, and Proverbs is not rewritten as
an unqualified endorsement of physical beauty. The two can be read together as desire's
praise beside wisdom's warning about what can ground lasting esteem. That is an
interpretive synthesis; their different emphases remain in the source texts.

## Significance

Praise can recognize the incomparable worth of a person without reducing her to rank or
possessions. The Song also gives the women who might be imagined as rivals a voice of
admiration; rivalry itself is not narrated. Proverbs requires acknowledgment of the
woman's work and fear of Yahweh. See [[Praise and Song]], [[Blessing]], [[Love]], and
[[Seeing and Being Seen]].
