# Do Not Be Afraid, You Have a Son - Rachel and Phinehas' Wife

## Passages involved

- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:16–19
- [[The Ark Is Captured]] — 1 Samuel 4:19–22

## Analysis

Two women die in childbirth in the books stored so far with a scene described in detail, and the two
scenes share their words.

| | Genesis 35 | 1 Samuel 4 |
|---|---|---|
| The labor | *She had hard labor* (16) | *she bowed herself and gave birth; for her pains came on her* (19) |
| The comfort | the midwife: *Don't be afraid, for now you will have another son* (17) | the women who stood by her: *Don't be afraid; for you have given birth to a son* (20) |
| Hebrew (WLC) | *ʾal-tîrʾî kî-ḡam-zeh lāḵ bēn* | *ʾal-tîrʾî kî ḇēn yālāḏt* |
| The death | *As her soul was departing (for she died)* (18) | *About the time of her death* (20) |
| The name | *she named him Benoni* (18) | *She named the child Ichabod* (21) |
| After | *but his father named him Benjamin* (18) | the father, Phinehas, is already dead (11) |

Source: stored WEB Genesis 35:16–19; 1 Samuel 4:11, 19–22. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-17.

**The comfort refused.** Both mothers are told the same thing, and neither takes it as comfort. Rachel
answers with a name; Phinehas' wife *didn't answer, neither did she regard it* (*wəlōʾ šāṯâ libbāh*, *did
not set her heart*, WLC) before she names him. The birth of a son, which elsewhere in these books ends
barrenness and grief (Hannah, 1 Samuel 1:20), is here outweighed by what has happened around it.

**The name.** Each mother names the child for her loss. *Benoni* is usually glossed *son of my sorrow*
(conventional gloss; the Genesis page discusses it); *Ichabod* carries *kāḇôḏ*, *glory*, and its own
explanation, *The glory has departed from Israel* (21). Rachel's grief is her own; the second mother's is
Israel's, and her final sentence names only the ark (22). Jacob overrides the name; Ichabod has no father
left to rename him, and the name stands.

**What the text does not say.** 1 Samuel does not cite Genesis, and the shared words are formulaic
reassurance that a midwife might give anywhere. The parallel is a reader's, made on the strength of the
identical opening and the identical sequence of labor, comfort, death, and naming.

## Significance

- The scene of 1 Samuel 4 ends the house of Eli the way the last birth of the patriarchs ended Rachel's
  life: a son is gained as the mother is lost, and the name keeps the loss.
