## Analysis

**Chapters 7 and 8 are set side by side on purpose, and chapter 9 makes the pairing explicit.** The
father ends chapter 7 on the strange woman's house as *"the way to Sheol"* (7:27); chapter 8 opens
with *"Doesn't wisdom cry out?"* (8:1). Read together, the two speeches answer each other point for
point. Hebrew from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*; English from the stored WEB.

| | the strange woman (Proverbs 7) | Wisdom (Proverbs 8) |
|---|---|---|
| **where** | *"now in the streets, now in the squares, and lurking at every corner"* (7:12) | *"On the top of high places by the way, where the paths meet, she stands. Beside the gates"* (8:2–3) |
| **when** | *"in the twilight … in the middle of the night and in the darkness"* (7:9) | no hour; in the open |
| **to whom** | one young man *"void of understanding"* (7:7) | *"To you men … to the sons of mankind"* (8:4); the simple and fools (8:5) |
| **her words** | *"persuasive words … the flattering of her lips"* (7:21) | *"There is nothing crooked or perverse in them"* (8:8) |
| **seeking** | *"to diligently seek your face, and I have found you"* (7:15, *ləšaḥēr … wāʾemṣāʾekkā*) | *"Those who seek me diligently will find me"* (8:17, *ûməšaḥăray yimṣāʾunənî*) |
| **love** | *"let's take our fill of loving … solace ourselves with loving"* (7:18, *ʾŏhāḇîm*) | *"I love those who love me"* (8:17, *ʾōhăḇay ʾēhāḇ*) |
| **money** | a husband gone with *"a bag of money"* (7:20) | *"I fill their treasuries"* (8:21) |
| **the end** | *"Her house is the way to Sheol"* (7:27) | *"whoever finds me, finds life"* (8:35, the speech's close) |

**The same places, the same verbs, opposite directions.** Both women are in public. Wisdom's first
speech was *baḥûṣ … bārəḥōḇôṯ*, *in the street … in the squares* (1:20), and the strange woman is in
exactly those two places (7:12). Both *seek* (*šiḥēr*) and *find* (*māṣāʾ*), but the strange woman
does the seeking, and Wisdom is the one sought. Both speak of love, but the strange woman's is a
night's *loving*, and Wisdom's is a lasting reciprocal love. The strange woman lurks (*ʾāraḇ*, 7:12,
the gang's verb at 1:11, 18); Wisdom stands (*niṣṣāḇâ*, 8:2).

**Chapter 9 turns the pairing into two portraits with one script.** Wisdom *"cries from the highest
places of the city"* (9:3, *mərōmê qāreṯ*); the foolish woman *"sits at the door of her house, on a
seat in the high places of the city"* (9:14, the same phrase). Both say *"Whoever is simple, let him
turn in here!"* and both address *"him who is void of understanding"* (9:4, 16) — the phrase that
named the young man of 7:7. The foolish woman is *loud* (9:13, *hōmiyyâ*), the word for the strange
woman at 7:11. Her guests *"are in the depths of Sheol"* (9:18), where the strange woman's house led.
The two figures of chapters 7–8 become Wisdom and Folly at their two tables.

**What the pairing does.** The son cannot tell the two apart by where they stand or how they talk —
both are public, both invite, both speak of love and of finding. He can tell them apart by what they
say and where their houses lead. That is why the lectures insist on keeping the father's words in the
heart (4:23; 7:1–3): the son will hear two voices from the same height, and only what he already
holds will tell him which one to answer.

**Update, Proverbs 8:22–36 ingested.** The second half of Wisdom's speech finishes the answer. The
father warned *"Don't come near the door of her house"* (5:8, *petaḥ bêṯāh*); Wisdom blesses the man
*"watching daily at my gates, waiting at my door posts"* (8:34). She closes with the father's own
formula, *wəʿattâ ḇānîm šimʿû-lî*, *"Now therefore, my sons, listen to me"* (8:32), the words that
opened his conclusion against the strange woman at 7:24, and sets the two ends side by side: *"whoever
finds me, finds life"* (8:35), *"All those who hate me love death"* (8:36). Chapter 7 ended in
*"the rooms of death"* (7:27); chapter 8 ends on *death* as the thing Wisdom's haters love. Folly in
9:14 will sit *"at the door of her house"* (*ləp̄eṯaḥ bêṯāh*). See [[Wisdom at the Creation]]. Hebrew
of 5:8; 7:24; 8:32, 34–36; 9:14 from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

**Update, Proverbs 9:1–18 ingested.** Chapter 9 completes the pairing, and it does so by copying.
The Hebrew of Folly's call in 9:16 is Wisdom's in 9:4 word for word, with two conjunctions added
(*mî-p̄eṯî yāsur hēnnâ … ḥăsar-lēḇ … ʾāmərâ lô*), and both call from *mərōmê qāreṯ*, *the high places
of the city* (9:3, 14). The difference is everything else:

| | Wisdom (9:1–6) | the foolish woman (9:13–18) |
|---|---|---|
| **posture** | builds, carves, slaughters, mixes, sets, sends | sits, at her door, on a seat |
| **the meal** | *"my bread … the wine which I have mixed"* (9:5) | *"Stolen water … Food eaten in secret"* (9:17) |
| **the end** | *"Leave your simple ways, and live"* (9:6) | *"her guests are in the depths of Sheol"* (9:18) |

The foolish woman is *ʾēšeṯ kəsîlûṯ*, *a woman of folly*, and *pəṯayyûṯ*, *simpleness* (9:13), and she
has the strange woman's marks — *loud* (7:11), at her door (5:8), bound for Sheol (2:18; 5:5; 7:27). The
wiki does not decide whether she is the strange woman under a wider name or a figure of folly that
includes her. Between the two scenes, 9:7–12 says who will answer which: the scoffer hates reproof, the
wise man loves it. **So the prologue's frame ends where this page said it would: two voices, one
script, and only the hearer's heart to tell them apart.** The three endings line up — *"Her house is
the way to Sheol"* (7:27), *"All those who hate me love death"* (8:36), *"her guests are in the depths of
Sheol"* (9:18). See [[The Two Feasts]] and
[[Come, Eat of My Bread - Proverbs 9, Isaiah 55, and the Gospel Banquets]]. Hebrew of 9:1–18 from
Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Proverbs 22 in context

Proverbs 22:14 reduces the strange woman of chapters 2, 5, 7, and 9 to one image: *"The mouth of an
adulteress is a deep pit. He who is under Yahweh's wrath will fall into it."* It is her speech —
the flattering words of 2:16 and 7:21 — that is the pit. The saying puts Yahweh's wrath before the
fall without saying what brought it. Proverbs 23:27 repeats the image (see [[Do Not Envy Sinners]]):
*"For a prostitute is a deep pit; and a wayward wife is a narrow well."*

See [[A Good Name]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 2:16; 7:21; 22:14; 23:27; no new
source-language claim.

### Proverbs 23 in context

Proverbs 23:26–28 compresses the prologue's strange woman into three lines. In Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26), 23:27's *"deep pit"* is *šûḥāh ʿămuqqāh*, the phrase of
22:14; its *"wayward wife"* is *noḵriyyāh*, the *foreign woman* of 2:16; and 23:28's *"lies in wait"*
is *teʾĕrōḇ*, the form 7:12 uses of her *"lurking at every corner"* — the verb the gang used at 1:11
(*"Let's lay in wait for blood"*). The block sets her beside the drunkard (23:29–35), and both are
introduced by the father's *"give me your heart"* (23:26).

See [[Do Not Envy Sinners]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 1:11; 2:16; 7:12; 22:14; 23:26–28.

## Significance

The pairing is the frame of Proverbs 1–9: two women calling in the same streets, one to life and one
to death, and the book's reader as the passer-by both address. It is the background of the New
Testament's personified Wisdom (see
[[Wisdom Sends Prophets - Proverbs 1.20-33, Luke 11.49, and Matthew 23.34-37]]). See [[Wisdom]],
[[Folly]], and [[Sexual Sin]].
