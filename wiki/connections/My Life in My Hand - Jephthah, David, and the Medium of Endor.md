# My Life in My Hand - Jephthah, David, and the Medium of Endor

## Passages involved

- **Judges 12:3** — [[Shibboleth]] — Jephthah to Ephraim: *When I saw that you didn't save me, I put my life in my
  hand, and passed over against the children of Ammon, and Yahweh delivered them into my hand* (stored WEB)
- **1 Samuel 19:5** — [[Jonathan Speaks for David]] — Jonathan to Saul, of David: *he put his life in his hand, and
  struck the Philistine, and Yahweh worked a great victory for all Israel* (stored WEB)
- **1 Samuel 28:21** — the medium at [[Endor]] to Saul: *your servant has listened to your voice, and I have put my
  life in my hand, and have listened to your words which you spoke to me* (stored WEB; not yet ingested)
- **Job 13:14** — [[Though He Slay Me]] — *Why should I take my flesh in my teeth, and put my life in my hand?*
  (stored WEB; Hebrew not checked)

## Analysis

**The idiom is *śîm nep̄eš bǝḵap*, *to set one's soul in one's palm*.** In the Hebrew files checked (`Gen` through
`2Sam` and `Ruth`) it occurs three times: *wāʾāśîmâ napšî bǝḵappî* (Judges 12:3), *wayyāśem ʾeṯ-napšô ḇǝḵappô*
(1 Samuel 19:5), and *wāʾāśîm napšî bǝḵappî* (1 Samuel 28:21) (WLC). Two other verses in those files have *nep̄eš* and
*kap* together, Deuteronomy 28:65 (*the sole of your foot*) and 1 Samuel 25:29 (*the hollow of a sling*), and neither
is the idiom. Job 13:14 has the same English in the WEB; its Hebrew is outside the files checked. The idiom always
names a risk already taken, not one being considered. It is said after the act, to someone who is in the speaker's
debt.

| | **Judges 12:3** | **1 Samuel 19:5** | **1 Samuel 28:21** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **speaker** | Jephthah, of himself | Jonathan, of David | the medium, of herself |
| **hearer** | the men of Ephraim | Saul | Saul |
| **the risk** | crossing against Ammon alone | facing the Philistine | calling up Samuel against Saul's own decree (28:3, 9) |
| **the claim** | *Yahweh delivered them into my hand* | *Yahweh worked a great victory* | *your servant has listened to your voice* |
| **what is asked** | *Why then have you come up … to fight against me?* | *Why then will you sin against innocent blood?* | *listen also to the voice of your servant*, and eat (28:22) |
| **what follows** | war; forty-two thousand Ephraimites die (12:4–6) | Saul swears David's life (19:6) | Saul eats, after refusing (28:23–25) |

(Stored WEB; WLC.)

**Each time, the risk becomes the ground of a claim, and the claim is followed by *why*.** Jephthah and Jonathan both
add the same second clause: the man took the risk, and Yahweh gave the victory. Both then turn on the hearer with
*lāmmâ*, *why* (Judges 12:3; 1 Samuel 19:5, WLC). The one who risked his life is owed something, and the hearer is
proposing the opposite. Jephthah's hearers are coming to fight him; Jonathan's hearer is planning to kill David.

**Saul hears the idiom twice, and both times it is followed by *listen*.** At 19:6 *Saul listened to the voice of
Jonathan*, *bǝqôl*. At 28:21–23 the medium says she *listened to your voice*, *bǝqôlekā*, asks him to *listen also to
the voice of your servant*, and *he listened to their voice* (WLC; stored WEB). The first time, the life put in a hand
is David's, and it is spoken for by someone else. The second time, it is the medium's own, and what she asks of the
king is only that he eat. Between them the book runs from Saul's first spoken order to kill David to his last night
alive.

### What the parallel does not settle

- **Whether the three are related or only idiomatic.** The phrase is too rare in the files checked to count as common,
  and too short to show borrowing. The wiki records a shared idiom, not an allusion.
- **Job 13:14.** The WEB uses the same English, and [[Though He Slay Me]] reads it as the idiom of mortal risk. The
  Hebrew of Job was not checked here, and the wiki does not claim the construction is identical.
- **Whether David *put his life in his hand* is Jonathan's phrase or the narrator's view.** 17:1–54 never uses it; it
  enters only in Jonathan's speech.

## Significance

- **It makes the courage at Elah a legal ground.** The idiom appears when someone has to argue that a risk taken
  creates a claim. Jonathan uses it as evidence that David *has not sinned*. See [[Jonathan Speaks for David]] and
  [[Mediation and Advocacy]].
- **It shows a phrase about deliverance turned toward a king's guest.** Jephthah and David risked their lives against
  Israel's enemies. The medium risked hers against Israel's law, at the request of the king who made it. See [[Saul]].
- **It belongs with the book's language of life held in someone else's hand.** Abigail will say that David's life is
  *bound in the bundle of life with Yahweh*, and his enemies' slung out *as from the hollow of a sling* (25:29, stored
  WEB; not yet ingested), the other verse in the files checked with *nep̄eš* and *kap*. See
  [[His Life Is Bound Up in the Boy's Life - Nephesh Tied to Nephesh]].
