# A Day for a Year - Numbers 14.34 and Ezekiel 4.6

## Passages involved

- [[The People Refuse the Land]] — Numbers 14:33–34
- Ezekiel 4:4–6 — *each day for a year* *(WEB and WLC checked, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

*Yôm laššānâ yôm laššānâ* occurs with its doubling only in these two verses by the WLC tags. Both
have *nāśāʾ ʿāwōn*, *bear iniquity*, and both have forty.

| | Numbers 14 | Ezekiel 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | forty days become forty years | years of iniquity become days |
| Who bears | the people, *you will bear your iniquities* (34) | the prophet, *you shall bear the iniquity* (4–6) |
| The forty | the fathers' sentence | *the house of Judah* (6) |
| The other number | none | *three hundred ninety days* for Israel (5) |

**The conversion runs in opposite directions.** In Numbers a day of sin earns a year of consequence.
In Ezekiel the years are compressed into days of one man's lying on his side. WEB's *each day for a
year* uses the same phrase in the Ezekiel passage, and *years … to be to you a number of days* (5)
shows which way it goes.

**The prophet takes the people's place.** The verb that Numbers 14:18–19 used for Yahweh's forgiving
and 14:33–34 for the people's punishment is used in Ezekiel of a man acting out a burden. The text
does not say that this removes anything from Israel, and it is a sign, not an atonement.

**Later use.** Historicist calculation of prophecy has taken these two verses as a rule that a
prophetic *day* means a year, often in connection with Daniel 9:24's *weeks*. That use is recalled.
Neither verse states a rule of interpretation. Each states a specific assignment.

**Sources:** WEB of Ezekiel and Daniel checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-02; Hebrew from WLC
(morphhb), by tags across all 39 books.

## Significance

- Numbers measures the punishment by the sin, and the correspondence is the point.
- Ezekiel reuses the formula to place the iniquity on a prophet.
