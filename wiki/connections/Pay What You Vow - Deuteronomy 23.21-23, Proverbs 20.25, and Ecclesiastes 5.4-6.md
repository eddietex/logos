# Pay What You Vow - Deuteronomy 23.21-23, Proverbs 20.25, and Ecclesiastes 5.4-6

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 28:20, 22** — *"Jacob vowed a vow, saying, “If God will be with me … then this stone,
  which I have set up for a pillar, will be God’s house. Of all that you will give me I will surely
  give a tenth to you.”"* — [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]]
- **Genesis 35:3** — *"Let us arise, and go up to Bethel. I will make there an altar to God, who
  answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me on the way which I went."* —
  [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]]
- **Deuteronomy 23:21–22** — *"When you vow a vow to Yahweh your God, you shall not be slack to pay
  it; for Yahweh your God will surely require it of you; and it would be sin in you. But if you
  refrain from making a vow, it shall be no sin in you."* — [[Fugitives, Interest, Vows, and a Neighbor's Produce]]
- **Deuteronomy 23:23** — *"You shall observe and do that which has gone out of your lips."*
- [[Vows of Men and Women]] — Numbers 30:2, *"he shall not break his word. He shall do according to all
  that proceeds out of his mouth."* *(stored WEB)*
- [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]] — Leviticus 27:2–13, what a vow costs; it does not call a vow
  rash, but sets the price and bars exchange.
- **Job 22:27** — *"You shall make your prayer to him, and he will hear you. You shall pay your
  vows."* — [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]]
- **Proverbs 20:25** — *"It is a snare to a man to make a rash dedication, then later to consider
  his vows."* — [[Wine Is a Mocker]]
- **Ecclesiastes 5:4–6** — *"When you vow a vow to God, don’t defer to pay it; for he has no
  pleasure in fools. Pay that which you vow. It is better that you should not vow, than that you
  should vow and not pay. Don’t allow your mouth to lead you into sin."* —
  [[Let Your Words Be Few]]

## Analysis

**The same three clauses in law and wisdom.** In the WEB, Deuteronomy 23:21–23 and Ecclesiastes
5:4–6 make the same three points in the same order: when you vow, do not delay payment; not
vowing is no fault; what has gone out of your mouth binds you. Both use the cognate phrase *vow a
vow*. Deuteronomy's sanction is legal — God *"will surely require it of you; and it would be sin
in you."* Ecclesiastes keeps the word *sin* (6) but gives a wisdom motive as well: God *"has no
pleasure in fools"* (4). An unpaid vow is a fool's act before it is a breach.

**Proverbs moves the decision earlier.** Proverbs 20:25 does not address payment at all. Its snare
is the order of operations: dedicating first and considering afterward. Ecclesiastes 5:2's *"don’t
let your heart be hasty to utter anything before God"* makes the same point in the verses just
before the vow sayings. Read together, the three texts cover the whole act: think before vowing
(Proverbs, Ecclesiastes 5:2), do not delay paying (Deuteronomy, Ecclesiastes 5:4), and do not
reclassify the vow afterward as a mistake (Ecclesiastes 5:6).

**Jacob's vow as the narrative case.** The first vow in Scripture is conditional, made at the place
Jacob names *"God’s house"* (Genesis 28:22), and the narrative records its partial payment: God
tells him to go up to Bethel and build an altar (35:1), and Jacob says he will, to the God *"who
answered me in the day of my distress"* (35:3). Genesis does not narrate the tenth being paid. The
wiki records that gap rather than filling it. Ecclesiastes 5:1's *"God’s house"* and Genesis 28:22's
*"God’s house"* coincide in the WEB's English; their Hebrew has not been compared here, and no
literary dependence is claimed.

**Job's promise.** Eliphaz promises Job that if he returns to the Almighty, his prayers will be heard
and he will pay his vows (22:27) — paying a vow presupposes that what was asked for has been
granted. It is a promise from a speaker whom the book of Job later corrects (42:7), so it is cited
here as evidence of the custom, not as the book's verdict on Job.

**The rash vow.** Judges 11 (Jephthah) and 1 Samuel 14 (Saul's oath over the army) are the
narratives most often read beside Proverbs 20:25. They are not stored or ingested, and the wiki
neither quotes them nor treats them as the setting of the proverb.

**Numbers 30:2 comes first in the canon.** The law's own rule for a man is *he shall not break his word. He shall
do according to all that proceeds out of his mouth* (Numbers 30:2), and Deuteronomy 23:23's *that which has
gone out of your lips* says the same in the WEB's English. Numbers 30 then adds what neither Deuteronomy nor
Ecclesiastes says: a woman's vow under a father or husband can be voided on the day he hears it (30:3–15). See
[[Vows of Men and Women]].

### Deuteronomy 23 in context

Now ingested, Deuteronomy 23:21–23 can be read in its setting. Its stored WEB matches the text quoted
above from bible-api.com. Two verses earlier, 23:18 forbids paying a vow at Yahweh's house with *"the
hire of a prostitute, or the wages of a male prostitute"*. The law governs what a vow is paid with as
well as when. In the Hebrew, *"that which has gone out of your lips"* is *môṣāʾ śǝpātêkā* (WLC 23:24).
Numbers 30:13 (WEB 30:12) uses the same construct of a woman's vows, *môṣāʾ śǝpātêhā*. Numbers 30:3
(WEB 30:2) instead has the participle, *hayyōṣēʾ mippîw*. Deuteronomy 8:3 uses the same noun of
*"every word that proceeds out of Yahweh's mouth"* (WLC, morphhb). The earlier claim on this page that
Numbers 30:2 and Deuteronomy 23:23 "say the same in the WEB's English" stands. In Hebrew, the closer
match is Numbers 30:13. *"Will surely require it"* (21) is *dāraš*, the verb of 18:19's *"I will
require it of him"*.

(morphhb).

## Significance

Vows in Scripture are never required and never casual. Law, narrative, and wisdom agree that the
freedom lies entirely before the vow is made; after it, the speaker's own words govern him. See
[[Oaths and Vows]], [[Language and Speech]], [[The Fear of God]], and
[[Peace Offerings and Paid Vows - Proverbs 7.14 and Leviticus 7.16]], where a paid vow is used as
the opening line of a seduction.
