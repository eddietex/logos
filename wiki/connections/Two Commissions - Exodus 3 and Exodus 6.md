# Two Commissions - Exodus 3 and Exodus 6

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 3:1–4:17** — at Horeb, from the bush: the name, the patriarchs, the affliction seen, the
  promise of land, the commission to Pharaoh, five objections, and signs — [[The Burning Bush]]
- **Exodus 6:2–13** — in Egypt, after the failed audience: the name, the patriarchs, the groaning
  heard, the promise of land, the commission to Pharaoh, one objection, and no sign —
  [[I Am Yahweh]]
- **Exodus 6:14–27** — the genealogy that interrupts it, placing Moses and Aaron in Levi's line —
  [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]]
- **Exodus 6:28–7:7** — the second commission's resumption after the genealogy: the objection
  repeated, and Aaron given as Moses' prophet *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The two scenes share almost every element:**

| element | Exodus 3–4 | Exodus 6 |
|---|---|---|
| the name | *"I AM WHO I AM"*; *"Yahweh … this is my name forever"* (3:14–15) | *"I am Yahweh"*, four times (6:2, 6, 7, 8) |
| the patriarchs | *"the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob"* (3:6, 15) | *"I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, as God Almighty"* (6:3) |
| the affliction | *"I have surely seen the affliction … and have heard their cry"* (3:7) | *"I have heard the groaning"* (6:5) |
| deliverance | *"I have come down to deliver them"* (3:8) | *"I will rid you out of their bondage"* (6:6) |
| the land | *"to a good and large land … the place of the Canaanite"* (3:8, 17) | *"the land which I swore … for a heritage"* (6:8) |
| the commission | *"I will send you to Pharaoh"* (3:10) | *"Go in, speak to Pharaoh"* (6:11) |
| the objection | *"I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue"* (4:10) | *"who am of uncircumcised lips"* (6:12, 30) |
| Aaron | *"He will be to you a mouth"* (4:16) | *"Aaron your brother shall be your prophet"* (7:1, not yet ingested) |

**The differences are just as clear.** Exodus 3 has a place (Horeb), a vision (the bush), a
dialogue, signs, and five objections each answered. Exodus 6 has no place, nothing seen, one
objection, and no answer until 7:1. Exodus 6 adds what Exodus 3 lacks: the covenant (*established*,
*remembered*), *ʾĒl Šadday*, the verbs *redeem* and *take*, the covenant formula, and the refrain
*you shall know*.

**Two readings, both kept:**

1. **Source-critical.** Exodus 3–4 is usually assigned to the older non-Priestly strands (J and E).
   Exodus 6:2–7:7 is the Priestly account of the same call. On this reading, Exodus 6:3's claim that
   the name was unknown to the patriarchs is the Priestly writer's version of the moment Exodus 3:14
   narrates. The two scenes are doublets, and the editor kept both.
2. **Narrative.** Read in sequence, Exodus 6 is a renewal. The first commission ended with a believing
   people (4:31). The audience with Pharaoh made everything worse, the people cursed Moses, and Moses
   accused God (5:21–23). **God answers by saying it all again**, now in the language of covenant and
   redemption, to a man whose own question (*"Why is it that you have sent me?"*) has gone
   unanswered. The repetition is the answer: the promise has not changed because the circumstances
   have.

The two readings do not exclude each other. The first explains how the text came to repeat
itself. The second explains what the repetition does where it now stands.

## Significance

- **The second call is spoken into failure, not into wonder.** At the bush, Moses turned aside to see
  (3:3). In Egypt he has nothing to look at except bricks and his own people's anger.
- **The second time, the people do not listen** (6:9). The first time they believed (4:31). The same
  promise, repeated, does not produce the same result.
- **And the man's objection is the same both times.** He cannot speak. God's answer both times is
  Aaron. See [[Moses]], [[Aaron]], and [[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]].
