# The Ten Commandments Retold - Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5

## Passages involved

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:1–21.
- Deuteronomy 5:1–33 — Moses retells the covenant speech and the request for mediation; verified
  WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

The two accounts share the deliverance preface and the sequence of duties toward God and neighbor.
Both include the warning about generations, the protection of the divine name, Sabbath rest for the
household, honor for parents, and prohibitions of murder, adultery, theft, false testimony, and
coveting. Deuteronomy presents this within Moses' address to the living assembly (5:1–5).

**The Sabbath reasons differ.** Exodus 20:11 recalls God's six days of creation and seventh-day
rest. Deuteronomy 5:14 expressly seeks rest for servants equal to their master's and 5:15 recalls
slavery in Egypt and God's rescue. Shared rest is already present in Exodus 20:10; Deuteronomy makes
the liberation rationale explicit. These are different stated grounds for the same duty, not
contradictory commands. See [[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]].

**The household list is not reproduced word for word.** Exodus 20:17 names the neighbor's house
first, then the wife and the rest of the household. Deuteronomy 5:21 puts the wife first, then house
and field, followed by servants and animals. The WEB uses “covet” and “desire” in Deuteronomy; no
conclusion about distinct Hebrew verbs is made here. The addition of the field and changed
arrangement should remain visible.

**Deuteronomy expands the people's request.** Exodus 20:19 asks Moses to speak lest the people die;
20:20 interprets the encounter as a test. Deuteronomy 5:24–27 first reports the people's recognition
that they have heard God and survived, then their fear of hearing more. God approves what they have
said (5:28), longs for enduring fear and obedience (5:29), and distinguishes their return to the
tents from Moses' continuing attendance (5:30–31). This is fuller narration, not grounds for calling
Exodus's request unbelief.

Deuteronomy also recalls two written stone tablets (5:22). Exodus 20:1–21 itself narrates spoken
commands and the people's response; the writing and handing over must not be moved into this unit
simply because the later retelling includes them.

## Significance

The comparison preserves both continuity and variation: one covenant address remembered with
different emphases. [[Sabbath]], [[Slavery and Servitude]], [[Law and Obedience]], and
[[Mediation and Advocacy]] gain detail from the retelling without replacing the Exodus wording.
