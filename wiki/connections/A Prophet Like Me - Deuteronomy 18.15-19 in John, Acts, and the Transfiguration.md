# A Prophet Like Me - Deuteronomy 18.15-19 in John, Acts, and the Transfiguration

## Passages involved

- [[A Prophet Like Moses]] — Deuteronomy 18:15–19; *"Yahweh your God will raise up to you a prophet
  from among you, of your brothers, like me. You shall listen to him"* (15).
- Deuteronomy 34:10 — *"Since then, there has not arisen a prophet in Israel like Moses, whom Yahweh
  knew face to face"* (stored WEB, not yet ingested).
- John 1:21, 25 — *"Are you the prophet?"* … *"Why then do you baptize, if you are not the Christ, nor
  Elijah, nor the prophet?"*
- John 6:14; 7:40 — *"This is truly the prophet who comes into the world"*; *"This is truly the
  prophet."*
- Acts 3:22–23 — Peter quotes Deuteronomy 18:15, 19.
- Acts 7:37 — Stephen quotes Deuteronomy 18:15.
- Matthew 17:5; Luke 9:35 — the voice from the cloud: *"Listen to him."*

## Analysis

**In Deuteronomy the promise is open.** 18:15–19 promises *"a prophet"* raised from among the
Israelites, like Moses, with Yahweh's words in his mouth. It does not say whether the singular is an
office filled again and again or one figure. The surrounding law of offices — judges, king, priests,
prophet — reads naturally as an office; the test of 18:20–22 assumes many prophets. But 34:10, at the end
of the book, says that none like Moses has arisen, and so leaves the promise unfulfilled at the close of
the Torah. See the readings on [[A Prophet Like Moses]].

**By the Gospels, "the prophet" is a title.** In John 1:19–25 the delegation from Jerusalem asks John
the Baptist three separate questions — the Christ, Elijah, *"the prophet"* — and treats them as three
figures: *"Why then do you baptize, if you are not the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the prophet?"* (1:25). John
does not cite Deuteronomy, but the definite article and the separate place in the list show an expected
figure. After the feeding of the five thousand, the crowd says *"This is truly the prophet who comes into
the world"* (6:14), and at the feast of tents some say *"This is truly the prophet"* (7:40). Linking these
titles to Deuteronomy 18 is the usual reading; the Gospel verses quoted here do not make the link in
words.

**Acts makes the link in words.** Peter, in the temple after the healing at the Beautiful Gate, says:
*"For Moses indeed said to the fathers, 'The Lord God will raise up a prophet for you from among your
brothers, like me. You shall listen to him in all things whatever he says to you. It will be that every
soul that will not listen to that prophet will be utterly destroyed from among the people'"* (Acts
3:22–23). Stephen: *"This is that Moses, who said to the children of Israel, 'The Lord our God will raise
up a prophet for you from among your brothers, like me'"* (7:37). Both apply the promise to Jesus.

|---|---|---|
| Who raises | *"Yahweh your God"* (15) | *"The Lord God"* |
| From whom | *"from among you, of your brothers"* (15) | *"from among your brothers"* |
| The command | *"You shall listen to him"* (15) | *"You shall listen to him in all things whatever he says to you"* |
| The sanction | *"I will require it of him"* (19) | *"will be utterly destroyed from among the people"* |

The sanction differs most. Deuteronomy says Yahweh will *require* it; Acts reads closer in sense to the
Torah's cutting-off formula, as in Leviticus 23:29, *"shall be cut off from his people"* (stored WEB). This
is a comparison of the English; the Greek of Acts and of the Septuagint of Deuteronomy 18 was not checked
for this page, and whether Acts follows a Greek text or combines verses cannot be said from it.

**The Transfiguration is an allusion, not a quotation.** On the mountain, with Moses and Elijah present,
the voice from the cloud says *"This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Listen to him"*
(Matthew 17:5), and in Luke *"This is my beloved Son. Listen to him!"* (9:35). *"Listen to him"* is the
command of Deuteronomy 18:15 in WEB's English. The scene puts a cloud, a voice, and Moses together, as
at Horeb, where the people asked not to hear the voice again (18:16). Whether the evangelists intend the
echo is an interpretive judgment; many readers take it so.

**What the New Testament adds and what it assumes.** It assumes the one-figure reading of 18:15, or at
least that the office culminates in one figure. Deuteronomy itself permits but does not require that
reading. Hebrews 3:5–6 sets Moses as *"faithful in all his house as a servant"* and Christ *"faithful as a
Son over his house"* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com) — a comparison of the two, not a citation of 18:15.

## Significance

This is one of the few laws of Deuteronomy the New Testament reads as prophecy. The law's own frame is
practical — whom Israel is to listen to instead of the diviners — and its promise stays open at 34:10.
John shows the title in popular expectation; Acts gives the explicit application. The page records the
quotations and the differences; it does not decide between the office reading and the one-figure reading
of Deuteronomy. See [[Prophecy and Vision]], [[Mediation and Advocacy]], and
[[Mouth to Mouth - Numbers 12.6-8, Exodus 33, and Deuteronomy 34]].
