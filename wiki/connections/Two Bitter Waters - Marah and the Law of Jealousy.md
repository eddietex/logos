# Two Bitter Waters - Marah and the Law of Jealousy

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 15:23–25** — [[Bitter Water at Marah]]: *"they couldn't drink from the waters of Marah, for they were bitter … the waters were made sweet."*
- **Numbers 5:18, 24, 27** — [[The Suspected Wife]]: *"the water of bitterness that brings a curse"*.

## Analysis

The Torah has two scenes in which bitter water matters, and they run in opposite directions. At
Marah the water is bitter already, and a tree shown by Yahweh makes it sweet (Exodus 15:25). In
Numbers 5 the water is named bitter by the priest's rite, and it becomes bitter inside a guilty
woman (*ûḇāʾû … ləmārîm*, 24, 27; WLC). Marah ends in drinking made possible. Numbers 5 ends in
drinking made compulsory.

Both scenes use the same adjective, *mārîm* (WLC). The phrase *mê hammārîm* belongs only to Numbers
5 (by adjacent Strong's tags), and Exodus 15:23 uses the word as a predicate.

**A limit on the comparison.** [[Bitter Water at Marah]] notes that both waters are connected with a
test. Exodus 15:25 says Yahweh *tested them*, and the verb is *nissâ*. Numbers 5 does not use that
verb (WLC). Calling the rite a test is a description, and the text does not call it one. It calls it
*the law of jealousy* (29).

## Significance

Neither passage refers to the other, and the pairing is the wiki's. It is kept because in both the
water does what no human power there can do: it gives Israel something to drink, or it answers a
question nobody can answer. See [[Testing]], [[Curse]], and
[[Bitter Water Made Sweet - Marah and Elisha's Spring]].
