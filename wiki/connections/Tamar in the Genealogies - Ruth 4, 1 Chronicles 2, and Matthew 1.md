# Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 38:27–30** — Perez and Zerah born to Tamar — [[Judah and Tamar]]
- **Genesis 46:12; Numbers 26:19–22** — the five sons of Judah, and the clans counted
- **Ruth 4:12** — *"let your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah"*
- **Ruth 4:18–22** — *"this is the history of the generations of Perez"*, ending on David
- **1 Chronicles 2:3–5** — Judah's line restated, with *Bath-shua the Canaanitess* and Er's verdict
- **Matthew 1:3** — *"Judah became the father of Perez and Zerah by Tamar"*
- **Luke 3:33** — Perez in the other genealogy, with no woman named

## Analysis

**Four books keep this chapter, and each keeps a different part of it.**

**Numbers keeps the clans.** The census of Judah at 26:19–22 counts the Perezites, the Zerahites, and
the Shelanites, and adds the one editorial note Genesis 38 needs: *"Er and Onan died in the land of
Canaan."* **The two men Yahweh killed are in the register with a cause of death and no cause.**

**Chronicles keeps the scandal.** 1 Chronicles 2:3–5 is a genealogy, and genealogies are the natural
place to lose an inconvenient story — **and the Chronicler does not.** He names *"Bath-shua the
Canaanitess"*, repeats Er's verdict verbatim (*"was evil in Yahweh's sight; and he killed him"*), and
writes **"Tamar his daughter-in-law bore him Perez and Zerah"** — ***daughter-in-law***, the word that
makes the relationship unmistakable, in a list that could simply have said *and Judah's sons were*.

**Ruth keeps the comparison, and makes it itself.** The elders at the gate of Bethlehem bless Boaz's
marriage in these terms: *"Let your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, of the
offspring which Yahweh will give you of this young woman."* **This is not a connection the wiki is
drawing. It is a blessing invoking Genesis 38 by name over a second levirate marriage in the same
town**, and it is spoken about a Moabite. See
[[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].

**Then the book closes on a genealogy that starts where Genesis 38 ended:**

> Perez → Hezron → Ram → Amminadab → Nahshon → Salmon → Boaz → Obed → Jesse → **David**

**Ten names, and the first is the child of the roadside.** Ruth could have started the list at Judah,
or at Abraham, or at Adam as Chronicles does. **It starts at [[Perez]].**

### Matthew

**Matthew 1 names four women before Mary, and the choice is deliberate in a form that normally names
none.**

| | who | what the genealogy declines to hide |
|---|---|---|
| 1:3 | **Tamar** | a daughter-in-law who conceived by her father-in-law at a roadside |
| 1:5 | **Rahab** | a Canaanite of Jericho, called a prostitute in Joshua 2 |
| 1:5 | **Ruth** | a Moabite, of a nation excluded by name to the tenth generation (Deuteronomy 23:3) |
| 1:6 | **"her of Uriah"** | Bathsheba, named only by the husband David had killed |

**Three of the four are outside Israel or on its edge, and all four are attached to an irregularity in
the line's transmission.** Luke's genealogy, which also runs through Perez (3:33), **names no women at
all** — so this is a choice by one evangelist, not a convention.

**Matthew also keeps [[Zerah]]**, who is not an ancestor: *"Judah became the father of Perez and Zerah
by Tamar."* **The genealogy carries the twin it does not need**, which is what Genesis does too, and
what Chronicles does. **The brother who came out second and was born first is not dropped from the
record anywhere.**

## Significance

**The wiki's observation is about what the later texts do not do.** Genesis 38 is a story a tradition
might reasonably be expected to quietly lose: a patriarch's Canaanite marriage, two sons executed by
God without explanation, a withheld levirate, a veiled daughter-in-law, a summary death sentence, and
a confession. **Four separate books keep it, and three of them name the woman.**

**Nor do any of them apologise for it.** Ruth 4:12 invokes it as a *blessing*. Chronicles calls her
*daughter-in-law* in the register. Matthew puts her in the first paragraph of the New Testament.
**None of the four adds a verdict**, favourable or otherwise — the only verdict any text supplies is
[[Judah]]'s own, *"she is more righteous than I"*, and it is against himself.

**And the line that ends in the kingship begins in the two places the covenant's own marriage rule
excluded.** Judah marries a Canaanite; the son he needs comes from a woman whose nationality Genesis
refuses to state; Boaz marries a Moabite; **and the elders of Bethlehem bless the second marriage in
the first one's name.** See [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]], [[Kingship and Empire]], and
[[Call and Election]].
