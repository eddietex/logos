# God Made Man Upright - Ecclesiastes 7.29 and Genesis 1-3

## Passages involved

- [[Righteousness and Wisdom Under Question]] — Ecclesiastes 7:29.
- [[The Creation of the World]] — Genesis 1:27, 31.
- [[The Fall]] — Genesis 3:1–13.

## Analysis

> Behold, I have only found this: that God made man upright; but they search for many schemes.” (Ecclesiastes 7:29)

> God created man in his own image. In God’s image he created him; male and female he created them. (Genesis 1:27)

> God saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. There was evening and there was morning, a sixth day. (Genesis 1:31)

**The same object.** In the Hebrew of both Ecclesiastes 7:29 and Genesis 1:27 the object of
God's making is *'et-hā'ādām* — humanity, with the article — read from Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-27. The verbs differ: Genesis 1:27 *created*, Ecclesiastes
7:29 *made*. This is a shared noun, not a quotation, and no literary dependence is claimed.

**A good making, then a turn.** Genesis declares everything made very good (1:31), then
narrates the woman and the man taking fruit *"to be desired to make one wise"* (3:6) and
shifting the blame when questioned — the man to the woman, the woman to the serpent
(3:12–13). Ecclesiastes compresses the same shape into one sentence: God made man upright, *but they* search for many schemes. The plural *they*
after a singular *man* treats humanity as many persons, much as Genesis 1:27 moves from *him*
to *them*.

**What Ecclesiastes does not do.** It does not name Adam, the garden, the serpent, or the
tree, and it does not say when the turn happened. The connection is a reading of 7:29 against
Genesis; Ecclesiastes itself does not cite Genesis here.

**A tension within Ecclesiastes 7.** Fifteen verses earlier, 7:13 asks *"who can make that
straight, which he has made crooked?"* — attributing the crooked to God's work. 7:29
attributes human crookedness to human searching. The wiki keeps both: Ecclesiastes ascribes
the conditions of life to God's making and holds humanity accountable for its schemes,
without explaining how the two relate.

**Wisdom sought.** In Genesis 3:6 the tree is desired *to make one wise*; in Ecclesiastes
7:23–25 the Preacher resolves to be wise and seeks the *scheme* of things, and finds instead
that humanity has sought many *schemes*. The resonance is thematic and is noted, not pressed.

Sources: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 7:13, 23–29; Genesis 1:27, 31; 3:1–13. Hebrew of Ecclesiastes
7:25–29 and Genesis 1:27 read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-27.

## Significance

Ecclesiastes says little about the origin of human wrong. 7:29 is the nearest it comes to the
question in the chapters ingested so far, and it agrees in outline with Genesis 1–3: an upright
making, followed by a human turn. See [[Image of God]], [[Creation]], [[Sin]], and
[[Righteousness]].
