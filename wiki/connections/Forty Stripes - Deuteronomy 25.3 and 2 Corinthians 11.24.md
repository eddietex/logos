# Forty Stripes - Deuteronomy 25.3 and 2 Corinthians 11.24

## Passages involved

- [[Justice, Family Duty, Honest Weights, and Amalek]] — Deuteronomy 25:2–3; *"He may sentence him to
  no more than forty stripes. He shall not give more"*.
- **2 Corinthians 11:24** — *"Five times from the Jews I received forty stripes minus one."* *(not yet
  ingested)*
- [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]] — Proverbs 17:10; a rebuke against *"a hundred lashes"* given to a
  fool.

## Analysis

**The limit in Deuteronomy.** The judge makes the guilty man lie down and has him beaten *"before his
face, according to his wickedness, by number"* (25:2). The number has a ceiling: *ʾarbāʿîm yakkennû
lōʾ yōsîp*, *forty he may strike him; he shall not add* (WLC 25:3). The reason is given: *"lest … your
brother will be degraded in your sight"*. The verb is the niphal of *qālâ*, *be made light*. The
limit protects the convicted man's standing as a brother, not only his body.

**Paul's count.** In a list of his sufferings, Paul writes that he received *"forty stripes minus
one"* five times *"from the Jews"* (2 Corinthians 11:24, WEB). He does not cite Deuteronomy. The number
matches a practice of stopping one short of forty so that the law's limit would not be crossed by a
miscount. That practice is reported from general knowledge, and the usual citation is Mishnah
*Makkot* 3:10; it was not checked. The Greek of 2 Corinthians was not checked.

**What links them.** The connection is a number and a legal setting. Paul's thirty-nine strokes, given *"from
the Jews"*, kept inside Deuteronomy's forty. Neither text refers to the other, and the
link depends on the later practice. The wiki records it as an allusion by way of that practice, not
as a quotation.

**Proverbs' hundred.** [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]] already set Proverbs 17:10 beside this verse:
the proverb's *"hundred lashes"* is a comparison, not a sentence, and Deuteronomy caps a real sentence
at forty.

## Significance

Deuteronomy limits punishment for the sake of the person punished. Paul's count shows that limit still
shaping punishment in his time, even as he received it five times. See [[Judgment]],
[[Brotherhood]], and [[Suffering]].
