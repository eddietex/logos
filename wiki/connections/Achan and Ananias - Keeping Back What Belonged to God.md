# Achan and Ananias - Keeping Back What Belonged to God

## Passages involved

- [[The Sin of Achan]] — Joshua 7:1–26 (stored WEB).
- Acts 5:1–3 — *"But a certain man named Ananias, with Sapphira, his wife, sold a possession, and kept
  back part of the price, his wife also being aware of it, and brought a certain part, and laid it at
  the apostles' feet. But Peter said, 'Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy
  Spirit, and to keep back part of the price of the land?'"* *(WEB, verified at bible-api.com,
  2026-11-10; not yet ingested)*
- Acts 5:4–11 — Peter: *"While you kept it, didn't it remain your own? After it was sold, wasn't it in
  your power? … You haven't lied to men, but to God."* Ananias *"fell down and died. Great fear came on
  all who heard these things"*; Sapphira follows. *(same)*

## Analysis

**Two beginnings broken by concealment.** Achan's theft comes immediately after Israel's first
victory in the land; Ananias's comes in the first days of the Jerusalem church. In each, property
consecrated or given to God is partly withheld, the act is hidden, the hiddenness is exposed by divine
disclosure rather than human investigation, and death follows. Joshua 7:11 lists *stolen* and
*deceived*; Acts 5:3 names lying *to the Holy Spirit*.

**Differences matter.** Achan took from what was *ḥērem* by command; Ananias kept back from what was
his own to give (Acts 5:4). Achan is executed by Israel after confession; Ananias falls dead on
hearing Peter's words, with no human execution, and Sapphira after him. The whole camp of Israel is
routed for Achan's sin; Acts reports *great fear* on the assembly (5:11), not defeat.

**A verbal link — unverified.** It is frequently said that the Greek verb of Acts 5:2–3 (*nosphizō*,
*keep back for oneself*) is the same verb the Septuagint uses in Joshua 7:1 for Achan's trespass. That
claim is recalled, not checked against an identified Septuagint or Greek New Testament edition during
this ingest, and should be treated as unverified until it is. The typological parallel above does not
depend on it.

## Significance

Both narratives treat the holiness of a community at its founding as something one person's hidden
greed can endanger. The parallel is a reader's comparison; Acts does not cite Joshua. See
[[Holiness]], [[Theft and Restitution]], [[Achan]], and
[[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]].
