# All Foods Clean - Leviticus 11, Deuteronomy 14, Mark 7, and Acts 10

## Passages involved

- [[Clean and Unclean Animals]] — Leviticus 11:1–47.
- [[A Holy People and Its Food]] — Deuteronomy 14:1–21.
- Mark 7:14–23 — what defiles a person (not yet ingested).
- Acts 10:9–16, 28 — Peter's vision (not yet ingested).

## Analysis

Leviticus 11 and Deuteronomy 14 give Israel a list of clean and unclean animals and ground it in
Israel's holiness (Leviticus 11:44–45; Deuteronomy 14:2, 21). Two New Testament passages take up
the same list.

**Mark 7.** Jesus says, *"There is nothing from outside of the man, that going into him can defile
him; but the things which proceed out of the man are those that defile the man"* (7:15). Food
*"doesn't go into his heart, but into his stomach, then into the latrine, thus purifying all
foods"* (7:19). Both WEB, verified from bible-api.com. English versions differ on whether the last
clause of 7:19 is Jesus' words or the narrator's comment; the wiki has not checked the Greek and
makes no claim about its wording.

**Acts 10.** Peter sees a sheet of animals and is told to kill and eat. He answers, *"Not so, Lord;
for I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean"* (10:14), and is told, *"What God has
cleansed, you must not call unclean"* (10:15). In Cornelius's house he applies it: *"God has shown
me that I shouldn't call any man unholy or unclean"* (10:28). All WEB, verified from bible-api.com.

**What changes and what does not.** In Deuteronomy, food marks Israel off from *"all peoples who are
on the face of the earth"* (14:2), and the *nəbēlâ* that Israel may not eat can go to the foreigner
(14:21). In Acts, Peter reads the vision first as being about people: the distinction between Jew
and Gentile is what is set aside (10:28). The two passages connect the food law to the boundary
between Israel and the nations, from opposite sides.

The wiki does not say how far the New Testament passages set aside the Torah's food law for Jewish
believers; Christian traditions disagree on that, and the passages above do not settle every case.
Acts 15:20, 29, which keeps some food restrictions for Gentile believers, is not ingested or
verified here.

verified from bible-api.com (WEB) without storing those books.

## Significance

The food law is about [[Holiness]] and the separation of Israel from [[The Nations]]; the New
Testament reads its end in those terms. See also [[Clean and Unclean]],
[[Two Lists of Clean Animals - Leviticus 11 and Deuteronomy 14]], and
[[Delivered to Be Holy - Exodus 19 and Leviticus 11]].
