# Two Kinds - Leviticus 19.19 and Deuteronomy 22.9-11

## Passages involved

- [[Holy Living in Field and Community]] — Leviticus 19:19; animals, field, and garment.
- [[Care for the Neighbor and Ordered Distinctions]] — Deuteronomy 22:9–11; vineyard, plow team, and
  cloth.

## Analysis

Both law codes have one verse or group of verses forbidding the mixing of kinds, and each has three
cases. Leviticus 19:19 opens with *"You shall keep my statutes"* and then: *"You shall not cross-breed
different kinds of animals. … You shall not sow your field with two kinds of seed; … Don't wear a
garment made of two kinds of material"* (stored WEB). Deuteronomy 22:9–11: *"You shall not sow your
vineyard with two kinds of seed, lest all the fruit be defiled … You shall not plow with an ox and a
donkey together. You shall not wear clothes of wool and linen woven together"* (stored WEB).

| | Leviticus 19:19 | Deuteronomy 22:9–11 |
|---|---|---|
| Animals | not to be bred as two kinds | ox and donkey not to plow together |
| Seed | field not sown with two kinds | vineyard not sown with two kinds |
| Cloth | garment of two kinds, *šaʿaṭnēz* | *šaʿaṭnēz*, wool and linen together |
| Consequence | none stated | the yield *"defiled"* — Hebrew *tiqdaš*, *becomes holy* |

**Shared vocabulary.** *Kilʾayim*, *two kinds*, occurs in the Torah only in these two places — three
times in Leviticus 19:19 and once in Deuteronomy 22:9 — and *šaʿaṭnēz* only in Leviticus 19:19 and
Deuteronomy 22:11 (WLC lemmas 3610, 8162; morphhb, retrieved 2026-11-08). The overlap of rare words
makes it likely that one list knows the other or that both draw on a shared formulation; which way any
dependence runs is not recoverable from the two verses and is not argued here.

**Differences.** Deuteronomy changes the field to a vineyard and the breeding of animals to their
yoking. It defines *šaʿaṭnēz* as *"wool and linen"*, which Leviticus does not. And it alone gives a
consequence: the mixed vineyard's yield is forfeited, with the verb *qādaš* (WLC), which WEB renders
*"be defiled"*. Leviticus instead frames its three laws as statutes, among the laws of a chapter that
begins *"You shall be holy; for I, Yahweh your God, am holy"* (19:2, stored WEB). The holiness that
Leviticus puts at the head of the chapter, Deuteronomy puts into the consequence: the mixed crop
*becomes holy* in the sense of being removed from ordinary use.

**What neither text says.** Neither explains why mixing is forbidden. Proposed reasons — that it
preserves the created order of kinds, that it marks Israel's own separation from the nations, that it
avoids practices associated with other peoples' worship, or that the ox and donkey law is about the
weaker animal — are all from interpretation, not from these verses. The kinds of Genesis 1 use a
different word (*mîn*, *"after their kind"*), so the creation account is not verbally behind either
list; the link is conceptual where it is made at all.

## Significance

Both codes treat ordinary farming and dress as places where Israel's distinctness is kept. See
[[Holiness]], [[Clean and Unclean]], and [[Creation]]. Paul's *"Don't be unequally yoked with
unbelievers"* (2 Corinthians 6:14, WEB, bible-api.com, 2026-11-08) is often read with Deuteronomy
22:10, but Paul does not cite it, and the Greek of 2 Corinthians was not checked here.
