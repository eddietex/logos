# Once, Yes Twice - Job 33.14, 40.5, and Psalm 62.11

## Passages involved

- **Job 33:14** — Elihu: *"For God speaks once, yes twice, though man pays no attention."* —
  [[Elihu Speaks in Anger]]
- **Job 33:29** — Elihu: *"Behold, God does all these things, twice, yes three times, with a man"* — same
  passage
- **Job 40:5** — Job to Yahweh: *"I have spoken once, and I will not answer; Yes, twice, but I will proceed no
  further."* — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Job 5:19** — Eliphaz: *"He will deliver you in six troubles; yes, in seven no evil shall touch you."* —
  [[Eliphaz's First Speech]]
- **Psalm 62:11** — *"God has spoken once; twice I have heard this, that power belongs to God."*
- **Proverbs 6:16** — *"There are six things which Yahweh hates; yes, seven which are an abomination to
  him"* — [[The Surety, the Sluggard, and the Seven Abominations]]; the list that follows has seven
  items, and the weight falls on the seventh, *"he who sows discord among brothers"* (6:19). See
  [[Six Things Yahweh Hates - Proverbs 6.16-19 and the Ten Commandments]]

## Analysis

**The *x, x+1* pattern is a fixed form of Hebrew wisdom** — six and seven, three and four, one and two — in
which the second number does not correct the first but intensifies it. Job's book uses it three times, and
the one-two form twice: Elihu of God, and Job of himself.

**Elihu says God speaks *"once, yes twice"* (33:14)** — *bəʾaḥat … ûbištayim* — by dream and by pain, and
man does not notice. It is his explanation of why Job thinks God silent: God has been speaking, and Job has
not been listening.

**Job says he has spoken *"once … yes, twice"* (40:5)** — *ʾaḥat … ûštayim* — and will not go on. Whether
the author meant the echo cannot be proved, since the formula is common; but the book places it in the only
two speeches addressed to Job's silence. Elihu used it to say God had already spoken; when God actually
speaks, Job uses it to say he himself is finished. **The numbers that measured God's speech in Elihu's mouth
now measure Job's**, and the comparison is to Job's disadvantage, which is what he intends: *"I am of small
account"* (40:4).

**Psalm 62:11 sets the two sides together.** *"God has spoken once; twice I have heard this."* God speaks
once and the hearer hears twice — the right proportion, on the psalm's account, between God's word and a
human being's reception of it. Job, who spoke more than anyone in the book, ends by reducing his own word
to the psalmist's measure: once, twice, no further.

## Significance

- A small verbal thread, recorded because it ties Elihu's otherwise unanswered speech to the moment of
  Job's first reply: Elihu's formula turns out to describe what Job does, not what God does. See
  [[Elihu the Buzite]] and [[Language and Speech]].
- The *x, x+1* form is not evidence by itself; the claim here is only that the book uses the one-two form
  twice and that the two uses face each other.
