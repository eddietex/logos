# I Will Save You No More - Deuteronomy 32.37-38, Joshua 24, and Judges 10.13-14

## Passages involved
- [[The Covenant at Shechem]] — Joshua 24:15, 22. *Choose today whom you will serve*; *You are
  witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen Yahweh yourselves, to serve him* (stored WEB).
- [[The Song of Moses]] — Deuteronomy 32:37–38. *He will say, "Where are their gods, the rock in
  which they took refuge; which ate the fat of their sacrifices, and drank the wine of their drink
  offering? Let them rise up and help you! Let them be your protection"* (stored WEB).
- [[Israel's Repentance Under Ammon]] — Judges 10:13–14. *Yet you have forsaken me, and served other
  gods. Therefore I will save you no more. Go and cry to the gods which you have chosen. Let them save
  you in the time of your distress!* (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**The choice.** At Shechem the choice is put to Israel in the verb *bāḥar*: *baḥărû lākem hayyôm*,
*choose for yourselves today* (Joshua 24:15), and Israel's answer is recorded as *bəḥartem*, *you
have chosen* Yahweh (24:22). Judges 10:14 uses the same form of the same verb, *ʾăšer bəḥartem bām*,
*which you have chosen* (all WLC). But its object is the gods. The witness Israel bore against
itself at Shechem is turned around: the people who said they had chosen Yahweh are told to call on
the gods they actually chose.

**The taunt.** The Song of Moses foresees the scene. Yahweh will ask, *Where are their gods*, and
answer, *Let them rise up and help you* (Deuteronomy 32:37–38). Judges 10:14 says *Let them save you*,
with *yāšaʿ*. The Song's verb is *ʿāzar*, *help* (WLC). The thought is the same; the words differ.
Judges does not quote the Song, and the wiki does not claim it alludes to it.

**What follows.** In the Song the next lines turn to Yahweh's own power, *I kill and I make alive*
(32:39). Verse 36 had already said *Yahweh will judge his people, and have compassion on his
servants, when he sees that their power is gone*. In Judges the next verses report Israel's second
confession and *his soul was grieved for the misery of Israel* (10:16). Both texts set the taunt
just before a turn toward Israel. Neither describes that turn as the taunt being withdrawn.

| | Joshua 24 | Deuteronomy 32:36–38 | Judges 10:13–16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| The gods | *the gods which your fathers served … the gods of the Amorites* | *their gods, the rock in which they took refuge* | *the gods which you have chosen* |
| The verb | *bāḥar*, choose | *ʿāzar*, help | *bāḥar*, chosen; *yāšaʿ*, save |
| What follows | the covenant | *I kill and I make alive* | *his soul was grieved* |

## Significance
- Judges 10 is the book's closest approach to Yahweh's withdrawing from Israel. He uses Israel's own
  word of choosing against it.
- The refusal is not carried out. Jephthah saves Gilead in the next chapter, though the text never
  says Yahweh raised him up. See [[Redemption]] and [[Idolatry]].
