## Analysis

### The purchase remembered — Deuteronomy 23:4–5

Deuteronomy names the purchase outright: Ammon and Moab *"hired against you Balaam … to curse you"* (4),
and *"Yahweh your God turned the curse into a blessing to you, because Yahweh your God loved you"* (5).
The bought curse becomes a permanent ground of exclusion (3), as Genesis 12:3, *"I will curse him who
treats you with contempt"* (stored WEB *"treates"*), and Numbers 24:9 would lead one to expect.

See [[The Assembly and the Holy Camp]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 23:3–5; Genesis 12:3.

### Balaam’s final oracles

The third blessing closes with the protection of those who bless Israel and the curse on those who
curse it (24:9). Balak’s dismissal and loss of promised honor (24:10–13) expose the failure of
patronage. The blessing continues before the oracle turns against Moab; no accepted payment is
narrated.

Source: stored WEB Numbers 23:27–24:25; Genesis 49:1, 9–10 for the royal and future-horizon
comparisons.


### The first two oracles — Numbers 22:36–23:26

The first two oracles enact the restriction of 22:12. Repeated sacrifices and a changed vantage
point do not alter Israel’s blessed status (23:8, 20). Israel’s uncountable dust (23:10) also
recalls Genesis 13:16. This protection does not erase the judgment on Israel in Numbers 14; the
WEB’s denial of iniquity in 23:21 remains in tension with that history.

See [[Balaam's First Two Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:36–23:26.


Genesis 12:1–3 promises blessing to Abram and a divine response to those who oppose him. Numbers
6:22–27 assigns priests words of blessing but reserves the act of blessing to Yahweh. Numbers 22:6
presents Balak’s confidence in Balaam’s power to bless and curse; verse 12 answers that Israel is
already blessed and must not be cursed. These texts connect divine commitment with the limit
placed on hostile speech. This is a thematic comparison, not an explicit citation of Genesis by
the narrator.

The contrast concerns agency: Balak offers rewards to obtain a desired pronouncement, while God
gives the instruction that controls Balaam’s response. Israel’s blessing is not purchased by a
superior counteroffer. Neither this protection nor the priestly blessing cancels the wilderness
accounts of judgment on Israel.

## Significance

The blessing belongs to God’s commitment before it becomes the object of a king’s attempted
manipulation. Sources: stored WEB Genesis 12:1–3 and Numbers 6:22–27; 22:1–21.
