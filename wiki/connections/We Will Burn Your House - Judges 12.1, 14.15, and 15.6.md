# We Will Burn Your House - Judges 12.1, 14.15, and 15.6

## Passages involved
- [[Shibboleth]] — **Judges 12:1** — Ephraim to Jephthah: *We will burn your house around you with
  fire!* (stored WEB)
- **Judges 14:15** — the Philistines to Samson's wife: *Entice your husband, that he may declare to us the
  riddle, lest we burn you and your father's house with fire.* (stored WEB) *(not yet ingested; see
  "Samson's Riddle" on [[Judges]])*
- **Judges 15:6** — *The Philistines came up, and burned her and her father with fire.* (stored WEB)
  *(not yet ingested; see "Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi" on [[Judges]])*
- [[The Fall of Abimelech]] — **Judges 9:52** — Abimelech *came near to the door of the tower to burn it
  with fire* (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**The same threat, in the same form.** The verb is *śārap*, *burn* (lemma 8313), with *bāʾēš*, *with
fire*. In the WLC of Genesis–Judges its first-person plural imperfect, *nisrōp*, *we will burn*, occurs
twice, at Judges 12:1 and 14:15:

| | Judges 12:1 | Judges 14:15 |
|---|---|---|
| speakers | the men of Ephraim | the Philistine guests |
| threatened | Jephthah and his house | Samson's wife and her father's house |
| words | *bêtəkā nisrōp ʿālêkā bāʾēš* | *pen-nisrōp ʾôtāk wəʾet-bêt ʾābîk bāʾēš* |
| grievance | not called to the battle | the riddle they cannot answer |
| outcome | Ephraim is defeated at the fords | carried out: *burned her and her father with fire* (15:6) |

Both threats are made against a house over a matter of honor. In the first, an Israelite tribe threatens
an Israelite judge, and the threat is not carried out because Jephthah wins. In the second, the
Philistines threaten a Philistine woman, and they do it.

**Burning in Judges.** *Śārap* with *bāʾēš* also appears when Abimelech comes *to the door of the tower to
burn it with fire* (9:52), just before a woman drops a millstone on him, and when Dan burns Laish (18:27).
At 9:49 Abimelech's men burn the stronghold of Shechem's tower with a different verb, *yāṣat* (WLC). The
threats of 12:1 and 14:15 sit between the burning of a tower by an Israelite king and the burning of a
city by an Israelite tribe.

## Significance

The two threats belong to the last stories of Judges, when Israel's quarrels turn inward and fire is
turned on households. In each, a group that feels shamed threatens the house of one person. The pairing
is the wiki's observation; Judges does not connect the two verses. See [[War]], [[Strife and Peacemaking]],
[[Retribution]], and [[Ephraim]].
