# Wine, Oil, and the Joy of Love

## Passages involved

- [[Love Better Than Wine]] — Song of Solomon 1:1–4.
- [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]] — Proverbs 5:18–19.
- [[One Event Comes to All]] — Ecclesiastes 9:7–9.
- Song of Solomon 4:10 — stored WEB, verified but not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Song 1:2 and 1:4 value the beloved's love above wine, while 1:3 moves through fragrance
to the beloved's name. The images make pleasure a measure of love's worth, not evidence
of its wrongness. Later in the same book, 4:10 joins love better than wine to perfumes
better than spices in an address to the bride. The recurrence is visible in the stored
WEB; it does not require deciding who speaks every line of the opening.

Proverbs 5:18–19 commands a man to rejoice in his wife and be captivated by her love.
Ecclesiastes 9:7–9 sets joyful wine, oil, and life with the wife one loves together.
The Song shares their affirmation of embodied delight, while changing the speaking
position: its opening is the woman's desire expressed directly rather than counsel
given to a man. Fragrance, wine, and love belong together without being reduced to
symbols for a different subject.

**Differences retained.** Proverbs names a wife and contrasts her with an adulteress
(5:18–20). Ecclesiastes names a wife within life's passing portion (9:9). Song 1:1–4
states neither marital status nor that mortal framework. Their common delight cannot
be used to add an unmentioned wedding to the Song, or to cancel the warnings about
adultery in Proverbs. This is a thematic comparison, not a claim of quotation,
literary dependence, identical Hebrew vocabulary, or prophecy and fulfillment.

## Significance

[[Love]] and [[Joy and Enjoyment]] include bodily desire, rejoicing, and sensory pleasure.
[[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]] brings a first-person voice to material previously encountered as advice.
The comparison recognizes a shared affirmation while keeping each passage's stated
relationships and limits intact.
