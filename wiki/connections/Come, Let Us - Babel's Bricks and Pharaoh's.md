# Come, Let Us - Babel's Bricks and Pharaoh's

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 11:3–4** — *"Come, let's make bricks, and burn them thoroughly." They had brick for
  stone, and they used tar for mortar … "Come, let's build ourselves a city … lest we be scattered
  abroad"* — [[The Tower of Babel]]
- **Exodus 1:10, 14** — *"Come, let us deal wisely with them, lest they multiply"*; *"they made their
  lives bitter with hard service, in mortar and in brick"* — [[The Oppression in Egypt]]
- Compare **Exodus 5:7–19** — straw withheld and the quota of bricks kept *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Two speeches open with the same word, give their reason with the same conjunction, and end in the
same building materials.**

| | Babel (Genesis 11) | Egypt (Exodus 1) |
|---|---|---|
| the call | ***hābâ*** *nilbənâ ləbēnîm* — *"Come, let's make bricks"* (11:3); ***hābâ*** *nibneh-lānû ʿîr* — *"Come, let's build ourselves a city"* (11:4) | ***hābâ*** *nitḥakkəmâ lô* — *"Come, let us deal wisely with them"* (1:10) |
| the fear | ***pen*** *nāpûṣ* — *"lest we be scattered"* (11:4) | ***pen*** *yirbeh* — *"lest they multiply"* (1:10) |
| the materials | *ləbēnâ* (brick) and *ḥōmer* (mortar) (11:3) | *ḥōmer* and *ləbēnîm*, *"mortar and brick"* (1:14) |
| the builders | the builders themselves, for their own name | a subject people, for Pharaoh's storehouses |
| God's response | *"Come, let's go down"* (11:7) — scattering | *(Exodus 3:8)* — *"I have come down to deliver them"* |

*Hābâ* as a cohortative call to joint action is not rare in itself, but **the combination of *hābâ*,
*pen*, and brick-and-mortar occurs in these two places**, and both are the speech of a power
organising against God's purpose for the earth. Babel resists being *scattered* — the dispersal
God's blessing intended (*"fill the earth"*, Genesis 1:28; 9:1). Pharaoh resists Israel being
*multiplied* — the same blessing's other verb. **Each fears exactly what God has commanded.**

**The difference is who carries the bricks.** At Babel the builders make their own bricks to make
themselves a name. In Egypt the empire makes someone else make them, to fill its granaries. **The
first is pride; the second is pride with slaves.** See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]],
[[City and Civilization]], and [[Slavery and Servitude]].

The Babel passage page notes that *ḥēmār*, *tar*, appears in the Torah only at Genesis 11:3, 14:10,
and Exodus 2:3, the basket of the infant Moses. **So Babel's other material is the one that floats
the child who will undo Egypt's bricks.** That is chapter 2's half of this link — see
[[The Birth of Moses]] and [[The Two Arks - Noah's Ship and Moses' Basket]].

The wiki does not claim that Exodus 1 is written as a deliberate quotation of Genesis 11; the
evidence is lexical and structural, and the page records it at that strength.

## Significance

**Egypt is the second Babel.** The primeval history's last story of human self-assertion becomes the
form of the first empire Israel suffers under — and later the name *Babylon* becomes the form of the
last (see [[Babel and Babylon the Great]]). In both stories God's answer is to *come down*: at Babel
to scatter, in Egypt to deliver.
