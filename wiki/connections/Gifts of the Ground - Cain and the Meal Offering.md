# Gifts of the Ground - Cain and the Meal Offering

## Passages involved

- [[Cain and Abel]] — Genesis 4:3–7.
- [[The Meal Offering]] — Leviticus 2:1–16.

## Analysis

Cain brings produce of the ground, while Abel brings firstborn animals and their fat.
Genesis 4:4–5 records regard for Abel and his offering and lack of regard for Cain and his
offering. It gives no grain-offering recipe or stated rule requiring an animal instead.
Leviticus 2 explicitly prescribes flour, grain, oil, and prepared foods, with the burned
memorial described as a pleasant aroma (2, 9). Produce therefore cannot be rejected simply
because acceptable worship must always involve an animal's death.

The comparison establishes that limited point, not a retrospective diagnosis of Cain's
procedure. Genesis does not say he omitted oil, frankincense, salt, or a priest. Applying
Leviticus's recipe to him as an already binding law would import information the narrative
does not give. Genesis 4:7 addresses doing well and the threat of sin; it does not enumerate
a corrected offering recipe. See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].

## Significance

The later grain law prevents an unsupported explanation of the earlier rejection without
pretending to settle all questions about Cain. The distinction is between a permitted kind
of gift and the particular giver and offering that Genesis says God did not regard.
