# Coveting and Love - The Commandments in Romans 7 and 13

## Passages involved

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:13–17.
- Romans 7:7–12 — coveting exposes sin and becomes an occasion sin exploits.
- Romans 13:8–10 — love of the neighbor fulfills the law; both Romans units verified WEB, not yet
  ingested.

## Analysis

Romans 7:7 quotes the prohibition of coveting to explain how law makes sin known. The following
verses distinguish the command from the sin that exploits it: sin produces coveting through the
commandment and brings death, while the law and commandment remain holy, righteous, and good
(7:8–12). Paul is not calling the commandment itself sinful.

This creates a tension with the intended prevention of sin in Exodus 20:20. Sinai's encounter is
meant to keep the people from sinning; Romans describes sin using a good command as an opportunity
to produce the opposite result. The distinction between the law's character and sin's agency is
Paul's stated distinction, not an explanation that Exodus itself gives. The identity and narrative
scope of the first-person speaker in Romans 7 are not settled by this short comparison.

Romans 13:9 then cites adultery, murder, theft, and coveting together and gathers them under love of
neighbor. Verse 10 identifies love's refusal to harm the neighbor as fulfillment of law. The wording
of the summary also points to Leviticus 19:18, here noted by reference only; the Exodus commands are
the particular prohibitions Paul enumerates. The passage does not say that loving intentions make
those prohibitions irrelevant.

## Significance

The two uses belong together: [[Sin]] can exploit a good command, and [[Love]] can fulfill its claim
on the neighbor. [[Envy]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]] therefore concern more than forbidden
acquisitions; desire itself is answerable to the law.
