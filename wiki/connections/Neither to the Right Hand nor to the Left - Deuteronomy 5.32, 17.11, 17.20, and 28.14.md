# Neither to the Right Hand nor to the Left - Deuteronomy 5.32, 17.11, 17.20, and 28.14

## Passages involved

- [[Blessings for Obedience (Deuteronomy)]] — Deuteronomy 28:1–14.

- [[The People's Request for a Mediator]] — Deuteronomy 5:32: *"You shall not turn away to the
  right hand or to the left."*
- [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]] — Deuteronomy 17:11: *"You shall not turn away from the
  sentence which they shall show you, to the right hand, nor to the left."*
- [[The Law for the King]] — Deuteronomy 17:20: *"that he not turn away from the commandment, to the
  right hand, or to the left"*.
- Deuteronomy 28:14 *(now ingested)* — *"and shall not turn away from any of the words which I

- [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]] — Deuteronomy 2:27; and [[Edom Refuses Passage]], Numbers 20:17:

## Analysis

**A road image made into a rule.** In Numbers 20:17 and Deuteronomy 2:27 the words are literal:
Israel asks to pass along the king's highway without leaving it. Deuteronomy takes the same picture
and applies it to the law. The command is a road, and obedience is keeping to it.

**One rule for every rank.** Deuteronomy uses it of three parties:

| Who | Verse | Turned from |
|---|---|---|
| the people | 5:32 | what Yahweh commanded |
| the litigants | 17:11 | the court's verdict |
| the king | 17:20 | the commandment |
| the people | 28:14 | the words commanded today, *to go after other gods* |

In the WLC each uses *sûr*, *turn aside*, with *yāmîn ûśəmōʾl*, *right and left* (5:32; 17:11, 20;
28:14; morphhb). The king is held to the same line as the people and the parties to a lawsuit. That
fits the purpose clause of 17:20, *"that his heart not be lifted up above his brothers"*: the same
road is set for all of them.

**28:14 names the ditch.** Turning aside there is turning *"to go after other gods"*. The road image
is not about precision for its own sake; leaving it means leaving Yahweh.

**After Moses.** Joshua 1:7 gives the command to Moses' successor with the law as the road, and ties
it to success, as 17:20 ties it to a long reign. Proverbs 4:27 uses it as wisdom's counsel to a son.
Neither is claimed as a quotation of Deuteronomy; the image is common.

### Blessings now ingested — Deuteronomy 28:1–14

The now-ingested blessing section ends with the same directional warning (14). Its function here is to retain exclusive allegiance within the promises of prosperity and national standing; no amount of abundance makes departure to other gods permissible. This addition compares the stored WEB, without adding a source-language claim.

## Significance

The phrase carries [[Law and Obedience]] across the offices of Deuteronomy, from people to court to
king. It also bears on [[Walking with God]], where obedience is pictured as a way. See
[[The Law for the King]].
