# Whoever You Find Your Gods With — Two Rash Oaths Over a Search

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 31:30–35** — [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]]; [[Jacob]] to [[Laban]]: *"Anyone you find your
  gods with shall not live"*, and the narrator's *"For Jacob didn't know that Rachel had stolen them"*
- **Genesis 44:1–13** — [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]; the brothers to [[Joseph's Steward]]:
  *"With whomever of your servants it is found, let him die, and we also will be my lord's slaves"*
- **Genesis 35:16–19** — [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]]; [[Rachel]] dies in
  childbirth four chapters after the first oath
- **Judges 11:30–40** — Jephthah's vow, the other Old Testament oath that binds a speaker to a person
  he did not have in mind
- **1 Samuel 14:24–45** — Saul's oath, and Jonathan eating the honey
- **Leviticus 5:4; Ecclesiastes 5:2–6; Matthew 5:34–37** — the law and the counsel on rash speech

## Analysis

**Genesis stages the same scene twice, thirteen chapters apart, with the same family and the same
mistake.**

| | Genesis 31:30–35 | Genesis 44:4–13 |
|---|---|---|
| **the accusation** | *"why have you stolen my gods?"* | *"Why have you rewarded evil for good?"* |
| **who swears** | [[Jacob]] | **his ten elder sons** |
| **the oath** | *"Anyone you find your gods with **shall not live**"* | *"With whomever… it is found, **let him die**, and we also will be my lord's slaves"* |
| **the certainty behind it** | he is sure no one in his household took them | they are sure no one in their party took it |
| **the search** | tent by tent — Jacob's, Leah's, the servants', **Rachel's last** (31:33) | **sack by sack — *"beginning with the oldest, and ending at the youngest"*** (44:12) |
| **the hidden object** | the *teraphim*, under [[Rachel]] on a camel's saddle | the *gābîaʿ*, in the sack of **Rachel's son** |
| **what the searcher finds** | **nothing** | **the cup** |
| **who was right** | the accuser | the accuser, and he planted it |

**The parallel is not a resemblance of theme. It is the same mechanism run twice**: a man swears a
death sentence on an unknown party, in the confidence that the search will clear him, **and the search
is aimed at the household of [[Rachel]] both times.**

### The oath Jacob did not know he was making

> **32** Anyone you find your gods with shall not live. Before our relatives, discern what is yours
> with me, and take it." For Jacob didn't know that Rachel had stolen them. (31:32)

**The narrator's closing clause is the kind of aside Genesis almost never supplies.** It has been
content to let Isaac not know which son he is blessing and Judah not know whom he has slept with; **here
it stops the scene to tell the reader that the man swearing does not know what he is swearing about.**
The clause exists to make the oath land on a specific person, **and the person is the wife he worked
fourteen years for.**

**Laban does not find them**, because Rachel sits on them and pleads a reason he cannot argue with
(31:34–35). **The oath therefore never operates — and Genesis has been read for two millennia as
though it did.** She dies bearing [[Benjamin]] at 35:16–19, four chapters later, **and a standing
reading takes the death as the discharge of 31:32.** The wiki records that reading without adopting
it: **Genesis draws no line between the two verses**, offers no interval, and gives Rachel's death the
ordinary cause of a hard labour. See [[Rachel]] and [[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]].

### The oath his sons make, and the servant who defuses it

> **9** With whomever of your servants it is found, let him die, and we also will be my lord's slaves.
> (44:9)

**They are volunteering, and they are volunteering the same sentence.** The difference is that this
time **the object really is in one of the bags**, and the bag belongs to **the only other child
[[Rachel]] bore.**

**What stops it is a servant with no name.** [[Joseph's Steward]] answers *"Now also let it be
according to your words"* and then states terms that are not their words at all — *"he with whom it is
found will be my slave; and you will be blameless"* (44:10). **He halves the penalty and dissolves the
solidarity in one sentence.** **It is the only place in Genesis where anybody edits an oath before it
can take effect**, and it is done by a functionary executing a script that did not cover it.

**The second oath therefore fails the way the first one did — not because the men who swore it were
wrong about the object, but because someone else declined to enforce it.** In 31 the hidden thing was
never found; in 44 it was found and the sentence was rewritten. **Rachel survives the first by
concealment and her son survives the second by an amendment.**

### Two searches, one order

**Both searches are narrated as sequences, and both sequences put the true holder last.**

- *"Laban went into Jacob's tent, into Leah's tent, and into the tent of the two female servants; but
  he didn't find them. He went out of Leah's tent, and entered into Rachel's tent"* (31:33).
- *"He searched, beginning with the oldest, and ending at the youngest"* (44:12).

**Laban searches in an order he chooses and finds nothing.** **The steward searches in an order
[[Joseph]] chose** — the same birth order Joseph had used to seat them at 43:33 — **and finds what
Joseph put there.** The first sequence is suspense with no payoff; **the second is theatre, and the
man directing it knows the contents of every sack.** See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

### The family's habit

**Rash speech is this household's recurring instrument, and it is always aimed at someone the speaker
cannot see.**

- [[Isaac]] blesses irrevocably and cannot take it back (27:33, 37) — see
  [[Yes, He Will Be Blessed - The Irrevocable Word]]
- [[Jacob]] swears over the *teraphim* (31:32)
- [[Judah]] sentences [[Tamar]] to burn on hearsay, in a case where the evidence is his own (38:24)
- **the ten swear over the cup** (44:9)

**In each the speaker is confident, the confidence is misplaced, and the sentence falls — or nearly
falls — inside his own house.** Genesis never moralises about it. **It simply has the words keep
coming back.** See [[Oaths and Vows]] and [[Language and Speech]].

## Significance

**The rash oath is one of the Old Testament's standing forms, and Genesis supplies the two cases that
have no legislation attached.** Jephthah swears before he sees who will come out of the door (Judges
11:30–31) and his daughter comes out; Saul swears a curse on anyone who eats before evening and
Jonathan, who did not hear it, eats the honey (1 Samuel 14:24–27). **In both, the man who swore is
bound by words he would not have chosen if he had known the subject** — **which is exactly the
position Jacob is in at 31:32 and his sons at 44:9.**

**Leviticus 5:4 eventually names the category** — *"if anyone swears rashly with his lips to do evil
or to do good … and it is hidden from him"* — and provides a guilt offering for it. **Ecclesiastes
5:2 gives the counsel** (*"let your words be few"*), **and Matthew 5:34–37 removes the instrument
altogether**: *"don't swear at all … let your 'Yes' be 'Yes', and your 'No', 'No'."*

**Genesis has none of that apparatus and does not want it.** What it has is a man who swears over a
saddle he does not think to look under, **a set of sons who swear over sacks a stranger has packed**,
and, in both cases, **an outcome decided by somebody other than the swearer.** [[Providence]] in these
two chapters looks exactly like other people's discretion.
