# Incense and Prayer - Exodus 30, Psalm 141, Luke 1, and Revelation 8

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 30:1–10, 34–38** — the gold incense altar *"before the veil … where I will meet with
  you"*, and *"a perpetual incense before Yahweh"* burned morning and evening
  ([[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]])
- **Exodus 29:38–42** — the daily lamb, morning and evening, at *"the door of the Tent of Meeting"*
  ([[The Ordination of the Priests]])
- **Psalm 141:2** — *"Let my prayer be set before you like incense; the lifting up of my hands like
  the evening sacrifice."*
- **Malachi 1:11** — *"in every place incense will be offered to my name, and a pure offering"*
- **Luke 1:8–11** — Zechariah's lot *"to enter into the temple of the Lord and burn incense"*; *"The
  whole multitude of the people were praying outside at the hour of incense"*; the angel *"standing
  on the right side of the altar of incense"*
- **Revelation 5:8** — *"golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints"*
- **Revelation 8:3–5** — incense added *"to the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar which
  was before the throne"*, its smoke going up *"with the prayers of the saints"*, and then fire from
  the altar thrown on the earth

## Analysis

**Exodus never calls the incense prayer.** It gives the altar, the recipe, the times, and the
penalty for copying it. Its stated location is *"where I will meet with you"* (30:6, 36), the
same promise made of the mercy seat (25:22) and of the tent door (29:42). The incense is
*qəṭōret tāmîd*, *"a perpetual incense"* (30:8, MAM), and it is offered at the same two times as the
daily lamb (29:38–42). The link between incense and the people's words is made later.

**Psalm 141 makes the comparison explicitly.** *"Let my prayer be set before you like incense; the
lifting up of my hands like the evening sacrifice"* (141:2). The psalmist asks that prayer be treated
*like* the evening incense and offering. The verse is a simile, and it takes the twice-daily
service of Exodus 29–30 as its point of reference.

**Luke 1 puts the two side by side in time.** A priest burns incense inside; *"the whole multitude
of the people were praying outside at the hour of incense"* (1:10). The angel appears *"on the right
side of the altar of incense"* (1:11), the altar of Exodus 30 in its temple form. Luke does not say
the incense *is* the prayer; he places the prayer at the time of the incense.

**Revelation identifies them.** The bowls of incense *"are the prayers of the saints"* (5:8). In 8:3–4
the incense is added to the prayers on *"the golden altar which was before the throne"*, and the two
rise together. The scene then turns: fire from the same altar is thrown on the earth, followed by
thunder and earthquake (8:5). The prayers are answered in judgment.

## Significance

- The thread runs from rite to simile to identification. Exodus gives a practice without a
  meaning; Psalm 141 compares prayer to it; Luke synchronizes them; Revelation equates them in a
  vision. Each step is a reading of the one before, and the wiki records them in order rather than
  reading the last back into the first.
- Malachi 1:11 widens the place. Incense in Exodus may be burned only by Aaron's line, only on one
  altar, only in the one recipe (30:7, 37–38). Malachi foresees incense offered *"in every place"*
  among the nations. How that is to be read — literally, or of prayer — is a question Malachi does
  not answer in the verse.
- The exclusiveness of the Exodus incense is the other side of the thread. See
  [[Strange Incense - Exodus 30.9, Leviticus 10, Numbers 16, and Uzziah]].
