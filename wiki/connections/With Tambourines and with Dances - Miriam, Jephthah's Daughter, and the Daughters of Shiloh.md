# With Tambourines and with Dances - Miriam, Jephthah's Daughter, and the Daughters of Shiloh

## Passages involved
- [[The Song of the Sea]] — Exodus 15:20. *Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine
  in her hand; and all the women went out after her with tambourines and with dances* (stored WEB).
- [[The Golden Calf]] — Exodus 32:19. *He saw the calf and the dancing* (stored WEB).
- [[Jephthah's Vow]] — Judges 11:34. *His daughter came out to meet him with tambourines and with dances*
  (stored WEB).
- [[Wives for Benjamin]] — Judges 21:21 *(stored WEB)* — *if the daughters of Shiloh come out to dance in
  the dances, then come out of the vineyards, and each man catch his wife of the daughters of Shiloh*.
- [[Saul's Jealousy of David]] — **1 Samuel 18:6** *(stored WEB)* — *the women came
  out of all the cities of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet king Saul, with tambourines, with joy,
  and with instruments of music*.

## Analysis

**The same words twice.** *Bətuppîm ûbimḥōlōt* (Exodus 15:20) and *bətuppîm ûbimḥōlôt* (Judges 11:34),
*with tambourines and with dances*, differ only in spelling (WLC). In the WLC of Genesis through Judges
the pair occurs in these two verses and nowhere else. *Məḥōlâ*, *dance*, occurs in two more: the dancing
at the calf (Exodus 32:19) and the dances of Shiloh (Judges 21:21). *Tōp*, *tambourine*, occurs in one
more: Laban's *with mirth and with songs, with tambourine and with harp* (Genesis 31:27).

**Women going out.** In each of the main verses women *go out*, *yāṣāʾ*. The women *went out after
her* (Exodus 15:20). Jephthah's daughter *came out to meet him* (Judges 11:34). The daughters of Shiloh
*come out to dance* (Judges 21:21). In 1 Samuel 18:6 the WEB has *the women came out … to meet king
Saul*; its Hebrew was not checked for this page.

**What follows each going out.**

- At the sea, the dance is praise after deliverance: *Sing to Yahweh, for he has triumphed gloriously*
  (Exodus 15:21).
- At Mizpah, the dance greets a deliverance too. It is cut short by the vow, because the one who goes
  out first is the one the vow named (Judges 11:31, 34).
- At Shiloh, the dancing daughters are seized as wives for Benjamin (Judges 21:21). The book that gave
  one daughter's dance to a vow ends with other daughters taken from their dance.
- In 1 Samuel the women's song for David begins Saul's jealousy (1 Samuel 18:6–9, WEB; not yet
  ingested and not quoted further here).

## Significance

The women's dance with tambourines is the way Israel greets a victory. Miriam leads it as *the
prophetess*, and it is the earliest picture in the wiki of the custom. Judges uses the same words for
the moment the victory turns into a loss. The thread is the wiki's observation from the shared words;
Judges does not cite Exodus. See [[Miriam]], [[Jephthah's Daughter]], [[Praise and Song]], and
[[The Prophetess - Miriam and Deborah]].

### Judges 21 now ingested — the dancers seized

*Lāḥûl bamməḥōlôṯ* (21:21), *to dance in the dances*, and *hamməḥōləlôṯ*, *those who danced* (21:23, WLC):
the verb *ḥûl* (lemma 2342 a) joins the noun. The daughters *come out*, *yēṣəʾû* (21:21), and the men
lying in wait *come out*, *wîṣāʾṯem*, from the vineyards to meet them — the same verb for both. The feast
is *from year to year* (21:19), as the daughters of Israel's lament for Jephthah's daughter is (11:40); see
[[From Year to Year - Jephthah's Daughter, the Feast at Shiloh, and Elkanah]].

1 Samuel 18:6 can now be checked in the WLC: *wattēṣeʾnâ hannāšîm … lāšîr wəhamməḥōlôṯ … bəṯuppîm*, WEB *the
women came out … singing and dancing, to meet king Saul, with tambourines* (bible-api.com, 2026-11-17). It has the going out, the dances, and the
tambourines, so the pair *tambourines … dances* of Exodus 15:20 and Judges 11:34 recurs there, separated by
other words. See [[Wives for Benjamin]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 11:40; 21:19–23; Hebrew WLC
(OpenScriptures morphhb, including `1Sam.xml`), 2026-11-17. WEB 1 Samuel 18:6 rechecked at bible-api.com,
2026-11-17.

### 1 Samuel 18:6–7 now ingested — Miriam's verb

The women of 18:7 *sang to one another*, *wattaʿănênâ* (WLC; morphhb lemma 6030 c, *sing*). In the files checked
(Genesis through 2 Samuel, and Ruth) that root occurs at Exodus 15:21, *Miriam answered them*; Exodus 32:18, at the
calf; Numbers 21:17, the song of the well; and 1 Samuel 18:7; 21:11 WEB (21:12 WLC); 29:5. So the song of 18:7 has
Miriam's verb as well as her tambourines and dances, and *məḥōlâ* now counts seven verses in those files, three of them
this one song (18:6; 21:12 WLC; 29:5). The victory song that at the sea praised Yahweh praises two men here, and one
of them hears it as a threat. See [[Saul's Jealousy of David]], [[Singing Women (of Saul's time)]], and
[[Saul Has Slain His Thousands - 1 Samuel 18.7, 21.11, and 29.5]]. Source: stored WEB Exodus 15:21; 32:18; Numbers
21:17; 1 Samuel 18:6–8; 21:11; 29:5. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.
