## Analysis

**The phrase.** *Bayiṯ neʾĕmān*, *a sure house*, a noun with the Niphal participle of *ʾāman*, occurs in
the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings at 1 Samuel 2:35, 1 Samuel 25:28, and 1 Kings 11:38. Numbers 12:7
has the same pair in another construction: Moses is *neʾĕmān* in Yahweh's *house*, *bəḵol-bêṯî neʾĕmān*.
Nathan's oracle uses the verb, *wəneʾman bêṯəḵā*, *your house … will be made sure* (2 Samuel 7:16). (WLC,
OpenScriptures morphhb, searched 2026-11-17.)

**Priest, prophet, king.** The phrase moves across offices. At 2:35 it is promised to *a faithful priest*,
*kōhēn neʾĕmān*, who will walk before Yahweh's *anointed*. At 3:20 Samuel is *neʾĕmān* as a prophet (the
WEB's *established*). At 25:28 Abigail promises David *a sure house*, and in 2 Samuel 7:16 Nathan makes the
promise Yahweh's. At 1 Kings 11:38 the same promise is offered conditionally to Jeroboam, *as I built for
David*. The priestly promise of 1 Samuel 2 thus stands at the head of a sequence that becomes the royal
promise to David.

**Whose house?** 1 Samuel 2:35 does not name the faithful priest. Readers identify him with Samuel (3:20),
with Zadok, whom Solomon puts *in the place of Abiathar* (1 Kings 2:35, WEB checked), or, in Christian
reading, with a priest fulfilled in the Messiah. The *sure house* fits Zadok's line, which serves in the
temple after the house of Eli is removed; it fits Samuel's less well, since his sons *didn't walk in his
ways* (8:3, stored WEB). The wiki reports the readings and settles none. See
[[The Word Against the House of Eli - 1 Samuel 2.27-36 and 1 Kings 2.27]].

**Forever, and conditions.** The house of Eli had been told it would *walk before me forever* and lost the
promise (2:30). David's house is promised *forever* (2 Samuel 7:16); Jeroboam's sure house is offered *if
you will listen to all that I command you* (1 Kings 11:38). The same vocabulary carries an unconditional
and a conditional promise in different places, and the relation of the two is a standing question for
readers of Samuel and Kings, not settled here.

## Significance

- The connection ties the book's opening crisis of priesthood to its later crisis of kingship: Yahweh
  replaces a failed house with a faithful one, first at the altar and then on the throne.
