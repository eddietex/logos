# Wrestlings of God - Rachel at Naphtali's Birth and Jacob at the Jabbok

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 30:8** — *"Rachel said, 'I have wrestled with my sister with mighty wrestlings, and have
  prevailed.' She named him Naphtali."*
- **Genesis 32:24–28** — *"Jacob was left alone, and wrestled with a man there until the breaking of the
  day. When he saw that he didn't prevail against him, he touched the hollow of his thigh… 'Your name
  will no longer be called Jacob, but Israel; for you have fought with God and with men, and have
  prevailed.'"*
- Genesis 32:22 — *"his eleven sons"* cross the ford: the children of 29:31–30:24, present for the second
  scene
- Hosea 12:3–4 — the prophets' reading: *"in his manhood he contended with God… he struggled with the
  angel, and prevailed"*

## Analysis

**Two people in one household claim, two chapters apart, to have wrestled with *ʾĕlōhîm* and won.**

***Naptûlê ʾĕlōhîm niptaltî ʿim-ʾăḥōtî gam-yākōltî*** (30:8). **Word for word: *wrestlings of God I
have wrestled with my sister, and I have also prevailed.*** The WEB renders the first phrase *"mighty
wrestlings"*, and that is a defensible reading — **Hebrew uses *ʾĕlōhîm* as an intensifier** (*a mighty
prince* at 23:6, *a great trembling* at 1 Samuel 14:15) — **but the word on the page is *God*.** The
alternative reading takes the phrase at face value: the contest [[Rachel]] has been conducting is with
the one who *"has withheld from you the fruit of the womb"* (30:2), and her sister is only the visible
opponent. **Genesis does not decide, and this page does not either.**

**What is not ambiguous is the verb, and what happens to it two chapters later.**

| | **Rachel, 30:8** | **Jacob, 32:24–28** |
|---|---|---|
| the struggle | *naptûlê* — twisting, from ***patal*** | *wayyēʾābēq* — from ***ʾābaq***, to grapple in the dust |
| with whom | *"with my sister"* — and *ʾĕlōhîm* in the phrase | *"a man"*, then *"with God and with men"* |
| the outcome verb | ***yākōltî*** — *I have prevailed* | ***yākāltā*** — *you have prevailed*, and *lōʾ yākōl*, *he did not prevail*, of her opponent |
| what it produces | **a name**: Naphtali | **a name**: Israel |
| the cost | none stated | a dislocated hip, and a limp he keeps |

**The roots for *wrestle* are different**, which is why this page is filed as a verbal parallel rather
than a quotation: *patal* is twisting or being crooked, *ʾābaq* is grappling in dust. **The verb of
victory is the same, and so is the shape of the sentence** — a struggle, *ʾĕlōhîm*, and a claim to have
prevailed, discharged into a name.

### What the pairing says, and what it does not

**It does not say Rachel prefigures Jacob**, and the wiki declines to say it. Genesis makes no
connection, no character refers back, and Hosea 12:3–4 — the one prophetic text that revisits the
Jabbok — mentions only Jacob.

**What it does supply is a household vocabulary.** This is a family whose founding events are contests:
a birthright bought, a blessing stolen, an oracle about two nations struggling in a womb (25:22, where
the verb is *wayyitrōṣăṣû*, *they crushed one another*), fourteen years of contracted labour, a wage
dispute, and two sisters counting sons. **When somebody in this family gets a new name, it is usually
because they won something** — and the two sentences that say so in these words are a woman's, over a
child she did not bear, and a man's, at a river he is afraid to cross.

**And the second scene has the first one standing beside it.** Genesis 32:22 counts them: *"his two
wives, and his two servants, and his **eleven sons**"* — **the entire population of
[[The Birth of Jacob's Children]], sent over the ford before Jacob is left alone.** The children named
out of a contest between two women are physically present for the contest that renames their father.

### The one asymmetry worth stating

**Jacob's victory costs him.** He is touched at the hip, walks with a limp for the rest of the book,
and the blessing has to be extracted — *"I won't let you go, unless you bless me."* **Rachel's is
stated flatly and costs nothing, and Genesis then spends fourteen more verses on her still being
barren.** Naphtali is Bilhah's second son; Rachel's own does not arrive until 30:22, **and when he does
the text credits *God remembered* rather than anything she prevailed at.** **The claim to have prevailed
is made in the middle of a wait that is not over**, which is the honest reading of 30:8 and the one this
page keeps.

## Significance

- **It puts a woman's sentence and the patriarch's renaming in the same idiom**, in a book that almost
  never lets its women's speech carry that weight — and it does so without any text pointing at it.
- **It complicates the Jabbok in advance.** Genesis 32's *"you have fought with God and with men, and
  have prevailed"* reads differently once 30:8 has used *ʾĕlōhîm* and *yākōl* about a domestic quarrel.
  **The renaming of Israel is not the first time this household has claimed a victory over God**; it is
  the first time the claim is granted by the other party.
- **It leaves the ambiguity of 30:8 open, which is the point.** *Mighty wrestlings* and *wrestlings of
  God* are both available, and **which one is right decides whether Rachel thinks she is fighting her
  sister or the one who closed her womb.** See [[Barrenness]] and [[Names and Naming]].
