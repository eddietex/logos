# From the Wilderness Sentence to the Crossing of Zered

## Analysis

Numbers 14:26–35 announces the death of the numbered, murmuring generation in the wilderness,
with Caleb and Joshua excepted and the children promised entry. Deuteronomy 1:34–40 recalls
the exclusion oath and the continuing promise. Deuteronomy 2:14–16 then reports the military
generation’s death as the fulfillment of Yahweh’s oath, at the end of the thirty-eight years
from Kadesh Barnea to Zered.

Numbers 21:12 names a camp in the valley of Zered without explaining its chronological
significance. Deuteronomy narrates crossing the brook and attaches the generation’s passing
to it. These are complementary emphases; the itinerary alone does not provide the elapsed time.

The thirty-eight-year interval and the forty years in Deuteronomy 2:7 have different stated
scopes. The text does not demand that every duration be reduced to the same endpoints. Nor
should the statement that all the men of war died erase the exceptions explicitly named in
Numbers 14:30 and Deuteronomy 1:36–38.

## Significance

[[Judgment]] and [[Providence]] coexist: the people lacked nothing during the wilderness
journey (Deuteronomy 2:7), yet the condemned cohort did not survive it (2:14–16). The future
continues through the children. Sources: stored WEB Numbers 14:26–35; 21:10–15; Deuteronomy
1:34–46; 2:1–23.
