# Where It Pleases Him Best - The Runaway Slave and the Place Yahweh Chooses

  choose out of all your tribes"* (5); *"the place which Yahweh chooses in one of your tribes"* (14);
  and there *"your male servant, your female servant"* eat before Yahweh (18).

## Analysis

**One formula, two choosers.** In chapters 12–18 Deuteronomy says *"the place which Yahweh your God
shall choose"* again and again. In the Hebrew of the book, the verb form *yibḥar*, *he will choose*,
occurs 23 times. In 22 of them the subject is Yahweh: the place for sacrifice, tithe, feast, and
judgment, the king (17:15), and the Levite's place of service (18:6). The twenty-third is 23:16 (WLC
23:17), *bammāqôm ʾăšer-yibḥar*, and the subject is a runaway slave (WLC lemma search; subjects
read from stored WEB). The wording is the sanctuary's wording. The place is *"within one of your
gates"*, *bǝʾaḥad šǝʿārêkā*, which is built like 12:14's *bǝʾaḥad šǝbāṭêkā*, *"in one of your
tribes"*.

**What the echo does and does not show.** The text does not comment on the repetition, and the
formula's words are ordinary Hebrew. A slave choosing a town did not need a special idiom. So the
echo could be coincidence. What it does show is that within this book the right to choose where to
settle is given to two parties: Yahweh, for his name, and a fugitive slave, for himself. *"Where it
pleases him best"*, *baṭṭôb lô*, *in what is good to him*, puts the decision in his hands. The master
is not consulted, and Israel is not. The only command to Israel is negative: *"You shall not
oppress him"*. The verb, *yānâ*, is the one Exodus 22:21 and Leviticus 19:33 use for the alien.

**Servants at the chosen place.** In 12:18 and 16:11, *"your male servant, your female servant"* go up
with the household and rejoice *"in the place which Yahweh your God shall choose"*. In those laws the
slave goes where the household goes. In 23:16 the slave who has fled a master goes where he chooses.
The two kinds of law sit in the same book and the book does not reconcile them. Read together, they
put the servant twice in the sanctuary's language: once rejoicing at Yahweh's place, once choosing his
own.

**Delivered and escaped.** The verse before the slave law ends the camp law: Yahweh walks in the
camp *"to deliver you"* (23:14), *lǝhaṣṣîlkā*, a hiphil of *nāṣal*. The slave law begins with one who
*"has escaped"*, *yinnāṣēl*, the niphal of the same verb (WLC 23:15–16). Israel was delivered;
Israel is not to hand back a man who has escaped. The connection is in the vocabulary. The text does
not draw it.

**Choose life.** 30:19 gives Israel the same verb, *"Therefore choose life"*, *ûbāḥartā*. In
Deuteronomy, three parties choose: Yahweh his place, the fugitive his town, and Israel life or death.

## Significance

Deuteronomy's central formula for divine choice is lent, once, to the least powerful person in its
laws. That is the reason this page exists. It is recorded as an observation about wording, not as a
claim about the author's intent. Whether the law covers only foreign runaways or every slave is
unsettled; see [[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[The Two Flights of Hagar]]. See also
[[Within Your Gates - The Levite, the Foreigner, the Fatherless, and the Widow]].
