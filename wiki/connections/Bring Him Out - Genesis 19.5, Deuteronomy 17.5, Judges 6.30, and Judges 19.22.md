# Bring Him Out - Genesis 19.5, Deuteronomy 17.5, Judges 6.30, and Judges 19.22

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 19:4–5** — *"the men of the city, the men of Sodom, surrounded the house … 'Where are the men
  who came in to you this night? Bring them out to us, that we may have sex with them.'"* —
  [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]].
- **Deuteronomy 17:2–5** — a man or woman who *"has gone and served other gods"*: *"then you shall bring
  out that man or that woman, who has done this evil thing, to your gates … and you shall stone them to
  death with stones."* — [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]].
- **Judges 6:30** — *"Then the men of the city said to Joash, 'Bring out your son, that he may die,
  because he has broken down the altar of Baal, and because he has cut down the Asherah that was by
  it.'"* — [[Gideon Throws Down Baal's Altar]].
- **Judges 19:22** — *"the men of the city, certain wicked fellows, surrounded the house, beating at the
  door; and they spoke to the master of the house, the old man, saying, 'Bring out the man who came into
  your house, that we can have sex with him!'"* — [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]].

All quotations from stored WEB.

## Analysis

**The same demand in the same form.** Each text has the hiphil of *yāṣāʾ*, *bring out*: *hôṣîʾēm*,
*bring them out* (Genesis 19:5); *wəhôṣēʾtā*, *you shall bring out* (Deuteronomy 17:5); and the
imperative *hôṣēʾ* in Judges 6:30 and 19:22. Sodom, Ophrah, and Gibeah also share the subject, *ʾanšê
hāʿîr*, *the men of the city* (Genesis 19:4; Judges 6:27–30; 19:22). All from the Westminster Leningrad
Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb).

**The law's sentence turned around.** Deuteronomy 17 tells Israel to bring out the one who served other
gods and put him to death. At Ophrah the men of an Israelite city demand that a man be brought out to
die for the opposite act, for destroying another god's altar. The town keeps the form of the law and
reverses its content. Joash answers in the law's own form: *He who will contend for him, let him be put
to death* (*yûmat*, 6:31, WLC), the hophal used in the law's death sentences.

**Sodom and Gibeah.** Genesis 19 and Judges 19 are close parallels: a guest in a house at night, the men
of the city surrounding it, the demand *bring out* the guest, and a host who offers women instead. Judges
6:30 shares only the demand and the speakers. It has no guest, no house surrounded, and no sexual threat.
It is the mildest of the three, and the earliest in Judges. Whether the narrator of Judges meant the
reader to hear Ophrah in Gibeah is not stated; the comparison is the wiki's.

**Who is brought out.** At Sodom and Gibeah the demand is for strangers. At Ophrah it is for a son of the
town, demanded from his own father. The law of Deuteronomy 13:6–9 had said that the one who entices to
other gods, even *your son*, must be put to death, *your hand shall be first on him*. Joash is asked to
give up his son, and refuses.

## Significance

- **It measures Ophrah against the law.** The town applies the law's procedure to the wrong offense.
- **It anticipates Gibeah.** The pattern of the men of an Israelite city demanding a man be *brought out*
  appears early in Judges, before its worst form in chapter 19, where Gibeah repeats Sodom.
- **It explains Joash's reply.** His *let him be put to death* answers a legal demand with a legal
  sentence.
