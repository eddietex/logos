# Cuttings for the Dead - Leviticus 19.28, 21.5, and Deuteronomy 14.1

## Passages involved

- [[Holy Living in Field and Community]] — Leviticus 19:28.
- [[The Holiness of the Priests]] — Leviticus 21:5.
- [[A Holy People and Its Food]] — Deuteronomy 14:1.
- 1 Kings 18:28 — the prophets of Baal cut themselves (not yet ingested).
- Jeremiah 16:6 — cutting and baldness as mourning in Judah (not yet ingested).

## Analysis

Three Torah laws forbid marking the body in connection with the dead:

- **Leviticus 19:28**, to all Israel: *"You shall not make any cuttings in your flesh for the dead,
  nor tattoo any marks on you. I am Yahweh."* The cutting is *śereṭ* (WLC, morphhb).
- **Leviticus 21:5**, to the priests: *"They shall not shave their heads or shave off the corners of
  their beards or make any cuttings in their flesh."* The verse uses *qorḥâ* for the shaved head and
  the verb *śāraṭ* for the cutting (WLC, morphhb).
- **Deuteronomy 14:1**, to all Israel: *"You are the children of Yahweh your God. You shall not cut
  yourselves, nor make any baldness between your eyes for the dead."* The cutting is the verb
  *gādad* (*titgōdədû*), and the baldness is *qorḥâ* (WLC, morphhb).

So Deuteronomy takes the baldness that Leviticus 21:5 forbids to the priests and forbids it to the
whole people. It grounds the ban in the people's status — sons of Yahweh and *"a holy people"*
(14:1–2) — where Leviticus 19:28 grounds it only in *"I am Yahweh"*. That widening fits
Deuteronomy's language for Israel at 7:6 and 14:2. The wiki notes the fit as its own reading; the
text does not comment on it.

**The verb in Deuteronomy 14:1 recurs at Carmel.** 1 Kings 18:28 says the prophets of Baal *"cut
themselves in their way with knives and lances, until the blood gushed out on them"* (WEB, verified
from bible-api.com). The Hebrew is *wayyitgōdədû*, the same verb in the same reflexive form (WLC,
morphhb). There, cutting is part of worship, not mourning. Neither Torah law names a foreign
practice as its reason, so the Carmel scene shows one setting the practice had, not the reason for
the ban.

**The practice continued.** Jeremiah 16:6 foretells that in the coming judgment no one will
*"lament for them, cut themselves, or make themselves bald for them"* (WEB, verified from
bible-api.com). Jeremiah names these as normal mourning acts without objecting to them at that
point. The wiki records the gap between the law and the practice the prophet describes, and does
not resolve it. The Hebrew of Jeremiah 16:6 has not been checked here.

**What is not forbidden.** None of the three laws forbids grief. Israel weeps for Moses thirty days
(Deuteronomy 34:8), and the priests may still become unclean for their nearest kin (Leviticus
21:1–4).

## Significance

The body of a holy people is not to carry the marks of death. See [[Mourning and Lament]],
[[Holiness]], [[Life and Death]], and [[Adoption]] for the *children of Yahweh* clause.
