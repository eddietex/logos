# God Repaid - Adoni-Bezek, Joseph's Brothers, and Abimelech

## Passages involved

- [[Judah Goes Up First]] — **Judges 1:7** — *Adoni-Bezek said, "Seventy kings, having their thumbs and
  their big toes cut off, scavenged under my table. As I have done, so God has done to me."* (stored WEB)
- [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] — **Genesis 50:15** — *When Joseph's brothers saw that their father
  was dead, they said, "It may be that Joseph will hate us, and will fully pay us back for all the evil
  which we did to him."* (stored WEB)
- [[Jotham's Fable]] — **Judges 9:16** — *if you have dealt well with Jerubbaal and his house, and have
  done to him according to the deserving of his hands* (stored WEB)
- [[The Fall of Abimelech]] — **Judges 9:56–57** — *Thus God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech, which he
  did to his father, in killing his seventy brothers; and God repaid all the wickedness of the men of
  Shechem on their heads* (stored WEB)

## Analysis

**Seventy and seventy.** Judges opens and closes its first long section on the number seventy and on
repayment. Adoni-Bezek maimed *seventy kings* and says *As I have done, so God has done to me* (1:7).
Abimelech killed *seventy* brothers, and the narrator says *God repaid* (9:56). The verbs differ: 1:7
has *šillam*, *šālam*, *to repay in full*; 9:56–57 has the hiphil of *šûb*, *to bring back* (WLC). The
shape is the same. A man does to seventy, and God does to him. In 1:7 the man says it himself. In 9:56
the narrator says it.

**Brothers who fear repayment.** In Genesis–Judges *šûb* and *rāʿâ*, *evil*, are used together of
repayment only in Genesis 50:15 and Judges 9:56–57 (WLC; Exodus 32:12 also has both, but of Yahweh
turning from evil). Joseph's brothers say *hāšēb yāšîb lānû ʾēt kol-hārāʿâ ʾăšer gāmalnû ʾōtô*, *he will
fully pay us back for all the evil which we did to him*. The verb *gāmal*, *to deal out*, is the root of
Jotham's *gəmûl*, *the deserving of his hands* (9:16). Both stories are about brothers who wronged a
brother. Joseph's brothers fear that he will repay them, and he does not: *you meant evil against me,
but God meant it for good* (Genesis 50:20, stored WEB). Abimelech's victims cannot repay, and God does.

**The evil sent and the evil returned.** In Judges 9 *rāʿâ* frames the judgment. God sends *an evil
spirit*, *rûaḥ rāʿâ*, between Abimelech and Shechem (9:23), and at the end he *returns* the *evil* each
has done (9:56–57). The narrator also says who the wrong was done to: *which he did to his father*. The
house of Jerubbaal, to which Israel showed no *kindness* (8:35), is repaid by God.

## Significance

Judges keeps a tally the characters do not see. Adoni-Bezek, a Canaanite king, is the first in the
book to say that God pays back. Abimelech, an Israelite who made himself king, never says it, and the
narrator says it for him. Genesis 50 shows the other way such a story can end, with a brother who
leaves repayment to God and forgives. See [[Retribution]] and [[Brotherhood]].
