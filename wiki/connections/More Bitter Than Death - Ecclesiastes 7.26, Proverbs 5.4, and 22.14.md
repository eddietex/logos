# More Bitter Than Death - Ecclesiastes 7.26, Proverbs 5.4, and 22.14

## Passages involved

- [[Righteousness and Wisdom Under Question]] — Ecclesiastes 7:26.
- [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]] — Proverbs 5:3–5.
- [[A Good Name]] — Proverbs 22:14.
- [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]] — Proverbs 7:22–27.

## Analysis

Ecclesiastes 7:26 describes a woman in the vocabulary Proverbs uses for the adulteress —
bitterness, death, snares, and a fall that depends on one's standing before God:

> I find more bitter than death the woman whose heart is snares and traps, whose hands are chains. Whoever pleases God shall escape from her; but the sinner will be ensnared by her. (Ecclesiastes 7:26)

> but in the end she is as bitter as wormwood, and as sharp as a two-edged sword. Her feet go down to death. Her steps lead straight to Sheol. (Proverbs 5:4–5)

> The mouth of an adulteress is a deep pit. He who is under Yahweh’s wrath will fall into it. (Proverbs 22:14)

**Bitterness and death.** Proverbs 5:4–5 makes the adulteress bitter *in the end* and her
feet go down to death. Ecclesiastes compresses the two into one comparison: more bitter than
death. Proverbs 7:27 names her house *"the way to Sheol, going down to the rooms of death"*.

**Snare.** Proverbs 7:22–23 likens the young man to *"a fool stepping into a noose"* and *"a
bird hurries to the snare"*. Ecclesiastes moves the snare into the woman herself: her heart is
snares and traps, her hands chains.

**Who falls.** Proverbs 22:14 says the one under Yahweh's wrath falls into the pit;
Ecclesiastes says the one who pleases God escapes and the sinner is caught. In both, the fall
is not simply an accident of circumstance; it is tied to the person's standing before God. In
neither text is the order of cause spelled out — whether the fall is the judgment or reveals
it.

**What differs.** Proverbs addresses a son and warns him away. Ecclesiastes reports a
finding in the course of an inquiry (*"I find"*) and follows it at once with the much-debated
count of 7:28 and the general statement of 7:29. Proverbs' adulteress is identified by
adultery; Ecclesiastes 7:26 does not use the words *adulteress*, *strange*, or *foreigner* in
the WEB. The identification of the two figures is a reading supported by the shared imagery,
not a claim of the text. The comparison is in the English of the WEB; no Hebrew identity of
vocabulary between these verses is asserted.

## Significance

The parallel bears on how 7:26 is read. Taken alone, the verse can be heard as a verdict on
women; taken beside Proverbs, its relative clause — *"whose heart is snares and traps"* —
points to a particular figure the wisdom tradition already knew, and the one escaping her is
defined by pleasing God, not by avoiding women. See
[[Two Voices in the Street - The Strange Woman and Wisdom in Proverbs 7-9]], [[Sexual Sin]],
[[Sin]], and [[Life and Death]].
