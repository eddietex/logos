# Life Like a Shadow - Job 14.1-2 and Ecclesiastes 6.12

## Passages involved

- [[Though He Slay Me]] — Job 14:1–6, 21.
- [[Who Knows What Is Good]] — Ecclesiastes 6:12.
- [[From Dust to Dust]] — Ecclesiastes 3:22, the related question of what follows a person.

## Analysis

Job 14:2 says, in stored WEB, “He also flees like a shadow, and doesn’t continue.”
The image accompanies the flower cut down and a life of few days and trouble (1–2).
Job makes this brevity the basis of a plea: why bring such a creature into judgment,
and why not let him finish his allotted day in rest (3–6)? Later in the chapter the
man does not know the changing fortunes of his sons (21).

Ecclesiastes 6:12 places the shadow inside a question: who knows what is good for a
person during a vain life? It ends by asking who can tell what will be after him under
the sun. The preceding hypothetical two thousand years in 6:6 have not given humanity
mastery of good or of what follows. The image concerns life's passing; it does not
specify a number of years or identify the shadow with a soul.

The settings differ. Job addresses God and seeks relief from scrutiny. Ecclesiastes
poses questions without naming God in these verses or requesting a particular reprieve.
Ecclesiastes 3:22 supplies an internal comparison: enjoyment of present works is commended
even though a person cannot be shown what follows. The present portion and the unknown
future stand together, not as answers to every question about death.

Sources: immutable stored WEB Job 14:1–6, 21; Ecclesiastes 3:22; 6:6, 10–12. The
quotation is copied exactly from Job 14:2. Shared imagery is not proof of direct literary
dependence; no Hebrew wording or unverified external quotation is asserted.

## Significance

[[Life and Death]] gives the image its force; [[Wisdom]] and [[Vanity]] name the limits
it exposes in Ecclesiastes. Neither text allows the reader to turn the image alone
into a complete account of what a person experiences after death.
