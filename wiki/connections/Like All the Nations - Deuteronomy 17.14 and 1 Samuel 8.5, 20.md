# Like All the Nations - Deuteronomy 17.14 and 1 Samuel 8.5, 20

## Passages involved

- [[The Law for the King]] — Deuteronomy 17:14: *"I will set a king over me, like all the nations
  that are around me"*.
- [[Israel Asks for a King]] — 1 Samuel 8:5 — the elders to Samuel: *"Now make us a king to judge us like all the
  nations."*
- [[Israel Asks for a King]] — 1 Samuel 8:19–20 — *"No; but we will have a king over us, that we also may be like all
  the nations, and that our king may judge us, and go out before us, and fight our battles."*
- [[Saul Chosen by Lot at Mizpah]] — 1 Samuel 10:24–25 — *"Do you see him whom Yahweh has chosen …?"* … *Then Samuel
  told the people the regulations of the kingdom, and wrote it in a book, and laid it up before Yahweh.* (stored WEB)
- [[Kings Will Come Out of You]] — the promise of kings to Abraham, Sarah, and Jacob, before any
  request.

## Analysis

**The same request, foreseen and then made.** Deuteronomy 17:14 puts words in Israel's mouth for a
time after the conquest. 1 Samuel 8 has the elders say almost the same words. In the WLC the two
share the verb *śîm*, *set*, with *melek*, *king*, and the phrase *kəkol-haggôyim*, *like all the
nations*:

| | Hebrew (WLC) | WEB |
|---|---|---|
| Deuteronomy 17:14 | *ʾāśîmâ ʿālay melek kəkol-haggôyim ʾăšer səbîbōtāy* | *"I will set a king over me, like all the nations that are around me"* |
| 1 Samuel 8:5 | *śîmâ-lānû melek ləšopṭēnû kəkol-haggôyim* | *"make us a king to judge us like all the nations"* |
| 1 Samuel 8:20 | *wəhāyînû gam-ʾănaḥnû kəkol-haggôyim* | *"that we also may be like all the nations"* |

The phrase is the link; whether 1 Samuel was written with Deuteronomy in view, or the reverse, is a
dating question the wiki does not settle.

**The two answers differ.** Deuteronomy grants the request with conditions and does not call it
sin: the king must be chosen by Yahweh, an Israelite, not a multiplier of horses, wives, or gold,
and a reader of the law. In 1 Samuel 8:7 Yahweh tells Samuel *they have not rejected you, but they
have rejected me as the king over them*, and Samuel warns of what a king will take (8:11–18). The king is
given anyway. Readers have joined the two in different ways: Deuteronomy permits what 1 Samuel
regrets; or 1 Samuel condemns the motive (to be like the nations) and not the office; or the two
reflect different views of kingship in Israel. None is adopted here.

**What the people add.** In 1 Samuel 8 the people say what they want the king for: *"to judge us"*
(8:5) and to *"go out before us, and fight our battles"* (8:20). Deuteronomy gives the king neither
job. Judging belongs to the judges in the gates and the court at the chosen place (16:18–17:13), and
the speech before battle to the priest and officers (20:2–9). The king of 17:14–20 reads the law;
the king of 1 Samuel 8:20 is wanted for war.

**"Like the nations" elsewhere in Deuteronomy.** The same chapters warn Israel not to *"learn to
imitate the abominations of those nations"* (18:9) and not to ask how *"these nations serve their
gods"* so as to do likewise (12:30). The request of 17:14 is the one place where wanting to be like
the nations is allowed, and the law at once makes the king unlike theirs.

**Two further contacts, now that 1 Samuel 8 is ingested.** Deuteronomy's king is the one *whom
Yahweh your God chooses*, *ʾăšer yiḇḥar YHWH* (17:15, WLC). Samuel's warning ends on *your king whom
you will have chosen for yourselves*, *ʾăšer bəḥartem lāḵem* (8:18, WLC): the same verb, with the
people as its subject. And Deuteronomy's first limit is *he shall not multiply horses* (17:16);
Samuel's first item is that the king will take Israel's sons *for his chariots, and to be his
horsemen* (8:11). What the law withholds from a king is what Samuel says a king will do. Whether the
two passages were composed with each other in view is still not settled here.

**At Mizpah the law's two conditions are met in words.** Samuel presents Saul as *him whom Yahweh has chosen*,
*ʾăšer bāḥar-bô YHWH* (1 Samuel 10:24, WLC). The construction is Deuteronomy's *ʾăšer yiḇḥar YHWH … bô* (17:15,
WLC), with the same verb and the same preposition. After the acclamation Samuel *wrote it in a book*, *wayyiḵtōḇ
bassēp̄er* (10:25, WLC), and Deuteronomy's king is to *write himself a copy of this law in a book* (*wəḵāṯaḇ lô …
ʿal-sēp̄er*, 17:18, WLC). In 1 Samuel the writer is the prophet, not the king, and what is written is *mišpaṭ
hammelūḵâ*, *the regulations of the kingdom*, not *this law*. Its contents are not given. Samuel says the request
was a rejection of God (10:19), and still the king given is described in Deuteronomy's terms. The wiki records both
and does not reconcile them. See [[Saul Chosen by Lot at Mizpah]].

## Significance

The phrase is the hinge of [[Kingship and Empire]] in Israel: the request Deuteronomy anticipates is
the one that brings Saul. It also bears on [[The Nations]], since the desire to be like them is both
granted and limited. See [[The Law for the King]].

### 1 Samuel 16:8–13 — the one Yahweh chooses, from among his brothers

The law of the king had two conditions: *whom Yahweh your God chooses*, *ʾăšer yiḇḥar YHWH*, and *one from among your
brothers*, *miqqereḇ ʾaḥeḵā* (Deuteronomy 17:15, WLC). At Bethlehem Samuel says *Yahweh has not chosen*, *lōʾ-bāḥar
YHWH*, three times (16:8–10), and anoints David *in the middle of his brothers*, *bəqereḇ ʾeḥāw* (16:13, WLC). In 1
Samuel 16 the *brothers* are David's actual brothers, and the phrase is not a quotation. The law's two words, *choose*
and *in the midst of the brothers*, both appear in the scene. Saul was *him whom Yahweh has chosen* (10:24), and the
people's *king whom you have chosen* (12:13); here Yahweh's choice is made without the people. See
[[Samuel Anoints David]]. Sources: stored WEB Deuteronomy 17:15; 1 Samuel 10:24; 12:13; 16:8–13. Hebrew WLC
(OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.
