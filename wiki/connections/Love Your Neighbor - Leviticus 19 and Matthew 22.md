# Love Your Neighbor - Leviticus 19 and Matthew 22

## Passages involved

- [[Be Holy and Love Your Neighbor]] — Leviticus 19:17–18.
- Matthew 22:34–40 — verified WEB comparison; passage not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Leviticus 19:18 commands love of the neighbor as oneself after forbidding vengeance
and grudges. Verse 17 has already rejected hatred and required rebuke. Love therefore
belongs to a concrete set of communal obligations, not merely a statement of feeling.

In Matthew 22:34–40, a lawyer tests Jesus by asking about the greatest commandment.
Jesus first names wholehearted love of God, then names love of the neighbor as a
second commandment like it (39), and says that the law and prophets depend on the
two (40). The wording of the neighbor command corresponds to Leviticus 19:18.

This is a later explicit use of the command, not a prophecy-and-fulfillment pair.
Matthew’s summary does not erase the particular protections that surround the
command in Leviticus, and Leviticus 19:18 does not itself narrate Jesus’ exchange.

## Significance

The reception gives neighbor-love a summarizing role while retaining its origin within Torah.
