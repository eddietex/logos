# The Widow's Borders - Proverbs 15.25 and the Ancient Boundary Stone

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 15:25** — *"Yahweh will uproot the house of the proud, but he will keep the widow's borders
  intact."* — [[A Gentle Answer]]
- **Proverbs 22:28** — *"Don't move the ancient boundary stone, which your fathers have set up."*
  — [[Thirty Excellent Things]]
- **Proverbs 23:10–11** — *"Don't move the ancient boundary stone. Don't encroach on the fields of the
  fatherless: for their Defender is strong. He will plead their case against you."* — [[Thirty Excellent Things]]
- **Job 24:2–3** — *"There are people who remove the landmarks. They violently take away flocks, and feed
  them. They drive away the donkey of the fatherless, and they take the widow's ox for a pledge."* —
  [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]

  [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]]
- **Deuteronomy 19:14; 27:17** — the law against moving a neighbour's landmark, and a curse on whoever
  does. *(Not yet stored; paraphrased, not quoted.)*
- [[Thirty Excellent Things]] — Proverbs 22:22–23, 28; 23:10–11.

## Analysis

**A boundary is the only thing standing between a widow and her land.** In an agrarian society a field's
edge was marked, not fenced, and the mark could be moved by anyone with the strength to do it and no one
to stop him. The widow and the fatherless are the people Exodus names as having no protector, and the
law's response is that God takes the protector's place: *"they cry at all to me, I will surely hear their
cry"* (Exodus 22:23). **Proverbs 15:25 puts that promise in the language of property: Yahweh *"will keep
the widow's borders intact"*.** He is not only the hearer of her cry but the keeper of her boundary line.

**The line is set against the proud man's house.** The first half has Yahweh *uproot* the house of the
proud; the second has him *keep* the widow's borders. The proud man's estate is the threat from which the
widow's small one needs keeping, and the saying reverses the expected outcome: the large holding is torn
up, the small one stays.

**Proverbs turns the promise into a command twice.** *"Don't move the ancient boundary stone"* (22:28;
23:10), and the second time it names the victim and the reason: *"Don't encroach on the fields of the
fatherless: for their Defender is strong. He will plead their case against you"* (23:10–11). The
*Defender* of 23:11 is the keeper of 15:25 seen from the encroacher's side. In the WEB, 22:22–23 says the
same of the poor: *"Yahweh will plead their case"*.

**Job 24 shows the crime unpunished.** Job's list of the oppressors opens with exactly this act — *"There
are people who remove the landmarks"* — and moves straight to the fatherless and the widow: their donkey
driven off, her ox taken *"for a pledge"* (24:2–3). His complaint in that chapter is that God does not
visibly intervene: the chapter opens by asking why those who know God *"don't see his days"* (24:1). **So
the two books state the same fact from opposite ends.** Proverbs 15:25 says what Yahweh will do; Job 24 says
what Job has watched happen while he did not appear to. The wiki keeps both, as it has kept the other
promises of the sentence collection beside Job's counterexamples: the proverb is a general truth about
God's character, and Job's chapter is the testimony that it is not always visible in time.

### Proverbs 22–23 in context

In [[Thirty Excellent Things]] the boundary-stone command frames a block of commands. It stands at 22:28
with its reason in the fathers who set the stone, and returns at 23:10 fused with the fatherless and their
strong Defender. In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26) the two verses open
with the identical clause, and 22:23 and 23:11 share the phrase *yārîb rîbām*, *he will plead their case*;
23:11's *Defender* is *gōʾēl*. So the block's first and last commands name the same advocate in the same
words, for the poor man in court and the fatherless in his field.

## Significance

The connection ties [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] to [[Land and Sojourning]] at the smallest scale — not
the promised land, but one widow's field — and makes [[God]] the guarantor of a boundary no human court
was protecting. It also gives [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] a concrete form: the proud man is the one whose
house grows by moving other people's lines. See also
[[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]].
