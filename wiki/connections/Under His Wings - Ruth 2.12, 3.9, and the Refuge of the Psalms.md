# Under His Wings - Ruth 2.12, 3.9, and the Refuge of the Psalms

## Passages involved

- [[Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz]] — Ruth 2:12; Boaz to Ruth: *"May Yahweh repay your work, and a full
  reward be given to you from Yahweh, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."*
- **Ruth 3:9** *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)* — Ruth to Boaz at the threshing floor: *"I am Ruth your
  servant. Therefore spread the corner of your garment over your servant; for you are a near kinsman."*
- **Psalm 36:7** *(Psalms not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"How precious is your loving
  kindness, God! The children of men take refuge under the shadow of your wings."*
- **Psalm 57:1** — *"Yes, in the shadow of your wings, I will take refuge, until disaster has passed."*
- **Psalm 61:4** — *"I will take refuge in the shelter of your wings."*
- **Psalm 91:4** — *"He will cover you with his feathers. Under his wings you will take refuge."*
- Compare [[Jotham's Fable]] — Judges 9:15; the bramble's offer, *come and take refuge in my shade*.
- Compare [[On Eagles Wings - Exodus 19 and Deuteronomy 32]] — wings as rescue and carrying, not shelter.

## Analysis

**The vocabulary.** Ruth 2:12 has *laḥăsôṯ taḥaṯ kənāpāyw*, *to take refuge under his wings* (WLC).
*Ḥāsâ*, *take refuge* (lemma 2620), is rare outside poetry: in the WLC files read (Genesis–Ruth,
1 Samuel, Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes) it occurs at Deuteronomy 32:37, Judges 9:15, Ruth 2:12,
twice in Proverbs, and twenty-five times in Psalms. With *wings* (*kānāp̄*, lemma 3671) it appears
in Psalms 36:7, 57:1, 61:4, and 91:4 (English numbering; one higher in Hebrew for 36, 57, and 61). The
exact phrase *taḥaṯ kənāpāyw*, *under his wings*, occurs in those files only at Ruth 2:12 and Psalm 91:4.
Ruth 2:12 is the one narrative verse among them, and the one in which a speaker says the refuge has
already been taken (*bāʾṯ*, *you have come*, perfect).

**The pun the book makes.** *Kānāp̄* is a bird's wing and also the edge or corner of a garment. At 3:9
Ruth asks Boaz, *ûp̄āraśtā kənāp̄əḵā ʿal-ʾămāṯəḵā*, *spread your corner over your servant* (WLC; singular
*kənāp̄əḵā*, where 2:12 had the plural *kənāpāyw*). The man who blessed her by Yahweh's wings is asked to
spread his own. The text does not comment on the repetition. Read together, the two verses make Boaz the
means of the blessing he spoke. That is an inference from the shared noun, but the book uses no other
word for either.

**Covering and marriage.** Spreading a garment over a woman is elsewhere language of marriage: Yahweh
says to Jerusalem in Ezekiel 16:8 that he *spread my garment over you* (reported by reference; Ezekiel is
not stored and the verse was not checked here). Deuteronomy 22:30 (23:1 in Hebrew) forbids a man to
*uncover his father's skirt*, and *skirt* there is *kənap̄*, the same noun (WLC). See [[Marriage]] and
[[Nakedness and Covering]].

**The Psalms.** In Psalms 36, 57, 61, and 91 the one who shelters under wings is a worshiper in danger,
and the wings are God's. Psalm 36:7 sets the wings beside *your loving kindness*, *ḥasdəḵā*, the word
Naomi uses of Yahweh or of Boaz at Ruth 2:20. Psalm 57's heading places it in David's flight from Saul
(reported; Psalms not ingested). In Ruth, a Moabite widow is said to have done what the psalmists pray to
do.

**Limits.** Ruth does not quote the Psalms, and the Psalms do not mention Ruth. Which texts are earlier
is not argued here. The wings image may be a bird's, as Psalm 91:4's *feathers* suggests, or the winged
figures of the sanctuary (a reading found in commentators; reported, not assessed). Jotham's bramble
(Judges 9:15) offers the same verb of refuge as a mockery: a shade that a bramble cannot give.

## Significance

The phrase gives Ruth's choice in 1:16, *your God my God*, a name from Israel's prayer: she has come to
take refuge under the wings of *the God of Israel*. Boaz says it; the narrator does not. In chapter 3 the
same noun turns the blessing into a request Boaz can answer himself. See [[The Nations]], [[Redemption]],
[[Ruth (person)]], and [[Boaz]].
