# Who Is the Cushite Woman - Numbers 12.1, Exodus 2, and Habakkuk 3

## Passages involved

- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:1; *the Cushite woman whom he had married*
- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — Exodus 2:21; *He gave Moses Zipporah, his daughter*
- [[Jethro's Counsel]] — Exodus 18:2–6; Zipporah brought back
- **Habakkuk 3:7** — *I saw the tents of Cushan in affliction. The dwellings of the land of Midian trembled.* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The question.** Numbers 12:1 says Moses married a Cushite woman, and it says so twice. The only wife named is Zipporah, a Midianite. So either this is Zipporah under another description, or it is a second wife whom the text does not otherwise mention.

**For one wife.** Habakkuk 3:7 sets *kûšān* beside *ʾereṣ midyān* in poetic parallel (WLC), so a name like *Cush* could reach Midian. The narrator's silence about a second marriage fits this too. Against it, *kûšān* is a different word from *kûš* by the tags, and it occurs only at this one verse.

**For a second wife.** Elsewhere the name *Cush* means the land south of Egypt, and Rahlfs has *Αἰθιοπίσσης*. The double statement in the verse sounds like news. Against it, *lāqaḥ*, the verb, has no time marker, and the wife could be an old matter. Nothing says Zipporah had died or been left, though Exodus 18:2's *sent her away* stays unexplained.

**Why it matters less than it seems.** Yahweh's answer does not mention her, and it does not rebuke or defend the marriage. On either reading, the wiki's page for her can say only what verse 1 says. Her page is [[Cushite Woman (of Moses' time)]], filed apart from [[Zipporah]] so that the identification is not smuggled in by a filename.

**The objection itself is not stated.** Later readers have seen race, foreignness, or a slight to Zipporah. The text states none of these. Amos 9:7 and other prophets show Cushites as a people before God, but that is not an answer to this verse.

## Significance

The wiki does not decide. The two readings are kept on [[Zipporah]], [[Cush]], and the page for the woman herself. See [[Marriage]] and [[The Nations]].
