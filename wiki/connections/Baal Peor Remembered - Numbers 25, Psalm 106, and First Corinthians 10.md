## Analysis

Psalm 106:28–31 retells attachment to Baal Peor, the plague, and Phinehas’s intervention,
crediting it to him for righteousness across generations. Numbers 25:12–13 instead explicitly
formulates the covenant of peace and enduring priesthood. These are complementary emphases, not
identical wording.

First Corinthians 10:8 warns against sexual immorality and reports 23,000 falling in one day. Its
placement among wilderness warnings makes Peor the likely reference, though that verse does not
name the site. **Numerical tension:** Numbers 25:9 gives 24,000 plague deaths, while Paul gives
23,000 in one day. The time qualifier could distinguish the scope, but neither text supplies the
extra day or subgroup needed to demonstrate that reconciliation. Preserve both figures.

**Numbers' own retrospect.** Before any later text, Numbers 31:16 looks back on Peor: the Midianite women
caused the trespass *through the counsel of Balaam, … and so the plague was among the congregation of
Yahweh*. Neither Psalm 106 nor 1 Corinthians 10 names Balaam. See
[[The Counsel of Balaam - Numbers 31.16 and Its Later Readings]].

**Deuteronomy's retrospect.** Deuteronomy 4:3–4 recalls Peor to people who saw it: *all the men who
followed Baal Peor, Yahweh your God has destroyed them from among you. But you who were faithful to
Yahweh your God are all alive today.* It names neither Phinehas, the Midianite women, nor Balaam, and
gives no number. Where Psalm 106 makes Peor a story about intervention and Numbers 31:16 about
Balaam's counsel, Deuteronomy makes it a proof that obedience is life (4:1). Source: stored WEB
Deuteronomy 4:3–4.

## Significance

The comparison preserves the distinct claims of each passage while tracing judgment, intervention,
and the survival of the congregation.
