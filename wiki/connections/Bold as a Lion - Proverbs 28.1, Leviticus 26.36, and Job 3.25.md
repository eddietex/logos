# Bold as a Lion - Proverbs 28.1, Leviticus 26.36, and Job 3.25

## Passages involved
- [[The Wicked Flee When No One Pursues]] — Proverbs 28:1, 14, 18; flight, boldness, fear, and
  safety.
- Leviticus 26:36 — flight without a pursuer in the covenant warning; verified WEB, not yet
  ingested.
- [[Job of Uz]] — Job 1:1; Job’s integrity affirmed by the narrator.
- [[The Second Test]] — Job 2:3, 7; integrity affirmed amid undeserved ruin and disease.
- [[Let the Day Perish]] — Job 3:25–26; fear, trouble, and the absence of rest.

## Analysis

Proverbs 28:1 says: “The wicked flee when no one pursues; but the righteous are as bold as a lion.”
Leviticus 26:36 places a related picture inside the warning of judgment among enemies: “The sound of
a driven leaf will put them to flight”; “They will fall when no one pursues.” The resemblance is
visible in the verified WEB. Proverbs generalizes the insecurity of wickedness; Leviticus addresses
a covenant community under judgment. Neither literary dependence nor an exact Hebrew quotation is
established here.

**The fear of Job prevents a diagnostic reversal.** Job 1:1 calls him blameless and upright, and God
affirms his continuing integrity while describing ruin without cause in 2:3. Yet Job 3:25–26 speaks
of feared disaster arriving and of having no rest. Proverbs 28:18’s assurance that the blameless
person is kept safe stands against Job 2:7’s bodily affliction. These are an explicit tension, and a
contradiction if the sayings are made exceptionless guarantees. The reader cannot infer wickedness
simply from someone’s fear or suffering.

The chapter itself keeps fear from having only one meaning. Verse 14 blesses the person who always
fears, setting him against the hardened heart. Its WEB wording leaves the object unspecified; moral
vigilance is a contextual reading. Verses 12 and 28 also describe people hiding when the wicked
rise, without calling the hiding people wicked. Righteous boldness in verse 1 is therefore a moral
contrast, not a complete psychological profile of every righteous person.

## Significance

The comparison joins a wisdom saying to a covenant warning while preserving Job’s counterexample. It
strengthens [[Righteousness]] , [[Guilt and Conscience]] , and [[Retribution]] without making fear
proof of wrongdoing. It also distinguishes the caution opposed to hardening from the panic attached
to wickedness. See [[The Fear of God]] and [[Hardening of the Heart]].
