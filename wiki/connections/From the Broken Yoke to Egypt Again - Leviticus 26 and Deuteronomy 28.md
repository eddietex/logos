# From the Broken Yoke to Egypt Again - Leviticus 26 and Deuteronomy 28

## Passages involved
- [[Blessings for Obedience (Leviticus)]] — Leviticus 26:13.
- [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] — Leviticus 26:14–45.
- [[The Law for the King]] — Deuteronomy 17:16.
- [[Curses for Disobedience]] — Deuteronomy 28:47–48, 62–68.

## Analysis

Leviticus 26:13 grounds freedom in the exodus and the breaking of the yoke. Deuteronomy 28:48
threatens an iron yoke and service to enemies; verse 68 completes the reversal with return to
Egypt by ship. In the stored WEB the people offer themselves as slaves and find no buyer.
Deuteronomy 17:16 already forbids a king to send people back to Egypt for horses. Here return
is a threatened sanction, not a permitted royal policy.

Both covenant chapters threaten disease, failed harvests, defeat, consumption of children,
scattering, and fear abroad. Yet Leviticus 26:40–45 moves to confession and remembered covenant,
whereas Deuteronomy 28 ends at the nadir of attempted self-sale. Its destruction language must
be held alongside Leviticus 26:44’s refusal of utter destruction. The few survivors of
Deuteronomy 28:62 and the insecure life of 65–67 keep destruction from meaning simply that
no one remains. Neither warning should be rewritten to suppress the other’s emphasis.

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 26; Deuteronomy 17:16; 28:15–68. This is a comparison of the
English text, without an assertion of exact source-language identity or a named historical fulfillment.

## Significance

The loss is measured against liberation already received: land, family, freedom, and secure
life are undone. The reversal sharpens covenant responsibility without canceling the separate
witness to covenant preservation. See [[Covenant]], [[Curse]], and [[Slavery and Servitude]].
