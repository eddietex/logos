# Naked and Ashamed - Genesis 3 and Genesis 9

## Passages involved

- [[The Fall]] — Genesis 3:1–24, and 2:25 immediately before it
- [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]] — Genesis 9:20–27

## Analysis

The two scenes run the same five beats in the same order, and Genesis 9 is the only place in the book
that repeats the shape of Genesis 3 completely.

| | Genesis 3 | Genesis 9 |
|---|---|---|
| **The plant** | a tree whose fruit is *"good for food… a delight to the eyes"* (3:6) | a vineyard, the first in Scripture (9:20) |
| **The eating** | *"she took some of its fruit, and ate"* (3:6) | *"He drank of the wine and got drunk"* (9:21) |
| **The exposure** | *"their eyes were opened, and they both knew that they were naked"* (3:7) | *"He was uncovered within his tent"* (9:21) |
| **The covering** | fig leaves, then *"Yahweh God made coats of animal skins… and clothed them"* (3:21) | *"Shem and Japheth took a garment… and covered the nakedness of their father"* (9:23) |
| **The curse** | on the serpent and the ground (3:14, 3:17) | on Canaan (9:25) |

Both scenes turn on **eyes**. In Genesis 3 the serpent promises that *"your eyes will be opened"* and
they are, and the first thing they see is their own nakedness. In Genesis 9 the whole moral weight is
carried by who looks: Ham *saw* and told; his brothers took four clauses' worth of trouble not to see.
Knowledge in both cases is what ruins the scene, and in both cases the remedy is a garment placed by
someone else.

**What changes is who does everything.** In Genesis 3 the fruit is forbidden by God, the exposure is
discovered by God's question, the covering is made by God, and the curse is God's. In Genesis 9 God
does not appear at all — the vineyard is planted by a man, the exposure is self-inflicted (the verb at
9:21 is reflexive), the covering is done by brothers, and the curse is pronounced by a human being for
the first time in Scripture. The primeval history has handed the whole apparatus over to people, and
the result is worse: God's curse in Genesis 3 fell on a serpent and on soil and spared the couple,
while Noah's falls on a grandson who was not in the room.

**A third scene completes the group.** Genesis 19:30–38 has Lot made drunk in a cave by his daughters
and uncovered, producing Moab and Ammon — Israel's other two neighbours explained by a drunkenness.
The Bible's second and third episodes of intoxication both end in an etiology of a hostile people, and
both are told without a word of narratorial comment.

## Significance

The parallel is what makes Genesis 9:18–29 legible as more than an embarrassing anecdote. Placed
against Genesis 3 it is the primeval history's demonstration that the flood changed the world's
population and not its problem: God has just promised never to drown it again (9:11) precisely because
*"the imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth"* (8:21), and the very next paragraph proves
the premise using the best man alive.

It also fixes the reading of Genesis 3 in one respect. If nakedness there were merely about sex, the
Genesis 9 echo would make no sense, since nothing sexual is stated in the tent. What both scenes share
is **exposure before someone who should not be looking, and a covering supplied from outside** — which
is the shape [[Nakedness and Covering]] follows to Ezekiel 16:8, Zechariah 3:3–5, and Revelation 3:18.

Finally, the pair sets up the book's larger argument. Genesis 3 ends with humanity outside a garden;
Genesis 9 ends with humanity divided into a blessed line, an enlarged line, and a slave line. The next
two chapters scatter it across the earth. Every one of those is a step further from the world of
Genesis 1, and Genesis 12:3 answers all of them at once, through one man out of Shem's line: *"All
the families of the earth will be blessed through you."*
