# An Altar of Uncut Stones - Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 27, and Joshua 8

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 20:24–26** — an altar of earth, or of stones that no tool has touched, and no steps —
  [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]]
- **Deuteronomy 27:5–7** — the altar to be built on Mount Ebal after the crossing of the Jordan
  *(not yet ingested)*
- **Joshua 8:30–31** — Joshua builds it *"as it is written in the book of the law of Moses"*
  *(not yet ingested)*
- **Exodus 27:1–8** — the tabernacle's altar of acacia wood and bronze, a different provision —
  [[The Altar and the Court]]

## Analysis

**The law of Exodus 20:25 is commanded again for a specific place, and then carried out.** WEB text
was fetched from bible-api.com (`translation=web`) on 2026-10-23, and Hebrew was checked against
the Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures `morphhb`).

- Exodus 20:25: *"If you make me an altar of stone, you shall not build it of cut stones; for if you
  lift up your tool on it, you have polluted it."*
- Deuteronomy 27:5–6: *"There you shall build an altar to Yahweh your God, an altar of stones. You
  shall not use any iron tool on them. You shall build Yahweh your God's altar of uncut stones. You
  shall offer burnt offerings on it to Yahweh your God."*
- Joshua 8:31: *"as Moses the servant of Yahweh commanded the children of Israel, as it is written
  in the book of the law of Moses, an altar of uncut stones, on which no one had lifted up any iron.
  They offered burnt offerings on it to Yahweh and sacrificed peace offerings."*

**The verbal link is close in Hebrew.** Exodus 20:25 has *hēnaptā*, *you lift up*, and Deuteronomy
27:5 has *tānîp*, both with the lemma of *nûp*, *wave* or *lift* (WLC). Exodus names the implement
*ḥarbəkā* (lemma *ḥereb*, *sword*), and Deuteronomy names it *barzel*, *iron*. Deuteronomy 27:6
calls the stones *ʾăbānîm šəlēmôt*, *whole stones*. That adjective carries a different lemma number
from *šəlāmîm*, the *peace offerings* of Exodus 20:24 and Deuteronomy 27:7. This page notes the
resemblance in sound without claiming a wordplay the text does not mark. Joshua 8 was not checked in
Hebrew in this pass.

**Both later texts keep both offerings of Exodus 20:24.** Exodus names burnt offerings and peace
offerings. Deuteronomy 27:6–7 commands burnt offerings and peace offerings, with eating and
rejoicing, and Joshua 8:31 reports both.

**The crafted altar is now ingested.** [[The Altar and the Court]] describes its acacia planks,
metal covering, horns, grating, and carrying poles. The instruction for hollow planks (27:8)
does not state an earth filling. Such a filling may be proposed to reconcile the altar with
20:24, but is not supplied by this text. The distinct prescriptions remain in view without
treating either as a mistaken summary of the other.

## Significance

**This is the clearest case of the Exodus 20 altar used *"in every place"*, away from the
tabernacle.** Exodus 27:1–8 prescribes a crafted altar of acacia wood overlaid with bronze for the
sanctuary, and the two provisions are not reconciled in Exodus (see
[[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]]). The Ebal altar shows that the uncut-stone law was understood to
be in force for altars outside the sanctuary, at least by the writers of Deuteronomy and Joshua. It
does not by itself show how Exodus 20:24's *"every place"* relates to Deuteronomy 12's *"the place
which Yahweh your God shall choose"*. That question stays open.

**The Ebal altar is at Shechem's mountains**, between which the blessings and curses are read (see
[[Shechem (city)]]), where Abram built his first altar in Canaan (Genesis 12:6–7). See also
[[The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem]], [[Sacrifice and Offering]], and [[Holiness]].

**A second provision about nakedness at the altar.** Exodus 20:26 forbids steps to the altar
*"that your nakedness may not be exposed to it"*. Exodus 28:42–43 gives the priests linen
breeches *"to cover the flesh of their nakedness"* when they come near the altar, *"that they
don't bear iniquity, and die"*. Exodus does not relate the two rules or say how the larger
sanctuary altar of 27:1–8 was approached; reading them as guarding the same exposure is an
inference, not a statement of either text. See [[The Priestly Garments]].
