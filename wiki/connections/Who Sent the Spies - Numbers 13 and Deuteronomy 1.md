## Analysis

Moses tells this story twice, and the two tellings differ.

| | Numbers 13 | Deuteronomy 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Who starts it | *Yahweh spoke to Moses* (1) | *You came near to me, everyone of you* (22) |
| The purpose | see what the land and people are (18–20) | *the way by which we must go up, and the cities* (22) |
| Moses | sends them *according to the commandment of Yahweh* (3) | *The thing pleased me well* (23) |
| How far | Zin to Rehob (21) | *the valley of Eshcol* (24) |
| The verb | *tûr* | *ḥāpar*, *search* (22), and *rāgal* (24) (WLC) |
| The report | mixed, then *an evil report* (27–33) | *It is a good land which Yahweh our God gives to us* (25) |
| The fear | spoken by the spies (31–33) | spoken by the people, quoting *our brothers* (27–28) |

**The usual harmony** is that the people asked, Moses took it to Yahweh, and Yahweh allowed it,
which makes *send for yourself* a concession. That is reported, and it is not said in either
chapter. Numbers does not deny a request, and Deuteronomy does not deny a command.

**Each telling has a reason to choose as it does.** Numbers shows a mission Yahweh ordered, so the
failure lies entirely with what the men made of it. Deuteronomy addresses the sons of that
generation and says *you*, so the initiative and the fault both lie with the people. That reading of
purpose is the wiki's.

**The verdict does not differ.** Deuteronomy 1:26 says *Yet you wouldn't go up, but rebelled against
the commandment of Yahweh your God*. Numbers 14 says the same in other words; see [[The People Refuse the Land]], whose sentence Deuteronomy 1:34–40 retells, adding Moses' own exclusion at 1:37. Neither account says that wanting
information was the sin.

## Significance

- The two accounts agree on the facts that carry the judgment: twelve men, fruit, the fear, and the
  refusal.
- They differ on who began it, and the wiki keeps both claims.
