# No Covenant Yet a Binding Oath - Deuteronomy and Gibeon

## Passages involved
- [[Laws for Going to War]]
- [[The Gibeonite Deception]]
- Source comparisons: stored WEB Joshua 2, 6, 9; Deuteronomy 7:1–2; 20:10–18.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 7:1–2 forbids a covenant with the named peoples of the land, including Hivites.
Deuteronomy 20:10–18 allows peace and forced labor for distant cities but distinguishes the cities
of those peoples. Joshua 9:6–7 makes distance the precise issue in negotiations; the lie temporarily
places the envoys in the wrong category.

Once their identity is known, the princes insist that the oath by Yahweh prevents killing them and
that its violation risks wrath (18–20). Joshua both rescues them and curses them to continuing labor
(23–27). The treaty conflicts with the earlier prohibition; survival under oath does not erase that
conflict. No divine ruling here explains how to rank the two obligations.

## Significance

The law supplies the stakes of the deception. The narrative shows a costly response to an improperly
made treaty rather than a repeal of the conquest command.
