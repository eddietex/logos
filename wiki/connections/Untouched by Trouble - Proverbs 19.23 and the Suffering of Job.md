# Untouched by Trouble - Proverbs 19.23 and the Suffering of Job

## Passages involved

- [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] — Proverbs 19:3, 23.
- [[Job of Uz]] — Job 1:1.
- [[The Wager in Heaven and the Four Messengers]] — Job 1:8, 13–22.
- [[The Second Test]] — Job 2:3, 7.

## Analysis

The stored WEB of Proverbs 19:23 reads:

> The fear of Yahweh leads to life, then contentment; he rests and will not be touched by trouble.

It promises more than moral wisdom: it describes contentment, rest, and protection. The last clause
must not silently be rewritten to say merely that the sufferer has a good attitude.

**Job supplies the counterexample on the same moral premise.** Job 1:1 calls him blameless, upright,
and God-fearing. Yahweh confirms that judgment in 1:8. The losses in 1:13–19 then take his property,
servants, and children, and 2:7 adds painful sores. Yahweh's statement in 2:3 says he maintains
integrity despite being ruined without cause. These are not the claims of an unreliable friend; the
narrative and divine speech establish the premise before and during the affliction.

**The contradiction is in an exceptionless reading.** If Proverbs 19:23 means every God-fearing
person is always protected from present trouble, the two accounts conflict. Reading it as a general
wisdom assurance lets the proverb keep its direction without making it a diagnostic law. That is an
interpretive judgment, not a qualifier inserted into the verse. The texts do not here explain a
timetable that resolves every instance.

Proverbs 19:3 offers a different case: a fool wrecks his own way and rages against Yahweh. It cannot
be substituted for Job's stated innocence merely because Job later protests. Nor does Job's eventual
restoration make the earlier loss unreal. The wiki therefore keeps the promise and its narrative
counterexample together.

## Significance

[[The Fear of God]] cannot be inferred from a person's present comfort, and [[Retribution]] cannot
be read backward from catastrophe to guilt. [[Suffering]] requires attention to what the source says
about the sufferer, including the explicit testimony about [[Job (person)]] .
