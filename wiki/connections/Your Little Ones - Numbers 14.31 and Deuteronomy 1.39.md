# Your Little Ones - Numbers 14.31 and Deuteronomy 1.39

## Passages involved

- [[The People Refuse the Land]] — Numbers 14:3, 23 (Rahlfs), 31, 33
- [[The Sentence and the Presumptuous Ascent]] — Deuteronomy 1:34–46.

## Analysis

### Deuteronomy’s sentence and ascent

Deuteronomy 1:39 is now ingested within the sentence: the children their parents feared would be
captured or killed are promised possession. Their lack of knowledge of good or evil is stated, but
no numerical age threshold is given here. Numbers 14:29–31 supplies the twenty-year census boundary;
the two formulations are retained without turning Deuteronomy’s phrase into a universal age rule.

Source for this update: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 14 and 20.

**The accusation is quoted back.** The fathers said it first: *nāšênû wəṭappēnû yihyû lābaz*
(Numbers 14:3, WLC). Yahweh returns it: *wəṭappəkem ʾăšer ʾămartem lābaz yihyeh* (14:31). Moses
repeats that verbatim at Deuteronomy 1:39, *wəṭappəkem ʾăšer ʾămartem lābaz yihyeh*. So their stated
reason for going back was the one group the sentence spares. Their wives drop out of Yahweh's
answer.

**Deuteronomy adds a line, and Greek Numbers has it too.** *Ûbənêkem ʾăšer lōʾ-yādəʿû hayyôm ṭôb
wārāʿ* (Deuteronomy 1:39, WLC), *your children, who today have no knowledge of good or evil*. WLC's
Numbers 14 lacks this. Rahlfs's Numbers 14:23 has a long plus: *τὰ τέκνα αὐτῶν ἅ ἐστιν μετ᾿ ἐμοῦ
ὧδε, ὅσοι οὐκ οἴδασιν ἀγαθὸν οὐδὲ κακόν, πᾶς νεώτερος ἄπειρος, τούτοις δώσω τὴν γῆν*. So the Greek
brings the idea into Numbers. No apparatus was read, and whether this harmonizes to Deuteronomy or
preserves something older cannot be said here.

**The exemption rests on a line.** The sentence reaches those twenty and older (14:29), matching the
census of [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Deuteronomy gives a different basis, not an age
but having no knowledge of good or evil. The phrase *good or evil* recalls the tree of
[[The Garden of Eden]], which the wiki notes as an echo of wording only, and neither passage draws
on the other.

**The children still pay.** Numbers 14:33 makes them shepherds in the wilderness for forty years.
Their inclusion is not an escape from what their fathers did. See
[[Visiting the Iniquity of the Fathers - Job 21.19, Jeremiah 31, and Ezekiel 18]].

**Sources:** Hebrew from WLC (morphhb); Greek from Rahlfs 1935 (eliranwong LXX-Rahlfs-1935); WEB of
Numbers from `raw/bible/`, and of Deuteronomy from bible-api.com, 2026-11-02.

## Significance

- The generation that entered was the one its parents had given up for lost.
