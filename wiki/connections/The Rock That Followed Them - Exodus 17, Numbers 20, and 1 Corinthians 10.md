# The Rock That Followed Them - Exodus 17, Numbers 20, and 1 Corinthians 10

## Analysis

**Two stories, forty years apart in the narrative, both ending in a place called Meribah.** In Exodus 17 the
people quarrel at Rephidim, God says *strike*, Moses strikes once, and no one is punished. In Numbers 20 the
people quarrel at Kadesh, God says *"speak to the rock"*, Moses says *"Hear now, you rebels! Shall we bring
water out of this rock for you?"* and strikes it twice — and he and Aaron are told they will not bring the
assembly into the land.

| | Exodus 17:1–7 | Numbers 20:2–13 |
|---|---|---|
| Place | Rephidim; *"the rock in Horeb"* | Kadesh, in the wilderness of Zin |
| Complaint | *"Why have you brought us up out of Egypt?"* | *"Why have you made us to come up out of Egypt?"* |
| Against | Moses | Moses and Aaron |
| Word for *rock* | ***ṣûr*** | ***selaʿ*** |
| Command | *"You shall strike the rock"* | *"Speak to the rock before their eyes"* |
| What Moses does | *"Moses did so in the sight of the elders"* | *"struck the rock with his rod twice"* |
| Witnesses | the elders | the whole assembly |
| God's glory | not mentioned | *"Yahweh's glory appeared to them"* |
| Outcome for Moses | none | barred from the land |
| Name | Massah and Meribah | *"the waters of Meribah"* |

(Hebrew words from the WLC: *ṣûr* at Exodus 17:6; *selaʿ* at Numbers 20:8, 10, 11.)

**What Moses did wrong at Kadesh is not stated in one place**, and the readings differ: he struck instead of
speaking; he struck twice; he said *"Shall we bring water"* as though the power were his and Aaron's; he called
the people *"rebels"*. God's own verdict names unbelief and a failure *"to sanctify me in the eyes of the
children of Israel"* (Numbers 20:12); Psalm 106:33 says *"he spoke rashly with his lips"*. **Exodus 17 is the
background every reading assumes**: the first time, striking was the command. The wiki records the readings
and adopts none. **Many scholars read the two as two tellings of one tradition**; the text presents them as
two events at two places, and the wiki follows the text.

**The Psalms fold the two together and make the rock the measure of God's power to provide.** Psalm 78 uses
both words — *ṣûrîm* and *selaʿ* in 78:15–16, *ṣûr* in 78:20 (WLC) — and turns the water into the people's
argument for doubting: *"Behold, he struck the rock, so that waters gushed out … Can he give bread also?"*
**Psalm 114:8 makes it creation reversed**: *"who turned the rock into a pool of water"*.

**Deuteronomy turns *ṣûr* into a title.** Moses' song calls God ***haṣṣûr***, *"The Rock"* (Deuteronomy 32:4;
WLC), and repeats it through the song (32:15, 18, 30, 31). **The first *ṣûr* in Scripture is the rock God stood
on at Horeb** (Exodus 17:6; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). The song does not mention Massah; the line from
the one to the other is drawn by later readers.

**Paul draws it explicitly.** *"They drank of a spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ"*
(1 Corinthians 10:4). The Greek word is ***petra*** (SBLGNT). **Three things are notable**: the rock is
*following* (*akolouthousēs*), which neither Exodus nor Numbers says; it is *spiritual* (*pneumatikēs*); and
it *was* Christ, in the past tense, so that Christ is placed in the wilderness with Israel. **The *following*
rock is commonly connected with later Jewish traditions of a well or rock that went with Israel through the
wilderness; the wiki has not checked those sources.** The two stories, at the start and the end of the
wilderness, may themselves have suggested a rock that was there both times. Paul's purpose is a warning, not
a doctrine of the rock: *"However with most of them, God was not well pleased"* (10:5; WEB), and
four verses later, *"Let us not test Christ, as some of them tested"* (10:9) — the sin of Massah. See
[[Do Not Test Yahweh - Massah in Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and Hebrews]] and
[[Baptized into Moses - The Sea in 1 Corinthians 10 and Hebrews 11]].

**John 7:37–38 is commonly read against the same background**: on the last day of the feast Jesus offers
drink to anyone thirsty and speaks of *"rivers of living water"*. **John does not cite Exodus 17**, and the
Scripture Jesus refers to (*"as the Scripture has said"*) is not identified in the text; the wiki records the
reading and does not adopt it. Compare [[Bread from Heaven - The Manna and John 6]], where John does name the
manna.

## Significance

**The rock is struck once and obeyed, then struck twice and disobeyed, and Scripture remembers both.** Exodus 17
gives the wilderness its first picture of God providing out of what cannot provide — water from stone, on the
rock where he stood. Numbers 20 makes the same act the reason the lawgiver does not enter the land. Deuteronomy
makes *the Rock* a name of God; Paul makes the rock Christ. **The thread runs from a camp with no water to a
name for God, and the turn in it is a single verb: *strike* or *speak*.**
