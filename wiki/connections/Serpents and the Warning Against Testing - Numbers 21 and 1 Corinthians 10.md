# Serpents and the Warning Against Testing - Numbers 21 and 1 Corinthians 10

## Passages involved
- [[The Brass Serpent]] — Numbers 21:4–9.
- [[The Fire at Taberah]] — Numbers 11:1–3, narrative comparison.
- 1 Corinthians 10:9 — verified WEB; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Numbers 21 moves from complaint to deadly snakes, confession, intercession, and life.
The complaint–prayer–relief sequence recalls Numbers 11:1–3, but Taberah's fire abates,
whereas Numbers 21 describes survival through looking without saying the snakes vanish.

First Corinthians 10:9 recalls those who tested and perished by serpents as a warning
to its hearers. The WEB reads Christ as the one not to test. This page reports that
translation without adjudicating the verse's textual variants or asserting a Greek reading.
Numbers 21:5 names God and Moses as the targets of the speech; Paul's formulation is
a later application, not a quotation of that sentence.

Paul's warning foregrounds the deaths, while John 3:14–17 foregrounds the lifted serpent
and life; see [[Lifted Up - The Brass Serpent and the Son of Man]]. Neither emphasis
should remove the other part of the Numbers narrative.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers 11:1–3 and 21:4–9; bible-api.com WEB 1 Corinthians 10:9
and John 3:14–17, verified 2026-11-04.

## Significance

The rescue story also warns against testing: provision of a remedy does not make the deaths unreal.
