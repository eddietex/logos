# The Scepter and Shiloh - Genesis 49.10 and Its Readings

## Passages involved

- Genesis 49:10 — *"The scepter will not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his
  feet, until he comes to whom it belongs. To him will the obedience of the peoples be"*
- Genesis 17:6; 35:11 — kings promised to [[Abraham]] and to [[Jacob]], with no tribe named
- Numbers 24:17 — Balaam's *"a scepter will rise out of Israel"*, the same noun ***šēbeṭ***
- Ezekiel 21:27 — *"until he comes whose right it is; and I will give it to him"*
- Psalm 60:7 / 108:8 — *"Judah is my scepter"*
- 1 Chronicles 5:2 — *"Judah prevailed above his brothers, and from him came the prince"*
- Joshua 18:1; 1 Samuel 1–4 — Shiloh, the sanctuary town, for the place-name reading
- Revelation 5:5; Hebrews 7:14 — the tribe named in the New Testament

## Analysis

**Three Hebrew words carry the weight of the whole verse, and nobody can read them with
confidence:** ***ʿad kî-yābōʾ šîlōh***.

**Everything before them is uncontested.** ***Šēbeṭ*** is a rod — a ruler's mace, and also the word
for a *tribe*, which is the pun the verse is built on: **the scepter will not depart from the
scepter-tribe.** ***Məḥōqēq***, *the ruler's staff*, is the marshal's baton, and ***mibbên
raglāyw***, *from between his feet*, is where a seated ruler rests it. **The sentence to that point
says that rule stays with [[Judah]].** **Everything after them is uncontested too**: *"to him will
the obedience of the peoples be"* assigns that rule an object.

**The four readings of the three words:**

| reading | what *šîlōh* is taken as | result |
|---|---|---|
| ***šellōh*** | relative *še-* + *lô*, *that which is to him* | *"until he comes to whom it belongs"* — **WEB, and the Septuagint's sense** |
| ***šay lōh*** | two words, *tribute to him* | *"until tribute is brought to him"* |
| **Shiloh** | the sanctuary town of Joshua 18:1 | *"until he comes to Shiloh"* |
| **a title** | a personal designation of the coming one | *"until Shiloh come"* — **KJV, and the older reading on both sides** |

**The Septuagint reads the first**: *heōs ean elthē ta apokeimena autō*, *until the things stored up
for him come* — which requires *šellō* and treats the subject as plural. **The Samaritan Pentateuch
and the Syriac agree in substance.** **Ezekiel 21:27 is the closest thing to an ancient quotation**
— *ʿad-bōʾ ʾăšer-lô hammišpāṭ*, *"until he comes whose right it is"* — **and it uses the ordinary
relative *ʾăšer lô* where Genesis has *šîlōh*, which is the strongest single argument that the
prophet read Genesis the way WEB does.

**Against that:** the place name Shiloh is spelled this way, the Hebrew consonants are exactly the
place name, and **Shiloh is where the ark and the tent stand for the whole period of the judges**
(Joshua 18:1; 1 Samuel 4:3) — **so a reader who knew the book of Judges could take 49:10 as marking
the end of Judah's pre-monarchic priority.** The difficulty is that nothing in Israel's rule arrives
at Shiloh; **Shiloh is where the priesthood is, and it is destroyed** (Jeremiah 7:12–14).

**Against the *tribute* reading:** it is elegant, it suits *"the obedience of the peoples"* in the
next clause, and it has no ancient version behind it.

## Significance

**All four readings have been read messianically, and the wiki records that rather than adjudicating
it.** The rabbinic tradition treats *Shiloh* as a name of the Messiah (Genesis Rabbah 98:8; the
Targums render it *the Messiah, whose is the kingdom*); the Christian tradition from Justin onward
reads it the same way and adds Revelation 5:5; **and the *"to whom it belongs"* reading yields the
same result by a different route** — something that belongs to Judah's line arrives, and the peoples
obey.

**What the verse indisputably does is attach rule to a tribe.** [[Abraham]] was told *"kings will
come out of you"* (17:6) and [[Jacob]] the same (35:11), **with no tribe named**; **Genesis 49:10 is
where the promise acquires an address**, three centuries before Israel has a king and while the man
speaking is dying in Egypt. **1 Chronicles 5:2 is the only verse in Scripture that states the
division outright** — the birthright to [[Joseph]], the prince from [[Judah]] — **and it is
commenting on Genesis 48 and 49 read together.**

**The wiki adopts the WEB's rendering** because it follows the oldest versional evidence and the
Ezekiel echo, **and states plainly that the Hebrew consonants do not decide between the four.** See
[[Jacob Blesses His Sons]], [[Kingship and Empire]], [[Judah]], [[Prophecy and Vision]],
[[Kings Will Come Out of You]] and [[The Lion of Judah - Genesis 49.9 and Revelation 5.5]].
