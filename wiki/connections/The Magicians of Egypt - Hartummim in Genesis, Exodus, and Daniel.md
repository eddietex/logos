# The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 41:8, 24** — *"all of Egypt's magicians and wise men … there was no one who could interpret
  them to Pharaoh"* — [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Exodus 7:11, 22** — the magicians match the rod-to-serpent and the water-to-blood
- **Exodus 8:7** — they match the frogs
- **Exodus 8:18–19** — **they fail at the gnats**: *"This is God's finger"*
- **Exodus 9:11** — *"the magicians couldn't stand before Moses because of the boils"*
- **Daniel 1:20; 2:2, 10–11, 27; 4:7, 9; 5:11** — the same word, in a Babylonian guild, failing the same
  way
- **Isaiah 19:3, 11–14; 44:25; 47:12–13** — the prophets on the collapse of Egyptian and Babylonian
  counsel
- **2 Timothy 3:8** — *"Jannes and Jambres"*, the names later tradition gave the two of Exodus 7

## Analysis

**The word is ***ḥarṭummîm***, and it occurs in only three places in the Hebrew Bible: Genesis 41,
Exodus 7–9, and Daniel 1–2.** **Every one of them is a scene in which a foreign king's professional
establishment is put beside an Israelite and comes off worse.**

**The etymology is probably Egyptian — *ḥry-tp*, *chief*, in the title *ḥry-tp ḥry-ḥb*, the *chief lector
priest***, the temple official responsible for the ritual books. **That is the point of the word.** It
does not mean *conjuror*. **It means a credentialed member of a learned profession**, the holder of an
office with a training, a library and a state salary. **The WEB's *magicians* is the traditional
rendering and it undersells the institution.**

### Genesis: the guild is summoned and produces nothing

**41:8 is careful to say that the whole apparatus was called** — *"all of Egypt's magicians and wise
men"* — **and that Pharaoh told them the dreams** (so they had the data), **and that not one could
interpret.**

**This deserves noticing, because the dreams are not obscure.** **Seven of a thing and seven of another
thing, in the two commodities the country lives on, both coming up out of the Nile.** Egypt had manuals
for exactly this work — **the Chester Beatty III dream papyrus is a two-column lookup table** of the form
*if a man sees himself doing X, good/bad: it means Y* — **and this dream is far plainer than most of its
entries.**

**So Genesis is not claiming the puzzle was hard.** **It is claiming that the profession was beside the
point**, because *"don't interpretations belong to God?"* (40:8). **The failure is not of competence but
of access.** See [[Wisdom]] and [[Prophecy and Vision]].

### Exodus: the guild competes, and the text keeps score

**Exodus is the only book that lets the *ḥarṭummîm* succeed, and it does so deliberately, three times,
before stopping.**

| plague | what they do |
|---|---|
| **the rods** (7:11–12) | **they match it** — *"they also did the same thing with their enchantments"* — **and Aaron's rod swallows theirs** |
| **blood** (7:22) | **they match it** |
| **frogs** (8:7) | **they match it** — which only adds frogs |
| **gnats** (8:18–19) | **they fail**, and say ***"This is God's finger"*** |
| **boils** (9:11) | **they cannot stand up**, because they have boils |

**The arc is exact and it is an argument.** **They are allowed to replicate the signs that are
displays**, and **they break at the first one that is small, dirty and creaturely.** Then they stop being
practitioners and become **patients**: 9:11 has them unable to appear, **afflicted along with everyone
else.** **The profession is not refuted, it is retired.**

**And their one line is a confession** — *ʾeṣbaʿ ʾĕlōhîm hîʾ*, *this is the finger of God* — **spoken by
the opposition**, which is the same device Genesis used at 41:38 when **Pharaoh, not Joseph, supplied the
theological verdict.** See [[A Man in Whom Is the Spirit of God]].

### Daniel: the same word, the same failure, in Babylon

**Daniel 2 moves the scene to Babylon and keeps the vocabulary.** Nebuchadnezzar's ***ḥarṭummîm*** are
listed alongside enchanters, sorcerers and Chaldeans (2:2), **and they are asked for something harder
than Pharaoh asked**: to state the dream as well as interpret it.

**Their answer is the most honest sentence any of these guilds is given**:

> *"There is not a man on the earth who can show the king's matter … there is no other who can show it
> before the king, except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh."* (Daniel 2:10–11)

**They are right, and the book agrees with them.** **What they do not know is that the exception has an
address**: Daniel's *"there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets"* (2:28) **concedes their premise and
denies their conclusion.**

**The structural repetition of Genesis 41 is complete**: a troubled king (Daniel 2:1 uses the same verb
as Genesis 41:8, *wattitpāʿem rûḥô*), a failed establishment, a Hebrew produced by someone else, a
disclaimer, a correct reading, and a promotion. See
[[Do Not Interpretations Belong to God - Joseph and Daniel]] and
[[The Signet Ring and the Second Chariot - Joseph, Mordecai, and Daniel]].

### What the thread is not

**It is not a polemic against learning.** **The same texts use *ḥăkāmîm*, *wise men*, of the failed guild
and of Joseph in the same chapter** (41:8; 41:33, 39) — **and Joseph is appointed precisely for being
*nābôn wəḥākām*.** **Daniel is trained in the Chaldean curriculum for three years and comes out top of
his class** (Daniel 1:4, 20). **Neither book suggests the Israelite wins by being untrained.**

**The claim is narrower and it is made every time**: **there is a class of knowledge that is given, not
acquired**, and **no professional body has a procedure for obtaining it.** **Isaiah makes the same point
about the counsellors of Egypt** — *"the princes of Zoan are fools … how do you say to Pharaoh, 'I am the
son of the wise'?"* (19:11) — **and about Babylon's astrologers at 47:13.**

## Significance

**Three empires, three professional establishments, one word, and the same outcome each time.** The
*ḥarṭummîm* are the Bible's standing figure for **expertise that is real, credentialed, and
structurally unable to deliver what is being asked of it** — and their best moments are the two occasions
they say so themselves: *"This is God's finger"* (Exodus 8:19) and *"the gods, whose dwelling is not with
flesh"* (Daniel 2:11).

**Genesis 41 is where the pattern is set, and it sets it with a blank**: the whole guild of Egypt in one
room, given a dream about cows, **with nothing to say.**
