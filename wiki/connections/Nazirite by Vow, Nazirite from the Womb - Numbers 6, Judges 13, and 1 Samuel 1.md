# Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1

## Passages involved

- [[The Nazirite Vow]] — Numbers 6:1–21.
- [[The Announcement of Samson's Birth]] — Judges 13:4–5, 7, 14 — *"No razor shall come on his head; for the child shall be a Nazirite
  to God from the womb."* (stored WEB)
- [[Samson and Delilah]] — Judges 16:17 — *"I have been a Nazirite to God from my mother's womb."* *(same)*
- [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] — 1 Samuel 1:11 — *"then I will give him to Yahweh all the days of his life, and no razor shall come on
  his head."* *(same)*
- Amos 2:11–12 — *"But you gave the Nazirites wine to drink"* *(same)*
- Luke 1:15 — of John: *"he will drink no wine nor strong drink."* *(same)*
- [[Samson's Riddle]] — Judges 14:8–10; honey from a lion's carcass, and a seven-day *mišteh*.

## Analysis

**The law and the narratives do not describe the same thing.** Numbers 6 has a person who chooses, a
term that ends, and a shaving at the Tent. Samson has none of these. He is named a Nazirite before
conception by Yahweh's angel, and the term is *to the day of his death* (Judges 13:7). The word is the
same, and Judges 13:14 uses the rare phrase of Numbers 6:4, *gepen hayyayin*, found only in these two
verses by Strong's tags (WLC).

**The restrictions move.** In Judges 13 the angel's food rule binds the mother (13:4, 7, 14), and it adds
*any unclean thing*. Of Numbers 6's three rules, the angel states the razor for the child. The wine rule
is told to her, and the corpse rule is not repeated. Samson's later contact with the lion's carcass
(14:8–9) and with the men he kills is not called a breach by the narrator. Only the hair is presented
as the point of the matter (16:17, 19), and its regrowth is noted at 16:22.

**The razor word changes.** Numbers 6:5 and 8:7 have *taʿar*. Judges 13:5, 16:17, and 1 Samuel 1:11
have *môrâ*, with the verb *ʿālâ*, and those are the only three uses of that noun by Strong's tags
(WLC). Hannah's clause therefore matches Samson's wording, not Numbers.

**Samuel is not called a Nazirite in the Masoretic text.** WLC 1 Samuel 1:11 has *neder* and *môrâ* but
no *nāzîr*. The razor clause and the lifelong gift suggest the same category, but that is an inference.
Other textual witnesses of 1 Samuel have not been compared here. Hannah's denial of wine at 1:15 is
about herself and is not part of the vow.

**John is not called one either.** Luke 1:15 says *no wine nor strong drink*, and says nothing of hair,
corpses, or a vow. The verse echoes the pattern and goes no further.

**Amos** sets Nazirites beside prophets as something Yahweh raised up, and his charge is that Israel
*gave the Nazirites wine to drink*. This assumes the wine rule is known and was meant to be kept, and
that a Nazirite was a public sign that could be attacked.

### 1 Samuel 1 read in place

Ingesting [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] confirms the razor and *nāzîr* findings above against the stored WEB and the WLC, and
places the vow in the barren-mother pattern of Judges 13 with one inversion: in Judges the razor rule is
announced to the mother by the angel (13:5), and in 1 Samuel the mother offers it to God herself (1:11).
Samuel's term is *all the days of his life*, in the vow's own words, where Samson's *to the day of his
death* came from his mother's report (13:7). Source: stored WEB Judges 13:5, 7; 1 Samuel 1:11, 15; Hebrew
WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### Judges 13 read in place

Ingesting [[The Announcement of Samson's Birth]] confirms the analysis above against the stored text and adds two details. **The woman's
report changes the terms.** The angel told her of the razor and that the child *shall begin to save
Israel* (13:5); she reports neither, and adds *to the day of his death* (13:7). So the lifelong term
rests on her words, not the angel's, and the narrator lets it stand. **The rules are repeated three
times and always to or about her** (13:4, 7, 14). Manoah asks what the child's *way of life* will be
(13:12, *mišpaṭ*, WLC) and is told only what his wife must avoid. The child's own obligation, in the
chapter, is the razor alone.

Source: stored WEB Judges 13:4–14; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-16.

### The day of his death — Judges 16:30–31

[[The Death of Samson]] closes the term his mother reported, *to the day of his death* (13:7). The razor
had already come on his head (16:19), and *the hair of his head began to grow again* (16:22); the text does
not say whether the Nazirite status was restored, lost, or simply continued to its term. Numbers 6:9–12
provides for a Nazirite defiled by a sudden death to shave and begin again, but Samson's case, a razor on
a Nazirite from the womb, is not the case the law describes, and Judges does not apply it. See
[[Samson and Delilah]].

Source: stored WEB Numbers 6:9–12; Judges 13:7; 16:19, 22, 30–31.

## Significance

Numbers makes the office open and temporary, and the narratives show God using the same name for what
he imposes from birth. The law has no section for that, so the wiki records two forms and does not
force them into one. Judges 13 is now ingested (below); 1 Samuel is not, so the Samuel side should be revised then.
See [[Oaths and Vows]] and [[A Person Vowed to Yahweh - Leviticus 27.1-8, Judges 11, and 1 Samuel 1]].

Sources: stored WEB Numbers; Judges, 1 Samuel, Amos, and Luke at bible-api.com; Hebrew from WLC
(morphhb) by Strong's tags, 2026-10-31.

### Judges 14 — the carcass and the feast

The first chapter of Samson's adult life puts him beside two of the Nazirite's boundaries without saying
he crossed either. He takes honey *out of the lion's body* (14:9). Numbers 6:6 forbids the Nazirite to
*go near a dead body*, and the WLC there reads *nepeš mēt*, a dead person; Leviticus 11:27 makes the
carcass of *Whatever goes on its paws* unclean to whoever touches it, *until the evening*. He holds a
*mišteh*, a feast (14:10, WLC), the noun commonly derived from *šātâ*, *to drink* (reported, not
checked), and the chapter does not say he drank. The razor, the rule chapter 13 stated first (13:5), is
not touched until 16:19. The text records two silences, *he didn't tell* his parents (14:6, 9), and
leaves the verdict out. See [[Samson's Riddle]]. Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 11:27; Numbers 6:6; Judges 13:5; 14:6–10;
16:19. Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-16.
