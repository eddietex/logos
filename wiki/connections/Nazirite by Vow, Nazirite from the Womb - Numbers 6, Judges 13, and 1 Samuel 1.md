# Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1

## Passages involved

- [[The Nazirite Vow]] — Numbers 6:1–21.
- Judges 13:4–5, 7, 14 — *"No razor shall come on his head; for the child shall be a Nazirite to God
  from the womb."* *(WEB, bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- Judges 16:17 — *"I have been a Nazirite to God from my mother's womb."* *(same)*
- 1 Samuel 1:11 — *"then I will give him to Yahweh all the days of his life, and no razor shall come on
  his head."* *(same)*
- Amos 2:11–12 — *"But you gave the Nazirites wine to drink"* *(same)*
- Luke 1:15 — of John: *"he will drink no wine nor strong drink."* *(same)*

## Analysis

**The law and the narratives do not describe the same thing.** Numbers 6 has a person who chooses, a
term that ends, and a shaving at the Tent. Samson has none of these. He is named a Nazirite before
conception by Yahweh's angel, and the term is *to the day of his death* (Judges 13:7). The word is the
same, and Judges 13:14 uses the rare phrase of Numbers 6:4, *gepen hayyayin*, found only in these two
verses by Strong's tags (WLC).

**The restrictions move.** In Judges 13 the angel's food rule binds the mother (13:4, 7, 14), and it adds
*any unclean thing*. Of Numbers 6's three rules, the angel states the razor for the child. The wine rule
is told to her, and the corpse rule is not repeated. Samson's later contact with the lion's carcass
(14:8–9) and with the men he kills is not called a breach by the narrator. Only the hair is presented
as the point of the matter (16:17, 19), and its regrowth is noted at 16:22.

**The razor word changes.** Numbers 6:5 and 8:7 have *taʿar*. Judges 13:5, 16:17, and 1 Samuel 1:11
have *môrâ*, with the verb *ʿālâ*, and those are the only three uses of that noun by Strong's tags
(WLC). Hannah's clause therefore matches Samson's wording, not Numbers.

**Samuel is not called a Nazirite in the Masoretic text.** WLC 1 Samuel 1:11 has *neder* and *môrâ* but
no *nāzîr*. The razor clause and the lifelong gift suggest the same category, but that is an inference.
Other textual witnesses of 1 Samuel have not been compared here. Hannah's denial of wine at 1:15 is
about herself and is not part of the vow.

**John is not called one either.** Luke 1:15 says *no wine nor strong drink*, and says nothing of hair,
corpses, or a vow. The verse echoes the pattern and goes no further.

**Amos** sets Nazirites beside prophets as something Yahweh raised up, and his charge is that Israel
*gave the Nazirites wine to drink*. This assumes the wine rule is known and was meant to be kept, and
that a Nazirite was a public sign that could be attacked.

## Significance

Numbers makes the office open and temporary, and the narratives show God using the same name for what
he imposes from birth. The law has no section for that, so the wiki records two forms and does not
force them into one. Judges and 1 Samuel are not yet ingested, so this page should be revised then.
