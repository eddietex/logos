# For This Very Purpose - Exodus 9.16 in Romans 9.17

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 9:16** — *"but indeed for this cause I have made you stand: to show you my power, and that
  my name may be declared throughout all the earth"* — [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]
- **Exodus 33:19** — *"I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will show mercy on whom I
  will show mercy"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Romans 9:14–18** — *"For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, 'For this very purpose I caused you to be
  raised up, that I might show in you my power, and that my name might be proclaimed in all the
  earth.' So then, he has mercy on whom he desires, and he hardens whom he desires"* (9:17–18)
  *(not yet ingested)*
- **Romans 9:19, 22** — *"Why does he still find fault? For who withstands his will?"*; *"willing to
  show his wrath, and to make his power known"* *(not yet ingested)*

Exodus is quoted from `raw/bible/`; Romans from the WEB at bible-api.com. The Hebrew of Exodus 9:16
was checked in the WLC. **No claim is made about the Greek of Romans, or about whether Paul follows
the Septuagint of Exodus 9:16 or departs from it**; that comparison is not made here.

## Analysis

### In Exodus: why Pharaoh is still standing

Exodus 9:14–16 is God's explanation, given to Pharaoh before the hail, of why the plagues have not
yet killed him. **The pestilence that killed Egypt's livestock could have been for him**: *"For now I
would have stretched out my hand, and struck you and your people with pestilence, and you would have
been cut off from the earth"* (9:15). He has not been, ***baʿăbûr zōʾt heʿĕmadtîkā***, *"for this
cause I have made you stand"* (9:16) — the hiphil of *ʿāmad*, in a chapter where the magicians
*"couldn't stand before Moses"* (9:11) and Pharaoh promises Israel *"you shall stay no longer"* (9:28,
the same verb; WLC).

There are two purposes:

1. ***"To show you my power"*** — *harʾōtəkā ʾet-kōḥî*, Pharaoh as the one who sees.
2. ***"That my name may be declared throughout all the earth"*** — *sappēr šəmî bəkol-hāʾāreṣ*, the
   world as the one that hears.

**The second goes beyond the refrain of the earlier plagues.** Before this, the knowledge was
Pharaoh's or Egypt's: *"In this you shall know that I am Yahweh"* (7:17), *"the Egyptians shall
know"* (7:5). Here it is the whole earth. The Song of the Sea will report the news reaching the
nations: Philistia, Edom, Moab, and Canaan (15:14–15, not yet ingested). In Joshua, Rahab will say
that Jericho has heard what Yahweh did to Egypt (Joshua 2:10, not yet ingested; paraphrased, not
quoted).

**And the verse sits beside the first narrated divine hardening.** Three verses earlier, at 9:12,
*"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"*, the first time Exodus narrates the hardening as Yahweh's
act. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

### In Romans: the example of hardening

Paul is arguing in Romans 9 that God's promise has not failed even though much of Israel has not
believed. His sequence is:

- **Isaac and Jacob** — *"The elder will serve the younger"* (9:12), and Malachi's *"Jacob I loved,
  but Esau I hated"* (9:13). See [[The Elder Will Serve the Younger - Genesis 25.23 in Malachi and Romans]].
- **The objection** — *"Is there unrighteousness with God? May it never be!"* (9:14).
- **Moses** — Exodus 33:19, *"I will have mercy on whom I have mercy"* (9:15), and the conclusion, *"it
  is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy"* (9:16).
- **Pharaoh** — Exodus 9:16 (9:17), and the conclusion, *"he has mercy on whom he desires, and he
  hardens whom he desires"* (9:18).
- **The next objection** — *"Why does he still find fault? For who withstands his will?"* (9:19).

**Moses is the example of mercy and Pharaoh the example of hardening**, and both are taken from
Exodus. Paul introduces the verse as *"the Scripture says to Pharaoh"*, so the scripture itself
speaks in the place of God.

### What changes between the two

- ***"I have made you stand"* becomes *"I caused you to be raised up"*** in the WEB of Romans. In
  Exodus the hiphil of *ʿāmad* can mean *kept you standing* (alive, after the pestilence) or *set you
  up* (brought you onto the stage). The WEB of Romans reads in the second direction. **Which wording
  Paul's Greek has, and whether it is the Septuagint's, is not checked here**; the difference is
  recorded only as it appears between the two WEB translations.
- ***"To show you my power"* becomes *"that I might show in you my power"***. In Exodus Pharaoh is the
  one shown; in Romans' English he is the one *in* whom it is shown. Again this is reported in the
  WEB only.
- **The name declared in all the earth** is kept in both.

### Hardening in both texts

Exodus shows the hardening in four forms: God forecasting it (4:21; 7:3), the heart hardened with no
agent named (7:13 onward), Pharaoh hardening his own heart (8:15, 32; 9:34), and Yahweh hardening it
(9:12 onward). **Romans 9:18 uses the last form as the summary.** Romans 9:19 then raises the obvious
question against it. The Exodus chapter Paul is quoting also ends with *"he sinned yet more, and
hardened his heart"* (9:34), which keeps Pharaoh responsible. **Neither text has God's act cancel
Pharaoh's.** How the two are reconciled is a long-standing theological question and is not settled
here. See [[Hardening of the Heart]] for the readings.

## Significance

- **It is the New Testament's clearest use of Pharaoh**, and it uses him as the example of being
  hardened, not of being punished.
- **It makes Exodus 9 a primary text in the debate over divine sovereignty and human
  responsibility.** The chapter where God first hardens Pharaoh is also the chapter where Pharaoh
  first says *"I have sinned"* (9:27) and then *"sinned yet more"* (9:34).
- **The purpose clause outlives the story.** *"That my name may be declared throughout all the
  earth"* is fulfilled in part simply by Paul quoting it to Rome.
