# Sow in the Morning - Proverbs 20.4, Genesis 8.22, and Ecclesiastes 11.4-6

## Passages involved

- [[Cast Your Bread on the Waters]] — **Ecclesiastes 11:4, 6** — *"He who observes the wind won’t
  sow; and he who regards the clouds won’t reap"*; *"In the morning sow your seed, and in the
  evening don’t withhold your hand; for you don’t know which will prosper, whether this or that,
  or whether they both will be equally good."*
- [[Wine Is a Mocker]] — **Proverbs 20:4** — *"The sluggard will not plow by reason of the winter;
  therefore he shall beg in harvest, and have nothing."*
- [[The Waters Recede]] — **Genesis 8:22** — *"While the earth remains, seed time and harvest, and
  cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night will not cease."*
- **Galatians 6:9** — *"Let us not be weary in doing good, for we will reap in due season, if we
  don’t give up."* Verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

**Two reasons not to sow.** Proverbs 20:4 and Ecclesiastes 11:4 both describe a farmer who fails
to sow because of the weather. In Proverbs the reason is discomfort: it is winter, and the
sluggard will not plow. In Ecclesiastes the reason is caution: the observer watches the wind and
the clouds, waiting for a sign that the time is right. The two sayings condemn different faults,
laziness and over-caution, but they reach the same result: no harvest. Proverbs states the
consequence directly, begging at harvest with nothing. Ecclesiastes leaves it implied: *"won’t
reap."*

**The answer in Ecclesiastes is not certainty but persistence.** Ecclesiastes 11:5–6 does not
promise that a well-timed sowing will succeed. It says that no one knows which sowing will
prosper, and so commands sowing in the morning and in the evening. The remedy for not knowing is
to sow more than once, not to wait until one knows.

**The ground of that persistence.** Genesis 8:22 promises that seedtime and harvest will not
cease while the earth remains. It does not promise any one farmer a crop. The cycle is kept; the
outcome of each field is not. Ecclesiastes 11:3–6 assumes such an order, with rain falling from
full clouds and a harvest to be reaped, while insisting that its details remain beyond human
knowledge. The two texts fit together without either quoting the other. The wider comparison of
Genesis 8:22 with the recurring world of Ecclesiastes 1 is recorded on
[[The Earth Remains - Genesis and Ecclesiastes 1]].

**Paul's version.** Galatians 6:9 takes the language of sowing and reaping into the moral life:
do not grow weary, for the harvest comes *"in due season, if we don’t give up."* Its preceding
verses (6:7–8, not quoted here) speak of reaping what one sows. Galatians shares the call to
persist with Ecclesiastes 11:6, but its confidence about the harvest is stronger than anything
Ecclesiastes says. No literary dependence on Ecclesiastes is claimed.

## Significance

The connection separates two failures that end in the same empty field: the sluggard who will
not work in bad weather, and the cautious man who waits for perfect weather. Proverbs answers
the first with the threat of hunger. Ecclesiastes answers the second with the limits of
knowledge, and turns those limits into a reason to work. See [[Work]], [[Providence]],
[[Wisdom]], and [[Folding the Hands - Proverbs 6.10, 24.33, and Ecclesiastes 4.5]].
