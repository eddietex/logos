# The Attire of a Prostitute - Genesis 38 and Proverbs 7.10

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 38:14–15** — [[Judah and Tamar]]: *"She took off her the garments of her widowhood, and
  covered herself with her veil, and wrapped herself, and sat in the gate of Enaim, which is by the
  way to Timnah … When Judah saw her, he thought that she was a prostitute, for she had covered her
  face"* (*wayyaḥšəḇehā ləzônâ kî ḵissəṯâ pānêhā*)
- **Genesis 38:21** — *"Where is the prostitute, that was at Enaim by the road?" They said, "There
  has been no prostitute here."*
- **Proverbs 7:10–13** — [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]: *"Behold, there a woman met him
  with the attire of a prostitute, and with crafty intent … So she caught him, and kissed him. With
  an impudent face she said to him"* (*šîṯ zônâ ûnəṣurat lēḇ … hēʿēzâ p̄ānêhā*)

- Compare **Song of Solomon 1:7** — [[Dark and Lovely]]: *"why should I be as one who is veiled
  beside the flocks of your companions?"* A possible echo; see the analysis.

## Analysis

**Two women who are not prostitutes, dressed as prostitutes, by the road.** Tamar is a widow who
has been denied the marriage owed her; the woman of Proverbs 7 is a wife whose husband is away
(7:19). Both are taken for what their clothes say, and both meet the man *by the way* — Tamar
*"by the way to Timnah"*, the strange woman on *"the way to her house"* (7:8). In both, the word
*zônâ* names what the woman appears to be, not what she is: Judah's friend is told *"There has been
no prostitute here"* (38:21), and Proverbs says the woman wore *the attire* of one. The Hebrew of
Genesis 38:14–15 and Proverbs 7:10, 13 is read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

**The faces are opposite.** Tamar *covered her face* (*kissəṯâ pānêhā*); the strange woman *made her
face strong* (*hēʿēzâ p̄ānêhā*). Tamar hides who she is; the strange woman hides what she wants —
*nəṣurat lēḇ*, *guarded of heart* — and shows her face boldly.

**The verdicts are opposite.** Genesis vindicates Tamar: *"She is more righteous than I"* (38:26),
and her sons enter the line of Judah. Proverbs condemns the strange woman without remainder: *"Her
house is the way to Sheol"* (7:27). The difference lies in what each woman was doing. Tamar acted
to obtain the marriage and offspring the law of the brother-in-law owed her; the strange woman acts
to betray a marriage she already has. **The wiki records the parallel of dress and road and does not
assimilate the two women.** Proverbs gives no sign of thinking of Tamar, and Genesis 38 is not a
warning text.

**A disputed third case.** In Song of Solomon 1:7 the woman does not want to be *"as one who is
veiled"* beside the flocks of the beloved's companions. One common reading takes the line in light of
Genesis 38:15: a covered woman alone among men could be taken for a prostitute, and she wants to
avoid that. Other readers take the line as wandering or searching. If the first reading is right,
the Song adds a third use of the same signal. Tamar adopts it deliberately, the woman of Proverbs 7
dresses in it, and the Song's woman fears being mistaken for it. The text does not make the
link explicit. No Hebrew comparison was made in this pass, so the echo is recorded as possible, not
established. Genesis 24:65 is a reminder that a veil in Genesis can also mean modesty before a
bridegroom.

## Significance

The pair marks the range of [[Deception]] in sexual matters within Scripture's own judgments: the
same disguise is vindicated in Genesis and condemned in Proverbs, according to its purpose. It also
adds to [[Sexual Sin]] the observation that Proverbs' strange woman is not a professional, but a
married woman using a prostitute's dress.
