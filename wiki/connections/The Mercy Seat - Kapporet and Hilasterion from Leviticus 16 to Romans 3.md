## Analysis

**One object, two functions.** In Exodus 25 the *kappōret* is where God *speaks* (25:22). In Leviticus
16 it is where blood is put to make atonement, once a year, by the high priest alone. Numbers 7:89 shows
the first function in use. **The place of the word and the place of the blood are the same slab of
gold.**

**The Hebrew word.** ***Kappōret*** (Exodus 25:17; Leviticus 16:2; MAM) shares its consonants with
*kāpar*. That verb is *to cover with pitch* in Genesis 6:14, and in the piel, *kipper*, it is the
ordinary verb for *making atonement* in Leviticus. Whether the noun means *lid* (from *cover*) or *place
of atonement* (from *kipper*) is disputed. Both are standard proposals, and the wiki does not decide.
**Leviticus 16 at least makes it the place where *kipper* happens.**

**The Greek word.** The Septuagint renders *kappōret* as ***hilastērion*** — *hilastērion epithema* at
its first occurrence (Exodus 25:17) and *hilastērion* alone after that (25:18–22; Rahlfs). The word is
related to *hilaskomai*, *to propitiate* or *expiate*. **The translators chose the atonement sense over
the lid sense.** The English *"mercy seat"*, usually credited to Tyndale after Luther's *Gnadenstuhl*,
follows the same choice (common report, not checked).

**Hebrews uses *hilastērion* for the object.** *"Cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat"* —
*kataskiazonta to hilastērion* (Hebrews 9:5, SBLGNT). The verb *kataskiazō*, *overshadow*, is close to
the Septuagint's *syskiazontes* at Exodus 25:20 (Rahlfs). Hebrews then declines to say more: *"of which
things we can't speak now in detail"*.

**Paul uses it of a person.** God *"sent [Christ] to be an atoning sacrifice"* — *hon proetheto ho theos
hilastērion* (Romans 3:25, SBLGNT; the verb is *set forth* or *put forward*). **Whether Paul means *mercy
seat* is disputed.**

- One reading takes *hilastērion* in Romans in its Septuagint sense, the *kappōret*. Christ is the place
  where God deals with sin, set out publicly where the old one was hidden behind a veil.
- Another takes it in a general sense, *means of propitiation* or *atoning sacrifice*. The WEB's rendering
  follows this one.

The Septuagint background is real and checked. That Paul has the Day of Atonement in mind is a reading.
The wiki records both.

**Exodus 26 locates the meeting place behind the veil.** The ark and mercy seat are placed in
the most holy space (26:33–34). This supplies the architectural setting for Leviticus 16:2's
warning about entry, without yet giving the atonement ritual. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]] and [[The Veil - Separation in Exodus and Access in Hebrews]].

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

Exodus 37:6–9 reports the gold mercy seat and its two integral cherubim actually made,
following 25:17–20. It supplies their dimensions, wings, and orientation, not a blood rite
or a new promise of speech. The incense altar's horns are also made (37:25), for the altar
whose annual atonement was prescribed in 30:10. The manufacture belongs between instruction
and use; it does not by itself resolve the word history or the interpretations of Romans
already distinguished above. No new Hebrew or Greek wording claim is inferred from it.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The rite performed — Leviticus 16

Now ingested: the mercy seat is where Yahweh appears in the cloud (16:2), where incense smoke
covers it *"so that he will not die"* (13), and where the bull's and the people's goat's blood
are sprinkled on it *"on the east"* and seven times before it (14–15). Verse 16 names the
purpose as atonement for the Holy Place because of Israel's uncleanness and transgressions.
**This confirms the Leviticus side of the thread from stored text; it does not settle the word
history or the reading of Romans 3:25 above.** See [[The Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB
Leviticus 16, 2026-10-29.

## Significance

- **It links the tabernacle's furniture to the New Testament's language of atonement.** The *kappōret* is
  the one piece where Exodus's *meeting* and Leviticus's *atonement* coincide.
- **It shows how much depends on a translator's choice.** The New Testament's *hilastērion* comes from the
  Septuagint, not from the Hebrew, so its meaning in Romans turns partly on what the Septuagint
  translators thought *kappōret* meant.
- **It keeps speech and sacrifice together.** The place where God speaks to Moses is the place where blood
  is brought. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]] and [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
