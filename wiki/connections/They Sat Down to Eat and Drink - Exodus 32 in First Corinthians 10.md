# They Sat Down to Eat and Drink - Exodus 32 in First Corinthians 10

## Passages involved

- [[The Golden Calf]] — Exodus 32:6.
- 1 Corinthians 10:6–8 — Paul's examples and warning against idolatry.

## Analysis

Paul explicitly introduces a written text in 1 Corinthians 10:7 and quotes the people sitting down
to eat and drink and rising to play. That sequence is Exodus 32:6, the celebration after Aaron
builds the altar and proclaims a feast. Paul gives the quotation a stated application: his readers
must not become idolaters as some of the wilderness people did.

The correspondence is verified in the WEB rather than asserted as exact Greek wording. Exodus's
stored wording includes “to eat and to drink”; the API's 1 Corinthians rendering has “to eat and
drink.” The small English difference does not obscure the explicit quotation and its narrative
source.

The following verse (1 Corinthians 10:8) moves to sexual immorality and twenty-three thousand
deaths. That number is not Exodus 32:28's approximately three thousand, and the neighboring
warning should not be used as proof that Exodus 32:6's play was sexual activity. This comparison
leaves the further wilderness episode to its own treatment.

## Significance

The New Testament itself uses the calf episode as a warning for a later worshipping community. A
shared meal and celebration can belong to forbidden worship; the practice's setting and object
matter. See [[Idolatry]], [[Sacrifice and Offering]], [[Praise and Song]], and [[Festivals and First Fruits]].
