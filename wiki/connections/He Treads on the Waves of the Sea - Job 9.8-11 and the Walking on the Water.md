# He Treads on the Waves of the Sea - Job 9.8-11 and the Walking on the Water

## Passages involved

- [[Show Me Your Glory]] — Exodus 33:1–23; presence, mediated speech, and promised revelation.

- **Job 9:8, 11** — *"He alone stretches out the heavens, and treads on the waves of the sea… Behold,
  he goes by me, and I don't see him. He passes on also, but I don't perceive him."* —
  [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]]
- **Job 4:15** — *"Then a spirit passed before my face."* Eliphaz's vision, with Job's second verb
  (*ḥālap*). — [[Eliphaz's First Speech]]
- **Job 11:10** — *"If he passes by (yaḥălōp), or confines, or convenes a court, then who can oppose
  him (mî yəšîbennû)?"* Zophar quoting 9:11–12, the passing God turned from absent to judging —
  [[Zophar's First Speech]]
- **Job 38:16** — *"Have you entered into the springs of the sea? Or have you walked in the recesses
  of the deep?"* God's question back. — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Exodus 33:19–23; 34:6** — *"I will make all my goodness pass before you… while my glory passes
  by… and Yahweh passed by before him, and proclaimed."*
- **1 Kings 19:11** — *"Behold, Yahweh passed by"* — to Elijah at Horeb, not in the wind, the
  earthquake, or the fire.
- **Psalm 77:19** — *"Your way was through the sea, your paths in the great waters. Your footsteps
  were not known."*
- **Habakkuk 3:15; Isaiah 43:16; 51:10** — Yahweh treading the sea, making a way through it.
- **Mark 6:45–52** — Jesus *"came to them, walking on the sea; and he would have passed by them"*,
  and says *"It is I"* (*egō eimi*). Parallels in **Matthew 14:22–33** and **John 6:16–21**, neither
  of which has *passed by*.

## Analysis

**Job 9:8 is one of the few places in the Hebrew Bible where God walks on the sea.** *Dôrēk
ʿal-bāmŏtê yām* — literally *treading on the backs of the sea*, the verb of trampling a conquered
enemy, and the sea here is the power God subdued at creation (see
[[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]]; Rahab's helpers stoop five verses later).
The Septuagint renders it *peripatōn hōs ep' edaphous epi thalassēs*, *walking on the sea as on dry
ground* — and that Greek was the Bible of the evangelists.

**Three verses later, God passes by.** *Yaʿăbōr ʿālay wəlōʾ ʾerʾeh*, *he goes by me and I do not see
him* (9:11). *ʿĀbar* is the verb of the two great theophanies of passing-by: Moses in the cleft of the
rock, allowed to see the back of the glory as it passes (Exodus 33:22; 34:6), and Elijah at the cave
mouth, when *"Yahweh passed by"* and was not in the wind (1 Kings 19:11). In both of those the
passing *is* the revelation; the prophet is hidden and then shown something. In Job the passing is
the absence of one. **God goes past Job the way he went past Moses, and Job sees nothing at all.**
Psalm 77:19 has the same paradox, of God crossing the waters with *"footsteps not known."*

Job's second verb is sharper still, because it is Eliphaz's. *Wəyaḥălōp* — *he passes on* — is the
verb of *"a spirit passed (yaḥălōp) before my face"* (4:15). Eliphaz had a presence pass him in the
night and came away with a doctrine. Job has God himself pass, and comes away with nothing.

**Mark's sea-walking puts the two verses back together.** Jesus comes to the disciples *"walking on
the sea; and he would have passed by them"* (Mark 6:48) — *ēthelen parelthein autous*, with
*parerchomai*, the Septuagint's verb for the passing of the glory in Exodus 33:22 and of Yahweh in
1 Kings 19:11. They see him and think he is a ghost; he says *"It is I"* — *egō eimi*, the divine
self-naming of Exodus 3:14 in its Greek form. Read with Job beside it, the episode does what Job 9
says cannot happen: **the one who treads on the sea passes by, and this time he is seen, and he
speaks.** Whether Mark had Job 9 in mind specifically, or the whole scriptural complex of
sea-treading and passing-by (Job, Exodus, Kings, Psalm 77, Isaiah 43), cannot be settled; the
convergence in two adjacent verses of Job is the closest single match.

**And God's own speech answers 9:8 with a question.** *"Have you walked (hithallaktā) in the recesses
of the deep?"* (38:16). Job had described God walking where no man can. The whirlwind asks Job
whether he has. It is one of a dozen places where the speeches of chapters 38–41 pick up an image
from Job's own mouth and return it to him.

### Exodus 33 in its narrative setting

[[Show Me Your Glory]] supplies the promised passing in Exodus 33:19–23. God sets the terms
of revelation and protects Moses while withholding his face. This is a promise of a future
encounter within the scene; the passing is narrated in 34:6. The comparison with Job’s
unperceived passing and the Gospel sea narratives is a literary reading, not an explicit
citation identified by Exodus or a prediction stated to Moses. This addition rests on the
stored WEB of Exodus and makes no new lexical claim.

## Significance

Job 9:11 is the Bible's clearest statement of the *hiddenness* of a God who is nonetheless present —
he goes by, close enough to be *ʿālay*, beside or over me, and is not perceived. It sets up the
book's resolution in the vocabulary of sight: *"I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now
my eye sees you"* (42:5). The man who said *"he goes by me, and I don't see him"* ends by saying he
sees. For Christian readers, Mark 6 places Jesus inside the sentence of Job 9:8 and resolves 9:11
by the same move the book does: the passing God is seen, and identifies himself. See
[[Seeing and Being Seen]] and [[The Names of God]].
