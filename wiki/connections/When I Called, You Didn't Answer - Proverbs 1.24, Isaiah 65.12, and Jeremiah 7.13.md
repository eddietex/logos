# When I Called, You Didn't Answer - Proverbs 1.24, Isaiah 65.12, and Jeremiah 7.13

## Passages involved

- [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]] — Proverbs 1:24, 28: *"Because I have called, and you have refused;
  I have stretched out my hand, and no one has paid attention … Then will they call on me, but I will
  not answer."* Wisdom personified, to the simple.
- Isaiah 65:12; 66:4 *(not yet ingested)* — *"because when I called, you didn't answer. When I spoke,
  you didn't listen; but you did that which was evil in my eyes, and chose that in which I didn't
  delight"* (65:12); 66:4 repeats it in the third person, *"because when I called, no one answered"*
  (WEB, checked against bible-api.com). God, to Jerusalem.
- Isaiah 65:2 *(not yet ingested)* — *"I have spread out my hands all day to a rebellious people"*
  (WEB, checked against bible-api.com), quoted at Romans 10:21, *"All day long I stretched out my
  hands to a disobedient and contrary people"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com).
- Jeremiah 7:13 *(not yet ingested)* — *"I spoke to you, rising up early and speaking, but you didn't
  hear; and I called you, but you didn't answer"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com). God, at the
  temple gate.
- Psalm 2:4 *(not yet ingested)* — *"He who sits in the heavens will laugh. The Lord will have them
  in derision"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com).
- Micah 3:4; Jeremiah 11:11 *(not yet ingested)* — the people *"will cry to Yahweh, but he will not
  answer them"* (Micah 3:4); *"they will cry to me, but I will not listen to them"* (Jeremiah 11:11)
  (WEB, checked against bible-api.com).

## Analysis

**Wisdom's indictment uses the opening words of a divine one.** Proverbs 1:24 begins *yaʿan
qārāʾṯî wattəmāʾēnû*, *because I called and you refused*; Isaiah 65:12 has *yaʿan qārāʾṯî wəlōʾ
ʿănîṯem*, *because I called and you did not answer* (both checked against Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah*). The same causal particle, the same verb in the same person, then a
refusal. Jeremiah 7:13 and Isaiah 66:4 have the idea in their own words. The four passages share a
shape:

1. **The call** — *I called*; and in Proverbs and Isaiah 65:2, *I stretched out / spread out my
   hand(s)* — the gesture of invitation (Proverbs *nāṭîṯî yāḏî*; Isaiah *pēraśtî yāḏay*, a different
   verb).
2. **The refusal** — *you refused / did not answer / did not hear*.
3. **The reversal** — the one who was not answered now does not answer. Proverbs 1:28 (*"they will
   call on me, but I will not answer"*, *wəlōʾ ʾeʿĕneh*), Micah 3:4, Jeremiah 11:11. The call goes
   unanswered in both directions.
4. **The laughter** (Proverbs and Psalm 2 only) — *ʾeśḥāq … ʾelʿaḡ* in Proverbs 1:26, *yiśḥāq …
   yilʿaḡ* in Psalm 2:4, the same two verbs (checked in the same edition).

**What Proverbs changes is the speaker.** In Isaiah, Jeremiah, Micah, and the Psalm, the one who
calls, is refused, and then refuses to answer is God, and the prophet relays it with *"says
Yahweh"*. In Proverbs it is Wisdom, in her own voice, in the city gates, and Yahweh is spoken of in
the third person (*"the fear of Yahweh"*, 1:29). **The wisdom tradition has taken over the
prophetic oracle of judgment and given it to a personification.** The wiki records this as a
feature of the text; it draws no conclusion about which tradition borrowed from which.

**And what Proverbs keeps is the logic.** The prophetic passages are about a nation and a covenant
(Jeremiah 7 at the temple, Isaiah 65 to a people who *"chose that in which I didn't delight"*);
Proverbs is about individuals and their choices (*"they didn't choose the fear of Yahweh"*, 1:29 —
*bāḥārû*, the verb of Isaiah 65:12's *bəḥartem*). Both say the refusal is a *choice*, and both
describe the result as the refusers receiving what they chose.

**A tension recorded rather than resolved.** Against this pattern stand the promises that those who
seek will find: Deuteronomy 4:29 and Jeremiah 29:13 (*"You shall seek me, and find me, when you search
for me with all your heart"*, WEB, checked against bible-api.com), Proverbs' own 8:17, and Isaiah
55:6's *"Seek Yahweh while he may be found"* — which implies a time when he may not be. The wiki's
reading is on [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]]: Proverbs 1:28 describes seeking as a way out of the
storm, after it has come, and the finding-passages require the whole heart; but the texts do not
say this in so many words.

## Significance

The pattern is the Bible's account of how a summons refused becomes a silence. In the passages
already in the wiki it has a narrative form: Pharaoh, called again and again by Moses and refusing,
until *"Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart"* (see [[Hardening of the Heart]]). Proverbs states it as a
general rule for anyone, and Jesus' lament over Jerusalem gives it its New Testament form — *"How
often I would have gathered your children together … and you would not!"* (Matthew 23:37) — see
[[Wisdom Sends Prophets - Proverbs 1.20-33, Luke 11.49, and Matthew 23.34-37]].
