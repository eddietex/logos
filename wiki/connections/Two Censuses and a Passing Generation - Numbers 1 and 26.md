# Two Censuses and a Passing Generation - Numbers 1 and 26

## Passages involved
- [[The Second Census]] — Numbers 26.
- [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]
- [[The People Refuse the Land]]

## Analysis

Numbers 1:46 totals 603,550 men; Numbers 26:51 totals 601,730, a decrease of 1,820.
The similar totals conceal the generational rupture explicitly stated at 26:64–65.
Numbers 14:29–30 sentenced the counted adults who murmured, except Caleb and Joshua;
the later census names those same exceptions. This concerns the earlier military cohort,
not a claim that every person alive at the exodus died: Moses and Eleazar conduct this count,
and Levi has a separate register and threshold.

The tribal distribution has also changed. Simeon falls from 59,300 to 22,200; Manasseh rises
from 32,200 to 52,700 and now exceeds Ephraim's 32,500 (Numbers 1:23, 35; 26:14, 34, 37).
Numbers 25 gives a plague toll but no tribal distribution. Assigning Simeon's entire loss
to that plague goes beyond the text. Population renewal does not remove judgment,
and these figures do not by themselves establish the causes of every tribal change.

Sources: stored WEB in Numbers and Exodus; no source-language claim is made.

## Significance

The second count joins fulfillment of judgment to preparation for inheritance. The people continue,
but survival of the nation must not obscure the deaths of the condemned generation.
