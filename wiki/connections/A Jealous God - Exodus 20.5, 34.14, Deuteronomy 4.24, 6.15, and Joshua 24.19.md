# A Jealous God - Exodus 20.5, 34.14, Deuteronomy 4.24, 6.15, and Joshua 24.19

## Passages involved

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:5: *"you shall not bow yourself down to them, nor serve
  them, for I, Yahweh your God, am a jealous God"*.
- [[The Covenant Renewed]] — Exodus 34:14: *"for you shall worship no other god: for Yahweh, whose
  name is Jealous, is a jealous God."*
- [[No Form Seen and No Image Made]] — Deuteronomy 4:24: *"For Yahweh your God is a devouring fire,
  a jealous God."*
- [[The Ten Commandments Recounted]] — Deuteronomy 5:9, repeating Exodus 20:5.
- [[Do Not Forget When You Are Full]] — Deuteronomy 6:15: *"for Yahweh your God among you is a
  jealous God; lest the anger of Yahweh your God be kindled against you"*.
- [[The Covenant at Shechem]] — Joshua 24:19: *"You can't serve Yahweh, for he is a holy God. He
  is a jealous God. He will not forgive your disobedience nor your sins."*

## Analysis

**Always attached to other gods.** In every one of the texts gathered here, *jealous God* gives the
reason for a ban on other gods or images. Exodus 20:5 and Deuteronomy 5:9 ground the ban on bowing
to and serving images. Exodus 34:14 grounds *worship no other god*, and makes *Jealous* a name.
Deuteronomy 4:24 follows a warning against making a carved image (4:23). Deuteronomy 6:15 follows
*You shall not go after other gods* (6:14). Joshua 24:19 answers a people who have just been told to
choose between Yahweh and the gods of the fathers and of the Amorites. Jealousy here is the claim of
exclusive covenant loyalty, not a general temperament.

**Joshua's use is the only one here that discourages.** Moses uses the phrase as a warning. Joshua
uses it as an objection to the people's own pledge: *You can't serve Yahweh*. The same ground that
supports the command in Exodus and Deuteronomy is turned, at Shechem, into a reason to doubt the
people can keep it. He pairs it with *holy*, and Deuteronomy 4:24 pairs it with *devouring fire*;
Joshua 24:20's *consume you* is close to that image in the WEB, but this page claims no shared
Hebrew wording.

**Mercy in the same breath.** Exodus 20:6 follows the jealous God with *showing loving kindness to
thousands of those who love me*, and Exodus 34:6–7 proclaims Yahweh *merciful and gracious … 
forgiving iniquity and disobedience and sin*. Joshua 24:19 says *He will not forgive*. The wiki
records this as a tension and does not resolve it; see [[Forgiveness]] and the note on
[[The Covenant at Shechem]]. Exodus 34:7 itself continues *will by no means clear the guilty*.

## Significance

- The thread fixes the meaning of divine jealousy in the Torah and Joshua as exclusive claim within
  a covenant. See [[Idolatry]], [[Covenant]], and [[Envy]].
- It links [[Holiness]] and jealousy: both mark Yahweh as one who cannot be served alongside others.
- [[The Names of God]]: Exodus 34:14 is the only text here that makes *Jealous* a name.
