# Saul's Wars and David's Wars - 1 Samuel 14.47-52 and 2 Samuel 8

## Passages involved

- [[Saul's Wars and Household]] — 1 Samuel 14:47–52; *Wherever he turned himself, he defeated them*.
- 2 Samuel 8:1–18 — David's wars and officials; *Yahweh gave victory to David wherever he went* (not yet ingested;
  WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-19).
- Judges 2:14, 16 — *their enemies all around*; *saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them* (stored
  WEB).

## Analysis

Each of Israel's first two kings gets a summary notice: a paragraph that steps out of the story to list the
king's wars, then his household or officials. Saul's comes early, at 1 Samuel 14:47–52, before his rejection;
David's comes at 2 Samuel 8, after the promise of a house (2 Samuel 7, recalled). The two are built from the same
parts, and the differences are in what each says about who wins.

| | Saul — 1 Samuel 14:47–52 | David — 2 Samuel 8 |
|---|---|---|
| **enemies** | Moab, Ammon, Edom, *the kings of Zobah*, the Philistines (47); Amalek (48) | the Philistines (1), Moab (2), *Hadadezer … king of Zobah* (3), Syria of Damascus (5–6), Edom (14); Ammon and Amalek in the list of plunder (12) |

| **deliverance** | *delivered Israel out of the hands of those who plundered them* (48) | nations become *servants to David, and brought tribute* (2, 6) |

### The same frame, a different subject

Both refrains use *bəḵōl ʾăšer*, *in all where*, with a verb of motion: Saul *turned*, David *went* (WLC). In
Saul's, the king is the subject of the victory verb; in David's, Yahweh is, and David the object. The verb of
Saul's victory, *yaršîaʿ*, is elsewhere in Genesis–2 Samuel a legal *condemn* (Exodus 22:9 WEB / 22:8 WLC;
Deuteronomy 25:1); David's, *yōšaʿ*, is *save*. The two words share their last two consonants. That the
Septuagint reads *he was saved* in 1 Samuel 14:47, which would make the two refrains closer still, is commonly
reported (recalled, not checked; no Septuagint was consulted). The wiki records the Hebrew as it stands and does
not reconstruct a reading.

### The same enemies

Five of Saul's six named enemies are in David's summary, and Zobah, which appears nowhere else before 2 Samuel 8
in the WLC files consulted, is in both. The overlap means David's summary is, at least in part, a list of the wars
Saul had already fought. For Saul the text says only that he fought and prevailed; for David it adds garrisons,
tribute, and spoil dedicated to Yahweh (8:6, 11–12, 14). Whether Saul's victories were less complete, or only told
more briefly, the text does not say.

### A judge's vocabulary for a king

Saul's summary is closer to Judges than David's is. *Those who plundered them*, *šōsēhû* (14:48), is the participle
of the programme of Judges, *raiders who plundered them … saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them*
(Judges 2:14, 16), and outside those verses occurs in Genesis–2 Samuel only at 1 Samuel 23:1 (WLC; morphhb lemma
8154). *On every side against all his enemies*, *sāḇîḇ bəḵol-ʾōyəḇāyw* (14:47), echoes *their enemies all around*,
*ʾôyəḇêhem missāḇîḇ* (Judges 2:14, WLC). David's summary has no such echo; it ends with *justice and
righteousness* (8:15) and a list of officers of state.

## Significance

- **It shows that the narrator gave Saul a full reign summary.** The book does not withhold credit from Saul: the
  military record of 14:47–48 is wide, and five of its enemies are David's.
- **It locates the difference in the theology, not the battlefield.** The same frame, *wherever*, is filled with a
  king who prevails and with Yahweh who saves.
- **It marks Saul as the last of the deliverers as well as the first of the kings.** His summary speaks the language
  of Judges 2; David's speaks the language of a state.
- **It places Saul's summary before his rejection.** David's notice follows Yahweh's promise to his house; Saul's
  follows the sentence that his kingdom *will not continue* (13:14) and precedes chapter 15. The parallel is not
  in the same narrative position.
