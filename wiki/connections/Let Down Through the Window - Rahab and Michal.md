# Let Down Through the Window - Rahab and Michal

## Passages involved

- [[Rahab and the Spies]] — **Joshua 2:3–6, 15–21** — *The king of Jericho sent to Rahab, saying, "Bring out the
  men"* (3); *Then she let them down by a cord through the window; for her house was on the side of the wall* (15);
  *the window which you used to let us down* (18); *She sent them away, and they departed. Then she tied the scarlet
  line in the window* (21). Stored WEB.
- [[Michal Helps David Escape]] — **1 Samuel 19:11–17** — *Saul sent messengers to David's house, to watch him, and to
  kill him in the morning* (11); *So Michal let David down through the window. He went away, fled, and escaped* (12);
  *Why have you deceived me thus, and let my enemy go …?* (17). Stored WEB.
- **2 Samuel 6:16** — *Michal the daughter of Saul looked out through the window*, saw David dancing before Yahweh,
  *and she despised him in her heart*. WLC only; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB stored.
- **Acts 9:23–25; 2 Corinthians 11:32–33** — *They watched the gates both day and night that they might kill him, but
  his disciples took him by night, and let him down through the wall, lowering him in a basket* (Acts 9:24–25);
  *Through a window I was let down in a basket by the wall, and escaped his hands* (2 Corinthians 11:33). WEB checked
  at bible-api.com, 2026-11-28; not stored.

## Analysis

**One sentence, twice.** Joshua 2:15 reads *wattôriḏēm baḥeḇel bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn*, *she let them down by the cord
through the window*. 1 Samuel 19:12 reads *wattōreḏ mîḵal ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn*, *Michal let David down through
the window* (WLC). In the Hebrew files checked (Genesis through 2 Samuel, and Ruth), the *hiphil* of *yāraḏ*, *bring
down*, stands in the same verse as *ḥallôn*, *window*, only at Joshua 2:15, 18 and 1 Samuel 19:12. *Ḥallôn* itself
occurs in those files in eight verses (Genesis 8:6; 26:8; Joshua 2:15, 18, 21; Judges 5:28; 1 Samuel 19:12; 2 Samuel
6:16), and these two women are the only people in them who let anyone out through a window.

**The same parts.** The two scenes are built from the same pieces, and several of them share words.

| | Rahab | Michal |
|---|---|---|
| **the king sends** | *The king of Jericho sent*, *wayyišlaḥ* (Joshua 2:3) | *Saul sent messengers*, *wayyišlaḥ* (19:11, 14, 15) |
| **to the house** | *who have entered into your house* (2:3) | *to David's house* (19:11) |
| **the wall** | her house *bǝqîr haḥômâ*, *on the side of the wall* (2:15) | the spear *baqqîr*, *into the wall*, that night (19:10) |
| **let down** | *by a cord through the window* (2:15) | *through the window* (19:12) |
| **sent away** | *she sent them away*, *wattǝšallǝḥēm*, *and they departed* (2:21) | *you let my enemy go*, *wattǝšallǝḥî* (19:17); *he went away* (19:12) |
| **the false word** | *Where the men went, I don't know* (2:5) | *He is sick* (19:14); *He said to me, "Let me go!"* (19:17) |
| **the hiding** | the spies under the flax on the roof (2:6) | the teraphim under clothes in the bed (19:13) |

All verb and noun pairs from the WLC. The *wall* in the third row is not the same wall: Rahab's is the city's, and
Saul's spear struck the wall of the room where he sat. The wiki records the shared word and draws nothing from it.

**Who the women are.** Rahab is a foreigner inside a city under the ban, and she sides with Israel's spies against
her own king. Michal is the king's daughter, and she sides with her husband against her father. The king's men in
both stories are deceived at the door. Rahab's story ends with her household saved by the scarlet line *in the
window* (Joshua 2:18, 21; 6:22–25). Michal's does not end with a reward. Saul gives her to another man while David is
in flight (25:44, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and the window returns at 2 Samuel 6:16 as the place she looks out from
and despises him. The scarlet line marks Rahab's window for rescue. The narrator gives Michal's window no sign.

**The New Testament.** The Damascus escape repeats the pieces once more: men who *watched the gates both day and night
that they might kill him* (Acts 9:24), as Saul's messengers were sent *to watch him, and to kill him in the morning*
(19:11); escape *by night*; a man *let down through the wall* (Acts 9:25), *through a window* (2 Corinthians 11:33);
and *escaped his hands*. The man let down is Saul of Tarsus. The Greek of Acts and 2 Corinthians was not checked, and
the wiki does not claim that Luke or Paul has Joshua 2 or 1 Samuel 19 in view. It records that the oldest king named
Saul sent men to watch a house and kill the man in it, and that the man named Saul in Acts is the one let down.

## Significance

The two scenes make the same point by the same means: the king's power stops at a woman's window. In Joshua the
woman is the most unlikely ally Israel could have, and she is rewarded and remembered (Joshua 6:25; Hebrews 11:31;
James 2:25). In 1 Samuel she is the king's own daughter, and her act is narrated without a verdict. Neither text
praises the lie told to the king's men. Both commend, or at least record, the saving of life. See
[[The Midwives and Rahab - Deceiving the King to Save Life]] for the larger pattern,
[[Through the Window - Proverbs 7.6, Genesis 26.8, and Judges 5.28]] for the window as a place of watching,
[[Rahab]], [[Michal]], [[David]], [[Deception]], and [[Marriage]].
