# Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh

## Passages involved

- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 14:24; Yahweh *confused the Egyptian army*.
- Exodus 23:27 — *I will … confuse all the people to whom you come*.
- Deuteronomy 2:15; 7:23 — *Yahweh's hand was against them*; *will confuse them with a great confusion*.
- [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]] — Joshua 10:10; *Yahweh confused them before Israel*.
- [[Deborah and Barak]] — Judges 4:15; *Yahweh confused Sisera, all his chariots, and all his army*.
- 1 Samuel 7:10 — *Yahweh thundered … on the Philistines, and confused them* *(WEB checked, not yet
  ingested)*.

## Analysis

The verb is *hāmam* (WLC lemma 2000). A lemma search of the WLC files for Genesis, Exodus, Numbers,
Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, and 1 Samuel finds it seven times, and every time Yahweh is the subject
and an enemy army or people the object:

| Text | Subject | Object | WEB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exodus 14:24 | Yahweh | the camp of Egypt | *confused the Egyptian army* |
| Exodus 23:27 | Yahweh (promise) | all the people Israel comes to | *will confuse* |
| Deuteronomy 2:15 | Yahweh's hand | the wilderness generation's fighting men | *to destroy them* |
| Deuteronomy 7:23 | Yahweh (promise) | the nations of the land | *will confuse them with a great confusion* |
| Joshua 10:10 | Yahweh | the five kings' armies at Gibeon | *confused them* |
| Judges 4:15 | Yahweh | Sisera, his chariots, his camp | *confused* |
| 1 Samuel 7:10 | Yahweh | the Philistines | *confused them* |

**Exodus 14 and Judges 4.** The closest pair. Both are chariot armies: Pharaoh's *six hundred chosen
chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt* (Exodus 14:7), Sisera's *nine hundred chariots of iron*. In
both, Yahweh *confused* the camp (*wayyāhom … ʾet-maḥănēh*, Exodus 14:24; *wayyāhom …
ʾet-kol-hammaḥăneh*, Judges 4:15, WLC). Both end with the same clause: *lōʾ nišʾar bāhem ʿad-ʾeḥād*, *There
remained not so much as one of them* (Exodus 14:28), and *lōʾ nišʾar ʿad-ʾeḥād*, *There was not a man
left* (Judges 4:16). Both have water: the sea returns on Egypt, and the Song of Deborah says *The river
Kishon swept them away* (Judges 5:21). Both victories are followed by a song led by a woman called
*prophetess*; see [[The Prophetess - Miriam and Deborah]].

**The promise and its keeping.** Exodus 23:27 and Deuteronomy 7:23 promise the verb for the conquest.
Joshua 10:10 is the first time it is kept in the land, and Judges 4:15 the second. The verb never
belongs to Israel's army: Israel pursues and strikes, and Yahweh confuses.

**Deuteronomy 2:15 turns it around.** There the object is Israel's own fighting men, the generation
that refused to enter: *Yahweh's hand was against them, to destroy them from the middle of the camp*
(stored WEB). The WEB translates *hāmam* there as *destroy*. The same act Yahweh does to Israel's
enemies he did to Israel.

**1 Samuel 7:10 adds thunder.** *Wayyarʿēm YHWH bəqôl-gādôl … wayhummēm* (WLC): Yahweh thunders and
confuses. Judges 4 names no weather, but the Song has *the clouds dropped water* (5:4) and *From the
sky the stars fought* (5:20). The commander's name, Barak, is usually read as *lightning*; that is
reported, not verified, and the text does not make the pun.

## Significance

The verb marks the battles where Israel's victory is said to be Yahweh's act and not Israel's
strength, and especially the battles against chariots. Judges 1:19 left Judah unable, or unwilling,
to face chariots of iron; Judges 4 has nine hundred of them destroyed in one day, and gives the reason
in one verb. See [[War]], [[Forest to Clear and Chariots to Face]],
[[Yahweh Fights for Israel - The Promise and the Day at Gibeon]], and [[Signs and Wonders]].
