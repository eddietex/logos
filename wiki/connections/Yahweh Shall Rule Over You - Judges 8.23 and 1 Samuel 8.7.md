## Analysis

Israel asks for a ruler at least twice in the historical books. The first time is to Gideon, after a
victory. The offer is hereditary, *you, and your son, and your son's son*, and the reason given is *you
have saved us*. The second is to Samuel, and Yahweh's answer is
that Israel has *rejected me as the king over them*. Gideon's refusal states, before the request is ever
made to Samuel, the principle Samuel will be told: the ruler of Israel is Yahweh.

The vocabulary differs. Judges 8:22–23 uses *māšal*, *rule*, four times, and neither *melek*, *king*,
nor *mālak*, *reign* (WLC). WEB's 1 Samuel 8:7 says *king*; its Hebrew was not checked in this pass,
since 1 Samuel was not searched. The wiki therefore claims an agreement of idea, not of wording. In the WLC of Genesis–Judges, Judges 8:23 is the
only verse in which Yahweh is the subject of *māšal*.

The irony in Judges 8 is that the offer itself breaks the rule Judges 7 set. Yahweh reduced the army
so that Israel would not say *My own hand has saved me* (7:2). The men of Israel do not credit their own
hand; they credit Gideon's, *you have saved us* (*hôšaʿtānû*, the verb of 7:2's *saved*, WLC). Gideon's
answer corrects them. The narrator's closing verse corrects them again: Israel *didn't remember Yahweh
their God, who had delivered them* (8:34).

Joseph's brothers used the same verb in Genesis 37:8, *māšôl timšōl bānû*, alongside *mālak* (WLC).
They refused a rule no one had offered. Israel offers a rule Gideon refuses. In both, the question of
who will rule over a family of Israel is raised and not settled by the person asked.

Deuteronomy 17 permits a king and limits him. Gideon refuses the title and then has *many wives* and
seventeen hundred of gold (Judges 8:26, 30), which are two of the three things Deuteronomy 17:16–17
withholds from a king. His son Abimelech takes what his father refused (9:2–6, not yet ingested).

## Significance

- **Judges' kingship question is asked in Gideon's story before it is asked by the refrain.** The book
  later says four times *In those days there was no king in Israel* (17:6; 18:1; 19:1; 21:25). Judges
  8:23 says why there is none: Yahweh rules.
- **The refusal and the conduct are both recorded.** Gideon's words and his ephod, wives, and son's name
  sit in one pericope. The wiki does not reduce either to the other.
- **1 Samuel 8 will read differently with this in view.** When that pericope is ingested, its *they have
  rejected me* should be read against Gideon's *Yahweh shall rule over you*; the wiki has not yet
  checked the Hebrew of 1 Samuel.
