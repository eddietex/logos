# Let Me Hear Your Voice - Song of Solomon 2.14-17 and 8.13-14

## Passages involved

- [[Rise Up, My Love]] — Song of Solomon 2:8–17, especially 2:14, 17.
- [[You Are All Beautiful]] — Song of Solomon 4:6.
- [[Make Haste, My Beloved]] — Song of Solomon 8:13–14.

## Analysis

In 2:14 the beloved asks to see a face and hear a voice; in 8:13 the final exchange
again requests a voice, now with friends in attendance. The ending gives the request
an answer in the form of another invitation, if 8:14 is read as the woman's response.
This speaker assignment is probable within the poem, not labelled in the stored WEB.

The beloved is compared to a roe or young deer in 2:9 and 2:17. At 8:14 the WEB uses
gazelle and young stag. Both scenes join the animal comparison to mountains and an
address to the beloved. This is a comparison of the verified English text, not a
claim about identical Hebrew words. The mountains of spices also recall the mountain
of myrrh and hill of frankincense in 4:6, without identifying all three as one location.

The recurrence does not resolve direction: the closing summons does not narrate an
arrival, a departure, or a final separation. The poem can end with desire still speaking
without cancelling its earlier scenes of mutual belonging and intimacy.

Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 2:8–17; 4:6; 8:13–14, read on 2026-10-29.

## Significance

The last exchange gathers earlier sounds and images into a renewed invitation. Its
closure is literary recurrence rather than a narrated account of the lovers' future.
