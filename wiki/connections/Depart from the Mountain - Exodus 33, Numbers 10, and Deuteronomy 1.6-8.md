# Depart from the Mountain - Exodus 33, Numbers 10, and Deuteronomy 1.6-8

## Passages involved
- [[Show Me Your Glory]] — Exodus 33:1–3; the command to depart after the golden calf
- [[The March from Sinai]] — Numbers 10:11–13; the departure narrated
- [[Leave Horeb and Possess the Land]] — Deuteronomy 1:6–8; the departure remembered as Yahweh's word

## Analysis

Israel's leaving of the mountain is told three times in the stored Torah, each time in a different
mode.

**Exodus 33:1–3 — commanded, under a shadow.** After the golden calf, Yahweh tells Moses:
*"Depart, go up from here, you and the people that you have brought up out of the land of Egypt,
to the land of which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, saying, 'I will give it to your
offspring.'"* An angel will go before them and the peoples of the land will be driven out, *"for I
will not go up among you, for you are a stiff-necked people, lest I consume you on the way."* The
destination and the oath are intact; the presence is in question, and the rest of Exodus 33 turns on
it.

**Numbers 10:11–13 — narrated, by the cloud.** *"In the second year, in the second month, on the
twentieth day of the month, the cloud was taken up from over the tabernacle of the testimony."*
Israel goes out of the wilderness of Sinai, the cloud settles in the wilderness of Paran, and
*"they first went forward according to the commandment of Yahweh by Moses."* The departure is
dated, ordered, and visibly led; the tabernacle now stands in the camp.

**Deuteronomy 1:6–8 — remembered, as one word.** Moses recalls: *"You have lived long enough at
this mountain. Turn, and take your journey …"* He gives no date, no cloud, and no angel. The word
carries a map out to Lebanon and the Euphrates and ends on the same three-name oath as Exodus 33:1.

The three are compatible but not identical. Exodus 33 gives a command in the wake of judgment;
Numbers 10 gives the actual setting-out about a year after arrival; Deuteronomy compresses the
whole into a single remembered word without saying which occasion it quotes. Reading
Deuteronomy 1:6 as a verbatim transcript of either earlier text would claim more than the texts say.

Sources: stored WEB Exodus 33:1–3; Numbers 10:11–13; Deuteronomy 1:6–8.

## Significance

- **The oath survives the calf.** Exodus 33:1 repeats the promise to the three fathers immediately
  after Israel's worst failure at the mountain, and Deuteronomy 1:8 opens the address to the next
  generation with the same oath. The land is not re-earned between the two.
- **Remembering selects.** Deuteronomy omits the calf, the threat of withdrawn presence, and the
  cloud in this recollection. The calf is not erased from the book — the plan places its retelling
  in Deuteronomy 9 — but the speech opens with the command to go, not the sin at the mountain.
- **"Long enough"** frames Horeb as a stage that was finished rather than a place to stay. The law
  received there travels with the people; the mountain does not.
