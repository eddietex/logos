# Blessed for His Sake - Laban's House and Potiphar's

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 12:1–3** — *"I will bless those who bless you… in you all the families of the earth will
  be blessed"*: the promise that makes one man's blessing spill outward — [[The Call of Abram]]
- **Genesis 30:27, 30** — *"Yahweh has blessed me **for your sake**"*; *"Yahweh has blessed you
  wherever I turned"* — [[Jacob's Flocks]]
- **Genesis 39:5** — *"Yahweh blessed the Egyptian's house **for Joseph's sake**"* *(not yet
  ingested)*
- **Genesis 26:12–14** — Isaac's hundredfold in Gerar, and the envy that follows it —
  [[Isaac in Gerar]]
- **Genesis 41:53–57; 47:13–26** — a famine survived by a whole region because of the same man
  *(not yet ingested)*
- **2 Samuel 6:11** — *"Yahweh blessed Obed-Edom and all his house"* while the ark stayed there

## Analysis

**Two verses in Genesis use the same preposition for the same phenomenon, one chapter apart in the
lives of a father and a son.**

> **30:27** Laban said to him, “If now I have found favor in your eyes, stay here, for I have divined
> that **Yahweh has blessed me for your sake**.”

> **39:5** Yahweh blessed the Egyptian’s house **for Joseph’s sake**. (*not yet ingested*)

The Hebrew in both is ***biglal*** — *on account of, because of.* ***Biglālekā*** at 30:27,
***biglal yôsēp*** at 39:5. **It is not a common construction, and Genesis puts it in exactly these
two places**: an Aramean stock-breeder explaining why his herds grew, and the narrator explaining
why an Egyptian official's household prospered.

**What the two cases share is more than a word.**

| | [[Laban]] and [[Jacob]] | Potiphar and [[Joseph]] |
|---|---|---|
| **the man's status** | hired shepherd, no property of his own | bought slave |
| **who notices** | the master — *"I have divined"* | the master — *"his master saw that Yahweh was with him"* (39:3) |
| **what grows** | flocks, from *little* to *a multitude* (30:30) | *"all that he had in the house and in the field"* (39:5) |
| **the master's response** | refuse to release him; keep him on wages | hand him everything and stop counting (39:6) |
| **how it ends** | the man leaves under pursuit (31) | the man is thrown into prison (39:20) |

**In both, the foreign master reads the situation correctly.** Laban does not think he has grown
rich by luck; he attributes it to *Yahweh*, by name, and to the presence of one man. Potiphar
*"saw that Yahweh was with him"*. **Neither master is converted, and neither is condemned.** Genesis
gives them the right diagnosis and lets them act on it in the way a proprietor would: **acquire more
of the asset.**

**And in both, the blessing's carrier owns nothing.** Jacob's complaint at 30:30 is *"when will I
provide for my own house also?"*; Joseph is in charge of a house that is not his and is described in
39:6 as owning nothing but the food on the table. **The promise of 12:3 works through men with no
property, inside estates belonging to men who do not share their God.**

**The difference is who says it.** At 30:27 the sentence is **spoken by the beneficiary**, and he
uses it as an argument for detention — *"stay here, for…"*. At 39:5 the sentence is **the
narrator's**, and it is offered as plain fact with nobody to persuade. **Genesis 12:3 read aloud by
the man it enriched, and Genesis 12:3 stated by the book itself.**

**Isaac's version, one generation earlier, has the same mechanism and the opposite outcome.** *"Yahweh
blessed him"* in Gerar (26:12), the Philistines envy him, and Abimelech expels him — *"Go away from
us, for you are much mightier than we"* (26:16). **Three households, three correct diagnoses, and
three different reactions: expel, detain, promote.** See
[[Mightier Than We - Isaac in Gerar and Israel in Egypt]].

## Significance

**This is the pattern by which Genesis 12:3 actually operates in Genesis**, and it is not what the
promise sounds like. *"In you all the families of the earth will be blessed"* is fulfilled, in the
book's own reporting, as **livestock, grain, and a household inventory belonging to somebody else.**
It is economic before it is anything else, and it happens to people who never asked for it and are
not said to worship the God who supplies it.

**It also sets up the bondage.** Laban's response to discovering that the blessing works is to keep
the man. Pharaoh's response, four hundred years later, is the same one at national scale: *"Come, let
us deal wisely with them, lest they multiply"* (Exodus 1:10) — **a host who has noticed the increase
and will not let it leave.** **The verb Jacob uses at 30:25 is the verb Moses will use at Exodus
5:1**: ***šallaḥ***, *send away, let go.* See [[Blessing]] and
[[All the Families of the Earth - Genesis 12.3 in the New Testament]].

**And it is the wiki's standing observation about the book's foreigners.** [[Melchizedek]] blesses
Abram in the name of God Most High; [[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]] is told he is a dead man and
answers with a plea of integrity; Laban divines Yahweh's hand and Potiphar sees it. **Genesis keeps
giving accurate theology to people outside the covenant line and never explains where they got it.**
See [[The Nations]].
