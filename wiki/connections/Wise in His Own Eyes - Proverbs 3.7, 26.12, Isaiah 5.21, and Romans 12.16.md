## Analysis

**One phrase, a single fault.** *Ḥāḵām bəʿênāyw*, *wise in his own eyes*, is the Hebrew of Proverbs
3:7 (in the second person, *bəʿêneḵā*), 26:5, 12, and 16, and 28:11 is rendered the same way in the
WEB. (Hebrew of 3:7 and 26:5, 12, 16 from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, read
2026-10-26; 28:11 was not read in Hebrew in this pass.) The fault is not having wisdom but being the
only judge of it. Proverbs sets it against the fear of Yahweh at its first appearance: *"Don't be wise
in your own eyes. Fear Yahweh"* (3:7). The man who trusts his own judgment has put himself where
Yahweh should be.

**Worse than a fool.** Proverbs ranks the fool very low, but 26:12 puts this man lower: *"There is
more hope for a fool than for him."* The fool can still be corrected, even if only by the rod of 26:3.
The self-assured man cannot, because he will not grant that he needs correcting. The same second sentence in the WEB comes back at 29:20 for the man *"hasty in his words"* (Hebrew identity has not been verified here) — the man who speaks
before he has listened. Proverbs 12:15 makes a related point with *right in his own eyes* (*yāšār
bəʿênāyw*): the fool's way looks straight to him, while the wise man *"listens to counsel"*.

**The phrase ties chapter 26 together.** In [[Answer a Fool]] it appears in each of the first two
portraits. The fool must be answered to keep him from becoming wise in his own eyes (5). The man
already wise in his own eyes is worse than any fool (12). And the sluggard, who will not lift food to
his mouth, rates himself above *"seven men who answer with discretion"* (16).

**Isaiah turns it into a woe.** Isaiah 5:21 uses the phrase in a series of woes against Judah:
*"Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes, and prudent in their own sight!"* What Proverbs gives as
counsel, the prophet pronounces as judgment on a nation.

**Romans restates it as community ethics.** Romans 12:16 closes a sentence about humility with *"Don't
be wise in your own conceits."* In the WEB this is near in sense to Proverbs 3:7, and the instruction
is commonly read as an echo of it. Romans does not cite Proverbs, and the Greek of Romans and the
Septuagint of Proverbs 3:7 were not checked in this pass. The wiki records a thematic parallel and does
not claim a quotation.

### Proverbs 28 in context

The wealthy person’s self-estimate is exposed by a poor person with understanding (11). The contrast
strips wealth of any automatic authority to certify wisdom. Verse 26 adds self-trust as folly, while
25 commends trust in Yahweh. These observations concern the stored WEB only; no new claim about
Hebrew wording is made.

### The hasty speaker and the self-assured man — Proverbs 29

The WEB comparison in 29:20 repeats the second sentence of 26:12, assigning less hope than a
fool’s to a different fault: haste in speech rather than being wise in one’s own eyes. The link
concerns refusal of reflection, but the two descriptions are not interchangeable. Chapter 29
surrounds haste with listening, correction, restraint, and the possibility of understanding
without responding (19).

See [[Where There Is No Vision]]. Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 29 and the cited local comparisons; no new
source-language claim.

## Significance

Proverbs treats self-assurance as the condition that makes all its other teaching useless, because
the man wise in his own eyes cannot be taught. It is the point at which [[Folly]] and
[[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] meet. Its remedy is the one 3:7 attaches to it: [[The Fear of God]].
