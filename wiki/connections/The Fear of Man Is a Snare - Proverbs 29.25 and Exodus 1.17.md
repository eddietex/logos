# The Fear of Man Is a Snare - Proverbs 29.25 and Exodus 1.17

## Passages involved

- [[Where There Is No Vision]] — Proverbs 29:25–26.
- [[The Oppression in Egypt]] — Exodus 1:15–21.
- [[The Second Test]] — Job 2:3, 7.

## Analysis

Proverbs 29:25 contrasts the snare of fearing people with safety through trust in Yahweh. Verse 26
follows it with many people seeking a ruler’s favor, yet justice coming from Yahweh. Read
together, they question dependence on human power without saying that every approach to a court is
wrong.

Exodus 1:17 offers a narrative comparison: the midwives fear God and refuse the king’s command to
kill the boys. Verse 21 connects God’s provision of families to that fear. This is an instance of
allegiance to God taking priority over royal pressure, not an event Proverbs explicitly cites.
Proverbs names trust in Yahweh, whereas Exodus names fear of God; those are related responses, not
identical phrases.

**Tension in outcomes:** Job is affirmed as God-fearing and upright yet ruined without cause and
physically afflicted (Job 2:3, 7; compare 1:1). That witness prevents safety in Proverbs 29:25
from becoming a test that blames every harmed person for lack of faith. The proverb gives no
timetable or definition of safety that resolves the tension. Its moral direction can stand without
inventing hidden guilt in Job.

## Significance

[[Faith]] changes whose approval governs action. [[The Fear of God]] supplies the Exodus
comparison, while [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Judgment]] remain accountable to Yahweh rather than royal
favor.
