# I Send My Messenger Before Your Face - Exodus 23, Malachi 3, and Mark 1

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 23:20** — *"Behold, I send an angel before you, to keep you by the way, and to bring you
  into the place which I have prepared"* — [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]
- **Malachi 3:1** — *"Behold, I send my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me"* (WEB;
  *not yet ingested*)
- **Mark 1:2–3** — *"As it is written in the prophets, 'Behold, I send my messenger before your
  face, who will prepare your way before you: the voice of one crying in the wilderness…'"* (WEB;
  *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**Mark's first quotation joins two Old Testament verses, and the first half of it reads closer to
Exodus 23:20 than to Malachi 3:1.** The source-language texts (checked 2026-10-23):

| Text | Edition | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Exodus 23:20 | MAM | *hinnēh ʾānōkî šōlēaḥ malʾāk ləpānêkā* — *behold, I am sending a messenger before you* |
| Exodus 23:20 | LXX (Rahlfs) | *idou egō apostellō ton angelon mou pro prosōpou sou* |
| Malachi 3:1 | MAM | *hinnənî šōlēaḥ malʾākî ûpinnâ-derek ləpānāy* — *I am sending my messenger, and he will clear a way before me* |
| Malachi 3:1 | LXX (Rahlfs) | *idou egō exapostellō ton angelon mou kai epiblepsetai hodon pro prosōpou mou* |
| Mark 1:2 | SBLGNT | *Idou apostellō ton angelon mou pro prosōpou sou, hos kataskeuasei tēn hodon sou* |

Mark's *apostellō … pro prosōpou sou*, *before your face*, matches the Greek of Exodus 23:20 word for
word, apart from the missing *egō*. The Greek of Malachi has a different verb (*exapostellō*) and
*before my face*. Mark's second clause, *who will prepare your way*, is Malachi's: Exodus has no
preparing of a way. And Mark 1:3 goes on to Isaiah 40:3, the voice in the wilderness.

**The attribution differs by Greek text.** The SBLGNT reads *in Isaiah the prophet*; the WEB, which
follows a different textual base, reads *"in the prophets"*. The difference is a known textual
variant. This page records it and does not try to settle it.

**What the combination does.** Exodus 23:20 is about a messenger who goes ahead of Israel through
the wilderness to bring it into the land. Malachi 3:1 is about a messenger who prepares the way for
God himself coming to his temple. Mark puts the two together at the opening of his Gospel and
applies them to John the Baptist in the wilderness. This page reports the wording; it does not
claim to know which verse Mark had chiefly in mind. Matthew 11:10 and Luke 7:27 use a similar
saying of John; they are named here and not quoted or checked.

## Significance

- **The wilderness road of Exodus becomes the setting of the Gospel's opening.** Mark begins where
  Israel was before it entered the land: in the wilderness, with a messenger sent ahead.
- **The shift from *you* to *me* matters.** Exodus sends the messenger before Israel; Malachi sends
  him before God. Mark's combined text keeps the *before your face* of Exodus and the prepared way of
  Malachi.
- **The identity question stays open on both ends.** The Exodus angel carries God's name
  ([[The Angel Sent Ahead]]); Malachi's sentence goes on to *"the messenger of the covenant"*. The
  wiki records both and does not merge either figure with the other. See
  [[The Angel of Yahweh]].
