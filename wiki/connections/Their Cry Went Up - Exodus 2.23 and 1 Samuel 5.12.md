# Their Cry Went Up - Exodus 2.23 and 1 Samuel 5.12

## Passages involved
- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — **Exodus 2:23** — *the children of Israel sighed because of the bondage, and
  they cried, and their cry came up to God because of the bondage.* (stored WEB)
- [[The Ark Among the Philistines]] — **1 Samuel 5:12** — *The men who didn't die were struck with the
  tumors; and the cry of the city went up to heaven.* (stored WEB)

## Analysis

Both verses use the verb *ʿālâ*, *go up*, with the noun *šawʿâ*, *cry for help*:

- Exodus 2:23 — *wattaʿal šawʿāṯām ʾel-hāʾĕlōhîm*, *their cry came up to God* (WLC)
- 1 Samuel 5:12 — *wattaʿal šawʿaṯ hāʿîr haššāmāyim*, *the cry of the city went up to heaven* (WLC)

In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel, *šawʿâ* (lemma 7775) occurs only in these two verses (lemma
search). Beyond them it is a word of the psalms: David's song at 2 Samuel 22:7 and its twin, Psalm 18:6 (WLC
18:7), and Psalms 34, 39, 40, 102, and 145 (WLC lemma search of `2Sam` and `Ps`).

The settings are reversed. In Exodus the cry is Israel's, from under the Egyptian oppressor, and the next
verses say *God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant* (2:24, stored WEB). In 1 Samuel the
cry is the oppressor's: the Philistines, who had held Israel in service (4:9; Judges 13:1), cry from under
Yahweh's hand. Their cry *went up to heaven*, but they address it to no god; the narrator records it, and
the next chapter has them consulting their own priests and diviners (6:2, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
The Philistines themselves had remembered Egypt (4:8), and their priests will (6:6); the verbal echo of
Exodus 2:23 is the narrator's, unremarked.

## Significance

Exodus 2:23 is where the deliverance from Egypt begins, with a cry heard. 1 Samuel 5:12 sets the enemy in
Israel's old place. The pairing is verbal and narrow, two verses with a rare noun and the same verb; the
wiki records it as such. See [[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]], which
notes Exodus 2:23 as the *šawʿâ* outlier in the motif, and [[Prayer]].
