# There Is No One Else - Deuteronomy 4.35-39, Isaiah 45, and Mark 12.32

## Passages involved

- [[The Song of the Sea]] — Exodus 15:11; *Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?*
- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:2–3; *You shall have no other gods before me.*
- [[Has Any People Heard God and Lived]] — Deuteronomy 4:35, 39; *There is no one else besides him.*
- Deuteronomy 6:4 — *Hear, Israel: Yahweh is our God. Yahweh is one.* (stored WEB; not yet ingested)
- Deuteronomy 32:39 — *There is no god with me.* (stored WEB; not yet ingested)
- Isaiah 45:5–6, 18, 21–22 — *I am Yahweh, and there is no one else.* (WEB, verified via bible-api.com; not yet ingested)
- Mark 12:29–33 — the scribe's *he is one, and there is none other but he.* (WEB, verified via bible-api.com; not yet ingested)

## Analysis

Moses gives the confession twice at the end of Deuteronomy 4:

> **Deuteronomy 4:35** It was shown to you so that you might know that Yahweh is God. There is no one else besides him.

> **Deuteronomy 4:39** Know therefore today, and take it to heart, that Yahweh himself is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath. There is no one else.

**Before it.** Exodus 15:11 asks *Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?*, and the first commandment
forbids having *other gods before me* (Exodus 20:3). Both speak of other gods and rank Yahweh above
them. Deuteronomy 4:35, 39 says something stronger in English: there is *no one else*. It does so in the
same chapter that predicts Israel will serve *gods, the work of men's hands* (4:28), and Deuteronomy 6:14
still speaks of *the gods of the peoples who are around you*. Whether the book means that other gods do
not exist, or that none of them is God in Yahweh's sense, is debated. This page records both kinds of
statement and does not decide.

**In the Song of Moses.** Deuteronomy 32:39 (stored WEB) puts it in Yahweh's mouth: *See now that I
myself am he. There is no god with me. I kill and I make alive.*

**In Isaiah.** Isaiah 45 repeats the formula several times in close English wording (WEB, verified via
bible-api.com 2026-11-05):

> **Isaiah 45:5** I am Yahweh, and there is no one else. Besides me, there is no God.

> **Isaiah 45:22** Look to me, and be saved, all the ends of the earth; for I am God, and there is no other.

Deuteronomy says the exodus was shown to Israel *so that you might know* (4:35). Isaiah 45:6 extends the
knowing outward: *that they may know from the rising of the sun, and from the west, that there is no one
besides me*. Isaiah 45:18 grounds it in creation, *Yahweh who created the heavens*; Deuteronomy 4:32
also starts its search at *the day that God created man on the earth*. These are resemblances of WEB
wording and themes; this page does not claim that Isaiah quotes Deuteronomy or make a claim about the
Hebrew.

**In Mark.** When Jesus cites Deuteronomy 6:4–5 as the greatest commandment, the scribe answers:

> **Mark 12:32** The scribe said to him, "Truly, teacher, you have said well that he is one, and there is none other but he,

(WEB, verified via bible-api.com 2026-11-05.) The scribe's *none other but he* joins the confession of
Deuteronomy 6:4 to wording like that of Deuteronomy 4:35. Whether he is echoing Deuteronomy 4:35, Isaiah
45, or both is not stated, and no claim is made here about the Greek.

**Knowing leads to keeping.** In Deuteronomy 4:39–40 the confession is followed directly by *You shall
keep his statutes*. In 6:4–5 *Yahweh is one* is followed by *You shall love*. In Mark 12 the scribe's
confession leads to the judgment that love is *more important than all whole burnt offerings and
sacrifices* (Mark 12:33, WEB, verified via bible-api.com 2026-11-05).

## Significance

The thread shows the first commandment's *no other gods* becoming a confession that there is *no one
else*. It runs from Deuteronomy's account of the exodus, through Isaiah's address to the nations, to a
scribe's conversation with Jesus. Each time the confession is tied to a demand, whether obedience, turning
to be saved, or love. It belongs with [[The Names of God]] and [[Idolatry]].
