# Love Beyond Purchase - Song of Solomon 8.6-7 and Proverbs 6.30-35

## Passages involved

- [[Love Is Strong as Death]] — Song of Solomon 8:6–7.
- [[The Lamp of the Commandment]] — Proverbs 6:30–35.

- [[Our Sister and My Own Vineyard]] — Song of Solomon 8:8–12.

## Analysis

The stored WEB uses “all the wealth of his house” in both Proverbs 6:31 and Song 8:7.
In Proverbs the hungry thief may have to surrender it in restitution; the following
adultery warning describes a jealous husband who refuses ransom and gifts. In the
Song, the attempt to give a household's entire wealth for love brings scorn. Both
passages put jealousy beside something that payment cannot secure.

The difference matters. Proverbs warns about the consequences of violating another
marriage; the Song voices attachment between lovers. The Song does not identify an
adulterer, threaten a rival, or authorize vengeance. Its refusal of purchase belongs
to the same praise of love as the fire that waters cannot quench. An English phrase
and a cluster of images justify comparison, but do not prove literary dependence.

See [[No Ransom for the Adulterer - Proverbs 6.29-35, Leviticus 20.10, and Deuteronomy 22.22]] and
[[Fire in the Lap and the Garment Left Behind - Proverbs 6.27-29 and Genesis 39]].

The next poem, [[Our Sister and My Own Vineyard]], gives Solomon’s vineyard a financial return
(8:11–12), beside the woman’s own vineyard. This does not undo 8:7: revenue for fruit
and the purchase of love are different claims. No sale of the woman is narrated.
Source for this addition: stored WEB Song 1:6; 4:12–16; 8:7–12.

## Significance

[[Wealth and Possessions]] reaches a limit at personal attachment. [[Love]] and
jealousy in [[Envy]] cannot be reduced to a price, although the moral situations of
these poems differ. The comparison preserves that distinction instead of treating
all intense desire as either adultery or virtuous love.

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 6:30–35 and Song of Solomon 8:6–7, read in this pass.
The quoted English phrase is verified in both; no Hebrew equivalence is asserted.
