## Analysis

One Hebrew verb, *nāśāʾ*, *to lift, carry, bear*, runs through Moses' complaint about the people and
his answer to their unbelief. Every Hebrew form below was read in *Miqra according to the Masorah* via
the Sefaria API on 2026-11-05.

| Verse | Who carries | WEB | Hebrew |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers 11:12 | Moses, as ordered? | *"Carry them in your bosom, as a nurse carries a nursing infant"* | *śāʾēhû … kaʾăšer yiśśāʾ hāʾōmēn* |
| Numbers 11:14 | Moses cannot | *"I am not able to bear all this people alone"* | *lāśēʾt* |
| Deuteronomy 1:9 | Moses cannot | *"I am not able to bear you myself alone"* | *śəʾēt* |
| Deuteronomy 1:12 | Moses cannot | *"How can I myself alone bear your problems"* | *ʾeśśāʾ* |
| Deuteronomy 1:31 | Yahweh did | *"Yahweh your God bore you, as a man does bear his son"* | *nəśāʾăkā … kaʾăšer yiśśāʾ-ʾîš ʾet-bənô* |
| Exodus 19:4 | Yahweh did | *"I bore you on eagles' wings"* | *wāʾeśśāʾ* |
| Deuteronomy 32:11 | Yahweh, as an eagle | *"he bore them on his feathers"* | *yiśśāʾēhû* |

**Moses refuses the nurse's role.** In Numbers 11:12 Moses asks, *"Have I conceived all this people?
Have I brought them out, that you should tell me, 'Carry them in your bosom, as a nurse carries a
nursing infant'?"* The question implies the answer: he did not beget them and cannot carry them. The
one who did beget them, by implication, should.

**Deuteronomy 1 gives the answer.** Moses repeats the complaint in his own retelling (1:9, 12), and
nineteen verses later says that Yahweh had been carrying them all along, *"in all the way that you
went, until you came to this place"* (1:31). The two comparisons share a construction — *kaʾăšer
yiśśāʾ*, *as … carries* — with a nurse and a nursing infant in Numbers and a man and his son in
Deuteronomy. Whether Deuteronomy 1:31 consciously answers Numbers 11:12 is the wiki's reading; the
texts set the phrases side by side but neither cites the other.

**The argument at Kadesh.** In Deuteronomy 1 the image is a rebuttal. The people have just said that
Yahweh brought them out *"because Yahweh hated us"* (1:27). Moses replies that a father carried them.
The carrying is offered as evidence, something *"you have seen"*, which makes the unbelief of 1:32
unbelief against experience.

**Later echoes.** Isaiah 46:3–4 calls the house of Jacob those *"carried from the womb"* and has Yahweh
say *"even to gray hairs I will carry you. I have made, and I will bear. Yes, I will carry"* (WEB). The
Hebrew has *nāśāʾ* there too (*hannəśuʾîm*, *ʾeśśāʾ*), alongside *ʿāmas* and *sābal*. Isaiah 63:9
says of the exodus generation, *"He bore them, and carried them all the days of old"* (WEB), where the
second verb is *nāśāʾ* in the piel (*wayənaśśəʾēm*). Hosea 11:3 has a related picture of Yahweh
teaching Ephraim to walk and taking them *"by his arms"* (WEB); it uses a different verb and is listed
only as a parallel image.

## Significance

- The verb ties Moses' limit to Yahweh's provision: what Moses could not carry, Yahweh had carried.
- The father image in Deuteronomy 1:31 answers the charge of hatred in 1:27, inside one speech.
- The eagle of Exodus 19:4 and Deuteronomy 32:11 is the same claim in another picture; see
  [[On Eagles Wings - Exodus 19 and Deuteronomy 32]].
- Moses' complaint that he cannot bear the people leads in Exodus 18 and Numbers 11 to shared
  leadership; see [[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]].
