# The High Priest Once a Year - Leviticus 16 and Hebrews 9

## Passages involved

- [[The Day of Atonement]] — Leviticus 16:1–34.
- **Hebrews 9:7** — *"but into the second the high priest alone, once in the year, not without
  blood, which he offers for himself, and for the errors of the people."*
- **Hebrews 9:11–12** — Christ entered *"nor yet through the blood of goats and calves, but
  through his own blood, … once for all into the Holy Place, having obtained eternal
  redemption."*
- **Hebrews 9:24–26** — not *"often, as the high priest enters into the holy place year by year
  with blood not his own"*, but *"once at the end of the ages"*.
- **Hebrews 10:3–4** — *"But in those sacrifices there is a yearly reminder of sins. For it is
  impossible that the blood of bulls and goats should take away sins."*

(The Hebrews verses are quoted from the WEB as fetched individually from bible-api.com on
2026-10-29; Hebrews is not yet ingested.)

## Analysis

**Hebrews reads the details of Leviticus 16 closely.** *"The high priest alone"* matches 16:17,
where no one may be in the tent; *"once in the year"* matches 16:34; *"not without blood"* matches
14–15; *"for himself, and for the errors of the people"* matches the order of the bull for
Aaron and his house (6, 11) before the people's goat (15).

**It then argues from contrast.** The repetition Leviticus prescribes as an everlasting statute
(16:34) Hebrews reads as a sign of incompleteness — a *"yearly reminder"* (10:3) — set against a
single entry *"once for all"* (9:12). That is Hebrews' interpretation of the rite, not a claim
Leviticus makes about itself: Leviticus 16:30 promises that *"from all your sins you shall be
clean before Yahweh"* without qualification.

**A tension to keep visible.** Leviticus states that the day cleanses Israel from all its sins
(16:30); Hebrews 10:4 states that the blood of bulls and goats cannot take away sins. The wiki
does not resolve these by overwriting either. Readings that harmonize them (for example,
ritual cleansing for access versus final removal of guilt, or effective by anticipation) are
interpretations, recorded here as such.

**What Hebrews 9 does not draw on.** The quoted verses mention goats' blood but not the live
goat sent into the wilderness (16:20–22). Whether other New Testament texts allude to it is
not assessed here.

## Significance

Leviticus 16 is the Old Testament text Hebrews 9–10 builds on most directly, so the details of
the chapter — one priest, once a year, blood, his own sins first — become the frame of its
Christology. See [[Priesthood]], [[Mediation and Advocacy]],
[[The Veil - Separation in Exodus and Access in Hebrews]], and
[[The Mercy Seat - Kapporet and Hilasterion from Leviticus 16 to Romans 3]].
