## Analysis

**One Hebrew verb, *māḥâ*, *to wipe out*, runs from the flood to the book of life, and from Exodus 17 onward it
keeps company with two other words: *sēper*, *book*, and *kātab*, *write*.** All forms below were checked
against the Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures `morphhb`); the English is the WEB.

### The flood: wiping the earth

**In Genesis *māḥâ* occurs in three verses, all at the flood** — God's resolve, *"I will destroy (ʾemḥeh) man whom I
have created from the surface of the ground"* (6:7); the warning, *"I will destroy (ûmāḥîtî) every living thing"*
(7:4); and the event, *"Every living thing was destroyed (wayyimaḥ) … They were destroyed (wayyimmāḥû) from the
earth"* (7:23), twice in one verse (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The WEB translates every one of them *destroy***, so the English reader cannot see that the flood
and the sentence on Amalek use one word. **The flood's range is also the phrase of Exodus 17:14**: God brings the
waters *"to destroy all flesh … from under the sky"* (Genesis 6:17) — *mittaḥat haššāmāyim*, though with a
different verb there (*šāḥat*; WLC).

### Exodus 17: the first book

**Exodus 17:14 puts the verb beside writing for the first time.** *"Write (kətōb) this for a memorial in a book
(bassēper) … that I will utterly blot out (māḥōh ʾemḥeh) the memory (zēker) of Amalek from under the sky"* (WLC).
**It is the first *kātab* in Scripture** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The paradox is built in**: a
name is to be erased, and the erasure is to be written down so it is never forgotten. **Deuteronomy states it in
two clauses** — *"you shall blot out the memory of Amalek from under the sky. You shall not forget"* (25:19) — and
changes the subject from God (*ʾemḥeh*, *I will blot out*) to Israel (*timḥeh*, *you shall*). See
[[The Battle with Amalek]] and [[Amalek - From a Concubine's Son to Israel's First Enemy]].

### Exodus 32: a name in God's book

**At the golden calf the same three words come back, and the book is now God's.** Moses: *"forgive their
sin—and if not, please blot me out (məḥēnî) of your book (missiprəkā) which you have written (kātābtā)"*. God:
*"Whoever has sinned against me, him will I blot out (ʾemḥennû) of my book (missiprî)"* (Exodus 32:32–33; WLC).
**What was a human scroll recording a war in Exodus 17 is, fifteen chapters later, a divine book recording
people.** **Moses asks to be erased if Israel is not forgiven; God assigns erasure to the sinner.** See
[[Mediation and Advocacy]].

**Deuteronomy uses *blot out their name from under the sky* for Israel itself**: after the calf God says to Moses,
*"Leave me alone, that I may destroy them, and blot out their name from under the sky"* (Deuteronomy 9:14). **The
phrase spoken against Amalek in Exodus 17 is turned on Israel**, and averted by Moses' intercession. **And
Deuteronomy 29:20 joins all three words for the individual apostate**: the curse *"written in this book"*, and
*"Yahweh will blot out his name from under the sky"*.

### The Psalms: the book of life

**Psalm 69:28 names the book**: *"Let them be blotted out (yimmāḥû) of the book (missēper) of life, and not be
written (yikkātēbû) with the righteous"* (WLC, 69:29). **Here the three words have fully become the vocabulary of
judgment on persons.** The New Testament's *book of life* (e.g. Revelation 3:5, by reference; *not yet ingested*,
Greek not checked) continues the image.

### The thread

| | who blots out | what | the book |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis 6:7; 7:4, 23 | God | every living thing | — |
| Exodus 17:14 | God (*"I will"*) | the memory of Amalek | Moses' memorial *"in a book"* |
| Exodus 32:32–33 | God | Moses (offered), or *"whoever has sinned"* | *"your book which you have written"* |
| Deuteronomy 9:14 | God (threatened) | Israel's name | — |

### Exodus 32 now ingested

In [[The Golden Calf]], the attempt at atonement is introduced as uncertain (32:30). Moses asks
for forgiveness and, if it is refused, for his own erasure (32:32). **Qualification of the
substitution claim above:** the sentence can express solidarity in Israel’s fate as well as an
offered exchange; it does not specify a mechanism by which his erasure would obtain pardon. God’s
answer assigns erasure to the sinner (32:33). Romans 9:1–3 offers a thematic comparison: Paul
expresses willingness to be accursed for his relatives. It is not presented as an explicit
quotation of Moses. Romans checked in WEB at bible-api.com on 2026-10-24; paraphrase only, no
Greek claim.

## Significance

- **It is invisible in the WEB at the start**: the flood's *destroy* and Exodus 17's *blot out* are one word, and
  only the Hebrew shows the thread begins at Noah.
- **Exodus 17 is the turn**: the first writing in Scripture is a record of erasure, and from then on *māḥâ* travels
  with *book* and *write*.
- **The thread moves from a people to persons** — Amalek's memory, then Moses' offer, then the sinner's name, then
  the book of life — and in Deuteronomy 9:14 the sentence on Amalek is pronounced on Israel and lifted only by
  intercession.
