## Analysis

**The full phrase *forty days and forty nights* gathers around one mountain.** In the books stored
in the wiki so far, it occurs twice in the Flood (Genesis 7:4, 12) and twice at Sinai (Exodus 24:18;
34:28), and nowhere else. Exodus 34:28 repeats it for the second
tablets and adds the fast: no bread, no water. Deuteronomy 9:9–11 has Moses retell the first stay in
the same terms, so the fast is attached to both. Elijah walks *forty days and forty nights* to the
same mountain, under its other name, *"Horeb, God's Mountain"* — the name Exodus gives it at 3:1 and
the *"God's Mountain"* of 24:13. Jesus fasts *forty days and forty nights* in the wilderness before
he is tested.

**Exodus 24 says nothing about a fast.** The fast is stated for the second stay (34:28) and, in
Deuteronomy's retelling, for the first (9:9). This page keeps the two apart: Exodus 24:18 gives only
the length of the stay.

**This list is not a concordance.** The occurrences above were read in the WEB; other books may use
the phrase or the number forty in related ways (forty years in the wilderness, forty days of spying,
forty days in Jonah). They are left for the passages where they occur.

### The renewal in its narrative setting — Exodus 34

The second forty-day stay is now narrated in [[The Covenant Renewed]] (34:28), after the replacement stones
are cut and the renewed covenant words given. Abstention from both bread and water is explicit
here, unlike Exodus 24:18. The duration and fast characterize this encounter; the text does not
command Israel to imitate the fast or explain its physiological conditions.

## Significance

- **In each case the period ends in a new beginning given from God's side**: the waters recede, the
  tablets are given, Elijah hears God at Horeb, and Jesus begins his public work.
- **Elijah and Jesus are both placed in Moses' pattern.** Elijah reaches Moses' mountain; Matthew's
  forty days are followed by Jesus answering the tempter three times from Deuteronomy, the book of
  Moses' retelling (Matthew 4:4, 7, 10; the sources of the answers were not checked in this pass).
  The wiki notes the pattern and does not build a typology on it.
- See [[Horeb]] and [[Testing]].
