# Scattering and Increasing - Proverbs 11.24-26 and 2 Corinthians 9

## Passages involved

- [[A False Balance]] — Proverbs 11:17–18, 24–28.
- [[Do Not Withhold Good]] — Proverbs 3:27–28.
- [[Job's Oath of Innocence]] — Job 31:16–25.
- 2 Corinthians 9:6–11 — WEB verified; not yet ingested.
- [[Cast Your Bread on the Waters]] — Ecclesiastes 11:1–2.

## Analysis

**The paradox concerns what possessions are for.** Proverbs 11:24 contrasts scattering that
increases resources with excessive withholding that produces poverty. Verse 25 adds reciprocal
refreshment, and verse 26 names grain: the community curses the withholder but blesses the seller.
This is not a blanket rejection of commerce or prudent storage. It commends generosity and access to
necessities; verse 28 then rejects reliance on riches as a final security.

Proverbs 3:27–28 already forbids postponing available good due to a neighbor. Job 31:16–23 describes
a man who denies withholding from poor people, eating alone while the fatherless lacked food, or
leaving the needy unclothed; verses 24–25 deny making gold his confidence. Yet this is the oath of a
man who has lost his prosperity. His testimony stands against turning Proverbs’ promise into a
guaranteed financial return on gifts.

**Paul also uses sowing and harvest to encourage giving.** Second Corinthians 9:6–11 joins generous
sowing to generous reaping, voluntary giving, divine provision, and renewed generosity. Verse 10
links the supplied seed with an increase in the fruits of righteousness, and verse 11 directs
enrichment toward generosity and thanksgiving. The point of having more is the capacity to do
further good. The passage contains a quotation at verse 9, but this comparison does not identify
Proverbs 11 as the source of that quotation or claim direct borrowing from Proverbs.

Sources: Proverbs 3:27–28; 11:17–18, 24–28 and Job 31:16–25 checked in stored WEB. Second
Corinthians 9:6–11 checked through bible-api.com with `translation=web`, 2026-10-25; no unreached
book was added to raw.

**Ecclesiastes sends bread on the waters.** Ecclesiastes 11:1 commands *"Cast your bread on the
waters; for you shall find it after many days"*, and 11:2, *"Give a portion to seven, yes, even
to eight; for you don’t know what evil will be on the earth."* If these are read as generosity,
they share Proverbs 11:24's paradox that giving does not end in loss. Their reason is different,
though: not a promise that the giver will be refreshed, but ignorance of what disaster may come.
They can also be read as advice to spread commercial risk, and the text does not decide between
the readings. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 11:1–2.

## Significance

Both passages challenge the assumption that keeping everything is the surest way to flourish. Job
prevents the ethical encouragement from becoming an accusation against generous people who suffer
loss. See [[Wealth and Possessions]], [[Compassion and Mercy]], [[Famine and Plenty]], and
[[The Poor and the Vulnerable]].
