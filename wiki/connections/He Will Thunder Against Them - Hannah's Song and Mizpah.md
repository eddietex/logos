# He Will Thunder Against Them - Hannah's Song and Mizpah

## Passages involved

- [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] — 1 Samuel 1:6; *Her rival provoked her severely, to irritate her*.
- [[The Song of Hannah]] — 1 Samuel 2:10; *Those who strive with Yahweh shall be broken to pieces. He will
  thunder against them in the sky.*
- [[Samuel Judges Israel at Mizpah]] — 1 Samuel 7:10; *Yahweh thundered with a great thunder on that day on
  the Philistines, and confused them.*
- 2 Samuel 22:14 — David's song: *Yahweh thundered from heaven. The Most High uttered his voice.* (WEB at
  bible-api.com; not stored)
- Psalm 18:13 — the same line in the Psalter: *Yahweh also thundered in the sky.* (same)

## Analysis

**One verb, three times in 1 Samuel.** The verb is *rāʿam* (WLC lemma 7481). In the WLC files of Genesis
through 2 Kings, Ruth, and Psalms it occurs in 1 Samuel only at 1:6, 2:10, and 7:10, and elsewhere at 2 Samuel
22:14 and Psalms 18:14 (English 18:13); 29:3; 96:11; 98:7 (lemma search).

| Text | Form (WLC) | Subject | WEB |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Samuel 1:6 | *harrəʿimāh* | Peninnah, against Hannah | *to irritate her* |
| 1 Samuel 2:10 | *ʿālāw baššāmayim yarʿēm* | Yahweh, against those who strive with him | *He will thunder against them in the sky* |
| 1 Samuel 7:10 | *wayyarʿēm YHWH bəqôl-gāḏôl … ʿal-pəlištîm* | Yahweh, on the Philistines | *thundered with a great thunder* |
| 2 Samuel 22:14 | *yarʿēm min-šāmayim YHWH* | Yahweh | *thundered from heaven* |
| Psalm 18:14 (Eng. 13) | *wayyarʿēm baššāmayim YHWH* | Yahweh | *thundered in the sky* |

**From the song to the battle.** Hannah's line is a promise in the future: *He will thunder against them in
the sky*. Seven verses of narrative later it is a deed: *Yahweh thundered … on that day*. The object changes
from *those who strive with Yahweh*, *məriḇāw* (2:10, WLC), unnamed, to the Philistines; the occasion is
Hannah's own son praying and offering at Mizpah. The narrator does not point back to the song. The link is the
verb, which 1 Samuel uses only in these places.

**The rival's thunder.** At 1:6 the same root, in the hiphil with an object, describes what Peninnah does to
Hannah: *to irritate her*, perhaps *to make her rage* or *to make her fret* (glosses, not checked against a
lexicon). Whether the narrator means a link between the provocation and the song's thunder is not stated. It
is recorded here because the lemma search puts all three in one book and in the story of one family. Hannah is
provoked (1:6); she sings that Yahweh thunders against his adversaries (2:10); her son's prayer is answered
with thunder (7:10).

**The frame with David's song.** Hannah's song and David's are commonly read as framing the books of Samuel;
both end on *his king* and *his anointed* (1 Samuel 2:10; 2 Samuel 22:51). Thunder is in both: *yarʿēm* in
Hannah's last verse, and *yarʿēm min-šāmayim* in David's account of his deliverance (2 Samuel 22:14), a line
repeated as Psalm 18:13. See [[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].

**Thunder and confusion.** At 7:10 the thunder comes with *hāmam*, *confused them*, the verb of Yahweh's
victories at the Sea, Gibeon, and the Kishon. See
[[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].

## Significance

- **The song is answered inside the book.** Hannah's song reaches past her own story to the king and the
  anointed (2:10); its thunder is the first line of it that the narrative fulfils, and it is fulfilled for her
  son before there is a king.
- **The victory is assigned to Yahweh.** Israel only pursues (7:11). The verb puts the battle in the class of
  victories the Psalms celebrate as Yahweh's voice from heaven. See [[Signs and Wonders]] and [[War]].
- **What the page does not claim.** It does not claim that the narrator wrote 7:10 with 2:10 in view, or that
  1:6 is meant as wordplay. The verbal links are in the Hebrew; their intent is not stated.
