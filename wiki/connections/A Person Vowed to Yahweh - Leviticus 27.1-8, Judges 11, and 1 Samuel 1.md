# A Person Vowed to Yahweh - Leviticus 27.1-8, Judges 11, and 1 Samuel 1

## Passages involved

- [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]] — Leviticus 27:2–8, the valuation of a vowed person.
- Judges 11:30–31 — *"whatever comes out of the doors of my house to meet me ... it shall be
  Yahweh's, and I will offer it up for a burnt offering."* *(WEB, bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- Judges 11:39 — her father *"did with her according to his vow which he had vowed."* *(same)*
- 1 Samuel 1:11 — *"I will give him to Yahweh all the days of his life."* *(same)*
- 1 Samuel 1:28 — *"As long as he lives he is given to Yahweh."* *(same)*

## Analysis

**All three use *neder*, and all three involve a person (WLC).** Jephthah and Hannah both *vow a vow*,
*wayyiddar neder* and *wattiddōr neder*, the phrase also found at Genesis 28:20. Leviticus prices a
vowed person in silver. Hannah pays in person, by handing over the child. Jephthah's story ends in the
disputed sentence of 11:39.

**Neither story cites Leviticus 27.** Hannah's vow names lifelong giving, with no silver. Jephthah's
names a burnt offering, and Judges does not mention a valuation. The frequent claim that he could
simply have paid is an inference; his vow, as worded, was not the vow of 27:2. Readers still ask why the option of 27:2–8 never comes up.
The narrative does not say.

**Whether Jephthah sacrificed his daughter** is an old dispute, and Judges 11:39 stays reticent. If he
did, Leviticus 18:21 and 20:2–5 stand against it, and so does the pattern of
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]]. This page does not settle it.

**Hannah's razor clause** is taken up at
[[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]].

**Compare [[Yes, He Will Be Blessed - The Irrevocable Word]]**, which uses Jephthah as a case of a word
that cannot be recalled, and [[Pay What You Vow - Deuteronomy 23.21-23, Proverbs 20.25, and Ecclesiastes 5.4-6]].

## Significance

Leviticus sets a person's vow into a schedule. Hannah and Jephthah show two vows that leave that
schedule, one in gift and one in grief. Judges and 1 Samuel are not yet ingested.
