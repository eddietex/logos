## Analysis

**Proverbs 3:9 is built from two pieces of the Sinai law.** Its verb is the fifth commandment's —
*kabbēḏ ʾeṯ-*, *honour*, in the same form as *"Honor your father"* (Exodus 20:12) — with *Yahweh* as
its object; and its offering is the first fruits of Exodus 23:19 and 34:26, *rēʾšîṯ*. The lecture
it belongs to promises *"length of days"* (Proverbs 3:2), the fifth commandment's reward, and ends
by calling Yahweh a father (3:12). Whether the father who speaks the lecture meant his son to hear the
commandment in it is not stated; the shared forms are the evidence.

**The father widens the offering.** The law asks for the first fruits *of your ground*; Proverbs asks
Yahweh to be honoured *"with your substance"*, *hôn*, a general word for wealth, and *"the first fruits
of all your increase"*. Deuteronomy 26 makes the first fruits a confession — the worshipper recites
the story of the exodus over the basket — and Proverbs makes them an act of *honour*, the word for what
is owed to a parent or a king. In either case the first portion goes to Yahweh before the rest is
used.

**The promise is Deuteronomy's blessing, given to one man.** *ʾĂsāmîm*, *barns*, is a rare word; it is
the word of the covenant blessing at Deuteronomy 28:8, *"Yahweh will command the blessing on you in
your barns"*. Proverbs 3:10 takes the national blessing and gives it to the son who honours Yahweh
with his goods. Malachi 3:10 goes further and calls it a test the people may set God: *"test me now in
this"*. That is the only one of these texts that invites testing, and the wiki notes the contrast
with *"You shall not tempt Yahweh your God"* (Deuteronomy 6:16; see
[[Do Not Test Yahweh - Massah in Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and Hebrews]]) without resolving it.
The contrast is sharper in English than in Hebrew: Malachi's verb is *bāḥan* (*ûḇəḥānûnî*), Deuteronomy's
is *nāsâ* (*lōʾ ṯənassû*).

**The rich fool reverses the sequence.** Proverbs puts honour first and full barns second. Jesus'
parable has a man whose ground *"produced abundantly"*, whose barns are too small, and who builds
bigger ones to keep everything — no first fruits, no honour, and no tomorrow. God calls him
*"foolish"*, the word of Proverbs' whole vocabulary of folly; the parable closes, in Luke 12:21, on the
man *"not rich toward God"*. The full barn is not condemned; the barn that was never honoured with its
first portion is.

**A tension flagged, not resolved.** Proverbs 3:10 promises full barns; the next verse but one says
Yahweh reproves the son he loves (3:11–12), and Job, who offered for his children *"continually"* (Job
1:5), lost his flocks in a day. The promise states the ordinary order of blessing; the wiki keeps its
exceptions on [[Retribution]] and does not soften the verse.

### Grain gifts and the altar

Leviticus 2:11–16 distinguishes first-fruits gifts from what may be burned on the altar. Yeast and honey may be brought as first fruits but not burned for a pleasant aroma; parched fresh grain receives its own memorial rite. This adds preparation and destination to the first-fruits theme, without making all first-fruits gifts one recipe. See [[The Meal Offering]].

Source for this addition: stored WEB Leviticus 2:1–16.

## Significance

- It ties [[Festivals and First Fruits]] to wisdom: the harvest law becomes a principle for the use of
  all wealth.
- It ties [[Wealth and Possessions]] to [[Law and Obedience]]: the first claim on what one owns is
  Yahweh's.
- It places Proverbs 3:10 between Deuteronomy's national blessing and Malachi's challenge, and against
  the parable that shows the same barns as a fool's.
