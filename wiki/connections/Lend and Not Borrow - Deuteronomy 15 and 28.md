# Lend and Not Borrow - Deuteronomy 15 and 28

## Passages involved
- [[The Year of Release and the Open Hand]] — Deuteronomy 15:1–11.
- [[Blessings for Obedience (Deuteronomy)]] — Deuteronomy 28:12–13.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 15:5–6 links diligent obedience with blessing, lending to many nations without
borrowing, and ruling rather than being ruled. Deuteronomy 28:12–13 repeats the lending
promise beside seasonal rain and the image of being head rather than tail. Economic
independence forms part of the promised national standing.

The earlier setting matters: chapter 15 requires debt release and an open hand toward poor
neighbors, even while announcing abundance (1–11). The promise to lend therefore does not
cancel the commands regulating creditors. Neither passage presents every personal debt as
proof of disobedience, nor says the poor may be neglected because blessing was promised.

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 15:1–11 and 28:1–14; the comparison is of the English text.

## Significance

The repeated promise joins national prosperity to obligations toward vulnerable members of the community; prosperity cannot replace those obligations.
