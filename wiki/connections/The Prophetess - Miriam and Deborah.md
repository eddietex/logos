# The Prophetess - Miriam and Deborah

## Passages involved

- [[The Song of the Sea]] — Exodus 15:20; *Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron*.
- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:1–8; Miriam claims Yahweh has spoken through her.
- [[Deborah and Barak]] — Judges 4:4; *Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth*.
- [[The Song of Deborah]] — Judges 5:1–31.

## Analysis

*Nəbîʾâ*, *prophetess*, occurs twice in the WLC files for Genesis through Judges and 1 Samuel, by lemma
search: Exodus 15:20 and Judges 4:4. Each woman is introduced by a relation to a man: Miriam as *the
sister of Aaron*, Deborah as *the wife of Lappidoth*.

**Both stand beside a victory over chariots.** Miriam's title comes right after Pharaoh's chariots are
drowned (Exodus 15:19–20). Deborah's comes before Sisera's nine hundred chariots are confused and
destroyed. In both, the verb is *hāmam* and the last clause is *not one remained*; see
[[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].

**Both are tied to a song.** Miriam leads the women with tambourine and answers *Sing to Yahweh*
(Exodus 15:20–21). *Then Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam sang on that day* (Judges 5:1), and the
Song calls to her *Awake, awake, Deborah! Awake, awake, utter a song!* (5:12). Neither text reports
an oracle from Miriam. Deborah, by contrast, speaks for Yahweh in narrative: she quotes his command
to Barak (4:6–7), foretells *a woman's hand* (4:9), and names the day (4:14).

**They differ in office.** Miriam has no public office beyond leading the song, and in Numbers 12 she
is punished after she and Aaron ask *Has Yahweh indeed spoken only with Moses?* (12:2). Deborah also *judged Israel*, and Israel *came up to her for
judgment* (4:4–5). She is the only woman in the books read so far who holds the office of judge.

**The Song in her voice.** Judges 5:1 has *wattāšar*, *and she sang*, a feminine singular verb
before *Deborah and Barak* (WLC), as Exodus 15:1 has the masculine *yāšîr* before *Moses and the
children of Israel*. In both the verb agrees with the leader named first. Miriam answers the men
with the refrain (Exodus 15:21); Deborah speaks in the first person, *until I, Deborah, arose; until
I arose a mother in Israel* (Judges 5:7). The Song calls her not *prophetess* but *mother*. See
[[Prose and Song - The Sea in Exodus 14-15 and the Kishon in Judges 4-5]].

## Significance

Judges presents a woman who judges and prophesies, and does not remark on it as unusual. The
comparison with Miriam shows a pattern the text does not state: Yahweh's victories over chariot
armies are followed by a prophetess's song. See [[Prophecy and Vision]], [[Praise and Song]],
[[Miriam]], and [[Deborah (prophetess)]].
