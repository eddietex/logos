# Saul Has Slain His Thousands - 1 Samuel 18.7, 21.11, and 29.5

## Passages involved

- [[Saul's Jealousy of David]] — **1 Samuel 18:7–8** — *The women sang to one another as they played, and said, "Saul
  has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands." Saul was very angry, and this saying displeased him.* Stored WEB.
- **1 Samuel 21:11** (21:12 WLC) — *The servants of Achish said to him, "Isn't this David the king of the land? Didn't
  they sing to one another about him in dances, saying, 'Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten
  thousands?'"* Stored WEB; not yet ingested.
- **1 Samuel 29:5** — *Isn't this David, of whom people sang to one another in dances, saying, 'Saul has slain his
  thousands, and David his ten thousands?'* Stored WEB; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

**One couplet, three hearers.** The words of the song are the same each time: *hikkâ šāʾûl baʾălāp̄āyw wəḏāwiḏ
bəriḇəḇōṯāyw* (18:7; 21:12 WLC; 29:5, WLC, allowing for the written and read forms of *baʾălāp̄āyw*). So is the verb of
singing, *ʿānâ* in the sense morphhb tags as lemma 6030 c, *sing* or *answer in song*, and in 21:12 and 29:5 the dances,
*bamməḥōlôṯ*, are named with it (WLC). The first hearer is Saul, who hears a rival. The second is Achish's court at
Gath, which hears an Israelite king: *Isn't this David the king of the land?* (21:11). The third is the Philistine
princes at Aphek, who hear a soldier who will turn in the battle (29:4–5). Saul fears the song because it puts David
above him; the Philistines fear it because it puts David against them.

**What the song says.** The second line has the larger number. In the files checked (WLC, Genesis through 2 Samuel,
and Ruth), the pair *ʾelep̄ / rəḇāḇâ* is otherwise a blessing (Genesis 24:60, *the mother of thousands of ten
thousands*), a prayer (Numbers 10:36, *Return, Yahweh, to the ten thousands of the thousands of Israel*), a muster (Judges 20:10), a taunt about a rout (Deuteronomy 32:30, *How could one chase a thousand, and two
put ten thousand to flight*), and a tribal blessing that gives the ten thousands to Ephraim, the younger, and the
thousands to Manasseh (Deuteronomy 33:17) (stored WEB). Read as parallelism, the couplet may praise both men; read as a
count, it ranks them. 1 Samuel lets Saul read it as a count (18:8) and never says what the women meant.

**What the Philistines add.** Achish's servants call David *the king of the land*, *meleḵ hāʾāreṣ* (21:12 WLC). Saul
had asked *What can he have more but the kingdom?* (18:8); the Philistines answer as if he already has it. Yahweh had told Samuel *I have provided a
king for myself among his sons* (16:1, stored WEB); at Gath a foreign court says it of David aloud.

## Significance

The song is the hinge between David's victory and Saul's enmity, and the narrator keeps it audible across the rest of
the book, so that the reader hears Saul's reign measured in it even in Philistine mouths. It belongs with the women's
songs of victory (see
[[With Tambourines and with Dances - Miriam, Jephthah's Daughter, and the Daughters of Shiloh]]), and shows what such
a song can do once a second name is in it. See [[Singing Women (of Saul's time)]], [[Saul]], [[David]], [[Envy]], and
[[Praise and Song]].
