# Never Yoked - The Red Heifer and the Philistine Cows

## Passages involved
- [[The Red Heifer]] — **Numbers 19:2** — *a red heifer without spot, in which is no defect, and which was never
  yoked*, *ʾăšer lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿāleyhā ʿōl* (stored WEB; WLC)
- [[An Unsolved Killing and the Broken-Necked Heifer]] — **Deuteronomy 21:3** — *a heifer of the herd, which
  hasn't been worked with, and which has not drawn in the yoke*, *ʾăšer lōʾ-māšəḵâ bəʿōl* (stored WEB; WLC)
- [[The Ark Returns to Israel]] — **1 Samuel 6:7** — *two milk cows, on which there has come no yoke*, *ʾăšer
  lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿălêhem ʿōl* (stored WEB; WLC); **6:14** — *offered up the cows for a burnt offering to Yahweh*

## Analysis

The relative clause of 1 Samuel 6:7 is word for word the clause of Numbers 19:2, with only the pronoun
changed from singular to plural: *ʾăšer lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿāleyhā ʿōl* / *ʾăšer lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿălêhem ʿōl* (WLC). Literally:
*on which a yoke has not come up*. Deuteronomy 21:3 states the same condition with different words, *which
has not drawn in the yoke*, and adds *which hasn't been worked with*.

| | Animal | Never yoked | What happens to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers 19:2 | *pārâ*, red heifer, without defect | *lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿāleyhā ʿōl* | slaughtered outside the camp, burned; ashes for the water of cleansing; *It is a sin offering* (19:3, 9) |
| Deuteronomy 21:3 | *ʿeḡlâ*, heifer of the herd | *lōʾ-māšəḵâ bəʿōl* | neck broken in an untilled valley, for unsolved bloodguilt (21:4–9) |
| 1 Samuel 6:7 | *pārôṯ ʿālôṯ*, two milk cows | *lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿălêhem ʿōl* | pull the ark to Beth Shemesh; then a burnt offering (6:14) |

In all three, the animal untouched by work is set apart for a use that touches the holy or the guilty. The
Philistine priests are not said to know Numbers; the narrator gives them its language. Their purpose is
different: an animal never broken to the yoke should not pull a cart straight, and cows separated from their
calves should turn home. The condition makes the test hard, and so makes the outcome a sign. The men of Beth
Shemesh, whether or not they thought of Numbers 19, treat the cows as fit for the altar.

The noun is the same in Numbers 19 and 1 Samuel 6, *pārâ*, *cow, heifer* (lemma 6510, WLC); Deuteronomy has
*ʿeḡlâ*. The verb *ʿālâ*, *come up*, recurs through 1 Samuel 6: the yoke that has not *come up* on the cows
(7), the ark that *goes up* to Beth Shemesh (9), the burnt offering that is *offered up*, *heʿĕlû ʿōlâ* (14,
WLC). The clause of 6:7 is one instance of a verb the chapter keeps using.

## Significance

The parallel shows the Philistines' test built from Israel's sacrificial vocabulary: what Israel's law set
apart for cleansing and for bloodguilt, the Philistines set apart to carry the ark, and Israel turns into a
burnt offering. The narrator does not cite Numbers. See [[Sacrifice and Offering]], [[Holiness]], and
[[Signs and Wonders]].
