# Kidnapping and Sale - Joseph and the Law of Exodus 21

## Passages involved

- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:26–28: the brothers' decision to sell and Joseph's
  sale for twenty pieces of silver.
- [[The Cupbearer and the Baker]] — Genesis 40:15: Joseph describes himself as stolen from his homeland.
- [[Laws of Violence and Injury]] — Exodus 21:16: death for kidnapping followed by sale or continued possession.
- [[Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning]] — Deuteronomy 24:7; kidnapping an Israelite brother and enslaving or
  selling him.

## Analysis

Judah proposes sale as a way to avoid killing Joseph (Genesis 37:26–27). The narrative records
a sale, and Joseph later describes his removal as theft (40:15). Exodus 21:16 makes kidnapping
and sale, or kidnapping with the victim still held, a capital offense. The later law therefore
challenges any reading of the brothers' transaction as harmless simply because it spared
Joseph's immediate death.

This is a comparison of narrative and later law, **not a report that Genesis tried the brothers
under a Sinai statute**. Genesis 37:28 also leaves a difficulty about the actors in the sale,
recorded on its passage page; the comparison need not settle that difficulty to recognize
Joseph's involuntary captivity. Deuteronomy 24:7 explicitly names a brother from Israel as the
victim and requires removal of the offender. The WEB wording in Exodus 21:16 names *someone*
without that explicit Israelite qualification.

Exodus's prohibition of kidnapping stands beside its regulation of purchased servitude in 21:2.
These are not an abolition decree and an immediate repeal of it: the collection condemns seizure
while continuing to recognize other forms of human ownership. That distinction explains the
shape of the legislation without removing its moral tension.

**Deuteronomy's wording and Joseph's case.** Deuteronomy 24:7 reads *"If a man is found stealing any of
his brothers of the children of Israel, and he deals with him as a slave, or sells him; then that thief
shall die."* Exodus 21:16 has *gōnēb ʾîš*, *one who steals a man*; Deuteronomy has *gōnēb nepeš*, *one
who steals a life*, and specifies *"his brothers"* (WLC). In Deuteronomy *"brothers"* means fellow
Israelites. Joseph's sellers were his brothers in the literal sense too. The law does not mention them,
and the comparison remains the wiki's. See [[Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 24:7; WLC
Exodus 21:16; Deuteronomy 24:7.

## Significance

The sale that moves Genesis toward Egypt becomes a kind of act the delivered people must punish.
See [[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[Law and Obedience]]. This is a literary and ethical comparison,
not a claim of an explicit quotation or prophecy fulfillment.
