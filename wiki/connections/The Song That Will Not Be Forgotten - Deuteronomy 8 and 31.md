# The Song That Will Not Be Forgotten - Deuteronomy 8 and 31

## Analysis

### The song now delivered

The song now voices the prediction: abundance becomes abandonment (32:13–18). Its conclusion requires teaching children to obey (46–47), so remembered testimony remains an urgent summons rather than a guarantee of faithfulness.

Deuteronomy 8:10–20 warns that fullness in a good land can lead to forgetting Yahweh, claiming
wealth as one's own achievement, and serving other gods. Deuteronomy 31:20 predicts the
corresponding betrayal: the people will eat, become full, grow fat, and turn to other gods.
The gift is not withdrawn from the account; its recipients despise its giver.

There is a pointed difference between forgetting Yahweh and forgetting a song. The song will
not disappear from descendants' mouths (31:21), even when covenant loyalty fails. Its survival
therefore supplies testimony against the people, not evidence that they have remained faithful.

The law's recurring reading includes children who have not known (31:12–13); the song likewise
moves beyond Moses through teaching and remembered speech (31:19–22). These provisions share
a concern for transmission, but this unit particularly stresses the song's accusing role.

## Significance

[[Remembrance]] and [[Law and Obedience]] cannot be equated mechanically. A community can
retain sacred words that expose its departure from them. [[Praise and Song]] includes witness
and warning as well as celebration; [[Wealth and Possessions]] remains a test of allegiance.
