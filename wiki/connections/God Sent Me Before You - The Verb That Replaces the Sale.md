# God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:28, 36** — *"sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver"*;
  *"the Midianites sold him into Egypt"* — ***mākar*** — [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]
- **Genesis 45:4–8** — *"I am Joseph, your brother, whom you **sold** into Egypt… for God **sent** me
  before you to preserve life… **God sent me** before you to preserve for you a remnant… **it wasn't
  you who sent me here, but God**"* — [[Joseph Reveals Himself]]
- **Genesis 50:19–20** — *"You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good"* — ***ḥāšab*** twice
  — [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] and
  [[You Meant Evil Against Me - The Two Intentions at Genesis 50.20]]. **The second answer keeps the
  brothers' verb where this one drops it**
- **Psalm 105:17** — *"He **sent** a man before them; Joseph was **sold** for a servant"* — **both
  verbs in one verse, in that order**
- **Acts 7:9–10** — *"The patriarchs, moved with jealousy against Joseph, sold him into Egypt. God was
  with him, and delivered him out of all his afflictions"*
- **Genesis 47:25** — [[Joseph and the Famine]]. ***"You have saved our lives!"*** —
  ***heḥĕyitānû***, **the Egyptians using Joseph's own verb of preservation about themselves**, in
  the act of becoming Pharaoh's servants. **The doctrine he states twice about his own life is
  stated back to him once, by the people it cost their land**

## Analysis

**Genesis 45:4–8 is built on the substitution of one verb for another, and it performs the
substitution in the open.**

| verse | subject | verb | object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **45:4** | *you* | ***məkartem*** — **sold** | *me, into Egypt* |
| **45:5** | *you* | ***məkartem*** — **sold** | *me, here* |
| **45:5** | ***ʾĕlōhîm*** | ***šəlāḥanî*** — **sent me** | *before you, to preserve life* |
| **45:7** | ***ʾĕlōhîm*** | ***wayyišlāḥēnî*** — **sent me** | *before you, for a remnant* |
| **45:8** | *not you… but* ***hāʾĕlōhîm*** | ***šəlaḥtem*** — **sent** | *me here* |

**Two occurrences of *sell* and three of *send*, in five verses, with the same man as object.**
Joseph does not deny the first verb — **45:4 is the only accusation he makes in the whole chapter, and
he makes it before he says anything consoling** — **but he stops using it after 45:5 and never uses it
again.**

***Šālaḥ*** **is the ordinary verb for dispatching a messenger, a servant or an army.** Applied to
Joseph it converts twenty-two years of slavery, false accusation and imprisonment into **an errand**,
and it converts the brothers from perpetrators into **an unwitting delivery mechanism.** **This is
generous and it is also, read flatly, false** — they did send him, by selling him, as 45:4 has just
established.

**Three ways of taking 45:8 are available and Genesis supports all three:**

1. **The Hebrew comparative.** Biblical Hebrew regularly says *not X but Y* where the sense is *not so
   much X as Y* — as at Hosea 6:6, *"I desire mercy and not sacrifice"*, which the rest of the Torah
   forbids reading as an abolition of sacrifice. **45:8 would then be a statement of relative weight,
   not of exclusive agency.**
2. **Deliberate overstatement, pastorally aimed.** The audience is eleven men who *"couldn't answer
   him; for they were terrified at his presence"* (45:3). **A sentence that leaves them culpable is a
   sentence that leaves them on the floor.** *"Don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves"* (45:5) is
   the stated purpose of the whole speech.
3. **A claim about the order of causes.** The sale is the proximate cause and the sending is the
   final one; **both are true of the same act and they do not compete.**

**What settles the question is that Genesis says it again, five chapters later, in a form that
concedes everything reading 1 and 2 leave out.**

> **50:20** You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to save many people alive, as is
> happening today.

***Ḥāšab*** **is used of both parties.** *You reckoned* — ***ḥăšabtem*** — *evil; God reckoned it for
good.* **One event, two intentions, both named, neither cancelled.** ***"You meant evil"*** **stands
as an indictment in the same sentence that absolves nobody and forgives everyone.** **50:20 is
therefore the controlling statement and 45:8 is its first, warmer draft** — and the fact that the
brothers need the doctrine restated at all (50:15, *"It may be that Joseph will hate us"*) is the
evidence that 45:5–8 did not finish the job.

**Psalm 105:17 puts both verbs in one line and in the right order:** ***šālaḥ lipnêhem ʾîš*** *— he
sent a man before them —* **immediately followed by** *"Joseph was sold for a servant."* **The psalm
is quoting 45:5's idiom** (*before you*, ***lipnêkem***) **and then declining to drop the sale.** See
[[Sold for a Servant - Joseph's Sale in Psalm 105 and Acts 7]].

**Acts 7:9–10 does the same and adds the motive Genesis withheld.** Stephen says the patriarchs were
***zēlōsantes*** — *moved with jealousy* — **which is 37:11's *"his brothers envied him"* supplied as
a cause**, and then says *"God was with him"*, the formula of 39:2. **Neither text takes the option of
saying the brothers did not really do it.**

## Significance

- **The Bible's standard grammar for providence is set here: two agents, one act, and no subtraction.**
  Acts 2:23 applies the identical structure to the crucifixion — *"delivered up by the determined
  counsel and foreknowledge of God, you have crucified"* — **and the sentence keeps both halves**, as
  50:20 does. Romans 8:28 states it as a rule without narrating a case.
- **The difference between 45:8 and 50:20 is the difference between comfort and accounting**, and
  Genesis supplies both rather than choosing. **A reader who has only 45:8 has a doctrine that can be
  used to tell victims their injury was an errand.** 50:20 is the verse that makes that impossible.
- **Neither statement is the narrator's.** Genesis attributes no event in the Joseph cycle to God
  between 39:23 and 46:2. **Both of the book's great providence sentences are spoken by a character
  who has every reason to want them to be true**, and Genesis declines to underwrite them. See
  [[Providence]].
- **The verb governs how later Scripture reads the sale.** Psalm 105 and Acts 7 both retell it, and
  both keep *sold* — **the tradition preserved Joseph's reframing without letting it erase the record.**
