# The Mizpah Benediction - Genesis 31.49 and How It Is Read

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 31:48–52** — *"Yahweh watch between me and you, when we are absent one from another"*,
  and the four verses around it — [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]]
- **Genesis 21:22–32** — the other oath between this family and an outsider, also over property, also
  at a named site — [[The Treaty at Beersheba]]
- **Numbers 6:24–26** — the actual blessing formula the Torah supplies, for comparison
- **1 Samuel 7:5–6; Judges 20:1; 2 Kings 25:23** — the later Mizpahs, which are other places

## Analysis

**One sentence out of Genesis 31 has had a career of its own**, printed on rings and lockets, said at
railway stations in wartime, and read at the end of services and at gravesides:

> *"Yahweh watch between me and you, when we are absent one from another."*

**In that use it means: may God look after you while we are apart.** It is affectionate, it is a
parting, and the watching is protective.

**In Genesis it is none of those things.**

***"Mizpah"*** is ***miṣpâ***, from ***ṣāpâ***, *to keep watch, to look out* — a **watchtower**, and
the etiology is the clause itself: *yiṣep YHWH*, **let Yahweh keep watch.** **The two men who say it
have just spent seven days chasing and being chased**, one of them has searched the other's tents on
his hands and knees, and neither believes the other. **What they want is a witness.**

**The context settles it in both directions.** The verse before names the heap ***a witness***
(31:48). **The verse after says what the watching is for**: *"if you afflict my daughters, or if you
take wives besides my daughters, no man is with us; behold, **God is witness** between me and you"*
(31:50). **And three verses later the boundary is drawn in both directions**: *"I will not pass over
this heap to you, and… you will not pass over this heap and this pillar to me, for harm"* (31:52).

***"When we are absent one from another"*** is literally ***when we are hidden, each from his
neighbour*** — ***nissātēr ʾîš mērēʿēhû***, the niphal of *sātar*, to be concealed. **The clause is
not about missing each other. It is about not being able to see what the other one is doing** — and
the whole point of appointing God as the watcher is that he can.

***ʿĒd***, *witness*, occurs **five times in eight verses.** There is no court on that mountain and no
third party, **so the parties appoint a heap of stones and the God of their grandfathers, and the
sentence engraved on the jewellery is the clause that empanels them.** See [[Oaths and Vows]] and
[[Galeed]].

## Significance

**The wiki records the popular reading because it is nearly universal, and records that the text will
not bear it.** This is not a case where two readings are both defensible; **it is a case where a
sentence has been detached from four verses that say what it means.**

**Why it detaches so easily is worth stating, because it is instructive.** Read alone, every word in
it is warm: *Yahweh*, *watch*, *between me and you*, *absent one from another*. **Nothing inside the
sentence marks it as adversarial.** The adversarial content is entirely in the surrounding clauses —
the witness, the daughters, the second wife, the boundary — **and a verse lifted out of a chapter
takes none of its chapter with it.**

**The Torah does supply a benediction, and it is not this one.** Numbers 6:24–26 — *"Yahweh bless you
and keep you"* — **is a blessing in form and function**, given to be said over people. **Genesis 31:49
is a treaty clause in the middle of a non-aggression pact between a man and his father-in-law.**

**Two things can be said in the popular reading's favour, and the wiki says them.** First, **the
appeal is genuine**: two men who cannot enforce anything against each other hand the enforcement to
God, which is a real act of faith even when the motive is mutual suspicion. Second, **the parting is
real too** — 31:55 has Laban kissing his daughters and grandchildren and blessing them, and it is the
last thing he ever does in Scripture. **The affection at the end of the chapter is not invented by
the jewellers.** But it is at 31:55, and the *Mizpah* clause is at 31:49, **and six verses is the
distance between a farewell and a surveillance agreement.**
