# Remember Lot's Wife

## Passages involved

- Genesis 19:17, 26 — the command not to look back, and the one clause in which it is broken
- Luke 17:26–33 — the days of Noah, the day of Lot, *"Remember Lot's wife"*, and the saying about
  saving one's life
- Wisdom 10:6–7 — the deuterocanonical retelling that first supplies her a disposition
- 1 Clement 11 — the pillar as *"a sign"*, in the earliest Christian use outside the New Testament

## Analysis

**Genesis gives her a verb; Luke gives her a meaning.** The wiki's interest in this pair is the gap
between the two, which is measurable almost to the word.

Genesis 19:26 in full:

> **26** But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt.

No name, no motive, no reaction from anyone, and no comment. The verb *wattabbēṭ* is the verb of the
prohibition three sentences earlier — *"Don't look behind you"*, *ʾal-tabbîṭ ʾaḥăreykā* (19:17) — so
the offence and the command are written in the same word, and that is the entire literary
apparatus. The chapter does not even pause: the next verse is about Abraham getting up early.

### What Luke does with it

Luke 17:32 is three words in Greek — *mnēmoneuete tēs gynaikos Lōt* — and it is embedded in a
sequence that supplies everything Genesis withheld.

| Luke 17 | |
|---|---|
| 26–27 | *the days of Noah* — eating, drinking, marrying, until the flood |
| 28–29 | *the days of Lot* — *"they ate, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they built"*, until fire and sulfur rained |
| 30–31 | the day of the Son of Man; and *"let him who is on the housetop, and his goods in the house, not go down to take them away"* |
| **32** | **"Remember Lot's wife."** |
| 33 | *"Whoever seeks to save his life loses it, but whoever loses his life preserves it."* |

**The saying is bracketed by two statements about property and self-preservation**, and that is the
sense it takes: she is the person who turned back toward what was behind her. Genesis does not say
she did that. Luke's reading is an interpretation of a bare verb, and it is the earliest one on
record in the New Testament.

Note what Luke pairs the two catastrophes for. The indictment in 17:28 is not wickedness — it is
**ordinariness**: buying, selling, planting, building. The point of the pair is the suddenness of
the interruption, not the character of the interrupted, and Lot's wife serves it as the one person
who was already out and did not stay out.

### Wisdom and the tradition

Wisdom of Solomon 10:6–7 has the rescue of *"a righteous man"* from the fire, and then the region's
scars, *"and a pillar of salt standing as a monument to an **unbelieving soul**"* — *apistousēs
psychēs*. **This is where unbelief enters**, some two centuries before Luke and roughly a millennium
after the narrative. 1 Clement 11 follows: she is set up *"as a sign"*, and the moral drawn is that
*"those who are double-minded … become a judgment and a sign to all generations."*

So the sequence is legible, and the wiki records it as a sequence rather than as a single meaning:

1. **Genesis** — an act, and its consequence, with no interior at all.
2. **Wisdom** — the act read as unbelief.
3. **Luke** — the act read as looking back toward possessions, in a discourse about the suddenness
   of the end.
4. **Later homiletics** — grief, nostalgia, curiosity, attachment to the city, and much else. None
   of it in the text.

## Significance

**The verse is the New Testament's shortest citation of a person, and the person has no name.** That
is worth stating on its own: the canon's memory of Genesis 19 keeps a nameless woman's single
gesture and makes it a proverb, while Lot — who has forty verses, a rescue, and a bargain — is cited
only twice in the whole New Testament.

For the wiki's method, this is the clean case of something it flags repeatedly: **Genesis narrates
without grading, and the later canon grades without narrating.** The same pattern governs
[[Righteous Lot - 2 Peter's Verdict and Genesis's Silence]], where the verdict runs the other way
and is generous. Here it runs against her, and it comes from a source outside Genesis in both
directions.
