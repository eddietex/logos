# Joshua and Jesus - One Name

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 17:9–14** — the name's first appearance: *"Moses said to Joshua, 'Choose men for us, and go out, fight
  with Amalek'"* — [[The Battle with Amalek]]
- **Numbers 13:8, 16** — *"Of the tribe of Ephraim, Hoshea the son of Nun"*; *"Moses called Hoshea the son of Nun
  Joshua"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Matthew 1:21** — *"You shall call his name Jesus, for it is he who shall save his people from their sins"*
  *(not yet ingested)*
- **Acts 7:45** — *"which also our fathers, in their turn, brought in with Joshua"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Hebrews 4:8** — *"For if Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward of another day"* *(not
  yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Joshua and Jesus are the same name.** Hebrew ***yəhôšuaʿ*** (Exodus 17:9; WLC) comes into Greek as
***Iēsous***, and the New Testament uses that one form for both men. **The WEB, like most English versions,
translates the same Greek word two ways**: *"Jesus"* at Matthew 1:21 (*kaleseis to onoma autou Iēsoun*), and
*"Joshua"* at Acts 7:45 (*meta Iēsou*) and Hebrews 4:8 (*ei gar autous Iēsous katepausen*) (SBLGNT, checked this
run). **A Greek reader of Hebrews 4:8 meets the sentence *"if Jesus had given them rest"*,** and has to tell from
context that the Jesus meant is the son of Nun.

### What the name says

**Numbers makes the name a renaming**: *"Moses called Hoshea the son of Nun Joshua"* (Numbers 13:16; WEB) —
***hôšēaʿ*** to ***yəhôšuaʿ***, with the divine name *Yah/Yahô* added at the front (WLC). **Both forms are commonly
derived from *yāšaʿ*, *to save***, so that *Hoshea* is *salvation* or *he saves* and *Joshua* *Yahweh saves* or
*Yahweh is salvation*; **that etymology is standard in the lexica, but neither Numbers nor Exodus states it.**
The verb *yāšaʿ* is the verb of the sea: *"Thus Yahweh saved (wayyôšaʿ) Israel that day"* (Exodus 14:30; WLC);
its noun *yəšûʿâ* first appears in Jacob's *"I have waited for your salvation"* (Genesis 49:18; WLC). See
[[I Have Waited for Your Salvation - The First Yeshuah in Scripture]].

**Matthew 1:21 gives the reason for the name**: *"You shall call his name Jesus, for it is he who shall save his
people from their sins"* — *autos gar sōsei* (SBLGNT). **In Greek the clause explains the name without a
wordplay**, since *Iēsous* and *sōzō* do not sound alike; **the pun works only in Hebrew or Aramaic**, between a
form of *Yəhôšuaʿ/Yēšûaʿ* and *yāšaʿ*. **Many commentators therefore read Matthew 1:21 as presupposing a Semitic
etymology of the name**; the wiki reports that reading and does not quote any Hebrew or Aramaic form of Matthew,
which does not exist in the manuscript tradition.

### The typology the New Testament draws, and the one it does not

**Hebrews draws a contrast, not a likeness.** *"If Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward
of another day"* (Hebrews 4:8): the first Joshua brought Israel into the land but not into the *rest* Psalm 95
promised, so the rest remains (4:9). **The point of the shared name in Hebrews is that the first bearer was not
the last.** **Acts 7:45 uses the name in passing**, in Stephen's speech: what *"our fathers … brought in with Joshua"*
(the antecedent is in 7:44, given here by reference). **No New Testament text says *Joshua was a type of Jesus* in so many words**; the explicit typology —
Joshua leading the people into the land as a figure of Jesus leading them into salvation — is developed by later
Christian interpreters (often cited from the second century onward; those sources are not checked here).

### What Exodus 17 adds

**The first scene in which the name appears is a deliverance in which the man named *Yahweh saves* fights and
wins, while the victory depends on a raised hand above him** — *"When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed"*
(17:11). **Christian readers have read the raised hands and the man below them typologically**; the text itself
makes no such move, and its own interest is in the memorial, the altar, and the war that does not end. See
[[The Battle with Amalek]] and [[Joshua (son of Nun)]].

## Significance

- **It is a fact about a name before it is an interpretation**: one Hebrew name, one Greek form, two English
  renderings. The WEB's *"Joshua"* at Hebrews 4:8 and *"Jesus"* at Matthew 1:21 translate the same word.
- **The New Testament's own use of the parallel is a contrast** (Hebrews 4:8), not a claim of likeness; the fuller
  typology is later.
- **It explains why Joshua's renaming in Numbers 13:16 has been read as significant**, and why Exodus 17's narrator, who uses the later name from the first line, has been read as writing
  with it in view. Both are readings; the text records only the name.
