# Out of the Heart - Proverbs 4.23, Matthew 15, and Luke 6

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 4:23–24** — *"Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring of
  life. Put away from yourself a perverse mouth. Put corrupt lips far from you."* —
  [[Keep Your Heart]]
- **Matthew 15:18–19** — *"But the things which proceed out of the mouth come out of the heart, and
  they defile the man. For out of the heart come evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, sexual sins,
  thefts, false testimony, and blasphemies."* (WEB, verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)
- **Mark 7:21–23** — *"For from within, out of the hearts of men, proceed evil thoughts …"* (WEB,
  verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)
- **Luke 6:45** — *"The good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings out that which is
  good, and the evil man out of the evil treasure of his heart brings out that which is evil, for
  out of the abundance of the heart, his mouth speaks."* (WEB, verified at bible-api.com; not yet
  ingested)

## Analysis

**The direction is the same.** Proverbs 4:23 makes the heart the place from which life goes out,
and the very next verse turns to the mouth. Jesus' sayings run the same way: what comes out of the
mouth comes out of the heart (Matthew 15:18), and the mouth speaks from the heart's abundance
(Luke 6:45). In all three the heart is the source and speech and action are its outflow.

**The emphasis differs.** Proverbs is an instruction to guard a source that can be kept; the
father assumes the son's heart can hold his words (4:21) and be kept with diligence. Matthew 15 and
Mark 7 list what already comes out of the human heart — evil thoughts, murders, adulteries — in a
dispute about ritual washing, to locate defilement within rather than in food. Luke's saying holds
both possibilities, the good treasure and the evil. Proverbs' image of a spring that must be
guarded sits closest to Luke's.

**Not a quotation.** None of the Gospel passages cites Proverbs, and the wiki claims only a shared
picture of heart and mouth, not dependence.

## Significance

Proverbs 4:23 is the Old Testament's most compact statement that conduct flows from the inner
life. The Gospels presuppose the same anthropology and press it further: the heart that Proverbs
asks to be guarded is, in Matthew 15, the source of the very evils the commandments forbid. See
[[Murder, Adultery, and the Heart - Exodus 20 and Matthew 5]] for the same move made on the
commandments themselves.
