# It Was a Chance - Miqreh in 1 Samuel 6, Ruth 2, and Ecclesiastes

## Passages involved
- [[The Ark Returns to Israel]] — **1 Samuel 6:9** — *if not, then we shall know that it is not his hand that
  struck us. It was a chance that happened to us*, *miqreh hûʾ hāyâ lānû* (stored WEB; WLC)
- [[Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz]] — **Ruth 2:3** — *she happened to come to the portion of the field
  belonging to Boaz*, *wayyiqer miqrehā*, literally *her chance chanced* (stored WEB; WLC)
- **1 Samuel 20:26** — Saul: *Something has happened to him. He is not clean*, *miqreh hûʾ* (stored WEB; WLC;
  not yet ingested)
- [[The Wise and the Fool Share One End]] — **Ecclesiastes 2:14–15** — *one event happens to them all*
  (stored WEB; WLC)
- [[From Dust to Dust]] — **Ecclesiastes 3:19** — *that which happens to the sons of men happens to animals*
  (stored WEB; WLC)
- [[One Event Comes to All]] — **Ecclesiastes 9:2–3** — *There is one event to the righteous and to the
  wicked* (stored WEB; WLC)

## Analysis

*Miqreh* (lemma 4745), from *qārâ*, *to meet, happen*, means *what befalls*, an occurrence not planned by the
one it falls on. In the WLC files of Genesis through 2 Kings, Ruth, Psalms, and Ecclesiastes it occurs only in
the verses above (lemma search): twice in 1 Samuel, once in Ruth, five times in Ecclesiastes.

The three books use it differently.

- **1 Samuel 6:9** sets *miqreh* against *his hand*. The Philistine priests build a test with two outcomes:
  the cows go to Beth Shemesh and the plague was Yahweh's, or they do not and it was *chance*. The text
  treats the two as exclusive. The cows go straight.
- **Ruth 2:3** uses the noun with its own verb, *wayyiqer miqrehā*, *her chance happened*. The narrator says
  the meeting with Boaz was chance and in the next verse brings Boaz from Bethlehem with a blessing in
  Yahweh's name (2:4); Naomi will read it as Yahweh's *kindness* (2:20). The word is used, and the story
  undercuts it without denying it.
- **1 Samuel 20:26** is Saul explaining David's absence as a ritual mishap: *Something has happened to him*.
  The reader knows it is not chance.
- **Ecclesiastes** uses *miqreh* for the single end, death, that *happens* to wise and fool, man and beast,
  righteous and wicked (2:14–15; 3:19; 9:2–3). There it is not an accident against providence, but the
  sameness of the outcome regardless of conduct.

Ecclesiastes 9:11, *time and chance happen to them all*, uses a different noun, *pegaʿ* (WLC), not *miqreh*;
see [[Time and Chance and the Prepared Horse]].

## Significance

The Philistines' alternative, Yahweh's hand or chance, is the question Ruth's narrator answers by story and
the Teacher answers by observation. 1 Samuel resolves it in one direction by a sign; Ruth leaves the word
standing and shows Yahweh's kindness working through what *happened*; Ecclesiastes uses it of death's
indifference to merit. The three uses are reported side by side; the wiki does not reconcile them. See
[[Providence]], [[Signs and Wonders]], and
[[One Event Happens to Them All - Job 21.23-26 and Ecclesiastes 2.14-16]].
