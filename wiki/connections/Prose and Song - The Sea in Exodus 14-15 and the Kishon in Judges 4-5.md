# Prose and Song - The Sea in Exodus 14-15 and the Kishon in Judges 4-5

## Passages involved

- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 14; the prose account.
- [[The Song of the Sea]] — Exodus 15:1–21; the song.
- [[Deborah and Barak]] and [[Jael and Sisera]] — Judges 4; the prose account.
- [[The Song of Deborah]] — Judges 5; the song.

## Analysis

Twice in the books read, a victory is told first in prose and then again in a song, and the two are
left side by side. The pairs are alike in more than form.

| | Exodus 14–15 | Judges 4–5 |
|---|---|---|
| The enemy's strength | *Pharaoh's chariots and his army* (15:4) | *nine hundred chariots of iron* (4:3) |
| Yahweh's act in the prose | *confused the Egyptian army* (14:24) | *Yahweh confused Sisera, all his chariots* (4:15) |
| None left | *There remained not so much as one of them* (14:28) | *There was not a man left* (4:16) |
| Water | *The sea covered them* (15:10) | *The river Kishon swept them away* (5:21) |
| Heaven or nature fighting | *Yahweh fights for them* (14:25); the wind (15:10) | *From the sky the stars fought* (5:20) |
| The singers | *Moses and the children of Israel* (15:1); *Miriam the prophetess* (15:20–21) | *Deborah and Barak* (5:1); Deborah, *a prophetess* (4:4) |
| The verb of singing | *ʾāz yāšîr*, *then … sang* (15:1, WLC) | *wattāšar*, *and she sang* (5:1, WLC) |

The shared verb *hāmam*, *confused*, and the *not one left* are already on
[[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]]; the two prophetesses are on
[[The Prophetess - Miriam and Deborah]]. This page records the larger shape the two pairs share.

**What the song adds.** In both, the prose tells the event in order and gives the cause in one clause.
The song gives the cause at length and adds what the prose left out. Exodus 15 adds Yahweh as *a man of
war* and looks forward to the nations' terror and the holy mountain. Judges 5 adds the theophany from
Seir, the roll of the tribes, the stars and the river, Meroz, the blessing on Jael, and Sisera's mother.
Neither song is a summary of its prose.

**Where they differ.** Exodus 15 is sung by all Israel, with Miriam leading the women; Judges 5 by two
named people, with the verb agreeing with Deborah. Exodus 15 looks forward to the land; Judges 5 looks
inward at the tribes and divides them. And Exodus 14–15 have no Jael: the enemy dies in the water, not in
a tent. In Judges the prose and the song also differ in detail — Sisera asleep (4:21) or falling at Jael's
feet (5:27), two tribes (4:10) or six (5:14–18) — and the wiki records those differences on
[[The Song of Deborah]] rather than harmonizing them.

## Significance

Israel remembered its great deliverances twice: as a story and as a song. The pairing tells the reader
how to read both. The prose says what happened; the song says what it meant and who is to be praised
or blamed. See [[Praise and Song]] and [[War]].
