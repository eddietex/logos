# A Land That Eats Up Its Inhabitants - Numbers 13.32, Leviticus 18 and 26, and Ezekiel 36

## Passages involved

- [[Defilement of Body and Land - Leviticus 15 and 18]] — Leviticus 18:25, 28; *the land vomited out
  her inhabitants*
- [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] — Leviticus 26:38; *The land of your enemies will
  eat you up*
- [[The Twelve Spies]] — Numbers 13:32; *a land that eats up its inhabitants*
- Ezekiel 36:3, 13–14 — *You are a devourer of men* *(WEB and WLC checked, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The land as an agent.** Leviticus 18:25 says *the land vomited out her inhabitants*, with the verb
*qîʾ* and the object *ʾet-yōšəbêhā* (WLC). Numbers 13:32 says *ʾereṣ ʾōkelet yôšəbêhā*, with *ʾākal*
and the same noun. The two are not in the same kind of speech: Yahweh speaks the first, and the
narrator tells us the second is a *dibbâ*. But the picture of a land acting on its people was
already in Israel's law.

**What the phrase means in the mouth of the spies** is unclear. It might mean poor land, plague, or
constant war. It also fights with the next clause, since the same land grows *men of great stature*.
The wiki keeps that as an inconsistency in the report and does not resolve it.

**Leviticus 26:38 is the threat.** *You will perish among the nations. The land of your enemies will
eat you up.* There the eating is exile, and it comes for disobedience. So the spies describe the
promised land in the words of a curse on a foreign one.

**Ezekiel 36 turns it around.** The mountains of Israel are addressed. Verse 3 says they are *the
evil report of the people*, *dibbat-ʿām*, the same noun as Numbers 13:32. Verse 13 says *Because
they say to you, "You are a devourer of men, and have been a bereaver of your nation"*, *ʾōkelet
ʾādām*, the same participle. Verse 14 answers: *therefore you shall devour men no more*. Both words
of Numbers 13:32 occur in one chapter, and Yahweh ends the slander by a promise. Whether Ezekiel
means to echo Numbers cannot be proved; the wiki records the shared pair.

**The answer at Numbers 14:9.** Joshua and Caleb reply *kî laḥmēnû hēm*, *they are bread for us*
(WLC). It is the eating image turned round, and Rahlfs has *κατάβρωμα*. Psalm 14:4 has the wicked
eating God's people *as they eat bread* (WEB checked), so the image is not tied to one side. See
[[The People Refuse the Land]].

## Significance

- The report borrowed a real idea, the land's power over those on it, and left out the reason
  Leviticus gave for it, which was sin.
- Ezekiel treats the slander of the land as something God answers.
