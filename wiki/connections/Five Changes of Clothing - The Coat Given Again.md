# Five Changes of Clothing - The Coat Given Again

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:3–4** — *"Israel loved Joseph more than all his children… and he made him a coat of
  many colors. His brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, and they hated
  him"* — [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]
- **Genesis 43:34** — *"Benjamin's portion was **five times** as much as any of theirs"* —
  [[The Second Journey]]
- **Genesis 45:22** — *"He gave each one of them changes of clothing, but to Benjamin he gave three
  hundred pieces of silver and **five** changes of clothing"* — [[Joseph Reveals Himself]]
- **Genesis 45:24** — *"See that you don't quarrel on the way"*

## Analysis

**Genesis begins with a father giving one son a garment the others do not get, and that gift is the
stated cause of everything that follows.** The narrator does not leave it to be inferred: *"his
brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, and they hated him"* (37:4).
**The coat is evidence of a preference, and the preference is the motive.**

**Eight chapters later the same asymmetry is performed twice more, by the son the first one was
performed on.**

| | who gives | what | to whom | to the others |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **37:3** | **[[Jacob]]** | *a coat of many colors* | **[[Joseph]]** | nothing |
| **43:34** | **[[Joseph]]** | **five** portions | **[[Benjamin]]** | one portion each |
| **45:22** | **[[Joseph]]** | **five** changes of clothing, **300** pieces of silver | **Benjamin** | one change each |

**The number five recurs and the wiki does not know why.** Genesis uses it for Benjamin twice and for
nothing else in the cycle — the fivefold portion at the table, the five changes of clothing at the
parting — **and offers no comment on either.** (It is also the fifth of the harvest Joseph takes at
41:34 and 47:24, which may be nothing.)

**What is not in doubt is the shape.** **Both of Joseph's gifts single out the only other son of
[[Rachel]], in front of the ten sons of the other three women**, and the second one uses the same
object as 37:3: ***śəmālôt***, **clothing.** **The man whose coat was stripped off him, dipped in
goat's blood and carried to his father as proof of death** (37:23, 31–33) **hands out garments, and
gives five to his full brother and one to each of the men who did it.**

**Three readings are available and the wiki holds them open.**

1. **Unreflective repetition.** Joseph loves Benjamin, gives accordingly, **and does not notice that he
   is doing to his brothers what was done to him.** The simplest reading, and the one that makes
   45:24's *"don't quarrel on the way"* an afterthought rather than a warning.
2. **A last test, or a last provocation.** **Ten men have just watched a brother receive five times
   what they received, twice in three chapters, and this time they cannot do anything about it** —
   they are in his country, fed at his expense, and travelling home in his wagons. **On this reading
   45:22 is the final re-run of 37:3 and 45:24 is the point of it.** The objection is that the
   disclosure has already happened; the testing apparatus of chapters 42–44 is dismantled by 45:4, and
   **Genesis gives no sign that anything is still being measured.**
3. **Restitution rather than favour.** **Three hundred pieces of silver is fifteen times the twenty
   Joseph was sold for** (37:28). On this reading the silver is aimed at the price and not at
   Benjamin, **and the generosity is a settling of an account nobody has mentioned.** The difficulty
   is that the money goes to the brother who was not sold.

**45:24 is the verse that keeps the question open.** ***ʾAl-tirgəzû badderek*** — *don't be agitated
on the way* — **carries either *don't be afraid* or *don't fall out with each other***, and the
translations split. **WEB reads *"See that you don't quarrel on the way"*, and if that is the sense,
then the last thing Joseph says to his brothers is a warning about the effect of the gifts he has just
given them** — **which means he knows exactly what he has done.**

## Significance

- **Genesis declines to resolve whether the cycle's founding sin is repaired or repeated in its
  reconciliation scene**, and the ambiguity is not an oversight: the book that stated the cause of the
  hatred in a narratorial aside at 37:4 **says nothing at all at 45:22.**
- **Favouritism is the one family trait Genesis never breaks.** [[Isaac]] preferred [[Esau]] and
  [[Rebekah]] preferred [[Jacob]] (25:28); Jacob preferred [[Rachel]] over [[Leah]] (29:30) and Joseph
  over the rest (37:3); **Joseph prefers Benjamin.** **Four generations, and the book records each one
  without ever condemning it in its own voice.** See [[Envy]] and [[Love]].
- **The clothing motif is the cycle's spine and this is its last turn.** A coat that is taken and used
  as false evidence (37:23, 31–33), a cloak that is left behind and used as false evidence
  (39:12–18), linen and a gold chain put on by a king (41:42), **and garments given away by the man
  who has been stripped twice.** See [[A Garment Used as Evidence - The Coat and the Cloak]] and
  [[Nakedness and Covering]].
- **If reading 2 is right, the brothers pass.** They arrive in Canaan together (45:25), deliver one
  report between them (45:26), and **Genesis records no quarrel** — **which is the same kind of
  silence as the nine words at 44:13.**
