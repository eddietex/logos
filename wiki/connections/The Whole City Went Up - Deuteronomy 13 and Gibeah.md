# The Whole City Went Up - Deuteronomy 13 and Gibeah

## Passages involved
- [[Do Not Follow Other Gods]] — Deuteronomy 13:12–18; the law of the Israelite city whose people *certain
  wicked fellows* have drawn away to other gods.
- [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]] — Judges 19:22; *the men of the city, certain wicked fellows*.
- [[The War Against Benjamin]] — Judges 20:3, 12–13, 37–40, 48; the inquiry, the demand, and the city
  struck and burned.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 13 ends with a law for one of Israel's own cities (stored WEB, English verse numbers):

> **13:12–13** If you shall hear about one of your cities, which Yahweh your God gives you to dwell there,
> that certain wicked fellows have gone out from among you, and have drawn away the inhabitants of their
> city, saying, “Let us go and serve other gods,” which you have not known;

> **13:15–16** you shall surely strike the inhabitants of that city with the edge of the sword, destroying
> it utterly, with all that is therein and its livestock, with the edge of the sword. You shall gather all
> its plunder into the middle of its street, and shall burn with fire the city, and all of its plunder, to
> Yahweh your God. It shall be a heap forever. It shall not be built again.

Judges 19–20 contains the law's pieces, most of them in its Hebrew (WLC; Deuteronomy's Hebrew verse numbers
run one ahead of the English):

| Deuteronomy 13 (Hebrew verse) | Gibeah |
|---|---|
| *ʾănāšîm bənê-ḇəliyyaʿal*, *certain wicked fellows* (13:14) | *ʾanšê ḇənê-ḇəliyyaʿal* (19:22); *hāʾănāšîm bənê ḇəliyyaʿal* (20:13) |
| a report heard of a city (13:13), and *you shall inquire … and ask diligently* (13:15) | *Tell us, how did this wickedness happen?* (20:3); *What wickedness is this?* (20:12) |
| *ûḇiʿartā hāraʿ*, *remove the evil* (13:6, the same chapter's formula) | *ûnəḇaʿărâ rāʿâ miyyiśrāʾēl*, *put away evil from Israel* (20:13) |
| *hakkēh takkeh … ləp̄î-ḥāreḇ*, *strike … with the edge of the sword* (13:16) | *wayyak ʾet-kol-hāʿîr ləp̄î-ḥāreḇ* (20:37); *wayyakkûm ləp̄î-ḥereḇ* (20:48) |
| *wəʾet-bəhemtāh*, *and its livestock* (13:16) | *ʿaḏ-bəhēmâ*, *and the livestock* (20:48) |
| *wəśārap̄tā ḇāʾēš ʾet-hāʿîr … kālîl*, *burn with fire the city … whole* (13:17) | *ʿālâ ḵəlîl-hāʿîr haššāmaymâ*, *the whole city went up* (20:40); *šilləḥû ḇāʾēš*, *set on fire* (20:48) |

**The word *kālîl*.** The WEB of Deuteronomy 13:16 reads *burn with fire the city, and all of its plunder, to
Yahweh your God*; the Hebrew has *kālîl* before *to Yahweh*, the word for a *whole* burnt offering
(Deuteronomy 33:10; 1 Samuel 7:9, where Samuel offers a lamb *ʿôlâ ḵālîl*). In the WLC files of Genesis
through 1 Samuel and Hosea searched, *kālîl* occurs at Exodus 28:31 and 39:22 (*all of blue*), Leviticus
6:15–16 (Hebrew; English 6:22–23, the priest's meal offering *wholly burned*), Numbers 4:6, Deuteronomy 13:17 and 33:10,
Judges 20:40, and 1 Samuel 7:9 (lemma 3632). Judges 20:40 is the only one in a narrative of a city. The
WEB renders it *the whole city went up in smoke to the sky*.

**Bənê ḇəliyyaʿal.** In those files the phrase *bənê-ḇəliyyaʿal* with *men* occurs in the Torah only at
Deuteronomy 13:14, and next at Judges 19:22 and 20:13 (lemma 1100; *bəliyyaʿal* alone also at
Deuteronomy 15:9). The phrase that opens the law's case is the phrase that names Gibeah's men.

**What differs.** Deuteronomy's city is guilty of other gods: *Let us go and serve other gods*. Nothing in
Judges 19–20 says Gibeah served other gods; its crime is rape and murder, called *lewdness and folly*
(20:6). The law tells Israel to investigate before it acts; in Judges the assembly takes an oath to act
(20:8–10) on the account of one witness, the Levite, before it sends to Benjamin (20:12). And the law is
for one city; verse 48 burns *all the cities which they found*. The law also says the city *shall not be
built again* (13:16, English); Judges says nothing of Gibeah being rebuilt, and Gibeah is Saul's town in
1 Samuel 10:26 (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored).

**What the text does not say.** The narrator does not cite Deuteronomy 13, and no one in the assembly does.
The verbal contacts are in the text; reading the war as the law of Deuteronomy 13 applied to Gibeah, or
misapplied, is the wiki's reading. Another reader may take *edge of the sword*, *livestock*, and *fire* as
the ordinary language of conquest and weigh only *bənê ḇəliyyaʿal* and *kālîl*.

## Significance

The wiki already set Deuteronomy 13 beside Laish: Dan *searched out*, struck, and burned a foreign town with
no recorded gods, and then rebuilt it for an image. See
[[Searched Out, Struck, and Burned - Deuteronomy 13 and Laish]]. The second story of the epilogue gives the
law's other half. Here the city is Israelite, the men are *bənê ḇəliyyaʿal*, the evil is put away *from
Israel*, and the city goes up *kālîl*. Read beside Deuteronomy 13, the two stories show the law of the
condemned city used against the wrong city in chapter 18 and, in chapter 20, against the right kind of city
for a different crime, and then extended to a whole tribe.

Deuteronomy 13 closes with its reason: Israel is to do this *that Yahweh may turn from the fierceness of his
anger … when you listen to Yahweh your God's voice … to do that which is right in Yahweh your God's eyes*
(13:17–18, English). The refrain around Judges 20 is *Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes*
(17:6; 21:25). See [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].
