# Why Have You Deceived Me - Laban, the Gibeonites, Michal, and the Medium

## Passages involved

- [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]] — **Genesis 29:25** — *In the morning, behold, it was Leah! He said to Laban, "What is
  this you have done to me? Didn't I serve with you for Rachel? Why then have you deceived me?"* Stored WEB.
- [[The Gibeonite Deception]] — **Joshua 9:22** — *Joshua called for them, and he spoke to them, saying, "Why have you
  deceived us, saying, 'We are very far from you,' when you live among us?"* Stored WEB.
- [[Michal Helps David Escape]] — **1 Samuel 19:17** — *Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me thus, and let my
  enemy go, so that he is escaped?"* Stored WEB.
- **1 Samuel 28:12** — *When the woman saw Samuel, she cried with a loud voice; and the woman spoke to Saul, saying,
  "Why have you deceived me? For you are Saul!"* Stored WEB; not yet ingested.
- **2 Samuel 19:27** (English 19:26) — Mephibosheth to David: *my lord, O king, my servant deceived me*, Ziba having
  gone without him. WLC only; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB stored.

## Analysis

**The verb.** *Rāmâ* in the *piel*, *to deceive, beguile* (morphhb lemma 7411 b). In the Hebrew files checked (Genesis
through 2 Samuel, and Ruth) the *piel* occurs in exactly these five verses. Morphhb also files Numbers 15:30's *bǝyāḏ
rāmâ*, *with a high hand*, under the same number, but that is a *qal* participle of a different word, and the wiki
leaves it out.

**The question.** Four of the five are one question: *lāmmâ rimmîṯānî*, *why have you deceived me?* (Genesis 29:25,
with *wǝ*-; 1 Samuel 19:17, with *kāḵâ*, *thus*; 28:12), or *lāmmâ rimmîṯem ʾōṯānû*, *why have you deceived us?*
(Joshua 9:22) (WLC). Each is asked to the deceiver's face, by the one deceived, after the deception has done its work.
Jacob has already spent the night with Leah; Joshua has already sworn to the Gibeonites; David is already out of the
window; Samuel has already come up. The question changes nothing. It does not undo the deception; it names it.
Mephibosheth's sentence is the exception: he is speaking of a third party, Ziba, to the king, in his own defense.

**Each deception has a covering.** In every one of the four questions, someone has been dressed or covered to look
like what they are not.

| | the deceiver | the deceived | the covering |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis 29 | Laban | Jacob | Leah brought *in the evening* (29:23); the text names no veil |
| Joshua 9 | the Gibeonites | Joshua and the princes | *old garments*, *old and patched shoes*, *dry and moldy* bread (9:4–5) |
| 1 Samuel 19 | Michal | Saul | the teraphim in the bed, *covered … with clothes*, *babbeḡeḏ* (19:13) |
| 1 Samuel 28 | Saul | the medium | *Saul disguised himself and put on other clothing*, *bǝḡāḏîm ʾăḥērîm* (28:8) |

(Stored WEB; WLC.) Genesis gives darkness, not a disguise; the other three name the garment.

**The reversals.** The question moves between the two sides of each story. Jacob, the deceiver of Isaac (Genesis 27),
is the one who asks it, and his questioner's answer is a custom about the younger and the firstborn (29:26). See
[[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]]. Joshua asks it, and Israel keeps the oath sworn under the
deception anyway (Joshua 9:19–20, 26). Saul asks it of his daughter in 1 Samuel 19. Nine chapters later the question is
asked *of him*, by a woman, in the dark, at the end of a disguise of his own making. In 1 Samuel the asker becomes the
asked.

## Significance

The thread is a small one, a single verb in five verses, but it holds a consistent narrative shape. Deception in
these books is rarely judged by the narrator. It is named by the person deceived, as a question, after it has
succeeded. The question is also how the deceived learns what is true. Jacob learns that the woman is Leah, Joshua
that the Gibeonites live *among us*, Saul that his enemy has escaped, and the medium that her client is the king. The
answers, where they come, are evasions: Laban's custom (Genesis 29:26), the Gibeonites' *we were very afraid for our
lives* (Joshua 9:24), Michal's invented threat (1 Samuel 19:17), Saul's *Don't be afraid* (28:13). See [[Deception]],
[[Michal]], [[Saul]], [[Jacob]], and [[Laban (person)]].
