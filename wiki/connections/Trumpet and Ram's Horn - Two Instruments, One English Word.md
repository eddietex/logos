## Analysis

### Joshua 6 now ingested

Joshua 6 is now ingested. The stored WEB explicitly specifies seven priests with seven trumpets
of rams’ horns (4, 6, 13), followed by a long blast and the people’s shout (5, 20). Numbers 10:2
specifies two beaten silver trumpets. The physical descriptions alone distinguish the instruments;
this addition makes no new claim about Hebrew wording. The march is not simply an instance of
using the two silver trumpets.


WEB mostly says *trumpet* for two Hebrew words, and the English reader cannot see which is meant. All locations
below are from WLC by morphhb tags, so they describe that tagging.

| | *ḥăṣōṣərâ* | *šôpār* |
|---|---|---|
| Where described | made of beaten silver, by command (Numbers 10:2) | *trumpets of rams' horns* in WEB at Joshua 6:4 |
| Occurrences | 29 in 27 verses | 72 in 63 verses |
| Torah | Numbers 10; 31:6 only | Exodus 19:16, 19; 20:18; Leviticus 25:9 only |
| Who blows | priests by statute | priests at Jericho; Ehud, Gideon, and Joab in war; watchmen; Yahweh himself |
| Character | regulated; camp, war, worship | wider; war, warning, coronation, and worship |

**Sinai is not Numbers 10.** No one is said to blow the sound at the mountain (Exodus 19:16, 19). It is *šôpār*, with
*yôbēl* at 19:13. The silver trumpets are made after that and put in human hands, so the wiki sees a contrast. Sinai's
sound frightens the camp, and Numbers 10's sound organizes it. Hebrews 12:19 recalls the first. See
[[Sinai and Zion - Exodus 19 and Hebrews 12]].

**Jericho is not Numbers 10 either.** WEB's *trumpets* there are *šôpār* and *yôbēl*. Priests blow them, as in Numbers
10:8, and a war in the land is under way, as in 10:9, but the instrument is the other one. Joshua 6 does not use
*ḥăṣōṣərâ* at all. After Numbers 31:6 the word next appears at 2 Kings 11:14, by the book order of the Hebrew canon as
printed in English. See [[The Priests' Trumpets - Numbers 10 from the Camp to the Second Temple]].

**Leviticus 23 has no instrument.** WEB's *a memorial of blowing of trumpets* is *ziḵrôn tərûʿâ* (23:24, WLC), a memorial
of a signal or shout. Numbers 29:1 is *yôm tərûʿâ*, also with no instrument. WEB's *trumpets* in both verses is an
interpretation, and so which instrument, if any single one, is unstated. Leviticus 25:9 does name the *šôpār*.
[[Trumpets and the Day of Atonement]] says the verse supplies no detail of the instrument, and the Hebrew agrees.

**Where the two meet.** Four verses name both: 1 Chronicles 15:28; 2 Chronicles 15:14; Psalm 98:6, *With trumpets and
sound of the ram's horn*; and Hosea 5:8, *Blow the cornet in Gibeah, and the trumpet in Ramah!* WEB does separate them in
those verses. So the two were different instruments, and the wiki does not treat them as synonyms.

**Same grammar of signal.** Numbers 10:5–7 separates *tāqaʿ* with an alarm from *tāqaʿ* without. Joel 2 does this on the
*šôpār*: 2:1, *Blow the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm*, against 2:15, *Blow the trumpet in Zion! Sanctify a fast.
Call a solemn assembly.* Ezekiel 33:3 and Amos 3:6 have the *šôpār* as the alarm of a city in danger.

**God as the player.** Zechariah 9:14 has *the Lord Yahweh will blow the trumpet*, and it is *šôpār* (WLC). Isaiah 27:13
has *a great trumpet* blown, passive, and the scattered *shall come* to worship in Jerusalem. That is a *šôpār* doing the
work of Numbers 10:3: a sound that gathers.

**The New Testament.** It uses one word in WEB, *trumpet*, and the wiki has not checked the Greek. Paul's *if the
trumpet gave an uncertain sound, who would prepare himself for war?* (1 Corinthians 14:8) depends on a signal the
hearer can tell apart, which is what Numbers 10:7 asks of Israel. He does not cite Numbers. Matthew 24:31 has a trumpet
that gathers, and 1 Corinthians 15:52 and 1 Thessalonians 4:16 have one that raises the dead. Revelation 8:2 gives
seven to angels. These are the assembly and alarm of Numbers 10 at the widest scale, but that is the wiki's comparison
and none of the texts cites it.

## Significance

The English word joins what the Hebrew keeps apart. Quoting WEB's *trumpet* as evidence about the instrument is
therefore unsafe in either direction. Numbers 10 cannot be used to explain Jericho, Sinai, or Psalm 81 on the
strength of the English, and those passages do not describe the silver trumpets.
