# His Life Is Bound Up in the Boy's Life — Nephesh Tied to Nephesh

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 44:30–31** — [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]; [[Judah]] of [[Jacob]] and
  [[Benjamin]]: *"since his life is bound up in the boy's life … it will happen, when he sees that
  the boy is no more, that he will die"* — ***wənapšô qəšûrâ bənapšô***
- **1 Samuel 18:1** — *"the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him
  as his own soul"* — ***niqšərâ bənepeš***
- **Genesis 37:34–35; 42:38; 44:29** — Jacob's three refusals to survive the loss of a son
- **Genesis 2:7; 9:4–5** — *nepeš* as the living creature and as the life that is in the blood
- **Deuteronomy 6:5; 1 Samuel 18:3; 20:17** — *nepeš* as the whole of what a person has to give
- **1 Samuel 25:29** — Abigail: *"bound in the bundle of life with Yahweh your God"* — the other
  *qāšar* idiom of a life tied to another

## Analysis

### The idiom occurs twice in the Hebrew Bible

***Qāšar***, *to bind, to tie, to knot*, is an ordinary verb — **it is what you do to a cord, a
scarlet thread on a twin's wrist (38:28), the words of the law on your hand (Deuteronomy 6:8), or a
conspiracy** (2 Kings 9:14 and a dozen others, where *qešer* is the noun for a plot). **Used with
*nepeš* on both sides of it, it occurs exactly twice.**

| | Genesis 44:30 | 1 Samuel 18:1 |
|---|---|---|
| **the bond** | ***napšô qəšûrâ bənapšô*** — *his life is tied to his life* | ***nepeš yəhônātān niqšərâ bənepeš dāwid*** |
| **between** | a father and his youngest son | **two men who have just met** |
| **who reports it** | **[[Judah]]**, to a foreign court, as evidence | the narrator, flatly, as a fact |
| **what it costs** | *"when he sees that the boy is no more … he will die"* | Jonathan's crown, and eventually his father's favour |
| **the verb's other life** | tying, and conspiracy | tying, and conspiracy |

***Nepeš*** **is not a soul in the later sense.** It is what Genesis 2:7 calls the man once God has
breathed into the dust — ***nepeš ḥayyâ***, *a living creature* — and what 9:4 locates in the blood.
**It is the life itself**, which is why the WEB renders it *life* in Genesis 44 and *soul* in 1 Samuel
18 and is right both times. **Two *nepeš* tied together is not a sentiment. It is a statement that one
of them cannot continue without the other**, and in both passages the text means it literally.

### Judah is not being poetic; he is stating a mechanism

**The sentence is the load-bearing step of a legal argument.**

> **30** Now therefore when I come to your servant my father, and the boy is not with us; since his
> life is bound up in the boy's life; **31** it will happen, when he sees that the boy is no more,
> that he will die.

**Judah is not asking for mercy on Benjamin's behalf.** He has conceded that the court can do what it
likes (44:16–18). **What he does instead is tell the judge that the sentence has a second defendant**,
three hundred miles away, **who has not been charged, is not present, and will not survive it.** The
*nepeš* clause is the premise; *"he will die"* is the conclusion; **and the whole of 44:32–34 follows
from it.**

**And he has the evidence.** [[Jacob]] has said it himself, three times, in the words Judah has just
quoted: *"I will go down to Sheol to my son mourning"* (37:35), *"you will bring down my gray hairs
with sorrow to Sheol"* (42:38), **and again in the quotation at 44:29.** **The father has been
announcing this outcome for twenty-two years and Judah is the first person to treat it as a fact about
the world rather than as grief talking.** See [[Sheol]] and [[Suffering]].

### Jonathan, and a bond that costs a kingdom

**1 Samuel 18:1 uses the idiom of a bond that is chosen rather than given**, and the chapter
immediately makes it expensive: Jonathan *"stripped himself of the robe that was on him, and gave it
to David, with his clothing, even to his sword, and to his bow, and to his belt"* (18:4). **The heir
hands the next king his own coat.**

**Genesis has the other version of that scene.** *"He made him a coat of many colors"* (37:3) — **a
father's gift to a favoured son, which the brothers strip off him and dip in blood** (37:23, 31).
**Both garments mark a succession and both are transferred**; the difference is that Jonathan takes
his off himself.

**The two passages therefore give the Old Testament's two pictures of a life tied to another life**:
**one involuntary and one chosen**, one in a father who cannot let go of a boy and one in a prince who
gives away his claim. **In both the bond is stated with the same verb, and in both the person who has
it ends up paying for it.** See [[Love]] and [[Brotherhood]].

### Abigail's bundle

**The only other place Scripture ties a life to something with this verb is 1 Samuel 25:29**, a few
chapters later and in the same David narrative: *"the soul of my lord will be bound in the bundle of
life with Yahweh your God."* ***Ṣərôr haḥayyîm*** — **the bundle, or pouch, of the living** — **is a
picture of valuables knotted into a cloth for safekeeping**, and it moves the idiom from human
attachment to divine custody.

**Set the three together and the progression is the whole distance the phrase can travel**: Jacob's
life tied to a boy's (44:30), Jonathan's tied to a friend's (18:1), **and David's tied into God's
purse** (25:29). **Genesis supplies the first and the most breakable.**

## Significance

**The idiom is the reason the Joseph cycle can end the way it does.**

**Judah's argument is not that Benjamin is innocent — he never claims it — and not that the penalty is
unjust.** It is that **the man who will actually serve the sentence is not in the room**, and the
proof is a bond nobody in an Egyptian court has any way to verify. **He asks the judge to take his
word for the state of an old man's *nepeš*.**

**The judge is the old man's son.** **He does not need to take anyone's word for it**, and the next
verse he speaks is *"I am Joseph! Does my father still live?"* (45:3). **The question is the same
question the *nepeš* clause was about.**

**What Genesis has set up, without saying so, is that the argument Judah cannot prove is the one thing
in the chapter his listener already knows.** **He is describing to Joseph the interior of a father
Joseph has not seen in twenty-two years, and getting it right** — and the description is what breaks
the disguise, not the offer of substitution that follows it. See
[[Mediation and Advocacy]], [[Providence]], and
[[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]].
