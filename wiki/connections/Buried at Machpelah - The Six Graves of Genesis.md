# Buried at Machpelah - The Six Graves of Genesis

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 23:1–20** — Abraham buys the field of Ephron for four hundred shekels and buries Sarah
- **Genesis 25:7–10** — Abraham is buried there by Isaac and Ishmael
- **Genesis 35:27–29** — Isaac dies at Mamre, Kiriath Arba, and is buried by Esau and Jacob
- **Genesis 49:29–32** — Jacob's burial instructions, which recite the deed and name the occupants
- **Genesis 50:12–14** — Jacob carried up from Egypt and buried in the field
- Genesis 35:19–20 — Rachel buried on the way to Ephrath, with a pillar and no purchase
- Genesis 33:19; Joshua 24:32 — Jacob's *other* purchase, at Shechem, and Joseph's bones
- Acts 7:16 — Stephen's version, which merges the two purchases

## Analysis

**Genesis buries six people in one field, and it makes a point of telling you it was paid for every
single time.**

| generation | buried | reference | who buries them |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sarah** | 23:19 | Abraham |
| 1 | **Abraham** | 25:9–10 | Isaac and Ishmael |
| 2 | **Isaac** | 35:29; 49:31 | Esau and Jacob |
| 2 | **Rebekah** | 49:31 | not narrated |
| 3 | **Leah** | 49:31 | not narrated |
| 3 | **Jacob** | 50:13 | his sons, from Egypt |

Four of the six are reported only in Jacob's dying speech, and two of the burials Genesis *does*
narrate are performed by **estranged brothers standing at the same grave**: Isaac and Ishmael at
25:9, Esau and Jacob at 35:29. Genesis says nothing about either reunion beyond the fact of it —
no reconciliation scene, no word exchanged, just two names and one verb. **A grave is the only place
in the book where those pairs appear together.**

### The deed is recited every time

This is the strand's real signature. Genesis does not simply record that the patriarchs are buried
at Hebron; it keeps restating **how the ground was acquired**.

- 23:17–18, 20 — the conveyance, stated twice, with the second statement placed *after* the burial so
  the chapter ends on the title
- 25:10 — *"the field which Abraham purchased of the children of Heth"*
- 49:30 — *"in the cave that is in the field of Machpelah, which is before Mamre, in the land of
  Canaan, which Abraham bought with the field from Ephron the Hittite as a burial place"*
- 49:32 — *"the field and the cave that is therein, which was purchased from the children of Heth."*
- 50:13 — *"the cave of the field of Machpelah, which Abraham bought with the field, as a possession
  for a burial site, from Ephron the Hittite, near Mamre"*

**Five recitations of one transaction, spread across twenty-seven chapters.** No other event in
Genesis is repeated in this way. The reason is legal rather than sentimental: the burials are the
demonstration that the family holds ground in Canaan by title, and every time the book returns to
the tomb it returns to the receipt. See [[Machpelah]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].

**Jacob's version raises the stakes.** He is dying in Egypt as the second man of the kingdom, with
every resource of Pharaoh's court available for a tomb; the Egyptians embalm him and mourn him
seventy days. And what he asks for is a cave in a foreign hill country his family left seventeen
years earlier, on the strength of a purchase made three generations back — and he makes Joseph swear
to it (47:29–31). The funeral procession that results, *"chariots and horsemen"* and *"a very great
company"* (50:9), is the largest thing in Genesis, and it exists to execute a deed. See
[[Burial and the Grave]].

### The two who are not there

**Rachel** dies on the road near Ephrath and is buried where she falls, with a pillar over the grave
(35:19–20) — no field, no purchase, and Jacob is careful to explain the fact to Joseph decades later
(48:7). **Joseph** dies in Egypt, is embalmed and put in a coffin (50:26), and his bones are carried
out at the exodus (Exodus 13:19) and buried at **Shechem**, *"in the parcel of ground which Jacob
bought of the sons of Hamor the father of Shechem for a hundred pieces of money"* (Joshua 24:32).

**So Genesis has two patriarchal land purchases, and the family is split between them**: Abraham's
field at Hebron, and Jacob's parcel at Shechem (33:19). Both are bought, both are paid for in
public, and both become graves.

### The discrepancy at Acts 7:16

Stephen, compressing the patriarchal history into a single speech, says that Jacob went down into
Egypt and died there, *"himself and our fathers"*, and that they were carried over to **Shechem** and
laid in a tomb that **Abraham** bought for a price in silver **from the sons of Hamor** (Acts
7:15–16). *(Wording follows the WEB; no New Testament text is held in this repository, so the
quotation is not verifiable here in the way the Genesis quotations are.)*

**Two purchases have become one.** Genesis has Abraham buy at Hebron from Ephron the Hittite, and
Jacob buy at Shechem from the sons of Hamor; Stephen attributes the Shechem purchase to Abraham. He
also has *"our fathers"* — the patriarchs generally — buried at Shechem, where Genesis 50:13 buries
Jacob at Machpelah.

The readings on offer:

1. **Compression in a summarising speech.** Stephen is running through two thousand years in a few
   minutes; conflating two purchases in one clause is the kind of thing a summary does, and Luke is
   reporting the speech, not endorsing its details.
2. **An independent tradition** about the burial of the twelve sons at Shechem — for which Joshua
   24:32 gives partial support (Joseph, at least, is buried there) and Josephus gives a version in
   which the brothers are buried at Hebron.
3. **A genuine harmonisation** — that Abraham had also bought at Shechem, where he built his first
   altar (12:6–7), and that Jacob's purchase at 33:19 was a re-purchase. Nothing in Genesis says so.

**The wiki flags the divergence and does not resolve it**, per the schema's rule on contradictions:
both claims are recorded with their sources, and neither page is quietly edited to agree with the
other. See [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]].

## Significance

**The land promise is fulfilled, in Genesis, exactly once, and it is fulfilled as a cemetery.**

That is not a pious flourish; it is what the book reports. Abraham enters Canaan at 12:5 and dies at
25:8, and the only ground he ever holds by title is a field he bought to bury his wife in. Isaac
never leaves the country and buys nothing. Jacob buys one parcel and then insists on being carried
back to his grandfather's. **Three generations, two purchases, and six graves — against a promise of
the whole country from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates.**

What the graves do is make the promise *addressed*. A family with no land has nothing to come back
to; a family with a tomb has a reason to carry a body four hundred miles. When Joseph, at the very
end of the book, tells his brothers *"God will surely visit you, and bring you up out of this land to
the land which he swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob"* and makes them swear to carry his bones
(50:24–25), he is doing what Jacob did and what Abraham started: **treating a purchase as a
down payment and a burial as a claim.**

Hebrews 11:13 reads the whole strand in one sentence: *"These all died in faith, not having received
the promises, but having seen them and embraced them from afar."* See
[[A Stranger and a Foreigner - The Sojourner's Confession]].
