# One Man Against the Philistines - Shamgar's Ox Goad and Samson's Jawbone

## Analysis

The two accounts share their shape: one named man, the Philistines, a tool that is not a weapon, and
a round number of the dead.

| | Shamgar (3:31) | Samson (15:15–16) |
|---|---|---|
| Tool | *malmad habbāqār*, *the goad of the cattle* | *ləḥî-ḥămôr ṭəriyyâ*, *a fresh jawbone of a donkey* |
| Verb | *wayyak*, *he struck* | *wayyak bāh*, *he struck with it*; *hikkêtî*, *I have struck* |
| Dead | *šēš-mēʾôt ʾîš*, six hundred men | *ʾelep ʾîš*, a thousand men |
| Saving | *wayyōšaʿ gam-hûʾ ʾet-yiśrāʾēl*, *he also saved Israel* | 13:5, *lᵉhôšîaʿ ʾet-yiśrāʾēl miyyad pəlištîm*, *to save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines* |

(Hebrew from the WLC.) The verb *nākâ*, *strike*, is the same in both. The verb *yāšaʿ*, *save*,
joined with *Israel*, links Shamgar's verse to the promise at Samson's birth. In 13:5 Samson will
only *begin* (*yāḥēl*, WLC) to save Israel from the Philistines. Shamgar's verse says he *saved* them,
with no such limit, though the Song of Deborah remembers his days as a time of empty roads (5:6).

## Significance

- **The book's Philistine war opens and closes with one man.** Judges 3:3 put *the five lords of the
  Philistines* first among the nations left to test Israel. The first and last deliverers to fight them
  in Judges are men who fight alone, with what comes to hand. No tribe is mustered in either verse.
- **What differs.** Samson's Spirit is named: *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him* (15:14, stored
  WEB) just before the jawbone. Shamgar's verse names neither Yahweh nor the Spirit. Samson speaks a
  victory couplet; Shamgar says nothing.
- **Not a claim of dependence.** The two passages share a pattern. The text does not say one is
  modelled on the other, and the wiki does not claim it.
- **Shammah, the same English opening.** David's mighty man is introduced in the same words as
  Shamgar: *After him was Shammah the son of Agee a Hararite. The Philistines had gathered together
  into a troop, where there was a plot of ground full of lentils; and the people fled from the
  Philistines. But he stood in the middle of the plot and defended it, and killed the Philistines; and
  Yahweh worked a great victory* (2 Samuel 23:11–12, WEB, checked at bible-api.com). In both, one man
  holds against the Philistines while no army is named. Whether the Hebrew openings match was not
  checked; the 2 Samuel WLC file was not read.
