# I Will Take You as My People - The Covenant Formula

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 17:7–8** — *"I will establish my covenant between me and you and your offspring after
  you throughout their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God to you and to your
  offspring after you. … I will be their God."* The first half of the formula, said to one man and
  his offspring — [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]]
- **Exodus 6:7** — *"and I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God; and you
  shall know that I am Yahweh your God, who brings you out from under the burdens of the
  Egyptians."* Both halves for the first time, said to a people — [[I Am Yahweh]]
- **Exodus 29:45–46** — *"I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. They shall
  know that I am Yahweh their God, who brought them out of the land of Egypt, that I might dwell among
  them: I am Yahweh their God."* The formula joined to the tabernacle — [[The Ordination of the Priests]]
- **Exodus 25:8** — *"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them."* The dwelling
  announced, without the formula, four chapters before 29:45 joins them —
  [[The Offering for the Sanctuary]]
- [[Blessings for Obedience]] — Leviticus 26:12; *"I will walk among you, and will be your God, and
  you will be my people"*. *Wəhāyîtî lākem lēʾlōhîm* matches Exodus 6:7 (MAM), and 26:13 likewise
  follows with the bringing out of Egypt.
- **Jeremiah 7:23; 11:4; 24:7; 30:22; 31:33** — the formula as the content of the old covenant's
  demand and of the new covenant's promise *(not yet ingested; paraphrased)*
- **Ezekiel 11:20; 36:28; 37:27** — the formula after the gift of a new heart and spirit, and over
  God's dwelling *(not yet ingested; paraphrased)*
- **2 Corinthians 6:16; Hebrews 8:10; Revelation 21:3** — the formula in the New Testament: the
  church as God's temple, Jeremiah's new covenant quoted, and God dwelling with people in the new
  creation *(not yet ingested; paraphrased)*

## Analysis

**The formula has two halves, and Scripture gives them separately before it gives them together.**
Genesis 17 has only God's half, *"to be a God to you"*. Its Hebrew at 17:8, *wəhāyîtî lāhem
lēʾlōhîm*, is the clause of Exodus 6:7 with a different pronoun: *wəhāyîtî lākem lēʾlōhîm*. Exodus
6:7 adds the people's half first, *"I will take you to me for a people"*, *wəlāqaḥtî ʾetkem lî
ləʿām*. (Both checked in the Westminster Leningrad Codex.) **A family promise becomes a national one
at the moment the family has become a nation in slavery.**

**In Exodus 6 the formula is surrounded by acts.** The verbs around it (*bring out*, *rescue*,
*redeem*, then *bring in*, *give*) describe a journey. The formula sits in the middle of the seven,
between the leaving and the arriving. **The relationship is not the reward at the end of the exodus.
It is what happens in the middle of it**, before the land and before Sinai. The verb *lāqaḥ*,
*take*, is the ordinary Hebrew verb for taking a wife (Genesis 4:19; 11:29; 24:67). Some readers
hear a marriage in 6:7 for that reason. Exodus does not draw the image; the prophets later do, at
length.

**The formula is bound to knowledge, and later to presence.** Exodus 6:7 continues *"and you shall
know that I am Yahweh your God, who brings you out"*. Exodus 29:45–46 repeats the same sentence
almost word for word and adds the purpose: *"that I might dwell among them"*. **The exodus happened
so that God could live with the people he brought out.** That is the thread the formula carries from
here to its last occurrence in Revelation 21:3, where God's dwelling is with people. The first half of that purpose
is announced at 25:8, *"that I may dwell among them"* (*wəšākantî bətôkām*, MAM), and 29:45 repeats
the same verb with the formula attached. See
[[That I May Dwell Among Them - Tabernacle, Temple, Flesh, and New Creation]].

**In the prophets it becomes both the measure of failure and the promise of renewal.** As the
references above show (paraphrased; not yet ingested), Jeremiah gives it as what God commanded at
the exodus and Israel did not keep, and then as the content of the new covenant written on the
heart. Ezekiel attaches it to the new heart and spirit God will give. Hebrews quotes Jeremiah's new
covenant passage. Paul applies the formula to the church as God's temple. The wiki will check each
wording when its book is ingested.

## Significance

- **It is the shortest statement of what a covenant is in Scripture**: a mutual belonging, said
  entirely by one party. In Exodus 6 God says both halves. Israel is not asked to agree until
  Sinai.
- **It arrives at a moment of failure.** It is spoken just before *"they didn't listen to Moses for
  anguish of spirit"* (6:9). The people who will be *taken* cannot yet hear that they are being
  taken.
- **It ties the exodus to the tabernacle.** Exodus 6:7 and 29:45–46 are the same sentence at two ends
  of the book's story, the first about bringing out and the second about dwelling. See [[Covenant]]
  and [[Call and Election]].
