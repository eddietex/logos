## Analysis

**Exodus 25 twice says that Moses was shown a design without describing the form of the showing.**
At 25:9 God says *"all that I show you"* (*marʾeh*, a hiphil participle: *I am showing*). At 25:40 it is *"which you are being shown on
the mountain"* (*morʾeh*, hophal participle; both MAM). The noun is ***tabnît***, from *bānâ*, *to
build*: a *plan*, *model*, or *form*. **The text does not say whether Moses saw a heavenly sanctuary, a
model, a vision, or a set of instructions.** It says the design came from God and the work must follow
it.

**The Greek gives the New Testament its word.** The Septuagint translates *tabnît* at 25:40 as
***typos*** — *hora poiēseis kata ton typon ton dedeigmenon soi en tō orei* (Rahlfs). At 25:9 it had
*paradeigma*. ***Typos*** is a mark, an impression, a model, or a form. **Both New Testament texts use
it.**

**Stephen uses it as history.** In Acts 7:44 the tabernacle was made *kata ton typon hon heōrakei*,
*according to the pattern that he had seen* (SBLGNT). In context Stephen is answering the charge that he
spoke against *"this holy place"* (Acts 6:13). He moves from the tent built to a pattern, to Solomon's
house, to *"the Most High doesn't dwell in temples made with hands"* (7:48). The pattern shows the
tabernacle was God's idea; it does not make any building God's address.

**Hebrews uses it as an argument.** It quotes 25:40 nearly as the Septuagint has it, with two
differences (SBLGNT against Rahlfs):

| | Septuagint, Exodus 25:40 | Hebrews 8:5 |
|---|---|---|
| object | — | ***panta***, *everything* |
| participle | *dedeigmenon*, perfect | *deichthenta*, aorist |
| rest | *hora poiēseis kata ton typon … soi en tō orei* | the same |

The word *panta* is the bigger change: *everything* must follow the pattern. Hebrews then draws the
conclusion the quotation is cited for. The earthly priests *"serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly
things"* (*hypodeigmati kai skia*, SBLGNT). **On Hebrews' reading the pattern is a heavenly reality, and
the tabernacle a copy of it.** Hebrews 9:11, 24 speak of *"the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not
made with hands"* and of *"holy places made with hands, which are representations of the true"*.

**What Exodus supports.** That the design came from God, that it was shown and not only told, and that
it was shown *"on the mountain"*. **What Exodus does not say.** That there is a sanctuary in heaven of
which the tent is a copy. Hebrews may be drawing out what *"shown"* implies, or reading Exodus through a
later picture. Readers divide, and the wiki records both without deciding.

**Where the *panta* may come from.** Exodus 25:9 already has it. The Hebrew reads *kəkōl ʾăšer ʾănî
marʾeh*, *according to all that I show you* (MAM). The Septuagint reads *kata panta hosa egō soi
deiknyō en tō orei*, and adds *on the mountain* there too (Rahlfs). Hebrews' wording may combine 25:9
and 25:40, or follow a Greek text of 25:40 that already had *panta*. Other Greek witnesses were not
checked in this pass. At 26:30 the Septuagint has a third word, *eidos*, *form* (Rahlfs).

**Hebrews has the opposite word too.** The holy places *"made with hands"* are ***antitypa*** *of the
true* (Hebrews 9:24, SBLGNT; the WEB's *"representations"*). The *typos* was shown on the mountain, and
the tent built from it is its *antitypos*.

**The requirement governs erection too.** Exodus 26:30 moves from individual furnishings to
setting up the tabernacle according to what Moses was shown. The dimensions and joins of the
preceding instructions serve an assembled whole, and 26:33–35 also fixes the furniture's positions.
See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]]. This does not settle what form the mountain showing took.

**The altar carries the same requirement.** Exodus 27:8 directs that its hollow planks be
made as shown on the mountain, extending the repeated design requirement to the outer altar.
The verse does not describe the form of the showing or the altar's internal fire surface.
See [[The Altar and the Court]]. This observation is based on the WEB, without a new claim
about the verse's exact Hebrew or Greek vocabulary.

### The enclosure made — Exodus 36:8–38

The making in Exodus 36:8–38 closely reproduces the enclosure's specified counts, measurements,
and materials. It demonstrates practical conformity without repeating 26:30's description of
the showing on the mountain or identifying what Moses saw. The entrance-pillar descriptions
also require care: 26:37 commands gold overlay on the pillars; 36:38 specifies their capitals
and fillets without saying that these alone received gold. The accounts correspond closely
but are not identical transcripts. Neither the correspondence nor this difference settles
Hebrews' interpretation of the pattern as heavenly reality.

See [[The Tabernacle Built]]. Source: Exodus 26 and 36:8–38 checked in local raw WEB,
2026-10-24; later Exodus references checked in the same source.

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The furnishings now made in Exodus 37 correspond closely to the specifications in Exodus
25 and 30. The making report does not repeat the command of 25:40 or explain what form the
mountain showing took. The material correspondence demonstrates the design's implementation;
it supplies no independent claim that the makers saw a heavenly sanctuary. The later reading
in Hebrews therefore remains an interpretation at its own point in the canon, not something
newly stated by the manufacture report.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38

Exodus 38:1–7 and 9–20 closely enact the altar and court design of 27:1–19. The basin’s making in
38:8 answers 30:18 and adds the mirror source. Verse 38:22 expressly credits compliance with what
Yahweh commanded Moses. None of these statements describes the mountain showing or says the workers
saw a heavenly prototype. The later canonical interpretation remains distinct from this
manufacturing report.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]]. Source: the cited Exodus passages
checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

The garments in 39:1–31 closely follow chapter 28 and repeatedly affirm obedience to
Yahweh’s command to Moses. That correspondence includes material details but also differences
of description, tracked in [[The Garments Commanded and Made - Exodus 28 and 39]]. The unit
neither describes what Moses saw on the mountain nor says that the garment makers saw a heavenly
prototype. The later interpretation in Hebrews remains distinct from the manufacturing report.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

Moses sees that the finished work follows Yahweh’s command (39:43). The recipient of
the design thus assesses the result, turning the instruction into a narrated verdict of
compliance. This verse gives neither an inspection procedure nor another description of what
was shown on the mountain. It does not independently establish the later interpretation of a
heavenly prototype.

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

## Significance

- **It is the main New Testament use of the tabernacle instructions.** Hebrews builds its whole argument
  about priesthood and sanctuary (8–10) on the claim that the tent was a copy.
- **It sets Moses as a man who saw.** He is shown the pattern, as he is the one who comes near (Exodus
  24:2) and sees (33:18–23; [[Show Me Your Glory]]). See [[Moses]] and
  [[They Saw God - Exodus 24, Exodus 33, and John 1.18]].
- **It makes the obedience formula precise.** Everything is made *"after their pattern"*; at the end of the
  book everything is made *"as Yahweh commanded"*. See
  [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].
