# Thunders in Harvest - Exodus 9 and 1 Samuel 12

## Analysis

**The word.** *Qōlōṯ*, the plural of *qôl*, *voice, sound*, means *thunders* (lemma 6963). In the WLC files of
Genesis through 2 Samuel the plural occurs only in Exodus 9 (23, 28, 29, 33, 34), at Sinai (Exodus 19:16; 20:18),
and at 1 Samuel 12:17–18. (1 Samuel 7:10 uses the singular, *bəqôl-gāḏôl*, with the verb *rāʿam*, WLC.)

**The same verb and the same pair.** *Wayhwh nāṯan qōlōṯ ûḇārāḏ*, *Yahweh sent thunder, hail* (Exodus 9:23, WLC).
*Wayyittēn YHWH qōlōṯ ûmāṭār*, *Yahweh sent thunder and rain* (1 Samuel 12:18, WLC). *Nāṯan* with *qōlōṯ* is the
idiom in both, and *māṭār*, *rain*, is the third element of Exodus 9:33–34, where *the thunders and hail ceased,
and the rain was not poured on the earth*.

**The people ask the prophet to pray.** Pharaoh says *Pray to Yahweh*, *haʿtîrû* (Exodus 9:28, WLC); Israel says
*Pray for your servants*, *hiṯpallēl* (1 Samuel 12:19, WLC). The verbs differ. In both, the thunder is understood
as judgment, the one who called it is asked to pray it away, and the asker confesses: Pharaoh *I have sinned this
time* (Exodus 9:27), Israel *we have added to all our sins this evil* (1 Samuel 12:19) (stored WEB).

**Ceasing.** In Exodus the thunders *cease*, *ḥāḏal* (9:29, 33, 34, WLC). In 1 Samuel the word is Samuel's, *far
be it from me … in ceasing to pray for you*, *mēḥăḏōl ləhiṯpallēl* (12:23, WLC). What ceases in Exodus is the
thunder; what Samuel will not let cease is the prayer.

**The outcome.** Pharaoh, once the thunder stops, *sinned yet more, and hardened his heart* (Exodus 9:34). Moses
had told him *I know that you don't yet fear Yahweh God* (9:30). Israel *greatly feared Yahweh and Samuel*
(12:18), and Samuel answers *Don't be afraid* and *Only fear Yahweh* (12:20, 24) (stored WEB). At Sinai the
thunders made the people tremble and stand far off, and Moses said *Don't be afraid … that his fear may be before
you, that you won't sin* (Exodus 20:20, stored WEB).

### What the page does not claim

It does not claim that 1 Samuel 12 was written with Exodus 9 in view. The shared noun is rare in these books and
the verb is the same; the narrator does not cite Exodus. Rain in wheat harvest is usually explained as out of
season, which is what makes it a sign (recalled, not checked; the text says only *Isn't it wheat harvest
today?*).

## Significance

- **Samuel is shown as a prophet like Moses.** He brings down thunder by calling, and is asked to pray it off. The
  people fear *Yahweh and Samuel* as at the sea they believed *in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses* (Exodus 14:31,
  stored WEB). See [[Samuel]], [[Moses]], and [[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].
- **The sign judges Israel as the plague judged Egypt**, but with the opposite end: Pharaoh hardens, Israel
  confesses and is told it will not be forsaken (12:22). See [[Signs and Wonders]], [[The Fear of God]], and
  [[Hardening of the Heart]].
- **Thunder is Samuel's sign.** It routed the Philistines at Mizpah (7:10), as Hannah sang *He will thunder against
  them in the sky* (2:10, stored WEB). At Mizpah it fought for Israel; here it falls on Israel. See
  [[He Will Thunder Against Them - Hannah's Song and Mizpah]].
