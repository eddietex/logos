# Blessing on Gerizim and Curse on Ebal - Deuteronomy 11, 27, and Joshua 8

## Passages involved

- [[Set These Words Before You]] — Deuteronomy 11:29–30; *"you shall set the blessing on Mount
  Gerizim, and the curse on Mount Ebal"*, with the two mountains located by Gilgal and the oaks of
  Moreh.
- **Deuteronomy 27:1–26** *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)* — plastered stones with the law written
  on them and an altar of uncut stones on Ebal (2–8); six tribes on Gerizim *"to bless the people"*,
  six on Ebal *"for the curse"* (12–13); the Levites pronounce twelve curses, each answered
  *"Amen"* (14–26).
- **Joshua 8:30–35** *(verified from bible-api.com, WEB; not yet ingested)* — Joshua builds the
  altar on Ebal, and all Israel stands *"half of them in front of Mount Gerizim, and half of them in
  front of Mount Ebal, as Moses the servant of Yahweh had commanded"* (33). He reads *"all the words
  of the law, the blessing and the curse"* (34).

## Analysis

### One command, three stages

Deuteronomy 11:29 is the bare command: a blessing on one mountain, a curse on the other, once Israel
is in the land. It says nothing about who stands where, what is said, or what is built. Deuteronomy
27 supplies all three, and Joshua 8 reports it done, explicitly *"as Moses … had commanded"*
(Joshua 8:33). The thread is one of the clearest command-and-fulfilment chains between the Torah
and Joshua.

### What the stored texts actually contain

The three passages are not uniform, and the differences are worth keeping in view.

- **Only curses are spelled out in Deuteronomy 27.** The six tribes on Gerizim stand *"to bless the
  people"* (27:12), but the liturgy that follows is twelve curses with the people's *"Amen"*
  (27:15–26). No matching blessing formulas appear in the chapter. Chapter 28 opens with blessings,
  but it is not presented as the Gerizim liturgy. The wiki records the asymmetry and does not
  explain it.
- **The altar is on the mountain of the curse.** Deuteronomy 27:4–7 puts the written stones, the
  altar, the burnt offerings, and the peace offerings on Ebal, and Joshua 8:30 builds the altar
  there. See [[An Altar of Uncut Stones - Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 27, and Joshua 8]].
- **The people stand differently.** Deuteronomy 27:12–13 puts the tribes *on* the mountains. Joshua
  8:33 has the people *"in front of"* each mountain, on either side of the ark. The wiki takes this
  as a difference of description and does not argue that one contradicts the other.
- **Who reads changes.** In Deuteronomy 27:14 the Levites speak the curses. In Joshua 8:34 Joshua
  reads the whole law, *"the blessing and the curse"*.

### The place

Deuteronomy 11:30 locates the mountains *"beside the oaks of Moreh"*, the Shechem landmark where
Abram built his first altar (Genesis 12:6–7). The first covenant ceremony Israel is commanded to
perform in the land is therefore set where the land was first promised. See
[[The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem]] and [[Shechem (city)]].

The phrase *"near Gilgal"* in the same verse is a difficulty. Joshua's Gilgal is *"on the east
border of Jericho"* (Joshua 4:19, verified WEB), well south of the mountains. The wiki leaves the
question open; see [[Gilgal]].

## Significance

Deuteronomy 11 sets a blessing and a curse before Israel as words (26–28) and then gives them a
place (29). The choice is to be staged on the actual ground of the land, between two visible
mountains. Joshua 8 shows the generation that crossed doing exactly that, with foreigners, women,
and little ones present (Joshua 8:35). The ingest of Deuteronomy 27 and of Joshua will revisit this
page.
