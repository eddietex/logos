# Before the Sun Goes Down - The Pledged Garment and the Day's Wage

## Passages involved

- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:26–27; *"If you take your neighbor's garment as
  collateral, you shall restore it to him before the sun goes down, for that is his only covering … What
  would he sleep in? It will happen, when he cries to me, that I will hear, for I am gracious."*
- [[Be Holy and Love Your Neighbor]] — Leviticus 19:13; *"You shall not oppress your neighbor, nor rob
  him. The wages of a hired servant shall not remain with you all night until the morning."*
- [[Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning]] — Deuteronomy 24:6, 10–15, 17; the millstone, the pledge
  received at the door and returned *"when the sun goes down"*, the wage paid *"in his day"*, and the
  widow's clothing never taken.
- [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] and [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]] — Job 22:6; 24:3, 9; pledges
  taken from the poor, the fatherless, and the widow.
- Amos 2:8 *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"they lay themselves down beside every
  altar on clothes taken in pledge"*.
- Jeremiah 22:13 *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"Woe to him … who uses his
  neighbor's service without wages, and doesn't give him his hire"*.
- Malachi 3:5 *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"against those who oppress the
  hireling in his wages, the widow, and the fatherless, and who deprive the foreigner of justice"*.
- Matthew 20:8 *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"When evening had come, the lord of
  the vineyard said to his manager, 'Call the laborers and pay them their wages'"*.
- James 5:4 *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"the wages of the laborers who mowed
  your fields, which you have kept back by fraud, cry out, and the cries of those who reaped have entered
  into the ears of the Lord of Armies"*.

## Analysis

**The deadline.** Three laws give a creditor or employer until the end of a day. Exodus 22:26 returns the
pledged garment *"before the sun goes down"*. Leviticus 19:13 forbids keeping the hired man's wage *"all
night until the morning"*. Deuteronomy 24 puts both under the sunset: the poor man's pledge is returned
*"when the sun goes down"* (13), and the wage is paid *"in his day … neither shall the sun go down on it"*
(15). In the Hebrew, Exodus 22:25 (WEB 22:26) has *ʿad bōʾ haššemeš*, *until the coming of the
sun*, and Deuteronomy 24:13 *kǝbōʾ haššemeš*, *at the coming of the sun*, and Deuteronomy 24:15 *lōʾ-tābôʾ ʿālāyw haššemeš*, *the sun shall
not come upon it* (WLC). Leviticus sets the limit at morning, Deuteronomy at sunset; the texts give no
reason for the difference.

**The reason given.** Each law names what the poor person lacks. Exodus 22:27: *"that is his only
covering … What would he sleep in?"* Deuteronomy 24:13: *"that he may sleep in his garment"*. Deuteronomy
24:15: *"for he is poor, and sets his heart on it"*. Deuteronomy 24:6 gives the same kind of reason for
the millstone: *"he takes a life in pledge"*.

**The cry.** Exodus 22:27 and Deuteronomy 24:15 both expect the poor man to cry to God. In Exodus Yahweh
answers: *"I will hear, for I am gracious."* In Deuteronomy the result is *"it be sin to you"*, the
phrase used also for the loan refused before the year of release (15:9), where the poor brother likewise
*"cry to Yahweh against you"*. James 5:4 takes up the cry: the withheld wages *"cry out"*, and the
reapers' cries *"have entered into the ears of the Lord of Armies"*. Whether James alludes to Deuteronomy
24:15 in particular, or to the Torah's wage laws in general, the text of James does not say; the shared
elements are the withheld wage, the cry, and the Lord who hears.

**The prophets' charge.** Amos 2:8 names the abuse Exodus 22 and Deuteronomy 24 forbid: garments taken in
pledge and not returned, slept on beside altars. Jeremiah 22:13 pronounces woe on the king who does not
pay his workers. Malachi 3:5 lists, in one verse, the hired man's wage, the widow, the fatherless, and the
foreigner deprived of justice: the same four as Deuteronomy 24:14–17, though not in the same order.
Malachi does not cite Deuteronomy, and whether it draws on it is not settled here.

**The evening payment.** In Matthew 20:8 the laborers are paid *"when evening had come"*. The parable does
not cite a law, and its point is the equal wage, not the hour. That the evening payment matches
Deuteronomy 24:15 is an observation, not a claim of the text.

## Significance

The pledge and the wage are the two places where a poor person's livelihood passes into another's hand,
and the law sets a time limit on both. The Torah treats one night without a coat or a day's pay as harm
enough to bring God's hearing. The prophets and James use the same laws as charges. See
[[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], [[Work]], and [[Wealth and Possessions]]; for the related loan laws,
[[Lending Without Interest - Exodus 22.25 and the Poor Brother]] and
[[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]].
