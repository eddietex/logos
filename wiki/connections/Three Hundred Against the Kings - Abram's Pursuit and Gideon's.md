# Three Hundred Against the Kings - Abram's Pursuit and Gideon's

## Passages involved
- [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] — Genesis 14:14–24. *He led out his trained men, born in his
  house, three hundred and eighteen, and pursued as far as Dan. He divided himself against them by night*
  (14:14–15); *Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine* (14:18) (stored WEB).
- [[Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel]] — Judges 8:4–21. *The three hundred men who were with him, faint, yet
  pursuing* (8:4); *Please give loaves of bread to the people who follow me* (8:5) (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**The shape.** In both stories a man of Israel's line, with about three hundred men, pursues foreign
kings who have struck his kin, beyond the edge of the land, and defeats them. Abram's kings carried off
Lot (Genesis 14:12); Gideon's kings killed his brothers at Tabor (Judges 8:18–19). Abram strikes *by
night* (14:15). Gideon divided his men *into three companies* by night in 7:16, and in 8:11 strikes a camp
that *was secure*.

**Bread.** Coming back, Abram is met by Melchizedek, who *brought out bread and wine* unasked, and by the
king of Sodom (14:17–18). Gideon, still pursuing, asks two towns of his own people for *loaves of bread*
and is refused (8:5–8). The bread given by a foreign king and the bread refused by Israelite towns are the
two ends of the comparison. [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] already recorded Judges 8:4–9 as *the
inverse* of 14:18.

**The spoil.** Abram refuses the king of Sodom's goods: *I will not take a thread nor a sandal strap nor
anything that is yours* (14:23). Gideon takes the crescents from the kings' camels (8:21), and then asks
for the gold earrings of the spoil and makes an ephod (8:24–27, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The wiki
will finish this part of the comparison when 8:22–35 is ingested.

**The numbers.** Three hundred and eighteen against three hundred. The wiki notes the closeness and reads
nothing into the numbers; Judges does not mention Abram.

## Significance

The comparison sets two victories over kings side by side and shows the difference in what follows them:
blessing and refused spoil for Abram, refused bread, punishment, and taken gold for Gideon. See
[[Hospitality]], [[War]], [[Wealth and Possessions]], [[Abraham]], and [[Gideon]].
