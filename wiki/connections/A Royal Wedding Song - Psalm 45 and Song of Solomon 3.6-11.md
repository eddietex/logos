# A Royal Wedding Song - Psalm 45 and Song of Solomon 3.6-11

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 3:6–11** — [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]]: a king's carriage comes up with an
  armed escort, and the daughters of Zion are told to see him crowned *"in the day of his weddings"*.
- **Psalm 45** *(not yet fetched or ingested)* — a song addressed to a king on his wedding to a
  princess.

Psalm 45 WEB was checked at bible-api.com (`translation=web`) on 2026-10-28. Psalm 45 has a
superscription that the bible-api WEB text does not include, so English verse numbers here are the
WEB's, and Hebrew verse numbers are one higher. Hebrew of both read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to
the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

## Analysis

**Two royal wedding poems.** Psalm 45 is the psalm most plainly written for a royal wedding. The Song's
3:6–11 is the only place the Song names a wedding. The two share a set of images.

| | Song of Solomon 3:6–11 | Psalm 45 |
|---|---|---|
| The sword | *"Every man has his sword on his thigh"* (3:8) | *"Strap your sword on your thigh, mighty one"* (45:3) |
| Mighty men | *"Sixty mighty men"*, *gibbōrîm* (3:7) | *"mighty one"*, *gibbôr* (45:3; Hebrew 45:4) |
| Myrrh | *"perfumed with myrrh and frankincense"* (3:6) | *"All your garments smell like myrrh, aloes, and cassia"* (45:8) |
| Gold | *"its bottom of gold"* (3:10) | *"the queen stands in gold of Ophir"* (45:9) |
| Daughters | *"daughters of Jerusalem"*, *"daughters of Zion"* (3:10–11) | *"Kings' daughters"*, *"daughter"*, *"The daughter of Tyre"* (45:9–12) |
| Gladness | *"the day of the gladness of his heart"*, *śimḥaṯ* (3:11) | *"With gladness and rejoicing they shall be led"*, *biśmāḥōṯ* (45:15; Hebrew 45:16) |
| Procession | the carriage comes up *"from the wilderness"* (3:6) | *"She shall be led to the king … They shall enter into the king's palace"* (45:14–15) |

The sword on the thigh is the closest verbal link. The Song's *ḥarbô ʿal-yǝrēḵô*, *his sword on his
thigh* (3:8), and the psalm's *ḥăḡôr-ḥarbǝḵā ʿal-yārēḵ*, *gird your sword on the thigh* (Hebrew
45:4), use the same nouns (Sefaria).

**What is different.** Psalm 45 has both partners: it praises the king (45:2–9) and then speaks to
the bride, *"Listen, daughter … Forget your own people, and also your father's house"* (45:10), and
describes her procession to the palace (45:13–15). The Song's procession has no bride in view at
all. The psalm also gives the king a divine blessing and an everlasting throne (45:2, 6), which the
Song does not. The Song's king is named; the psalm's is not.

**"A song of loves."** The psalm's Hebrew heading calls it *šîr yǝḏîḏōṯ* (Hebrew 45:1; Sefaria),
usually rendered *a song of loves*. *Yǝḏîḏōṯ* comes from the same root as *Jedidiah*, the name given
to Solomon in 2 Samuel 12:25 (see [[Solomon]]). Readers sometimes connect the two. The psalm does not
name its king, and the wiki does not identify him with Solomon.

## Significance

The two poems show that Israel had a form for celebrating a king's wedding, with swords, perfume,
gold, the women of the court, and a procession made with gladness. The Song uses that form once,
inside a book that is otherwise about two lovers who are never plainly royal. Psalm 45 has been read
messianically, in Hebrews 1:8–9 (not verified here), and the Song has been read allegorically, but
this page records only the shared form. It will be revised when Psalm 45 is ingested. See
[[Myrrh, Aloes, and Cinnamon - Proverbs 7.17, Song of Solomon 4.14, and Psalm 45.8]].
