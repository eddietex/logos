# Blasphemy Forbidden and Judged - Exodus 22 and Leviticus 24

## Passages involved

- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:28.
- [[The Blasphemer and One Law for All]] — Leviticus 24:10–23.

## Analysis

Exodus 22:28 prohibits blaspheming God and cursing a ruler, without assigning
a penalty in that verse. Leviticus 24 narrates an offender’s custody while
Yahweh’s will is awaited, then gives a capital sentence and a general rule
covering foreigner and native-born (12–16). The later case supplies a judicial
application rather than merely another abstract prohibition.

The pending question is not identified. We cannot decide from verse 12 alone
whether the community lacked the penalty, its procedure, or a ruling on this
particular defendant. Nor does the pairing establish that every offense against
a human ruler has the blasphemy penalty: the Leviticus ruling names Yahweh.

Sources: stored WEB Exodus 22:28 and Leviticus 24:10–23; paraphrases throughout.
No claim about exact Hebrew wording is needed for this comparison.

## Significance

The prohibition and the narrative together distinguish knowing a command from
receiving judgment in a particular case. See [[The Names of God]],
[[Language and Speech]], and [[Judgment]].
