# Aaron on Hor and Moses on Nebo - Numbers 20 and Deuteronomy 34

## Passages involved
- [[The Death of Aaron]] — Numbers 20:22–29.
- [[Moses Commanded to Ascend Nebo]] — Deuteronomy 32:48–52, which names Aaron's death as the
  precedent.
- [[The Death of Moses]] — Deuteronomy 34:1–12.
- [[Meribah's Sentence and Aaron's Death]] — the shared offense.

## Analysis

The two brothers excluded at Meribah die the same way. Each goes up a mountain at Yahweh's word,
dies there, and is mourned by Israel for thirty days. Each leaves an appointed successor in place.

| | Aaron — Numbers 20 | Moses — Deuteronomy 34 |
|---|---|---|
| Mountain | Mount Hor, by Edom (22–23) | Mount Nebo, top of Pisgah, opposite Jericho (1) |
| Command | *"bring them up to Mount Hor"* (25) | 32:49–50, *"Go up … Die on the mountain"* |
| Witnesses | *"in the sight of all the congregation"* (27) | Alone with Yahweh; no human witness named |
| Successor | Eleazar, in Aaron's garments (26, 28) | Joshua, *full of the spirit of wisdom* by Moses' hands (9) |
| Burial | Not narrated in Numbers 20; Deuteronomy 10:6 has burial at Moserah | Buried opposite Beth Peor; *no man knows where his tomb is* (6) |
| Mourning | *"they wept for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel"* (29) | *"The children of Israel wept for Moses … thirty days"* (8) |

**Seen, and unseen.** Aaron's death is public: the congregation watches him climb, and Moses and
Eleazar come down without him (Numbers 20:27–28). Moses climbs with no companion named, Yahweh
alone shows him the land, and the grave is unknown. The priest's succession is visible (the
garments pass before witnesses); the prophet's was made earlier, before Eleazar and the
congregation (Numbers 27:19–23), and is only recalled here.

**The location tension carries over.** Deuteronomy 32:50 agrees with Numbers that Aaron died on
Mount Hor, while Deuteronomy 10:6 says Moserah. That conflict is recorded on
[[Aaron at Moserah and Mount Hor - Deuteronomy 10 and Numbers 20 and 33]] and is not resolved here.

**Thirty days and other periods.** The thirty days are the same for both brothers. Jacob's
mourning was seventy days in Egypt and seven at the threshing floor of Atad (Genesis 50:3, 10);
see [[Seven Days and Seven Nights - The Week of Mourning]]. The Torah gives no rule that fixes
thirty days for a leader; the two notices simply agree.

## Significance

Moses' death is shaped by Aaron's: Deuteronomy 32:50 says so directly. The parallel keeps the
Meribah sentence in view to the end — both men die in sight of the land and outside it — while
Deuteronomy 34 then sets Moses apart in what follows: the hidden grave and the epitaph that no
prophet like him has risen. See [[Burial and the Grave]] and [[Mourning and Lament]].
