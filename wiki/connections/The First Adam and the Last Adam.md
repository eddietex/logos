# The First Adam and the Last Adam

## Passages involved

- [[The Garden of Eden]] — Genesis 2:7
- [[The Fall]] — Genesis 3:1–24
- Romans 5:12–21 · 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49 *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

Paul does something with Genesis 3 that Genesis 3 does not do with itself: he makes it universal,
and he makes it comparative. [[Adam]] is not just the first man who sinned; he is a *type*, one of
two figures whose single act determines the standing of everyone attached to him.

**Romans 5:12–21 — the parallel of two acts.**

| Adam | Christ |
|---|---|
| one man's trespass | one man's act of righteousness |
| disobedience | obedience |
| death reigned | grace reigns, and those who receive it reign in life |
| condemnation to all | justification of life to all |

Paul's own qualifier runs through the passage: the two sides are *not* equal — *"the free gift is
not like the trespass"* (5:15). The comparison exists to be broken. The gift is larger than the
damage, and the word *much more* carries the argument four times.

The hinge is 5:12: *"through one man sin entered into the world, and death through sin; and so
death passed to all men, because all sinned."* That last clause is one of the most argued in the
New Testament — the Greek *eph' hō* has been read as "because", "in whom", and "with the result
that". Augustine, working from a Latin text that rendered it *in quo* ("in whom"), read it as all
humanity sinning *in* Adam, which became the backbone of Western original-sin doctrine. Eastern
Christian tradition, reading the Greek, has generally taken the inheritance to be *mortality*
rather than *guilt* — we die because Adam died, and we sin because we die. The verse itself does
not adjudicate.

**1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49 — the parallel of two bodies.**

Here the comparison serves the resurrection rather than justification. *"For since death came by
man, the resurrection of the dead also came by man. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all
will be made alive"* (15:21–22). Then Paul quotes Genesis 2:7 directly and completes it:

> *"The first man, Adam, became a living soul." The last Adam became a life-giving spirit.*
> (15:45)

The quotation is exact — *nephesh ḥayyāh*, the phrase from Genesis 2:7 — and the second half has
no Old Testament source at all. Paul supplies it as the counterpart the verse was waiting for.
The pairing runs: first is the natural, then the spiritual; the first man of the dust, the second
man from heaven; *"as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we will also bear the image of
the heavenly"* (15:49). The dust of Genesis 2:7 and the return to dust of Genesis 3:19 are both in
view, and both are answered by a body that does not return to it.

**"Last", not "second".** *Eschatos Adam* — Paul's point is not that there have been two of them
but that there will be no third. The line that began in a garden ends in a person.

**What Genesis supplies and what Paul adds.** Genesis 3 gives: one command, one transgression,
consequences that reach the ground and the generations, exile from the tree of life. It does not
say that Adam's guilt transfers, does not use the word *sin*, and does not generalize beyond the
narrative. Every universalizing move — *all* died, *all* sinned, *in Adam* — is Paul's, made in
service of a claim about Christ. Read Genesis first and Paul second, and the typology is a
theological construction built on the story; read Paul first and Genesis second, as most of
Christian history has, and the story looks like it was always shaped for the construction.

## Significance

This is the New Testament's single largest interpretive investment in Genesis 3, and it is the
reason the chapter carries doctrinal weight that its own words do not obviously assign it. Almost
every later dispute about original sin, inherited guilt, infant baptism, and the necessity of the
incarnation runs back through Romans 5 to this passage, not directly to Genesis. Luke's genealogy
makes the same connection narratively rather than argumentatively, running Jesus's descent back to
*"Adam, the son of God"* (Luke 3:38) and placing the temptation in the wilderness immediately
after it.
