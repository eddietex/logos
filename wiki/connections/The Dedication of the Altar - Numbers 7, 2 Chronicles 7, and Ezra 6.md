# The Dedication of the Altar - Numbers 7, 2 Chronicles 7, and Ezra 6

## Passages involved

- [[The Offerings of the Twelve Princes]] — Numbers 7:10–11, 84, 88; *the dedication of the altar*
- 1 Kings 8:63 — *"So the king and all the children of Israel dedicated Yahweh’s house."* *(WEB,
  bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- 2 Chronicles 7:5, 9 — *"they kept the dedication of the altar seven days, and the feast seven days."*
  *(same)*
- Ezra 6:16–17 — *"for a sin offering for all Israel, twelve male goats, according to the number of the
  tribes of Israel."* *(same)*
- Nehemiah 12:27 — *"to keep the dedication with gladness."* *(same)*
- Psalm 30, heading — *šîr ḥănukkat habbayit* (WLC, where the heading is verse 1); bible-api's WEB text
  of the psalm does not carry the heading, so no English is quoted
- Deuteronomy 20:5 — a man who *"has built a new house, and has not dedicated it"* *(same)*
- John 10:22 — *"It was the Feast of the Dedication at Jerusalem."* *(same; Greek not checked)*

## Analysis

The Hebrew noun *ḥănukkâ* is tagged in seven verses of the WLC: four in Numbers 7 (10, 11, 84, 88), and
then 2 Chronicles 7:9, Nehemiah 12:27, and the heading of Psalm 30. Ezra 6:16–17 has the Aramaic
counterpart, tagged as a separate word. The verb is rare too: Deuteronomy 20:5, Proverbs 22:6, 1 Kings
8:63, and 2 Chronicles 7:5. The same root covers a private house, training a child, an altar, a temple,
and a city wall, so it is not a cultic term in every use.

Numbers 7 is the first public dedication the Bible reports. The later altar and temple dedications
give their own totals:

| Occasion | Who gives | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers 7 | Twelve princes, a day each | 252 animals over twelve days |
| 1 Kings 8:63; 2 Chronicles 7:5 | King Solomon | 22,000 cattle and 120,000 sheep |
| Ezra 6:17 | The returned exiles | 100 bulls, 200 rams, 400 lambs, 12 goats |

The difference in who gives is sharper than the difference in number. In Numbers the tribes give one at
a time and equally. In Kings and Chronicles the king gives. Ezra then returns to the tribes: the returned
exiles offer twelve goats *according to the number of the tribes of Israel*. It is the
wiki's reading that the twelve goats answer to the twelve-tribe form of Numbers 7. Ezra does not cite Numbers.

2 Chronicles 7:9 keeps the phrase of Numbers 7 exactly, *ḥănukkat hammizbēaḥ*, and it is the only other
verse that does. Its seven days are not the twelve of Numbers, and Chronicles gives no comment.

John 10:22 is the name of a later feast, which is commonly identified with the Maccabean rededication.
That identification and the Greek word were not checked for this page. It is noted here for the English
word only, not as a link the text draws.

## Significance

Israel marks a new start in worship by giving at the altar, and it uses this word for it. The series also
shows what stayed fixed and what changed: the object, an altar or house, and the gift, and then who stands
at the head of it. Numbers 7 has no king, and it gives no tribe more than another.
