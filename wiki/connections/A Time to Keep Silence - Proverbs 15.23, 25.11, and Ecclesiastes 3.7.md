# A Time to Keep Silence - Proverbs 15.23, 25.11, and Ecclesiastes 3.7

## Passages involved

- [[A Gentle Answer]] — Proverbs 15:23.
- [[The Glory of Kings]] — Proverbs 25:11.
- [[A Time for Every Purpose]] — Ecclesiastes 3:7, within 3:1–15.
- **Ecclesiastes 5:2** — *"Therefore let your words be few."* — [[Let Your Words Be Few]]

## Analysis

Proverbs 15:23 values a reply not only for its content but for its occasion:

> Joy comes to a man with the reply of his mouth. How good is a word at the right time!

Proverbs 25:11 compares a fitting word to a precious arrangement:

> A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in settings of silver.

Ecclesiastes 3:7 places speech within the larger catalogue of contrasting times:

> a time to tear, and a time to sew; a time to keep silence, and a time to speak;

Proverbs praises the word that fits; Ecclesiastes gives silence a place alongside speech.
The sayings can illuminate each other without becoming a rule that the reader can apply
mechanically. None of these verses tells a person in every situation exactly when to speak.
Ecclesiastes' next reflection also denies access to God's work from beginning to end (3:11).
Appropriate human action therefore need not depend on a comprehensive view of all events.

## Significance

[[Language and Speech]] includes both the good of a timely word and the place of restraint.
[[Wisdom]] must attend to circumstances without claiming that the poem of times supplies a
complete timetable. Silence should not automatically be treated as failure, or speaking as
success.
