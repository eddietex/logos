# Nadab, Abihu, and the Guarded Entry - Leviticus 10 and 16

## Passages involved

- [[The Generations of Aaron and the Levites Given]] — Numbers 3:4; the deaths reported in a line, with *no children*.
- [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]] — Leviticus 10:1–11.
- [[The Day of Atonement]] — Leviticus 16:1–34.

## Analysis

Leviticus 16:1 dates the Day of Atonement law *"after the death of the two sons of Aaron, when
they came near before Yahweh, and died"*. The intervening purity laws of chapters 11–15 are
framed, on this reading, as a parenthesis; the narrative thread of chapter 10 resumes here.

The two chapters share their objects. Nadab and Abihu each take a censer, put fire and incense
in it, and offer *"strange fire before Yahweh, which he had not commanded them"* (10:1). Aaron
takes a censer *"full of coals of fire from off the altar before Yahweh"* and two handfuls of
incense within the veil, *"so that he will not die"* (16:12–13). Both chapters warn of death in
approach — 10:9 for priests who drink before entering the tent, 16:2 for Aaron entering the
Most Holy Place at all times.

**What chapter 16 does not do** is explain the sons' offense further. It does not say whether
their fire came from the wrong source, at the wrong time, or into the wrong space; it answers
the event by prescribing the permitted approach. Inferring the precise offense from the
contrast (for example, that they entered within the veil) goes beyond what either chapter
states.

Chapter 10:3 has Moses relay *"I will show myself holy to those who come near me"*; chapter 16
shows the coming near that is allowed — once a year, washed, in linen, with commanded incense,
blood, and nobody else in the tent (16:17).

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:1–11 and 16:1–34; comparison is paraphrase except where quoted.

### Numbers 3:4

The third account is the shortest. It keeps *strange fire* and *before Yahweh*, gives the place, and adds that the brothers had no children. It says nothing about the entry rules, but the chapter goes on to set the Levites around the Tent and repeat the death sentence for the outsider (10). See [[The Stranger Who Comes Near - Numbers 1.51, 3.10, 18.7, and Leviticus 10]].

## Significance

The pairing keeps the Day of Atonement from being read as a freestanding ritual. It is the
answer to a death at the sanctuary, which is why its first concern is how the high priest
survives his own entry. See [[Holiness]], [[Priesthood]], and
[[Strange Incense - Exodus 30.9, Leviticus 10, Numbers 16, and Uzziah]].
