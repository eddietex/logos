# A Good Name - Proverbs 22.1 and Ecclesiastes 7.1

## Passages involved

- [[Love Better Than Wine]] — Song of Solomon 1:3, comparison of a name with fragrant oil.

- [[A Good Name]] — Proverbs 22:1.
- [[The House of Mourning]] — Ecclesiastes 7:1–2.

## Analysis

Both sayings rank a good name above a valued possession:

> A good name is more desirable than great riches, and loving favor is better than silver and gold. (Proverbs 22:1)

> A good name is better than fine perfume; and the day of death better than the day of one’s birth. (Ecclesiastes 7:1)

Proverbs sets the name against riches, silver, and gold, and pairs it with loving favor:
reputation and goodwill among people outweigh wealth. Ecclesiastes sets it against fine
perfume and pairs it at once with a second comparison, the day of death above the day of
birth. Verse 2 supplies the reason for that pairing: death is the end of all, and the living
should take it to heart. In Ecclesiastes, then, the good name is weighed with the end of a
life in view.

**Tension within Ecclesiastes.** The same book has said that the wise man is not remembered
longer than the fool (2:16) and that there is no memory of former or later generations
(1:11). Ranking a good name above perfume does not retract those observations; the text
does not claim the name lasts forever. The comparison values a name without promising
its permanence. That distinction is a reading of the juxtaposition, not a statement of
either verse.

The parallel is in the WEB's English phrase *a good name*. This page does not assert that the
Hebrew expressions are identical or that either book borrows from the other.

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 22:1; Ecclesiastes 1:11; 2:16; 7:1–2. Both quotations are exact
stored WEB excerpts. No source-language claim is made.

### A name as fragrant oil — Song of Solomon 1:3

[[Love Better Than Wine]] extends the comparison: the beloved's fragrant oils lead to
his name likened to oil poured out, and to the virgins' love for him. Ecclesiastes ranks
name above perfume; the Song uses fragrance to express the name's attraction. Proverbs
22:1 sets reputation above wealth. These are related images with different purposes,
not the same maxim or a demonstrated chain of borrowing. The Song does not attach
Ecclesiastes' death-day comparison to its own image. Source: stored WEB Song 1:1–4;
Proverbs 22:1; Ecclesiastes 7:1–2. No Hebrew wordplay is asserted.

## Significance

[[Names and Naming]] here concerns reputation rather than an act of naming, and
[[Wealth and Possessions]] is subordinated to it in both books. Ecclesiastes adds
[[Life and Death]] and [[Remembrance]] to the comparison: a name is weighed against
mortality and the forgetting the book has already observed.
