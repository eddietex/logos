# Forced Labor at Gezer and the Command for the Inherited Cities

## Passages involved
- [[The Inheritance of Ephraim]] — Joshua 16:10.
- [[Laws for Going to War]] — Deuteronomy 20:10–18.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 20:10–15 allows forced labor following a distant city’s peaceful surrender.
Verses 16–18 then distinguish the inherited cities of the named peoples, including Canaanites,
and command their destruction. Joshua 16:10 reports that Ephraim did not drive out Gezer’s
Canaanites and that they remained as forced laborers.

**The command and the reported result are in tension.** Forced labor is not evidence that the
distinct command for the inherited cities was fulfilled. Nor does the survival notice mean
freedom: residence and coerced labor coexist. Joshua 16 does not narrate a peace offer, treaty,
or reason for non-removal. It does not supply the protective oath found in Joshua 9.

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 20:10–18 and Joshua 16:10; no source-language claim is made.

## Significance

The allotment records an unresolved difference between commanded removal and actual settlement.
It also shows why continued residence must not be mistaken for release from domination.
