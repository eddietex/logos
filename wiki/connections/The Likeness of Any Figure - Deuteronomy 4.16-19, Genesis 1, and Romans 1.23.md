# The Likeness of Any Figure - Deuteronomy 4.16-19, Genesis 1, and Romans 1.23

## Passages involved

- [[The Creation of the World]] — Genesis 1:14–28: the lights, the water creatures and birds, the land
  animals and creeping things, and man *male and female* in God's image.
- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:4: no image of anything in the heavens, the earth, or the water
  under the earth.
- [[No Form Seen and No Image Made]] — Deuteronomy 4:15–19: the forbidden likenesses, listed creature by
  creature, then the sun, moon, and stars.
- Romans 1:22–25 — glory exchanged for the likeness of man, birds, four-footed animals, and creeping
  things; not yet ingested (WEB verified via bible-api.com, 2026-11-05).

## Analysis

Exodus 20:4 forbids images in three zones:

> **Exodus 20:4** “You shall not make for yourselves an idol, nor any image of anything that is in the heavens above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth:

Deuteronomy 4 spells the zones out as a list of creatures:

> **Deuteronomy 4:16** lest you corrupt yourselves, and make yourself a carved image in the form of any figure, the likeness of male or female,

> **Deuteronomy 4:17** the likeness of any animal that is on the earth, the likeness of any winged bird that flies in the sky,

> **Deuteronomy 4:18** the likeness of anything that creeps on the ground, the likeness of any fish that is in the water under the earth;

and then turns to the sky (4:19): the sun, the moon, the stars, *all the army of the sky*.

**The list read against Genesis 1.** Every item has a counterpart in the creation account, and in
close English wording:

| Deuteronomy 4 | Genesis 1 (WEB) |
|---|---|
| *male or female* (16) | *male and female he created them* (27) |
| *any animal that is on the earth* (17) | *animals of the earth* (24–25) |
| *any winged bird that flies in the sky* (17) | *every winged bird* (21); *birds fly … in the open expanse of the sky* (20) |
| *anything that creeps on the ground* (18) | *everything that creeps on the ground* (25) |
| *any fish that is in the water* (18) | *the fish of the sea* (26, 28) |
| *the sun and the moon and the stars* (19) | *the two great lights … He also made the stars* (16) |
| *all the army of the sky* (19) | *all their vast array* (2:1) |

The order is close to the reverse of Genesis: Deuteronomy starts with humanity, the last thing made,
and ends with the lights of day four. It is not an exact reversal — Genesis makes birds with the water
creatures on day five and creeping things with the land animals on day six, so Deuteronomy's *birds*
before *creeping things* is out of strict sequence. The wiki records the resemblance in the English
lists and does not claim that Deuteronomy was composed from Genesis 1, nor make any claim about the
Hebrew vocabulary.

**What the echo does.** Every item on the list is something God made. To make its likeness and worship
it is to worship the creature; and the first item is the creature Genesis 1:27 says God made *in his own
image*. The text does not draw that conclusion itself. It grounds the ban on Horeb, not on creation:
*you saw no kind of form* (4:15). The creation echo is the wiki's observation about the list, and is
labelled as such.

**Romans 1 uses a shorter list of the same kind:**

> **Romans 1:23** and traded the glory of the incorruptible God for the likeness of an image of corruptible man, and of birds, and four-footed animals, and creeping things.

> **Romans 1:25** who exchanged the truth of God for a lie, and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever. Amen.

Man, birds, four-footed animals, creeping things — four of Deuteronomy's five creature types, without
fish and without the sky. Romans 1:25 then states outright what Deuteronomy's list implies, worship of
*the creature rather than the Creator*. Paul's *worshiped and served* matches Deuteronomy 4:19's
*worship them, and serve them* in WEB English. Many readers also hear Psalm 106:20 (the calf as the
image of an ox) behind Romans 1:23; that verse is cited here by reference only. No claim is made about
the Greek of Romans or the Greek translation of Deuteronomy it might echo.

**Where they differ.** Deuteronomy addresses Israel, which heard the voice at Horeb, and warns of what
it will do *when you … have been long in the land* (4:25). Romans 1 describes humanity at large, which
perceived God *through the things that are made* (Romans 1:20) and exchanged that knowledge. One
is a covenant warning to a people; the other a diagnosis of the nations. Deuteronomy even says the sky's
host is what Yahweh has *allotted to all the peoples* (4:19), which Romans does not echo.

## Significance

- It shows [[Idolatry]] in Deuteronomy as a misuse of [[Creation]]: the forbidden forms are the
  creatures of the six days.
- It sets the [[Image of God]] next to the ban on images: the one image God made is humanity, and an
  image of humanity is the first one forbidden.
- It links the Horeb argument ([[Seeing and Being Seen]]: no form was seen) to Paul's argument from what
  can be seen in creation. The two reach the same verdict from different starting points.
- See also [[The Voice Out of the Fire - Exodus 19-20 and Deuteronomy 4]] for the formless voice on
  which Deuteronomy 4:15 rests.
