## Analysis

Proverbs 12:17 contrasts truthful testimony with a lying witness; 12:19 contrasts the endurance of
truthful lips with a momentary lying tongue. Verse 22 gives the divine evaluation:

> Lying lips are an abomination to Yahweh, but those who do the truth are his delight.

The positive half concerns people who do the truth, widening the portrait from accurate utterance to
trustworthy conduct. Earlier sayings join just thoughts and rescuing speech against deceitful advice
and words that lie in wait for blood (5–6).

Exodus 20:16 prohibits false testimony against a neighbor. Exodus 23:1–8 develops the courtroom
dangers: malicious witness, crowd pressure, partiality, false charges, and bribes. Its protection of
innocent and righteous people against a false charge (7) shows how speech can threaten life, the
danger Proverbs 12:6 names in compressed form. Exodus refuses both favoritism toward the poor (3)
and denial of their justice (6); truth is not simply loyalty to one’s preferred side.

This is ethical continuity between law and wisdom, not an explicit citation or demonstrable
direction of borrowing. Proverbs gives a portrait of the witness and Yahweh’s delight; Exodus gives
commands and cases. Neither comparison supplies an unstated penalty for every kind of lie.

**Proverbs 14 brackets a chapter with the witness.** *"A truthful witness will not lie, but a false witness pours out lies"* (14:5), and *"A truthful witness saves souls, but a false witness is deceitful"* (14:25). The second saying gives testimony the life-and-death weight Exodus 23:7 assumes when it protects the innocent from a false charge. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 14:5, 25.

**Proverbs 17 moves from the witness to the hearer and the judge.** *"An evildoer heeds wicked lips. A
liar gives ear to a mischievous tongue"* (17:4) puts the fault in the listener who wants the lie;
*"lying lips"* do not fit *"a prince"* (17:7). Then the verdict: *"He who justifies the wicked, and he
who condemns the righteous, both of them alike are an abomination to Yahweh"* (17:15), the inversion
of Exodus 23:7's *"I will not justify the wicked"*. The bribe that drives it is traced on
[[A Bribe Blinds - Proverbs 17.23, Exodus 23.8, and Deuteronomy 16.19]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs
17:4, 7, 15; Exodus 23:7.

### The ruler who hears lies and the witness who stays silent — Proverbs 29

A ruler who listens to lies makes all his officials wicked (29:12): corruption reaches the whole
administration through the hearer’s preference. Verse 24 then exposes withheld testimony by a
thief’s accomplice. Exodus 23:1–8’s refusal of false reports, pressure, and unjust verdicts
concerns both patterns; withholding known evidence is developed separately in [[The Accomplice Who Will Not Testify - Proverbs 29.24 and Leviticus 5.1]].

See [[Where There Is No Vision]]. Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 29 and the cited local comparisons; no new
source-language claim.

### Agur’s words — Proverbs 30:6, 8, 10

Adding to God’s words risks exposure as a liar (6), and Agur asks for falsehood to be removed (8).
Verse 10 forbids slandering a servant to his master and warns of the servant’s curse and the
slanderer’s guilt. The socially weaker person remains someone whose complaint matters; compare
Exodus 23:1’s prohibition of a false report and Job 31:13–15’s obligation to hear servants. No
judicial procedure is specified.

no new source-language identity is claimed.

## Significance

[[Language and Speech]], [[Integrity]], and [[Deception]] meet in the public responsibility of
testimony. [[Law and Obedience]] protects neighbors through truthful judgment, while wisdom commends
the person whose words and conduct can be trusted.
