# Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 1:2, 9–10** — darkness over the deep; *"let the dry land appear … God called the dry land
  'earth'"* — [[The Creation of the World]]
- **Genesis 7:11, 22** — *"all the fountains of the great deep burst open"*; *"All on the dry land, in
  whose nostrils was the breath of the spirit of life, died"* — [[Noah and the Flood]]
- **Genesis 8:1** — *"God made a wind to pass over the earth. The waters subsided"* — [[The Waters Recede]]
- **Exodus 4:9** — the Nile's water poured *"on the dry land"* becomes blood — [[The Burning Bush]]
- **Exodus 14:16, 20–22, 29** — the sea *"divided"*, a wind *"all night"*, *"dry land"*, the darkness and
  the light, the waters *"a wall"* — [[The Crossing of the Sea]]
- **Exodus 15:19** — *"the children of Israel walked on dry land in the middle of the sea"* *(not yet
  ingested)*
- **Psalm 106:9** — *"He rebuked the Red Sea also, and it was dried up; so he led them through the depths,
  as through a desert"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Hebrews 11:29** — *"By faith, they passed through the Red Sea as on dry land"* (WEB) —
  [[Baptized into Moses - The Sea in 1 Corinthians 10 and Hebrews 11]]

## Analysis

**Exodus 14 tells the crossing in the vocabulary of the first two chapters of the world.** Four Hebrew
words carry it, each checked in the WLC with a lemma search of Genesis–Deuteronomy:

| Word | Creation | Flood | Exodus 14 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***yabbāšâ***, *dry land* | *"let the dry land appear"* (1:9–10) | — | *"on dry ground"* (14:16, 22, 29) |
| ***ḥārābâ***, *dry ground* | — | *"All on the dry land … died"* (7:22) | *"made the sea dry land"* (14:21) |
| ***bāqaʿ***, *split* | — | *"the fountains of the great deep burst open"* (7:11) | *"divide it … the waters were divided"* (14:16, 21) |
| ***rûaḥ***, *wind* | *"God's Spirit was hovering over the surface of the waters"* (1:2) | *"God made a wind to pass over the earth"* (8:1) | *"a strong east wind all night"* (14:21) |

- ***Yabbāšâ*** occurs in the Torah only at Genesis 1:9–10, Exodus 4:9, 14:16, 22, 29, and 15:19. **Outside
  the third day of creation, every Torah use is in the Moses story** — and the first of those is a sign
  turning water to blood *on* dry land, before the sea turns to dry land for Israel.
- ***Ḥārābâ*** occurs in the Torah only at Genesis 7:22 and Exodus 14:21. **In the flood the dry land is
  where everything that breathes dies; at the sea it is where Israel walks through alive.**
- ***Bāqaʿ*** occurs in the Torah at Genesis 7:11; 22:3; Exodus 14:16, 21; Numbers 16:31. **The niphal
  *nibqəʿû*, *were split*, is used of the fountains of the deep (Genesis 7:11) and of the sea's waters
  (Exodus 14:21)** — the first opening the deep to drown the world, the second opening the sea to make a
  road. Numbers 16:31 will split the ground under Korah, the opposite turn.
- ***Rûaḥ*** is too common to count, and the wiki notes only the three scenes. **Genesis 1:2's *rûaḥ
  ʾĕlōhîm* is translated *"God's Spirit"* in the WEB and *wind* by some readers**; the wiki adopts neither
  reading on [[The Creation of the World]] and does not here.

**And the night of Exodus 14:20 has *ḥōšek*, darkness, the word of Genesis 1:2**, with light in it — the
pairing of the first day (*ḥōšek* at Genesis 1:2, 4, 5, 18; Exodus 10:21–22; 14:20 are its only uses in
Genesis–Leviticus; WLC lemma search). See [[Light and Darkness]].

**The text never says *as at the creation*.** The links are lexical, and the wiki holds them to that.
**What the words show is that Exodus describes the sea with the nouns Genesis used for separating water
from land, and for drowning the land again** — and that the sea crossing reverses the flood's use of them:
the flood split the deep and killed what was on the dry land; the sea is split and what is on the dry land
lives. [[The Flood as Un-Creation]] reads the flood as creation run backwards; **on that reading Exodus 14
runs it forwards again, for one people, for one night.**

## Significance

- **It sets the exodus inside the creation story's frame**, a move the Psalms make openly (Psalm 106:9;
  136:13–15; *not yet ingested*) and Exodus makes only in its choice of words. See [[Creation]].
- **It pairs with the ark and the basket.** [[The Flood and Baptism]] and
  [[The Two Arks - Noah's Ship and Moses' Basket]] follow the vessel carried *through* judgment by water;
  this page follows the ground made *in* it.
- **Hebrews 11:29 keeps the dry land** — *"as on dry land"*, *ὡς διὰ ξηρᾶς γῆς* (SBLGNT) — and gives the
  crossing to faith. See [[Faith]].
