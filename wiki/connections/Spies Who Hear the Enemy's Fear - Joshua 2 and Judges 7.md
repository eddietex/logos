# Spies Who Hear the Enemy's Fear - Joshua 2 and Judges 7

## Passages involved
- [[Rahab and the Spies]] — Joshua 2:9–11, 24. Rahab tells the two spies, *I know that Yahweh has given
  you the land, and that the fear of you has fallen upon us, and that all the inhabitants of the land
  melt away before you* (9). They report to Joshua, *Truly Yahweh has delivered all the land into our
  hands. Moreover, all the inhabitants of the land melt away before us* (24) (stored WEB).
- [[The Dream of the Barley Loaf]] — Judges 7:9–15. Yahweh says *You will hear what they say* (11).
  Gideon hears a Midianite interpret a dream: *God has delivered Midian into his hand, with all the
  army* (14). He returns and says, *Arise; for Yahweh has delivered the army of Midian into your hand!*
  (15) (stored WEB).
- Related: [[Twelve Spies and Two - Numbers 13 and Joshua 2]], where the Kadesh spies saw the land's
  strength and did not hear its fear.

## Analysis

**The same shape.** In both stories Israelites go secretly into enemy territory before a battle. In
both they learn something from the enemy's own mouth: the enemy already expects to lose. And in both
the report back is the same formula, *Yahweh has delivered … into our / your hand*.

| | Joshua 2 | Judges 7:9–15 |
|---|---|---|
| Who goes | two men, sent by Joshua (1) | Gideon and Purah, sent by Yahweh (9–11) |
| When | they come in *tonight* (2) and are let down *by a cord through the window* (15) | *That same night* (9) |
| Who speaks | Rahab, a Canaanite | two Midianite soldiers |
| What is heard | *the fear of you has fallen upon us … our hearts melted* (9, 11) | a dream, and *God has delivered Midian into his hand* (13–14) |
| Report | *Truly Yahweh has delivered all the land into our hands* (24) | *Arise; for Yahweh has delivered the army of Midian into your hand!* (15) |

**The formula.** Joshua 2:24 reads *kî-nātan YHWH bəyādênû ʾet-kol-hāʾāreṣ*. Judges 7:15 reads
*kî-nātan YHWH bəyedkem ʾet-maḥănēh midyān* (WLC). The two clauses have the same conjunction, verb,
subject, and preposition. Only the person of the suffix and the object differ. The formula *give into
the hand* is common in the war narratives of these books (Judges 4:14 has it too), so the shared wording
alone does not show that Judges depends on Joshua. The likeness is in the whole scene, not in the
phrase.

**The differences.** Rahab speaks to the spies on purpose and makes a bargain with them. The Midianites
do not know Gideon is listening. Rahab names Yahweh and confesses him as *God in heaven above, and on
earth beneath* (Joshua 2:11). The interpreter says only *God* (*hāʾĕlōhîm*, WLC) and makes no
confession. The spies were sent by Joshua to gather information. Gideon is sent by Yahweh to be
strengthened, *if you are afraid* (7:10). The point of the trip is the commander's courage, not facts
about the enemy.

**The fear reversed.** In Numbers 13 the spies brought back fear into Israel's camp. In Joshua 2 and
Judges 7 the spies find that the fear is in the enemy's camp instead. Judges 7 makes the turn inside a
single chapter. In 7:3 the *fearful* of Israel went home, and in 7:13–14 the enemy is the one dreaming of
defeat.

## Significance
- Judges does not cite Joshua 2. The parallel is in the structure of the scene and in a common formula,
  and both are stated here without a claim of literary dependence.
- Both scenes show Yahweh's promise confirmed by an outsider. A Canaanite woman and a Midianite soldier
  speak the outcome before Israel has fought.
