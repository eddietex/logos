# Hazor Burned and the Northern Land Still Remaining

## Passages involved
- [[The Northern Kings at the Waters of Merom]] — Joshua 11:1–15.
- Joshua 13:1–6 — read in stored WEB, not yet ingested.
- [[The Southern Conquest and the Land Still Remaining]] — comparable southern tension.

## Analysis

Joshua 11:8 pursues the coalition to Sidon and Misrephoth Maim, and 11:12–14 describes
the destruction of its kings and inhabitants. Yet 13:4–6 still describes northern territory
and its inhabitants, including the Sidonians and the region to Misrephoth Maim, as remaining
to be possessed. The tension remains visible: a pursuit limit is not a claim that every
city on the route was taken or permanently occupied. The text supplies no detailed
chronology of settlement, reoccupation, or subsequent loss.

Hazor’s burning is explicitly exceptional among the cities on their mounds (11:13).
The total language about inhabitants cannot be transferred without qualification to
physical destruction of buildings or to every population in the broader region.

## Significance

The northern campaign joins military success to obedience while retaining distinctions
between pursuit, capture, destruction, and enduring possession. Reading the later notice
beside the victory preserves the book’s unresolved scope and chronology.
