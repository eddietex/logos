# Yes, He Will Be Blessed - The Irrevocable Word

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 27:33** — *"Isaac trembled violently, and said, 'Who, then, is he who has taken venison,
  and brought it me, and I have eaten of all before you came, and have blessed him? **Yes, he will be
  blessed.**'"*
- **Genesis 27:37** — *"Behold, I have made him your lord, and all his brothers I have given to him
  for servants. I have sustained him with grain and new wine. What then will I do for you, my son?"*
- **Genesis 9:25–27** — Noah's curse on Canaan and blessing on Shem and Japheth, spoken on waking
  from wine, and never modified.
- **Numbers 23:20** — Balaam: *"Behold, I have received a command to bless. He has blessed, and I
  can't reverse it."*
- **Numbers 22:6; 24:9** — *"he whom you bless is blessed, and he whom you curse is cursed"*, and
  27:29's curse-and-bless clause spoken back in the same order.
- **Joshua 9:19–20** — the Gibeonites obtain a treaty by fraud, and Israel's leaders keep it: *"We
  have sworn to them by Yahweh, the God of Israel. Now therefore we may not touch them."*
- **Judges 11:35** — Jephthah: *"I have opened my mouth to Yahweh, and I can't go back."*
- **1 Samuel 15:29** — *"Also the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent; for he is not a man,
  that he should repent."*
- **Isaiah 55:11** — *"so is my word that goes out of my mouth: it will not return to me void."*
- **Romans 11:29** — *"For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable."*

## Analysis

**The Bible's clearest statement that a blessing cannot be recalled is made by the man it was stolen
from, two verses after he worked out how.**

Isaac has just reconstructed the entire fraud aloud — *who then is he who has taken venison, and
brought it me, and I have eaten … and have blessed him?* — in one interrogative sentence with four
verbs in it. He has the facts, he has the motive to reverse it, and he has the authority that made
the blessing in the first place. **His conclusion is *gam-bārûk yihyeh*: indeed, blessed he shall
be.**

**This is not fatalism and it is not magic.** It is a specific claim about what kind of act a
blessing is, and Genesis makes the claim by putting it under maximum pressure. **A word of this kind
is performative**: like an oath, a verdict, or a marriage formula, it does not describe a state of
affairs, it creates one — and once created, the state of affairs no longer belongs to the speaker.
**Isaac's intention was to bless Esau. The blessing he actually performed was over Jacob. Genesis
sides with the act and not with the intention.** See [[Language and Speech]] and [[Blessing]].

### Where else the canon does this

**The pattern is broad, and it is not confined to blessings.**

| text | the word | why it could have been withdrawn | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 27:33** | Isaac's blessing on Jacob | obtained by disguise and a flat lie | *"Yes, he will be blessed"* |
| **Genesis 9:25** | Noah's curse on Canaan | pronounced on waking from wine, on a grandson who did nothing | never modified; Genesis 10 lists Canaan's line without reference to it |
| **Numbers 23:20** | God's blessing on Israel | a king has paid a prophet to reverse it | *"He has blessed, and I can't reverse it"* |
| **Joshua 9:19** | Israel's oath to Gibeon | the treaty was obtained by a deliberate deception | *"we may not touch them"* |
| **Judges 11:35** | Jephthah's vow | it costs him his daughter | *"I can't go back"* |

**Joshua 9 is the closest structural parallel to Genesis 27**, and the wiki flags it as such: **a
binding word obtained by fraud, discovered as fraud, and honoured anyway.** The Gibeonites arrive in
worn-out shoes with mouldy bread and are found out three days later; Israel keeps the treaty for
centuries, and 2 Samuel 21 has famine sent because Saul broke it. **In both stories the deceiver's
gain is not clawed back, and the deceived party's word is what holds.**

### The counter-cases, which matter

**Scripture does not hold that every declared word is irreversible**, and a page that only collected
the examples above would misrepresent it.

- **God relents.** Jonah 3:10 — Nineveh is spared after a flat forty-day sentence. Exodus 32:14 —
  *"Yahweh repented of the evil which he said he would do to his people."* Genesis 6:6 has him
  grieved at having made humanity. **The prophetic threat is conditional in a way the patriarchal
  blessing is not**, and Jeremiah 18:7–10 states the principle outright. See
  [[Divine Grief and Relenting]].
- **Vows can be annulled.** Numbers 30 gives a father or a husband the power to void a woman's vow on
  the day he hears it, and Leviticus 27 provides a schedule of redemption prices for things rashly
  dedicated. **The law builds in an exit that Jephthah either did not know or did not use.**
- **The blessing itself is not always finite.** Genesis 27:37 — *"What then will I do for you, my
  son?"* — treats it as a stock that has been spent, and **that is the only place in the Bible where
  a blessing runs out.** Jacob blesses twelve sons and two grandsons in two chapters (48–49) without
  exhausting anything. **The wiki records the finitude as a feature of Genesis 27's scene rather than
  as the canon's doctrine.**

**So the principle is narrower than "words cannot be taken back."** What Genesis 27 establishes is
that **a formally performed blessing, once released over a person, stands independently of the
speaker's intention and of the means by which it was procured** — and Numbers 23:20, Joshua 9:19, and
Romans 11:29 are the three places the rest of Scripture says the same thing about God's own
commitments.

### The theological use Paul makes of it

***"The gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable"*** — ***ametamélēta*** (Romans 11:29),
literally *not to be regretted, not subject to a change of mind.* **Paul is arguing that God has not
withdrawn from Israel**, and the noun behind the adjective is *metánoia*, the same word Hebrews 12:17
uses of what Esau could not find: *"he found no place for a change of mind."*

**The two verses are the same word from opposite ends.** Esau cannot produce a reversal; God will not
produce one. **In both cases the fixed point is the word already spoken**, and in both cases the
person who wants it changed is the one who has to live with it. See
[[Esau's Birthright in Hebrews]] and [[Call and Election]].

## Significance

- **It is what makes Genesis 27 more than a family scandal.** If the blessing could have been
  reassigned once the fraud came out, the chapter would be an anecdote. Because it cannot, the
  chapter is the mechanism by which the covenant line reaches the third generation — **and Genesis
  refuses to launder the mechanism.** See [[The Stolen Blessing]].
- **It separates the act from the actor's deserving, which is this book's standing position on
  blessing.** Abram is enriched out of a ruse (12:16); Isaac reaps a hundredfold two verses after
  being caught in one (26:12); Jacob keeps a blessing he lied for. **Nobody in Genesis is ever
  blessed because they deserved it**, and 27:33 is the sharpest instance.
- **It does not make the deceit approved.** Isaac names it *deceit* two verses later; Esau's grief
  gets eight verses; Jacob loses twenty years, his mother, and eventually a hip. **The word stands
  and the cost is charged**, and Genesis holds both without reconciling them. See [[Deception]] and
  [[Retribution]].
