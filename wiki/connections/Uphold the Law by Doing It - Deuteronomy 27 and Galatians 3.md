# Uphold the Law by Doing It - Deuteronomy 27 and Galatians 3

## Passages involved

- [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]] — Deuteronomy 27:26.
- Galatians 3:10–14 — WEB verified from bible-api.com, 2026-11-10; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

The final curse in Deuteronomy 27 gathers the preceding cases into the obligation to uphold
the law by doing it. Galatians 3:10 cites this sanction in arguing that those who are of the
works of the law are under a curse. In the verified WEB, Paul’s citation speaks of continuing
in all things written in the book of the law; the stored WEB of Deuteronomy 27:26 instead
speaks of upholding the words of this law by doing them. The difference in English wording
is retained. No explanation from an unverified Hebrew or Greek edition is asserted.

Paul then contrasts law and faith and describes Christ redeeming people from the law’s curse
by becoming a curse for them (11–13). His second citation concerns hanging on a tree,
Deuteronomy 21:23, a distinct text from the closing curse of chapter 27. The movement reaches
the blessing of Abraham for the Gentiles and the promise of the Spirit through faith (14).
See [[Hanged on a Tree - Genesis 40, Deuteronomy 21, and Galatians 3]].

Deuteronomy prescribes a covenant assembly’s response; it does not itself name Christ or
expound Paul’s argument about justification. Galatians is a later theological use of its
sanction, not an account of the ceremony on Ebal.

## Significance

The connection distinguishes the curse on failure to do the law from the curse associated with hanging, then follows how Paul joins them.
