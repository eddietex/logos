## Analysis

*Bone and flesh*, *ʿeṣem* and *bāśār* together, is the Hebrew Bible's phrase for being one family. In
the WLC of Genesis–Judges it occurs as a kinship formula only three times: the man's words over the
woman (Genesis 2:23), Laban's welcome to Jacob (29:14), and Abimelech's appeal to Shechem (Judges 9:2).
2 Samuel 5:1 has the same pair, *ʿaṣməkā ûbəśārəkā*, *your bone and your flesh* (WLC).

The uses run in two directions.

| Passage | Who says it | To what end |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 2:23 | the man, of the woman | recognition; the first marriage |
| Genesis 29:14 | Laban, to Jacob | welcome into the household, and then a month's work and a wage (29:15) |
| Genesis 37:27 | Judah, of Joseph (*our brother, our flesh*) | against killing him, and for selling him |
| Judges 9:2 | Abimelech, to Shechem | to be chosen as ruler over his father's seventy sons |
| 2 Samuel 5:1 | the tribes, to David | to make him king over all Israel |

In Genesis the formula brings someone into a household. In Judges 9 and 2 Samuel 5 it is the reason
given for a king. Abimelech says *I am your bone*. The tribes say to David *we are your bone*. The
claim of kinship comes from opposite sides. Abimelech asks for the crown on the strength of the
kinship. In 2 Samuel the tribes give it, and they add a second reason, Yahweh's word, *You will be
shepherd of my people Israel* (5:2). No word from Yahweh is cited at Shechem.

Deuteronomy 17:15 sets both conditions in one verse: a king *whom Yahweh your God chooses*, and *one
from among your brothers*. Shechem's *He is our brother* (Judges 9:3) meets the second and is silent
on the first. The wiki notes that the 2 Samuel scene cites both: kinship (5:1) and Yahweh's word
(5:2). Whether 2 Samuel 5 was written with Judges 9 in view is not decidable from the texts, and the
Hebrew of 2 Samuel was checked only for 5:1's pair of nouns.

Genesis 37:27 is not the same formula. Judah says *our brother, our flesh*, without *bone*. It belongs
here because its *he is our brother* (*ʾāḥînû hûʾ*, WLC) is exactly Shechem's two words in Judges 9:3.
Judah used kinship to stop a killing and arrange a sale. Shechem uses it to back a killing.

## Significance

- **Kinship can be an argument either way.** The same appeal welcomes Jacob, spares Joseph's life,
  backs a fratricide, and unites the tribes under David. The words alone decide nothing. See
  [[Brotherhood]].
- **Judges 9:6 is the first time in Genesis–Judges that anyone is *made king*, and it rests on kinship
  and money.** The hiphil of *mālak* occurs in those books only at Judges 9:6, 16, 18 (WLC). Abimelech
  is chosen because *he is our brother* and is paid from Baal Berith's house (9:4). See
  [[Kingship and Empire]].
- **It sharpens Deuteronomy 17:15.** The law's brother clause can be met while its first clause, Yahweh's
  choice, is ignored.
