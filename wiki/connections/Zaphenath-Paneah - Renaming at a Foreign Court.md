# Zaphenath-Paneah - Renaming at a Foreign Court

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 41:45** — *"Pharaoh called Joseph's name Zaphenath-Paneah"* —
  [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Genesis 17:5, 15; 32:28** — the renamings God performs: Abram to Abraham, Sarai to Sarah, Jacob to
  Israel — [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]],
  [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]]
- **2 Kings 23:34** — Pharaoh Necoh renames Eliakim *Jehoiakim* and takes his brother to Egypt
- **2 Kings 24:17** — Nebuchadnezzar renames Mattaniah *Zedekiah*
- **Daniel 1:6–7** — Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah renamed Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach and
  Abednego
- **Esther 2:7** — Hadassah, *"that is, Esther"*
- **Ezra 1:8; 5:14** — Sheshbazzar, a Babylonian name borne by a prince of Judah
- **Isaiah 62:2; 65:15; Revelation 2:17; 3:12** — a new name given by God at the end

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — **Genesis 45:3–4, 16**: **the Egyptian name is absent from the one
  chapter where his family is in the room.** He introduces himself as ***ʾănî yôsēp*** twice (45:3, 4),
  dictates a message signed *"your son Joseph"* (45:9), **and even the Egyptian court calls him by the
  Hebrew name**: *"Joseph's brothers have come"* (45:16). **Genesis uses *Zaphenath-Paneah* once, at
  41:45, and never again** — and the chapter that undoes the disguise is where the disuse becomes
  conspicuous

## Analysis

**Genesis has renamed people before, and the difference is who does it.**

**[[Abraham]], [[Sarah]] and [[Jacob]] are renamed by God**, at the covenant, with the new name
explained in the verse (17:5; 17:15; 32:28). **Joseph is renamed by an employer**, on the day he is
promoted, **with no explanation offered at all.** ***Ṣāpənat Paʿnēaḥ*** **is left standing in Hebrew
script as a foreign noise** — the one renaming in the Bible where the text does not say what the name
means.

### What it probably means

**The most widely accepted reconstruction is Egyptian *ḏd-pȝ-nṯr-iw.f-ʿnḫ*, *the god speaks and he
lives***, a real and well-attested name type. **Older proposals include *ṣāpnat paʿnēaḥ* read as Hebrew —
*revealer of secrets*, which is how the Septuagint and Josephus took it — and various *sustainer of
life* readings tied to the famine.**

**The wiki records the proposals and adopts none.** What is not in doubt is that **the name is Egyptian
and probably contains an Egyptian god**, which is of a piece with the rest of verse 45: **an Egyptian
wife, an Egyptian father-in-law, and an Egyptian priesthood, all in one sentence.** See [[Idolatry]].

### The name Genesis never uses again

**This is the striking fact and it is easy to miss.**

**After 41:45 the narrator calls him Joseph, and so does everyone else** — Pharaoh (45:17),
the Egyptians (41:55), his brothers, his father, and the book's own headings — **for nine more chapters
and to the last verse of Genesis.** ***Zaphenath-Paneah occurs once in the Bible and then vanishes.***

**Compare Daniel**, where **the narrator does use the imposed names** — chapter 3 is *Shadrach, Meshach
and Abednego* throughout, not Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah — **and Esther**, where **Hadassah is
mentioned once and the book is titled after the Persian name.** **Genesis is the outlier: it records the
renaming and then refuses it.**

**The wiki does not claim this is a comment.** It may simply be that a narrator who has called a man
Joseph for four chapters keeps doing so. **But the effect on the reader is exact**: the Egyptian name is
put on him in public, in a verse about a chariot and a wife, **and the book itself does not adopt it.**

### What renaming is for

**In every case on this page the new name marks a change of owner.**

| renamer | renamed | what the name marks |
|---|---|---|
| **God** | Abram, Sarai, Jacob | **a covenant**, and the name is explained |
| **Pharaoh** | Joseph | **an office**, and it is not explained |
| **Pharaoh Necoh** | Eliakim → Jehoiakim | **a vassal installed on a throne** |
| **Nebuchadnezzar** | Mattaniah → Zedekiah | the same |
| **Ashpenaz** | Daniel and three others | **the training of captives for palace service** |

**Two of the five are conquerors renaming kings they have just installed**, which is the clearest case:
**the power to name is the power that owns.** **Daniel 1:7 is the most systematic** — **four Hebrew names
containing *ʾēl* and *yah* are replaced with four containing Babylonian deities**, and the chapter goes
straight on to the boys refusing the king's food. **The renaming they accept; the diet they do not.**

**Joseph's case is the mildest and the most complete.** **Nothing is taken from him** — he gains a
country — **and yet by the end of verse 45 his name, his wife, his clothes, his face and his job are all
Egyptian.** **41:14's *"he shaved himself"* is where it starts.** The counterweight is seven verses
later: **he calls the place *"the land of my affliction"*** (41:52), and names a son
[[Ephraim]] with a Hebrew verb out of Genesis 1.

### The last word on names

**Scripture ends the thread by giving it back to God.** Isaiah 62:2 — *"You will be called by a new name,
which Yahweh's mouth will name"*; Revelation 2:17 — *"a new name written on the stone, which no one knows
but he who receives it."* **The pattern of foreign courts assigning names to exiles is answered by a name
that no power can assign.**

## Significance

**Joseph is the first Israelite in Scripture to be given a foreign name by a foreign state, and the
prototype for every exile who follows.** The thread running from him through Daniel, Esther and Zedekiah
is one of the Bible's steadiest pictures of **what it costs to serve a power that is not yours** — and
Genesis 41:45 is the version in which the cost is invisible, **because everything was given and nothing
was taken.**

**The book's own quiet answer is to keep calling him Joseph.**
