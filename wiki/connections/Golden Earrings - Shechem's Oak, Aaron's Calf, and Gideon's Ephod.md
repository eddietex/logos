# Golden Earrings - Shechem's Oak, Aaron's Calf, and Gideon's Ephod

## Passages involved

- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — **Genesis 35:2–4** — *They gave to Jacob all
  the foreign gods which were in their hands, and the rings which were in their ears; and Jacob hid them
  under the oak which was by Shechem.* (stored WEB)
- [[The Golden Calf]] — **Exodus 32:2–4, 24** — *"Take off the golden rings, which are in the ears of
  your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them to me."* … *"so they gave it to me;
  and I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf."* (stored WEB)
- [[The Freewill Offering]] — **Exodus 35:22** — *brooches, earrings, signet rings, and armlets, all
  jewels of gold; even every man who offered an offering of gold to Yahweh.* (stored WEB)
- [[Gideon's Ephod and Death]] — **Judges 8:24–27** — *"I do have a request, that you would each give
  me the earrings of his plunder." … Gideon made an ephod out of it, and put it in Ophrah, his city. Then
  all Israel played the prostitute with it there* (stored WEB)

## Analysis

Four times in Genesis–Judges, Israel's earrings are gathered, and each time the gathering decides what
the gold will serve.

| Passage | Who gathers | What becomes of it |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 35:4 | Jacob | buried with the foreign gods *under the oak which was by Shechem* |
| Exodus 32:2–4 | Aaron | *a molten calf* — *These are your gods, Israel* |
| Exodus 35:22 | Moses, for Yahweh | the sanctuary, *an offering of gold to Yahweh* |
| Judges 8:24–27 | Gideon | an ephod in Ophrah, *a snare to Gideon, and to his house* |

The Hebrew ties Exodus 32 and Judges 8 most closely. *Nizmê hazzāhāb*, *the golden earrings*, occurs in
the WLC of Genesis–Judges only at Exodus 32:2, 3 and Judges 8:24, 26, with the singular *nezem zāhāb* at
Genesis 24:22. In both, a leader asks the people for the gold, and the people give it at once. In both,
the gold is *thrown*: Aaron *threw it into the fire* (Exodus 32:24), and the men of Israel *threw* the
earrings into the garment (Judges 8:25). Both verbs are hiphil of *šālak* (WLC). In both, a made object
follows: *he … made it a molten calf* (Exodus 32:4), *Gideon made an ephod out of it* (Judges 8:27).

The differences matter as much. Aaron's object is called *gods* by the people (32:4). Gideon's is an
*ephod*, the name of a priestly garment (Exodus 28:6), not an image, and the narrator does not say
Gideon intended it for worship. What it says is the result: *all Israel played the prostitute with it*.
Aaron makes his object at the people's demand (32:1). Gideon asks for the gold himself.

Genesis 35:4 goes the other way. There the earrings are put away with the foreign gods, as a step in
returning to God at Bethel, and buried at Shechem. Judges 8:31 places Gideon's concubine and son in
Shechem, and 8:33 has Israel take Baal Berith as its god, whose house is at Shechem (9:4, not yet
ingested). The narrator does not connect Jacob's buried earrings with any of this. The wiki records the
place, and draws no line from one to the other.

Exodus 35:22 shows the gold given rightly: *every man who offered an offering of gold to Yahweh*, for a
sanctuary built to a commanded pattern. See [[Gold for the Calf and Gold for the Sanctuary]].

## Significance

- **The same gift serves opposite ends.** Earrings are buried as idols' companions, melted into a calf,
  given to the tabernacle, and made into an ephod that becomes a snare. The material does not decide.
- **Judges 8 is Exodus 32's pattern without the people's demand.** That is what makes Gideon's case
  harder to read. He refused to rule (8:23), and then did what Aaron did when the people had no ruler.
- **The thread also answers a question left open in**
  [[They Turned Aside Quickly - The Golden Calf and the Cycle of Judges]]: Gideon's ephod does echo
  Exodus 32 in its Hebrew, in the earrings, the throwing, and the making.
