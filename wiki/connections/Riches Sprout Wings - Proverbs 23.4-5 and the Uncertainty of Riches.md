# Riches Sprout Wings - Proverbs 23.4-5 and the Uncertainty of Riches

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 23:4–5** — [[Thirty Excellent Things]]: *"Don't weary yourself to be rich. In your
  wisdom, show restraint. Why do you set your eyes on that which is not? For it certainly sprouts
  wings like an eagle and flies in the sky."*
- **Proverbs 11:4, 28** — [[A False Balance]]: *"Riches don't profit in the day of wrath"*; *"He who
  trusts in his riches will fall"*.
- **Proverbs 27:24** *(not yet ingested)* — *"for riches are not forever, nor does the crown endure to
  all generations."*
- **Job 1:21** — [[The Wager in Heaven and the Four Messengers]]: *"Naked I came out of my mother's
  womb, and naked shall I return there. Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away."*
- **Job 27:16–19** — [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]: *"Though he heap up silver as the dust … He lies down
  rich, but he shall not do so again. He opens his eyes, and he is not."*
- **Ecclesiastes 5:13–15** *(not yet ingested)* — *"wealth kept by its owner to his harm. Those riches
  perish by misfortune … As he came out of his mother's womb, naked shall he go again"*.
- **Matthew 6:19–21** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Don't lay up treasures for yourselves on the earth, where
  moth and rust consume, and where thieves break through and steal … for where your treasure is, there
  your heart will be also."*
- **1 Timothy 6:9–10, 17** *(not yet ingested)* — *"those who are determined to be rich fall into a
  temptation and a snare"*; the rich told not to *"have their hope set on the uncertainty of riches, but
  on the living God"*.

## Analysis

**Proverbs 23:4–5 warns against the effort, not the possession.** The command is *"Don't weary yourself
to be rich"*, and the reason is the eagle: wealth is *"that which is not"*, and it leaves on its own
wings. The same book promises wealth to humility and the fear of Yahweh (22:4) and does not retract it
here. What it forbids is exhausting a life to acquire something that will not stay. *"In your wisdom,
show restraint"* puts the restraint inside wisdom's own work.

**The thread runs through the wisdom books in three registers.** Proverbs states it as counsel (23:4–5;
27:24) and as a warning about trust (11:4, 28). Job lives it and then argues about it: he loses
everything in a day and blesses the name that gave and took (1:21), and in chapter 27 he describes the
wicked rich man who *"lies down rich, but he shall not do so again"* (27:19) — the same disappearance as
the eagle's, spoken of the man rather than the money. Ecclesiastes turns it into a lament about what
cannot be carried out of the world (5:13–15), using Job's image of the naked return.

**The New Testament keeps both of Proverbs' points.** Matthew 6:19–21 relocates the treasure rather than
abolishing it, and its reason is the moth, rust, and thief that make earthly treasure as unstable as
Proverbs' eagle; its last line moves from the treasure to the heart. 1 Timothy 6:9 warns against the
*determination* to be rich — the same target as Proverbs 23:4's wearying — and 6:17 names the
*"uncertainty of riches"* as the reason the rich should hope in God.

**Tension kept explicit.** Job 27:16–19 is spoken by Job about the wicked; Job 1 shows the same loss
falling on a man the narrator calls blameless. Riches flying away is not evidence of wickedness, which is
the misuse of the observation the wiki tracks under [[Retribution]].

## Significance

The thread gives [[Wealth and Possessions]] its counterweight to the wisdom books' promises of
prosperity: wealth is real and may be a blessing, but it does not stay, and a life spent chasing it is
spent on *"that which is not"*. It also connects [[Hope]] and [[Faith]] to the question of where a person
puts their security. See also [[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]].
