# Leshem Becomes Dan - Joshua 19 and Judges 18

## Passages involved
- [[The Lots of Asher, Naphtali, and Dan]] — Joshua 19:47; Leshem taken and renamed.
- Judges 18:1, 27–29 — the Danites seek an inheritance and take Laish (not yet ingested).
- Judges 1:34 — the Amorites confine Dan to the hill country (not yet ingested).
- [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] — Genesis 14:14; Abram pursues *as far as Dan*, the name used before the renaming.

## Analysis

### The last three lots — Joshua 19:24–48

Joshua closes Dan’s allotment with one sentence that goes outside it: *The border of the
children of Dan went out beyond them; for the children of Dan went up and fought against
Leshem, and took it, and struck it with the edge of the sword, and possessed it, and lived
therein, and called Leshem, Dan, after the name of Dan their forefather* (19:47).

Judges tells the same capture at length, under a different name for the city. The account
begins by saying that the Danites *sought an inheritance to dwell in; for to that day, their
inheritance had not fallen to them among the tribes of Israel* (Judges 18:1), and ends with
them striking Laish, *a people quiet and unsuspecting*, burning it, rebuilding it, and
calling it Dan after their father, *however the name of the city used to be Laish*
(18:27–29). Judges 1:34 adds that the Amorites forced the Danites into the hill country and
would not let them come down to the valley.

| | Joshua 19:47 | Judges 18 |
|---|---|---|
| Old name | Leshem | Laish |
| Action | fought, took, struck with the sword, possessed, lived | struck with the sword, burned, rebuilt, lived |
| New name | Dan, after Dan their forefather | Dan, after Dan their father |
| Reason given | the border *went out beyond them* | their inheritance *had not fallen to them* |

The two accounts agree on the outcome and the naming and differ in tone. Joshua reports the
capture as an extension of the border, in the plain language of capture, with no comment.
Judges sets it just after the Danites take *that which Micah had made, and the priest whom he
had* (18:27), and stresses that the victims were quiet, unsuspecting, and without a
deliverer (18:27–28). The wiki records both without choosing;
Judges will be compared in full when it is ingested. Leshem and Laish are treated as two
names for one city because both texts name it Dan after the tribe’s ancestor; no claim about
the Hebrew forms is made.

## Significance

Dan is the only tribe in Joshua whose lot is supplemented by a conquest of its own choosing,
and it is the tribe whose name becomes the northern limit in *from Dan to Beersheba*. Joshua
presents the move as completion; Judges presents it as a tribe still looking for an
inheritance in the days when *there was no king in Israel* (Judges 18:1). See
[[Dan (city)]] and [[Dan (person)]].
