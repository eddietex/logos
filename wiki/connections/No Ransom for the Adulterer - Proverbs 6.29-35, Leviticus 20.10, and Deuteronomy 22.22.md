# No Ransom for the Adulterer - Proverbs 6.29-35, Leviticus 20.10, and Deuteronomy 22.22

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 6:29–35** — [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]: *"So is he who goes in to his neighbor's
  wife. Whoever touches her will not be unpunished … He who commits adultery with a woman is void of
  understanding … For jealousy arouses the fury of the husband. He won't spare in the day of
  vengeance. He won't regard any ransom, neither will he rest content, though you give many gifts."*
- **Leviticus 20:10** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"The man who commits adultery with another
  man's wife, even he who commits adultery with his neighbor's wife, the adulterer and the
  adulteress shall surely be put to death."*
- **Deuteronomy 22:22** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"If a man is found lying with a woman
  married to a husband, then they shall both die … So you shall remove the evil from Israel."*
- **Exodus 20:14** — [[The Ten Commandments]]: *"You shall not commit adultery."*
- **Exodus 21:30** — [[Laws of Violence and Injury]]: *"If a ransom is laid on him, then he shall give
  for the redemption of his life whatever is laid on him."*
- **Numbers 35:31** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"Moreover you shall take no ransom for the
  life of a murderer who is guilty of death; but he shall surely be put to death."*
- **Exodus 23:8** — [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]]: *"You shall take no bribe, for a bribe blinds
  those who have sight and perverts the words of the righteous."*
- **Numbers 5:14** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"the spirit of jealousy comes on him, and he
  is jealous of his wife"*
- **Song of Solomon 8:6** — [[Love Is Strong as Death]] — *"for love is strong as death. Jealousy
  is as cruel as Sheol. Its flashes are flashes of fire, a very flame of Yahweh."*
- **Proverbs 7:19–20** — [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]: *"For my husband isn't at home. He has gone on a long
  journey … He will come home at the full moon."* The woman of the next lecture counts on the
  husband's absence, which is the only answer she gives to 6:34–35

## Analysis

**Shared vocabulary.** Proverbs 6:29–35 uses the law's words for the act and its parties. The
adulterer is *nōʾēp̄* (6:32), the participle Leviticus 20:10 uses (*mōṯ-yûmaṯ hannōʾēp̄
wəhannōʾāp̄eṯ*), from the verb of the commandment, *lōʾ ṯinʾāp̄* (Exodus 20:14; MT 20:13). The woman is
*ʾēšeṯ rēʿēhû*, *his neighbour's wife* (6:29), the phrase Leviticus 20:10 uses for her. The payment
refused is *kōp̄er* (6:35), the law's *ransom*: allowed for a life in Exodus 21:30, forbidden for a
murderer in Numbers 35:31 (*wəlōʾ-ṯiqḥû ḵōp̄er*). And the gifts the husband will not accept are
*šōḥaḏ* (6:35), the *bribe* Exodus 23:8 forbids (*wəšōḥaḏ lōʾ ṯiqqāḥ*). All of this Hebrew is read
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

**Different pictures of the consequence.** The law sets a sentence: death for both parties, by the
community (*"So you shall remove the evil from Israel"*, Deuteronomy 22:22). Proverbs names no court,
no witnesses, and no death sentence. Its consequences are wounds, dishonour, and a reproach that
*"will not be wiped away"* (6:33), and its enforcer is the husband — his jealousy (*qinʾâ*), his
fury, his *"day of vengeance"* (*yôm nāqām*, 6:34). **The wiki does not resolve how the two relate,
and records three readings the texts leave open:**

1. **Proverbs assumes the law.** The husband's refusal of ransom is a refusal to settle a capital
   case privately, which is what Numbers 35:31 forbids for murder. On this reading his vengeance
   is the law's sentence, pursued by the injured party.
2. **Proverbs describes life where the law was not enforced.** A case could be settled, hushed up,
   or never brought; the danger the son faces is then not the court but the man he wronged, who
   cannot be bought.
3. **Proverbs argues on the wisdom tradition's own ground.** Like [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]], the
   lecture appeals to consequence and reputation, not statute, and is not making a claim about legal
   procedure at all.

None of these makes Proverbs contradict the law. What all three agree on is the point Proverbs
presses: **there is no price for this.** The thief of 6:30–31 can pay, even sevenfold; the adulterer
has nothing the husband will take.

**The ransom idea.** Exodus 21:30 shows that *kōp̄er* could stand in for a forfeited life: an ox owner
whose negligence killed may pay what is laid on him. Numbers 35:31 excludes the murderer from it.
Proverbs 6:35 puts the adulterer, in the husband's eyes, on the murderer's side of that line. The
wiki's thread [[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]] follows *kōp̄er* toward
the ransom that God himself provides; this passage is the case where a human ransom is offered and
refused.

**Jealousy.** *Qinʾâ*, the husband's passion (6:34), is the word of the jealousy ordeal (*"the spirit of
jealousy"*, *rûaḥ-qinʾâ*, Numbers 5:14) and of Song of Solomon 8:6, where it stands beside love: *"love is strong
as death. Jealousy is as cruel as Sheol. Its flashes are flashes of fire"*. In Proverbs the fire of
6:27 comes back as this jealousy. See [[Envy]], where *qānāʾ* covers both envy and jealousy, and
where it becomes a name for God (Exodus 34:14).

**Song 8:5–7 now ingested.** [[Love Is Strong as Death]]:
The now-ingested Song also rejects the purchase of love (8:7). Proverbs describes
a husband refusing compensation for adultery; the Song describes love beyond price.
Their juxtaposition does not turn the Song into a threat against a rival.

## Significance

The passage shows the wisdom tradition and the law describing one wrong in two registers: the law
as a sentence, Proverbs as a consequence nobody escapes. Both locate adultery where no payment can
reach. See [[Sexual Sin]], [[Marriage]], [[Vengeance]], [[Redemption]], and [[Law and Obedience]].
