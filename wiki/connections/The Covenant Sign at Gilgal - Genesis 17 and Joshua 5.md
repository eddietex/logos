# The Covenant Sign at Gilgal - Genesis 17 and Joshua 5

## Passages involved

- [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]] — Genesis 17:8–14, 23–27.
- [[The Second Circumcision at Gilgal]] — Joshua 5:1–9.

## Analysis

Genesis 17 joins the promise of Canaan with circumcision as the sign of the covenant.
Abraham circumcises his household in response to the command. Joshua 5 brings the wilderness-born
children into observance after the nation has crossed into that land. Both accounts join a
divine command to a concrete act involving the male community; Joshua does not announce a
different sign or new covenant terms.

The continuity must not erase the break. Joshua 5:5 says the departing generation was
circumcised, and 5:6 says its men of war died because they did not listen to Yahweh. Possession
of the sign did not prevent judgment. Their children receive the sign that was omitted on the
journey. The second occasion concerns a new generation, not a second operation on the same men.

Genesis 17 prescribes the eighth day for future generations. Joshua 5 describes a collective
remedy for an existing omission, not an abolition of that age requirement. Neither passage
explains why the wilderness omission occurred.

Sources: stored WEB Genesis 17 and Joshua 4:19–5:9. These are thematic and narrative comparisons,
not a claim of verbal dependence verified against a Hebrew edition.

## Significance

The promised land and inherited covenant obligation meet at Gilgal. National continuity
includes both judgment on disobedience and renewal among the children. See [[Circumcision]],
[[Covenant]], and [[Land and Sojourning]].
