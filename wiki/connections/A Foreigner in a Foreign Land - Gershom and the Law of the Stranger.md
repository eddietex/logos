# A Foreigner in a Foreign Land - Gershom and the Law of the Stranger

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 15:13** — *"Know for sure that your offspring will live as foreigners in a land that is
  not theirs, and will serve them."* — [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]
- **Genesis 41:51–52** — Joseph names his sons in exile: Manasseh, *"God has made me forget all my
  toil, and all my father's house"*; Ephraim, *"God has made me fruitful in the land of my
  affliction"* — [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Exodus 2:22; 18:3** — *"He named him Gershom, for he said, 'I have lived as a foreigner in a
  foreign land'"* — [[Moses Flees to Midian]]
- **Exodus 22:21** — *"You shall not wrong an alien or oppress him, for you were aliens in the land of
  Egypt."*
- **Exodus 23:9** — *"You shall not oppress an alien, for you know the heart of an alien, since you
  were aliens in the land of Egypt."*
- **Leviticus 19:34** — *"The stranger who lives as a foreigner with you shall be to you as the
  native-born among you, and you shall love him as yourself; for you lived as foreigners in the land
  of Egypt."*
- **Deuteronomy 10:19** — *"Therefore love the foreigner; for you were foreigners in the land of
  Egypt."*
- **Acts 7:29** — *"Moses fled at this saying, and became a stranger in the land of Midian, where he
  became the father of two sons."*
- **Exodus 12:48–49** — *"When a stranger shall live as a foreigner with you, and will keep the Passover
  to Yahweh … he shall be as one who is born in the land … One law shall be to him who is born at home,
  and to the stranger who lives as a foreigner among you."* **The first law about the *gēr*, given before
  the ethic of 22:21** — [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]],
  [[One Law for the Native and the Stranger]]

## Analysis

**One noun, *gēr*, runs from a prophecy to a child's name to a law.**

***Gēr*** is a resident alien — someone living lawfully among a people not his own, without land or
kin to protect him. **God's first word about Israel's future in Egypt uses it**: Abram's offspring
will be *gēr* in a land not theirs (Genesis 15:13). Exodus 1 then describes what that meant — service
and affliction — without using the word at all.

**Moses is the first of Abraham's offspring to say it of himself, and he says it in Midian.**
*Gēr hāyîtî bəʾereṣ nokriyyâ*, *I have been a sojourner in a foreign land*, explains the name
*Gēršōm*, heard as *gēr šām*, *a sojourner there*. **The speech does not say which land is foreign.**
Midian, where he is a fugitive married into a priest's household, is the obvious reading. But Moses
was raised in Pharaoh's house and is called *"an Egyptian"* by the women he rescues (2:19), and
Egypt is the land Genesis 15:13 called *not theirs*: **the naming can be heard as the exile's verdict
on both countries at once.** The wiki records the two readings and does not choose.

**Joseph had named his sons in exile before him**, and the comparison is pointed.

| | Joseph (Genesis 41:51–52) | Moses (Exodus 2:22; 18:4) |
|---|---|---|
| first son | Manasseh — *God has made me forget* my father's house | Gershom — *a sojourner there* |
| second son | Ephraim — *God has made me fruitful* in the land of my affliction | Eliezer — *My father's God was my help* (named at 18:4) |
| God in the name | in both speeches | only in the second, after the deliverance |
| attitude to exile | forgetting home; flourishing abroad | not belonging |

**Joseph's names settle into Egypt; Moses' first name refuses to settle into Midian**, though he
stays forty years. And Joseph was in Egypt to save his family by staying; Moses is in Midian waiting,
though he does not know it, to be sent back.

**Then the word becomes the law, and the law gives the reason in the first person plural.** Exodus
22:21 and 23:9 forbid oppressing the *gēr* because *"you were aliens in the land of Egypt"*;
23:9 adds *"you know the heart of an alien"* — ***yədaʿtem ʾet-nepeš haggēr***, *you know the
life, the soul, of the sojourner*. **Leviticus 19:34 and Deuteronomy 10:19 turn the prohibition into
a command to love.** The memory of being *gēr* is made the ground of Israel's treatment of the *gēr*
— **and the lawgiver's own firstborn carried the word in his name.**

**The New Testament keeps the word as Moses' biography.** Stephen: Moses *"became a stranger in the
land of Midian"* (Acts 7:29) — *paroikos*, the Septuagint's regular word for *gēr*, and the word
Hebrews 11:9 and 1 Peter 2:11 use of the patriarchs and the church. See
[[A Stranger and a Foreigner - The Sojourner's Confession]].

## Significance

- **It connects a personal confession to a national memory.** Abraham called himself *gēr* to buy a
  grave (Genesis 23:4); Moses calls himself *gēr* to name a son; Israel is told to remember that it
  was *gēr* every time it meets one. The word moves from an individual's status to a people's ethic.
- **It grounds the law of the stranger in experience, not principle.** *"You know the heart of an
  alien"* is an appeal to something Israel lived, and Gershom's name is that experience carried by
  one family into the wilderness.
- **It marks Moses as Abraham's offspring in the very place he looks least like it** — married to a
  Midianite, called an Egyptian, keeping another man's sheep — by the word God used to Abram.
