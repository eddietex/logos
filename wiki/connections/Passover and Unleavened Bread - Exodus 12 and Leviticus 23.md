# Passover and Unleavened Bread - Exodus 12 and Leviticus 23

## Passages involved

- [[The Passover]] — Exodus 12:1–28, especially 6, 15–20.
- [[Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread]] — Leviticus 23:1–8.

## Analysis

Exodus 12 places the Passover animal's slaughter on the fourteenth evening and describes
seven days of unleavened bread, from the fourteenth evening to the twenty-first evening
(6, 15, 18). Leviticus 23:5–6 dates Passover to the fourteenth evening and the feast of
Unleavened Bread to the fifteenth. These are corresponding evening and feast-day markers,
not grounds for silently changing either account's dates.

Both accounts appoint holy convocations on the first and seventh days. Exodus 12:16 bans
work with an explicit exception for what each person must eat; Leviticus 23:7–8 forbids
regular work without restating that exception. The difference in wording is preserved.
Neither passage supplies a complete classification of permitted festival tasks.

The settings and emphases differ. Exodus gives household blood, a departure meal, removal
of yeast, sanctions, and remembered deliverance. Leviticus gives a concise calendar and
adds the seven-day requirement for offerings made by fire (23:8), without specifying the
animals or quantities here. It does not retell the death of the firstborn or command
another application of blood to doorposts.

Source: both passages checked in the repository's raw WEB Exodus and Leviticus texts,
2026-10-30. No Hebrew equivalence or historical theory of composition is asserted.

## Significance

[[Festivals and First Fruits]] brings remembered deliverance into recurring sacred time.
The parallel shows continuity without reducing two differently detailed laws to identical
instructions. Read the festival work restrictions beside [[Sabbath]], not as proof that
all sacred days carry exactly the same rules.
