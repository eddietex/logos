## Analysis

### Joshua 2 now ingested

The now-ingested chapter makes the dependence of the final report on Rahab’s speech especially clear (9, 24). The mission is secret and its report goes to Joshua (1, 23); an intention to prevent a second public panic remains an inference. Rahab speaks a generation after Numbers 13, so her testimony reverses its literary perspective without proving what the earlier inhabitants actually thought at that earlier date.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Joshua 2:1–24; comparisons to stored Exodus and Numbers.


Joshua is one of the two spies of Kadesh who were faithful, and when he sends spies himself, he
sends two.

| | Numbers 13 | Joshua 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Sent by | Moses, at Yahweh's word | Joshua, *secretly* |
| How many | twelve, all named | two, unnamed |
| Reporting to | the whole congregation (26) | Joshua (23) |
| Word | *tûr* | *məraggəlîm* (WLC) |
| Fruit shown | a cluster on a pole | none; a woman's testimony |
| Fear | *we were … as grasshoppers* | *all the inhabitants of the land melt away before us* (2:24) |
| Result | refusal, and forty years | the crossing |

**The report answers the old one.** Joshua 2:24 begins *Truly Yahweh has delivered all the land into
our hands*, naming Yahweh as the first report did not. The fear runs the other way, in the land that
the spies said looked down on them. The contrast is the wiki's reading, though the shared situation
is plain.

**One difference is usually drawn from the numbers.** A private report to one commander could not
turn a congregation. Joshua 2 does not say that was the reason, so it remains an inference.

## Significance

- The later scene repairs the earlier one with the same office and opposite speech.
- It lets the reader test the sentence *so we were in their sight*, because Rahab says what was in
  fact in their sight.
