# A Multitude of Counselors - Proverbs 11.14 and Exodus 18

## Passages involved

- [[Wine Is a Mocker]] — Proverbs 20:5, 18, 19, 24.

- [[Whoever Loves Correction]] — Proverbs 12:15.

- [[A False Balance]] — Proverbs 11:9–14.
- [[Jethro's Counsel]] — Exodus 18:13–26.
- [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]] — Proverbs 24:5–6.

## Analysis

Proverbs 11:14 contrasts national collapse without wise guidance with victory in a multitude of
counselors. Its surrounding sayings place speech in public life: a mouth can destroy a neighbor or a
city, and wisdom can keep a confidence (11:9–13). The number of speakers is not an independent
guarantee of truth; the positive setting is wise guidance.

Exodus 18 narrates a related problem. Moses judges alone while people wait, and Jethro warns that
both Moses and the people will wear out. His counsel joins teaching and representation before God
with distributing judgment among capable, truthful men who fear God and hate unjust gain. Moses
accepts the advice and shares the load (18:24–26).

The analogy is limited but useful. Proverbs commends multiple counselors; Exodus describes advice
from one counselor that produces multiple judges. They are not identical administrative
arrangements, and the proverb is not a quotation of Jethro’s speech. Both resist making the
community dependent on one person’s unsupported judgment and capacity.

Source: Proverbs 11:9–14 and Exodus 18:13–26 checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### A hearer willing to listen — Proverbs 12:15

[[Whoever Loves Correction]] adds the individual counterpart to public counsel: a fool finds his own
way right, while the wise person listens. The contrast does not say that every counselor is right;
Proverbs 12:5 already describes deceitful advice. It identifies receptivity to sound correction as
part of wisdom. Proverbs 11:14 concerns a community’s guidance, while 12:15 concerns the hearer’s
disposition; neither makes numbers alone a test of truth. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 12:5, 15.

### Proverbs 20 in context

Proverbs 20:18 makes advice the means by which plans are established and wise guidance the
prerequisite for war. Verse 5 pictures understanding as drawing deep counsel from a person's
heart, while verse 19 warns against the indiscreet revealer of secrets. Listening well and
choosing trustworthy advisers belong together. Verse 24 prevents reading counsel as control
over the whole course of events. This extends the comparison with Proverbs 11:13–14 and Exodus
18; it does not make the king or council in Proverbs a named historical institution.

### Wisdom stronger than strength — Proverbs 24:5–6

*"A wise man has great power … for by wise guidance you wage your war; and victory is in many
advisors."* In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26) 24:6b, *ûṯəšûʿāh bərōḇ
yôʿēṣ*, is identical to 11:14b, and 24:6a varies 20:18b's *ûḇəṯaḥbulôṯ ʿăśēh milḥāmāh*. The saying
appears three times in the book; this time it serves an argument about power: counsel, not force,
wins.

## Significance

Wisdom has a public and cooperative dimension. Keeping confidences and listening to counsel belong
together as disciplines of trustworthy speech. See [[Wisdom]], [[Language and Speech]], and
[[City and Civilization]].
