# Sabbath and New Moon - Numbers 28, Ezekiel 46, Isaiah 66, and Colossians 2

## Passages involved

- **Numbers 10:10** — trumpets blown *"in the beginnings of your months"* over the burnt offerings —
  [[The Two Silver Trumpets]].
- **Numbers 28:9–15** — two lambs every Sabbath; two bulls, one ram, seven lambs, and a goat at each
  month's beginning — [[Daily, Sabbath, and New Moon Offerings]].
- **Ezekiel 46:1–7** — the inner east gate opened *"on the Sabbath day … and on the day of the new
  moon"*; the prince's Sabbath offering *"six lambs without defect and a ram without defect"*, and at
  the new moon *"a young bull without defect, and six lambs, and a ram"* (not yet ingested).
- **Isaiah 1:13–14** — *"new moons, Sabbaths, and convocations: I can’t stand evil assemblies. My soul
  hates your New Moons and your appointed feasts"* (not yet ingested).
- **Isaiah 66:23** — *"from one new moon to another, and from one Sabbath to another, all flesh will
  come to worship before me"* (not yet ingested).
- **Amos 8:5** — traders ask, *"When will the new moon be gone, that we may sell grain? And the
  Sabbath, that we may market wheat"* (not yet ingested).
- **Colossians 2:16–17** — *"a feast day or a new moon or a Sabbath day, which are a shadow of the
  things to come; but the body is Christ’s"* (not yet ingested).

(Numbers from stored WEB; the others verified at bible-api.com, `translation=web`, 2026-11-05.)

## Analysis

**Numbers sets the pair.** Before Numbers 28, the stored Torah has the Sabbath as a day of rest and
a sign, and the beginnings of months only as a time for trumpets (Numbers 10:10). Numbers 28:9–15 gives
both days their own public offerings, each added to the daily lambs. From here on the two days travel
together.

**Ezekiel keeps the pair and changes the numbers.** In Ezekiel 46 the prince provides six lambs and a
ram on the Sabbath, where Numbers has two lambs; at the new moon he provides one bull, six lambs, and a
ram, where Numbers has two bulls, one ram, seven lambs, and a goat. The meal offerings differ as well
(see [[Measures of Flour, Oil, and Wine - Exodus 29, Numbers 15, Numbers 28, and Ezekiel 46]]).
Ezekiel's text does not mention Numbers, and the wiki does not resolve the difference; it is flagged
here as a real discrepancy between the two prescriptions.

**The prophets use the pair in both directions.** Isaiah 1 lists the new moons and Sabbaths among
the observances God cannot bear when offered with evil; Amos 8:5 shows traders impatient for the
two days to end so that they can cheat. Isaiah 66:23 uses the same pair to picture worship by *all
flesh*, from new moon to new moon and Sabbath to Sabbath.

**Colossians names the pair as shadow.** Paul groups feast, new moon, and Sabbath and calls them *a
shadow of the things to come*, with the body belonging to Christ. He does not cite Numbers; the link is
the recurring triad, which Numbers 28–29 is the first in the wiki's sequence to furnish with offerings.

## Significance

The pairing that begins as an offering schedule becomes a shorthand for Israel's whole calendar of
worship — condemned when emptied, extended to the nations in Isaiah's vision, and relativized in
Colossians. See [[Sabbath]] and [[Festivals and First Fruits]].
