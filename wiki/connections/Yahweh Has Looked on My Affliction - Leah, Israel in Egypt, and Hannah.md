## Analysis

**The phrase.** *Rāʾâ*, *see*, with *ʿŏnî*, *affliction*, as its object, and God as subject. In the WLC
files of Genesis through Ruth and 1 Samuel, *ʿŏnî* occurs ten times; the six verses above are the ones
where it stands in the same verse as *rāʾâ*, and in each of them God is the one who sees, or is asked to.
The other four occurrences do not have the verb: Hagar's *Yahweh has heard your affliction* (Genesis
16:11), Joseph's *the land of my affliction* (41:52), *the affliction of Egypt* (Exodus 3:17), and the
*bread of affliction* (Deuteronomy 16:3).

**Two women and a people.** The pairs are of different sizes. Leah, the unloved wife, names her first son
for God's seeing (*rāʾâ … bəʿonyî*, Genesis 29:32, WLC) — and the son's name, *Rəʾûḇēn*, begins with the
verb. Hannah, the loved but barren wife, asks for the same seeing with the infinitive absolute, *rāʾōh
tirʾeh*, *if you will indeed look* (1 Samuel 1:11, WLC). Exodus 3:7 uses the same doubled form of a nation,
*rāʾōh rāʾîṯî*, *I have surely seen* (WLC). Hannah's request takes the shape of God's word at the bush.

**Seeing, then remembering, then a child.** Leah's seeing is announced as fulfilled, with a son already in
her arms. Hannah's is asked for first, with *remember me, and not forget* (1:11), and answered when
*Yahweh remembered her* (1:19). Exodus pairs the two verbs too: God *remembered his covenant* and *saw the children of
Israel* (2:24–25), and then says *I have surely seen* (3:7). The sequence in the Hannah story is the sequence of the exodus,
compressed into one woman's year. That last sentence is a reading.

**The rival wife beside each.** Leah is *hated* while Rachel is loved and barren (Genesis 29:31); Hannah is
loved and barren while Peninnah bears. The two stories reverse which wife says the words.

## Significance

The phrase gives a private grief the vocabulary of the exodus and of Israel's creed: the confession at
the offering of first fruits (Deuteronomy 26:5–10) tells the nation's story with *saw our affliction*, and
Hannah prays in the same words for a firstborn she will give to Yahweh. See [[Seeing and Being Seen]],
[[The Barren Matriarchs]], and [[Affliction and Flight - Hagar and the Exodus]].
