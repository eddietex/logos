# The Day of the Locust - Exodus 10 and Joel 1-2

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 10:5** — *"They shall eat the residue of that which has escaped, which remains to you from
  the hail"* — [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- **Exodus 10:14** — *"Before them there were no such locusts as they, nor will there ever be again."*
  — [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- **Exodus 10:15, 22** — *"the land was darkened"*; *"a thick darkness"*, *ḥōšek-ʾăpēlâ* —
  [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- **Joel 1:4** — *"What the swarming locust has left, the great locust has eaten. What the great

- **Joel 2:25** — *"I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten, the great

- **Deuteronomy 28:38** — *"for the locust will consume it"* (WEB) —

## Analysis

**Joel describes a locust plague on Judah in the words of the eighth and ninth plagues on Egypt.** The
links are in the Hebrew, checked in the WLC.

**1. *"There has never been the like."*** Exodus 10:14 says of the locusts, *ləpānāyw lōʾ-hāyâ kēn
ʾarbeh kāmōhû wəʾaḥărāyw lōʾ yihyeh-kēn* — *before them there was none like it, and after them there
will not be so*. Joel 2:2 says of the army, *kāmōhû lōʾ nihyâ min-hāʿôlām wəʾaḥărāyw lōʾ yôsēp* —
*like it there has not been from of old, and after it there will not be again*. **Same pronoun
*kāmōhû*, same *before* and *after*.** The two superlatives cannot both be literally true of different
swarms; Joel's seems to cite Exodus's, and the reuse is the point.

**2. *Darkness and gloom.*** The ninth plague is *ḥōšek-ʾăpēlâ* (Exodus 10:22). Joel's day is *yôm
ḥōšek waʾăpēlâ* (Joel 2:2), the same two nouns. In the Torah *ʾăpēlâ* occurs only at Exodus 10:22 and
Deuteronomy 28:29 (WLC lemma search). **Joel puts into one verse what Exodus spread over two plagues.**
In Exodus the locusts themselves first darken the land (*wattehšak hāʾāreṣ*, 10:15); in Joel the
heavens darken before the army (2:10).

**3. *What is left.*** Exodus 10:5 has the locusts eat *yeter happəlēṭâ*, *the remainder of what
escaped* the hail. Joel 1:4 is built on *yeter* three times over: *the remainder of the cutting locust,
the swarming locust ate*, and so down the list (WLC). **Both describe a plague that comes after another
and takes what the first left.**

**4. The word *ʾarbeh*.** It is the locust of Exodus 10 (seven times), and one of Joel's four locust
words (1:4; 2:25). It also appears in Deuteronomy's curse, *"the locust will consume it"* (28:38).

**What changes is the target.** In Exodus the locusts and the darkness fall on Egypt, while *"all the
children of Israel had light in their dwellings"* (10:23). **In Joel they fall on Judah.** Deuteronomy
28 had warned that this could happen; see
[[The Diseases of Egypt - The Plagues in Deuteronomy 28]]. And in Joel, as in Exodus, the plague is
God's own: Exodus says Yahweh *"brought"* the east wind (10:13); Joel calls the locusts *"my great army,
which I sent among you"* (2:25).

**What does not change is the possibility of reversal.** Pharaoh asks Moses to pray, and the locusts
are taken away (Exodus 10:16–19). Joel calls Judah to return, and God promises, *"I will restore to you
the years that the swarming locust has eaten"* (2:25). **Exodus ends the locusts with a wind; Joel
ends them with a restoration.** Joel is not yet ingested, so the call to return (Joel 2:12–13, *"turn to me with all your heart"*, WEB) is
named here by reference only.

## Significance

- **It shows the plagues becoming a vocabulary.** By Joel, *locust*, *darkness*, and *never the like*
  are a way of saying *the day of Yahweh*. See [[Judgment]] and [[Light and Darkness]].
- **It turns Exodus's distinction around.** The plagues once marked the difference between Egypt and
  Israel; in Joel they fall on Israel. See [[Call and Election]].
- **It carries the darkness forward.** Joel's darkened sun and moon (2:10, 31) are taken up later in
  the New Testament; that line is not traced here until those books are ingested.
- **No claim is made about the date of Joel or the direction of borrowing** beyond what the wording
  shows: the phrase-level match in Joel 2:2 reads as a citation of Exodus 10:14, 22.
