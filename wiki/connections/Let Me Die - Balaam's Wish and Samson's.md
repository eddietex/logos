# Let Me Die - Balaam's Wish and Samson's

## Passages involved
- [[Balaam's First Two Oracles]] — **Numbers 23:10** — *Who can count the dust of Jacob, or count the fourth
  part of Israel? Let me die the death of the righteous! Let my last end be like his!* (stored WEB)
- [[The War Against Midian]] — **Numbers 31:8** — *They also killed Balaam the son of Beor with the sword.*
  (stored WEB)
- [[The Death of Samson]] — **Judges 16:30** — *Samson said, "Let me die with the Philistines!" He bowed
  himself with all his might; and the house fell on the lords, and on all the people who were therein.*
  (stored WEB)

## Analysis

The two wishes begin with the same two Hebrew words: *tāmōt napšî*, *let my soul die* (Numbers 23:10, WLC,
defective spelling), and *tāmôt napšî* (Judges 16:30, WLC, full spelling). In the WLC files of Genesis
through Judges and 1 Samuel, the pair occurs only in these two verses. The WEB renders both *Let me die*.

| | Balaam (Numbers 23:10) | Samson (Judges 16:30) |
|---|---|---|
| the speaker | a foreign seer hired to curse Israel | Israel's judge, blind and captive |
| the wish | to die *the death of the righteous*, *môt yəšārîm* | to die *with the Philistines*, *ʿim-pəlištîm* |
| who he would die with | Israel, whom he was paid to curse | Israel's enemies, who paid to take him |
| how he dies | *with the sword* among Midian's slain (Numbers 31:8) | in the fallen house, among the Philistines (Judges 16:30) |

Each man asks to die among a people. Balaam names Israel and is killed among Israel's enemies. Samson names
Israel's enemies and gets exactly that. The wish of the outsider is not granted in the form he asked; the
wish of the judge is granted to the letter.

The texts do not compare the two men, and neither verse alludes to the other. The parallel rests on the
shared phrase and on what follows each.

## Significance

The phrase is a wish about company in death, and both narratives answer it with the company each man
actually kept. Balaam's *last end*, *ʾaḥărîtî* (23:10), is told seven chapters later in a single clause
among the kings of Midian. Samson's is told in the verse that holds the wish. Neither narrator comments.
Whether Balaam's death answers his wish ironically, or simply records it, the text does not say, and the
wiki does not decide. See [[Balaam]] and [[Samson]].
