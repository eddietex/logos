# According to His Work - Proverbs 24.12, Psalm 62.12, and Romans 2.6

## Passages involved

- [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]] — **Proverbs 24:12** — *"If you say, 'Behold, we didn't know
  this'; doesn't he who weighs the hearts consider it? He who keeps your soul, doesn't he know it?
  Shall he not render to every man according to his work?"*
- [[Elihu on God's Justice]] — **Job 34:11** — *"For the work of a man he will render to him, and
  cause every man to find according to his ways."*

- **Romans 2:6** — *"who 'will pay back to everyone according to their works:'"* (WEB verified at

## Analysis

**One sentence, three speakers before Paul.** The formula *render to each according to his work* is
a question in Proverbs, an assertion in Elihu's mouth, and a confession in a psalm. In Proverbs
24:12 it is the third of three rhetorical questions that answer a bystander's excuse — *"we didn't
know"* — by appeal to a God who weighs hearts and keeps souls. Its work there is to close an exit:
the plea of ignorance is itself one of the *works* to be weighed. In Job 34:11 Elihu states it as a
defence of God's justice against Job's complaint. In Psalm 62:12 it is joined, surprisingly, to
*"loving kindness"*: the psalmist's confidence in being repaid is part of his trust, not his fear.

**The wording differs in Hebrew.** In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26),
Proverbs 24:12 has *wəhēšîḇ ləʾāḏām kəp̄ŏʿŏlô* (*he returns to a man according to his deed*); Psalm
62:13 (English 62:12) has *təšallēm ləʾîš kəmaʿăśēhû* (*you repay a man according to his work*); Job
34:11 has *pōʿal ʾāḏām yəšallem-lô* (*the deed of a man he repays to him*). Proverbs and Job share
the noun root *pʿl*; the psalm and Job share the verb *šillēm*. The three are a shared idea with
varying wording, not one fixed quotation.

**Romans 2:6 marks its words as a quotation** (the WEB prints them in quotation marks), and commentators
usually identify the source as Psalm 62:12 and Proverbs 24:12 together. Which Greek wording Paul
follows was not checked in this pass against an edition of the Septuagint or of Romans, so the wiki
does not say which verse he is closer to. Matthew 16:27 applies the same judgment to the returning
Son of Man; whether it is meant as a quotation of either verse is not stated by the text.

**Proverbs uses the phrase twice, in opposite directions.** Seventeen verses after 24:12 gives the
formula to God, 24:29 forbids a man to say it of himself: *"I will render to the man according to
his work."* The Hebrew of the two clauses is the same phrase (*hēšîḇ … kəp̄ŏʿŏlô*; same edition).
What is justice in God's hand is revenge in the son's mouth. 20:22 says the same thing without the
formula: *"Don't say, 'I will pay back evil.' Wait for Yahweh, and he will save you."*

## Significance

The formula is the plainest statement of [[Retribution]] in Scripture, and the places it appears
keep it from being simple. Proverbs 24:12 makes it the reason a bystander cannot plead ignorance;
24:29 makes it a prerogative a man may not seize; Psalm 62 grounds it in loving kindness; and Job
puts it in the mouth of Elihu, whose general doctrine the book's ending neither endorses nor
condemns by name (see [[Elihu on God's Justice]]). Romans 2:6–11 then applies it to Jew and Gentile
alike as the ground of impartial judgment. See also [[Judgment]] and [[God]].
