# Where the Slain Are - Job 39.30 and Matthew 24.28

## Passages involved

- **Job 39:27–30** — *"Is it at your command that the eagle mounts up, and makes his nest on high? … From
  there he spies out the prey. His eyes see it afar off. His young ones also suck up blood. Where the slain
  are, there he is."* — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Matthew 24:28** — *"For wherever the carcass is, that is where the vultures gather together."*
- **Luke 17:37** — *"They, answering, asked him, 'Where, Lord?' He said to them, 'Where the body is, there
  will the vultures also be gathered together.'"*
- **Proverbs 30:17** — [[Four Things]] — *"the ravens of the valley shall pick it out, the young eagles shall eat it"*
- **Revelation 19:17–18** — the birds summoned to *"the great supper of God"*

## Analysis

**Job 39:30 ends God's first speech on carrion.** *Waʾăšer ḥălālîm šām hûʾ* — *where the slain are, there
he is*. The bird is the *nešer*, which covers both eagle and griffon vulture; the WEB's *eagle* at 39:27 and
the Gospels' *vultures* are the same kind of bird seen from two sides, the soaring hunter and the
scavenger. Its portrait is admiring: it nests on the cliff by God's command, sees from afar, and feeds its
young with blood. The speech that began with stars singing at the world's foundation (38:7) closes with
death inside the order God is proud of.

**Jesus' saying has the same shape and the same bird.** *"For wherever the carcass is, that is where the
vultures gather together"* (Matthew 24:28); in Luke it answers the disciples' *"Where, Lord?"* (17:37). It
is not a quotation of Job — the wording differs, and the saying reads as a proverb in its own right — but
it is the same proverb: the birds are always where the dead are, and need no telling. In Matthew it
follows the warning against false reports of the Messiah's coming *"in the wilderness"* or *"in the inner
rooms"* (24:26): the coming will be as unmistakable as lightning and as certain as vultures
over a body.

**The direction of use is reversed.** In Job the line is about God's care for a creature: even the
eagle's young, feeding on the dead, are fed by God's arrangement. In the Gospels it is about judgment:
where the body is, the birds come, and no one will need to be told where the end has arrived. Revelation
19:17–18 takes it to its end, the birds summoned to eat *"the flesh of kings, the flesh of captains"*.
Whether Jesus' hearers heard Job in the saying cannot be shown; that the proverb existed in Israel's
wisdom before the Gospels, in the mouth of God, can.

### The mocker's eye — Proverbs 30:17

With Proverbs 30 ingested, the eagle's feeding appears in a curse: the eye that mocks a father and
scorns a mother's obedience will be picked out by *"the ravens of the valley"* and eaten by *"the
young eagles"*. Job 39:30 describes the eagle's young feeding where the slain are as a fact of
creation; Proverbs 30:17 makes the same sight a consequence of contempt. See [[Four Things]].

## Significance

- It is one of the whirlwind's lines that passed into proverb, as the raven did
  ([[Who Provides for the Raven - Job 38.41, Psalm 147.9, and Luke 12.24]]).
- It marks how far Job 38–39 goes in refusing to sentimentalize creation: the speech that answers a man who
  wanted to die ends with an image of death as part of what God sustains. See [[Life and Death]] and
  [[Creation]].
