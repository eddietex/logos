# Who Is This Coming Up from the Wilderness - Song of Solomon 3.6, 6.10, and 8.5

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 3:6** — [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]]: *"Who is this who comes up from the
  wilderness like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all spices of the
  merchant?"*
- **Song of Solomon 6:10** *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)* — *"Who is she who looks out as the
  morning, beautiful as the moon, clear as the sun, and awesome as an army with banners?"*
- **Song of Solomon 8:5** *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)* — *"Who is this who comes up from the
  wilderness, leaning on her beloved?"*

Hebrew of all three read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

## Analysis

**One question, three times.** All three verses open with the same two Hebrew words, *mî zōʾṯ*,
*who is this* (feminine). The WEB renders them *"Who is this"* at 3:6 and 8:5 and *"Who is she"* at
6:10, so the English hides that the question is the same. At 3:6 and 8:5 the first four words are
identical: *mî zōʾṯ ʿōlâ min-hammiḏbār*, *who is this coming up from the wilderness*.

| | Song 3:6 | Song 6:10 | Song 8:5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question | *mî zōʾṯ ʿōlâ min-hammiḏbār* | *mî-zōʾṯ hannišqāp̄â* | *mî zōʾṯ ʿōlâ min-hammiḏbār* |
| What follows | *"like pillars of smoke"*, myrrh, frankincense, spices | *"as the morning"*, moon, sun, an army with banners | *"leaning on her beloved"* |
| The answer | *"Behold, it is Solomon's carriage!"* (3:7) | none given; the man has been praising her (6:4–9) | none given; the next words are addressed to *you* (masculine): *"Under the apple tree I aroused you"* |

**What *this* is.** At 6:10 and 8:5 *this* is plainly a woman: she looks out, and she leans on
*her* beloved (*dôḏāh*). At 3:6 it is not plain. The answer in 3:7 names a carriage, and the word for
it, *miṭṭâ*, is feminine, so *this* may be the carriage. But 8:5 uses the same words of a woman
coming up from the wilderness with her beloved. Read together, the three questions suggest that 3:6
also asks about a woman, perhaps the bride carried in the carriage. Read on its own, 3:6 answers its
own question with the carriage. The wiki records both readings and adopts neither.

**From the wilderness.** *Midbār* is the uninhabited land, the steppe and pasture land beyond the
towns. In the Song it is the direction from which someone comes up toward the settled land. Readers
often hear in it Israel's own coming up from the wilderness in Exodus and Numbers. The Song names no
exodus, and the wiki does not import one. See
[[Pillars of Smoke - Song of Solomon 3.6, Joel 2.30, and the Wilderness Pillar]].

## Significance

The repeated question is one of the Song's ways of tying separate poems together without telling a
continuous story. Each time someone is seen coming, and each time the poem stops to ask who. At 3:6
the answer is royal splendor; at 6:10 it is a woman as bright as the sky; at 8:5 it is a woman
leaning on the one she loves. The progression is a reading; the repetition is in the text. This page
will be revised when 6:4–10 and 8:5–7 are ingested.
