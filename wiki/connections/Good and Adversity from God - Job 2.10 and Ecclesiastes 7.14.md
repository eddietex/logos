# Good and Adversity from God - Job 2.10 and Ecclesiastes 7.14

## Passages involved

- [[The Second Test]] — Job 2:10.
- [[The House of Mourning]] — Ecclesiastes 7:13–14.

## Analysis

Both texts attribute good and hard days alike to God:

> But he said to her, “You speak as one of the foolish women would speak. What? Shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil?” In all this Job didn’t sin with his lips. (Job 2:10)

> In the day of prosperity be joyful, and in the day of adversity consider; yes, God has made the one side by side with the other, to the end that man should not find out anything after him. (Ecclesiastes 7:14)

**Different speech acts.** Job's line is a question spoken to his wife after the loss of his
children and property and the affliction of his body; the narrator adds that he did not sin
with his lips. Ecclesiastes gives an instruction to the reader: be joyful in prosperity,
consider in adversity. Job speaks from within the adversity; the Preacher addresses both
kinds of day in advance.

**Different purposes stated.** Job 2:10 states none; the reader knows of the wager in heaven
(1:6–12; 2:1–6) and Job does not. Ecclesiastes 7:14 gives one purpose — that man should not
find out anything after him — and 7:13 calls the crooked God's work. Neither text explains
why a particular adversity comes to a particular person.

**Tension retained.** Job's acceptance in 2:10 is followed by his curse of his day in Job 3
and by long complaint; the acceptance is not his last word. Ecclesiastes' *consider* is not
the same as silent acceptance either. Both texts hold God's sovereignty over hard days
together with the continued human response to them.

The English differs — *evil* in Job 2:10, *adversity* in Ecclesiastes 7:14 (WEB). This page
does not claim the same Hebrew word stands behind both, and asserts no literary dependence.

Sources: stored WEB Job 1:6–12; 2:1–10; 3:1; Ecclesiastes 7:13–14. Both quotations are exact
stored WEB excerpts. No source-language claim is made.

## Significance

[[Providence]] in both books includes adversity as well as good, without supplying a
reason for any single case. [[Suffering]] and [[Joy and Enjoyment]] are given distinct
responses — joy and consideration — rather than one posture for every day.
