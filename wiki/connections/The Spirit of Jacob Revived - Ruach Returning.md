# The Spirit of Jacob Revived - Ruach Returning

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 45:26–28** — *"His heart fainted, for he didn't believe them… When he saw the wagons which
  Joseph had sent to carry him, **the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived**"* — ***wattəḥî rûaḥ
  yaʿăqōb*** — [[Joseph Reveals Himself]]
- **Judges 15:19** — Samson at Lehi: *"when he had drunk, **his spirit came again**, and he revived"*
- **1 Samuel 30:12** — the abandoned Egyptian slave given a cake of figs and two clusters of raisins:
  *"when he had eaten, **his spirit came again to him**; for he had eaten no bread, nor drunk any
  water, three days and three nights"*
- **Habakkuk 1:4** — *"the law is slacked"* — ***tāpûg*** — **the verb of Jacob's fainting heart**

## Analysis

**Genesis 45:26 and 45:27 describe two opposite physiological events in consecutive verses, and both
are reported as things that happen to [[Jacob]] rather than as decisions he makes.**

> **26** …His heart fainted, for he didn't believe them. **27** …When he saw the wagons which Joseph
> had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived.

***Wayyāpāg libbô*** — *his heart went numb.* **The verb *pûg* is rare and means to grow slack, cold,
or unfeeling**; Habakkuk uses it of a law that has stopped working (1:4), and the Psalms and
Lamentations of a hand or an eye that will not stop. **It is not disbelief and it is not shock — it is
a system declining to process an input.** Genesis gives the reason in the same breath: *"for he didn't
believe them."*

***Wattəḥî rûaḥ yaʿăqōb ʾăbîhem*** — *and the spirit of Jacob their father came alive.* **The idiom
pairs *ḥāyâ*, the ordinary verb for living, with *rûaḥ*, breath or spirit**, and **Scripture uses it
for the return of physical vitality to a body that has run out, not for cheerfulness.** Both of its
close relatives prove the point:

| text | the man | the cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Judges 15:19** | **Samson**, *"very thirsty"* after killing a thousand men | **water from a split rock** |
| **1 Samuel 30:12** | **an Egyptian slave**, abandoned sick, three days without food | **figs and raisins** |
| **Genesis 45:27** | **Jacob**, whose heart has just gone slack | **wagons** |

**In two of the three the reviving agent is food or drink. In the third it is a line of carts.**

**That is the observation the passage page turns on.** [[Joseph]] composed a message for exactly this
moment — *"you shall tell my father of all my glory in Egypt, and of all that you have seen"* (45:13)
— and the brothers delivered it in full: *"they told him all the words of Joseph, which he had said to
them"* (45:27). **The verse does not say the words worked.** **It turns on *"when he saw"***, and what
he saw was ***ʿăgālôt***, **wagons, which were [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]]'s idea and not Joseph's
at all** (45:19).

**The parallel with the other great object in Jacob's life is exact and inverted.**

| | the object | what it produced |
|---|---|---|
| **37:31–33** | **a coat dipped in goat's blood** | *"He recognized it… Surely Joseph is torn in pieces"* |
| **45:27** | **wagons** | *"the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived"* |

**Both times he is handed a physical thing and believes it instantly; both times the human testimony
accompanying it is the part he does not weigh.** At 37:32 the brothers said almost nothing — *"we have
found this"* — and **let the object do the lying.** At 45:26 they say the true thing and he does not
believe them; **the cart is what he believes.** See
[[A Garment Used as Evidence - The Coat and the Cloak]].

**The name changes on the far side of the revival.** *"The spirit of **Jacob**, their father, revived.
**Israel** said, 'It is enough.'"* **Genesis alternates the two names from 32:28 onward without a
stated rule**, and here the switch lands precisely on the turn from a man things happen to into a man
who decides something. **The wiki records the placement without claiming the book intends a system.**
See [[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

## Significance

- **Scripture's idiom for coming back to life is physical, and Genesis applies it to a grief.** Samson
  and the Egyptian are literally dying of thirst and starvation; **Jacob has been in mourning since
  37:34 and the book treats his recovery in the same vocabulary as a rescue from dehydration.** See
  [[Suffering]].
- **What revives him is not the truth but the evidence.** **The chapter that finally tells Jacob what
  happened cannot make him believe it**; an object he can look at can. **Genesis makes no criticism of
  this** — it records the same mechanism working against him at 37:33 and for him here.
- ***"It is enough"*** — ***rab*** — **is a word about quantity**, and it is Jacob's verdict on the
  amount of evidence rather than a statement of faith. **What he asks for is not an explanation but a
  sight**: *"I will go and see him before I die."* **The man who said three times that he would go down
  to Sheol mourning** (37:35; 42:38; 44:29) **says here that he will go down to Egypt to look at his
  son.** See [[Hope]] and [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **The wagons are Pharaoh's.** **The one object in the Joseph cycle that persuades Jacob was supplied
  by a foreign king who had never met him**, at the king's own initiative and expense (45:19–20). See
  [[Favor and Grace]].
