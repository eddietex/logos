# Caleb and Joshua Receive Their Own Inheritances

## Passages involved
- [[Caleb Claims Hebron]] — Joshua 14:6–15.
- [[Joshua's Own Inheritance]] — Joshua 19:49–51.

## Analysis

Caleb’s individual request and grant appear near the opening of the western allotments
(Joshua 14:6–15); Joshua’s appear at their close (19:49–51). Caleb asks Joshua for the hill
country on the basis of the remembered promise, and Joshua grants Hebron. Israel gives
Joshua Timnathserah, the city he asks for, according to Yahweh’s command.

The two grants form a literary frame around the tribal lists. That is a comparison of
their placement, not an explicit explanation supplied by the narrator. Caleb’s account
includes his age, a recalled oath, and prospective conflict; Joshua’s brief notice
reports building and residence. It does not repeat Caleb’s oath or say that Joshua
received the identical promise concerning a particular city.

Joshua first appears as giver to Caleb and later as recipient among Israel. His grant
comes last in the narrated distribution, but the passage does not explain the timing
by a stated motive of humility or record a separate lot for his city.

Sources: stored WEB Joshua 14:6–15 and 19:49–51; comparisons are paraphrases.

## Significance

Leadership participates in the inheritance it administers. The common pattern of request
and grant preserves the distinct details of each account rather than turning either
into a generic reward story. See [[Land and Sojourning]] and [[Law and Obedience]].
