# Whose Is the Firstling - Exodus 13, Numbers 18, and Deuteronomy 15

## Passages involved

- [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] — Exodus 13:2, 12–15; *"It is mine"*, and the males are
  Yahweh's.
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:29–30; given to God on the eighth day.
- [[The Covenant Renewed]] — Exodus 34:19–20; *"the firstborn of cow and sheep"* claimed again.
- [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]] — Leviticus 27:26; no one may dedicate it, for it is
  already Yahweh's.
- [[The Duties and Portions of Priests and Levites]] — Numbers 18:15–18; not redeemed, blood and fat
  to the altar, meat to Aaron.
- [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]] — Deuteronomy 12:6, 17–18; brought to the place and eaten there.
- [[Tithes for Feasting and Provision]] — Deuteronomy 14:23; eaten with the tithe before Yahweh.
- [[Consecrate the Firstborn of the Herd and Flock]] — Deuteronomy 15:19–23; not worked or shorn,
  eaten by the household, and the defective one eaten at home.

## Analysis

### Every law agrees the firstling is Yahweh's

*"Sanctify to me all the firstborn … both of man and of animal. It is mine"* (Exodus 13:2). *"The
males shall be Yahweh's"* (13:12). *"All that opens the womb is mine; and all your livestock that is
male, the firstborn of cow and sheep"* (34:19). Leviticus 27:26 says the same thing from the other
side: *"no man may dedicate"* it, *"It is Yahweh's"*. Deuteronomy 15:19 tells the owner to dedicate
it, *taqdîš*, the verb Leviticus 27:26 negates, *lōʾ yaqdîš* (WLC, morphhb). Read in context, that
is probably not a contradiction: Leviticus forbids offering the firstling as a vow or freewill gift,
Deuteronomy commands treating it as holy. No verse states that distinction.

### Who eats it

The laws divide over what happens to the meat.

- **Numbers 18:17–18 gives it to the priest.** The clean firstborn is not redeemed; *"You shall
  sprinkle their blood on the altar, and shall burn their fat"*, and *"Their meat shall be yours, as
  the wave offering breast and as the right thigh"*. *Yours* is Aaron, to whom the chapter is
  addressed.
- **Deuteronomy gives it to the household.** *"You shall eat it before Yahweh your God year by year …
  you and your household"* (15:20); likewise 12:17–18 and 14:23.

**Both claims are recorded as they stand.** Two lines of reconciliation are commonly offered, and
neither is stated in either text. One reads Deuteronomy's *"you shall eat"* as addressed to Israel
as a whole, so that the priests eating it is Israel eating it. The other notes that Numbers
addresses priests about their portions, while Deuteronomy addresses households about pilgrimage
meals, and supposes the arrangements changed or were combined. The wiki records the tension and does
not choose. The tithe has a parallel difference; see
[[Three Tithe Laws - Leviticus 27, Numbers 18, and Deuteronomy 14]].

### When it is given

Exodus 22:30 gives the firstborn of cattle and sheep on the eighth day: *"Seven days it shall be
with its mother, then on the eighth day you shall give it to me."* Leviticus 22:27 accepts any newborn
offering *"from the eighth day and thenceforth"*. Deuteronomy says *"year by year"* (15:20), and
forbids working or shearing the firstling (15:19), a ban that makes most sense if the animal could be
kept for some time. Whether *"year by year"* is an age or the yearly visit to the place is not
stated; this is an inference. See [[The Eighth Day - Circumcision and the Firstborn Given to God]].

### The defective firstling

Only Deuteronomy rules on a firstling with a defect. It may not be sacrificed (15:21), in line with
Leviticus 22:20–22, but it is not redeemed or sold either. It is eaten at home, clean and unclean
alike, *"as the gazelle, and as the deer"* (15:22). Numbers 18 has no such provision. See
[[No Blemish in You - Song of Solomon 4.7, Leviticus 22.21, and 2 Samuel 14.25]].

### Redemption

Exodus 13:13 and 34:20 and Numbers 18:15–16 require the firstborn of people and of donkeys (Numbers:
*"unclean animals"*) to be redeemed. Numbers 18:17 forbids redeeming the clean firstborn. Deuteronomy
15 does not mention redemption at all; it deals only with the herd and flock. See
[[In Place of All the Firstborn - Exodus 13 and Numbers 3, 8, and 18]] and
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]].

## Significance

- The firstborn law touches [[Birthright and the Firstborn]], [[Sacrifice and Offering]],
  [[Priesthood]], and [[Holiness]]. Every version grounds it in Yahweh's claim; they differ in who
  receives the meat and when.
- The unresolved difference between Numbers 18 and Deuteronomy 15 is the same kind as the tithe
  difference between Numbers 18 and Deuteronomy 14. Deuteronomy in both cases puts at the household's
  table what Numbers gives to the priests and Levites.
