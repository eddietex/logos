# The God Who Has Fed Me - The First Time God Is a Shepherd

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 48:15** — *"the God who has fed me all my life long to this day"* — ***hārōʿeh ʾōtî***,
  *the one shepherding me* — [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]]
- **Genesis 49:24** — *"from there is the shepherd, the stone of Israel"* — the noun, one chapter later
- **Genesis 30:31–43; 31:38–41** — [[Jacob]]'s twenty years as a hired shepherd, itemised under oath —
  [[Jacob's Flocks]], [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]]
- **Psalm 23:1** — *"Yahweh is my shepherd; I shall lack nothing"*
- **Psalm 80:1** — *"Hear us, Shepherd of Israel, you who lead Joseph like a flock"*
- **Psalm 78:52–53** — *"But he led out his own people like sheep, and guided them in the wilderness
  like a flock"*
- **Isaiah 40:11** — *"He will feed his flock like a shepherd. He will gather the lambs in his arm"*
- **Ezekiel 34:1–31** — against the shepherds of Israel, and *"I myself will be the shepherd of my
  sheep"*
- **Micah 5:4; Jeremiah 23:1–4; Zechariah 13:7** — the shepherd of the flock, and the one struck
- **John 10:11, 14** — *"I am the good shepherd"*
- **Hebrews 13:20** — *"the great shepherd of the sheep"*; **1 Peter 2:25; 5:4** — *"the Shepherd and
  Overseer of your souls"*
- **Revelation 7:17** — *"the Lamb … will be their shepherd"*

## Analysis

**The most familiar metaphor for God in the Bible enters it in a blessing over two children, and the man
who coins it is a professional shepherd describing his own career.**

### The verse

> **15** He blessed Joseph, and said, "The God before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, the God
> who has fed me all my life long to this day,

**WEB's *"has fed me"* renders *hārōʿeh ʾōtî*, a participle of *rāʿâ*, *to shepherd*.** **Every earlier
occurrence in Genesis is about livestock and a hired man** — [[Abel]] *"a keeper of sheep"* (4:2), the
flocks at the well (29:2–9), [[Jacob]] feeding [[Laban]]'s flock (30:31, 36), [[Joseph]] and his
brothers feeding their father's (37:2, 12–16), and the family telling [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]]
*"your servants are shepherds"* (46:32–34; 47:3). **48:15 is the first time in Scripture that the
subject of the verb is God.**

**And the object is a person, not a people.** *"Who has fed **me** all my life long to this day."*
**Almost every later use makes God the shepherd of Israel, or of the nations, or of the church.**
**Jacob says it about himself alone**, and Psalm 23:1's *"Yahweh is **my** shepherd"* is the only other
verse in the Old Testament that does.

### Why this man

**Jacob is the only patriarch who worked for wages, and the work was shepherding.** **Genesis gives the
job description in his own words, in the speech where he confronts [[Laban]]:**

> **38** These twenty years I have been with you. Your ewes and your female goats have not cast their
> young, and I haven't eaten the rams of your flocks. **39** That which was torn by animals, I didn't
> bring to you. I bore its loss. Of my hand you required it, whether stolen by day or stolen by night.
> **40** I was in the day consumed with drought, and frost by night; and my sleep fled from my eyes.
> (31:38–40)

**He knows what the metaphor costs.** **A shepherd in Genesis 31 absorbs the losses, stands the weather,
and does not sleep** — **and that is the office Jacob assigns to God over his own life at 48:15.**
**The wiki notes that the figure is not chosen for its gentleness.** See
[[The Shepherd's Liability - Genesis 31.39 and the Law of Exodus 22]].

### The noun, one chapter later

**48:15 has the participle; 49:24 has the title.** *"But his bow remained strong … by the hands of the
Mighty One of Jacob, (from there is the shepherd, the stone of Israel)."* **Two verses in two chapters,
both in Jacob's mouth, both in a blessing over [[Joseph]] or his sons**, and **between them they supply
the verb and the noun on which Psalm 23, Psalm 80, Ezekiel 34 and John 10 are built.**

**Psalm 80:1 puts both halves of Genesis 48–49 into one line**: *"Hear us, **Shepherd of Israel**, you
who lead **Joseph** like a flock."* **The psalm addresses God by Jacob's title and names Jacob's son as
the flock**, which is as close as the Psalter comes to citing these verses.

### The shape of the thread

| stage | what the figure does |
|---|---|
| **Genesis 48:15; 49:24** | **one man's biography**: the God who shepherded *me* |
| **Psalms 23; 78:52; 80:1** | **a person, then a nation**, led, fed, and brought through wilderness |
| **Isaiah 40:11; Jeremiah 23; Ezekiel 34** | **a polemic**: Israel's human shepherds have failed, so God takes the flock himself |
| **John 10; Hebrews 13:20; 1 Peter 5:4** | **the claim made by, and about, one man** |
| **Revelation 7:17** | **the Lamb shepherds**, and the metaphor closes on itself |

**The movement is from autobiography to office.** **Nothing in Genesis 48 is programmatic; it is an old
man's summary of his own life** — *"all my life long to this day"* — **and the whole tradition is
downstream of it.** See [[Providence]].

### A note on *"all my life long to this day"*

**The same man told [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] eleven verses earlier that his years had been
*"few and evil"*** (47:9), **and tells God in the next breath that he was *"redeemed … from all evil"***
(48:16). **Three verdicts on one life, two of them contradictory, all within two chapters, and Genesis
harmonises none of them.** **The shepherd clause makes no exceptions**: it covers the twenty years at
Laban's, the flight, the limp, [[Dinah]] at [[Shechem (city)]], the coat brought back bloody, and the
famine. **The wiki records that the claim is comprehensive and that the book lets the other two verdicts
stand beside it.** See [[Suffering]] and [[Hope]].

## Significance

**Psalm 23 is the best-known chapter in the Old Testament and its first three words are a quotation of a
dying man's blessing over two half-Egyptian boys.** **The wiki states the priority and not a dependence**
— the psalm does not cite Genesis, and the figure was available to anyone in a pastoral economy.

**What is worth stating is the direction.** **Scripture's shepherd-God does not begin as a title of
majesty and get applied to individuals.** **It begins as one man's account of his own hundred and
forty-seven years** (47:28), **and is afterwards enlarged to a nation** (Psalm 80:1), **to a polemic
against Israel's rulers** (Ezekiel 34), **and at last to a person who says *"I am the good shepherd"***
(John 10:11). **The man who starts it is the one member of the family who did the job for wages.**

**Genesis 49:24 is now ingested**, in [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]] — **the noun to 48:15's participle.**
At 48:15 God is ***hārōʿeh ʾōtî***, *the one shepherding me*; **here the participle stands alone as
*the shepherd***, and it is the last thing [[Jacob]] calls God. **It sits in the same four-word
parenthesis as *the stone of Israel*, which occurs nowhere else in the Bible.** See
[[The Mighty One of Jacob and the Stone of Israel]].
