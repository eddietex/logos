# The Lifted Face and the Hidden Face - Numbers 6 and Deuteronomy 31

## Passages involved

- [[Yahweh Bless You and Keep You]] — Numbers 6:25–26.
- Deuteronomy 31:17–18 — *"I will forsake them, and I will hide my face from them ... I will surely hide
  my face in that day for all the evil which they have done."* *(WEB, bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- [[Show Me Your Glory]] — Exodus 33:11, 14, 19–20.
- [[Cain and Abel]] — Genesis 4:5–7, 14.
- Deuteronomy 10:17; Job 42:8 — the idiom with another person's face as object. *(WEB, bible-api.com; not
  yet ingested)*

## Analysis

The priests' blessing asks Yahweh to turn his face on Israel, twice in two lines. Deuteronomy 31 says that
he will one day turn it away, and it uses the same noun for it, *pānîm* (WLC). The two passages share a
picture: what happens to the people depends on where Yahweh's face is. In Numbers 6 the face brings
grace and peace. In Deuteronomy 31 the hidden face is followed by being *devoured*, and by evils and
troubles.

The verbs differ. Numbers has *yāʾēr* and *yiśśāʾ*. Deuteronomy has *hastēr ʾastîr*, from *sātar* (WLC).
So this is a contrast of image and not a verbal echo, and neither text cites the other.

The contrast does not resolve into a sequence. Numbers gives a blessing with no condition in it.
Deuteronomy gives a reason for the hiding, *all the evil which they have done*, and Leviticus 26 and
Deuteronomy 28 lay out conditions at length. The priests are told to speak the blessing, and nothing
in Numbers 6 suspends it if the people sin. The two are left beside each other.

Genesis had already put a face at the center of a broken relationship. Cain's face falls (4:5–6), Yahweh
asks whether it will not be lifted up if he does well (4:7), and Cain goes out saying *I will be hidden
from your face* (4:14, WEB). *Śəʾēt* at 4:7 is tagged as a noun of its own (H7613), though it looks like
the same root as *nāśāʾ*. The face in Numbers is Yahweh's and the face in Genesis is Cain's, so the
parallel is limited.

Exodus 33 adds the pressure from the other side. *Yahweh spoke to Moses face to face* (33:11), *My presence
will go with you* (33:14, *pānay* in WLC), and *You cannot see my face* (33:20) all stand within ten
verses. Numbers 6 asks for a face turned toward the people, not for one seen by them.

The idiom must be kept separate. To lift another person's face is to accept that person: God does so
for Job (Job 42:8) and refuses to do so as a bribed judge would (Deuteronomy 10:17). Numbers 6:26 has
Yahweh lifting his own face toward someone, a different form. See the note on it in
[[Yahweh Bless You and Keep You]].

## Significance

Israel's whole well-being is pictured in these passages as a matter of being looked at. The wiki leaves
the tension open: the blessing is unconditional in form, and the threat of the hidden face is
conditional. Deuteronomy 31 will need a fuller treatment when it is ingested. Hebrew from WLC (morphhb);
no lexicon or commentary consulted.
