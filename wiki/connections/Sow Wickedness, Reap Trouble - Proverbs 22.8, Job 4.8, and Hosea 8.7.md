# Sow Wickedness, Reap Trouble - Proverbs 22.8, Job 4.8, and Hosea 8.7

## Passages involved

- [[A Good Name]] — Proverbs 22:8.
- **Proverbs 11:18** — [[A False Balance]]: *"Wicked people earn deceitful wages, but one who sows
  righteousness reaps a sure reward."*
- **Job 4:8** — [[Eliphaz's First Speech]]: *"According to what I have seen, those who plow
  iniquity, and sow trouble, reap the same."*
- **Hosea 8:7** *(not yet ingested)* — *"For they sow the wind, and they will reap the whirlwind."*
- **Hosea 10:13** *(not yet ingested)* — *"You have plowed wickedness. You have reaped iniquity. You
  have eaten the fruit of lies, for you trusted in your way, in the multitude of your mighty men."*
- **Galatians 6:7** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Don’t be deceived. God is not mocked, for whatever a man
  sows, that he will also reap."*

## Analysis

Proverbs 22:8 states the harvest principle in moral terms:

> He who sows wickedness reaps trouble, and the rod of his fury will be destroyed.

The image says that consequences grow out of conduct the way a crop grows from seed. Proverbs 11:18
gives the positive side: sowing righteousness reaps *"a sure reward"*. Hosea applies the image to a
nation, with escalation — wind sown, whirlwind reaped (8:7) — and gives a reason: Israel *"trusted in
your way, in the multitude of your mighty men"* (10:13). Galatians 6:7 restates it as a general rule
under God, who *"is not mocked"*.

**The same image used as an accusation.** Eliphaz uses it first, in his first speech to Job:
*"those who plow iniquity, and sow trouble, reap the same"* (Job 4:8). He sets it after a question —
*"whoever perished, being innocent?"* (4:7) — and turns the principle into a way of reading Job's
disaster backward: the harvest proves the seed. The narrator has already called Job *"a blameless
and an upright man"* (1:8), and the book ends with God telling Eliphaz that he and his friends have
not spoken of God what is right (42:7). The principle is not refuted; its use as a diagnosis is.
This is the same reversal the wiki traces in [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]] and
[[The Rod of Discipline - Proverbs 13.24 and the Father's Correction]].

**What the sayings claim and do not.** Proverbs 22:8 and Hosea 8:7 look forward from the deed to the
outcome. Neither tells an observer how to look back from someone's trouble to the cause. Read that
way, Proverbs 22:8 and Job 4:8 can stand together.

No claim about Hebrew or Greek wording is made here.

## Significance

The harvest image runs from wisdom to prophecy to the New Testament as a stable statement about
consequences, while Job keeps it from becoming a tool for judging sufferers. See [[Retribution]],
[[Judgment]], and [[Suffering]].
