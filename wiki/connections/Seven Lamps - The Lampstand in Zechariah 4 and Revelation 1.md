# Seven Lamps - The Lampstand in Zechariah 4 and Revelation 1

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 25:31–40** — a lampstand of pure gold with almond-blossom cups and *"its lamps seven"*, to
  *"give light to the space in front of it"* — [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]]
- **Exodus 27:20–21** — pure olive oil *"to cause a lamp to burn continually"*, kept by Aaron and his
  sons *"from evening to morning before Yahweh"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Zechariah 4:2, 6, 14** — *"a lamp stand all of gold, with its bowl on the top of it, and its seven
  lamps on it"*; *"Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit"*; *"the two anointed ones"* *(not yet
  ingested)*
- **Revelation 1:12, 20** — *"I saw seven golden lamp stands"*; *"The seven lamp stands are seven
  assemblies"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Revelation 2:5** — *"or else I am coming to you swiftly, and will move your lamp stand out of its
  place, unless you repent"* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Exodus gives the lampstand seven lamps and no meaning.** It is a hammered gold tree with almond
blossoms (25:31–36). It carries seven lamps (25:37) and is kept burning through the night (27:20–21).
The text names no symbolism. **The later books supply meaning, and each supplies a different one.**

**Zechariah sees one lampstand and hears about the Spirit.** *Mənôrat zāhāb kullāh*, *a lampstand all of
gold* (Zechariah 4:2; MAM). Its bowl is on top, and seven lamps have *"seven pipes to each of the
lamps"*. Two olive trees stand beside it (4:3, not quoted here). Asked what it means, the angel answers
with a word to Zerubbabel, who in the book is rebuilding the temple (4:9; not fetched in this pass): *"Not by might, nor by power, but by my
Spirit"* (4:6). The two olive trees are *"the two anointed ones"* (4:14). **The lamp burns on oil
supplied, not on effort.** The Septuagint of Zechariah 4:2 has *lychnia chrysē holē*, the same noun as
Exodus 25:31 (Rahlfs). The details of Zechariah's lampstand differ from Exodus's, and the wiki does not
harmonize them.

**Revelation splits it into seven.** John turns and sees *hepta lychnias chrysas*, *seven golden
lampstands* (Revelation 1:12; SBLGNT), with the risen Christ among them. They are explained at once: *"The
seven lamp stands are seven assemblies"* (1:20). **The lampstand is now the church**, and it can be
removed: *"I … will move your lamp stand out of its place, unless you repent"* (2:5). The noun *lychnia*
is the Septuagint's word for the *mənôrâ* (Exodus 25:31, Rahlfs).

**What stays the same.** Gold, the number seven, and a light that stands before God. **What changes.**
In Exodus the lampstand is furniture. In Zechariah it is a sign of God's Spirit working through his
anointed. In Revelation it is a community that can lose its place. The thread is in the image and its
vocabulary; the meanings are each book's own.

**The tree reading.** Many readers take Exodus's lampstand as a stylized tree, perhaps the tree of life
(see [[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]]). Zechariah's olive trees beside the lamp are
sometimes read as supporting that. Neither Exodus nor Zechariah says the lampstand is a tree of life.

**Exodus fixes the lampstand's location.** It stands on the south side opposite the table,
outside the veil (26:35), rather than beside the ark within the most holy space. The command
gives a practical arrangement for the light of 25:37; it supplies no additional symbolic
explanation. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].

## Significance

- **It gives the outer room's light a history.** The lamp lit *"from evening to morning"* becomes, by the
  end of the canon, an image of the people of God.
- **It shows how the New Testament reuses tabernacle furniture.** As with the mercy seat
  ([[The Mercy Seat - Kapporet and Hilasterion from Leviticus 16 to Romans 3]]) and the bread
  ([[The Bread of the Presence - David's Hunger and Matthew 12]]), the object becomes a figure for
  something living.
