# The Apple of the Eye - Deuteronomy 32.10, Psalm 17.8, and Proverbs 7.2

## Passages involved

- **Deuteronomy 32:10** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"He found him in a desert land, in the
  waste howling wilderness. He surrounded him. He cared for him. He kept him as the apple of his
  eye"* (*yiṣṣərenhû kəʾîšôn ʿênô*)
- **Psalm 17:8** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"Keep me as the apple of your eye. Hide me
  under the shadow of your wings"* (*šomrēnî kəʾîšôn baṯ-ʿāyin*)
- **Proverbs 7:2** — [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]: *"Keep my commandments and live! Guard
  my teaching as the apple of your eye"* (*wəṯôrāṯî kəʾîšôn ʿênêḵā*)
- **Proverbs 7:9** — the same chapter: *"in the middle of the night"* (*bəʾîšôn laylâ*)

## Analysis

**What the phrase means.** *ʾÎšôn* is a diminutive of *ʾîš*, *man* — *the little man* of the eye,
the pupil, where a person looking in sees himself reflected small. The Hebrew of all four verses is
read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*. The pupil is what a person protects without
thinking: the eyelid shuts before the mind decides. To keep something *as the pupil of the eye* is
to guard it by reflex and at any cost.

**Who guards whom.** In the song of Deuteronomy 32 it is God who keeps Israel so, in the
wilderness, with the verb *nāṣar*. In Psalm 17 the psalmist asks God to keep him so, with
*šāmar*. In Proverbs 7:2 the direction turns round: **the son is to guard the father's teaching as
the pupil of his own eye**, and Proverbs 7:1, 5 tell him why — the teaching kept will keep him
(*lišmorḵā*, 7:5). Proverbs does not say it borrows the image from either text, and the wiki does
not claim it does. It records the reversal: the care God shows his people is the care the son is to
show God's teaching.

**The word used twice in Proverbs 7.** *ʾÎšôn* returns at 7:9 in its other sense, the black centre
of the night — *bəʾîšôn laylâ*, *"in the middle of the night"*. The same chapter that tells the son
to guard the pupil of his eye shows the young man walking into the pupil of the night. Whether this
is deliberate wordplay cannot be proved, but the word is uncommon and occurs twice in eight verses.

## Significance

The image ties [[Law and Obedience]] to [[Seeing and Being Seen]]: the teaching is to be as precious
and as instinctively protected as sight itself. Set beside Deuteronomy 32:10, it also makes the
son's keeping of the law a reflection of God's keeping of Israel.
