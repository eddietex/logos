# Abaddon Has No Covering - Job 26.6, Proverbs 15.11, and Revelation 9.11

## Passages involved

- **Job 26:6** — *"Sheol is naked before God, and Abaddon has no covering."* —
  [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]
- **Job 28:22** — *"Destruction and Death say, 'We have heard a rumor of it with our ears.'"* — the
  same word, *ʾăbaddôn*, rendered *Destruction* by the WEB — [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]]
- **Job 31:12** — *"For it is a fire that consumes to destruction"* — *ʿad-ʾăbaddôn*, adultery as a
  fire that burns all the way down to it — [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]
- **Psalm 88:11** — *"Is your loving kindness declared in the grave? Or your faithfulness in
  Destruction?"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Proverbs 15:11** — *"Sheol and Abaddon are before Yahweh— how much more then the hearts of the
  children of men!"* — [[A Gentle Answer]]
- **Proverbs 27:20** — *"Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; and a man's eyes are never
  satisfied."* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Revelation 9:11** — *"They have over them as king the angel of the abyss. His name in Hebrew is
  'Abaddon', but in Greek, he has the name 'Apollyon'."* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

***ʾĂbaddôn* is a place-name made from a verb.** The root *ʾ-b-d* means *to perish, be lost*, and
*ʾăbaddôn* is *the place of perishing* — Destruction, as a location. It occurs only six times in the
Hebrew Bible, all in the wisdom books and the Psalms, and three of the six are in Job (26:6; 28:22;
31:12). It is never a place anyone goes to in a narrative; it is always named alongside [[Sheol]] or
Death, as the deepest and most final word the poet has for the realm of the dead. The WEB keeps the
name in Job 26:6 and in Proverbs and renders it *Destruction* elsewhere, so an English reader of
the WEB sees *Abaddon* in fewer places than the Hebrew has it.

**Its two uses in pairs are about what can be seen.** Job 26:6 and Proverbs 15:11 say the same
thing in two registers. Job: Sheol is *naked* (*ʿārôm*) before God and Abaddon has *no covering*
(*ʾên kəsût*) — the vocabulary of clothing, so that the underworld is pictured as a body God sees
undressed. Proverbs: Sheol and Abaddon are *before Yahweh* (*neged*, in front of, in view of) — and
then draws the inference Job's hymn leaves implicit: *"how much more then the hearts of the children
of men!"* **The deepest place in the cosmos is open to God, so the inside of a person is too.** Job's
hymn uses the fact to magnify God; the proverb uses it to warn the man who thinks his heart is
private. In Job's speech the inference cuts in his favour as well as against him: if God sees the
heart, God knows what Job has sworn to at 27:2–6.

**Its other uses are about what does not stop.** Proverbs 27:20 pairs Sheol and Abaddon with human
eyes as three things that are *never satisfied*. Job 31:12 uses Abaddon as the limit of a fire that
consumes everything on the way down. Job 28:22 gives it a voice: Destruction and Death have heard only
a rumour of wisdom. **Abaddon is the place that takes everything and knows nothing** — which is why
Psalm 88:11 can ask, as a rhetorical question expecting *no*, whether God's faithfulness is declared
there.

**Revelation turns the place into a person.** In Revelation 9:11 the locusts out of the abyss have a
king, *"the angel of the abyss"*, and his name is given in two languages: *Abaddon* in Hebrew, *Apollyon*
(*the destroyer*) in Greek. This is the only occurrence of the word in the New Testament, and the
move from place to personification is new. It has a precedent inside the Hebrew wisdom books
themselves — Job 28:22 already lets Destruction *speak* — but in Revelation the place of destruction
has become an agent of it, released for a limited time and under a limit (9:4–5, *not yet ingested*).
**The thread runs from a realm open to God's eye to a power on God's leash**, and the constant is that
in none of the texts is Abaddon outside God's sight or rule.

## Significance

- **It is the sharpest form of the Bible's claim that death is not a hiding place.** Job had wished
  to be hidden in Sheol (14:13); Amos 9:2 and Psalm 139:8 say God's hand reaches there; Job 26:6 says
  the place is *naked*. See [[Higher Than Heaven, Deeper Than Sheol - The Measure of the Unsearchable]].
- **It explains why Job's hymn starts at the bottom.** Bildad's hymn (25:2–3) looked up; Job's begins
  with the shades and with Abaddon, and only then rises to the north, the clouds and the heavens. The
  hymn's first claim is that nothing below is covered — said by the one man in the book who is hiding
  nothing.
- **It marks one of the few places a Job word reaches the last book of the Bible by name.** The
  Septuagint usually renders *ʾăbaddôn* as *apōleia*, *destruction*; Revelation instead transliterates
  the Hebrew and then supplies a Greek name of its own, which suggests the author expected readers to
  know the Hebrew word as a name.
- **In its chapter, the proverb is one of nine statements about Yahweh.** [[A Gentle Answer]] sets 15:11
  eight verses after *"Yahweh's eyes are everywhere, keeping watch on the evil and the good"* (15:3) and
  among sayings on what Yahweh detests and loves (15:8–9). Its *how much more* is the chapter's ground
  for treating the heart as answerable: the thoughts of the wicked are detested (15:26) because they are
  seen. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 15:3, 8–11, 26.
