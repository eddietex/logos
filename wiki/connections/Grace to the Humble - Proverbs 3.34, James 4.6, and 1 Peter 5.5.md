## Analysis

Proverbs 3:34 reads in the stored WEB:

> Surely he mocks the mockers, but he gives grace to the humble.

The subject continues the preceding statements about Yahweh. The line is part of the
teacher's answer to envy of violence: God's evaluation determines which way is desirable.
Humility receives grace rather than the mockery returned to mockers.

James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5 both introduce a scriptural citation rendered in the WEB:

> God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.

The English second halves closely match Proverbs, while the first half speaks of
resistance to the proud rather than mockery of mockers. This page identifies the
quotation relationship through the verified English texts; it does not reconstruct
the Hebrew, Greek, or an intermediary translation.

**James 4:6–10** follows the citation with submission to God, resistance to the devil,
drawing near, cleansing, mourning, and humility before the Lord. Grace is the ground
of an appeal to change one's conduct, not an exemption from it. The final promise
is that the Lord will exalt those who humble themselves.

**1 Peter 5:5–7** applies humility to the younger in relation to elders and to the whole
community in relation to one another. Humbling oneself under God's mighty hand is
joined to casting worries on him because he cares. Thus the citation supports both
social humility and confidence in God, a pairing that also belongs to Proverbs 3:21–35.
This similarity of setting does not establish that 1 Peter quotes the whole pericope.

### Pride before a fall — Proverbs 16:18–19

[[The Plans of the Heart]] states the other half of 3:34 as a sequence: *"Pride goes before destruction,
and a haughty spirit before a fall"* (16:18), and *"It is better to be of a lowly spirit with the poor,
than to divide the plunder with the proud"* (16:19). Where 3:34 has Yahweh mock the mockers and give
grace to the humble, 16:18 describes the fall without naming its agent; 16:5 supplies him — the proud
in heart *"shall certainly not be unpunished"*. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 3:34; 16:5, 18–19.

### Lowly spirit and honor — Proverbs 29

Proverbs 29:23 pairs pride’s abasement with honor for a lowly spirit, alongside 15:33 and 18:12.
Unlike 3:34, it does not explicitly name grace or its giver in the WEB. It belongs to the humility
thread but is not identified as the verse quoted by James or 1 Peter; that citation relationship
remains with 3:34.

See [[Where There Is No Vision]]. Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 29 and the cited local comparisons; no new
source-language claim.

## Significance

- [[Favor and Grace]] is explicitly associated with humility without being described
  as a wage earned by it.
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] meets divine resistance; honor is received from God.
- The later citations carry a wisdom saying into instructions for community life and
  dependence on God. They do not erase Proverbs' own neighborly and anti-violence setting.
