# The Place of Weepers - Allon Bacuth, Bochim, and Bethel

## Passages involved
- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:8. *Deborah, Rebekah's nurse,
  died, and she was buried below Bethel under the oak; and its name was called Allon Bacuth* (stored
  WEB).
- [[The Angel at Bochim]] — Judges 2:4–5. *The people lifted up their voice, and wept. They called the
  name of that place Bochim, and they sacrificed there to Yahweh* (stored WEB).
- **Judges 20:23, 26** — *The children of Israel went up and wept before Yahweh until evening*; then
  they *came to Bethel, and wept, and sat there before Yahweh, and fasted that day until evening; then
  they offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before Yahweh* (stored WEB; *not yet ingested*).
- **Judges 21:2–4** — *The people came to Bethel, and sat there until evening before God, and lifted
  up their voices, and wept severely.* The next day they built an altar there and offered burnt
  offerings and peace offerings (stored WEB; *not yet ingested*).

## Analysis

**One root, two names.** *ʾAllôn bākût*, *oak of weeping* (Genesis 35:8), and *bōkîm*, *weepers*
(Judges 2:1, 5), both come from *bākâ*, *to weep* (WLC, OpenScriptures morphhb, 2026-11-13). Genesis
puts the oak *below Bethel*. Judges does not locate Bochim. The Septuagint of Judges 2:1 is often
reported to add *to Bethel*, which would put the two names in the same district. That reading is a
claim, not verified here against an edition of the Septuagint.

**The same idiom at both ends of Judges.** At Bochim, *wayyiśʾû hāʿām ʾet qôlām wayyibkû*, *the people
lifted up their voice, and wept* (2:4). At Bethel, *wayyiśʾû qôlām wayyibkû bəkî gādôl*, *and lifted up
their voices, and wept severely* (21:2; both WLC). Both times sacrifice follows: *they sacrificed
there to Yahweh* (2:5); *burnt offerings and peace offerings* (20:26; 21:4). Both times the weeping
comes after a word or an event that cannot be undone. At Bochim it is the sentence that the peoples
will stay. At Bethel it is the loss of thousands in the war against Benjamin, and then a tribe nearly
gone (21:3).

**What the texts do not say.** Genesis does not say who wept at the oak. Judges 2 does not say the
weeping at Bochim was repentance, and nothing in the pericope changes because of it. Judges 21 has a
question, *why has this happened in Israel?* (21:3), but no confession. The three passages share a
root and, in the case of Judges, a setting at or possibly near Bethel. Whether the book of Judges
means its first and last scenes of weeping to frame it is a reading. The verbal match between 2:4 and
21:2 is in the text.

**A separate fact.** In Judges 20:26–27 the sacrifice at Bethel is explained: *the ark of the covenant
of God was there in those days* (stored WEB). Judges 2:5 gives no such explanation for Bochim.

## Significance
- A place named for weeping marks the beginning of Judges, and the same idiom of weeping at Bethel
  marks its end.
- None of the three texts treats the weeping as a solution. Each reports it, names it, or follows it
  with sacrifice, and moves on.
- Bethel's other names and history are on [[Bethel]] and
  [[Bethel from Jacob's Pillar to Jeroboam's Calf]].
