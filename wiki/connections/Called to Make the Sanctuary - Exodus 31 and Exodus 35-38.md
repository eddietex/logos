## Analysis

Exodus 31 names [[Bezalel]] and [[Oholiab]], attributes wisdom to the wider company, and assigns the
full set of sanctuary work. God's address to Moses is still instruction. The page must not report
the building as completed at the moment the workers are named.

Exodus 35:30–35 later takes the appointment into Moses' address to Israel. It repeats Bezalel's name
and genealogy, the Spirit-given wisdom, and the skills of metal, stone, and wood. It adds the
capacity to teach, naming both Bezalel and Oholiab (35:34), and describes engraving, embroidery, and
weaving (35:35). The later passage enlarges the description rather than merely duplicating chapter
31.

Exodus 36:1–2 joins wisdom and understanding to willingness to come to the work. Moses calls the two
named leaders and the wise-hearted workers; they receive the people's offerings (36:3). The workers
then tell him the supply exceeds the need, and he halts further contributions (36:4–7). A gift of
skill thus becomes shared, materially supplied work with an accountable stopping point.

Exodus 38:22 credits Bezalel with making all that Yahweh commanded Moses. The next verse names
Oholiab alongside him and specifies engraving and embroidery. That attribution can stand alongside
the narrative's wider company; it need not mean Bezalel personally made every item alone.

The framing also includes rest. Exodus 31:12–17 follows the appointment with Sabbath; Moses opens
the assembly's instructions with Sabbath in 35:1–3. The command to make is not permission to work
without limit. This is a literary and practical connection, not a claim that the text identifies the
sanctuary with the six days of creation in every detail.

### The offering now brought — Exodus 35:1–29

Before the named makers are announced, [[The Freewill Offering]] publicly calls the wise-hearted
to make the whole inventory and records women's skilled spinning (35:10–19, 25–26). The shared
labor is already visible in the supply of yarn. This distinguishes actual preparatory work from
the later making of the whole structure. The public Sabbath frame in 35:1–3 now belongs to an
ingested passage; the named commissioning and surplus now follow in
[[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]].

### The workers' report now reached

[[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]] makes the stopping point concrete. The workers are already
at their tasks when they report the excess (36:4–5); Moses' order halts contributions rather
than the work itself (36:6). The narrator confirms both sufficiency and surplus (36:7), but
provides no final inventory or disposal of the excess here. Teaching (35:34) and the broader
company (36:1–2) keep the credit from becoming a claim of solitary manufacture.

## Significance

The chain is command, appointment, public summons, shared work, and credited completion. [[Work]],
[[Wisdom]], [[Call and Election]], [[Sabbath]], and [[The Dwelling of God]] belong together.
Spiritual gifting is represented as useful ability and cooperation, without a statement that
training is unnecessary or that the gift expires when the project ends.
