# They Didn't Recognize Him - Marred Beyond Knowing

## Passages involved

- **Job 2:12** — *"When they lifted up their eyes from a distance, and didn't recognize him, they
  raised their voices, and wept"* — [[Seven Days of Silence]]
- **Isaiah 52:14** — *"his appearance was marred more than any man, and his form more than the sons
  of men"*
- **Isaiah 53:2–3** — *"he has no good looks or majesty… as one from whom men hide their face"*
- **Genesis 42:8** — *"Joseph recognized his brothers, but they didn't recognize him"* —
  [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]]
- **Job 19:19–20** — *"All my familiar friends abhor me… My bone clings to my skin and to my flesh"*
- **Job 30:10** — *"They abhor me, they stand aloof from me, and don't hesitate to spit in my face"*
- **Luke 24:16, 31** — on the Emmaus road, *"their eyes were kept from recognizing him"*, and then
  opened

## Analysis

**The Hebrew verb is *nākar*, and in the Hiphil it means to recognise, to identify, to acknowledge
as the person you knew.** The wiki has already traced its Genesis career, where it is almost always
applied to **an object presented as evidence of a person** — the bloodied coat brought to Jacob
(37:32–33), the signet and cord and staff brought to Judah (38:25–26). See
[[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]].

**Job 2:12 uses it of the man himself and negates it.** There is no token to examine; the friends are
looking straight at Job from a distance, and the identification does not happen. **The book gives no
physical description of Job anywhere**, and this is the reason it does not need one. *Wəlōʾ
hikkîrūhû* does the whole job in three syllables, and it does it from outside — every other account
of the disease in the book is Job's own (7:5, 19:20, 30:30), and a sufferer describing his own body
is not the same evidence as three acquaintances failing to place his face.

**Genesis 42:8 is the closest verbal parallel and the most instructive contrast.** Joseph's brothers
do not recognise him either, and the reason is the opposite one: he has been **elevated** past
recognition — Egyptian dress, an Egyptian name, an interpreter, the second chariot. **Non-recognition
in Genesis 42 is the product of glory, and in Job 2 of ruin**, and the two verses use the same verb
to mark the two directions a man can travel out of the life his family knew him in. In both, the man
who is not recognised says nothing about it.

**Isaiah 52:14 is the closest parallel in substance**, and the resemblance is why Job has been read
alongside the suffering servant since antiquity. Both are figures whose appearance is destroyed past
the human norm; both are looked at from a distance and reacted to before they are spoken to; in both
the disfigurement is read by observers as a verdict — *"yet we considered him plagued, struck by God,
and afflicted"* (Isaiah 53:4) is, word for word, what Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar are about to
conclude.

**The wiki states the parallel and refuses the identification.** Job is not a messianic figure and
the book makes no claim of the kind; the servant of Isaiah 53 is *"wounded for our transgressions"*,
and nothing Job suffers is said to be for anyone. **What the two texts genuinely share is a
mechanism**, and it is one the book of Job exists to expose: **a body read as evidence, and read
wrongly**. Isaiah 53:4 says outright that the reading was mistaken (*"yet we considered him
plagued"*), and Job 42:7 says the same thing about the same kind of reading, of a different man, by
name.

**Luke 24 belongs in the list for the shape and not the cause.** The disciples on the Emmaus road
fail to recognise a man they knew, walk with him, and are given their sight back at a specific
moment. It is the one entry here where the non-recognition is undone.

## Significance

- **It is the book's only external description of Job's condition**, and it consists entirely of what
  three witnesses failed to do.
- **It supplies the friends' evidence.** Their argument does not begin with a text or a tradition; it
  begins with **a man they could not identify**. Everything Eliphaz says from 4:1 is an attempt to
  account for what he saw at 2:12, and the doctrine of retribution is the only instrument he has. See
  [[Retribution]] and [[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]].
- **It is the hinge of the theme of sight in the book.** The friends *lift up their eyes* and fail;
  they *see* that his pain is great and succeed; Job will end at 42:5 with *"now my eye sees you."*
  See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **It puts Job and the servant of Isaiah 53 in the same frame without collapsing them** — two men
  whose ruined bodies were read as sentences, and two texts that say the reading was wrong.
