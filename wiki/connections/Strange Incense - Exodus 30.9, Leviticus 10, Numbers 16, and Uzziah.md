# Strange Incense - Exodus 30.9, Leviticus 10, Numbers 16, and Uzziah

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 30:9, 33, 37–38** — *"You shall offer no strange incense on it"*; the oil not to be put
  *"on a stranger"*; the incense not to be made privately, on pain of being *"cut off from his
  people"* ([[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]])
- **Leviticus 10:1–2** — Nadab and Abihu *"offered strange fire before Yahweh, which he had not
  commanded them. Fire came out from before Yahweh, and devoured them"*
- **Numbers 16:35–40** — fire *"devoured the two hundred fifty men who offered the incense"*; their
  censers beaten into a covering for the altar, *"a memorial … to the end that no stranger, who
  isn't of the offspring of Aaron, would come near to burn incense before Yahweh, that he not be as
  Korah"*
- **2 Chronicles 26:16–19** — King Uzziah *"went into Yahweh's temple to burn incense on the altar of
  incense"*; the priests: *"It isn't for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to Yahweh, but for the priests
  the sons of Aaron"*; leprosy breaks out on his forehead *"beside the altar of incense"*

## Analysis

**The word.** Exodus 30:9 forbids *qəṭōret zārâ*, *"strange incense"* (MAM). The adjective *zār*
means *outside, other, not belonging*; Exodus 30:33 uses it of the person, *"a stranger"*, on whom
the oil may not be put, and 29:33 of the *"stranger"* who may not eat the ordination meal. In
Leviticus 10:1 (MAM) Nadab and Abihu offer *ʾēš zārâ*, *"strange fire"*. Numbers 16:40 (WEB) uses
*"stranger"* of anyone *"who isn't of the offspring of Aaron"*; the Hebrew of that verse was not
checked in this pass.

**What is excluded.** Exodus names the exclusions without explaining them: incense of another
recipe (30:37–38), offerings that belong on the other altar (30:9), and any user outside Aaron's
line (30:7, 33). The three later stories each break one of these lines, or are read as breaking one:

- **Nadab and Abihu** are priests, and the fault is in the offering: fire *"which he had not
  commanded them"* (Leviticus 10:1). What exactly made it strange — the source of the fire, the time,
  the place, or the men's state — is not said in the verse, and interpreters differ.
- **Korah's company** are not priests, and the fault is in who offers. Numbers draws the lesson
  itself: the censers become *"a sign"* and *"a memorial"* so that *"no stranger"* comes near to burn
  incense (16:38, 40).
- **Uzziah** is a king, and the fault is again in who offers, with the reason given as pride:
  *"when he was strong, his heart was lifted up"* (26:16). The priests' objection quotes the Exodus
  rule almost as a principle: incense is *"for the priests the sons of Aaron, who are consecrated to
  burn incense"* (26:18).

**Fire answers in two of the three.** In Leviticus 10 and Numbers 16, *"fire came out"* from Yahweh
and consumed those offering. Uzziah is struck with leprosy instead. In each case the judgment falls
at the altar or the censer itself.

## Significance

- Exodus 30 is the rule; the others are cases. The connection lets the reader of Exodus see what
  the bare prohibition of 30:9 is later shown to mean, without reading the cases back into it.
- The stories concern access more than ingredients. Only Leviticus 10 involves priests, and even
  there the text locates the fault in what was *"not commanded"*. The incense altar is where the
  wiki's [[Holiness]] and [[Priesthood]] themes meet the question of who may come near.
- Nadab and Abihu are named in Exodus as Aaron's sons and among those who *"saw the God of
  Israel"* (24:9–10). Their deaths are not yet narrated in the wiki; see [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]] and
  [[Abihu]]. The Korah of Numbers 16 is [[Korah (son of Izhar)]].
