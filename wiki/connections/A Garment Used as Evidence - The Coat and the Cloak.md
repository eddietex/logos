# A Garment Used as Evidence - The Coat and the Cloak

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:3–4, 23, 31–33** — the *kətōnet passîm*, stripped, dipped in goat's blood, and handed to
  Jacob with *"Examine it, now"* — [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]
- **Genesis 39:12–18** — the *beged*, seized, kept until evening, and produced to Potiphar —
  [[Joseph in Potiphar's House]]
- **Genesis 38:18, 25–26** — the seal, cord and staff sent back to Judah with *"Discern, please, whose
  are these"* — [[Judah and Tamar]]
- **Genesis 27:15–16, 27** — Esau's clothes and the goatskins on Jacob's hands, worn to deceive a blind
  father — [[The Stolen Blessing]]
- **Exodus 22:13** — *"if it is torn in pieces, let him bring it for evidence"*: the herdsman's defence,
  which is a garment-as-proof rule in all but name
- **2 Samuel 13:18–19** — Tamar's *kətōnet passîm*, torn by the woman wearing it
- **Genesis 44:1–13** — [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]; **the planted object without a garment**:
  a silver *gābîaʿ* put into [[Benjamin]]'s sack and produced as proof of a theft that did not happen.
  **The third object in the cycle to be arranged as evidence and believed**, and the first one
  arranged by [[Joseph]]

## Analysis

**Twice in three chapters, a garment is taken off Joseph and produced as proof of something that did not
happen.** Genesis does not remark on the repetition. It simply does it again.

| | **Genesis 37** | **Genesis 39** |
|---|---|---|
| **the garment** | ***kətōnet passîm***, his father's gift | ***beged***, an ordinary garment |
| **who removes it** | his brothers strip him (37:23) | his master's wife seizes it (39:12) |
| **what is done to it** | dipped in the blood of a male goat | *"she laid up his garment by her"* (39:16) |
| **who is shown it** | [[Jacob]] | [[Potiphar]] |
| **the words used** | *"We have found this. Examine it, now"* (37:32) | *"he left his garment by me, and ran"* (39:15, 18) |
| **what it is taken to prove** | that Joseph is dead | that Joseph is guilty |
| **what is actually true** | neither | neither |
| **what Joseph says** | **nothing** | **nothing** |
| **where he ends up** | a pit, then Egypt | a prison |

**Eight rows, and the last three are the ones that make this a parallel rather than a coincidence of
props.**

### The garment testifies and the man does not

**In both scenes Joseph is absent from his own defence.** In chapter 37 he is physically absent — he is
in a caravan while the coat is in Hebron. **In chapter 39 he is standing in the house**, and Genesis
still records not one word from him after the garment leaves his hand. **He speaks at 39:8–9, before
anything is alleged, and then never again in the chapter.**

**What 42:21 later reveals about the first silence applies to both.** *"We saw the distress of his soul,
when he begged us, and we wouldn't listen"* — **Genesis withheld his pleading for five chapters and then
put it in the mouths of the men who ignored it.** The book's habit is to let the object speak and leave
the person unheard, and it does it twice to the same man.

### Neither hearer asks a question

**Jacob is handed a question and answers it himself.** The brothers say only *"we have found this"*,
which is false only by implication, and then ask *hakker-nāʾ*, *examine it*. **The sentence they had
drafted at 37:20 — *an evil animal has devoured him* — comes out of his own mouth at 37:33, unprompted
and verbatim.** He reasons correctly from real evidence to a false conclusion.

**Potiphar is handed a story and the text does not say what he concluded.** *"His wrath was kindled"*
(39:19) has no object in the Hebrew. **He does not execute a slave accused of assaulting his wife**, does
not sell him, and confines him in the royal prison inside his own house (40:3). **Genesis gives him no
dialogue anywhere in the book.**

**So the two hearers are not the same, and the difference is the interesting part.** **Jacob believes and
Genesis says so; Potiphar acts and Genesis withholds whether he believed.** The wiki adopts no reading of
39:19 — see [[Potiphar]].

### It is the family technique, and it is used on the family

**Nobody in either scene tells an outright lie.**

- **The brothers** say *we have found this*, which is true, and ask a question. **The lie is supplied by
  the listener.**
- **[[Potiphar's Wife]]** narrates a sequence that is accurate in every particular — he was in the house,
  they were alone, the garment came off in her hand, he ran outside — **and reverses one pronoun.** She
  does not invent an event; she inverts it.

**This is [[Deception]] as Genesis has practised it for four generations**: the true half-statement, the
substituted person, the borrowed clothing. **[[Jacob]] wore his brother's clothes and goatskins to a
blind father and never said *I am Esau* until asked** (27:15–24). **His sons used a goat and a garment on
him** (37:31). **An Egyptian woman uses a garment on the son he mourned.** See
[[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]].

### The third object, in the chapter between them

**Genesis 38 sits between the two garment scenes and does the same thing in reverse.** [[Tamar]] holds
[[Judah]]'s **seal, cord and staff** and sends them to him with ***hakker-nāʾ***, *"Discern, please,
whose are these"* — **the identical phrase the brothers used on Jacob one chapter earlier** — and this
time the object tells the truth and the man it convicts says so: *"She is more righteous than I"*
(38:26). See
[[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]].

**So the sequence across three consecutive chapters is:**

| chapter | the object | who is deceived | the outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **37** | a bloodied coat | a father, who supplies the lie himself | twenty-two years of mourning |
| **38** | a pledge | nobody — the object is truthful | a man convicts himself |
| **39** | a seized garment | a master, whose belief is not recorded | thirteen years in prison |

**Genesis puts a truthful object between two lying ones and does not comment.** The wiki records the
arrangement as a fact about the book's construction, **not as evidence of a design it can demonstrate** —
the placement of Genesis 38 is disputed on entirely separate grounds.

### Clothing as the Joseph cycle's index of status

**Every change in Joseph's condition is marked by what he is wearing or not wearing.**

- **The *kətōnet passîm*** (37:3) — a garment you cannot work in, given to one of eleven shepherds
- **Stripped** (37:23) — the first thing the brothers do, before the pit
- **The *beged* left in her hand** (39:12) — and he runs into a courtyard half-dressed in front of a
  household he had been running
- **Prison clothes**, implied by 41:14: *"he shaved himself, and changed his clothing"* before Pharaoh
- **Fine linen, a gold chain, and a signet ring** (41:42) — an Egyptian investiture, the only garment in
  the cycle nobody takes off him
- **Changes of clothing given to his brothers, and five to Benjamin** (45:22) — **the man whose coat
  started it hands out coats**

**The cycle begins with a garment that causes hatred and ends with garments given away by the man it was
given to.** See [[Nakedness and Covering]].

## Significance

**The parallel is the clearest structural evidence that Genesis 37 and 39 are one movement with a
digression between them**, and it does not depend on any source theory to see it. **The same man is
stripped, the cloth is produced, the hearer is convinced, and he goes down.** Twice.

**And it is the mechanism by which the cycle's whole problem is set.** Joseph's dreams are true and
nobody believes them; the evidence against him is false and everybody does. **The book spends thirteen
chapters on a man whose word carries no weight and whose belongings carry all of it**, and then resolves
it by making him the one person in Egypt whose interpretation of a dream is believed by a king. **The
cupbearer's *"I remember my faults today"* (41:9) is the first time in the narrative that a true account
of Joseph is given by somebody else**, and it takes two more years after the prison to arrive.

**What the two scenes share most exactly is the silence.** **Genesis gives Joseph no defence in either**,
and never records him being cleared of either charge. **He is not exonerated of the assault**; he is
promoted past it. **Jacob learns his son is alive at 45:26 and the book never stages a scene in which
anyone tells him what the coat was.** The wiki notes that both false verdicts stand, unretracted, inside
a narrative that ends in reconciliation. See [[Joseph]], [[Sin]], and [[Suffering]].
