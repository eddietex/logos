# A Fifth to Pharaoh - Joseph's Tax and the Rights of the King

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 41:33–36** — the fifth proposed, as a seven-year emergency measure —
  [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Genesis 47:20–26** — the fifth made permanent law, *to this day* — [[Joseph and the Famine]]
- **1 Samuel 8:10–18** — *"This will be the way of the king"*: a tenth of the seed, a tenth of the
  flocks, *"and you shall be his servants"*
- **1 Kings 12:3–4, 10–11** — *"Your father made our yoke grievous"*: the tax that splits the kingdom
- Compare **Deuteronomy 17:14–20** — the law of the king, which forbids accumulation instead of
  regulating it
- Compare **Genesis 14:20** and **Genesis 28:22** — the two tenths of Genesis, both voluntary and
  both religious — [[The Tithe Before the Law]]
- Compare **Leviticus 27:30–32**; **Numbers 18:21–24** — the tithe as Israel's own fixed levy

## Analysis

**Scripture contains exactly one detailed account of a national taxation system being designed, and
the designer is Joseph.**

**It is proposed at 41:34, in the middle of a dream interpretation, by a prisoner who has been in
the room for about five minutes.**

> **41:34** Let Pharaoh do this, and let him appoint overseers over the land, and take up the fifth
> part of the land of Egypt's produce in the seven plenteous years.

**In context this is a levy on surplus, for a fixed term, against a specific catastrophe.** The
seven good years are *"plenteous"*; the state takes one-fifth of an abnormally large harvest and
stores it. **Nothing about it is objectionable and everything about it works.**

**Thirteen or fourteen years later the same rate is reimposed on a population that now owns nothing,
with no terminal date.**

> **47:24** It will happen at the harvests, that you shall give a fifth to Pharaoh, and four parts
> will be your own …
> **47:26** Joseph made it a statute concerning the land of Egypt to this day, that Pharaoh should
> have the fifth.

**The rate did not change; everything else did.** **At 41:34 a fifth of a bumper crop was taken from
freeholders for seven years. At 47:24 a fifth of every crop is taken from tenants for ever**, and
what they are renting is the land they used to own. **Genesis states both and does not connect
them.**

### What a king does, according to Samuel

**Centuries later, Israel asks for a king and is given a description of one.** **It is the closest
thing in the Bible to a commentary on Genesis 47, and it reads like an abridgement of it.**

| | **Genesis 47** | **1 Samuel 8:11–17** |
|---|---|---|
| **the fields** | *"Joseph bought all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh"* (47:20) | *"He will take your fields, your vineyards, and your olive groves"* (8:14) |
| **the herds** | *"They brought their livestock to Joseph"* (47:17) | *"He will take the tenth of your flocks"* (8:17) |
| **the harvest** | *"You shall give a fifth to Pharaoh"* (47:24) | *"He will take the tenth of your seed"* (8:15) |
| **the people** | *"we and our land will be servants to Pharaoh"* (47:19) | *"and you will be his servants"* (8:17) |
| **the verdict** | *"You have saved our lives!"* (47:25) | *"You will cry out in that day because of your king"* (8:18) |

**The vocabulary of the last row is the same in both.** ***ʿăbādîm*** — *servants*, *slaves* — **is
what the Egyptians volunteer to become and what Samuel warns Israel it will become.** **The
difference is that Egypt says it in gratitude and Israel is told it will say it in grief.**

**And the rate is half.** **Samuel's king takes a tenth of the seed and a tenth of the flock and the
prophet presents it as intolerable. Joseph's Pharaoh takes a fifth of everything, permanently, and
the people who pay it call him their saviour.** **Neither text mentions the other, and the arithmetic
is unavoidable once both are on the page.**

### Rehoboam, and the tax that broke a kingdom

**1 Kings 12 is the same machinery running in Israel.** Solomon's forced labour and levies produce a
delegation at Shechem — *"Your father made our yoke grievous. Now therefore make the grievous service
of your father … lighter"* (12:4) — and the answer, *"My father chastised you with whips, but I will
chastise you with scorpions"* (12:11), **loses ten tribes in an afternoon.**

**What Egypt accepted from a foreigner in a famine, Israel would not accept from a son of David in a
time of peace.** **The books offer no explanation for the difference and the wiki proposes none**,
beyond the obvious one that a man who is starving values bread above a deed.

### Where the Torah lands

**The law given to Joseph's descendants does not regulate a royal fifth. It forbids the conditions
that make one possible.**

- **The land cannot be permanently alienated.** *"The land shall not be sold in perpetuity, for the
  land is mine; for you are strangers and live as foreigners with me"* (Leviticus 25:23), **with the
  jubilee to undo any sale that has happened.** **This is the precise reversal of Genesis 47:20.**
  See [[The Land Is Mine - Pharaoh's Freehold and the Jubilee]].
- **The king is placed under restriction rather than given prerogatives.** Deuteronomy 17:16–17
  forbids him many horses, many wives and much silver and gold, **and requires him to write out the
  law and read it all his life** (17:18–19). **The one royal law in the Torah is a list of things a
  king may not accumulate.**
- **The fixed levy Israel does owe is a tenth, and it is owed to God rather than to a throne**
  (Leviticus 27:30; Numbers 18:21).

## Significance

**The connection is not that Joseph was wrong.** **It is that Genesis describes, without comment, the
exact institution that the rest of the Old Testament spends its energy resisting** — a crown that
owns the land and takes a fixed share of everything grown on it — **and that the description is
supplied by the book's most competent and most vindicated character, at the height of his career, to
universal thanks.**

**Three readings are available and Scripture does not choose between them.**

- **Joseph saved a region from starvation and the price was the ordinary price of state power.**
  The text supports this: nobody dies, the seed is issued, four parts in five are left, and the
  people say so themselves.
- **Joseph built the apparatus that four hundred years later was turned on his own family.** The
  text supports this too, and does it by juxtaposition: 47:25 makes Egypt Pharaoh's servants and
  Exodus 1:11 makes Israel his. See
  [[Buy Us and Our Land - Egypt Sold into Servitude and Israel After It]].
- **Genesis is simply reporting how the world Israel came out of was organised**, with no verdict
  intended, and the verdicts arrive later in Samuel, Kings and the Torah's land law.

**The wiki holds all three and adopts none**, and notes that the first reader to put Genesis 47 and
1 Samuel 8 side by side did not need to add anything to either. See [[Kingship and Empire]],
[[Wealth and Possessions]] and [[Wisdom]].
