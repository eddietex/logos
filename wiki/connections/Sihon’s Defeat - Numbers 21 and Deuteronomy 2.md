# Sihon’s Defeat - Numbers 21 and Deuteronomy 2

## Passages involved
- [[Sihon and Og]]
- [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]]

## Analysis

Numbers 21:21–26 and Deuteronomy 2:24–37 share the request for passage, Sihon’s refusal,
battle at Jahaz, and Israel’s possession of his land. Numbers explains Heshbon’s earlier
transfer from Moab to Sihon. Deuteronomy begins with a command to fight and adds the embassy’s
origin at Kedemoth, the offer to pay for provisions, and Yahweh’s hardening of Sihon (30).

**Differences retained:** Numbers 21:22 promises not to drink well water; Deuteronomy 2:28
requests water for payment. These are compatible kinds of negotiated restraint, but the
speeches are not verbatim duplicates. Numbers 21:24 describes Ammon’s strong border;
Deuteronomy 2:37 emphasizes Yahweh’s prohibition. A military description and a theological
limit need not be competing explanations, but neither should replace the other.

Deuteronomy expressly includes women and little ones among the destroyed inhabitants (34).
Numbers 21:21–26 does not specify this for Sihon; its no-survivors statement in 21:35 concerns
Og. The added detail must not be misquoted as part of Numbers’ Sihon account.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers 21:21–35 and Deuteronomy 2:24–37.

## Significance

The retelling interprets a shared victory through command, hardening, and boundaries. It also makes
the civilian deaths explicit.
