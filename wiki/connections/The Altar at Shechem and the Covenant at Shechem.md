# The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 12:6–7** — Abram at *the oak of Moreh*, the first theophany in the land, and his first
  altar — [[The Call of Abram]]
- **Genesis 33:18–20** — Jacob buys a field at Shechem and builds *El Elohe Israel* *(not yet
  ingested)*
- **Genesis 35:4** — the household's foreign gods buried *"under the oak which was by Shechem"*
  *(not yet ingested)*
- **Deuteronomy 11:29–30** — Gerizim and Ebal located *"beside the oaks of Moreh"*
- **Deuteronomy 27:1–26** — the blessings and the curses to be pronounced from the two mountains
- **Joshua 8:30–35** — Joshua builds an altar on Ebal and reads the whole law between the mountains
- **Joshua 24:1–27** — the covenant renewal at Shechem, and the stone set up *"under the oak that was
  by the sanctuary of Yahweh"*
- **Joshua 24:32** — Joseph's bones buried in Jacob's field
- **1 Kings 12:1–19, 25** — Rehoboam loses ten tribes at Shechem; Jeroboam fortifies it
- **John 4:5–26** — Jesus at Jacob's well, and *"this mountain"*

## Analysis

One patch of ground, a pass between two mountains in the central highlands, carries more of Israel's
covenant history than anywhere except Jerusalem — and unlike Jerusalem it is never claimed as a
capital, never given a temple, and never called *chosen*.

### The tree

Genesis 12:6 puts Abram *"to the place of Shechem, to the oak of Moreh"*. *Môreh* is from *yārâ*, to
instruct or to give an oracle — the root of *tôrâ* — so the tree is *the oak of the teacher*, which
is what a Canaanite divination site would be called. Abram's first altar in the promised land goes up
beside somebody else's oracle tree, and Genesis says nothing about that at all.

The tree does not go away. Jacob buries the foreign gods and the earrings *"under the oak which was
by Shechem"* (35:4) — putting the family's idols into the ground at the site of an idolatrous
landmark. Joshua's witness-stone goes *"under the oak that was by the sanctuary of Yahweh"* (24:26),
which is the same tree given a new landlord. And Deuteronomy 11:30 fixes the location of Gerizim and
Ebal by it: *"beside the oaks of Moreh"*. Four texts, one tree, and its status changes from oracle
site to burial pit for idols to covenant witness without anyone remarking on the change.

### The altars

Every altar in this thread is built at a moment when somebody has just been told the land is theirs
and does not yet hold it.

- **Abram** builds *"to Yahweh, who had appeared to him"* (12:7), immediately after the promise *"I
  will give this land to your offspring"* — and immediately after the narrator has said *"at that
  time, Canaanites were in the land."* The altar is a claim staked by a man with no title.
- **Jacob** buys a field for a hundred pieces of silver and builds an altar he names *ʾĒl ʾĕlōhê
  yiśrāʾēl*, *God, the God of Israel* (33:18–20) — naming the altar after the name he was given at
  Peniel two chapters earlier. It is the second piece of ground a patriarch ever purchases, after
  Machpelah, and the only one bought for the living.
- **Joshua** builds on Mount Ebal *"an altar of uncut stones, on which no man had lifted up any iron"*
  (8:31), reads the blessings and the curses, and writes a copy of the law on the stones — the first
  official act after the conquest's opening campaign.

### The covenant

Joshua 24 is the thread's destination, and it is deliberately built on Genesis. It opens with
*"Your fathers lived of old time beyond the River, even Terah … and they served other gods. I took
your father Abraham from beyond the River"* (24:2–3) — Israel's own account of where it came from,
recited on the exact ground Abram stopped at when he got here. It runs the whole story forward to the
present, and then it demands a choice: *"choose today whom you will serve … but as for me and my
house, we will serve Yahweh"* (24:15). The people answer, Joshua writes it down, and the stone goes
under the oak *"lest you deny your God"* (24:27).

So the site frames the entire national story. Abram's altar is the first act of possession by a man
who possesses nothing; Joshua's stone is the last act of possession by the generation that finally
does. Both are set beside the same tree, and both are made to say the same thing about the land: that
it was given, not taken.

### And it is also where Israel comes apart

The thread has a second half that the first half does not prepare for.

- **Genesis 34** — Dinah, and the massacre of the city by Simeon and Levi, which Jacob is still
  condemning on his deathbed (49:5–7). The first covenant site in the land is also the site of the
  patriarchs' worst act.
- **Judges 9** — Abimelech is made king at Shechem by his mother's clan, murders seventy brothers on
  one stone, and destroys the city; Jotham's fable is delivered from Gerizim, the blessing mountain.
- **1 Kings 12** — *"Rehoboam went to Shechem, for all Israel had come to Shechem to make him king"*
  (12:1). He refuses the elders, and ten tribes walk out. The kingdom divides on the ground where
  Joshua had bound it together, and Jeroboam then rebuilds Shechem as his capital before moving on.

**Shechem is where Israel becomes one people and where it stops being one.** The same pass hosts the
altar of Genesis 12, the covenant of Joshua 24, and the secession of 1 Kings 12. Nothing in the text
points this out, and the shape is unmistakable once the passages are laid end to end.

### The last scene

John 4 sets Jesus *"near the parcel of ground that Jacob gave to his son Joseph"*, at a well outside
Sychar, with Gerizim directly above. The Samaritan woman raises the one question the site has been
generating for a millennium — *"Our fathers worshiped in this mountain, and you Jews say that in
Jerusalem is the place where people ought to worship"* — and the answer she gets dissolves the
question: *"the hour comes when neither in this mountain nor in Jerusalem will you worship the
Father … in spirit and truth"* (4:20–24). The thread that began with an altar beside a sacred tree
ends with a conversation about whether the place matters, held at the place.

## Significance

- **The first altar in the land is built on borrowed holy ground.** Abram does not clear the site,
  denounce it, or rename it; he builds beside it. Genesis's silence about that is consistent with how
  it handles the patriarchs generally — narrating what they did without measuring it against a law
  that does not yet exist. See [[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]].
- **Covenant in the Bible is renewed at places, and this is the chief one.** Joshua 24 does at
  Shechem what Exodus 24 did at Sinai and what Deuteronomy does on the plains of Moab, and it does it
  by rehearsing the story from Terah forward. See [[Covenant]] and
  [[Out of Ur - Terah's Journey and Abram's Call]].
- **The land is granted and disputed in the same verse, every time.** *"At that time, Canaanites were
  in the land"* (12:6) is the condition every altar in this thread is built under, and the condition
  Joshua 24's *"choose today"* is still addressing after the conquest. See [[Land and Sojourning]].
- **Holy places do not stay safe.** Shechem, like [[Bethel]], is a site of genuine revelation that
  becomes a site of murder, idolatry, and schism. The Bible neither denies the revelation nor
  protects the place because of it.
