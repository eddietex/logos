# The Bramble and the Thistle - Plant Fables in Judges 9 and 2 Kings 14

## Passages involved

- [[Jotham's Fable]] — **Judges 9:14–15** — *Then all the trees said to the bramble, 'Come and reign
  over us.' The bramble said to the trees, 'If in truth you anoint me king over you, then come and take
  refuge in my shade; and if not, let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of
  Lebanon.'* (stored WEB)
- **2 Kings 14:9** — *Jehoash the king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, "The thistle
  that was in Lebanon sent to the cedar that was in Lebanon, saying, 'Give your daughter to my son as
  wife.' Then a wild animal that was in Lebanon passed by, and trampled down the thistle.* (WEB checked
  at bible-api.com, 2026-11-15; not yet ingested)

## Analysis

The Hebrew Bible has two fables in which plants speak, and both are about kings. In both, a thorny
plant speaks to the cedars of Lebanon. Both are spoken by one party to another as a warning.

| | Judges 9 | 2 Kings 14 |
|---|---|---|
| Teller | Jotham, the one surviving son | Jehoash, king of Israel |
| Hearer | *the men of Shechem* | Amaziah, king of Judah |
| Low plant | *the bramble*, *hāʾāṭād* | *the thistle*, *haḥôaḥ* |
| Cedar | *the cedars of Lebanon*, threatened by its fire | *the cedar that was in Lebanon*, asked for a daughter |
| Ending | fire, if Shechem did not deal truly | a wild animal tramples the thistle |
| Point | the people chose a worthless king | the small king should not pick a fight with a greater |

The plants are different. *ʾĀṭād* and *ḥôaḥ* are different words (WLC, OpenScriptures morphhb,
Judges 9:14–15 and 2 Kings 14:9). The roles differ too. In Jotham's fable the thorn is the
pretender and the cedars are what it threatens. In Jehoash's fable the thorn is the pretender too,
but now it is the one destroyed. In both, the low plant claims what belongs to the high one: the
throne in one, a royal marriage in the other.

Each teller applies the fable himself. Jotham does not decode the good trees, only the bramble's
conditions (Judges 9:16–20). Jehoash's application is plainer: *You have indeed struck Edom, and your
heart has lifted you up* (2 Kings 14:10, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested).

## Significance

The two fables show the form being used in Israel's own history, by its own people, about kings. Both
are spoken from a position of weakness or strength to warn of what pride or a bad choice will cost.
Jotham's is fulfilled within the same chapter (Judges 9:56–57, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[Parables]].
