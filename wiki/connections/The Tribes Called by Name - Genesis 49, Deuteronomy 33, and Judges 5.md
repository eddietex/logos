# The Tribes Called by Name - Genesis 49, Deuteronomy 33, and Judges 5

## Passages involved

- [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]] — Genesis 49:1–28; Jacob's sayings on his twelve sons.
- [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]] — Deuteronomy 33:1–29; Moses' sayings on the tribes.
- [[The Song of Deborah]] — Judges 5:13–18; the tribes that came to the battle and those that stayed.
- See also [[Jacob's Blessing and the Blessing of Moses - Genesis 49 and Deuteronomy 33]].

## Analysis

**Three tribal poems, three purposes.** Genesis 49 is a father's last words to his sons, about *that
which will happen to you in the days to come*. Deuteronomy 33 is Moses' blessing before his death. Judges 5
is not a blessing at all but a roll call after a battle: each tribe is named for what it did when the
summons came. It is the only one of the three that praises and blames tribes for a single act.

| Tribe | Genesis 49 | Deuteronomy 33 | Judges 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Ephraim]] | within Joseph (22–26) | *the ten thousands of Ephraim* (17) | came; *whose root is in Amalek* (14) |
| [[Benjamin]] | *a ravenous wolf* (27) | *the beloved of Yahweh* (12) | came, *after you, Benjamin* (14) |
| [[Machir]] | — | within Joseph (*the thousands of Manasseh*, 17) | *governors come down out of Machir* (14) |
| [[Zebulun]] | *at the haven of the sea* (13) | *rejoice … in your going out* (18) | came; *jeopardized their lives to the death* (14, 18) |
| [[Issachar]] | *lying down between the saddlebags* (14) | *in your tents* (18) | came; *with Deborah* (15) |
| [[Naphtali]] | *a doe set free* (21) | *satisfied with favor* (23) | came; *on the high places of the field* (18) |
| [[Reuben]] | *you shall not excel* (4) | *let Reuben live, and not die* (6) | stayed; *among the sheepfolds* (15–16) |
| Gilead ([[Gad]]?) | Gad: raids (19) | Gad: *a lioness* (20) | stayed; *beyond the Jordan* (17) |
| [[Dan (person)]] | *a serpent on the trail* (17) | *a lion's cub* (22) | stayed; *in ships* (17) |
| [[Asher]] | *Asher's food will be rich* (20) | *blessed with children* (24) | stayed; *at the haven of the sea* (17) |
| [[Judah]], [[Simeon]], [[Levi]] | named | Judah and Levi named | not named |

*Gilead* for Gad is a common reading, not stated in the Song; Machir's own land in Joshua 17:1 was
*Gilead and Bashan*, so the Song's separation of Machir (who came) from Gilead (who stayed) is not
explained by the text. See [[Gilead]].

**The Song borrows Genesis' words for Zebulun and Issachar, and gives them to others.** In the
Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb):

- Genesis 49:13 says of Zebulun, *ləḥôp yammîm yiškōn … ləḥôp ʾŏniyyôt*, *will dwell at the haven of the
  sea … a haven of ships*. Judges 5:17 says of Asher, *yāšab ləḥôp yammîm … yiškôn*, *sat still at the
  haven of the sea … lived*, and asks of Dan why he stays in *ʾŏniyyôt*, *ships*. Zebulun's haven and
  ships from Genesis are divided between Asher and Dan, the two who did not come, while Zebulun itself
  *jeopardized their lives to the death*.
- Genesis 49:14 has Issachar *lying down bên hammišpətāyim*, *between the saddlebags*. Judges 5:16 asks
  Reuben, *Why did you sit bên hammišpətayim*, *among the sheepfolds?* The noun (lemma 4942) occurs only
  in these two verses in the WLC files read (Genesis through Joshua, Judges, Psalms). WEB translates it
  two ways; the Hebrew is the same. Issachar, who in Genesis lies down between them, in Judges goes to
  battle; Reuben sits there instead.

Whether the Song's poet knew Genesis 49 in its present form, or both draw on shared tribal sayings, the
wiki does not decide. The shared words are a fact of the text; the direction of borrowing is not.

**Who is missing.** Judah, Simeon, and Levi are not named in Judges 5 at all, neither praised nor
blamed. Judah went up first in Judges 1:2. The Song gives no reason for the silence; reading it as
distance, as a southern alliance elsewhere, or as a sign of the Song's date is interpretation, and none
is adopted here.

## Significance

All three poems make the tribes characters with their own natures. Judges 5 alone holds them to one
test, and it is the test of Judges as a whole: whether Israel will act as one people. The answer in 5
is *some*. By the end of the book the tribes are fighting each other (20–21). See
[[The Song of Deborah]], [[Those Who Did Not Come - Meroz, Succoth and Penuel, and Jabesh Gilead]], and
[[Brotherhood]].
