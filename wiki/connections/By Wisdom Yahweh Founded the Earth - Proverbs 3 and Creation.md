# By Wisdom Yahweh Founded the Earth - Proverbs 3 and Creation

## Passages involved

- [[Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth]] — Proverbs 3:13–20, especially 3:19–20.
- [[The Creation of the World]] — Genesis 1:1–2:3.
- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:23–28.
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:4–7, 25–28.
- [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]] — Exodus 31:3–6.
- [[Wisdom at the Creation]] — Proverbs 8:22–31; Wisdom at creation.
- Jeremiah 10:12–13; Psalm 104:24 — not yet ingested; WEB verified through bible-api.com.

## Analysis

**The wisdom worth seeking belongs to the world's making.** Proverbs 3:19–20 assigns the
founding of earth, the establishing of heavens, and the ordering of waters to God's wisdom,
understanding, and knowledge. This follows the praise of wisdom's human benefits in 3:13–18.
The connection between the two parts supplies a reason to seek wisdom: its worth is grounded
in God's creation, rather than only in the wealth it promises.

**Genesis describes the making; Proverbs names its wisdom.** Genesis 1 narrates divine
speech, separation, and ordered life. Proverbs 3 does not retell the six days or quote their
commands. It offers a complementary statement about the Creator's understanding. Calling
this a parallel does not establish the date or direction of literary dependence.

**Job holds the same wisdom beyond human mastery.** Job 28:23–27 connects God's knowledge
of wisdom with his ordering of wind, water, rain, and lightning. Verse 28 then gives humans
the fear of the Lord and departure from evil as wisdom and understanding. Job 38:4–7
questions the hearer's presence at the earth's founding, and 38:25–28 asks about rain and dew.
Proverbs urges a person to find wisdom; Job insists that a person cannot command the
knowledge by which God orders the world. **The tension remains:** Proverbs 3:13 announces a
successful finder, while Job 28:12–22 describes wisdom as hidden from living creatures.
Distinguishing lived wisdom from exhaustive cosmic knowledge is an interpretation supported
by Job 28:28, not a distinction Proverbs 3 spells out.

**The same three English terms describe the sanctuary maker's gift.** Exodus 31:3 describes
Bezalel as filled with the Spirit of God in wisdom, understanding, and knowledge. Proverbs
3:19–20 applies those terms to God's creation. The parallel supports a comparison between
divinely enabled craftsmanship and cosmic order, but neither passage explicitly identifies
the tabernacle as a model of the universe. This is a comparison of the verified WEB wording,
not a claim about an independently checked Hebrew word sequence.

**Other creation texts make the connection explicit.** Jeremiah 10:12–13 places God's power,
wisdom, and understanding alongside earth, heavens, and the movement of water and wind.
Psalm 104:24 similarly attributes the making of God's many works to wisdom. Their agreement
with Proverbs is clear at the level of the verified WEB text; these references do not require
a claim that one passage quotes another.

**Depths and dew.** The opening of the depths in Proverbs 3:20 can be compared with Genesis
7:11's bursting fountains, but the settings differ: Genesis describes a dated catastrophe;
Proverbs praises divine wisdom and pairs the depths with dew. The comparison is suggestive,
not enough to identify Proverbs 3:20 exclusively with the flood.

**Proverbs 8:22–31 develops the creation setting in Wisdom's own voice.** The verb of 3:19b,
*kônēn šāmayim*, *"he established the heavens"*, returns at 8:27 as *bahăḵînô šāmayim*, *"When he
established the heavens, I was there"*; the depths of 3:20 return as *təhōmôṯ* (8:24) and *təhôm*
(8:27–28). What 3:19 said in the third person — *by wisdom* — 8:30 says from beside God, as the
*ʾāmôn*, *"craftsman"* in the WEB, or *nursling* on another reading. See [[Wisdom at the Creation]]
and [[Possessed in the Beginning - Proverbs 8.22-31, John 1, and Colossians 1]]. Hebrew of 3:19 and
8:22–31 read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

## Significance

The same wisdom that makes life desirable is associated with the order of the world.
Job prevents that association from becoming a claim that the wise possess God's complete
explanation of events; Exodus gives it a practical counterpart in skilled making.
See [[Wisdom]], [[Creation]], and [[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]].
