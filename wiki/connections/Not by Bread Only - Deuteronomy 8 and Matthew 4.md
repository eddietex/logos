# Not by Bread Only - Deuteronomy 8 and Matthew 4

## Passages involved
- [[Remember the Wilderness and the Giver of Wealth]] — Deuteronomy 8:1–20, especially 3.
- [[Manna and Quail]] — Exodus 16:4, 15, 35.
- Matthew 4:1–4 — verified WEB; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus 16:4 joins the daily gift of food to testing whether Israel will walk in God’s law.
Deuteronomy 8:2–3 interprets the forty years through the same concern: hunger and manna teach
dependence on what Yahweh speaks. This does not oppose eating to faith; Yahweh feeds the people,
and the next paragraph promises a land with bread enough for all (8:7–10).

Matthew places Jesus in the wilderness, hungry after forty days and nights. The tempter asks
him to turn stones into bread, framing the challenge in terms of sonship. Jesus answers with
the written word corresponding to Deuteronomy 8:3. The resemblance of wilderness, hunger,
testing, and sonship invites a reading of Jesus’ trial alongside Israel’s experience.
That narrative comparison is an interpretation; the scriptural answer itself is explicit.
Forty days in Matthew and forty years in Deuteronomy remain different periods.

The argument does not require a claim about Greek or Hebrew wording. Nor does Matthew 4:1–4
by itself cite Deuteronomy’s later warning about wealth. The immediate issue is how hunger
is answered in a test of dependence.

## Significance

[[Testing]] and [[Providence]] meet in the manna lesson: dependence includes receiving food
according to God’s provision. Matthew’s scriptural answer carries that lesson into Jesus’
wilderness trial, while Deuteronomy carries it onward into the abundance of settled life.
