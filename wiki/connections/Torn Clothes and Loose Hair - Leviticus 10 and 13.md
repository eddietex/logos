# Torn Clothes and Loose Hair - Leviticus 10 and 13

## Passages involved
- [[The Holiness of the Priests]] — Leviticus 21:1–12.
- [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]] — Leviticus 10:6–7.
- [[Examining Diseases of the Skin]] — Leviticus 13:45–46.


## Analysis

After Nadab and Abihu die, Moses forbids Aaron and his surviving sons to let their hair
hang loose or tear their clothes. They must remain at the Tent of Meeting’s door because
the anointing oil is on them (10:6–7). The person declared leprous must wear torn clothes
and loose hair, cover the upper lip, announce uncleanness, and dwell outside the camp
(13:45–46).

The opposed clothing, hair, and location instructions make the contrast concrete.
The priests remain at their assigned holy service while the afflicted person is marked
for separation. This is a comparison of prescribed signs and boundaries, not a claim that
the disease is punishment for the same offense as the unauthorized fire. Chapter 13 does
not identify a moral offense causing the condition, and neither passage explains the
other as its fulfillment.

Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 10:6–7 and 13:45–46; comparison paraphrased.

### The standing distinction in Leviticus 21

Leviticus 21:10–12 repeats the loose-hair, torn-clothes, and sanctuary-departure prohibitions
for the anointed high priest, and bars corpse defilement even for parents. Ordinary priests
receive a close-kin exception (21:1–3). This differs in scope from the crisis command to
Aaron and his surviving sons in 10:6–7. The texts are not flattened into one universal
rule: chapter 10 addresses those priests in that event; chapter 21 differentiates offices.
Neither passage explicitly supplies a complete reconciliation of the scopes.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Leviticus 10:6–7; 21:1–12, paraphrased.

## Significance

The same bodily signs are forbidden in one situation and required in another. Their
meaning depends on the person’s ritual situation; outward appearance alone does not
provide a universal verdict about moral character. See [[Priesthood]], [[Clean and Unclean]],
and [[Suffering]].
