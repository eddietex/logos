# Edom and Sihon - Refused Passage and Different Outcomes

## Passages involved
- [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]] — Deuteronomy 2:24–37
- [[Sihon and Og]] — Numbers 21:21–35
- [[Edom Refuses Passage]] — Numbers 20:14–21

## Analysis

Both embassies offer to stay on the king’s highway and leave fields, vineyards, and wells alone.
Edom threatens force and comes out with a strong army; Israel turns away (20:17–21). Sihon gathers
his people, comes to Jahaz, and actually fights; Israel strikes him and occupies his territory
(21:22–25). The repeated proposal makes the divergent outcomes especially visible. Neither account
alone supplies a general rule that a refusal of transit authorizes conquest. The kinship appeal
belongs to the Edom episode and is absent from this embassy.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers, the references above. No source-language claim is made.

Deuteronomy 2:24–37 now supplies the explicit divine command to contend with Sihon, the hardening
explanation (30), and the prohibition concerning Ammon (37). The different outcomes therefore belong
to different territorial commands, not a general license to attack anyone who denies passage.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:24–37; Numbers 20:14–21.

Deuteronomy 20:10–15 now gives the general law for a city outside the land: *"proclaim peace to it"*
(10), and if it refuses, strike its males and take the rest as plunder (13–14). The embassy to Sihon
with *"words of peace"* (2:26) matches the first step. The outcome does not match the second: Sihon's
cities were destroyed *"with the women and the little ones"* (2:34), which 20:14 spares. Deuteronomy 2
grounds the war on a specific command, *"contend with him in battle"* (2:24), not on the general law,
and the wiki records the difference rather than harmonizing it. See [[Laws for Going to War]].

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:24, 26, 34; 20:10–15.

## Significance

The journey contains restraint as well as conquest. These accounts should neither be collapsed into
one battle pattern nor treated as contradictory reports of a single encounter.
