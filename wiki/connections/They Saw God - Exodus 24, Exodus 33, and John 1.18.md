## Analysis

**Scripture says both that people saw God and that no one can.** Exodus holds both statements nine
chapters apart. At 24:10–11 seventy-four people *"saw the God of Israel"*, twice over, and lived. At
33:20 God tells Moses *"man may not see me and live"*. The verb in 24:10 and in 33:20 is the same,
*rāʾâ* (MAM). 24:11 adds a second verb, *ḥāzâ*, often used of prophetic vision (MAM).

**Exodus 24 itself seems aware of the danger.** *"He didn't lay his hand on"* the nobles (24:11) only
makes sense if he might have. Jacob (*"my life is preserved"*), Manoah (*"We shall surely die"*), and
Isaiah (*"I am undone"*) all treat seeing God as something that should have been fatal. The danger is
assumed long before 33:20 states it as a rule.

**Exodus 24 describes nothing but the pavement.** The text tells what was *"under his feet"* (24:10),
not God. Ezekiel does the same: he describes a throne like *sappîr* — the same word as Exodus 24:10
(MAM) — and then only *"a likeness as the appearance of a man"*, three steps removed. The Septuagint of
Exodus 24:10–11 goes further and makes the elders see *the place* where God stood (Rahlfs). See the
passage page for the text.

**Readings.** Interpreters have reconciled the statements in several ways: the elders saw a form or
manifestation rather than God's *face*; *seeing* in 24:10 is visionary rather than physical; the
statements belong to different sources and were not meant to be harmonized; or, in Christian
reading, what was seen was the Son, who alone *"has declared him"* (John 1:18). These are reported as
standing options, not checked against a named commentary. The wiki records the tension and does not
resolve it.

### Exodus 33 in its narrative setting

[[Show Me Your Glory]] brings the tension inside a single chapter. The narrator says Yahweh
spoke to Moses face to face as a man speaks to his friend (33:11); God then says that Moses
cannot see his face and live (33:20). The comparison with friendship supports reading the
first as direct speech rather than unrestricted sight, but this is an interpretation of the
comparison. It does not cancel the explicit report of seeing in Exodus 24:10–11. Both texts
remain recorded.

Verses 21–23 promise a position on the rock, covering by God’s hand, and sight of the back
after the passing. They do not yet narrate that Moses saw it. The distinction matters:
chapter 33 sets the terms, while 34:5–7 narrates the passing and proclamation. Neither chapter
provides an anatomical explanation of the language of face, hand, and back.

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

The face Israel sees in Exodus 34:30, 35 is Moses' face, shining after divine conversation.
This does not assert that Moses saw the divine face or supply a solution to the earlier tension.
The distinction between seeing the mediator and seeing God is essential here. See
[[The Shining Face of Moses]] (WEB checked in local Exodus).

## Significance

- **The rule and its exceptions arrive together.** Exodus 33 states the rule and, in the same
  scene, lets Moses see God's back. Exodus 24 lets the elders see and lives with the consequences.
- **Seeing is followed by eating.** In Exodus 24 the vision ends in a meal, not in terror — unlike
  Manoah or Isaiah.
- **John 1:18 restates the rule and makes an exception in a person.** See
  [[Seeing and Being Seen]], and, for the naming that goes with these encounters,
  [[Why Do You Ask My Name - Genesis 32, Exodus 3, and Judges 13]].
