# My Own Vineyard - Song of Solomon 1.6 and 8.8-12

## Passages involved

- [[Dark and Lovely]] — Song of Solomon 1:6.
- [[Our Sister and My Own Vineyard]] — Song of Solomon 8:8–12.
- [[A Garden Locked and Opened]] — Song of Solomon 4:12–5:1.

## Analysis

In the stored WEB of 1:6, the woman explains that her mother's sons assigned her
vineyard work and she did not keep her own vineyard. At 8:12 her own vineyard is
before her, beside Solomon's vineyard and its payments. The recurrence brings the
opening complaint into contact with a closing assertion of possession or agency.

Both contexts also place the woman beside family voices: angry brothers in 1:6,
a plural voice planning for a little sister in 8:8–9, and the woman's answer in 8:10.
**Tension:** others describe an undeveloped sister, while she describes herself as
mature. The poem does not tell us whether this is remembered speech, competing
assessments, or another arrangement. It should not be harmonized into an invented
childhood-to-marriage timeline.

The garden explicitly represents the bride in 4:12, and she invites the beloved
into it in 4:16. That gives an internal basis for hearing the own-vineyard language
personally as well as agriculturally. Yet 8:11 describes a leased estate, and 8:12
still names payments. Personal and economic senses coexist; the text does not
explicitly identify a harem, a purchase of the woman, or her escape from one.

## Significance

The closing poem lets the woman speak about herself beside familial and royal claims.
[[Brotherhood]], [[Work]], [[Love]], and [[Wealth and Possessions]] meet in the vineyard
image. This is an internal literary correspondence, not a prediction and fulfillment.
