## Analysis

**The phrase.** In Judges 9:23 the Hebrew is *rûaḥ rāʿâ*, *an evil spirit*, sent by *ʾĕlōhîm* (WLC). In 1 Samuel the
spirit is *from Yahweh* (16:14; 19:9) or *from God* (16:15, 16; 18:10), and is named *the evil spirit*, *hārûaḥ
hārāʿâ*, when it leaves (16:23) (WLC). In the Hebrew files checked (Genesis through 2 Samuel, and Ruth) these are the
only places the spirit sent on a man is called *evil*. 1 Kings 22 is outside those files; its *lying spirit* is a
different phrase, *rûaḥ šeqer* (recalled, not checked against the Hebrew), and it is included here because it is the
other narrative in which a spirit is sent from Yahweh to bring a king down.

**What it does.** In Judges the spirit works *between* Abimelech and Shechem: the men of Shechem break faith, and
the chapter ends with both destroyed: *Thus God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech … and God repaid all the wickedness
of the men of Shechem on their heads* (Judges 9:56–57, stored WEB). In 1 Samuel it works *in* Saul: it
*troubled* him (*bāʿaṯ*, *terrify*), it is calmed by music, and twice it comes while Saul has a spear in his hand and
David is playing. In 1 Kings the spirit works through prophets, to *entice* Ahab to *go up and fall at Ramoth Gilead* (22:20).

**How the two men end.** Abimelech and Saul both die by asking their armor bearer to kill them. Abimelech, struck by a
millstone, says *Draw your sword, and kill me, that men not say of me, 'A woman killed him'*, and his young man does
(Judges 9:54). Saul, wounded at Gilboa, says *Draw your sword, and thrust me through with it*, and his armor bearer
*would not; for he was terrified* (1 Samuel 31:4). Both stored WEB. In 1 Samuel 16:21 David *became his armor bearer*.
The text does not connect the scenes; the wiki puts them together because the words *armor bearer*, *nōśēʾ ḵēlîm*,
are in all three (WLC).

**Yahweh's part.** None of these texts makes the spirit independent of God. Judges says *God sent*; 1 Samuel says
*from Yahweh*; 1 Kings has Yahweh ask *How?* and send the spirit. That is the claim of
[[Shall We Receive Good and Not Evil - Weal and Woe from One Hand]], that harm too comes from Yahweh's hand, put in
narrative. How later readers have reconciled it with God's goodness, whether by a permissive reading, by *rāʿâ* as
*harm* rather than *wickedness*, or by Yahweh's court of spirits, is not settled here (all recalled, not checked).

**What does not match.** Abimelech's spirit comes after he has killed his seventy brothers (Judges 9:5, 24), and the
text gives the cause: *that the violence done to the seventy sons of Jerubbaal might come* (9:24, stored WEB). Saul's
comes after the Spirit of Yahweh leaves him, two chapters after his rejection, and the text gives no cause in 16:14.
Ahab's is told by Micaiah as a vision of Yahweh's court, and its purpose is stated: that Ahab *may go up and fall at
Ramoth Gilead* (1 Kings 22:20).

## Significance

The thread records a hard thing the narrative says plainly three times: that a spirit from God can bring a ruler
to ruin. It also ties Saul to Abimelech, the first man in Israel to be *made … king* (Judges 9:6), more closely
than the text says outright. Both are troubled by a spirit from God, both have an armor bearer at the end, and both
die by the sword at their own request. See [[The Spirit of God]], [[Retribution]], and
[[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].

### 1 Samuel 18:6–16 now ingested

At 18:10 the evil spirit *came mightily*, *wattiṣlaḥ*, the verb of the Spirit of Yahweh's rush on Saul and on David
(10:6, 10; 11:6; 16:13), and Saul *prophesied* under it (WLC). The first spear-throw follows (18:11), and the narrator
gives the reason in theological terms, not psychological ones: *Saul was afraid of David, because Yahweh was with him,
and had departed from Saul* (18:12). The text does not connect the spirit to the jealousy of 18:8–9 in so many words;
it puts them on consecutive days, *on the next day* (18:10). See [[Saul's Jealousy of David]]. Source: stored WEB
1 Samuel 18:8–12. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### 1 Samuel 19:8–17 now ingested

The third occurrence in 1 Samuel is the shortest. *Wattǝhî rûaḥ YHWH rāʿâ ʾel-šāʾûl* (19:9, WLC), *Yahweh's evil
spirit was on Saul*: the divine name stands in construct with *spirit*, as nowhere else in the series, and the verb is
plain *was*, not *came mightily* (18:10). The harp is playing and the spear is thrown anyway (19:10); as at 18:10–11, the cure
of 16:23 does not work. See [[Michal Helps David Escape]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:23; 18:10; 19:9–10. Hebrew WLC
(OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.

### 1 Samuel 19:18–24 — the other spirit, fifteen verses later

The evil spirit of 19:9 is followed in the same chapter by *God's Spirit*, *rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* without *rāʿâ*, on Saul's
messengers and then on Saul, and he prophesies (19:20, 23, WLC). At 18:10 the evil spirit also made him prophesy, with
the same *hitpael* (WLC). So in 18:10–19:24 two different spirits come on Saul, and both are followed by prophesying.
The narrator names them differently and does not explain the relation. See [[Saul Among the Prophets at Naioth]].
