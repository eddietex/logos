## Analysis

### Curses now ingested

Both sides of the contrast are now ingested: Deuteronomy 28:7 has enemies flee seven ways; 28:25 has Israel flee seven ways. The warning belongs to the conditional national sanctions, not an account of a named campaign.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 28:15–68 and the stored comparison passages.


The niphal of *nāgap* with *lipnê*, *struck before*, is a fixed way of describing defeat (tags). With
*ʾōyəbêkem*, *your enemies*, the exact phrase comes at Leviticus 26:17, Numbers 14:42, and Deuteronomy
1:42. Deuteronomy 28:25 has the singular.

**The curse named before the land.** Leviticus 26:17 sets it among the penalties for breaking the
covenant: *you will be struck before your enemies*. Numbers 14 is the first narrative use. Moses'
warning at verse 42 is the curse of Leviticus 26, applied the morning after the people refused the
land, before they have lived in it. Numbers 14:37 used the noun *maggēpâ* for the plague on the ten.

**What it becomes.** 1 Samuel 4:3 has the elders ask why Yahweh *defeated* them, with the verb in the
active and Yahweh as its subject (*nəgāpānû YHWH*, WLC). 1 Kings 8:33 builds the phrase into Solomon's
prayer: when the people are *struck down before the enemy, because they have sinned against you*, and
*turn again*, then let Yahweh hear. That is the path Numbers 14 did not take. The people named their
sin and went up, and the prayer's sequence is to name it and turn. Deuteronomy 1:45 says the weeping
afterward was not heard.

### Blessings now ingested — Deuteronomy 28:1–14

Deuteronomy 28:7 supplies the positive counterpart to 28:25: enemies rising against Israel are defeated and flee in seven directions. In verse 25 Israel instead flees in seven directions. Both clauses are verified in the stored WEB; both units are now ingested. The contrast concerns which side suffers defeat, not a new account of a battle.

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 28:1–14 and the comparison verses cited above.

## Significance

- A curse clause from the law appears as Moses' plain forecast of a morning's battle.
- The defeat is not random. It is what the covenant said defeat would be.
