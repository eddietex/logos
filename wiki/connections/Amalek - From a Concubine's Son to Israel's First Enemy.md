# Amalek - From a Concubine's Son to Israel's First Enemy

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 36:12** — *"Timna was concubine to Eliphaz, Esau's son; and she bore to Eliphaz Amalek."*
  — [[The Descendants of Esau]]
- **Genesis 36:16** — *chief Amalek*, among the chiefs descended from Eliphaz
- **Genesis 14:7** — *"all the country of the Amalekites"*, generations before this Amalek is born
- **Exodus 17:8–16** — Rephidim; *"Yahweh will have war with Amalek from generation to generation"*
- **Numbers 24:20** — Balaam: *"Amalek was the first of the nations, but his latter end shall come to
  destruction"*
- **Deuteronomy 25:17–19** — *"Remember what Amalek did to you … you shall blot out the memory of
  Amalek from under the sky. You shall not forget."*
- **1 Samuel 15** — Saul spares Agag and the best of the livestock, and loses the kingdom
- **Esther 3:1** — *"Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite"*
- **Deuteronomy 23:7** — *"You shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother"*

## Analysis

**Nine words in a genealogy, and the Bible spends the next thousand years on them.**

> **12** Timna was concubine to Eliphaz, Esau’s son; and she bore to Eliphaz Amalek. These are the sons
> of Adah, Esau’s wife.

**That is the entire introduction of the nation Israel is commanded to exterminate.** No curse, no
prophecy, no foreshadowing, no adjective. The clause sits in the third tier of a register whose whole
tendency is generous — [[Esau]]'s line is given chiefs, kings, cities, and a country without a word of
disparagement — and the name at the bottom of it is [[Amalek]]. See
[[The Other Line First - The Order of the Toledot in Genesis]].

### The charge, and what it is actually for

**Exodus 17 records the attack; Deuteronomy 25 records the reason, and the reason is not that they
fought.**

> **17** Remember what Amalek did to you by the way as you came out of Egypt;
> **18** how he met you by the way, and struck the rearmost of you, all who were feeble behind you,
> when you were faint and weary; and he didn’t fear God. *(Deuteronomy 25:17–18)*

**Israel fights Sihon, Og, Moab's allies, and the whole Canaanite coalition without any of them being
put under a permanent sentence.** What distinguishes Amalek in the Torah's own account is **who they
attacked** — ***han-neḥĕšālîm***, the stragglers, the ones at the back — and the verdict attached to it:
*"he didn't fear God."* **It is a charge of predation on the helpless**, and it is the only place the
Bible gives a reason for a *ḥērem*. See [[The Fear of God]] and [[Vengeance]].

**And the command has a knot in it.** *"You shall blot out the memory of Amalek from under the sky.
You shall not forget."* **Israel is ordered to erase them and ordered to remember to** — which is why
the passage is read aloud in the synagogue every year on the Sabbath before Purim, and why the
tradition has never treated Amalek as a closed file.

### The thread

| | who | what |
|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 36:12** | a concubine's son | named, and nothing more |
| **Exodus 17:8–16** | the nation | the first to attack Israel after the Exodus; a war *from generation to generation* |
| **Numbers 24:20** | a foreign seer's verdict | *first of the nations*, and destruction at the end |
| **Deuteronomy 25:17–19** | the law | the memory to be blotted out, and not forgotten |
| **1 Samuel 15** | Saul | spares Agag; *"Yahweh has rejected you from being king"* |
| **1 Samuel 30** | David | an Amalekite raid on Ziklag, four hundred escape on camels |
| **2 Samuel 1** | an Amalekite | claims to have killed Saul, and is executed for it |
| **Esther 3–9** | Haman **the Agagite** | the last attempt, and the reversal |
| **1 Chronicles 4:43** | Simeonites in Hezekiah's reign | *"struck the remnant of the Amalekites who escaped"* |

**The Saul episode is the hinge.** The one command Israel's first king is destroyed for disobeying is
this one — and the man he spares is *Agag*, whose title comes back five centuries later attached to
the villain of Esther. **The book of Esther never mentions Amalek and does not have to**; *the
Agagite* is the whole argument, and the Jews' enemy is once again a descendant of Esau's
great-grandson.

### The difficulty the descent creates

**Amalek is Esau's great-grandson, which makes the Amalekites relatives** — and Deuteronomy, in the
same scroll that orders their memory blotted out, says: *"You shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is
your brother"* (23:7). **The Torah protects the great-uncle's line and proscribes the
great-grandson's.** Nothing in the text explains the difference, and the wiki does not manufacture an
explanation.

**There is also good reason to doubt the genealogy is doing genealogical work at all.**

- **Genesis 14:7** puts *"all the country of the Amalekites"* on the map in Abraham's lifetime —
  generations before the Amalek of 36:12 is born. **It is an anachronism of the same kind as *Dan* at
  14:14**, and it means the Bible's own chronology has Amalekites predating their eponym.
- **Numbers 24:20** calls them ***rēʾšît gôyîm***, *the first of the nations*, which sits badly with
  a late-arriving Edomite offshoot.
- **They range the Negev and Sinai**, not Seir — 1 Samuel 15:7 has them *"from Havilah as you go to
  Shur"*, which is Ishmaelite country (25:18), not Edomite.

**So Genesis 36:12 is plausibly an Israelite account of where a neighbour came from rather than a
record of where they came from** — the same operation the Bible performs on Moab and Ammon at 19:37–38
and on the Canaanites at 9:25. See [[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].

### What the placement does

**Putting Amalek's origin in a concubine's clause has an effect whether or not it was intended.** It
denies the enemy an origin story. **There is no scene, no offence, no founding crime** — nothing for
Israel's hatred to be anchored to in Genesis, where every other nation's character is established by
a narrative. Moab and Ammon get a cave; Canaan gets a curse; Ishmael gets a wilderness and a promise.
**Amalek gets a line in a list.**

**The Bible's most comprehensively condemned people is the one Genesis says least about.**

## Significance

- **It is the longest thread in the wiki that starts from a subordinate clause.** Nine words in a
  register carry Exodus 17, Deuteronomy 25, the fall of Saul, and the plot of Esther.
- **It shows the Torah giving a reason for a *ḥērem*, once.** Deuteronomy 25:18 is the only place the
  Bible says *why* a people is to be destroyed, and the answer is what they did to the exhausted at
  the back of the column.
- **And it shows Genesis declining to prepare the ground.** The book that will not introduce Canaan
  without a curse introduces Amalek without an adjective, in the middle of the most even-handed
  genealogy it ever writes.
