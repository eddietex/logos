# David's Count and Moses' Count - Numbers 1, 2 Samuel 24, and 1 Chronicles 21

## Passages involved

- [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]] — Numbers 1:1–54; God commands a count
- 2 Samuel 24:1, 10 — *Go, count Israel and Judah*; *I have sinned greatly* *(not yet ingested; WEB,
  bible-api.com)*
- 1 Chronicles 21:1 — *Satan stood up against Israel, and moved David to take a census of Israel*
  *(not yet ingested; WEB, bible-api.com)*
- 1 Chronicles 27:23–24 — *wrath came on Israel for this* *(not yet ingested; WEB, bible-api.com)*
- [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] — Exodus 30:12

## Analysis

**The same act has opposite verdicts.** At Numbers 1 a census is obedience: Yahweh orders it and
Israel does *according to all that Yahweh commanded Moses* (54). At 2 Samuel 24 David's heart strikes
him after the same act. Neither passage says that counting as such is sin.

**The difference is not stated.** 2 Samuel 24 does not say what was wrong. Three explanations are
common, and each is an inference: the Exodus 30 ransom was omitted; the king counted without a
command; or he trusted in his numbers. Numbers 1 cannot settle the first, because it mentions no
ransom either. The second fits the contrast best, but 2 Samuel 24:1 complicates it by saying Yahweh
*moved David against them*.

**The wrath is the same word.** Numbers 1:53 sets the Levites around the tabernacle so that there
will be no *qeṣep* on the congregation. 1 Chronicles 27:24 says of Joab's unfinished count that
*wrath came on Israel for this*, with the same noun (WLC). Chronicles does not cite Numbers.

**Both census traditions start at twenty.** 1 Chronicles 27:23 says David did not count those twenty
and under, because of the promise to *increase Israel like the stars of the sky*. That is the age
line of Numbers 1:3, and the reason given brings in [[Dust, Stars, and Sand - The Offspring Promises]].

See [[Ha-Satan - The Accuser in Job, Zechariah, and Chronicles]] for the difference between the
accounts in 2 Samuel and Chronicles.

## Significance

- The Bible does not treat counting as neutral. Who commands it matters, or at least the text
  invites that question.
