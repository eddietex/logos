# Wisdom Sends Prophets - Proverbs 1.20-33, Luke 11.49, and Matthew 23.34-37

## Passages involved

- [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]] — Proverbs 1:20–33: Wisdom calls in public, is refused, and
  pronounces the consequence.
- Luke 11:49 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Therefore also the wisdom of God said, 'I will send to them
  prophets and apostles; and some of them they will kill and persecute'"* (WEB, checked against
  bible-api.com). Jesus, attributing the saying to *"the wisdom of God"*.
- Matthew 23:34, 37 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Therefore behold, I send to you prophets, wise men, and
  scribes. Some of them you will kill and crucify …"* (23:34); *"Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the
  prophets, and stones those who are sent to her! How often I would have gathered your children
  together, even as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, and you would not!"* (23:37) (WEB,
  checked against bible-api.com). Jesus, in the first person.
- Matthew 11:19 · Luke 7:35 *(not yet ingested)* — *"wisdom is justified by her children"* / *"by all
  her children"* (WEB, checked against bible-api.com).

## Analysis

**Luke and Matthew give the same saying two different speakers.** In Luke 11:49 the Greek is *hē
sophia tou theou eipen*, *the wisdom of God said*, followed by *apostelō*, *I will send*. In Matthew
23:34 the same saying is introduced by *idou egō apostellō*, *behold, I send* — Jesus himself, with
no attribution to Wisdom (both checked against the SBLGNT). Whether Luke is quoting a lost Wisdom
writing, personifying God's wisdom, or presenting Jesus as Wisdom's mouthpiece is debated, and the
wiki does not decide it; what the two texts together show is that **one evangelist puts in Jesus'
own mouth what the other attributes to the wisdom of God.**

**Matthew then gives the saying Proverbs 1's shape.** *"How often I would have gathered … and you
would not"* — *ēthelēsa … ouk ēthelēsate*, *I willed … you did not will* — is the call and the
refusal of Proverbs 1:24–25, and the verse turns on a verb of willing as Proverbs does (*lōʾ ʾăḇîṯem*,
*you were not willing*, 1:25; *lōʾ-ʾāḇû*, 1:30). The consequence follows at once, *"Behold, your house
is left to you desolate"* (Matthew 23:38, *not yet ingested*; WEB, checked against bible-api.com). The figure of the hen gathering her chicks is not in
Proverbs; the wiki notes only that Matthew's image of protection under wings is the inverse of
Proverbs 1:27's exposure to the storm.

The verbal links are **conceptual and structural, not a quotation.** Neither Gospel cites Proverbs
1. The case for the connection rests on (1) Luke's explicit *"the wisdom of God said"*; (2) Matthew's
first-person version of the same saying; (3) the shared pattern of call, refusal, and consequence,
which Proverbs 1 is the Old Testament's fullest instance of in Wisdom's voice; and (4) the Wisdom
sayings of Matthew 11:19 and Luke 7:35, where Jesus' ministry is vindicated as Wisdom's.

## Significance

Proverbs 1 makes Wisdom a speaker who uses God's prophetic register without being God (see
[[When I Called, You Didn't Answer - Proverbs 1.24, Isaiah 65.12, and Jeremiah 7.13]]); the Gospels
take the next step and let the voice be Jesus'. That is the beginning of a line that runs through
Proverbs 8:22–31 (Wisdom present at creation; not yet ingested, see
[[The First of the Ways of God - Job 40.19 and Proverbs 8.22]]) to John 1 and Colossians 1, where the
language of Wisdom is used of the Son. The wiki records it at the point where it first becomes
visible.
