# The Heart Knows Its Own Bitterness - Proverbs 14.10 and Job's Complaint

## Passages involved

- [[A Way That Seems Right]] — Proverbs 14:10, 13.
- [[The Arrows of the Almighty]] — Job 7:11.
- [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]] — Job 10:1.
- [[Why Do the Wicked Live]] — Job 21:23–26.

## Analysis

Proverbs 14:10 sets a limit on what one person can know of another:

> The heart knows its own bitterness and joy; he will not share these with a stranger.

Verse 13 adds that the face can conceal the heart: *"Even in laughter the heart may be sorrowful, and
mirth may end in heaviness."* Neither saying contrasts the wise and the foolish. They are
observations about inwardness, and they caution against reading anyone's condition from outside.

**Job speaks from exactly that inner place.** *"I will complain in the bitterness of my soul"* (Job
7:11); *"I will speak in the bitterness of my soul"* (10:1). The bitterness is his own, and the
dialogue is largely the story of friends who would not take his word for it — who reasoned from what
they believed must be true of a sufferer to what must be true of Job. In chapter 21 Job uses the same
word for the unequal endings of two men: *"Another dies in bitterness of soul, and never tastes of
good"* (21:25), while one dies *"wholly at ease and quiet"* (21:23), and *"They lie down alike in the
dust"* (21:26).

**The proverb as a check on the friends.** Proverbs 14:10 does not mention suffering or innocence,
and it should not be made to answer Job. But it records, inside the book most given to confident
moral sentences, an admission that a heart's bitterness is known first to itself. That admission is
the one Job's friends did not make. The pairing is the wiki's; neither book refers to the other, and
the comparison rests on the shared English word *bitterness* in the WEB, not on a verified identity
of Hebrew terms.

Sources: Proverbs 14:10, 13; Job 7:11; 10:1; 21:23–26, checked in stored WEB.

## Significance

[[Mourning and Lament]] and [[Suffering]] are partly hidden from onlookers, which bears on
[[Retribution]]: if the heart's bitterness and joy are not fully shared, then outward condition is a
poor guide to inward state, in either direction.
