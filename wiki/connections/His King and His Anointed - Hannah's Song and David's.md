## Analysis

Hannah's song ends with a pair of nouns: *He will give strength to his king, and exalt the horn of his
anointed* (1 Samuel 2:10), *ləmalkô … məšîḥô* (WLC). David's song, near the end of 2 Samuel, ends with the
same pair: *He gives great deliverance to his king, and shows loving kindness to his anointed, to David and
to his offspring, forever more* (2 Samuel 22:51, WEB checked), *malkô … limšîḥô* (WLC).

| | Hannah, 1 Samuel 2 | David, 2 Samuel 22 |
|---|---|---|
| Rock | *nor is there any rock like our God* (2) | *Yahweh is my rock … God is my rock* (2–3) |
| Horn | *My horn is exalted in Yahweh* (1); *the horn of his anointed* (10) | *the horn of my salvation* (3) |
| Salvation | *I rejoice in your salvation*, *yəšûʿâ* (1) | *great deliverance*, *yəšûʿôṯ* (51) |
| King and anointed | *his king … his anointed* (10) | *his king … his anointed, to David* (51) |

**A frame for the books of Samuel.** Rock, horn, salvation, and the closing pair appear at both ends. The
two songs are commonly read as an inclusio around 1–2 Samuel, one sung before there is a king and one by
the king himself at the end of his life. The wiki reports that reading as widely held; it is not stated in
the text.

**From an unnamed king to a named one.** Hannah's *his king* has no name; the people have not yet asked
for one. David's song names him: *to David and to his offspring*. Between the two songs, the book tests
the anointed twice. Saul is anointed from *the vial of oil* (10:1) and called *Yahweh's anointed* by
David even while Saul hunts him (24:6; 26:9, 11, 16, 23, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and loses the
kingdom. David is anointed from *the horn of oil* (16:13), and the song at the end is his. The contrast
of vial and horn is often remarked on; the text does not comment on it.

**Anointed.** In the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth, *māšîaḥ* is used only of *the anointed priest*
(Leviticus 4:3, 5, 16; 6:22 WEB). 1 Samuel 2:10 is its first use of a king, and the man of God's word to
Eli a few verses later, *he will walk before my anointed forever* (2:35), is the second.

**Horn.** In the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth and 1 Samuel, a horn *exalted* (*qeren* with *rûm*)
occurs only at 1 Samuel 2:1 and 10. David's *horn of my salvation*, *qeren yišʿî* (2 Samuel 22:3, WLC), and
Samuel's *horn of oil*, *qeren haššemen* (1 Samuel 16:13, WLC), take the noun into the king's story. The
connection between the anointing horn and the song's horn is a reader's observation, not the text's.

**Psalm 18.** David's song appears again, with small differences, as Psalm 18. Its last verse (18:50 WEB,
18:51 WLC) has the same *his king … his anointed, to David and to his offspring* (WLC *malkô … limšîḥô*).

## Significance

- The first mention of a king in 1 Samuel is in a song of praise, not in a demand. The people's request in
  chapter 8 will be judged a rejection of Yahweh (8:7); Hannah's *his king* is Yahweh's gift. The book holds
  both. See [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]].
- *Anointed*, *māšîaḥ*, is the word behind *Messiah*. Its royal use begins here.
- See also [[No Rock Like Our God - The Song of Moses and the Song of Hannah]] and
  [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].

### 1 Samuel 10:1 — the first royal anointing

The first anointing Hannah's *his anointed* looked toward is now ingested in [[Samuel Anoints Saul]]. Samuel *took the
vial of oil, and poured it on his head, and kissed him*, and said *Hasn't Yahweh anointed you to be prince over his
inheritance?* (10:1). The vessel is *pak haššemen* (WLC). *Pak* occurs only here in the WLC files searched (lemma
6378). For David it is *qeren haššemen*, *the horn of oil* (16:13), the same noun *qeren* as Hannah's exalted *horn*
(2:1, 10). The wiki notes the difference in vessels and does not read a judgment into it. Yahweh's verb in 9:16 was
*ûməšaḥtô*, *you shall anoint him*; in 10:1 Yahweh is the subject, *məšāḥăḵā YHWH*, *Yahweh has anointed you* (WLC).
Samuel pours the oil, but the anointing is said to be Yahweh's. That is why Saul is later *Yahweh's anointed*, whom
David will not touch (24:6; 26:9–23, stored WEB; not yet ingested). Sources: stored WEB 1 Samuel 2:1, 10; 9:16;
10:1; 16:13; 24:6. Hebrew WLC (`1Sam.xml`), 2026-11-17.

### 1 Samuel 16:1–13 — the horn of oil, and the anointed mistaken

David's anointing is now ingested in [[Samuel Anoints David]]. Yahweh's order is *Fill your horn with oil*, *malleʾ
qarnəḵā šemen* (16:1), and Samuel takes *the horn of oil*, *qeren haššemen* (16:13) (WLC). In Genesis through 2 Samuel,
*qeren* is a vessel of oil only in these two verses (morphhb lemma 7161). Hannah's song had closed on *exalt the horn of
his anointed*, *qeren məšîḥô* (2:10, WLC). The title *məšîḥô*, *his anointed*, is spoken in this chapter before any oil
is poured, and about the wrong son: *Surely Yahweh's anointed is before him* (16:6), of Eliab. Its next uses in the book
are David's own, for Saul, whom he will not harm (24:6, 10; 26:9–23, stored WEB; not yet ingested). Sources: stored WEB
1 Samuel 2:10; 16:1, 6, 13; 24:6, 10. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.
