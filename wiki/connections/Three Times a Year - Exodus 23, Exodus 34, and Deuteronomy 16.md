# Three Times a Year - Exodus 23, Exodus 34, and Deuteronomy 16

- Deuteronomy 16:1–17 — Passover, Weeks, and Tents; verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus 23 names Unleavened Bread, Harvest, and Ingathering, joins the first to departure from Egypt
in Abib, and requires male appearance before Yahweh three times yearly. The hearer is not to appear
empty, and the first produce is brought to Yahweh's house. The agricultural year and the history of
rescue share one calendar.

Exodus 34:18–26 repeats much of this arrangement. In the raw WEB, 34:22 names Weeks with the first
fruits of wheat harvest and calls the year-end observance the feast of harvest. This differs from
the WEB's labels in 23:16; their seasonal descriptions make the comparison clear without silently
replacing either text's names. Chapter 34 adds a promise of security for the land during the three
appearances (34:24), and specifies the Passover sacrifice in its overnight rule (34:25), where 23:18
names festival fat.

Deuteronomy 16:1–17 calls the latter feasts Weeks and Tents, gives the seven-week count and a
seven-day celebration, and repeats three male appearances without coming empty. Its instructions
also include daughters, male and female servants, the Levite, foreigner, fatherless, and widow in
rejoicing. Required male appearance does not mean that women and dependents are excluded from the
feasts.

**The location differs in specificity.** Exodus 23:19 names Yahweh's house without identifying its
site. Exodus 20:24 allows the altar promise wherever God records his name. Deuteronomy 16:5–6
expressly prohibits local Passover sacrifice and directs it to the place Yahweh chooses. These
provisions should not be collapsed into an identical location rule. [[The Passover]] already records
the household-versus-chosen-place tension between Exodus 12 and Deuteronomy 16; Exodus 23 does not
resolve it by naming Jerusalem.

### The renewal in its narrative setting — Exodus 34

The calendar now belongs to [[The Covenant Renewed]]. Its placement after the calf matters: commanded annual
observances replace neither the exodus memory nor the obligation of exclusive worship. The
agricultural seasons are also bounded by seventh-day rest even during plowing and harvest
(34:21), before the pilgrimage and security promise (34:22–24). These are requirements and
assurances, not a report that Israel has already kept the feasts.

Exodus 34:1–28 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-24.

### The harvest calendar — Leviticus 23:9–22

Leviticus 23:9–22 now supplies the sheaf rite and the fifty-day count leading to the
new grain offering, two loaves, sacrifices, and an assembly without regular work.
Exodus 34:22 names Weeks in relation to wheat first fruits; Leviticus gives the count
without using that feast name in these verses. It adds priestly handling and reserved
gleanings (22), without restating the three male appearances. No fixed month-and-day
date is assigned here; the starting Sabbath's precise referent is left unresolved.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Leviticus 2, 19, 23; Exodus 23, 34.

### Gathered produce and remembered shelters — Leviticus 23:33–44

[[The Feast of Booths]] fixes the feast to the seventh month’s fifteenth day and connects
it with gathered produce (34, 39). Exodus 23:16 describes Ingathering at the year’s end;
Exodus 34:22 calls its year-end feast harvest in the stored WEB. These seasonal statements
and Leviticus’s month-and-day date are preserved without imposing a calendar-year theory.

Leviticus adds seven days of shelter-dwelling to teach later generations the departure
from Egypt (42–43). The agricultural and historical explanations reinforce one another.
The first day and the additional eighth-day assembly prohibit regular work (35–36);
seven-day rejoicing and shelter-dwelling remain distinct from that eighth day.

The native-born specification concerns shelter-dwelling (42), not an explicit ban on
foreign participation in the feast. This addition does not erase the participant list
already documented above for Deuteronomy 16. The summary at 37–38 also preserves gifts,
vows, freewill offerings, and Sabbaths alongside appointed festival offerings.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Leviticus 23:33–44; Exodus 13:3–10; 23:14–19;
34:21–24. No new quotation or source-language assertion from an unstored book is added.

## Significance

[[Festivals and First Fruits]] integrates [[Remembrance]], [[Work]], and
[[Sacrifice and Offering]]. The calendar is repeated with additional requirements and distinct
emphases; the differences in location, participants, and offerings remain visible.
