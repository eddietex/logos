# Three Times a Year - Exodus 23, Exodus 34, and Deuteronomy 16

## Passages involved

- [[The Passover]] — Exodus 12:14–20, the memorial and Unleavened Bread.
- [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] — Exodus 13:3–10, Abib and the remembered departure.
- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:14–19.
- Exodus 34:18–26 — the parallel calendar in the covenant renewal; checked in raw, not yet ingested.
- Deuteronomy 16:1–17 — Passover, Weeks, and Tents; verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus 23 names Unleavened Bread, Harvest, and Ingathering, joins the first to departure from Egypt
in Abib, and requires male appearance before Yahweh three times yearly. The hearer is not to appear
empty, and the first produce is brought to Yahweh's house. The agricultural year and the history of
rescue share one calendar.

Exodus 34:18–26 repeats much of this arrangement. In the raw WEB, 34:22 names Weeks with the first
fruits of wheat harvest and calls the year-end observance the feast of harvest. This differs from
the WEB's labels in 23:16; their seasonal descriptions make the comparison clear without silently
replacing either text's names. Chapter 34 adds a promise of security for the land during the three
appearances (34:24), and specifies the Passover sacrifice in its overnight rule (34:25), where 23:18
names festival fat.

Deuteronomy 16:1–17 calls the latter feasts Weeks and Tents, gives the seven-week count and a
seven-day celebration, and repeats three male appearances without coming empty. Its instructions
also include daughters, male and female servants, the Levite, foreigner, fatherless, and widow in
rejoicing. Required male appearance does not mean that women and dependents are excluded from the
feasts.

**The location differs in specificity.** Exodus 23:19 names Yahweh's house without identifying its
site. Exodus 20:24 allows the altar promise wherever God records his name. Deuteronomy 16:5–6
expressly prohibits local Passover sacrifice and directs it to the place Yahweh chooses. These
provisions should not be collapsed into an identical location rule. [[The Passover]] already records
the household-versus-chosen-place tension between Exodus 12 and Deuteronomy 16; Exodus 23 does not
resolve it by naming Jerusalem.

## Significance

[[Festivals and First Fruits]] integrates [[Remembrance]], [[Work]], and
[[Sacrifice and Offering]]. The calendar is repeated with additional requirements and distinct
emphases; the differences in location, participants, and offerings remain visible.
