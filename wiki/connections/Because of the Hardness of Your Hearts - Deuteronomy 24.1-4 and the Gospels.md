## Analysis

**What Deuteronomy says and what the Gospels' speakers say it says.** Deuteronomy 24:1–4 forbids one
thing: the first husband's taking back a wife who has since married another. The divorce itself is in
the conditions. In the WEB, verse 1's *"then it shall be … that he shall write her a bill of divorce"*
reads as a command; many readers hold the *then* until verse 4, and the Hebrew allows both (see
[[Divorce, Remarriage, and the Newlywed]]). The Gospel accounts give both readings. In Matthew 19:7 the
Pharisees ask *"Why then did Moses command us to give her a bill of divorce?"*, and Jesus answers that
Moses *"allowed"* it (19:8). In Mark 10:3 Jesus asks *"What did Moses command you?"*, the Pharisees
answer that Moses *"allowed"* the certificate (10:4), and Jesus says *"he wrote you this commandment"*
(10:5). The words *command* and *allow* move between the speakers in the two accounts; the WEB is
quoted, and the Greek was not checked.

**"For any reason."** Matthew's question, *"Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife for any
reason?"* (19:3), sounds like the dispute over Deuteronomy's *ʿerwat dābār*, *"some unseemly thing"*.
The Mishnah (*Gittin* 9:10) records the school of Hillel allowing divorce for anything that displeases
the husband, and the school of Shammai allowing it only for sexual misconduct. That the question in
Matthew is framed by that dispute is a common reading, reported from general knowledge; the Gospel
does not name either school, and the Mishnah reference was not checked. Matthew's exception, *"except
for sexual immorality"* (5:32; 19:9), is close to the Shammaite reading. Mark 10:11–12 and Luke 16:18
give no exception.

**Behind the law to the beginning.** Jesus answers from Genesis 1:27 and 2:24, *"he who made them from
the beginning made them male and female"* and *"the two shall become one flesh"* (Matthew 19:4–5), and
concludes *"What therefore God has joined together, don't let man tear apart"* (19:6). The law is
explained as made *"because of the hardness of your hearts"* (19:8). See
[[One Flesh - Genesis 2 and the New Testament on Marriage]].

**What Jesus' saying shares with Deuteronomy.** Deuteronomy 24:4 calls the woman *"defiled"* by her
second marriage, with respect to her first husband. Matthew 5:32 says the man who sends her away
*"makes her an adulteress"*, and the man who marries her *"commits adultery"*. Both texts treat the
second marriage as affecting her relation to the first husband, and neither describes the second
marriage as simply new. Whether Jesus' saying builds on Deuteronomy's *"defiled"* is a reading; the
Gospels do not cite 24:4.

**Mark 10:12 adds the wife.** *"If a woman herself divorces her husband, and marries another, she
commits adultery."* Deuteronomy 24 speaks only of a man sending out a woman. Mark's saying covers a
case Deuteronomy does not.

## Significance

The Gospel debate is about the conditions of Deuteronomy 24:1–4, which the law itself never rules on:
whether a man may divorce, and for what. Jesus' answer moves the question from Moses to creation, and
treats the law as a concession. The law's own ruling, that the first husband may not take her back,
does not come up in the Gospel exchange. See [[Marriage]], [[Divorce, Remarriage, and the Newlywed]],
and [[A Bill of Divorce - Deuteronomy 24.1-4, Isaiah 50.1, and Jeremiah 3]].
