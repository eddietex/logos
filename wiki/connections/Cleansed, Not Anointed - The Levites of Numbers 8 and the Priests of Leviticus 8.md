# Cleansed, Not Anointed - The Levites of Numbers 8 and the Priests of Leviticus 8

## Passages involved

- [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]] — Leviticus 8:1–36; the priests' rite carried out
- [[The Ordination of the Priests]] — Exodus 29; the priests' rite commanded
- [[The Lamps and the Levites Cleansed]] — Numbers 8:5–22; the Levites' rite
- Ezra 6:20; Nehemiah 12:30; 13:22 — priests and Levites purifying themselves *(not yet ingested; WEB
  verified)*
- 2 Chronicles 29:34 — *the Levites were more upright in heart to sanctify themselves than the priests*
  *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*
- Malachi 3:3 — *he will purify the sons of Levi* *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*

## Analysis

The two rites prepare two ranks of men for the same Tent, and each is told at length. Set side by side,
they show the difference between the ranks without the text ever stating it.

| | Priests (Leviticus 8) | Levites (Numbers 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Key word | *sanctify* (8:12, 30) | *cleanse* (8:6, 7, 15, 21) |
| Water | Moses washes them (8:6) | sprinkled, and they wash their clothes (8:7) |
| Hair | not mentioned | razor over all their flesh (8:7) |
| Clothing | new garments put on them (8:7–9, 13) | their own clothes washed (8:7, 21) |
| Oil | anointed (8:12, 30) | none |
| Blood on the person | ear, thumb, toe, and sprinkled (8:23–24, 30) | none |
| Sin offering | a bull (8:14) | a bull (8:8, 12) |
| Burnt offering | a ram (8:18) | a bull (8:12) |
| Who presents them | Moses | Aaron, for Israel (8:11) |
| Time | seven days at the door (8:33) | no term given |
| Hands of others | none on them | Israel's hands on them (8:10) |
| What is waved | portions, by Moses (8:27–29) | the men themselves (8:11, 21) |

**The direction differs.** Priests are brought to Moses and made holy. Levites are taken from Israel and
given to Aaron. In the tags no verse in the Hebrew Bible joins *māšaḥ*, *anoint*, with the Levites, and
Numbers 8 keeps *qādaš* for the firstborn (8:17).

**The bull is not a lesser animal.** The sin offering bull is the one required for the anointed priest and
the whole assembly at Leviticus 4:3, 14. So being cleansed is not a cheaper rite than being sanctified. The
Levites differ in kind and not in seriousness.

**Later books are looser.** Chronicles uses *qādaš* of Levites (1 Chronicles 15:12, 14; 2 Chronicles 29:5,
34; 30:15, 17), and at 2 Chronicles 29:34 they do it more readily than the priests. Ezra and Nehemiah use
*ṭāhēr* for both ranks together (Ezra 6:20; Nehemiah 12:30). So the distinction belongs to Numbers 8's
rite, and it is not a rule about the two words.

**Malachi takes up the word.** *Wəṭihar ʾet bənê lēwî*, *he will purify the sons of Levi* (Malachi 3:3,
WLC), uses the piel of the verb of *wəṭihartā ʾōtām*, *cleanse them* (Numbers 8:6, 15). There a refiner
does it, with fire and not water, and the result is that they offer *in righteousness*. Malachi does not
cite Numbers 8. The match is one common verb with one tribe, and an allusion is possible but unproved.
The tags show *ṭāhēr* and the name *Levi* together only there. Other verses use the gentilic *Levites*.

## Significance

Priesthood in the Torah has at least two grades, and the rites show that with oil and blood on one side
and water and a razor on the other. See [[Priesthood]], [[Clean and Unclean]], [[Holiness]],
