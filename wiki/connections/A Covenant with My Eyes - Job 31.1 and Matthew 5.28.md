# A Covenant with My Eyes - Job 31.1 and Matthew 5.28

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 3:6** — *"When the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to
  the eyes … she took some of its fruit, and ate"* — [[The Fall]]
- **Genesis 39:7** — *"his master's wife set her eyes on Joseph; and she said, 'Lie with me'"*; and
  39:9, Joseph's refusal, *"How then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?"* —
  [[Joseph in Potiphar's House]]
- **Job 31:1** — *"I made a covenant with my eyes, how then should I look lustfully at a young
  woman?"* — [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]
- **Job 31:7, 9** — *"if my heart walked after my eyes … If my heart has been enticed to a woman, and I
  have laid wait at my neighbor's door"* — [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]
- **Numbers 15:39** *(not yet ingested)* — the tassels, *"that you don't follow your own heart and your
  own eyes, after which you use to play the prostitute"*
- **2 Samuel 11:2** *(not yet ingested)* — David on the roof: *"he saw a woman bathing, and the woman
  was very beautiful to look at"*
- **Proverbs 6:25** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Don't lust after her beauty in your heart, neither let her
  captivate you with her eyelids."*
- **Matthew 5:27–29** *(not yet ingested)* — *"everyone who gazes at a woman to lust after her has
  committed adultery with her already in his heart. If your right eye causes you to stumble, pluck it
  out"*
- **2 Peter 2:14; 1 John 2:16** *(not yet ingested)* — *"having eyes full of adultery"*; *"the lust of
  the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life"*

## Analysis

**Scripture locates the beginning of the sin in the eye, and it does so from the first sin onward.**
Eve's fall is narrated as a sequence of seeing, desiring and taking (Genesis 3:6); Potiphar's wife
*"set her eyes"* on Joseph before she spoke (39:7); David saw before he sent (2 Samuel 11:2). The law
of the tassels names the same order — *heart* and *eyes*, then the *going after* that Numbers 15:39
calls prostitution.

**Job 31 is the Old Testament's most explicit statement that the look itself falls under the oath.**
Every other denial in the chapter concerns a deed or a word. The first concerns a glance, and Job does
not deny it as an act; he says he made a *covenant* against it — *bərît kāratî*, the idiom
of covenant-making, used of Yahweh and Abram (Genesis 15:18), made with a part of his own body. The rhetorical question
that follows, *"how then should I look …?"*, has the shape of Joseph's *"How then can I do this great
wickedness?"*: the thing is unthinkable to the man because of an obligation already in place. Job
then sets the same order as Numbers 15:39 inside the oath: *"if my heart walked after my eyes"*
(31:7), and only afterwards the neighbour's door (31:9).

**Matthew 5:28 states as teaching what Job 31:1 claims as conduct.** Jesus takes the commandment
against adultery and relocates it to the gaze and the heart, *"already in his heart"*, and then
prescribes something very like Job's covenant in its most violent form: if the eye causes stumbling,
remove it. Neither text treats desire as the same as the deed in its consequences; both treat it as
under the same jurisdiction. That is what makes Job's claim so large. **The friends accused Job of
public crimes against the poor (22:6–9); Job swears to a purity they never thought to question.**

## Significance

- It shows that Job's righteousness, as the book defines it, is inward as well as social. The oath runs
  from the eye (31:1) to the heart (31:7, 9, 27, 33) before it reaches the gate.
- It sets one of the Sermon on the Mount's sharpest sayings against a background already present in the
  Old Testament: the inwardness of Matthew 5:28 is a deepening of a tradition that already has Job 31:1
  and Numbers 15:39, not an innovation without precedent.
- It is one of the threads joining Job to [[The Fall]]: the chapter that opens with the eye ends its
  last denial with *"if like Adam I have covered my transgressions"* (31:33). See
  [[If Like Adam I Have Covered - Job 31.33 and Genesis 3]], [[Sexual Sin]], and
  [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
