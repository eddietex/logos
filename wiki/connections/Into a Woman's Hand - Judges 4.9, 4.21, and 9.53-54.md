# Into a Woman's Hand - Judges 4.9, 4.21, and 9.53-54

## Passages involved

- [[Deborah and Barak]] — Judges 4:9, the word spoken
- [[Jael and Sisera]] — Judges 4:21–22, the word fulfilled
- [[The Song of Deborah]] — Judges 5:24–27, the woman blessed
- Judges 9:53–54 — Abimelech and the millstone *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)*
- 2 Samuel 11:21 — Joab remembers Abimelech *(WEB checked at bible-api.com, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The word.** When Barak will not go without her, Deborah answers: *I will surely go with you.
Nevertheless, the journey that you take won't be for your honor; for Yahweh will sell Sisera into a
woman's hand* (4:9). *Sell*, *yimkōr* (WLC), is the verb of 4:2, where Yahweh *sold* Israel into
Jabin's hand. The prophecy does not name the woman. Spoken by a woman who is about to go to the battle,
it points first at Deborah herself.

**The fulfillment.** Deborah does nothing in the fighting after 4:14. The woman is Jael, introduced only
at 4:17. *Jael Heber's wife took a tent peg, and took a hammer in her hand* (4:21), *bəyādāh* (WLC):
the *hand* of 4:9 is literal. Barak, still pursuing, arrives to be shown the man he was sent to defeat,
already dead (4:22), and the honor Deborah said would not be his goes to someone not even of Israel.
The text does not draw the line from 4:9 to 4:21 itself; the arrangement does.

**The counter-echo.** Judges 9 ends the story of Abimelech, the son of Gideon who made himself king at
Shechem. At Thebez, *a certain woman cast an upper millstone on Abimelech's head, and broke his skull*.
He calls his armor bearer: *Draw your sword, and kill me, that men not say of me, "A woman killed him"*
(9:53–54, stored WEB). In the Hebrew the woman is *ʾiššâ ʾaḥat*, *one woman*, and his fear is
*ʾiššâ hărāgathû*, *a woman killed him* (WLC). Abimelech regards a woman's killing as a disgrace, the
thing Deborah told Barak about his honor. Generations later the disgrace is still remembered: Joab
expects David to say *Who struck Abimelech the son of Jerubbesheth? Didn't a woman cast an upper
millstone on him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez?* (2 Samuel 11:21, WEB, checked at
bible-api.com).

**Two directions.** In Judges 4 the woman's hand is the means of Yahweh's deliverance and Barak's lost
honor. In Judges 9 it is the end of a man who killed his brothers, *being seventy persons, on one stone* (9:5), and he tries to escape
the shame of it and is remembered for it anyway. In both a blow to the head ends a man's life: a
peg through the temple, a millstone on the skull.

**Head-crushing and Genesis 3:15.** Readers often connect Jael's blow, and the Song's *she struck
through his head* (5:26), with *he will bruise your head* in Genesis 3:15. The Hebrew verbs differ: 3:15
has *šûp*, Judges 5:26 *māḥaq* and *māḥaṣ*, and 4:21 *tāqaʿ* (WLC). The link is a reading of image,
not of vocabulary, and is not made by any text read so far. See
[[The Protoevangelium - Genesis 3.15 and the Seed of the Woman]].

**The Song's verdict.** Judges 5 adds what the prose withheld: *Jael shall be blessed above women*
(5:24). The *woman's hand* of 4:9 becomes *her hand to the tent peg, and her right hand to the
workmen's hammer* (5:26). Abimelech in 9:54 dreads the shame of *A woman killed him*; Sisera's death
at a woman's hand is, in the Song, the climax of praise. See [[The Song of Deborah]] and
[[Blessed Among Women - Jael in Judges 5.24 and Mary in Luke 1]].

## Significance

Deborah's prophecy is the only explicit prediction in Judges 4, and its fulfillment comes with a turn
Barak and the reader do not expect. Read with Judges 9, it shows Judges using a woman's hand twice
against a man's honor: once for Israel's deliverer, who loses it to another, and once against a
usurper, who could not escape it.
