# One Place or Every Place - Exodus 20.24, Leviticus 17, and Deuteronomy 12

## Passages involved

- [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]] — Exodus 20:24: *"You shall make an altar of earth for me …
  In every place where I record my name I will come to you and I will bless you."*
- [[The Place of Sacrifice and the Life in the Blood]] — Leviticus 17:3–9, 13–14: every bull,
  lamb, or goat killed in or outside the camp is brought to the door of the Tent of Meeting; the
  hunter pours out game blood and covers it with dust.
- [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]] — Deuteronomy 12:5–27: offerings only at *"the place which
  Yahweh your God shall choose"*, not *"in every place that you see"* (13); ordinary meat killed
  and eaten *"within all your gates"* (15, 21).
- [[Consecrate the Firstborn of the Herd and Flock]] — Deuteronomy 15:20–22: the firstling eaten *"in the place which Yahweh
  shall choose"*, but the defective one *"within your gates"*, *"as the gazelle, and as the deer"* —
  12:15, 22's category for home slaughter.

## Analysis

Three laws answer the question *where may Israel sacrifice and slaughter?*, and they answer it
differently.

| | Exodus 20:24–26 | Leviticus 17:1–14 | Deuteronomy 12:1–28 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setting | Sinai, after the Ten Words | the camp around the tent | the land, after rest from enemies |
| Altar | earth or uncut stone | Yahweh's altar at the tent door | Yahweh's altar at the chosen place |
| Where | *"every place where I record my name"* | the door of the Tent of Meeting | *"the place which Yahweh … shall choose"* |
| Ordinary slaughter | not addressed | brought to the tent as a peace offering (3–5) | permitted at home, like gazelle and deer (15, 20–22) |
| Blood | not addressed | on the altar; game blood poured out and covered (6, 13) | sacrificial blood on the altar (27); other blood poured on the ground like water (16, 24) |

**Exodus 20:24** speaks of more than one place. The plural *every place* is tied to God's
action — where *he* records his name — not to the worshiper's choice. Deuteronomy 12:13 forbids
offerings *"in every place that you see"*. Both verses say *every place*, but one is governed by
God's naming and the other by human sight. On the WEB wording, the two laws differ in number (many
places / one place) but agree that God, not the worshiper, determines the place. Whether Exodus
20:24 envisages simultaneous altars or successive sites is debated; the wiki does not settle it.
The question was first raised on
[[An Altar of Uncut Stones - Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 27, and Joshua 8]], since Deuteronomy itself
commands an altar on Mount Ebal (27:5–7).

**Leviticus 17** is stricter than Deuteronomy about ordinary meat. Any domestic animal killed
without being brought to the tent brings blood guilt: *"He has shed blood"* (17:4). Its stated
purpose is to end sacrifice in the open field and to the goat idols (17:5, 7). Deuteronomy 12
releases domestic slaughter from the sanctuary, giving distance as the reason (12:21) and an
enlarged border as the occasion (12:20). Its comparison, *as the gazelle and as the deer*, takes up
the category Leviticus 17:13 had already left outside the sanctuary: game, whose blood is poured
out on the ground.

The two laws are written for different settings, a camp around a single tent and a people spread
across a land. Read in canonical sequence, Deuteronomy adapts the camp rule to the land.
Historical-critical readers often take the difference as evidence of separate legal traditions,
with Deuteronomy's centralization the later one. The wiki records both readings and does not
decide between them.

**What all three keep.** None allows sacrifice to any god but Yahweh (Exodus 20:23; Leviticus
17:7; Deuteronomy 12:2–4). Leviticus and Deuteronomy agree that blood is not eaten, because it is
the life (Leviticus 17:11, 14; Deuteronomy 12:23).

## Significance

The sequence moves the question of worship from the altar's material (Exodus) to the sanctuary's
door (Leviticus) to the one chosen place (Deuteronomy). As the place narrows, ordinary eating is
separated from sacrifice. See [[The Dwelling of God]], [[Sacrifice and Offering]], [[Blood]],
[[Idolatry]], and [[Sacrifice to Yahweh Alone - Exodus 22 and Leviticus 17]].
