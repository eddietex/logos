# Elisheba Daughter of Amminadab - Aaron's Marriage into Judah

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 6:23** — *"Aaron took Elisheba, the daughter of Amminadab, the sister of Nahshon, as his
  wife"* — [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]]
- **Numbers 1:7; 2:3; 7:12; 10:14** — Nahshon son of Amminadab, head of Judah in the census
  ([[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]), the camp, the dedication offerings, and the march (later
  chapters by reference; Hebrew checked, not yet ingested)
- **Ruth 4:19–20; 1 Chronicles 2:10–11** — Amminadab and Nahshon in the line from Perez to David
  (by reference; not yet ingested) —
  [[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]]
- **Matthew 1:4; Luke 3:32–33** — the same two names in both genealogies of Jesus (by reference; not
  yet ingested)
- **Luke 1:5** — Elizabeth, Zechariah's wife, *of the daughters of Aaron* (paraphrased)

## Analysis

**Exodus 6:23 is the only verse in the genealogy that gives a wife both a father and a brother.**
Jochebed is identified by her relation to her husband (*"his father's sister"*, 6:20), and Eleazar's
wife only as *"one of the daughters of Putiel"* (6:25). Elisheba is *"the daughter of Amminadab, the
sister of Nahshon"*. **The list goes out of its way to say which family she came from**, and the
family is not Levite.

**Nahshon is the leading man of Judah in the wilderness.** In the Hebrew of Numbers he is the census
head for Judah (1:7, *lîhûdâ naḥšôn ben-ʿammînādāb*), the *nāśîʾ*, *prince*, of Judah's camp on the
east (2:3), the first of the twelve tribal heads to bring his offering when the altar is dedicated
(7:12, *hammaqrîb bayyôm hāriʾšôn*, *"the one who offered on the first day"*), and the commander over
the camp of Judah, which marches first (10:14). (Hebrew checked in the WLC; renderings are the
wiki's.) **Judah leads the camp, Nahshon leads Judah, and his sister is married to the high priest.**

**The same two men stand in the royal genealogy.** Ruth closes with the line *Perez → Hezron → Ram →
Amminadab → Nahshon → Salmon → Boaz → Obed → Jesse → David* (Ruth 4:18–22, by reference), and
Chronicles repeats it (1 Chronicles 2:10–15). **So Aaron's brother-in-law is David's
great-great-great-grandfather on the face of that list** — five generations by its count, which is
short for the span it covers, and the list is usually read as selective.

**The text draws no conclusion.** Exodus does not say why Aaron married into Judah or what it meant.
What the reader can see is that the priestly house and the house that will be royal are joined by
marriage in the first generation of the priesthood, and that the joining is recorded in the
genealogy whose whole purpose is to place the priesthood.

### Elisheba and Elizabeth

**The Greek form of *ʾĔlîšebaʿ* is *Elisabet*** — the name, in Luke 1:5, of the wife of the priest
Zechariah, described there as being *of the daughters of Aaron*. (The Septuagint form and the Lukan
wording are given from memory and paraphrased; neither has been checked.) **The first high priest's wife
and the priest's wife who opens Luke's Gospel share a name**, and the second is
identified by descent from the husband of the first. Luke draws no explicit line back to Exodus 6:23,
and the wiki records the shared name without claiming more.

## Significance

The wiki has followed the line from Perez to David since [[Judah and Tamar]]. Exodus 6:23 is where
that line first touches Levi's. **The two tribes Jacob's deathbed ranked most strangely — Levi cursed
with dispersal, Judah given the sceptre (Genesis 49:5–10) — meet in a marriage in the generation of
the exodus.** See [[Elisheba]], [[Nahshon]], [[Aaron]], [[Priesthood]], and
[[Kingship and Empire]].
