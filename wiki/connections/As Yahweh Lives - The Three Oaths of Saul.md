# As Yahweh Lives - The Three Oaths of Saul

## Passages involved

- **1 Samuel 14:39, 44–45** — [[Saul's Rash Oath]] — *as Yahweh lives, who saves Israel, though it is in Jonathan my
  son, he shall surely die*; then *God do so and more also*; the people answer with their own oath, *As Yahweh lives,
  there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground*, and *rescued Jonathan* (stored WEB)
- **1 Samuel 19:6** — [[Jonathan Speaks for David]] — *Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan: and Saul swore, "As
  Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death."* (stored WEB)
- **1 Samuel 28:10** — Saul to the medium at [[Endor]]: *Saul swore to her by Yahweh, saying, "As Yahweh lives, no
  punishment will happen to you for this thing."* (stored WEB; not yet ingested)

## Analysis

**Saul says *As Yahweh lives*, *ḥay-YHWH*, three times in 1 Samuel, and each oath is about a life.** In the Hebrew files
checked (`Gen` through `2Sam` and `Ruth`), *ḥay* with *YHWH* in Saul's mouth occurs only at 14:39, 19:6, and 28:10, and
Saul is the named subject of the *niphal* of *šāḇaʿ*, *swore*, only at 19:6 and 28:10 (WLC). The oaths are spread across
the reign: the first at its height, in the pursuit after Michmash; the second at the turn, when the order to kill
David has just been spoken aloud; the third on the night before Gilboa.

| | **14:39** | **19:6** | **28:10** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sworn to** | the people, at the lot | Jonathan, of David | the medium |
| **whose life** | Jonathan's | David's | the medium's |
| **form** | *kî môṯ yāmûṯ*, *he shall surely die* | *ʾim-yûmāṯ*, *if he is put to death* (negative) | *ʾim-yiqqǝrēḵ ʿāwōn*, *if punishment befalls you* (negative) |
| **sworn for** | a death | a life | a life |
| **what follows** | the people swear against it and rescue Jonathan (14:45) | the spear at 19:10, the messengers at 19:11 | no punishment is recorded |

(WLC; stored WEB.)

**The first oath is broken by others, the second by Saul himself.** At Michmash the people meet Saul's oath with their
own, the only *As Yahweh lives* in the book sworn against another oath, and *rescued*, *wayyip̄dû*, Jonathan
(14:45). Saul's *as Yahweh lives* is overruled and the text does not say he objected. At 19:6 no one overrules him. He
listens to Jonathan, swears David's life, and four verses later throws the spear (19:10, stored WEB). The oath that
was sworn to protect a man lasts less time than the oath that was sworn to kill one.

**The third oath is sworn by Yahweh in a house Yahweh's law forbids.** Saul had *sent away those who had familiar
spirits* (28:3), and the medium answers his request with *Why then do you lay a snare for my life?* (28:9). Saul,
disguised, swears *by Yahweh* (*bayhwh*, 28:10, WLC) that no punishment will come to her, and asks for Samuel. *Yahweh
didn't answer him by dreams, by Urim, or by prophets* (28:6, stored WEB). The oath by Yahweh's life
is the only thing in the scene that still calls on him. Of the three it is the only one the narrative does not
contradict.

**Between them the oaths trace who protects whom.** At 14:39 Saul swears against his son and the people protect him. At
19:6 his son protects David and Saul swears to it. At 28:10 Saul protects a woman his own decree had condemned, so
that she will help him. Jonathan's two appearances are the hinge: once the object of his father's oath, once the
reason for it.

### What the thread does not settle

- **Whether the second oath was sincere.** The narrator says *Saul listened* and *swore* and gives no inner thought,
  as he did three times in chapter 18 (18:17, 21, 25). At 19:9 *an evil spirit from Yahweh* is on him when he throws the
  spear (stored WEB). The text allows either reading, an oath broken in bad faith or an oath overcome; the wiki records
  both and decides neither.
- **Whether 28:10 is kept.** No punishment is narrated, and Saul dies the next day (28:19; 31:6, stored WEB). Kept
  and moot cannot be told apart.
- **Oaths in other forms.** Saul's *God do so and more also* (14:44) and his order *with an oath* (14:24, 28) are
  separate forms and are not counted here. See [[God Do So to Me - The Self-Curse Oath in Ruth and 1 Samuel]].

## Significance

- **It gives Saul's reign a spoken thread that runs from Michmash to Endor.** The book never lists Saul's oaths; they
  have to be collected. Collected, they show a king whose word about life and death is overruled once, broken once,
  and kept only where keeping it costs him nothing. See [[Saul]] and [[Oaths and Vows]].
- **It sets 19:6 against Jephthah's vow.** Jephthah's word could not be taken back, *I have opened my mouth to Yahweh,
  and I can't go back* (Judges 11:35, stored WEB). Saul's could, by others at 14:45 and by himself after 19:6. See
  [[A Father's Oath and His Child - Jephthah's Daughter and Jonathan]].
- **It shows Jonathan's advocacy as what produced the oath.** The one oath of the three sworn to spare an Israelite
  came because Saul *listened to the voice of Jonathan*. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]] and
  [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]].

### 1 Samuel 19:8–17 now ingested

The oath for David's life is broken in the next scene, and in its own vocabulary. Saul swore *ʾim-yûmāṯ*, *he shall not
be put to death* (19:6); Michal tells David *māḥār ʾattâ mûmāṯ*, *tomorrow you will be killed* (19:11), the same
*hophal* (WLC). Saul's order is *Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may kill him* (19:15). The text records no
word from Saul about the oath. See [[Michal Helps David Escape]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 19:6–17. Hebrew WLC
(OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.
