# Wisdom Better Than Strength - Proverbs 21.22, 24.5-6, and Ecclesiastes 9.13-18

## Passages involved

- [[The King's Heart]] — Proverbs 21:22.
- [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]] — Proverbs 24:5–6.
- [[The Plans of the Heart]] — Proverbs 16:32.
- [[Righteousness and Wisdom Under Question]] — Ecclesiastes 7:19.
- [[The Forgotten Wise Man]] — Ecclesiastes 9:13–18.

## Analysis

Proverbs ranks wisdom above force in the setting of war. *"A wise man scales the city of the
mighty, and brings down the strength of its confidence"* (21:22). *"A wise man has great power;
and a knowledgeable man increases strength; for by wise guidance you wage your war; and victory
is in many advisors"* (24:5–6). Proverbs 16:32 turns the comparison inward: the one who rules
his spirit is better than *"he who takes a city."* Ecclesiastes 7:19 agrees: *"Wisdom is a
strength to the wise man more than ten rulers who are in a city."*

Ecclesiastes 9:13–18 tells the defensive case. A great king besieges a little city of few men;
a poor wise man delivers it (14–15). The Preacher draws the same verdict as Proverbs —
*"Wisdom is better than strength"* (16), *"Wisdom is better than weapons of war"* (18) — the
city in Proverbs 21:22 is taken by wisdom, and the one in Ecclesiastes is saved by it.

**Agreement, with additions.** Ecclesiastes does not dispute the ranking. What it adds is
what wisdom does not win: *"no man remembered that same poor man"* (15); his wisdom is despised
and his words not heard (16); *"one sinner destroys much good"* (18). Proverbs 24:6 places
victory with many advisors; Ecclesiastes shows a single advisor whose standing — he is poor —
keeps him unheard. The two do not contradict each other: Proverbs speaks of what wisdom can
achieve, Ecclesiastes of the regard it receives.

Proverbs 21:30–31 elsewhere places victory with Yahweh rather than the prepared horse; see
[[Time and Chance and the Prepared Horse]]. Ecclesiastes 9:13–18 names no divine cause for the
deliverance and does not attribute it to chance either. That silence is left as it stands.

No literary borrowing or shared Hebrew expression is asserted; the parallel is one of theme.

## Significance

[[Wisdom]], [[War]], and [[City and Civilization]] meet in the besieged city. The comparison
sets the wisdom tradition's confidence in counsel beside an observation about
[[Remembrance]] and [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]]: wisdom can save a city without saving its
own reputation.
