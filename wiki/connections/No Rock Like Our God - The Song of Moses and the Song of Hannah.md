# No Rock Like Our God - The Song of Moses and the Song of Hannah

## Passages involved

- [[The Song of Moses]] — Deuteronomy 32:4, 15, 22, 31, 39 *(stored WEB)*
- [[The Song of Hannah]] — 1 Samuel 2:1–10 *(stored WEB)*
- Exodus 15:2, 11 — the Song of the Sea: *my salvation*; *who is like you … glorious in holiness* *(stored
  WEB)*

## Analysis

Hannah's song borrows more from the Song of Moses than from any other text in the books stored so far.
Four of its claims about God are claims Deuteronomy 32 makes first.

| Hannah (1 Samuel 2) | Moses (Deuteronomy 32) | Hebrew (WLC) |
|---|---|---|
| *nor is there any rock like our God* (2) | *The Rock, his work is perfect* (4); *their rock is not as our Rock* (31) | *ṣûr* |
| *I rejoice in your salvation* (1) | *the Rock of his salvation* (15) | *yəšûʿâ* |
| *Yahweh kills, and makes alive* (6) | *I kill and I make alive* (39) | *mûṯ* hiphil, *ḥāyâ* piel |
| *He brings down to Sheol, and brings up* (6) | *burns to the lowest Sheol* (22) | *šəʾôl* |

**The Rock.** In the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth, *ṣûr* as a name for God is concentrated in
Deuteronomy 32, where the noun occurs eight times (4, 13, 15, 18, 30, 31 twice, 37), several of them of
God. Hannah's *rock like our God*, *ṣûr kēʾlōhênû*, echoes the comparison at 32:31, *their rock is not as
our Rock*, *lōʾ ḵəṣûrēnû ṣûrām*: both deny that any other rock is like Israel's. David will open his song
with *Yahweh is my rock* (2 Samuel 22:2, WEB checked at bible-api.com); see
[[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].

**Kills and makes alive.** Deuteronomy 32:39 is God speaking in the first person, *I kill and I make
alive. I wound and I heal* (*ʾămîṯ waʾăḥayyeh*, WLC). Hannah turns it into third-person praise with
participles, *mēmîṯ ûməḥayyeh*, the same two roots in the same order. In Deuteronomy the line closes a
warning: no other god can deliver. In Hannah's mouth it opens the list of reversals that her own story
illustrates. See [[Shall We Receive Good and Not Evil - Weal and Woe from One Hand]].

**Sheol.** Deuteronomy 32:22 has God's anger burning *to the lowest Sheol*, the last of the seven
occurrences of *šəʾôl* in the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth, each of them a place one goes down to.
Hannah adds the return: *and brings up*. Whether she means resurrection or rescue from the brink, the
text does not say.

**Salvation and holiness from the Song of the Sea.** The other source is Exodus 15: *Yah … has become my
salvation* (15:2, *yəšûʿâ*) and *Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods? Who is like you, glorious in
holiness* (15:11). Hannah's *There is no one as holy as Yahweh* (2) is a statement where Moses asks a
question. *Yəšûʿâ* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth only at Genesis 49:18, Exodus 14:13,
15:2, and Deuteronomy 32:15, so three of its four occurrences before Hannah are in or beside these two
songs.

**What the comparison does not show.** Neither text cites the other, and the shared words are not rare
outside these books. The borrowing is inferred from the cluster: rock, salvation, kill and make alive,
and Sheol, all in a ten-verse song. The wiki claims the echo, not a date or a direction of dependence.

## Significance

- Hannah's song stands in the line of Israel's great songs: the sea (Exodus 15), Moses at the end of the
  wilderness (Deuteronomy 32), Deborah (Judges 5), and now a woman at Shiloh at the start of the kingdom.
  See [[Praise and Song]].
- The Song of Moses warned that Israel would forget *the Rock who became your father* (32:18). Hannah's
  song, after the book of Judges, remembers it.
- See also [[The Song of Moses in Romans and Hebrews]] and [[The Names of God]].
