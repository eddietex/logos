# Like Locusts for Multitude - Exodus 10, Deuteronomy 28, and the Midianite Raids

## Passages involved
- [[Locusts and Darkness]] — Exodus 10:5, 15. The locusts *shall eat the residue of that which has
  escaped … and shall eat every tree which grows for you out of the field*; *There remained nothing
  green, either tree or herb of the field, through all the land of Egypt* (stored WEB).
- [[The Diseases of Egypt - The Plagues in Deuteronomy 28]] — Deuteronomy 28:31, 33, 38, 42, 51. *Your
  ox will be slain before your eyes, and you will not eat any of it. Your donkey will be violently taken
  away*; *A nation which you don't know will eat the fruit of your ground and all of your work*; *for the
  locust will consume it*; *Locusts will consume all of your trees and the fruit of your ground*; *They
  also won't leave you grain, new wine, or oil, the increase of your livestock, or the young of your
  flock* (stored WEB).
- [[Midian's Oppression and the Prophet]] — Judges 6:3–5. The raiders *destroyed the increase of the
  earth … They left no sustenance in Israel, and no sheep, ox, or donkey … They came in as locusts for
  multitude* (stored WEB).
- [[The Dream of the Barley Loaf]] — Judges 7:12. *The Midianites and the Amalekites and all the children of
  the east lay along in the valley like locusts for multitude; and their camels were without number, as
  the sand which is on the seashore for multitude* (stored WEB).
- [[The Northern Kings at the Waters of Merom]] — Joshua 11:4. *Many people, even as the sand that is on
  the seashore in multitude* (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**The same insect, the opposite side.** In Exodus 10 the locusts (*ʾarbeh*, lemma 697) are Yahweh's
weapon against Egypt; Israel is spared. In Deuteronomy 28 the *ʾarbeh* becomes one of the curses that
will fall on Israel if it does not listen (28:38; WLC). In Judges 6 the curse arrives, not as insects
but as a people *as locusts*, *kədê-ʾarbeh lārōb* (6:5; WLC). The comparison is the narrator's.

**What is eaten.** The three passages describe the same loss in overlapping terms.

| | Exodus 10 | Deuteronomy 28 | Judges 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who eats | locusts | *a nation which you don't know*; the locust | Midian, Amalek, the children of the east, *as locusts* |
| What goes | every herb, all the fruit of the trees (15) | *the fruit of your ground and all of your work* (33); grain, wine, oil, livestock (51) | *the increase of the earth*; *no sustenance … no sheep, ox, or donkey* (4) |
| How much | *There remained nothing green* (15) | *until you are destroyed* (51) | *They left no sustenance* (4) |

The verb of Judges 6:4, *lōʾ yašʾîrû*, *they left no*, is the hiphil of *šāʾar* (lemma 7604, WLC). The
same verb is used of what the hail left for the locusts in Exodus 10:5 and 12 (*that which remains to
you*, niphal; *all that the hail has left*, hiphil; WLC), and of the enemy in Deuteronomy 28:51 (*won't
leave you*, *lōʾ-yašʾîr*, WLC). Exodus 10:15 says the same with another verb, *yātar* (lemma 3498):
*wəlōʾ-nôtar kol-yereq*, *there remained nothing green* (WLC).

**The ox and the donkey.** Deuteronomy 28:31 names *your ox* and *your donkey* and *your sheep* taken
before your eyes; Judges 6:4 says *no sheep, ox, or donkey* were left. The animals are the same three,
in a different order.

**"Without number."** The raiders' camels are *ʾên mispār*, *without number* (6:5; 7:12, WLC). Judges
7:12 adds *as the sand which is on the seashore*, the comparison of Yahweh's promise to Abraham
(Genesis 22:17, *as the sand which is on the seashore*) and of the Canaanite kings' armies at the
waters of Merom (Joshua 11:4). Here it is turned on Israel's enemies.

**Judges 7:12 and Joshua 11:4.** The sand clause is nearly identical in the two verses: *kaḥôl
šeʿal-śəpat hayyām lārōb* in Judges and *kaḥôl ʾăšer ʿal-śəpat hayyām lārōb* in Joshua (WLC). Only the
relative particle differs. Genesis 22:17 has the clause without *lārōb*, *for multitude* (WLC). So the
army Gideon faces is described in the words used for the last coalition Joshua defeated, and Judges 7:12
repeats 6:5's *kāʾarbeh lārōb* and *ʾên mispār* as well (WLC). In 6:5 the locust image described raiders
eating the land. In 7:12 it describes an army camped and waiting, the army the three hundred will rout.

## Significance
- Judges does not quote Deuteronomy 28, and it does not say the raid is the curse. It says Israel *did
  that which was evil* and Yahweh *delivered them into the hand of Midian* (6:1), and it describes the
  result in the images Deuteronomy had used. The prophet who follows supplies the reason: *you have not
  listened to my voice* (6:10), which is Deuteronomy's own condition, *if you will not listen to
  Yahweh your God's voice* (28:15).
- Exodus used the locust to show the difference between Egypt and Israel. Judges, like Joel later (see
  [[The Day of the Locust - Exodus 10 and Joel 1-2]]), uses it against Israel.
- The camels tie the image to the desert peoples. The raids are a migration with herds and tents
  (6:5), not a campaign.
