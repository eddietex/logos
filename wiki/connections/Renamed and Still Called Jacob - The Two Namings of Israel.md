# Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 32:28** — at the Jabbok, from a man who will not give his own name: *"Your name will no
  more be called Jacob, but Israel; for you have fought with God and with men, and have prevailed."*
  See [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]].
- **Genesis 35:9–10** — at Bethel, from God, with the promise attached: *"God appeared to Jacob
  **again**, when he came from Paddan Aram, and blessed him. God said to him, 'Your name is Jacob.
  Your name shall not be Jacob any more, but your name will be Israel.' **He named him Israel.**"* See
  [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]].
- **Genesis 17:5** — the control case: *"Your name will not any more be called Abram, but your name
  will be Abraham."* See [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]].
- **Genesis 17:15** — and Sarai to Sarah, the only woman God renames.
- **Genesis 35:21–22; 37:3; 43:6; 45:28; 46:2** — *Israel* and *Jacob* alternating in the narrator's
  own voice, sometimes in adjacent verses.
- **Genesis 46:2** — *"God spoke to Israel in the visions of the night, and said, '**Jacob, Jacob**.'"*
- **Exodus 3:6; 1 Kings 18:31; Hosea 12:2–4** — the later tradition, which keeps both names in play.
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — **Genesis 42:5, 29, 36**: **the alternation inside a
  single chapter, and the pattern is legible.** *Jacob* saw, *Jacob* said, *Jacob* withheld Benjamin
  (42:1–4); ***"the sons of **Israel** came to buy"*** the moment they cross out of Canaan (42:5); **and
  he is *Jacob* again for the whole of the grieving** — *"they came to **Jacob** their father"* (42:29),
  *"**Jacob**, their father, said… all these things are against me"* (42:36). **The covenant name travels
  with the twelve; the personal name stays with the frightened man**

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — **Genesis 45:21, 27–28**: **the switch lands on a verse boundary, at the
  exact moment of the recovery.** *"The spirit of **Jacob**, their father, revived. **Israel** said,
  'It is enough.'"* **The narrator uses the old name for the man things happen to and the new one for
  the man who decides something**, and does not mark the change. **The chapter also gives the brothers
  their national designation for the first time in a while** — *"the sons of Israel did so"* (45:21),
  ***bənê yiśrāʾēl*** — **in a verse about loading carts.** See
  [[The Spirit of Jacob Revived - Ruach Returning]]
- **Genesis 46:1–5** — [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]]. **The
  alternation runs four times in five verses** — *Israel traveled* (46:1), *God spoke to Israel* (46:2),
  *Jacob rose up* (46:5), *the sons of Israel carried Jacob* (46:5) — **and God, in the one speech He
  makes in the whole Joseph cycle, uses the old name twice**: *"Jacob, Jacob!"* **The narrator says
  *Israel* in the same verse in which the voice says *Jacob*, and Genesis does not remark on it**

## Analysis

### The same renaming, done twice, in two completely different registers

| | **32:28 — the Jabbok** | **35:10 — Bethel** |
|---|---|---|
| **who speaks** | *a man*, who refuses to give his own name | *God*, who states his: *ʾĒl Šadday* |
| **when** | night, in a wrestling hold | *"God appeared to Jacob again"* — a theophany |
| **reason given** | *"you have fought with God and with men, and have prevailed"* | **none at all** |
| **what comes with it** | a blessing, unspecified; a dislocated hip | nation, company of nations, kings, the land |
| **narrator's note** | none | *"He named him Israel"* — the naming attributed to God |
| **what Jacob does next** | limps into the meeting with Esau | sets a pillar, pours a drink offering |

**The second telling is not a correction of the first; it is the first one's missing half.** The
Jabbok gives a name and withholds a promise — the wiki has already noted that 32:28 is the one
covenant moment in the Jacob cycle with **no covenant content in it**. Bethel supplies the content and
withholds the reason. **Neither scene is complete on its own**, and Genesis lets them stand
side by side without cross-referencing them beyond the single adverb *again*.

**That adverb is the text's own acknowledgement that it is repeating itself.** *Wayyērāʾ … ʿôd* —
*appeared again* — is the narrator flagging a second occurrence rather than hiding one.

### The standard explanations, and what the wiki does with them

1. **Composite sources.** 32:22–32 and 35:9–15 are two accounts of the same tradition, one associated
   with the Jabbok and the other with Bethel, joined without harmonization. This is the mainstream
   critical reading, and it has the merit of explaining why the reason-clause appears in exactly one
   of them.
2. **Confirmation.** The name given in the dark by an unidentified assailant is ratified in daylight
   by God, who adds the promise — a pattern with parallels in the book, where a private assurance is
   later made public (26:24 after 26:2, or the repeated land-promises to Abram).
3. **A name given and a name kept.** The Jabbok grants it; Bethel is where Jacob is finally in a
   position to receive it, having done what 28:20–22 committed him to. The renaming waits for the vow.

**The wiki adopts none.** All three account for the doubling; none accounts for what happens
afterwards, which is the more interesting fact.

### The name does not take, and Abram's did

**This is the part that is easy to miss.** Genesis 17:5 renames Abram, and from 17:5 onward
**the name *Abram* never appears again in the narrative.** Not once in thirty-three chapters. The old
name is used afterwards only when the text is quoting history back — Nehemiah 9:7, and Genesis's own
retrospect — and never by the narrator in the present tense.

**With Jacob the opposite happens.** After 35:10 the book goes on calling him **Jacob** constantly,
and *Israel* only sometimes, with no visible rule:

> **35:21** *Israel* traveled … **35:22** *Israel* heard of it. Now the sons of ***Jacob*** were
> twelve.

**Two names for the same man in one verse.** The alternation runs to the end of the book — *Israel
loved Joseph* (37:3), *Jacob their father* (42:36), *Israel said* (43:6), *Jacob's spirit revived*
(45:27) and *Israel said* in the very next verse — and Genesis never marks a transition. **And God
himself uses the old name after giving the new one**: *"God spoke to **Israel** … and said, '**Jacob,
Jacob**'"* (46:2).

**Attempts to find the rule are numerous and none of them holds across all the cases.** The best
known — *Israel* for the patriarch-as-nation and in scenes of dignity, *Jacob* for the man and in
scenes of weakness — works often and fails at 35:22 and 46:2 among others. **The wiki records the
alternation as a fact of the text and does not supply a key for it.**

### What that asymmetry might mean, stated as a question rather than an answer

Abram's renaming is part of the circumcision covenant, sealed in flesh, and it replaces him. **Jacob's
is given twice and replaces nothing.** He is the man whose name was an accusation from the start —
*"Isn't he rightly named Jacob? For he has supplanted me these two times"* (27:36) — and the book that
grants him a better one goes on using the old one to the last chapter. See
[[Names and Naming]] and [[The Stolen Blessing]].

Hosea, the one prophet who retells the Jabbok, does the same thing: *"Yahweh has also a controversy
with **Judah**, and will punish **Jacob** according to his ways… In the womb he took his brother by
the heel; and in his manhood he contended with God"* (Hosea 12:2–3). **He uses the name the wrestling
was supposed to retire, in the sentence about the wrestling.**

## Significance

- **The name *Israel* enters Scripture twice and leaves its owner's own name in place**, which is not
  what happened to Abram and not what a renaming is normally for. The covenant people take the new
  name; the man keeps both.
- **The doubling is where the Jacob cycle's two theologies meet.** One says the name was seized in a
  fight, at night, from someone who would not identify himself, and cost a hip. The other says it was
  given, in daylight, by *ʾĒl Šadday*, with a nation and a land attached and nothing asked in return.
  **Genesis prints both and reconciles neither**, in a book that has already printed two creation
  accounts, two explanations of Jacob's flocks, and three wife-sister episodes. See
  [[Two Creation Accounts - Genesis 1 and Genesis 2]] and
  [[Two Explanations for One Flock - The Rods and the Dream]].
- **And the alternation afterwards is the book refusing to let the man become a symbol.** *Israel* is
  the nation; *Jacob* is the one who bought a birthright with soup, and Genesis will not stop saying
  so.

**Genesis 49 is now ingested**, in [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]], **and it is the book's most balanced
use of the two names: five *Jacob* and five *Israel* in thirty-three verses.** **Twice they stand in
one line** — *"I will divide them in **Jacob**, and scatter them in **Israel**"* (49:7) **and *"the
Mighty One of **Jacob** … the stone of **Israel**"*** (49:24). **49:2 inverts 48:2's division**: there
*Jacob* was told and *Israel* sat up; here the sons summoned are *Jacob's* and the father they are to
hear is *Israel*. **And the death is the plain name**: *"When **Jacob** finished charging his sons"*
(49:33).
