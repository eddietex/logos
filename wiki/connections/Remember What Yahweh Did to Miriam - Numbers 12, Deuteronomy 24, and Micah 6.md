# Remember What Yahweh Did to Miriam - Numbers 12, Deuteronomy 24, and Micah 6

- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:10–15
- **Deuteronomy 24:8–9** — *Be careful in the plague of leprosy … Remember what Yahweh your God did to Miriam, by the way as you came out of Egypt.* (WEB) — [[Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning]]
- **Micah 6:4** — *I sent before you Moses, Aaron, and Miriam.* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Two memories of one woman.** Deuteronomy remembers Miriam as a warning, and it sets her in a law on leprosy and obedience to the priests. Micah remembers her as a gift, one of three whom God sent, in a list of his saving acts. Neither denies the other. By the tags her name occurs in 13 verses, including 1 Chronicles 4:17, which appears to name someone else, and Micah 6:4 is its only use in the prophets.

**What Deuteronomy assumes.** The verse does not say what Miriam did or what was done. It expects Israel to know. Set after verse 8, the memory seems to teach that the rules about the disease are to be kept even with someone as honored as she was, and it may also warn about speech. The verse does not say which.

**After the seven days.** Numbers says nothing of her again until her death at 20:1. There is no word of her restored standing. Micah's line is the nearest thing to it.

**Remember Miriam, remember Amalek.** In the WLC, 24:9 and 25:17 open with the same words, *zākôr ʾēt
ʾăšer-ʿāśâ*, and close with the same words, *baddérek bǝṣēʾtǝkem mimmiṣrāyim*. In the WEB: *"Remember what
Yahweh your God did to Miriam, by the way as you came out of Egypt"*, and *"Remember what Amalek did to you
by the way as you came out of Egypt"*. The infinitive absolute *zākôr* occurs in Deuteronomy only at 7:18,
24:9, and 25:17 (WLC lemma search). One memory is of what Yahweh did to one of Israel's own leaders; the
other of what an enemy did to Israel. Whether the parallel is meant to be read, the text does not say. See
[[Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 24:9; 25:17.

## Significance

Israel's memory did not need to choose. The same person can be the sister who was struck and the leader who was sent. See [[Remembrance]] and [[Miriam]].
