# Sweet Sleep - Proverbs 3.24 and Ecclesiastes 5.12

## Passages involved

- [[Do Not Withhold Good]] — Proverbs 3:21–26, especially 24.
- [[The Labor Left to Another]] — Ecclesiastes 2:18–26, especially 23.
- [[The Lover of Silver and the Gift of Enjoyment]] — Ecclesiastes 5:12, 18–20.

## Analysis

Proverbs 3:21–26 associates retained wisdom and discretion with secure steps, freedom from
fear, and sleep that is sweet. Yahweh is named as the ground of confidence (26). The
promised rest belongs within instruction about how the son lives and whom he trusts.

Ecclesiastes 5:12 also calls sleep sweet, now for a laboring man whether he eats little or
much. The contrast is the rich person's abundance, which will not let him sleep. This is
not an assertion that the worker has practiced Proverbs' instruction or that every rich
person is a fool. The named difference is labor and abundance, not an explicit classification
of moral character.

Ecclesiastes 2:23 prevents a simple rule that labor always guarantees rest: there the
worker's heart has no rest even at night. The speaker is grieving labor that must pass to
another (2:18–21). Ecclesiastes can therefore portray labor both with sweet sleep and with
sleepless distress. The observations concern different situations; neither cancels the
other or warrants diagnosing a particular sleeper's righteousness.

Both Ecclesiastes units subsequently commend enjoyment from God (2:24; 5:18–20). The
wealthy are not excluded from that gift: 5:19 explicitly joins given wealth with power to
enjoy it. The sleep contrast exposes the inadequacy of abundance to secure well-being,
without making poverty or any particular occupation a guarantee of peace.

## Significance

[[Work]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]] must be assessed by what happens to the worker,
not only by the size of the store. [[Joy and Enjoyment]] can belong within labor's limits,
while [[Suffering]] can persist even where wisdom and work are present.
