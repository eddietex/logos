# Ashes and Conscience - Numbers 19 and Hebrews 9

## Passages involved
- [[The Red Heifer]] — Numbers 19:1–22
- Hebrews 9:13–14 — verified WEB; book not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Hebrews names the ashes of a heifer sprinkled on defiled persons and grants their efficacy
for bodily cleanness (9:13). Its comparison moves from that purification to Christ’s
self-offering and the cleansing of conscience for service to the living God (9:14).
Numbers supplies the actual rite: stored ashes, water, sprinkling, and cleansing after
contact with human death. Hebrews uses that rite in an argument of greater efficacy;
it does not claim the ashes failed to do what the law assigned them.

The distinction matters: Numbers 19 does not itself identify the heifer with a future
Messiah, and Hebrews does not explain the red color, cedar, scarlet, or every detail
as a separate prediction. The typology is explicit in Hebrews’ comparison, while any
further item-by-item symbolism would be interpretation beyond these verses.

Bodily purification and cleansing of conscience are related without collapsing their
