# Drawn Away from the City - Ai and Gibeah

## Passages involved

- [[The Capture of Ai]] — Joshua 8:5–6, 14–22: the ambush west of the city, the feigned flight,
  *"They flee before us, like the first time"*, the city left open, and the smoke that *"ascended up
  to heaven"* (stored WEB).
- Judges 20:13–48 — Israel against Benjamin at Gibeah (WEB, verses 13, 21, 25, and 29–38 verified at
  bible-api.com; not yet ingested).

## Analysis

The two battles follow the same plan, and Judges tells it in Joshua's vocabulary:

| | Ai (Joshua 8) | Gibeah (Judges 20) |
|---|---|---|
| A prior defeat | the rout of 7:4–5 | two days of defeat, 20:21, 25 |
| The ambush | *Set an ambush for the city behind it* (8:2) | *Israel set ambushes all around Gibeah* (20:29) |
| The defenders' confidence | *They flee before us, like the first time* (8:6) | *They are struck down before us, as at the first* (20:32) |
| The drawing away | *until we have drawn them away from the city* (8:6); *were drawn away from the city* (8:16) | *were drawn away from the city* (20:31); *draw them away from the city to the highways* (20:32) |
| Bethel on the road | the ambush between Bethel and Ai (8:9, 12) | *the highways, of which one goes up to Bethel* (20:31) |
| The smoke | *the smoke of the city ascended up to heaven* (8:20) | the appointed sign, *a great cloud of smoke* (20:38) |
| The defenders unaware | *he didn't know that there was an ambush* (8:14) | *they didn't know that disaster was close to them* (20:34) |

All WEB; the Judges column from bible-api.com, 2026-11-10, with 20:39–48 not yet checked beyond the
summary given here.

The wiki has not compared the Hebrew of Judges 20 with Joshua 8 in this pass, so the shared English
phrases are not claimed as shared Hebrew phrases. Whether one narrative was shaped on the other is
not something the texts state.

## Significance

At Ai the stratagem is Yahweh's (*Set an ambush*, 8:2) and is used against a Canaanite city after
Israel has dealt with sin in its own camp. At Gibeah the same stratagem is used by Israel against
one of its own tribes, after Benjamin refused to give up the men of Gibeah (Judges 20:13). The
parallel shows the conquest's methods turned inward at the end of Judges — a shape the wiki
notes now and will revisit when Judges is ingested.
