# Watching from Far Off - Hagar and Moses' Sister

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 21:14–19** — Hagar puts Ishmael under a shrub and *"sat down opposite him, a good way
  off, about a bow shot away. For she said, 'Don't let me see the death of the child'"* (21:16);
  *"God heard the voice of the boy"* (21:17); *"God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water"*
  (21:19) — [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]]
- **Exodus 2:3–6** — the mother lays the basket in the reeds; *"His sister stood far off, to see
  what would be done to him"* (2:4); *"behold, the baby cried"* (2:6) — [[The Birth of Moses]]

## Analysis

**Two children are set down near water to die, and in each case a woman of the family watches from
a distance.** The word is shared: Hagar sits *harḥēq*, *a good way off*; the sister stands
*mērāḥōq*, *far off* — both from *rāḥaq*, *to be far*.

| | Genesis 21 | Exodus 2 |
|---|---|---|
| the child | Ishmael, a boy cast out of Abraham's house | Moses, a boy born under a decree of death |
| set down | *"under one of the shrubs"* (21:15) | *"in the reeds by the river's bank"* (2:3) |
| the watcher | his mother, Hagar | his sister |
| her posture | *sat*, a bow shot away | *stood* (*hityaṣṣēb*, took her stand) |
| her purpose | *"Don't let me see the death of the child"* | *"to see"* — literally *to know* — *"what would be done to him"* |
| the cry | *"God heard the voice of the boy"* (21:17) | *"behold, the baby cried"* (2:6) |
| who answers | the angel of God, from the sky | Pharaoh's daughter, from the riverbank |
| the water | a well, after God *"opened her eyes"* (21:19) | the river itself, drawn out of |

**The postures are opposites.** Hagar has given up; she sits down and turns her face from what she
expects. The sister stands and waits to *know*. **Both children are saved, and in both the rescue
comes from outside — in Genesis from God by name, in Exodus from an Egyptian princess with no
mention of God.**

**The boy's cry is heard in both stories, and by different ears.** Genesis makes the hearing God's
(and Ishmael's name means *God hears*); Exodus gives the hearing to Pharaoh's daughter, who sees
*"the baby cried"* and pities him. **God's own hearing in Exodus waits until 2:24** — *"God heard
their groaning"* — when the whole people cries. See [[Hagar]] and [[Miriam]].

**Hagar is an Egyptian; the rescuer in Exodus is an Egyptian.** In Genesis an Egyptian mother is
helpless and God rescues her son; in Exodus an Egyptian princess is the rescuer of a Hebrew son. The
wiki already reads Hagar's story as an exodus in reverse — see
[[Affliction and Flight - Hagar and the Exodus]] — and this scene extends the mirror.

## Significance

The parallel is lexical and situational, not proof of direct literary dependence; the wiki records
it at that strength. It marks the shape of rescue the Torah keeps returning to — a child abandoned
to death by water or by thirst, a watcher who cannot act, and a cry that someone hears. See
[[Providence]], [[Seeing and Being Seen]], and [[Life and Death]].
