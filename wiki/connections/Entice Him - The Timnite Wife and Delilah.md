# Entice Him - The Timnite Wife and Delilah

## Passages involved

- [[Samson's Riddle]] — **Judges 14:15–17** — *Entice your husband, that he may declare to us the
  riddle, lest we burn you and your father's house with fire* (15); *You just hate me, and don't love
  me* (16); *she pressed him severely* (17). Stored WEB.
- Judges 16:4–21 — *Entice him, and see in which his great strength lies* (16:5); *How can you say, 'I
  love you,' when your heart is not with me?* (16:15); *When she pressed him daily with her words, and
  urged him, his soul was troubled to death* (16:16). Stored WEB; not yet ingested, see *Samson and
  Delilah* on [[Judges]].

## Analysis

The two scenes share their verbs (all WLC):

| | Judges 14 | Judges 16 |
|---|---|---|
| The command | *pattî ʾet-ʾîšēk*, *Entice your husband* (15) | *pattî ʾôtô*, *Entice him* (5) |
| Who gives it | the thirty companions, with a threat to burn her and her father's house | the lords of the Philistines, with eleven hundred pieces of silver each |
| What is sought | the riddle's answer | *in which his great strength lies* |
| Her charge | *You just hate me, and don't love me* (16) | *How can you say, 'I love you,' when your heart is not with me?* (15) |
| The pressure | *hĕṣîqathû*, *she pressed him* (17) | *hēṣîqâ lô*, *she pressed him* (16) |
| How long | *the seven days* (17) | *daily*, *kol-hayyāmîm* (16) |
| What he gives up | the lion and the honey | *No razor has ever come on my head* (17) |

*Pātâ* (lemma 6601) means *entice, deceive*: in the stored books it is the seducer of Exodus 22:16 (WEB;
22:15 in WLC numbering), *If a man entices a virgin*, and the warning of Deuteronomy 11:16, *Be careful,
lest your heart be deceived*. In the WLC data the same lemma number is also attached to *yapt*, *enlarge*,
in Genesis 9:27; that is a homonym, and it is set aside here. *Ṣûq* (lemma 6693) occurs in the WLC files
of Genesis through Judges only in these two verses and in Deuteronomy 28:53, 55, 57, *in the distress
with which your enemies will distress you* (stored WEB 28:53), of the siege.

The first scene is the smaller one in every way. The secret is a riddle and not his strength; the cost
is sixty garments and not his eyes; the woman acts under threat of fire and not for silver. But the
mechanism is identical: the Philistines cannot overcome Samson, so they work through the woman he chose,
and she works through his love.

**Two things differ.** The Timnite wife says nothing of Samson's love as a claim he made; she accuses him
of not loving her. Delilah quotes him: *How can you say, 'I love you'*. And Judges 16:4 says *he loved a
woman in the valley of Sorek*, the first time the book says Samson loved anyone; chapter 14 says only that
the Timnite *pleased* him (14:3, 7). The text gives the Timnite wife a motive, the threat of fire; it gives
Delilah one too, the silver. It passes no verdict on either.

## Significance

- **Samson does not learn.** He loses the riddle to a woman's pressing at Timnah and loses his hair to the
  same pressing at Sorek. The book tells both without commenting on the repetition.
- **The threat is carried out anyway.** The Timnite wife gives up the riddle to save herself and her
  father's house from fire; at 15:6 *the Philistines came up, and burned her and her father with fire*
  (stored WEB). Compliance does not save her.
- **Both scenes are framed by the Spirit or its absence.** The Timnah scene ends with *Yahweh's Spirit came
  mightily on him* (14:19). The Delilah scene ends with *he didn't know that Yahweh had departed from him*
  (16:20, stored WEB).
