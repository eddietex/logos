# A Place to Flee - Exodus 21 and Numbers 35

## Passages involved

- [[The Wicked Flee When No One Pursues]] — Proverbs 28:17.

- [[Laws of Violence and Injury]] — Exodus 21:12–14, 29–30: refuge for unintentional killing, no altar protection
  for planned murder, and a possible ransom for the negligent bull owner.
- Numbers 35:9–34 — cities of refuge, adjudication, witnesses, and prohibited ransoms; verified
  WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus 21:13 promises a place to which the unintentional killer may flee. Verse 14 denies even the
altar to a person who schemes to kill a neighbor. It names neither a city nor the procedure for
deciding the difference. Numbers 35:11–15 specifies six cities and makes refuge available to
Israelites, strangers, and resident foreigners. The congregation is to judge between the killer
and the avenger (35:24–25); one witness cannot establish a capital case (35:30).

Refuge is protection with conditions, not a declaration that nothing happened. The person judged
to have killed without hostility must remain until the high priest's death, and leaving the
boundary exposes him to the avenger (35:25–28). This develops the distinction Exodus already
makes between deliberate and unintentional killing; it does not turn every killer into the same
kind of offender.

**The ransom provisions must also stay distinct.** Exodus 21:29–30 permits an imposed ransom for
the owner who failed to restrain a bull known to be dangerous. Numbers 35:31 forbids ransom for a
murderer, and 35:32 separately forbids payment for an early return from refuge. Thus it is too
broad to say that the Torah refuses ransom in only one case. The cases have different grounds and
outcomes; money is not a universal means of canceling liability.

### Proverbs 28 in context

Proverbs describes the person tormented by life blood as a fugitive until death, without support.
Its brief saying gives no account of intent, witnesses, or trial. Exodus 21:12–14 expressly
distinguishes an unintentional killing from a planned one. Reading Proverbs as an instruction to
deny all refuge would contradict the protection Exodus grants; the proverb should not erase the
law’s distinctions. This addition compares the stored WEB of Proverbs and Exodus only.

## Significance

Refuge and punishment both require distinctions that an unrestricted cycle of retaliation would
ignore. See [[Whoever Sheds Man's Blood]], [[Judgment]], and [[Redemption]]. The two passages do
not supply evidence for identifying a modern refuge site or reconstructing all historical practice.
