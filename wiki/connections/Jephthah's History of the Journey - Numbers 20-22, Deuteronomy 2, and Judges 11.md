# Jephthah's History of the Journey - Numbers 20-22, Deuteronomy 2, and Judges 11

## Passages involved
- [[Jephthah's Message to the King of Ammon]] — Judges 11:12–28; the journey retold to Ammon's king.
- [[Edom Refuses Passage]] — Numbers 20:14–21; the embassy to Edom from Kadesh.
- [[The Journey to Moab]] — Numbers 21:10–20; camped beyond the Arnon, *the border of Moab*.
- [[Sihon and Og]] — Numbers 21:21–35; Sihon's refusal, Jahaz, and the land from the Arnon to the
  Jabbok.
- [[Balak Sends for Balaam]] — Numbers 22:1–21; Balak hires a curse.
- [[Around Seir and Past Moab and Ammon]] — Deuteronomy 2:1–23; Edom, Moab, and Ammon not to be

- [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]] — Deuteronomy 2:24–37; the embassy to Sihon, the hardening, and
  the battle.
- [[The Covenant at Shechem]] — Joshua 24:9; *Balak … arose and fought against Israel*.

## Analysis

Judges 11:15–26 retells the wilderness road from Egypt to the Jabbok. Jephthah tells it to win a border
dispute. (Joshua 24:2–13 retells more of the history, from Terah to the land, but passes over these
embassies.) Set beside Numbers and Deuteronomy, it agrees on most
points, follows one source's wording closely, and departs from both in two places.

| Point | Jephthah (Judges 11) | Numbers | Deuteronomy | Joshua |

**Where Jephthah follows Numbers.** His sentence on the Arnon has the same four Hebrew words as Numbers
21:13, *kî ʾarnôn gəbûl môʾāb* (WLC). His Sihon verse shares *Sihon gathered all his people*, Jahaz, and
*fought* with Numbers 21:23 (*wayyeʾĕsōp sîḥōn ʾet-kol-ʿammô … wayyillāḥem*, WLC). He gives the bounds
of the land as Numbers 21:24 does, *from the Arnon … to the Jabbok*.

**Where he follows Deuteronomy.** He credits Yahweh with the victory (11:21), as Deuteronomy 2:33 does
and Numbers 21:24 does not. His denial that Israel took Moab's or Ammon's land (11:15) is the outcome
Deuteronomy 2:9 and 2:19 command, though he does not cite the command.

**Where he gives his own reason.** Sihon *didn't trust* Israel, *lōʾ heʾĕmîn* (WLC). Numbers says Sihon
*would not allow*, *lōʾ nātan*; Deuteronomy says Yahweh *hardened his spirit*. Three accounts give three
descriptions of one refusal. They are not exclusive: a king can distrust, refuse, and be hardened at
once. But none of the three texts says the others' reason.

### The two departures

**1. The king of Moab.** Jephthah says Israel sent to Moab's king and *he would not* (11:17). Numbers
records no message to Moab. Deuteronomy 2:29 has Moses tell Sihon, *Just let me pass through on my feet,
as the children of Esau who dwell in Seir, and the Moabites who dwell in Ar, did to me*. On that reading
Moab, like Edom, let Israel pass and sold it food and water (2:28). Deuteronomy 23:3–4 then says the
opposite of Moab and Ammon: they *didn't meet you with bread and with water on the way*.

So the canon has three positions on Moab's response: no record (Numbers), cooperation (Deuteronomy
2:29), and refusal (Judges 11:17; compare Deuteronomy 23:4). Numbers 20 and Deuteronomy 2:29 already
differ the same way on Edom. A possible reconciliation distinguishes passage *through* a land, refused,
from trade along its edge, allowed. None of the texts draws that distinction, and the wiki records the
accounts without it.

**2. Balak.** Jephthah asks, *Did he ever strive against Israel, or did he ever fight against them?*
(11:25), expecting no. Joshua 24:9 says *Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab, arose and fought against
Israel*. The verb is the same in both, *lāḥam* (*nilḥōm nilḥam* in Judges 11:25, *wayyillāḥem* in Joshua
24:9, WLC). Numbers 22 narrates a hired curse and no battle. Joshua's next clause, *He sent and called
Balaam the son of Beor to curse you*, may explain its *fought* as the curse. Jephthah may mean that Balak
never went to war over the land north of the Arnon. Either way the two sentences, read plainly,
disagree, and both are recorded here. See [[Balak]].

### A third oddity: Chemosh

Jephthah tells Ammon's king, *Won't you possess that which Chemosh your god gives you to possess?*
(11:24). In the files read for this ingest (Genesis, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, WLC), Chemosh
occurs only here and in Numbers 21:29, *people of Chemosh*, of Moab. The likeliest link is the land
itself. Numbers 21:26 says Sihon took it from *the former king of Moab*. The king of Ammon claims land
that was once Moab's, and Jephthah answers with Moab's god. That is an inference; the text gives no
reason. See [[Chemosh]].

### The three hundred years

Jephthah's *three hundred years* (11:26) is the only number in this history. It is weighed with the other
chronological anchors in [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]]. The periods Judges itself
states from 3:8 to 10:8 add up to 319 years, before Joshua's years are counted. That sum is the wiki's,
and it does not settle whether Jephthah's figure is round or the book's periods overlap.

## Significance

- **The Torah's history is used as evidence.** Jephthah argues a border case from the road out of Egypt.
  Earlier in Judges the exodus is recalled by Yahweh's angel, a prophet, and Gideon (2:1; 6:8–9, 13);
  none of them tells the road beyond Egypt.
- **The retelling is close but not exact.** It agrees with Numbers in wording and with Deuteronomy in
  theology, and departs from both on Moab. The wiki treats the speech as one more witness, not as a
  correction of the Torah or a careless copy of it.
- **The quarrel is about who gives land.** Jephthah's central claim, *whoever Yahweh our God has
  dispossessed from before us, them will we possess* (11:24), is the same claim Deuteronomy 2 makes from
  the other side: Yahweh gave Seir to Esau and Ar and Ammon to Lot's sons (2:5, 9, 19). See
  [[Land and Sojourning]] and [[Ammon Protected and Ammonite Land Allotted]].
