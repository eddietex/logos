# To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 14:13** — *"One who had escaped came and told Abram, the Hebrew"* — ***happālîṭ*** —
  [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]
- **Genesis 45:7** — *"God sent me before you to preserve for you a **remnant** in the earth, and to
  save you alive by a **great deliverance**"* — ***šəʾērît*** and ***pəlêṭâ gədōlâ*** —
  [[Joseph Reveals Himself]]
- **Isaiah 10:20–22** — *"a remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God… only a
  remnant of them will return"*
- **Amos 5:15** — *"it may be that Yahweh, the God of Armies, will be gracious to the remnant of
  Joseph"*
- **Micah 2:12; 4:7; 5:7–8** — the remnant gathered *"as the sheep of Bozrah"*
- **Ezra 9:8** — *"now for a little moment grace has been shown from Yahweh our God, to leave us a
  remnant to escape"* — ***pəlêṭâ*** **and** ***šəʾērît*** **together, as at 45:7**
- **Romans 9:27; 11:5** — *"even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to
  the election of grace"*

## Analysis

***Šəʾērît*** — *remainder, what is left over* — **appears for the first time in Scripture at Genesis
45:7**, in a sentence [[Joseph]] speaks to his brothers about a famine. **Nothing about the context is
prophetic.** There is no judgment being survived, no nation being sifted, no covenant lawsuit. **There
is a household of about seventy people, five years of famine still to run, and a man explaining why he
is in Egypt.**

**The second noun in the verse is the one that carries most of the weight later.** ***Pəlêṭâ*** —
*escape, deliverance, an escaped body of people* — **is here modified as *great***: ***ûləhaḥăyôt lākem
liplêṭâ gədōlâ***, *and to keep you alive as a great escaped remnant.* **Genesis has used the
masculine form once**, at 14:13, for **the anonymous survivor of the battle of the kings who carries
the news to Abram** — ***happālîṭ***, *the escapee.* **Both of Genesis's uses of the root are about
somebody who gets out and thereby makes a story continue.**

**Every later occurrence adds the element Genesis 45 does not have: a judgment.**

| text | what is survived | who is left |
|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 45:7** | **a famine** | **a family** |
| **Isaiah 10:20–22** | **Assyria, and Israel's own apostasy** | ***šəʾār yāšûb*** — *a remnant will return* |
| **Amos 5:15** | **the day of Yahweh** | *"the remnant of Joseph"* |
| **Micah 2:12** | **exile** | **a flock gathered** |
| **Ezra 9:8** | **the exile, ending** | *"a remnant to escape"* — **both of Genesis 45:7's nouns** |
| **Romans 11:5** | **Israel's unbelief** | *"a remnant according to the election of grace"* |

**Amos 5:15 is the sharpest of them for this page, because it names Joseph.** *"The remnant of
Joseph"* — ***šəʾērît yôsēp*** — **is the northern kingdom**, called by the name of its dominant
tribes' father, **and the phrase Amos uses for what might be spared is the phrase Joseph himself
coined about the sparing of his family.** Whether Amos intends the allusion the text does not say;
**the wiki notes the coincidence of vocabulary and does not press it.**

**Isaiah 10:21–22 supplies the theology the noun eventually carries**, and it is double-edged: *"a
remnant will return"* is a promise and a threat in the same breath — ***only*** *a remnant.* **The
word acquires its grief after Genesis, not in it.** At 45:7 it is unambiguously good news.

**Ezra 9:8 is the closest verbal relative of Genesis 45:7 in the canon**, pairing ***pəlêṭâ*** and
***šəʾērît*** exactly as Joseph does, **and reading the return from Babylon as a survival granted
rather than earned** — *"for a little moment grace has been shown."*

**Romans 11:5 carries the noun into Greek as *leimma*** and attaches it to grace and election. **Paul
is quoting Isaiah 10:22 at Romans 9:27 and is not citing Genesis**, but the logic he is arguing — **God
preserves a line through an event that looks like the line ending** — **is the logic of the Joseph
cycle**, and Genesis 45:7 is where the vocabulary for it starts.

## Significance

- **A word that will describe the survival of a nation through exile is coined about the survival of a
  family through a famine.** Genesis supplies the vocabulary of remnant theology **before there is a
  nation to apply it to**, which is characteristic of the book: [[Covenant]], [[Blessing]] and
  [[Call and Election]] all get their terms in the patriarchal narratives and their stakes later.
- **The remnant is preserved in Genesis 45 by an act of human evil.** The mechanism of the deliverance
  is the sale of 37:28. **Every later remnant text assumes a judgment survived by grace**; this one has
  a crime survived by providence, **and the two are the same shape.** See
  [[God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale]].
- **The remnant is always smaller than what was.** 45:7 does not say so and every later text does.
  **Genesis's remnant is the whole family, intact, with nobody lost** — which makes it the only
  unqualified instance of the word in Scripture, **and the standard the prophets measure their
  diminished ones against.**
- **The family the remnant language is first used of ends up in [[Goshen]]**, and the survival it
  describes is the beginning of the bondage Exodus opens with. **Genesis 45:7's deliverance is four
  hundred years long and requires a second one.**
