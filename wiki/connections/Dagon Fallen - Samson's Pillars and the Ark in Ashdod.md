# Dagon Fallen - Samson's Pillars and the Ark in Ashdod

## Passages involved
- [[The Death of Samson]] — **Judges 16:23–30** — *The lords of the Philistines gathered them together to
  offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god, and to rejoice; for they said, "Our god has delivered Samson
  our enemy into our hand"* (23); *the house fell on the lords, and on all the people who were therein*
  (30). (stored WEB)
- **1 Samuel 5:1–7** — *The Philistines took God's ark, and brought it into the house of Dagon, and set it
  by Dagon. When the people of Ashdod arose early on the next day, behold, Dagon had fallen on his face to
  the ground before Yahweh's ark* (5:2–3); *the head of Dagon and both the palms of his hands were cut off
  on the threshold* (5:4); *his hand is severe on us, and on Dagon our god* (5:7). (WEB checked at
  bible-api.com, 2026-11-16; not stored, not yet ingested)

## Analysis

The two scenes are the only places in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges and 1 Samuel where Dagon is
named as a god (*dāgôn*, lemma 1712: Judges 16:23; 1 Samuel 5:2–5, 7). Both happen in Dagon's house, and both
begin with the Philistines holding a captive from Israel that they take as a trophy.

| | Judges 16 | 1 Samuel 5 |
|---|---|---|
| the captive | Samson, blind, brought from the prison | the ark, taken in battle at Ebenezer |
| the claim | *Our god has delivered Samson our enemy into our hand* (23) | none spoken; the ark is set *by Dagon* (5:2) |
| the place | Dagon's house; the city is not named in 16:23–30, Gaza by context | *the house of Dagon* in Ashdod (5:2) |
| what falls | the house, *wayyippōl habbayit* (30) | Dagon himself, *nōpēl … ʾarṣâ*, *fallen … to the ground* (5:3–4) |
| the agent | Samson, who prays and pulls | not stated; the image is found fallen *before Yahweh's ark* |
| the result | the lords and the people dead | the image broken; *Yahweh's hand was heavy on the people of Ashdod* (5:6) |

The verb is the same, *nāpal*, *fall* (Judges 16:30; 1 Samuel 5:3, 4, WLC). In Judges it is a man's act and
a man's death; in 1 Samuel no human hand is involved, and the narrator lets the morning show what happened.

The Philistines' boast in Judges 16:23–24 uses the formula Judges gives to Yahweh, *delivered … into your
hand* (3:28; 4:7; 7:9). In 1 Samuel 5:7 the men of Ashdod say the opposite of the feast's song: not *our
god has delivered*, but *his hand is severe on us, and on Dagon our god*. Both chapters answer the claim
that Dagon had beaten Israel's God.

## Significance

Judges 16 can be read as Samson's revenge alone, *for my two eyes* (16:28), and the narrator does not say
that Yahweh brought the house down. 1 Samuel 5 tells a second fall in Dagon's house with no Samson in it.
Read together, the two make the contest between Dagon and Yahweh explicit in a way Judges 16 leaves
implicit: the Philistines' god twice ends on the ground in his own house. The wiki records the parallel; it
does not claim that 1 Samuel was written to answer Judges.

The threshold note of 1 Samuel 5:5, *neither the priests of Dagon, nor any who come into Dagon's house, step
on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod, to this day*, is an etiology the Judges scene does not have, and belongs to the ingest of 1 Samuel.
