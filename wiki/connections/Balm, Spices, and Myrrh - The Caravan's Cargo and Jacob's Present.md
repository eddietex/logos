# Balm, Spices, and Myrrh — The Caravan's Cargo and Jacob's Present

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:25** — [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]; the Ishmaelite caravan *"bearing spices and balm
  and myrrh, going to carry it down to Egypt"*
- **Genesis 43:11** — [[The Second Journey]]; [[Jacob]]'s present — *"a little balm, a little honey,
  spices and myrrh, nuts, and almonds"*
- **Genesis 37:28** — the sale, on the same road
- **Jeremiah 8:22; 46:11; 51:8** — the balm of [[Gilead]]
- **Ezekiel 27:17** — Judah and Israel trading *"honey, oil, and balm"* to Tyre

## Analysis

### Two cargoes, and Genesis itemises no others

**Genesis names the contents of a shipment exactly twice, and three of the items are the same.**

| | **37:25** — the caravan | **43:11** — the present |
|---|---|---|
| | ***nəkōʾt*** — spices | spices |
| | ***ṣŏrî*** — **balm** | **balm** |
| | ***lōṭ*** — **myrrh** | **myrrh** |
| | | honey |
| | | nuts |
| | | almonds |
| **going** | *"down to Egypt"* | *"carry down … to the man"* in Egypt |
| **carrying** | **Joseph** | **the price of Joseph's brother** |

**The overlap is not a stock list.** ***Nəkōʾt*** and ***lōṭ*** — the latter a resin, conventionally
rendered *myrrh* or *ladanum* — **occur in the Hebrew Bible in these two verses and nowhere else.**
**Two of the three words in Genesis 37:25 exist in Scripture solely because Genesis 43:11 says them
again.** ***Ṣŏrî***, balm, is the one that travels: it is [[Gilead]]'s export in Jeremiah 8:22 and
46:11, and one of Israel's trade goods at Ezekiel 27:17.

### What each cargo is for

**The first is a going concern.** *"A caravan of Ishmaelites was coming from [[Gilead]], with their
camels bearing spices and balm and myrrh, going to carry it down to Egypt"* (37:25). Balm of Gilead
was a medicinal resin and an export commodity — Jeremiah's *"Is there no balm in Gilead? Is there no
physician there?"* (8:22) assumes both. **The brothers are sitting down to eat bread when they see
it, and what they see is a market.** Nine verses later the caravan has Joseph on it.

**The second is a bribe, and the man packing it is desperate.** *"Take from the choice fruits of the
land in your bags, and carry down a present for the man"* (43:11). In the second year of a famine
severe enough to have emptied his own stores, **[[Jacob]] sends aromatics and nuts to the one man in
the world who has grain.** The goods are useless as food. **They are the last good things in the
house, and their whole function is to make a stranger feel favourably toward [[Benjamin]].**

### Nobody in the story can see it

**This is the connection's point, and it is why Genesis says nothing.**

- **Jacob** does not know a caravan was involved. He was shown a bloodied coat (37:32–33) and told
  nothing else, and he will not learn otherwise until 45:26. **He chooses balm and myrrh because they
  are what a Canaanite household has that an Egyptian court might want**
- **The brothers** watched that caravan come over the hill and negotiated a price beside it (37:27–28).
  **They now load the same three commodities onto donkeys and take them down the same road**, and
  Genesis does not record one of them saying a word about it
- **Joseph** rode with the cargo. **The present is brought into his house at 43:26 and he never
  mentions it** — the chapter's last reference to the *minḥâ* is the brothers arranging it while they
  wait (43:25)

**The reader is the only one in a position to notice**, which is the Joseph cycle's characteristic
move: **[[Providence]] here is not a doctrine anybody states but a detail the narrative declines to
explain.**

### The second time is a reversal of terms

**In 37:25 the aromatics are the commerce and Joseph is added to it** — *"sold Joseph to the
Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver"* (37:28). **In 43:11 the aromatics are all the family has
and Joseph is the one being appealed to.** Twenty pieces of silver went one way; double money and the
choice fruits of a starving land go the other. See
[[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]].

**And both journeys are the same verb.** *"Going to carry it **down** to Egypt"* (37:25); *"carry
**down** a present for the man"* (43:11) — ***yārad*** in both, **the verb of every descent in the
book.** See [[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].

## Significance

**It is the clearest instance in Genesis of a detail planted for the reader alone.** Nothing turns on
it: the present is never acknowledged, the brothers never remember, Jacob never finds out. **Remove
the three overlapping words and no event in the story changes.** What changes is the reader's sense
of how tightly the narrative is wound — **the same three commodities, on the same road, in the same
direction, twenty-one verses apart in a scroll and roughly twenty-two years apart in the story.**

**It also sets the terms for how this wiki reads the cycle's silences.** The narrator stopped
attributing anything to God at 39:23 and will not resume until 46:2 (see [[Providence]]). **In that
stretch, this is what the book does instead of commentary**: it repeats a word, moves a cargo, and
lets the coincidence stand without a sentence to hold it down. The other instances are
[[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]] — the sheaves completed at 43:26 with
no remark — and the *raḥămîm* of 43:14 answered at 43:30. **Three unmarked correspondences in one
chapter.**
