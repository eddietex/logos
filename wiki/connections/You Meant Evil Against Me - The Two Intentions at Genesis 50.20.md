# You Meant Evil Against Me - The Two Intentions at Genesis 50.20

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 50:20** — *"As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring to
  pass, as it is today, to save many people alive"* — [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]
- **Genesis 45:5–8** — the first version of the same verdict — [[Joseph Reveals Himself]]
- **Genesis 15:6** — *"he believed in Yahweh, who credited it to him for righteousness"* — the same
  verb — [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]
- **Exodus 1:17–18** — the midwives *"saved the boy babies alive"*
- **Acts 2:23** — *"him … you have crucified and killed by the hand of lawless men"*
- **Acts 3:13–15**, **Acts 4:27–28** — the same structure, twice more
- **Romans 8:28** — *"all things work together for good for those who love God"*

## Analysis

**The sentence is built on one verb used twice.**

> ***ʾattem ḥăšabtem ʿālay rāʿâ — ʾĕlōhîm ḥăšābāh ləṭōbâ***
> *you reckoned against me evil — God reckoned it for good*

***Ḥāšab*** **means to reckon, to devise, to plan, to account.** **It is not a verb about outcomes;
it is a verb about intention.** **Genesis does not say the brothers did evil and God produced good
out of it afterwards** — **it says two parties formed two intentions about the same act, and both
intentions were real.**

**The distinction matters, because the two nearby ways of saying it are both weaker.**

| the sentence Genesis could have used | what it would mean | why it is not what 50:20 says |
|---|---|---|
| *you did evil, and God repaired it* | a rescue after the fact | **God's intention would post-date the act** |
| *you were God's instrument* | the brothers as tools | **the brothers' intention would not be theirs** |
| ***you reckoned it for evil; God reckoned it for good*** | **two intentions, one act, concurrent** | **both parties keep their own verb** |

**Joseph does not absolve them and does not accuse them.** **He states their intention in the second
person plural, without softening, and then states God's, and puts nothing between the two clauses
except the conjunction.**

### The same verb at Genesis 15:6

***Ḥāšab*** **is the verb of the most-argued sentence in the Abraham cycle**: *"He believed in
Yahweh, who **credited** (*wayyaḥšəbehā*) it to him for righteousness"* (15:6), **which Paul and
James spend the New Testament reading in opposite directions** (see
[[Credited to Him for Righteousness - Genesis 15.6 in Paul and James]]). **The verb that accounts a
man righteous is the verb that accounts an intention here.** **Genesis uses its accounting word
twice at the two hinges of its second half**, and the wiki notes the repetition without claiming the
book intends a link.

### *To save many people alive*

***Ləhaḥăyōt ʿam-rāb*** — **the hiphil of *ḥāyâ*, to cause to live.** **Its next weighty appearance
is Exodus 1:17–18**, where the Hebrew midwives *"feared God, and didn't do what the king of Egypt
commanded them, but **saved the baby boys alive**."* **The phrase that sums up a vizier's whole
career in Egypt is the phrase Exodus gives to two women who defy an Egyptian king.**

***ʿAm rāb***, ***a numerous people***, **is deliberately unspecific.** It can be read as the Egypt
and Canaan the grain fed (41:57), or as the seventy souls that became a nation (46:27; Exodus 1:7),
**and the sentence works either way.** See
[[To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11]].

### Against the first version

**Joseph answers the question twice and the answers are not the same**, which is the most
interesting thing about the pair.

| | **45:5–8**, five days after the disclosure | **50:20**, seventeen years later |
|---|---|---|
| **what he calls the act** | *"that you sold me here"* — subordinate, then dropped | ***"you meant evil against me"*** — the main clause |
| **agent of the sending** | *"God sent me"*, three times in four verses | God *reckoned*; the brothers still *reckoned* |
| **what is asked of them** | *"don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves"* | nothing; they have already asked |
| **their father** | alive, and about to be sent for | dead, and quoted at Joseph by men who are frightened |

**The first answer relieves them by relocating the act. The second forgives it.** **Only the second
one names the thing it is forgiving**, and it is said at the only moment in the story when Joseph has
nothing left to gain by saying it. See [[Forgiveness]] and
[[God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale]].

### In the New Testament

**Acts states the structure twice in three chapters and both times about the crucifixion.**

> *"him, being delivered up by the determined counsel and foreknowledge of God, you have crucified
> and killed by the hand of lawless men"* (Acts 2:23)

**Two agents, one event, neither clause cancelling the other** — and at Acts 4:27–28 the same again,
with Herod, Pilate, Gentiles and Israel gathered *"to do whatever your hand and your counsel
foreordained to happen."* **The grammar is Genesis 50:20's.**

**Romans 8:28 generalises it** — *"all things work together for good for those who love God"* —
**and in doing so loses the sharpness of the original**, which is not about all things but about one
thing done to one man on purpose. **The wiki records the family resemblance and the difference.**

## Significance

**This is the fullest statement of providence in the Hebrew Bible, and its whole force is that it
refuses to reduce.** **It does not make the brothers innocent; it does not make God complicit; it
does not say the sale was good.** **It says two things were true of the same morning outside
[[Dothan]], and that the man they were true about is the one saying so.**

**It is also the closing argument of Genesis.** **The book has spent fifty chapters on people whose
intentions produce something other than what they intended** — a fruit that opens eyes and closes a
garden, a tower that scatters, a lie that gets a blessing, twenty pieces of silver that put a Hebrew
over Egypt — **and 50:20 is the only place it says out loud how that has been working.** See
[[Providence]].

**And the verdict is given, not by the narrator, but by the person with standing to complain.**
**Genesis does not editorialise the sale at 37:28 and does not editorialise the enslavement of Egypt
at 47:20; it waits thirteen chapters and lets the victim say it.**
