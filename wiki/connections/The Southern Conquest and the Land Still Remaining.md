# The Southern Conquest and the Land Still Remaining

## Passages involved
- [[The Southern Cities Taken]] — Joshua 10:28–43.
- [[The Five Kings in the Cave at Makkedah]] — Joshua 10:16–27.
- [[The Whole Land Taken]] — Joshua 11:16–23.
- Joshua 13:1–3; 15:13–17; 16:10 — verified in stored WEB; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Joshua 10:40–42 summarizes a comprehensive southern victory. Yet 13:1–3 says much land
remains and includes Philistine territory with the Gazites, although 10:41 reaches to Gaza.
Joshua 15:13–17 reports Caleb driving out the sons of Anak at Hebron and Othniel taking Debir
after 10:36–39 has reported destruction of these cities. These are genuine tensions in the
narrative. Distinguishing campaign success from lasting occupation helps frame the issue;
reoccupation or overlapping accounts are possible explanations, not stated facts.

Gezer is a more limited case: 10:33 defeats Horam and his relief force, without reporting
the capture of Gezer itself. Canaanites still living there in 16:10 therefore do not directly
contradict that verse’s report about the expedition.

Hebron’s king also requires care: Hoham dies among the five kings in 10:23–26, but 10:37
again includes a king of Hebron among those killed. The text gives no name or succession
notice for the latter. Neither a second death for Hoham nor a newly crowned successor should
be asserted as an established fact.

Joshua 11:21–22 adds a third layer: Joshua cuts off the Anakim from Hebron and Debir, after
10:36–39 and before Caleb and Othniel's actions in 15:13–17. The remnant left in Gaza, Gath,
and Ashdod (11:22) matches the Philistine territory 13:2–3 lists as remaining. See
[[The Whole Land Taken]].

## Significance

The summary must be read alongside the book’s local qualifications. Neither suppressing the
claims of total destruction nor treating them as a complete census of permanent occupation
does justice to all the narrated evidence.
