# The Land Had Rest - Joshua 11.23, 14.15, and the Judges Formula

## Passages involved

- [[The Whole Land Taken]] — **Joshua 11:23** — *So Joshua took the whole land … The land had rest
  from war.* (stored WEB)
- [[Caleb Claims Hebron]] — **Joshua 14:15** — *Then the land had rest from war.* (stored WEB)
- [[Othniel the First Judge]] — **Judges 3:11** — *The land had rest forty years* (stored WEB)
- [[Ehud and Eglon]] — **Judges 3:30** — after Ehud: *Then the land had rest eighty years.* (stored
  WEB)
- **Judges 5:31** — after Deborah: *Then the land had rest forty years.* (stored WEB) *(not yet
  ingested)*
- **Judges 8:28** — after Gideon: *The land had rest forty years in the days of Gideon.* (stored WEB)
  *(not yet ingested)*
- **Judges 18:7, 27** — Laish, *quiet and secure*, then *a people quiet and unsuspecting* (stored WEB)
  *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

The verb is *šāqaṭ*, *to be quiet, at rest*. In the WLC files of Exodus through Judges, it occurs in
exactly these eight verses (OpenScriptures lemma `8252`):

| Verse | Hebrew (WLC) | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| Joshua 11:23 | *wəhāʾāreṣ šāqəṭâ mimmilḥāmâ* | the land, from war |
| Joshua 14:15 | *wəhāʾāreṣ šāqəṭâ mimmilḥāmâ* | the land, from war |
| Judges 3:11 | *wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ ʾarbāʿîm šānâ* | the land, forty years |
| Judges 3:30 | *wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ šəmônîm šānâ* | the land, eighty years |
| Judges 5:31 | *wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ ʾarbāʿîm šānâ* | the land, forty years |
| Judges 8:28 | *wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ ʾarbāʿîm šānâ bîmê gidʿôn* | the land, forty years, *in the days of Gideon* |
| Judges 18:7 | *šōqēṭ ûbōṭēaḥ* | the people of Laish |
| Judges 18:27 | *ʿam šōqēṭ ûbōṭēaḥ* | the people of Laish |

In Joshua the rest is *from war* and has no length. In Judges it is measured in years, and the war it
follows is the judge's.

## Significance

- **Joshua's rest, rationed.** In Joshua the land rested once, and the sentence comes twice, after the
  whole conquest (11:23) and after Caleb's grant (14:15). In Judges the same land rests again and
  again, for a set time, and each rest ends.
- **It stops after Gideon.** Jephthah and Samson have no rest notice. Gideon's is the last time the
  land is the subject of *šāqaṭ*. The book's own structure marks the cycle as breaking down. That this
  is deliberate is a reading, not a statement in the text.
- **The last rest in the book belongs to someone else.** The only uses after Gideon describe Laish,
  a city *quiet and secure* (18:7), which Dan strikes *with the edge of the sword* and burns (18:27).
  The word that described Israel's land at peace ends by describing a foreign people at peace, and
  Israel destroying it. See [[Dan Pressed from the Valley - Joshua 19, Judges 1, and Judges 18]] and
  [[Leshem Becomes Dan - Joshua 19 and Judges 18]].
- **A different word from *rest from all your enemies*.** Deuteronomy 12:10, Joshua 21:44, and 23:1
  speak of Yahweh giving *rest* with *nûaḥ* (see
  [[Rest from All Your Enemies - Deuteronomy 12, Joshua 1, 21-23, and Hebrews 4]]). *Šāqaṭ* describes
  quiet rather than a gift. The two threads are related in subject but not in vocabulary.
- *Forty* recurs three times, and is commonly read as a round number for a generation. That is a
  reading, not something the text states. Ehud's *eighty* (3:30) is the exception and the longest rest
  in the book; on the same reading it is two generations. It follows the only victory summary so far
  with *kānaʿ*, *Moab was subdued* (3:30, WLC), a verb the book repeats after Deborah (4:23), Gideon
  (8:28), and Jephthah (11:33), though Jephthah gets no rest notice.
