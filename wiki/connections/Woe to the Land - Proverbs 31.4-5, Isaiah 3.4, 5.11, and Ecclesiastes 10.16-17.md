# Woe to the Land - Proverbs 31.4-5, Isaiah 3.4, 5.11, and Ecclesiastes 10.16-17

## Passages involved

- [[Folly in High Places and Everyday Work]] — **Ecclesiastes 10:16–17** — *"Woe to you, land,
  when your king is a child, and your princes eat in the morning! Happy are you, land, when your
  king is the son of nobles, and your princes eat in due season, for strength, and not for
  drunkenness!"*
- [[The Words of King Lemuel]] — **Proverbs 31:4–5** — *"It is not for kings, Lemuel; it is not for
  kings to drink wine; nor for princes to say, ‘Where is strong drink?’ lest they drink, and forget
  the law, and pervert the justice due to anyone who is afflicted."*
- **Isaiah 3:4** — *"I will give boys to be their princes, and children shall rule over them."*
  Verified WEB, not yet ingested.
- **Isaiah 5:11** — *"Woe to those who rise up early in the morning, that they may follow strong
  drink; who stay late into the night, until wine inflames them!"* Verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

**Two marks of a failing court.** Ecclesiastes 10:16 names two: a king who is a *child* and princes
who *"eat in the morning"*. Isaiah has both images separately. In 3:4 God gives *"boys to be their
princes"* as part of a judgment on Jerusalem and Judah; in 5:11 a woe falls on those who *"rise up
early in the morning"* to follow strong drink. Ecclesiastes uses the same two images as a
description of a land's misfortune rather than an announced judgment, and adds a beatitude for
the opposite case (10:17).

**What is wrong with the morning.** Proverbs 31:4–5 gives the reason drinking is dangerous for
rulers: they *"forget the law"* and pervert the afflicted person's justice. Ecclesiastes 10:17
does not forbid wine to princes. It commends eating *"in due season, for strength, and not for
drunkenness"*, and two verses later it says that wine makes life glad (10:19). Its complaint is
about time and purpose: a court that begins the day feasting has given its strength away before
its work. Proverbs is the stricter text.

**Child and noble.** Whether Ecclesiastes' *child* is literally young, or immature in conduct, is
not decided by the verse. Its Hebrew word, *naʿar*, has a wide range of age (MAM); the contrast is
with a king who is *"the son of nobles"* (*ben-ḥôrîm*). Isaiah 3:4 sets *"boys"* beside
*"children"* in a parallel line. The Hebrew of Isaiah was not checked, so no shared vocabulary is
asserted between the two books.

## Significance

The thread shows [[Kingship and Empire]] measured by the conduct of the court, with
[[Joy and Enjoyment]] permitted and drunkenness rejected. It joins Proverbs' instruction to a king,
Ecclesiastes' observation of a land, and the prophetic woe, without making them the same kind of
speech. See also [[A King Under Instruction - Proverbs 31.1-5 and Deuteronomy 17.17-20]].
