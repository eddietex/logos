## Analysis

**One Hebrew word, many English ones.** Every passage above has *bəliyyaʿal* (WLC, lemma 1100), usually in a
phrase: *bənê ḇəliyyaʿal*, *sons of bəliyyaʿal* (Deuteronomy 13:14; Judges 19:22; 20:13; 1 Samuel 2:12; 10:27),
*baṯ-bəliyyaʿal*, *a daughter of bəliyyaʿal* (1 Samuel 1:16), *ʾāḏām bəliyyaʿal*, *a person of bəliyyaʿal* (Proverbs
6:12), *ʾîš bəliyyaʿal* (16:27), and *ʿēḏ bəliyyaʿal*, *a witness of bəliyyaʿal* (Proverbs 19:28). The WEB has *wicked fellows*, *wicked
men*, *a wicked woman*, *worthless fellows*, *a worthless person*, and *a corrupt witness*, and at Deuteronomy
15:9 *a wicked thought*. The English varies, but the Hebrew is the same word each time.

In the WLC files searched (Genesis–2 Samuel and Proverbs), it occurs seventeen times. It is absent from Genesis
through Numbers, Joshua, and Ruth. It begins with Deuteronomy's law against those who lead a town to other gods,
and it gathers in Judges 19–20 and 1–2 Samuel. 2 Samuel 16:7; 20:1; 22:5; 23:6 were read in the WLC only, because
2 Samuel is not yet stored.

The word is commonly explained as *bəlî*, *without*, and *yaʿal*, *profit* or *worth*, so *worthlessness*. That
etymology is recalled, not checked, and other derivations have been proposed. In later Jewish writing and in 2
Corinthians 6:15, *Belial* is a name for an evil power (cited by reference only; not stored and not checked). The
books stored so far use it as a description of people, not as a name.

**The thread through Gibeah.** Deuteronomy 13 orders that a town led astray by *sons of bəliyyaʿal* be
investigated and destroyed (13:12–15). Judges 19–20 tells of such a judgment, though the crime is different. The
*sons of bəliyyaʿal* of Gibeah surround the Levite's host (19:22). The tribes gather at Mizpah and demand them
from Benjamin (20:13), and Benjamin refuses. 1 Samuel 10 brings the same places back. Israel gathers at Mizpah,
a Benjamite of Gibeah is made king, he goes home to Gibeah, and the men who reject him are *sons of
bəliyyaʿal* (10:17, 26–27). The wiki notes the recurrence of places and of the word and does not claim the narrator
designed it. See [[The Whole City Went Up - Deuteronomy 13 and Gibeah]] and
[[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]].

**Who is called this.** The word is used of men who rape and murder (Judges 19–20), priests who despise the
offering (1 Samuel 2:12, 17), men who despise the king (10:27), and a churl who insults David's messengers (25:17,
25). It is also used of a thought that refuses to lend before the year of release (Deuteronomy 15:9). Hannah asks
Eli not to count her as *a daughter of bəliyyaʿal* (1:16), and in the next chapter Eli's own sons are called
*sons of bəliyyaʿal* (2:12). The narrator applies to the priest's house the word the priest nearly used of
Hannah. In Proverbs it is a man of crooked speech (6:12; 16:27) and a false witness (19:28).

## Significance

- **It names people who refuse rightful authority or obligation.** That covers Yahweh as God (Deuteronomy 13),
  the guest's protection (Judges 19), the offering (1 Samuel 2), the king (1 Samuel 10), and the poor brother
  (Deuteronomy 15:9). The wiki records this pattern as an observation from the passages listed, not as a
  definition of the word.
- **Saul's first opponents are named with Gibeah's old word.** The new king comes from the town whose men the
  word once described. Those who reject him are given the same name. See [[Saul]],
  [[Worthless Fellows (of Saul's time)]], and [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]].
- See also [[Folly]], since Nabal's name and his description at 25:25 put the two words side by side, and
  [[Sin]].
