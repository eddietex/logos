# The Neighbor's Door - Proverbs 5 and Job 31.9-12

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 5:8–10** — [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]]: *"Don't come near the
  door of her house, lest you give your honor to others, and your years to the cruel one; lest
  strangers feast on your wealth, and your labors enrich another man's house."*
- **Proverbs 5:22** — *"The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him. The cords of his sin hold him
  firmly."*
- **Job 31:9–12** — [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]: *"If my heart has been enticed to a woman, and I
  have laid wait at my neighbor's door, then let my wife grind for another, and let others sleep
  with her. For that would be a heinous crime. Yes, it would be an iniquity to be punished by the
  judges: For it is a fire that consumes to destruction, and would root out all my increase."*
- **Proverbs 6:27–29** — [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]: *"Can a man scoop fire into his lap, and
  his clothes not be burned? … So is he who goes in to his neighbor's wife."*
- **Job 31:8** — *"then let me sow, and let another eat. Yes, let the produce of my field be rooted
  out."*

## Analysis

**The same door.** Proverbs 5:8 tells the son not to come near the door of the strange woman's
house; Job 31:9 swears he has not lain in wait at his neighbour's door. Both put the sin at a
threshold — another household's entrance — and both treat the woman as belonging to someone else,
which supports the wiki's reading of the strange woman as another man's wife (see
[[If You Seek Her as Silver]]).

**The same cost, from two sides.** Proverbs lists the consequence as everything the son has
passing to others: honour, years, strength, labour (5:9–10). Job's oath names the same transfer as
the penalty he accepts if guilty: another eats what he sowed (31:8), another man has his wife
(31:10), and his increase is rooted out (31:12). **Proverbs predicts the loss as a consequence;
Job invokes it as a curse on himself.** The equivalence is close enough that Job 31 reads as the
oath of a man who heard Proverbs 5's warning and claims to have kept it.

**Self-consuming sin.** Proverbs 5:22 has the wicked caught in the cords of his own sin; Job 31:12
calls adultery *"a fire that consumes to destruction"*. Both describe the act as destroying its
doer from within rather than only drawing a penalty from outside — though Job 31:11 also names the
judges, so the outside penalty is not absent.

**Where the two books part.** Job's oath is the claim of a man who did keep away from the door,
and who lost his household anyway (Job 1:13–19). Proverbs 5 describes the loss that follows the
sin; it does not say that every lost household was lost through it. The wiki records the parallel
in the warning and keeps the tension in the outcome, as on
[[Heart, Eyes, and Feet - Proverbs 4 and Job 31]].

## Significance

Job 31 is the Old Testament's fullest self-examination, and its adultery clause matches the
first Proverbs lecture devoted to adultery point for point: the door, the loss to others, and the
self-destroying end. Read together they show the wisdom tradition's sexual ethic stated twice —
once as instruction, once as oath. See [[Sexual Sin]], [[Oaths and Vows]], and [[Retribution]].
