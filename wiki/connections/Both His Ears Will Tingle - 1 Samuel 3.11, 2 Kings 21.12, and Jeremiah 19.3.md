# Both His Ears Will Tingle - 1 Samuel 3.11, 2 Kings 21.12, and Jeremiah 19.3

## Passages involved

- **1 Samuel 3:11** — [[Yahweh Calls Samuel]]. *Behold, I will do a thing in Israel, at which both the
  ears of everyone who hears it will tingle.* *(stored WEB)*
- **2 Kings 21:12** — Yahweh's word against Jerusalem after Manasseh's reign: *Behold, I bring such evil on
  Jerusalem and Judah that whoever hears of it, both his ears will tingle.* *(WEB checked at bible-api.com,
  2026-11-17; not yet ingested)*
- **Jeremiah 19:3** — Jeremiah at the potsherd gate: *Behold, I will bring evil on this place, which
  whoever hears, his ears will tingle.* *(WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-17; not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**One image, three sentences of judgment.** The verb is *ṣālal*, *to tingle*, *to quiver* (WLC). In the
Westminster Leningrad Codex as tagged by OpenScriptures morphhb, it occurs in the files of Genesis through
1 Kings only at 1 Samuel 3:11, and it appears with *ears* again at 2 Kings 21:12 and Jeremiah 19:3 (lemma
search of those three books; the rest of the Hebrew Bible was not searched for this page).

| | spoken to | against | the phrase (WLC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Samuel 3:11 | the boy Samuel, at Shiloh | Eli's house and the sanctuary it served | *kol-šōmʿô təṣillênâ šəttê ʾozənāw* |
| 2 Kings 21:12 | *his servants the prophets* (21:10, WEB checked) | Jerusalem and Judah | *kol-šōmʿāh tiṣṣalnâ šəttê ʾozənāw* |
| Jeremiah 19:3 | the kings of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem | *this place* | *kol-šōmʿāh tiṣṣalnâ ʾozənāw* |

Morphhb tags the verb at 1 Samuel 3:11 as Hiphil and at the other two as Qal; the forms differ in pointing,
not in sense as the WEB renders them. 2 Kings 21:12 has the full *both his ears*, *šəttê ʾozənāw*, as 1
Samuel does; Jeremiah 19:3 has *his ears* without *both*.

**Shiloh as the pattern.** The first time the image is used, the object is the priestly house at the
sanctuary of Shiloh, and the sentence falls when the ark is taken and Eli's sons die (1 Samuel 4:11, stored
WEB; not yet ingested). The later two aim the same words at Jerusalem and its temple. Jeremiah elsewhere
makes the comparison explicit, sending his hearers to *my place which was in Shiloh* (Jeremiah 7:12; cited
by reference, not checked). The verbal echo and Jeremiah's explicit appeal to Shiloh are both on the page;
whether 2 Kings 21 and Jeremiah 19 consciously quote 1 Samuel 3 is a reader's inference.

**What hears.** In all three the hearer is general, *everyone who hears it*; the sentence is made public by
its size. In 1 Samuel 3 the first hearer is a child who has *not yet* known Yahweh (3:7), and the second is
the old priest whose house it condemns (3:18).

## Significance

- It ties the fall of the Shiloh sanctuary to the fall of Jerusalem in the language of the prophets, so
  that the opening of the books of Samuel and the end of Kings frame the same kind of judgment.
- In canonical order, Yahweh's word to Samuel is the earliest of the three uses of the image found here.
