# Take My Life - Moses, Elijah, and Jonah

## Passages involved

- [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] — Numbers 11:15; *"If you treat me this way, please kill me right now"*
- **1 Kings 19:4** — *"It is enough. Now, O Yahweh, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers."* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Jonah 4:3** — *"Therefore now, Yahweh, take, I beg you, my life from me; for it is better for me to die than to live."* (WEB)
  *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Three prophets ask God for death, and each is doing his work when he asks.** Moses has the whole people. Elijah has just won at Carmel,
and Jonah has just seen Nineveh repent. None of them is rebuked for the request as such. Moses gets seventy helpers. Elijah gets bread,
water, and a job (the wider chapter, by reference). Jonah gets a question.

**Their reasons differ.** Moses cannot carry what he was given. Elijah says he is no better than his fathers. Jonah is angry that God
was merciful. Job's wish in [[Let the Day Perish]] is akin, but he does not ask in this form.

**Wording.** Moses says *hārəgēnî nāʾ*, *kill me*, with the plain verb for killing (WLC). The WEB of the other two has *take*. Their Hebrew
was not compared for this page.

## Significance

The Bible gives these requests to people in its highest offices and does not hide them. God's answer in each is more help, or a
question, and no one is given death. See [[Prayer]], [[Suffering]], and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].
