# Three Transgressions, Yes Four - Proverbs 30 and Amos 1-2

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 30:15–31** — [[Four Things]]: four lists in the form *"three things … four"* (15, 18,
  21, 29), and one of *"four things"* alone (24).
- **Proverbs 6:16–19** — [[The Surety, the Sluggard, and the Seven Abominations]]: *"There are six
  things which Yahweh hates; yes, seven which are an abomination to him"* (6:16).
- **Job 5:19** — [[Eliphaz's First Speech]]: six troubles, *"yes, in seven"*.
- **Ecclesiastes 11:2** — [[Cast Your Bread on the Waters]]: *"Give a portion to seven, yes, even
  to eight"*.
- **Micah 5:5** *(Micah not yet ingested)* — *"seven shepherds, and eight leaders of men"*.
- **Amos 1:3–2:6** *(Amos not yet ingested)* — eight oracles against the nations, each opening
  *"For three transgressions of … yes, for four"*; the first is against Damascus (1:3), the last
  against Israel (2:6).

## Analysis

**One form, two uses.** The graded numerical saying — a number followed by the next one up — is
already recorded on [[Once, Yes Twice - Job 33.14, 40.5, and Psalm 62.11]] and
[[Six Things Yahweh Hates - Proverbs 6.16-19 and the Ten Commandments]]. Proverbs 30 uses it four
times in fifteen verses. Its Hebrew pairs *šālôš* or *šəlōšāh* with *ʾarbaʿ* or *ʾarbāʿāh* (Sefaria,
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, read 2026-10-27), and Amos 1:3 has the same pair of numbers,
*ʿal-šəlōšāh pišʿê dammeśeq wəʿal-ʾarbāʿāh* (same edition and date).

**In Proverbs the count is completed.** Each of Agur's lists names four items, and the fourth
often carries the weight: fire after Sheol, the womb, and the earth; *"the way of a man with a
maiden"* after the eagle, the serpent, and the ship. The device trains attention: the reader is
asked to find what the four share.

**In Amos it is not.** In the WEB, Amos 1:3 says *"For three transgressions of Damascus, yes, for
four, I will not turn away its punishment"*, and then names one crime: *"because they have threshed
Gilead with threshing instruments of iron"*. Amos 2:6 applies the same words to Israel and names
the selling of *"the righteous for silver, and the needy for a pair of shoes"*. Here the numbers do
not set up a list. They say that the transgressions have gone past counting, and the one crime
named stands for the rest. These remarks on Amos's structure rest on 1:3 and 2:6 alone; the other
oracles were not checked in this pass.

**What the shared form shows.** Agur's lists observe the world and invite wonder; Amos's oracles
announce judgment. The form belongs to neither, and no literary dependence in either direction is
claimed. What both share is a confidence that things can be counted, and that the counting reveals
a pattern — in nature and households for the sage, in the conduct of nations for the prophet.

**Without a list at all.** Ecclesiastes 11:2 uses the form with seven and eight,
*ləšiḇʿâ wəḡam lišmônâ* (MAM, read 2026-10-27), and names no items. The second number means
simply *as many as you can*: spread your portions, because you do not know what evil will come.
Micah 5:5 in the WEB has the same pair of numbers, *"seven shepherds, and eight leaders of
men"*, and also gives no list. These are a third use, beside the completed count of Proverbs and
the uncounted guilt of Amos: the form as an idiom for *more than enough*. The Hebrew of Micah
5:5 was not checked.

## Significance

The connection keeps a recognizable device from being read the same way everywhere. In Proverbs
30 the second number marks the fourth item as the one to notice; in Amos it marks an accumulated
guilt that the single example only represents. Amos's judgment on Israel for selling the needy also
meets Proverbs 30:14, the generation whose jaws *"devour the poor from the earth"*. See
[[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], [[Judgment]], and [[Wisdom]].
