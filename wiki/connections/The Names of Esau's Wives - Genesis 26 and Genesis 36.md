# The Names of Esau's Wives - Genesis 26 and Genesis 36

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 26:34–35** — *"he took as wife Judith, the daughter of Beeri the Hittite, and Basemath,
  the daughter of Elon the Hittite. They grieved Isaac's and Rebekah's spirits."* — [[Isaac in Gerar]]
- **Genesis 28:6–9** — Esau, seeing that Canaanite wives displease his father, marries *"Mahalath the
  daughter of Ishmael, Abraham's son, the sister of Nebaioth"* —
  [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]] and [[Mahalath]]
- **Genesis 36:2–3** — *"Esau took his wives from the daughters of Canaan: Adah the daughter of Elon
  the Hittite; and Oholibamah the daughter of Anah, the daughter of Zibeon the Hivite; and Basemath,
  Ishmael's daughter, sister of Nebaioth."* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Genesis 27:46** — Rebekah's *"I am weary of my life because of the daughters of Heth"*, which is
  what 26:35 is for — [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]]

## Analysis

### The lists

| father | **Genesis 26:34; 28:9** | **Genesis 36:2–3** |
|---|---|---|
| Elon the Hittite | **Basemath** | **Adah** |
| Beeri the Hittite | **Judith** | — |
| Anah / Zibeon the Hivite | — | **Oholibamah** |
| Ishmael | **Mahalath** (28:9) | **Basemath** |

**Three discrepancies, and they are of different kinds.**

1. **The same father, two daughters' names.** Elon the Hittite gives *Basemath* in the first list and
   *Adah* in the second. The patronymic is stable; the name is not.
2. **The same name, two fathers.** *Basemath* is Elon's daughter in the first list and Ishmael's in
   the second — and Ishmael's daughter is called *Mahalath* in the first.
3. **A wife who appears in only one list each way.** Judith daughter of Beeri is absent from Genesis
   36; Oholibamah daughter of Anah is absent from Genesis 26 and 28.

**Genesis states both lists flatly and never notices**, in exactly the way it explains the name
Beersheba twice (21:31; 26:33) without noticing. See
[[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]].

### The standing explanations, and the wiki adopts none

- **Two names per woman.** The oldest harmonization, and the one most commentaries reach for: women
  in this world could carry a personal name and a second name acquired at marriage or in the
  husband's household, so *Basemath* and *Adah* are one woman, *Mahalath* and *Basemath* another.
  **Its difficulty is that it needs the name *Basemath* to move from one woman to another between the
  two lists**, which is the one coincidence a two-names theory makes worse rather than better.
- **Separate sources.** Genesis 26:34–35 and 28:6–9 are usually assigned to the Priestly material and
  the chapter 36 register to a distinct genealogical source with its own tradition of Esau's
  marriages; the divergence is then not an error but two lists that were never reconciled. **This
  accounts for the data without needing any of the names to shift**, and it is the reading the pattern
  of the rest of Genesis most supports.
- **Textual transmission.** Some of the variation is attested differently in the ancient versions —
  the Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint differ from the Masoretic text at points in Genesis
  36:2 — so part of the divergence may be a copying history rather than an authorial one. **This
  explains some of it and not the *Basemath* transfer.**

### Why the wiki flags it rather than settling it

**This is one of the clearest internal disagreements in Genesis** — flatter and more checkable than
the two creation accounts, and unlike the Beersheba etiologies it involves proper names that cannot
both be right in the ordinary sense. **The wiki's standing practice is to note both claims and which
text supports each rather than to overwrite one**, and this is a case where the harmonization on
offer is not obviously better than the admission that the book preserves two registers.

### And the thing both lists agree on

**Esau married outside the family and inside the land**, which is what 24:3 was designed to prevent
and what 26:35 says the consequence of was. Genesis 36:2 makes the point in its own opening words —
*"Esau took his wives from the daughters of Canaan"* — a verdict the earlier passage delivers by
reporting his parents' grief instead. **The two registers disagree about the names and agree exactly
about the significance.** See [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] and [[Marriage]].

**And both agree that he corrected it in the wrong direction.** At 28:9, on seeing that the Canaanite
marriages displeased his father, he marries a daughter of [[Ishmael]] — **joining the two lines the
covenant passed over**, and doing it after the blessing has already gone. **He also keeps the first
two**: *"besides the wives that he had."* See [[Mahalath]].

## Significance

- **It is the wiki's first flagged name-level contradiction in Genesis**, and it is unusually clean:
  two lists, four women, and a name that changes owners between them.
- **It shows what the book does with its own registers.** Genesis does not audit itself. It preserved
  a marriage notice and a genealogical register that disagree, put them ten chapters apart, and left
  them.
- **And the disagreement does not touch the point either passage is making.** Whatever the women were
  called, Esau married two daughters of the land at forty, on his own initiative, against a rule his
  grandfather had made a servant swear to — and his mother used the grief it caused to get her other
  son out of the country.
