## Analysis

### Caleb’s grant at Gilgal

The newly ingested scene distinguishes three voices: Caleb’s self-description (8), Moses’ oath as Caleb recalls it (9), and the narrator’s explanation of the grant (14). The agreement matters: wholehearted loyalty is not only the applicant’s own assertion. This addition compares the stored WEB, without making a new source-language claim.


### Deuteronomy’s sentence and ascent

Deuteronomy 1:36 now supplies an ingested promise of the trodden land to Caleb and his children
because he wholly followed Yahweh. Verse 38 separately guarantees Joshua’s entry as Israel’s future
leader. The contrast is between the condemned generation and these exceptions, not a denial that
Joshua will enter.

Source for this update: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 14 and 20.

*Millēʾ ʾaḥărê*, literally *to fill after*, occurs in those verses only by the WLC tags, eight verses in all. It is the phrase of Caleb. Seven of the eight concern him or the sentence on his
generation.

| Verse | Speaker | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers 14:24 | Yahweh | Caleb |
| Numbers 32:11 | Yahweh, as Moses retells | the condemned, *not* |
| Numbers 32:12 | Yahweh, as Moses retells | Caleb and Joshua |
| Deuteronomy 1:36 | Yahweh, as Moses retells | Caleb |
| Joshua 14:8 | Caleb | himself |
| Joshua 14:9 | Moses, as Caleb retells | Caleb |
| Joshua 14:14 | narrator | Caleb |
| 1 Kings 11:6 | narrator | Solomon, *not*, *as David his father* |

**The phrase has a gap in it.** Literally it reads *fill after me*, and nothing says what was
filled. It may be elliptical for filling one's walking or going; that explanation is recalled, and no lexicon was consulted.

**Caleb says it about himself.** Joshua 14:8 has *I wholly followed Yahweh my God*, forty-five years
later. It sets his report against *my brothers who went up with me*, who *made the heart of the
people melt*.

**Solomon is the only king measured by it.** A phrase that belongs to one man at Kadesh returns as
the verdict on the builder of the temple. The tags do not show any use beyond that.

**Joshua's place.** Numbers 14:24 and Deuteronomy 1:36 give the phrase to Caleb alone, and only
Numbers 32:12 includes Joshua. See tension 1 on [[The People Refuse the Land]].

## Significance

- The Bible's name for loyalty at the moment of decision comes from one man's refusal to go along
  with the majority.
- Numbers 14:43 gives the opposite in the same chapter, *šabtem mēʾaḥărê YHWH*, *you turned back from
  following Yahweh*. See [[The Defeat at Hormah]].
