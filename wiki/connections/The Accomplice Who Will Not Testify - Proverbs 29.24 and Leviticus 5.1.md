# The Accomplice Who Will Not Testify - Proverbs 29.24 and Leviticus 5.1

## Passages involved

- [[Where There Is No Vision]] — Proverbs 29:24.
- [[Confession and Offerings According to Means]] — Leviticus 5:1–13, especially 1, 5–6.

## Analysis

Proverbs 29:24 describes complicity with a thief as hostility to one’s own soul. In the WEB the
accomplice takes an oath but dares not testify. The dilemma is not a neutral choice between
speaking and silence: participation in wrongdoing has made disclosure costly.

Leviticus 5:1 addresses a witness who hears an adjuration and fails to report what he has seen or
known; that omission incurs guilt. It provides a legal comparison for the silence in Proverbs,
without requiring that the proverb reproduce the law’s wording. Leviticus does not specify an
accomplice or a thief in this verse, and Proverbs does not state a ritual remedy.

The WEB translations describe the oath situation differently: taking an oath in Proverbs, hearing
an adjuration in Leviticus. This comparison does not settle their exact source-language
relationship. No Hebrew edition was consulted for this claim, so no lexical equivalence is
asserted.

### The legal remedy now ingested

Leviticus 5:5–13 supplies confession and offerings according to means after the cases
including withheld testimony. Proverbs 29:24 states no such remedy; the law therefore
adds a response rather than merely restating the proverb. The law does not identify
every silent witness as a thief’s accomplice.

## Significance

[[Theft and Restitution]] includes accomplices, and [[Language and Speech]] includes what a witness withholds.
[[Oaths and Vows]] can expose guilt rather than conceal it; loyalty to a wrongdoer does not make
