# The Least in My Father's House - Judges 6.15 and 1 Samuel 9.21

## Passages involved

- [[The Call of Gideon]] — Judges 6:15: *O Lord, how shall I save Israel? Behold, my family is the poorest in
  Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house.* Stored WEB.
- [[Saul Comes to the Seer]] — 1 Samuel 9:21: *Am I not a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel? And
  my family the least of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? Why then do you speak to me like this?* Stored
  WEB.
- Compare Genesis 43:33–34 — Benjamin, *the youngest according to his youth*, seated at Joseph's table and given a
  portion *five times as much*. Stored WEB.
- Compare 1 Samuel 15:17 — *Though you were little in your own sight, weren't you made the head of the tribes of
  Israel?* Stored WEB; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Both men answer a summons to save Israel by measuring their family. Gideon is told *Go in this your might, and save
Israel from the hand of Midian* (Judges 6:14); Saul has just been told that *all that is desirable in Israel* is
for him and his father's house (1 Samuel 9:20), after Yahweh told Samuel that he *will save my people out of the
hand of the Philistines* (9:16). Both answer with the same adjective in the same position:

| | Judges 6:15 | 1 Samuel 9:21 |
|---|---|---|
| **The unit** | *ʾalpî*, *my thousand* (WEB *my family*) | *mišpaḥtî*, *my family* |
| **Its rank** | *haddal*, *the poorest*, *in Manasseh* | *haṣṣəʿirâ*, *the least*, of the families of Benjamin |
| **The speaker** | *ʾānōḵî haṣṣāʿîr*, *I am the least*, in my father's house | *miqqəṭannê šiḇṭê yiśrāʾēl*, *of the smallest of the tribes* |
| **The question** | *bammâ ʾôšîaʿ*, *how shall I save?* | *wəlāmmâ dibbartā*, *why then do you speak to me like this?* |

Hebrew from the WLC. *Ṣāʿîr*, *least, youngest*, is the shared word. In the WLC files searched (Genesis through 2
Samuel, Ruth) it occurs elsewhere for Lot's younger daughter (Genesis 19:31–38), for Jacob over Esau (*the elder
will serve the younger*, 25:23), for Rachel against Leah (29:26), for Benjamin at Joseph's table (43:33), for
Ephraim under Jacob's right hand (48:14), and in Joshua's curse on Jericho's rebuilder, *with the loss of his youngest son*
(Joshua 6:26) (lemma 6810). It is the Bible's word for the one who should come last and does not.

The difference is in what follows. Gideon's objection is met with *Surely I will be with you* (Judges 6:16), and
he asks for signs. Saul's is not answered at all: Samuel *took Saul and his servant*, brings them to the guest room, and seats him at
the head of the table (1 Samuel 9:22). And where Gideon refused the crown offered to him and his sons (Judges
8:22–23; see [[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]]), Saul is being prepared for it.

Saul's *smallest of the tribes* also has a history the reader knows and he does not need to state. Benjamin was
reduced to six hundred men in [[The War Against Benjamin]], and the tribes wept at Bethel that *there should be one
tribe lacking in Israel today* (Judges 21:3); see [[Wives for Benjamin]]. The first king comes from the tribe that
nearly ceased to exist.

The motif returns against Saul. At Gilgal, after Amalek, Samuel will say *Though you were little in your own sight,
weren't you made the head of the tribes of Israel?* (1 Samuel 15:17, stored WEB; not yet ingested), turning the
humility of 9:21 into the measure of his fall. The wiki will take that up when 15 is ingested.

## Significance

Judges and 1 Samuel introduce their deliverers in the same register: Moses' *Who am I?* (Exodus 3:11), Gideon's
*the least in my father's house*, Saul's *the smallest of the tribes*. The pattern makes the reluctance part of the
call rather than an obstacle to it, and makes the reader watch what each man does with the elevation that follows.
See [[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]], [[Call and Election]], and
[[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].
