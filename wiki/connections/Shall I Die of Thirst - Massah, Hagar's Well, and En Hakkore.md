# Shall I Die of Thirst - Massah, Hagar's Well, and En Hakkore

## Passages involved

- [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]] — **Genesis 21:15–19** — *The water in the bottle was
  spent* (15); *Don't let me see the death of the child* (16); *God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of
  water* (19). Stored WEB.
- [[Water from the Rock]] — **Exodus 17:3–7** — *The people were thirsty for water there; and the people
  murmured against Moses, and said, "Why have you brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us, our children,
  and our livestock with thirst?"* (3); *You shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it* (6).
  Stored WEB.
- [[Jael and Sisera]] — **Judges 4:19** — *Please give me a little water to drink; for I am thirsty*.
  Stored WEB.
- [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]] — **Judges 15:18–19** — *now shall I die of thirst, and fall
  into the hands of the uncircumcised?* (18); *God split the hollow place that is in Lehi, and water came
  out of it* (19). Stored WEB.

## Analysis

**The words.** The verb *ṣāmēʾ*, *be thirsty* (lemma 6770), occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through
2 Samuel checked only at Exodus 17:3, Judges 4:19, and Judges 15:18. The noun *ṣāmāʾ*, *thirst* (lemma
6772), occurs in the same files at Exodus 17:3, Deuteronomy 28:48, and Judges 15:18. Exodus 17:3 and
Judges 15:18 share the pair: *wayyiṣmāʾ … baṣṣāmāʾ* (WLC), *was thirsty … of thirst*, and both speak of
dying by it. Genesis 21 has neither word; its thirst is told by the empty bottle.

| | Hagar (Genesis 21) | Massah (Exodus 17) | Sisera (Judges 4) | Samson (Judges 15) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| who thirsts | the boy | the people | Sisera | Samson |
| to whom they speak | no one; she weeps (16) | Moses: *Give us water* (2) | Jael | Yahweh (18) |
| who asks God | God hears *the voice of the boy* (17) | Moses cries to Yahweh (4) | no one | Samson (18) |
| the water | a well she is made to see (19) | the rock, struck (6) | milk, and a tent peg (19–21) | the hollow, split by God (19) |
| the name | — | Massah and Meribah, for the quarrel (7) | — | En Hakkore (19) |

**Asking.** At Massah the people *quarreled* and *tested Yahweh* (Exodus 17:2, 7). Samson's words in
Judges 15:18 also have an edge, *shall I die of thirst*, but he speaks to Yahweh, not to a leader, and he
calls the victory Yahweh's: *You have given this great deliverance by the hand of your servant*. Of the
four he is the only thirsty person who asks God for water himself.

**The water.** At Massah Moses strikes the rock. At Lehi no one strikes anything: *God split*, *wayyibqaʿ
ʾĕlōhîm* (Judges 15:19, WLC, *bāqaʿ*, lemma 1234), the verb of the sea divided (Exodus 14:16, 21) and the
ground that split under Korah (Numbers 16:31; WLC). Hagar's well is not made at all; it is shown: *God
opened her eyes*.

**The naming.** Massah and Meribah are named for the people's quarrel. *En Hakkore* is usually rendered
*the spring of the one who calls* (reported, not checked), which would name it for Samson's prayer.
Hagar had earlier named a spring for God's seeing, [[Beer Lahai Roi]] (Genesis 16:13–14); that naming is
in a different chapter from her second thirst.

**Sisera's request.** The one other *I am thirsty* in these books is the enemy general's. He asks a woman,
receives milk, and dies (Judges 4:19–21). Samson asks God, receives water, and revives. The contrast is
the wiki's; Judges does not draw it.

## Significance

- **The thirst after the victory.** Samson's thirst comes immediately after a thousand dead and a song
  about himself. The chapter sets his strength beside his need, and the answer to the need comes only by
  asking.
- **The pattern of the wilderness, inside the land.** Water from God for people who expect to die is a
  wilderness motif (Genesis 21; Exodus 17). Judges places it in Judah, for one man, at a named spring that
  still existed *to this day*. See [[The Wilderness]] and [[Prayer]].
