# A Brother to Jackals - Job 30.29 and the Creatures of the Ruins

## Passages involved

- **Job 30:29** — *"I am a brother to jackals, and a companion to ostriches."* — [[Now They Mock Me]]
- **Job 30:3–7** — the outcasts who gnaw *"the dry ground, in the gloom of waste and desolation"*,
  live in *"holes of the earth and of the rocks"* and *"bray"* among the bushes — [[Now They Mock Me]]
- **Micah 1:8** *(not yet ingested)* — *"For this I will lament and wail; I will go stripped and naked;
  I will howl like the jackals, and moan like the daughters of owls."*
- **Isaiah 13:21–22** *(not yet ingested)* — Babylon's ruin: *"their houses will be full of jackals.
  Ostriches will dwell there … and jackals in the pleasant palaces."*
- **Isaiah 34:13** *(not yet ingested)* — Edom's: *"it will be a habitation of jackals, a court for
  ostriches."*
- **Jeremiah 9:11** *(not yet ingested)* — *"I will make Jerusalem heaps, a dwelling place of
  jackals."*
- **Psalm 44:19** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Though you have crushed us in the haunt of jackals, and
  covered us with the shadow of death."*
- **Psalm 102:6** *(not yet ingested)* — *"I am like a pelican of the wilderness. I have become as an
  owl of the waste places."*
- **Lamentations 4:3** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Even the jackals offer their breast. They nurse their
  young ones. But the daughter of my people has become cruel, like the ostriches in the wilderness."*
- **Isaiah 43:20** *(not yet ingested)* — *"The animals of the field shall honor me, the jackals and the
  ostriches; because I give water in the wilderness and rivers in the desert."*
- **Job 39:13–18** *(not yet ingested)* — God's ostrich, who *"leaves her eggs on the earth"* and
  *"scorns the horse and his rider"*.
- **Job 38:27** *(not yet ingested)* — God's rain *"to satisfy the waste and desolate ground"*.

## Analysis

**Jackals and ostriches are a fixed pair, and they belong to ruins.** *Tannîm* and *bənôt yaʿănâ*
appear together in Isaiah's oracles against Babylon (13:21–22) and Edom (34:13), and jackals alone in
Jeremiah's against Jerusalem (9:11): **the animals that move in when a city has been emptied by
judgment.** They are the prophets' shorthand for a place where human society has ended. When Job says
he is their *brother* and *companion*, he is not choosing a pair of melancholy animals at random. He is
calling himself a ruined city.

**The rest of the chapter has prepared for it.** At 30:14 the mockers come *"As through a wide
breach"*, the breach in a city wall, and *"in the middle of the ruin"* (*šōʾâ*) they roll in; at 30:12
they throw up a siege ramp. **Job is besieged, breached and ruined, and in the ruin the creatures
arrive.** The image also closes a circle with the chapter's opening. The outcasts of 30:3–7 live in the
same places the jackals do — *"the gloom of waste and desolation"*, gullies, holes in the rocks — and
*bray* like animals in the brush. The man who despised the wilderness people at the start of the chapter
has joined the wilderness's animals at the end.

**Micah 1:8 is the closest verbal parallel, and it is about the sound.** The prophet uses the same pair
for the noise of his lament: *"I will howl like the jackals, and moan like the daughters of owls"* —
*bənôt yaʿănâ*, which the WEB renders *ostriches* in Job and Isaiah and *daughters of owls* in Micah.
(The identification is genuinely uncertain: ostrich and eagle-owl are both proposed, and the cry of
either at night would serve.) **So Job 30:29 may mean both things at once**: Job lives where they live,
and he makes the noise they make — which is how the next verse but one can turn his harp *"into the
voice of those who weep"* (30:31). The verse sits between *"I stand up in the assembly, and cry for
help"* (30:28) and the harp, and both neighbours are about sound.

**The lamenting psalmist reaches for the same zoology.** Psalm 102:6 makes the sufferer *"a pelican of
the wilderness … an owl of the waste places"*; Psalm 44:19 puts the beaten people *"in the haunt of
jackals."* Where the prophets use the ruin-animals of a city under judgment, the laments use them of a
person who has been left in that condition. Job's line belongs to the second use, and it borrows the
first use's weight.

**Lamentations 4:3 turns the ostrich into a moral.** The ostrich *"in the wilderness"* is cruel to her
young, and the jackal by contrast nurses hers. **God's own ostrich, in Job 39:13–18, has the same
reputation** — *"She deals harshly with her young ones, as if they were not hers"* (39:16) — but God
does not condemn her for it. He says he made her that way (*"God has deprived her of wisdom"*, 39:17)
and then admires her speed. The Hebrew there is a different word (*rənānîm*), so the whirlwind speech is
not quoting Job's verse, but it describes the same bird.

**And that is the turn the whirlwind will make.** Job uses the wilderness and its animals as the image
of being abandoned: no society, no water, no name. God's answer takes him into the same wilderness and
fills it with creatures God feeds, waters and sets free — the wild donkey given *"the salt land"* for a
home (39:6), rain sent *"to satisfy the waste and desolate ground"* (38:27), *šōʾâ ûmšōʾâ*, the very
pair Job used of the outcasts' gloom at 30:3. **Isaiah 43:20 says it outright**: *"the jackals and the
ostriches"* shall honour God *"because I give water in the wilderness."* For Job the jackal's country is
where God is absent. For Isaiah, and for God in Job 38–39, it is a place God tends.

**A translation note.** *Tannîm* (jackals) is one letter from *tannîn* (sea monster, dragon — the
*"sea monster"* of 7:12), and the two were confused early. The King James Version reads Job 30:29 *"I am
a brother to dragons, and a companion to owls"*, and older English and Greek renderings of the
ruin-oracles often have dragons or sirens where modern ones have jackals. The modern consensus is that
the ruin-texts mean the jackal; the wiki follows the WEB.

## Significance

- **It makes Job a ruined city.** The pair is the prophets' sign for a place after judgment, and Job
  applies it to himself, the end of a chapter built on breach and siege.
- **It joins the end of chapter 30 to its beginning.** The outcasts Job despised live in the jackals'
  country; by 30:29 he lives there too.
- **It is a lament's sound as well as a ruin's population.** Micah 1:8 uses the pair for wailing, and
  Job's verse sits between a cry for help and a harp turned to weeping.
- **It is answered from the whirlwind.** The wilderness Job uses as the image of God's absence is the
  place God describes, in Job 38–39, as stocked, watered and cared for — and the ostrich turns up in it.
  See [[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]] for the other creature God turns from
  Job's complaint into God's own description, and
  [[A Wild Donkey of a Man - Genesis 16.12 and Job 11.12]] for the wild donkey.
