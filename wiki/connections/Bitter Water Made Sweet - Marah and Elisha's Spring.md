# Bitter Water Made Sweet - Marah and Elisha's Spring

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 15:23–26** — *"they couldn't drink from the waters of Marah, for they were bitter … Yahweh
  showed him a tree, and he threw it into the waters, and the waters were made sweet … for I am Yahweh who
  heals you."* — [[Bitter Water at Marah]]
- **2 Kings 2:19–22** — *"the water is bad, and the land is barren." He said, "Bring me a new jar, and put
  salt in it." … He went out to the spring of the waters, and threw salt into it, and said, "Yahweh says,
  'I have healed these waters. There shall not be from there any more death or barren wasteland.'" So the
  waters were healed to this day, according to Elisha's word which he spoke.* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**Two prophets make bad water good by throwing something into it, and in both the verb is the same and the
act is called healing.** (Hebrew of Exodus 15:25–26 and 2 Kings 2:21–22 checked in the WLC.)

| | Marah (Exodus 15) | Jericho (2 Kings 2) |
|---|---|---|
| The problem | water *"bitter"* (*mārîm*) | water *"bad"* (*rāʿîm*), land *"barren"* (WLC) |
| Who asks | the people murmur: *"What shall we drink?"* | the men of the city report it to Elisha |
| The prophet | Moses *"cried to Yahweh"* | Elisha asks for *"a new jar"* and salt |
| The object | *ʿēṣ*, a tree or wood, *"shown"* by Yahweh | *melaḥ*, salt |
| The act | ***wayyašlēk***, *he threw it* into the waters | ***wayyašlek***, *he threw* salt into the spring |
| The result | *"the waters were made sweet"* | *"the waters were healed to this day"* |
| The healing word | ***"I am Yahweh who heals (rōpəʾekā) you"*** — of the people | ***"I have healed (rippîtî) these waters"*** — of the water |

**The verb is *šālak* in the hiphil in both** — *wayyašlēk* at Exodus 15:25 and *wayyašlek* at 2 Kings
2:21, the same form with a pause vowel (WLC). **And *rāpāʾ* closes both**, but differently: at Marah God is
the healer of the people, and the water is only *made sweet* (*mātaq*); at Jericho the *water* is what is
healed (*rippîtî*, piel; *wayyērāpû*, niphal, 2:22; WLC). **What Exodus says of the people, 2 Kings says of
the spring.**

**Elisha has just received Moses' kind of sign.** The verses before the spring have him strike the Jordan
with Elijah's mantle: *"When he also had struck the waters, they were divided apart, and Elisha went over"*
(2 Kings 2:14; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **The new prophet crosses water and then heals water, in that
order — the order of Exodus 14–15.** Whether 2 Kings intends the sequence, or the parallel arises from the stock of prophetic signs, the
text does not say (reported, not decided).

**The differences are as plain as the likenesses.** At Marah the remedy is *shown* by God (*wayyôrēhû*,
*yārâ*, the verb of teaching; WLC) and attached to a statute and a test; at Jericho Elisha chooses the
remedy himself, and nothing is asked of the city. **Marah's healing is conditional and covenantal; Jericho's
is a gift *"according to Elisha's word"***. And Jericho is a city under a curse (Joshua 6:26; by reference,
*not yet ingested*), which readers often connect with the *"barren"* land (reported, not decided).

**Christian reading has gone further with Marah's tree.** Early interpreters took the wood thrown into the
bitter water as a figure of the cross (reported, not checked in this pass). **The text supplies no such
reading**, and the wiki records it without adopting it. The nearest canonical image is Revelation's
tree of life by the river, whose *"leaves … were for the healing of the nations"* (Revelation 22:2; WEB;
*not yet ingested*) — tree, water, and healing together, but with no reference to Marah. See
[[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]].

## Significance

**Marah is the first making-good of water in Scripture — *mātaq*, *be sweet*, occurs nowhere else in the
Torah (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy).** **The Hebrew Bible speaks of water being *healed*
(*rāpāʾ*) in 2 Kings 2:21–22 and in Ezekiel's river from the temple** — *"the waters of the sea will be
healed, and everything will live wherever the river comes"* (Ezekiel 47:8–9; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*;
the WLC search covered Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, 2 Kings, and Psalms, not the whole canon). **Read
together, Marah and Jericho show the sign passing from the lawgiver to the prophets, and Ezekiel carries the
healed water forward to the end.** Marah ties the healing to the God who heals and to a condition; Jericho
ties it to the prophet's word and to the place. Both end in water anyone can drink.
