# Embalmed and Put in a Coffin - The Only Two Mummies in Scripture

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 50:2–3** — *"the physicians embalmed Israel"*; forty days, and seventy of weeping —
  [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]
- **Genesis 50:26** — *"they embalmed him, and he was put in a coffin in Egypt"* — the same pericope
- **Genesis 23:1–20** — the Hebrew alternative: a cave bought for cash —
  [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]]
- **Exodus 25:10** — the second *ʾārôn* in the Torah

## Analysis

***Ḥānaṭ***, ***to embalm***, **occurs three times in the entire Hebrew Bible and all three are in
Genesis 50** — twice of [[Jacob]] in verse 2, once of [[Joseph]] in verse 26. **There are exactly
two embalmed bodies in Scripture and they are the last two men in the book.**

**The word itself is not a burial word.** **Its only other use in the Hebrew Bible is agricultural**:
Song of Songs 2:13, *"the fig tree ripens her green figs"* — ***ḥānəṭâ***. **The root is about
something being brought to a preserved, finished state, and Hebrew evidently had no native
vocabulary for what Egyptian morticians did, so it borrowed one.** **The language's own poverty here
is the point: this is not a thing Israelites do.**

### Two burials, two nationalities, one chapter

| | **Jacob, 50:2–13** | **Joseph, 50:26** |
|---|---|---|
| **embalmed** | yes, by *the physicians* | yes, by an unnamed *they* |
| **mourned** | forty days of embalming, seventy days by Egypt, seven at [[Abel Mizraim]] | **nothing recorded** |
| **cortege** | Pharaoh's court, elders, chariots, horsemen | **none** |
| **carried by** | his twelve sons | **nobody, for four hundred years** |
| **buried** | [[Machpelah]], in the field Abraham bought | **not buried at all** |
| **final location** | [[Canaan]] | ***"in a coffin in Egypt"*** |

**Both men get the Egyptian treatment and only one of them gets a grave.** **Jacob is embalmed so
that a body can survive a four-hundred-mile journey; Joseph is embalmed so that a body can survive a
four-hundred-year wait.** **The same procedure serves a departure and a deferral.**

**And the chapter puts them at either end of itself.** **Verse 2 embalms Israel and verse 26 embalms
Joseph, and everything in between — the funeral, the forgiveness, the hundred and ten years — sits
inside a frame made of Egyptian mortuary practice.** **A book that has spent fifty chapters
establishing that this family buries its dead in a cave it paid for ends with both of its last two
patriarchs on an Egyptian embalming table.**

### What Genesis does not say about it

**The narrator passes no judgement.** **There is no *"this was evil in Yahweh's sight"*, no hint that
the procedure is a compromise, and no contrast drawn with [[Abraham]]'s cash purchase at Hebron.**
**Later Judaism would be considerably less relaxed about it**, and the Torah's own corpse-impurity
laws (Numbers 19:11–22) make an Israelite handling of a body for seventy days close to unthinkable.
**Genesis, written before any of that, reports the embalmings the way it reports the chariots: as
what a vizier's family in Egypt would naturally have.** The wiki records the silence and does not
fill it. See [[Burial and the Grave]] and [[Clean and Unclean]].

### The coffin

***ʾĀrôn*** **is the last significant noun in Genesis**, and **it is the word the Torah will use
about three hundred times for the ark of the covenant** (Exodus 25:10 and onward). **The only two
other uses of it outside that sense are Joseph's coffin here and the chest Jehoiada bores a hole in
for the temple collection at 2 Kings 12:9.**

**So the first *ʾārôn* in Scripture holds a dead man in Egypt, and the second carries the presence of
God out of the wilderness.** **No verse connects them.** **What connects them is that Israel walks
out of Egypt carrying one and spends forty years carrying the other**, and that both are boxes the
nation is defined by moving. **The wiki notes the vocabulary and declines to make it a doctrine.**

## Significance

**The embalmings are the clearest measure of how far the family has travelled from the cave at
[[Machpelah]].**

**Genesis 23 is a chapter about a man with no land refusing a gift and insisting on paying market
price for a burial plot, so that his wife's grave would be a legal possession in the country he had
been promised.** **Genesis 50 is a chapter in which that man's great-grandson is preserved by
Egyptian craft and left above ground in the Nile delta.** **The distance between those two chapters
is the distance the book ends on.**

**And the second embalming is what makes the ending forward-looking rather than final.** **A buried
man is finished with. A preserved one is waiting.** **Joseph's body is the only thing in Genesis
that is deliberately left unresolved**, and it is unresolved because he asked for it to be. Filed as
[[Carry Up My Bones - Joseph's Oath and Its Keeping]].
