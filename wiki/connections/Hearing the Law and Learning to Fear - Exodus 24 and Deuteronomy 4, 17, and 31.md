# Hearing the Law and Learning to Fear - Exodus 24 and Deuteronomy 4, 17, and 31

## Passages involved
- [[The Blood of the Covenant]] — Exodus 24:3–8.
- [[Hear the Statutes and Remember Horeb]] — Deuteronomy 4:9–10.
- [[Hear Israel and Love Yahweh]] — Deuteronomy 6:6–9.
- [[The Law for the King]] — Deuteronomy 17:18–20.
- [[Read the Law to All Israel]] — Deuteronomy 31:9–13.

## Analysis

Exodus 24:4, 7 depicts Moses writing Yahweh’s words and reading the covenant book to the
people, who answer with a commitment to obey. Deuteronomy 31:9–13 again joins writing and
public hearing, but establishes a recurring assembly after Moses’ leadership rather than
narrating the covenant ceremony at Sinai. The later provision mentions no blood rite.

Deuteronomy 4:10 recalls Horeb as an assembly to hear, learn to fear Yahweh, and teach children.
The same purposes organize the recurring gathering of 31:12–13, including children without
prior knowledge. This is a continuity of pedagogical purpose, not a claim that every gathering
repeats Horeb’s divine manifestation.

The king’s reading in 17:18–20 likewise aims at learning to fear and doing the law. His daily
reading and the community’s seven-year reading differ in audience and schedule, while both
place people under instruction. The priests figure in the custody of the written law in both.
Household teaching in 6:6–9 fills out the picture: festival reading does not restrict learning
to one occasion every seven years.

## Significance

Written preservation serves audible instruction and practiced obedience. Ruler, household,
priests, elders, children, and resident foreigners participate in different ways; possession
of a written text does not replace learning it. See [[Law and Obedience]], [[Remembrance]],
and [[The Fear of God]].
