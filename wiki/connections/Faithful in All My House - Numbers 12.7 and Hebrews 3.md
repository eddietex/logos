# Faithful in All My House - Numbers 12.7 and Hebrews 3

## Passages involved

- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:7; *My servant Moses is not so. He is faithful in all my house.*
- **Hebrews 3:2** — *who was faithful to him who appointed him, as also was Moses in all his house* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Hebrews 3:5** — *Moses indeed was faithful in all his house as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were afterward to be spoken* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Hebrews 3:6** — Christ as a son over his house (SBLGNT, paraphrased) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Hebrews takes its words from the Greek.** Rahlfs at Numbers 12:7 has *ὁ θεράπων μου Μωυσῆς ἐν ὅλῳ τῷ οἴκῳ μου πιστός ἐστιν*. Hebrews 3:5 has *Μωϋσῆς μὲν πιστὸς ἐν ὅλῳ τῷ οἴκῳ αὐτοῦ ὡς θεράπων* (SBLGNT). The three key words are the same: *πιστός*, *ἐν ὅλῳ τῷ οἴκῳ*, and *θεράπων*. Hebrews changes *my* to *his*. At 3:2 SBLGNT prints a shorter *ἐν τῷ οἴκῳ αὐτοῦ* and marks a variant there, and WEB has *in all his house*. The apparatus was not read.

**The Hebrew is close, and it carries a second idea.** *Bəkol-bêtî neʾĕmān hûʾ* (WLC) uses the niphal participle of *ʾāman*. Exodus 14:31 says the people *believed* (*wayyaʾămînû*) in Yahweh and in his servant Moses, so the same root makes him one who can be relied on.

**The argument turns the verse.** Numbers 12 uses the words to raise Moses above other prophets. Hebrews accepts that praise whole and uses it as the measure for someone higher. Moses is *in* the house, as a servant, and Christ is *over* it as a son. The contrast is in prepositions and titles, and it does not need Moses to have failed. His service is said to be *for a testimony* of what would be spoken later.

**The verse's house.** In Numbers, *my house* is not explained. In Hebrews, *whose house we are* (3:6) identifies it with people.

## Significance

The highest praise the Torah gives Moses becomes the starting point of the New Testament's comparison. It was spoken to defend him from his own brother and sister. Hebrews 3:7–19 then turns to the wilderness generation. See [[Faith]] and [[Priesthood]].
