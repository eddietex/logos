# The God of the Spirits of All Flesh - Numbers 16.22 and 27.16

## Passages involved

- [[The Rebellion of Korah]] — Numbers 16:22: *"God, the God of the spirits of all flesh, shall
  one man sin, and will you be angry with all the congregation?"* (stored WEB).
- [[Joshua Commissioned]] — Numbers 27:16: *"Let Yahweh, the God of the spirits of all flesh,
  appoint a man over the congregation"* (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**The title occurs only in these two verses among the books stored in `raw/`** (Genesis–Numbers,
Job, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon). Both are addressed to God at a crisis of
leadership, and both concern *the congregation*.

**The Hebrew phrase is the same.** Both verses read *ʾĕlōhê hārûḥōt ləkol-bāśār* (Miqra according
to the Masorah, via Sefaria). The vocatives differ: 16:22 opens with *ʾēl*, WEB *God*; 27:16 has
the divine name, WEB *Yahweh*.

**The appeals run in opposite directions.** In chapter 16 Moses and Aaron fall on their faces and
ask that the whole congregation not die for one man's sin: the title grounds an appeal to God's
discrimination in judgment. In chapter 27 Moses asks God to *appoint* one man over the
congregation: the title grounds an appeal to God's knowledge of whom to choose. In both cases the
one who knows every spirit is asked to distinguish one person from the many.

**What the text does not say.** Neither verse explains the title. Reading it as God's knowledge of
every person's inner life, or as his authority over the breath of every living thing, are
interpretations; both are compatible with the contexts, and neither is spelled out. The wiki does
not connect *spirits* here to the *Spirit* said to be in Joshua (27:18) beyond noting that both
use *rûaḥ*; the text draws no line between them.

## Significance

Moses' request at the succession borrows the language of the earlier intercession. The first
was for the people's lives; the second is for their leadership after his
death. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]], [[Prayer]], [[Moses]], and [[Aaron]].
