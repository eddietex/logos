# As White as Snow - Moses' Hand, Miriam, and Gehazi

## Passages involved

- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 4:6; *his hand was leprous, as white as snow*
- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:10; *Miriam was leprous, as white as snow*
- **2 Kings 5:27** — *He went out from his presence a leper, as white as snow* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **2 Chronicles 26:20** — Uzziah, *leprous in his forehead* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Three verses, one phrase.** By the WLC tags, the participle of *ṣāraʿ* and *kaššāleg* stand together in exactly three verses: *məṣōraʿat kaššāleg* (Exodus 4:6; Numbers 12:10) and *məṣōrāʿ kaššāleg* (2 Kings 5:27). The Hebrew has no word for *white*; WEB supplies it in all three.

| Who | What it is | How it ends |
|---|---|---|
| Moses' hand | A sign, given to convince | Restored in the next verse |
| Miriam | A sentence, for speaking against Moses | Seven days out; healing not stated |
| Gehazi | A sentence, for lying and taking gifts | *to you and to your offspring forever* |

**The sign and the judgment.** At the bush, Moses' own hand shows what Yahweh can do, and he is afraid to take up his office. In Numbers 12, the sister who challenges that office wears the same sign. The link is verbal, and Numbers does not refer back.

**Uzziah.** 2 Chronicles 26 belongs to the group by theme. A king goes into the Tent's successor to do a priest's work, and the leprosy appears in the forehead while he is angry. It has no snow. All three judged cases touch a boundary: who speaks for God, who profits from his gift, and who may burn incense.

## Significance

Leprosy in these stories is not only a medical condition. It separates a person in a visible way, and in the two sentences it comes from God at once. Leviticus 13–14 gives the ordinary case with no cause assigned, and these stories should not be read back into every case. See [[Clean and Unclean]] and [[Judgment]].
