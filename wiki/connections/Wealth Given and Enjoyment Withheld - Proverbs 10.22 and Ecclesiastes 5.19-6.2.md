# Wealth Given and Enjoyment Withheld - Proverbs 10.22 and Ecclesiastes 5.19-6.2

## Passages involved

- [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]] — Proverbs 10:22; blessing, wealth, and no added trouble.
- [[The Lover of Silver and the Gift of Enjoyment]] — Ecclesiastes 5:18–20, especially 19.
- [[Riches Without Enjoyment]] — Ecclesiastes 6:1–9, especially 2.
- [[The Labor Left to Another]] — Ecclesiastes 2:24–26; enjoyment and contrasting recipients.

## Analysis

**The adjacent contrast is explicit in the WEB.** Ecclesiastes 5:19 describes someone to
whom God has given riches and wealth and power to eat of it, take his portion, and rejoice.
Ecclesiastes 6:2 describes someone to whom God gives riches, wealth, and honor, yet gives
no power to eat of it; an alien eats it instead. The material inventory grows, while the
capacity to experience its good disappears. No identity between the two men is asserted.

This tests a possible simplification of the gift passages. If enjoyment comes from God,
it does not follow that every person who receives wealth receives enjoyment too. The
speaker calls the deprived condition vanity and an evil disease (6:2), rather than simply
instructing the man to choose a better attitude. The reason for God's withholding remains
unstated. The added children and years of 6:3–6 cannot supply the missing good.

**The tension with Proverbs must remain visible.** Proverbs 10:22 reads:

> Yahweh’s blessing brings wealth, and he adds no trouble to it.

Ecclesiastes 6:2 attributes wealth to God while describing a grievous condition. The texts
do not use precisely identical claims: Proverbs speaks of blessing, while Ecclesiastes
speaks of divine giving without calling it blessing. That distinction blocks a simple
formal contradiction, but it does not remove the interpretive tension. Wealth's divine
source cannot serve as an automatic test of trouble-free flourishing. Neither text here
explains the deprived man's moral standing or makes his suffering proof of wrongdoing.

**Do not import the moral division of Ecclesiastes 2:26.** That verse explicitly sets the
person who pleases God beside the sinner who gathers for another. In 6:2 the owner and
alien receive no such descriptions. The common image of one person consuming another's
accumulation supports comparison, not identification of the alien as righteous or the
owner as sinful. The cause of deprivation remains unresolved.

## Significance

[[Wealth and Possessions]], [[Blessing]], [[Providence]], and [[Joy and Enjoyment]] need a
shared qualification: possessing good things and experiencing their good are distinct.
The text supports gratitude for enjoyment and lament over its absence. Explaining every
absence as the sufferer's fault would erase the difficulty Ecclesiastes deliberately names.
