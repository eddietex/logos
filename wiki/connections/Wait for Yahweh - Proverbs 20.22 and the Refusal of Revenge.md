# Wait for Yahweh - Proverbs 20.22 and the Refusal of Revenge

## Passages involved

- [[Wine Is a Mocker]] — Proverbs 20:22.
- [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] — Genesis 50:15–21.
- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 14:13–14.
- [[Job's Oath of Innocence]] — Job 31:29–30.

## Analysis

Proverbs 20:22 forbids the speaker's promise to pay back evil and directs him to wait for
Yahweh's salvation. The promised outcome is rescue for the wronged person; the verse does not
explicitly promise the enemy's destruction. It therefore cannot be reduced to arranging revenge
through a different agent.

Genesis 50:15–21 supplies a narrative comparison. The brothers fear repayment for the evil they
did; Joseph refuses to occupy God's place, acknowledges their evil, and undertakes to nourish
their households. The good outcome does not excuse the original wrong. His refusal is active
care, not denial of what happened. Proverbs does not identify Joseph as its example, so this is
a comparison rather than a claimed allusion.

Exodus 14:13–14 also directs endangered people toward Yahweh's saving action. Its setting is an
army's pursuit, not a private decision to retaliate; the overlap is reliance on divine rescue.
Job 31:29–30 adds a narrower ethical parallel: Job denies rejoicing at an enemy's destruction
or asking for his death with a curse. None of these settings supplies a complete procedure for
civil justice or self-defense.

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 20:22; Genesis 50:15–21; Exodus 14:13–14; Job 31:29–30. No
external quotation or source-language equivalence is claimed.

## Significance

The command gives [[Vengeance]] a limit and [[Faith]] a concrete practice. It joins [[Strife and Peacemaking]] to
[[Providence]] without pretending that the evil suffered was good or that waiting specifies a
timetable.
