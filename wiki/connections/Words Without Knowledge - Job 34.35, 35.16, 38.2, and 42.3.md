# Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3

## Passages involved

- **Job 34:35** — Elihu, giving the wise men's verdict: *"'Job speaks without knowledge. His words are
  without wisdom.'"* — [[Elihu on God's Justice]]
- **Job 35:16** — Elihu again, closing his third speech: *"Therefore Job opens his mouth with empty
  talk, and he multiplies words without knowledge."* — [[Elihu on God's Justice]]
- **Job 38:2** *(not yet ingested)* — Yahweh, his first words out of the whirlwind: *"Who is this who
  darkens counsel by words without knowledge?"*
- **Job 42:3** *(not yet ingested)* — Job, quoting it back in his reply: *"You asked, 'Who is this who
  hides counsel without knowledge?' therefore I have uttered that which I did not understand, things
  too wonderful for me, which I didn't know."*
- **Job 42:7** *(not yet ingested)* — Yahweh to Eliphaz: *"you have not spoken of me the thing that is
  right, as my servant Job has."*
- **Job 15:2–3** — Eliphaz, the first to fault Job's words as wind: *"Should a wise man answer with vain
  knowledge, and fill himself with the east wind? Should he reason with unprofitable talk, or with
  speeches with which he can do no good?"* — [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]]
- **Job 40:8** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Will you condemn me, that you may be justified?"*, God asking
  the question Elihu asked at 34:17.

## Analysis

**One charge, made four times, by three speakers.** Elihu makes it twice (34:35; 35:16), God makes it
once (38:2), and Job makes it of himself (42:3). In Hebrew the phrase is the same at its core —
*lōʾ bədaʿat* at 34:35, *bibəlî-daʿat* at 35:16, *bəlî-dāʿat* at 38:2 and 42:3 — *without knowledge*,
attached three times to Job's *words* (*dəbārāyw* at 34:35, *millîn* at 35:16 and 38:2). No other
speaker in the book is charged with it.

The sequence is the problem. **The last thing Elihu says about Job before God speaks is the first
thing God says about Job.** Elihu ends chapter 35 on *"multiplies words without knowledge"*; chapters
36–37 are his fourth speech, and then *"Who is this who darkens counsel by words without
knowledge?"* (38:2). And Job, when he answers, does not dispute it: he takes God's question into his
own mouth and confesses, *"I have uttered that which I did not understand"* (42:3).

So on the charge itself the book is unanimous. Then, four verses later, it says the thing that
complicates every reading of Elihu: *"you have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant
Job has"* (42:7). **Job's words were without knowledge, and they were right.** Both are said by God,
within a single chapter's distance.

### What the two verdicts measure

The way through, which the wiki adopts as a reading rather than a settled conclusion, is that the two
verdicts measure different things.

- ***Without knowledge* is about scope.** Job spoke of things he could not see — the purpose of his
  suffering, the governance of the world, whether God was just in his case. The whirlwind's questions
  (*"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?"*, 38:4) are all questions about what
  Job knows, and Job's surrender at 42:3 is a confession of ignorance, not of sin: *"things too
  wonderful for me, which I didn't know"*.
- ***The thing that is right* (*nəkônâ*) is about truthfulness.** Job refused to say what he did not
  believe; the friends said what they did not know to be true, and said it *"for God"* (13:7–8).
  Job's words were honest and addressed to God. The friends' were confident and wrong.

On that reading, **Elihu has the charge right and the conclusion wrong.** He says Job speaks without
knowledge — God agrees — and concludes that Job is answering *"like wicked men"* and *"adds rebellion
to his sin"* (34:36–37). God draws no such conclusion. He questions Job, and then commends him.

### The other readings

- **Elihu as the whirlwind's herald.** Readers who take Elihu as the book's preparer for God's speech
  point to this thread first: God's opening words echo Elihu's closing ones, God's 40:8 asks Elihu's
  34:17, and Elihu's fourth speech ends in a storm and the wonders of the sky, which is where God
  begins. On this reading Elihu is not rebuked at 42:7–9 because he was right.
- **Elihu set aside.** Readers who take him as the most articulate version of the friends' error point
  to the same verses read the other way: God says *"without knowledge"* of Job, and then commends Job;
  Elihu is neither commended nor mentioned. On this reading the echo at 38:2 is God taking the charge
  out of Elihu's mouth and making it a question instead of a verdict — *who is this?*, not *this man
  is wicked*.
- **Elihu as later addition.** Scholars who regard chapters 32–37 as added to the book can read the
  echo as the addition's author anchoring his speaker to God's words, rather than God's words echoing
  him.

The wiki records all three and reads the book as received.

### Eliphaz first

The first speaker to fault Job's words was Eliphaz, and his terms are the same: *"vain knowledge"*
(*daʿat-rûaḥ*, windy knowledge) and *"unprofitable talk"* (15:2–3). The difference between Eliphaz and
God is not the observation but the tone and the sequel. Eliphaz's question was rhetorical; God's is
the beginning of four chapters of questions Job is meant to try to answer.

## Significance

- It is the strongest single piece of evidence for reading Elihu as close to God's view, and the
  evidence has to be weighed against 42:7 every time it is used.
- It makes the book's verdict on Job two-sided in the book's own words: wrong in what he claimed to
  know, right in what he said. Neither half cancels the other.
- It gives Job's surrender at 42:3 its exact content. He does not confess the sins the friends and
  Elihu named; he confesses the one charge God made.
