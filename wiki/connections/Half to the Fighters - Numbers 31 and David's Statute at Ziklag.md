# Half to the Fighters - Numbers 31 and David's Statute at Ziklag

## Passages involved

- [[The War Against Midian]] — Numbers 31:25–47; the plunder halved *between the men skilled in war, who
  went out to battle, and all the congregation* (27), with a levy from each half.
- 1 Samuel 30:24–25 — *as his share is who goes down to the battle, so shall his share be who stays with
  the baggage. They shall share alike.* … *he made it a statute and an ordinance for Israel to this day*
  (WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested).

## Analysis

**Two rules for the same problem: who owns what the fighters bring back.** In Numbers, Yahweh divides
the plunder in two equal halves, one for twelve thousand soldiers and one for the whole congregation
(31:27). The soldiers' share per head is far larger, but the people who did not fight receive half of
everything. Each half pays a levy: one in five hundred from the soldiers' half to Eleazar, one in fifty
from the congregation's to the Levites (28–30). Plunder taken *every man for himself* (53) sits outside
the division.

**At Ziklag**, after the raid on the Amalekites, David rules that those who stayed with the baggage share
alike with those who fought, and the narrator says it became *a statute and an ordinance for Israel* (1
Samuel 30:24–25). Both texts reject the view that the plunder belongs only to those who fought; both make
the rule a statute. They differ in shape — halves between army and congregation in Numbers, equal shares
inside the army at Ziklag — and 1 Samuel does not cite Numbers 31.

## Significance

The comparison is thematic, not a claim of literary dependence. Numbers grounds its rule in a command from
Yahweh; 1 Samuel presents David's as a new ordinance *from that day forward*. The wiki records both as
answers to one question and does not make one the source of the other.
