# Taberah Remembered - Numbers 11, Deuteronomy 9, and Psalm 78

## Passages involved

- [[The Fire at Taberah]] — Numbers 11:1–3; *"Yahweh's fire burned among them"*
- [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] — Numbers 11:33–34; the plague, and [[Kibroth Hattaavah]] named
- [[Remember the Calf and Moses' Intercession]] — Deuteronomy 9:7–29, especially verse 22.
- **Psalm 78:18–21** — *"Will he provide meat for his people?" Therefore Yahweh heard, and was angry. A fire was kindled
  against Jacob* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Deuteronomy names it, and Psalm 78 may recall it.** Only Deuteronomy 9:22 uses the name (tags). It stands first in a list
of three that is not in story order, because Massah (Exodus 17) comes before the other two. Moses is speaking, in that
chapter, about the calf and his own forty days. So the list is a charge sheet, and the order serves something other than
the timeline. What that is, the verse does not say. Deuteronomy calls all three the same thing, *you provoked Yahweh to wrath*.

**Numbers gives the name no words of complaint; Psalm 78 has a speech.** The psalm has the people ask for meat, then says
*Yahweh heard, and was angry*, and then *a fire was kindled*. Those are the same steps as Numbers 11:1, *heard*, *anger*,
*fire*, but the psalm does not name Taberah, and the demand for meat comes in Numbers at 11:4, after the fire. The Hebrew of
the psalm was not compared for this page. Either the psalm has run the two stories together, or it speaks of something
else. The wiki keeps both possible.

**The two namings.** Numbers 11:3 and 11:34 use the same sentence, *The name of that place was called* (WEB), a chapter
apart. Both come after a burst of Yahweh's anger (11:1, 33). The first ended with a prayer, and the second reports no prayer.

**With the second story in view.** [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] has the meat that Psalm 78:18–21 asks for, and Psalm 78:26–31 tells the quail after it. So the psalm does have
the quail, and the fire of verse 21 comes earlier in its telling than the quail. See
[[The Graves of Craving - Numbers 11, Psalms 78 and 106, and 1 Corinthians 10]].

**The wider Deuteronomy context now ingested.** In Deuteronomy 9:7–29, the three-place list
is followed by Kadesh’s rebellion and unbelief (23), then the summary that Israel has been
rebellious throughout Moses’ acquaintance with it (24). The list substantiates the denial of
merit in 9:4–6 rather than supplying an itinerary. The prayer that follows appeals to redemption
and the ancestral servants while acknowledging the people’s sin (26–29).

## Significance

Taberah is three verses in Numbers, and it could have dropped out of the memory of the wilderness. It stays because
Deuteronomy puts it first in the list. The place has no other story, and its only other mention is as a charge.
See [[Do Not Test Yahweh - Massah in Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and Hebrews]] for Massah in the same list.
