# Yahweh Will Provide - Moriah and the Temple Mount

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 22:2** — *"go into the land of Moriah"* — the first of the Bible's two occurrences of the
  name. See [[The Binding of Isaac]].
- **Genesis 22:8** — *"God will provide himself the lamb for a burnt offering"* — *ʾĕlōhîm yirʾeh-lô*
- **Genesis 22:14** — *"Abraham called the name of that place Yahweh Will Provide. As it is said to
  this day, 'On Yahweh's mountain, it will be provided.'"*
- **2 Chronicles 3:1** — *"Solomon began to build Yahweh's house at Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where
  Yahweh appeared to David his father, in the place that David had prepared in the threshing floor of
  Ornan the Jebusite"*
- **Exodus 23:17; 34:23–24; Deuteronomy 16:16** — *"three times in the year all your males shall
  appear before the Lord Yahweh"* — the passive of the same verb
- **Genesis 14:18; Psalm 76:2** — Melchizedek, king of **Salem**, and *"His tabernacle is in Salem"*

## Analysis

**Genesis 22 is one long conjugation of a single verb, and the verb is *rāʾâ*, to see.**

| verse | Hebrew | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | *wayyiśśāʾ … wayyarʾ* | Abraham lifts his eyes and **sees** the place, far off |
| 8 | *ʾĕlōhîm yirʾeh-lô* | God will **see to** the lamb — for himself |
| 13 | *wayyarʾ wəhinnēh* | Abraham lifts his eyes and **sees** the ram |
| 14a | *YHWH yirʾeh* | the place is named ***Yahweh sees*** |
| 14b | *bəhar YHWH yērāʾeh* | *on Yahweh's mountain* **it will be seen / provided / he will appear** |

**English has to choose between *see* and *provide*; Hebrew does not.** The two are one idea when God
is the subject — seeing to a thing is doing something about it — and English *provide* preserves the
same metaphor in Latin (*pro-videre*, to see ahead). The chapter is built on that identity and the
proverb in verse 14 is its punchline.

### The line that does the work is the last one

***Yērāʾeh*** is the passive, and in unpointed Hebrew it is the same three consonants as the active.
It can be read three ways, and all three are grammatical:

1. **it will be provided** — the WEB's choice, and the natural continuation of verse 8;
2. **it will be seen** — the flat passive;
3. **he will appear** — and this is the technical term for pilgrimage. *"Three times in the year all
   your males shall appear before the Lord Yahweh"* (Exodus 23:17) is *yērāʾeh*, exactly this form.

**So a verse about a mountain where something was provided is written in the precise idiom of a
mountain where Israel goes up to be seen by God.** Whether Genesis intended the pilgrimage sense is
not decidable; that the consonants carry it is not in dispute, and it is difficult to believe a
post-temple audience heard verse 14 without hearing the festival law in it.

### And Chronicles closes the circuit

**2 Chronicles 3:1 is the only other verse in the Bible that uses the word *Moriah*, and it puts the
temple on it.** It also supplies its own *appeared* — *"where Yahweh appeared to David his father"* —
using the same root a second time, and it names the site by a third route: the threshing floor of
Ornan the Jebusite, bought by David to stop a plague after an angel with a drawn sword stood over
Jerusalem (2 Samuel 24; 1 Chronicles 21). **Three narratives of a sword stopped over a place, and one
mountain.**

The identification is Chronicles', not Genesis's. **Genesis never says Jerusalem, never says Salem,
and never connects Moriah to Melchizedek** — which is the strongest argument that the link is a later
theological claim rather than the chapter's own geography. The strongest argument the other way is
that Chronicles had no obvious motive to invent a name found nowhere else, and 22:14's *"as it is
said to this day"* shows the saying was already attached to a known place.

### The counter-tradition

**The Samaritan Pentateuch reads *the land of Moreh*, not *Moriah*** — one consonant — putting the
binding beside Shechem, at Gerizim, where the Samaritan temple stood. Both communities used the
reading polemically for centuries. **The wiki records it as a real textual variant with a real
geography behind it, and adopts neither site.** See [[Moriah]].

## Significance

- **It is the mechanism by which the Abraham story becomes the temple's charter.** If Moriah is the
  temple mount, then every burnt offering in Jerusalem is offered on the spot where a son was not
  burned, and the ram *"instead of his son"* is the first of them. The whole sacrificial system
  acquires an origin story in which the substitution is the point. See
  [[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]] and [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
- **It is a case study in how a place-name etymology works in Genesis.** *Môrîyâ* probably derives
  from *yārâ*, to teach or point; the chapter treats it as though it came from *rāʾâ*, to see. **The
  narrative etymology is a play on the name, not a claim about its history**, which is equally true
  of Babel, Beersheba, and Isaac's own name. See [[Laughter and the Name Isaac]] and [[Beersheba]].
- **And it explains the chapter's one line of direct address to the reader.** *"As it is said to this
  day"* means the audience already had the proverb; Genesis is telling them where it came from. The
  chapter is not only a story about Abraham — it is an answer to a question its first hearers had
  about a saying they used.
