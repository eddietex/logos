# No Witness Against Her - Numbers 5, Exodus 22, and Deuteronomy 19

## Passages involved

- **Numbers 5:13** — [[The Suspected Wife]]: *"there is no witness against her, and she isn't taken in the act"*.
- **Exodus 22:10–11** — [[Laws of Property and Restitution]]: *"no man seeing it; the oath of Yahweh shall be between them both"*.
- **Deuteronomy 17:6** — [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]]: *"At the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death."*
- **Deuteronomy 19:15** — [[Landmarks, Witnesses, and False Testimony]]: *"At the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall a matter be established."*
- **Deuteronomy 22:13–19** — [[Marriage Accusations and Sexual Offenses]]: the husband who gives his wife *"a bad name"*.
- **1 Kings 8:31–32** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — an oath before the altar, and Yahweh asked to judge, *"condemning the wicked … and justifying the righteous"*.

## Analysis

The law has a recurring problem: what can it do when no one saw? Deuteronomy's answer for a court is
that one witness is not enough. That rule leaves a gap, and the oath before God fills it. Exodus
22:10–11 sends a dispute over a lost animal to *the oath of Yahweh*, and 1 Kings 8:31–32 asks God to
act on such an oath. Numbers 5 belongs in this series, and it goes further, because here the oath is
sealed with an act and a stated bodily outcome.

Numbers 5 is the Torah's sharpest case because the stakes are personal, and the matter is hidden by
nature. In Exodus 22 the owner must accept the oath and no one pays. In Numbers 5 the accused is the
one sworn, by a priest, and the accuser takes no oath at all.

**The accuser.** Deuteronomy 22:13–19 punishes a husband whose charge is shown to be false: he is
chastised, fined one hundred shekels, and cannot put her away. Numbers 5:31 says *the man shall be
free from iniquity*. These are different cases, one about proof of virginity and one about adultery,
and the Deuteronomy case has tokens to show. So they do not contradict each other, but the two laws
give the husband different exposure. **The wiki states the difference and leaves it unresolved.**

### Deuteronomy 17:2–7 now ingested

[[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]] gives the first form of the rule in Deuteronomy. It applies
to a capital charge, the worship of other gods, and adds two things. An inquiry comes first: *"you
shall inquire diligently. Behold, if it is true, and the thing certain"* (17:4). And the witnesses
must throw the first stones (17:7), so testimony that kills makes the witness the first executioner.
Numbers 35:30 had stated the one-witness rule for murder. 19:15 will extend it to *"any iniquity"*.
The gap this page is about is still open: where there is no witness at all, Deuteronomy 17 has no
procedure, and Numbers 5 gives the only one for that case.

### Deuteronomy 19:15–21 now ingested

19:15 extends the rule to *"any iniquity, or … any sin"*, as expected. The verses after it deal with the
opposite case from this page's: not too few witnesses, but a witness who lies. A disputed testimony is
brought *"before Yahweh, before the priests and the judges"* (17), and the false witness receives what he
*"had thought to do to his brother"* (19). Like Numbers 5, Deuteronomy 19:17 brings the hard case *"before
Yahweh"*. But in 19:18 the judges investigate, and no oath or ordeal is described. See [[Landmarks, Witnesses, and False Testimony]] and
[[Two or Three Witnesses - Deuteronomy 19.15, Matthew 18.16, and 2 Corinthians 13.1]].

### Deuteronomy 22:13–21 now ingested

[[Marriage Accusations and Sexual Offenses]] gives the case its evidence and its procedure. The husband *"accuses her of shameful
things"* — *ʿălîlōt dǝbārîm*, *deeds of words* (WLC) — and her father and mother answer him before *"the
elders of the city in the gate"*, where *"They shall spread the cloth"* (22:15–17). If the charge is false he
is chastised, fined a hundred shekels, and may never divorce her; if it is true, she is stoned (22:18–21).
Unlike Numbers 5, the accusing husband here can be punished; unlike 19:19, the false accuser is not given
what his charge would have done to her. The passage page records that second difference unresolved.

## Significance

Taken together, these texts show that the law does not treat what it cannot prove as settled. It
moves the question to God. See [[Oaths and Vows]], [[Judgment]], and [[Seeing and Being Seen]], and
for the law of a discovered adultery,
[[No Ransom for the Adulterer - Proverbs 6.29-35, Leviticus 20.10, and Deuteronomy 22.22]].
