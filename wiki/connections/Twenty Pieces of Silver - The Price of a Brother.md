# Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:28** — *"they drew and lifted up Joseph out of the pit, and sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites
  for twenty pieces of silver."*
- **Leviticus 27:5** — *"If the person is from five years old to twenty years old, then your valuation
  shall be for a male twenty shekels, and for a female ten shekels."*
- **Exodus 21:32** — *"If the bull gores a male servant or a female servant, thirty shekels of silver
  shall be given to their master."*
- **Zechariah 11:12–13** — *"So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver … 'Throw it to the
  potter, the goodly price that I was valued at by them.'"*
- **Matthew 26:15; 27:3–10** — *"they weighed out to him thirty pieces of silver"*, and the field bought
  with them.
- **Genesis 37:26** — *"What profit is it if we kill our brother and conceal his blood?"* — ***mah-beṣaʿ***,
  *what gain*.
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — **Genesis 42:25–28, 35**: **the silver comes back, and it
  terrifies them.** [[Joseph]] orders the purchase money returned into their sacks; **one man finds his
  at the lodging place and *"their hearts failed them"*** (42:28), and **the rest of it comes out in
  front of their father** (42:35). They sold a brother for twenty pieces and told [[Jacob]] nothing;
  **twenty-two years later unexplained silver is emptied onto the floor in his sight** — and the
  question they ask is *"What is this that God has done to us?"*
- [[The Second Journey]] — **Genesis 43:12, 15, 21–23**: **the silver goes back down, doubled.**
  [[Jacob]] tells them to *"take double money in your hand, and take back the money that was returned in
  the mouth of your sacks"*, and invents a word for how it got there — ***mišgeh***, *oversight*,
  **found nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible.** They rehearse the restitution at the door — *"our money in
  full weight. We have brought it back in our hand"* (43:21) — **and it is refused**:
  [[Joseph's Steward]] tells them their God put treasure in their sacks and that he was paid anyway
  (43:23).
  **Men who once took twenty pieces for a brother cannot now give money back for grain**
- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:1–2; **silver's fourth movement, and the only one
  nobody in the story ever mentions again.** The money goes back into the sacks a second time —
  **and this time a silver *gābîaʿ* goes in on top of it.** The returned money terrified them at
  42:28 and produced a rehearsed speech at 43:19–22; **here it is simply never spoken of.** The cup
  takes all the attention, **which is what it was for**

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — **Genesis 45:22**: **silver's last movement in the cycle, and it runs
  the opposite way.** Twenty pieces out (37:28), returned in the sacks (42:25), carried down doubled
  (43:12), planted back with the cup (44:1) — **and here three hundred pieces given to [[Benjamin]],
  fifteen times the sale price, to the brother who was not sold.** Whether the number is aimed at the
  price or simply at the boy, Genesis does not say; see
  [[Five Changes of Clothing - The Coat Given Again]]

## Analysis

**Genesis names a price for a human being exactly once, and it is the price the law will later fix for a
boy of precisely that age.**

Joseph is **seventeen** (37:2). Leviticus 27:5 sets the sanctuary valuation of a male **between five and
twenty years old at twenty shekels of silver.** **The brothers get the going rate.**

| | **the figure** | **what it values** |
|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 37:28** | 20 shekels | a seventeen-year-old, sold by his brothers |
| **Leviticus 27:5** | 20 shekels | a male aged 5–20, vowed to the sanctuary and redeemed |
| **Exodus 21:32** | 30 shekels | a slave killed by someone else's ox |
| **Zechariah 11:12** | 30 shekels | a shepherd's wages, called *"the goodly price"* sarcastically |
| **Matthew 26:15** | 30 pieces | Judas's fee |

### What the parallel with Leviticus is and is not

- **It is a real convergence on a number, and the wiki says so without dating anything.** Whether
  Leviticus 27:5 preserves an old customary valuation that Genesis 37:28 also reflects, or whether the
  narrator chose a figure his readers would recognise from the tariff, **is not decidable from either
  text.** Both readings leave the effect the same: **a reader who knows the law reads 37:28 as the market
  price, correctly paid.**
- **It is not a claim that the brothers did anything legal.** Leviticus 27 is about vows and redemption,
  not slave sales, and selling a free Israelite is capital under Exodus 21:16 and Deuteronomy 24:7 —
  *"He who kidnaps a man and sells him … shall surely be put to death."* **The law that supplies the
  figure also supplies the sentence.**
- **The tariff's other line is worth noting.** For a male over twenty Leviticus 27:3 sets **fifty
  shekels**; over sixty, fifteen. **Joseph was three years short of costing two and a half times as
  much.**

### Thirty and twenty

**The thirty pieces of Matthew 26:15 are not Joseph's twenty, and the tradition that pairs them is older
than the number.** Matthew's thirty comes from **Zechariah 11:12–13**, which he cites (attributing it to
Jeremiah), and Zechariah's thirty is most likely an allusion to **Exodus 21:32** — the compensation for a
slave gored by a bull, i.e. **the statutory value of a dead servant.** *"The goodly price that I was
valued at by them"* is sarcasm about a contemptible sum.

**So the two betrayals share the structure and not the figure.** Both are a brother or a master handed
over for silver by one of his own, both have a named instigator, and **in both cases the money's fate is
the part the narrative dwells on** — except that Genesis never says the brothers took it. **The chapter
gives the price and not the payee.** Matthew, by contrast, follows the thirty into the temple, out of it,
and into a field.

**The wiki records the resemblance and refuses the equation.** No New Testament text cites Genesis 37:28.
**Where the New Testament does narrate this sale — Acts 7:9, Psalm 105:17 — it mentions no sum at all.**
See [[Sold for a Servant - Joseph's Sale in Psalm 105 and Acts 7]].

### *Mah-beṣaʿ* — the argument the price answers

**[[Judah]] does not argue for mercy.** *"What profit is it if we kill our brother and conceal his
blood?"* — ***beṣaʿ*** is **gain, and characteristically dishonest gain**: it is the word in the
qualification for judges, *"men of truth, hating unjust gain"* (Exodus 18:21), and in the prophets'
complaints about men *"greedy for gain"* (Jeremiah 6:13; Ezekiel 22:27). **Judah's objection to murder is
that murder is unremunerative**, and he closes it with the chapter's one appeal to kinship — *"he is our
brother, our flesh"* — **deployed in favour of a sale.**

**Genesis passes no verdict on the speech, and it is the speech that saves Joseph's life.** The wiki
records both. See [[Judah]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]].

## Significance

- **It is the Bible's only priced brother, and the price is ordinary.** Nothing in Genesis 37:28 is
  exorbitant, symbolic, or round-numbered for effect. **Ten men value the eleventh at the going rate for a
  seventeen-year-old and take it**, and the flatness of the transaction is the chapter's most
  uncomfortable feature. See [[Slavery and Servitude]].
- **It is the point at which the family's language changes.** Before 37:26 the brothers speak of *this
  dreamer* and *our brother*; at 37:26 they speak of *profit*. **A person becomes a sum in one sentence**,
  and Genesis marks the change by giving the sum and withholding what became of it.
- **It supplies the one thing Genesis 37 will not: a verdict.** The narration never condemns the sale.
  **Leviticus 27:5 prices it, Exodus 21:16 makes it a capital crime, and Deuteronomy 24:7 repeats the
  sentence** — and the law is the wiki's only warrant for calling it what it is, because the story does
  not. See [[Sin]] and [[Retribution]].
- **It is also, read forward, the price of the whole nation's presence in Egypt.** The consignment paid
  for at Dothan is the first Israelite in Egypt, and Genesis 15:13 had already predicted the sojourn.
  **Twenty shekels is what the Exodus cost to start.** See
  [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]] and
  [[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].
