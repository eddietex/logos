# The Land Is Very Good - The Spies of Numbers 13-14 and Judges 18

## Passages involved
- [[The Twelve Spies]] — Numbers 13–14; *an exceeding good land* (14:7).
- [[The Danite Migration]] — Judges 18:2–10; *behold, it is very good* (18:9).
- [[Twelve Spies and Two - Numbers 13 and Joshua 2]] and
  [[The Spies at Jericho and at Bethel - Joshua 2 and Judges 1]] — the other spy stories the wiki
  compares.

## Analysis

Judges 18 tells the Danite migration in the shape of the conquest's spy stories, and borrows the words of
the first one.

| | Numbers 13–14 | Judges 18 |
|---|---|---|
| Who is sent | *Of every tribe … a man, every one a prince among them* (13:2) | *Five men of their family … men of valor* (18:2) |
| The task | *See the land, what it is; and the people who dwell therein, whether they are strong or weak* (13:18) | *To spy out the land, and to search it* (18:2) |
| What they find | Strong peoples, fortified cities, and the Anakim (13:28) | A people *quiet and secure*, with no one *possessing authority* (18:7) |
| The verdict | *The land … is an exceeding good land* (14:7, Joshua and Caleb) | *We have seen the land, and behold, it is very good* (18:9) |
| The appeal | *Only don't rebel against Yahweh, neither fear the people of the land* (14:9) | *Do you stand still? Don't be slothful to go and to enter in to possess the land* (18:9) |
| The ground | *Yahweh is with us* (14:9) | *God has given it into your hand* (18:10) |
| How far north | *To Rehob, to the entrance of Hamath* (13:21) | *The valley that lies by Beth Rehob* (18:28) |

**The words.** Numbers 14:7 has *ṭôḇâ hāʾāreṣ məʾōḏ məʾōḏ*, *very, very good is the land*; Judges 18:9
has *wəhinnēh ṭôḇâ məʾōḏ*, *and behold, very good* (WLC). The verb for spying differs: Numbers 13–14 uses
*tûr*, *explore* (14:7, *lātûr*), and Judges 18 uses *rāgal* with *ḥāqar* (18:2, WLC). *Rāgal* is the verb
of Deuteronomy 1:24's retelling of the same Numbers mission and of Joshua's spies at Jericho (Joshua 2:1)
(WLC). So the Danite story shares Caleb's verdict but the verb of the later accounts.

**The reversal.** In Numbers the report is true, the people refuse, and a generation dies in the
wilderness. In Judges the report is true, the tribe goes at once, and it wins. But Caleb's land was the
whole of Canaan, held by peoples the law named; the Danites' land is one town, *far from the Sidonians*,
with *no dealings with anyone else* and *no deliverer* (18:7, 28). The spies' rebuke of hesitation, the
assurance that God has given it, and the oracle *before Yahweh* (18:6) all come from the conquest; the
target does not. The narrator does not comment beyond *there was no king in Israel* (18:1).

**Rehob.** Moses' spies went as far north as *Rehob, to the entrance of Hamath* (Numbers 13:21); Laish
lay *in the valley that lies by Beth Rehob* (Judges 18:28). Whether the two names mean one place is a
standing proposal recorded on [[Rehob]], not something either text says.

**A smaller company.** Twelve princes in Numbers, two men in Joshua 2, *the house of Joseph* with spies at
Bethel (Judges 1:22–26), five men here. The spy stories narrow as the book goes on, from the nation to a
tribe to a clan of *six hundred men* (18:11).

## Significance

The Danite migration is the last conquest story in the Bible's narrative of the land, and it is told as
a parody of the first. The vocabulary of faithful spies — *very good*, *don't be slothful*, *God has given
it into your hand* — is spoken correctly and applied to a defenseless city by a tribe carrying a stolen
image. See [[Dan Pressed from the Valley - Joshua 19, Judges 1, and Judges 18]] and
[[Searched Out, Struck, and Burned - Deuteronomy 13 and Laish]].
