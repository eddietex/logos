## Analysis

**The comparison is the wisdom tradition's commonplace, and Job 28 is its fullest form.** Proverbs says
it in a line: wisdom is *better than* silver, gold and rubies (3:14–15; 8:10–11, 19; 16:16). Job 28 says it
in five verses and seven or eight named materials, and says something more than *better*: wisdom
*cannot be exchanged* for them at all (28:15–17). Proverbs' comparison is a ranking on a single scale.
Job's is a statement that there is no scale — *"Man doesn't know its price"* (28:13).

**Proverbs 2:4 and Job 28 use the same picture to opposite effect.** *"If you seek her as silver, and
search for her as for hidden treasures"* — Proverbs takes the miner's search as the model for seeking
wisdom and promises that it succeeds, because *"Yahweh gives wisdom"* (2:6). Job 28 describes that
search in loving detail (28:1–11) and then says it does not reach wisdom: the miner can go where the
falcon has not looked and still not find the place of understanding. **Both end in the same place** —
Proverbs 2:5 says the seeker *"will understand the fear of Yahweh"*, Job 28:28 says *"the fear of the
Lord, that is wisdom"* — **but Proverbs gets there by a search that succeeds, Job by one that fails and
a gift that follows.** See [[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]].

**In the Psalms, the thing above gold is the Law.** Psalm 19:10 and 119:72 rank Yahweh's ordinances and
*"the law of your mouth"* above gold and silver — the same comparison applied to Torah, which is the
identification later Jewish wisdom (Baruch 3; Sirach 24) makes explicit: the wisdom no one could find
is given to Israel as the Law.

**In the New Testament, the hidden treasure is found and bought.** Matthew 13:44 turns Job 28's
terms round: a treasure *hidden* in a field, found, and bought with everything a man has. Colossians
2:3 places *"all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge"* in Christ, and calls them *hidden* there —
the condition Job 28:21 gives wisdom. The New Testament does not say Job 28 was wrong that wisdom
cannot be bought; it says it has been given a location.

**Within Job, gold runs through the third cycle as a counter-image.** Eliphaz told Job to throw the gold
of Ophir into the wadi and have God as his treasure (22:24–25), on the assumption that Job had been
trusting in riches; Job answered that God's testing would bring him out *"like gold"* (23:10), refined
rather than discarded; and chapter 28 then lists the gold of Ophir among the things that cannot buy
wisdom (28:16). The man the friends accused of loving gold is the man 28:28 describes as wise. Job's
denial under oath that he trusted in gold is still to come (31:24–25). The refining image is traced
separately at [[I Shall Come Out Like Gold - Job 23.10 and the Refiner's Fire]].

### The praise now ingested — Proverbs 3:13–20

[[Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth]] extends the valuation beyond price:
wisdom holds riches in one hand, yet is herself more valuable than any desired good.
Her benefits include life and peace, and the praise culminates in Yahweh's creative wisdom.
The comparison is therefore a hierarchy of desires, not a cash return promised to every
wise person. Job's losses remain a counterexample to such a guarantee; see [[Retribution]].
Job 28:23–28 and Proverbs 3:19–20 also join wisdom to God's ordering of water and weather;
see [[By Wisdom Yahweh Founded the Earth - Proverbs 3 and Creation]].

Source for this addition: Proverbs 3:13–20 and Job 28 checked in the stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### Get understanding at any cost — Proverbs 4:5–7

[[Get Wisdom]] turns the valuation of 3:14–15 into an imperative. The WEB's 4:7 makes
understanding worth the cost of all possessions. This is acquisition language, but no
seller or transaction is described. It therefore need not contradict Job 28's refusal
to price wisdom or Proverbs 2:6's statement that Yahweh gives it. The wiki reads the
language as costly priority rather than literal purchase; the passages do not themselves
pause to explain the different metaphors. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 2:4–6; 3:13–15;
4:5–7 and Job 28:12–19, checked 2026-10-25.

### Wisdom names her own price — Proverbs 8:10–11, 18–21

In [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]] Wisdom repeats the father's valuation in her own voice. 8:11b is
3:15b almost word for word (*wəḵol-ḥăp̄āṣîm lōʾ yišwû-ḇāh*), and 8:19 rebuilds 3:14 around *piryî*,
*my fruit*. 8:10 puts the choice as an exclusion — *qəḥû mûsārî wəʾal-kāsep̄*, *take my instruction
and not silver* — and then 8:18–21 promises riches, honour, and full treasuries to those who love
her. **Proverbs therefore ranks wisdom above gold and also says wisdom brings gold**, in one speech;
Job 28 says neither. The wiki's reading, an order of desires, is the one 1 Kings 3:11–13 narrates
of Solomon, who asked for understanding and was given riches and honour besides. Source: stored WEB
Proverbs 3:14–16; 8:10–21; 1 Kings 3:11–13 WEB at bible-api.com; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### The price as an exclamation — Proverbs 16:16

[[The Plans of the Heart]] puts the comparison as a cry rather than a statement: *"How much better it is
to get wisdom than gold! Yes, to get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver"* (16:16). The
verb is *get* (*acquire*), the imperative of 4:5–7 now an exclamation, and the chapter places it beside
two *better than* sayings about money itself (16:8, 19). Source: stored WEB Proverbs 4:5–7; 16:8, 16,
19.

**Proverbs 17:16 says what the price cannot buy.** *Liqnôt ḥokmâ*, *to get wisdom*, is the verb of 4:7's
*"Get wisdom"* (*qənê ḥokmâ*; Hebrew from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*), and 4:7 had
added *"though it costs all your possessions"*. The fool has the money. What he lacks is *lēb*, a heart:
the price is real, but it is not paid in silver. See [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]]. Source: stored WEB
Proverbs 4:7; 17:16.

### Proverbs 20 in context

Proverbs 20:15, now ingested, gives the treasure comparison a speaker: gold and rubies may be
abundant, but knowledgeable lips are rare. Knowledge is valued as something heard from a
person, not only acquired as a possession. Its immediate setting includes a buyer's
self-serving disparagement and boast (14), so the valuable mouth differs from the mouth that
secures a bargain through deception. The contrast does not give knowledge a cash price or
promise its speaker riches. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 20:14–15.

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]].

### Bought and not sold — Proverbs 23:23

*"Buy the truth, and don't sell it. Get wisdom, discipline, and understanding."* In Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26), *buy* and *get* are one imperative, *qənēh*, the verb of
4:5, 7. The earlier verses set a price — *"though it costs all your possessions"* (4:7); this one adds
that what has been bought can be sold again, and forbids it.

See [[Do Not Envy Sinners]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 4:5–7; 23:23.

## Significance

The comparison sets two kinds of value against each other in a culture where gold was the measure of
everything, and in a book whose hero lost all his wealth. Job 28 is the most radical of its forms,
because it does not say that wisdom is a better investment — Eliphaz's way of putting it — but that it
is not on the market. That is also the book's answer to the accuser's question, *"Does Job fear God for
nothing?"* (1:9): the fear of God that is wisdom (28:28) is the one thing in the book that is neither
bought nor paid. See [[Wisdom]], [[Wealth and Possessions]] and [[The Fear of God]].
