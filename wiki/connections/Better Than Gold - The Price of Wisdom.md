# Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom

## Analysis

**The comparison is the wisdom tradition's commonplace, and Job 28 is its fullest form.** Proverbs says
it in a line: wisdom is *better than* silver, gold and rubies (3:14–15; 8:10–11, 19; 16:16). Job 28 says it
in five verses and seven or eight named materials, and says something more than *better*: wisdom
*cannot be exchanged* for them at all (28:15–17). Proverbs' comparison is a ranking on a single scale.
Job's is a statement that there is no scale — *"Man doesn't know its price"* (28:13).

**Proverbs 2:4 and Job 28 use the same picture to opposite effect.** *"If you seek her as silver, and
search for her as for hidden treasures"* — Proverbs takes the miner's search as the model for seeking
wisdom and promises that it succeeds, because *"Yahweh gives wisdom"* (2:6). Job 28 describes that
search in loving detail (28:1–11) and then says it does not reach wisdom: the miner can go where the
falcon has not looked and still not find the place of understanding. **Both end in the same place** —
Proverbs 2:5 says the seeker *"will understand the fear of Yahweh"*, Job 28:28 says *"the fear of the
Lord, that is wisdom"* — **but Proverbs gets there by a search that succeeds, Job by one that fails and
a gift that follows.** See [[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]].

**In the Psalms, the thing above gold is the Law.** Psalm 19:10 and 119:72 rank Yahweh's ordinances and
*"the law of your mouth"* above gold and silver — the same comparison applied to Torah, which is the
identification later Jewish wisdom (Baruch 3; Sirach 24) makes explicit: the wisdom no one could find
is given to Israel as the Law.

**In the New Testament, the hidden treasure is found and bought.** Matthew 13:44 turns Job 28's
terms round: a treasure *hidden* in a field, found, and bought with everything a man has. Colossians
2:3 places *"all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge"* in Christ, and calls them *hidden* there —
the condition Job 28:21 gives wisdom. The New Testament does not say Job 28 was wrong that wisdom
cannot be bought; it says it has been given a location.

**Within Job, gold runs through the third cycle as a counter-image.** Eliphaz told Job to throw the gold
of Ophir into the wadi and have God as his treasure (22:24–25), on the assumption that Job had been
trusting in riches; Job answered that God's testing would bring him out *"like gold"* (23:10), refined
rather than discarded; and chapter 28 then lists the gold of Ophir among the things that cannot buy
wisdom (28:16). The man the friends accused of loving gold is the man 28:28 describes as wise. Job's
denial under oath that he trusted in gold is still to come (31:24–25). The refining image is traced
separately at [[I Shall Come Out Like Gold - Job 23.10 and the Refiner's Fire]].

## Significance

The comparison sets two kinds of value against each other in a culture where gold was the measure of
everything, and in a book whose hero lost all his wealth. Job 28 is the most radical of its forms,
because it does not say that wisdom is a better investment — Eliphaz's way of putting it — but that it
is not on the market. That is also the book's answer to the accuser's question, *"Does Job fear God for
nothing?"* (1:9): the fear of God that is wisdom (28:28) is the one thing in the book that is neither
bought nor paid. See [[Wisdom]], [[Wealth and Possessions]] and [[The Fear of God]].
