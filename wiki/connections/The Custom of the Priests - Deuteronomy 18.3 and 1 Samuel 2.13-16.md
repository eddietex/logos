# The Custom of the Priests - Deuteronomy 18.3 and 1 Samuel 2.13-16

## Passages involved

- [[The Portion of the Priests and Levites]] — Deuteronomy 18:3–4, the priests' due.
- [[Fat, Blood, and the Priests’ Share]] — Leviticus 7:28–36, the breast and right thigh, and the fat
  that goes to the altar.
- **1 Samuel 2:12–17** *(not yet ingested)* — Eli's sons at Shiloh.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 18:3 begins *"This shall be the priests' due from the people, from those who offer a
sacrifice"*. 1 Samuel 2:13 begins *"The custom of the priests with the people was that when anyone
offered a sacrifice …"* (WEB). The English differs — *due*, *custom* — but the Hebrew opening is the
same: *mišpaṭ hakkōhănîm mēʾēt hāʿām mēʾēt zōbəḥê hazzebaḥ* (Deuteronomy 18:3) and *ûmišpaṭ
hakkōhănîm ʾet-hāʿām kol-ʾîš zōbēaḥ zebaḥ* (1 Samuel 2:13), both with *mišpāṭ*, *kōhănîm*, *ʿām*, and
the verb and noun *zābaḥ* / *zebaḥ* (WLC, morphhb). In Genesis–Deuteronomy, *mišpāṭ* followed directly
by *kōhănîm* occurs only at Deuteronomy 18:3 (WLC sequence search).

What follows differs. Deuteronomy names fixed cuts: *"the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the inner
parts"*. In 1 Samuel the priest's servant comes while the meat is boiling, *"with a fork of three
teeth in his hand"*, and *"the priest took all that the fork brought up for himself"* (2:13–14). Then,
*"before they burned the fat"*, he demands raw meat to roast, and threatens: *"if not, I will take it
by force"* (2:15–16). The narrator's verdict: *"the men despised the offering of Yahweh"* (2:17). All
WEB, checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-07.

Two faults are distinguishable in 1 Samuel 2. The first is the portion: whatever the fork brings up,
not a named cut. The second is the order: taking meat before the fat is burned, when Leviticus 7:31
says *"The priest shall burn the fat on the altar"* and only then names the priests' share. 1 Samuel
does not cite Deuteronomy or Leviticus, and the narrative does not say which rule, if any, the sons
broke. Whether *mišpaṭ hakkōhănîm* in 1 Samuel 2:13 describes the legitimate custom that the sons
then abused (verses 15–16 being the abuse), or the sons' own corrupt practice from the start, is read
both ways. The repeated phrase does not settle it.

## Significance

[[Priesthood]] is supported by the sacrifices, and the law names the share so that it is a right and
not a seizure. The same phrase heads both a law and a story of its abuse, and 1 Samuel's verdict falls
on the treatment of the offering, not on priestly support itself. See
[[Sacrifice and Offering]].
