# Murder, Adultery, and the Heart - Exodus 20 and Matthew 5

## Passages involved

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:13–17.
- Matthew 5:21–30 — Jesus cites the prohibitions of murder and adultery; verified WEB, not yet
  ingested.

## Analysis

Matthew 5:21 and 5:27 explicitly repeat the commands against murder and adultery. Jesus' following
teaching reaches anger, insulting speech, and the gaze directed toward lust (5:22, 28). The commands
are not discarded: their claims upon relationships are extended into dispositions and conduct that
precede the prohibited acts.

The murder paragraph moves toward reconciliation even before completing an offering (5:23–24), then
toward settling with an adversary (5:25–26). The adultery paragraph presses the seriousness of
avoiding sin through severe images involving eye and hand (5:29–30). These are parts of the
argument, not a new Exodus narrative or an account of an actual mutilation.

**Do not oppose a merely external Torah to a newly inward ethic.** Exodus 20:17 already forbids
coveting, and 20:6 places love alongside commandment keeping. Matthew's force concerns Jesus'
authoritative application of the commands, not the first biblical discovery that desire matters. The
adultery comparison also belongs alongside [[Sexual Sin]] and its earlier example in
[[Joseph in Potiphar's House]]; that episode already treats sexual betrayal as a wrong against God
as well as a master (Genesis 39:8–9).

## Significance

[[Life and Death]], [[Marriage]], [[Sexual Sin]], and [[Law and Obedience]] meet in conduct that
protects a neighbor. The prohibition of coveting prevents the comparison from reducing the earlier
law to external compliance.
