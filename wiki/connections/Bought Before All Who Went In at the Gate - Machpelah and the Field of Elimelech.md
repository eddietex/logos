# Bought Before All Who Went In at the Gate - Machpelah and the Field of Elimelech

## Passages involved

- [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]] — Genesis 23:10–18; [[Abraham]] buys the field and cave of
  [[Machpelah]] from [[Ephron the Hittite]] *in the presence of the children of Heth, before all who went
  in at the gate of his city* (23:18).
- [[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]] — Ruth 4:1–12; [[Boaz]] buys *all that was Elimelech's* and acquires Ruth
  before ten elders and *all the people who were in the gate* (4:9–11).
- [[The Covenant at Shechem]] — Joshua 24:22; *You are witnesses against yourselves … They said, "We are
  witnesses."*
- **Deuteronomy 25:7–10** — the widow *shall go up to the gate to the elders*; the shoe —
  [[Justice, Family Duty, Honest Weights, and Amalek]]

## Analysis

**Two purchases of a field, both done at a town gate, both told at length, and both bound up with a
death.** Abraham buys Machpelah to bury Sarah; Boaz buys Elimelech's parcel so that *the name of the dead
may not be cut off* (Ruth 4:10). In both the narrative slows to record the public steps of the
transaction, and in both the witnesses are the people at the gate.

| | **Genesis 23** | **Ruth 4:1–12** |
|---|---|---|
| where | *the gate of his city*, among the children of Heth (10, 18) | *the gate* of Bethlehem (1, 10, 11) |
| the witnesses | *all who went in at the gate of his city* (10, 18) | ten elders *and all the people* (2, 9, 11) |
| the seller | Ephron, a Hittite, who first offers the field as a gift (11) | Naomi, through the redeemer's right (3, 5, 9) |
| the buyer | Abraham, *a stranger and a foreigner* among them (23:4) | Boaz, a kinsman, the second in line (4:4) |
| the price | named: *four hundred shekels of silver*, weighed (16) | not named |
| the form | the field, cave, and trees listed and *deeded* (17–18) | the shoe drawn off and given (7–8) and a spoken declaration (9–10) |
| the purpose | a grave for Sarah | a name for the dead, and a wife |

**The vocabulary differs.** Genesis 23 uses *miqnâ*, *possession* (23:18, WLC), a noun usually derived
from *qānâ* (the derivation reported, not checked); Ruth 4 uses the verb *qānâ* itself for the field and the wife (4:4, 5, 8, 9, 10; WLC). Genesis
says *in the presence of* and *before all who went in at the gate*; Ruth has the formal *You are
witnesses today* and the answer *We are witnesses* (Ruth 4:9–11).

**The witness formula.** In the WLC files read (Genesis–Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel, Jeremiah), *ʿēḏîm ʾattem*,
*you are witnesses*, with the answer *ʿēḏîm*, occurs only at Ruth 4:9–11 and Joshua 24:22. At Shechem
the people bind themselves to serve Yahweh; at Bethlehem the town binds itself to a sale and a marriage.
The same exchange serves for a covenant with God and a contract between neighbors.

**The gate as the place of the levirate.** Deuteronomy 25:7 sends the refused widow *up to the gate to
the elders*; Ruth 4:1 sends Boaz *up to the gate* (*ʿālâ … haššaʿar*, both WLC). Ruth's gate scene is the
one in the Bible that shows the elders at work on a family's land and name. See
[[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].

## Significance

The comparison is the wiki's; Ruth does not mention Abraham or Machpelah. What it shows is a narrative
habit: where land changes hands in a way that matters for a family's future, the Bible tells the
transaction in public detail and names the witnesses. Abraham's field is the patriarchs' only owned
ground in Canaan, bought from foreigners for a grave; Boaz's is a lost family holding bought back for a
dead man's name, and with it comes a foreign widow. One purchase gives the family a place to be buried,
the other a line to be continued. See [[Land and Sojourning]] and [[Redemption]].
