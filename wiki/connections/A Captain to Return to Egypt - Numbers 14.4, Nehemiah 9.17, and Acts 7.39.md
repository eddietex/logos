# A Captain to Return to Egypt - Numbers 14.4, Nehemiah 9.17, and Acts 7.39

## Passages involved

- [[The People Refuse the Land]] — Numbers 14:3–4
- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 14:12
- Deuteronomy 17:16; 28:68 — never going back that way *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Nehemiah 9:16–17 — *appointed a captain to return to their bondage* *(WEB and WLC checked, not yet
  ingested)*
- Hosea 11:5 — *They won't return into the land of Egypt* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Acts 7:39 — *turned back in their hearts to Egypt* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The wish has a history.** At the sea it was *it would have been better for us to serve the
Egyptians* (Exodus 14:12), a regret. Numbers 14:3–4 makes it a plan: *wouldn't it be better*, and
then a leader to carry it out.

**Nehemiah's prayer narrates what Numbers only proposes.** *Nittənâ rōʾš wənāšûbâ miṣrāymâ* (Numbers
14:4, WLC) becomes *wayyittənû rōʾš lāšûb ləʿabdutām* (Nehemiah 9:17, WLC), *they appointed a head
to return to their bondage*. The words match, and a proposal has become a deed. Nehemiah also names
the destination for what it was. Numbers says *Egypt*, and the prayer says *their bondage*.

**A variant.** WLC has *bəmiryām*, *in their rebellion*, and WEB follows it. Some witnesses are
reported to read *in Egypt* here, with *bəmiṣrayim*, which would bring it closer to Numbers. That is
recalled, and no apparatus was read.

**The next clause.** The verse goes straight on to *You are a God ready to pardon, gracious and
merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness, and didn't forsake them*. So the prayer
has Numbers 14:4 and Exodus 34:6 in one verse, and it restores the *gracious and merciful* that
Moses left out of Numbers 14:18. Nehemiah 9 passes over the sentence of forty years here, though
9:21 goes on to the forty years of sustenance (by reference).

**Law and threat.** Deuteronomy 17:16 forbids a king to *cause the people to return to Egypt*, and
adds *You shall not go back that way again*, and 28:68 threatens that Yahweh himself will send them
back. Hosea 11:5 in WEB denies it, with *the Assyrian* as the alternative, though the sense of the line in context is reported to be disputed (recalled; the Hebrew was not checked).

**Stephen moves it to the heart.** Acts 7:39 has the fathers reject Moses and *turned back in their
hearts to Egypt*, and he sets that at the calf. The wiki notes that the wish fits Numbers 14 as
well, but Stephen does not cite it there.

**Sources:** Hebrew from WLC (morphhb); WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-02, and Exodus and
Numbers from `raw/bible/`. Nehemiah 9:21 cited by reference only.

## Significance

- The exodus could be reversed by appointment. The wiki's wilderness texts never treat going back as
  a neutral option.
