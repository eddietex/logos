# Help for an Enemy - Exodus 23, Proverbs 25, and Matthew 5

## Passages involved

- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:4–5.
- [[The Glory of Kings]] — Proverbs 25:21–22, food and water for an enemy.
- Matthew 5:43–48 — love and prayer for enemies; verified WEB, not yet ingested.
- Romans 12:20–21 — enemy-feeding instruction and overcoming evil with good; verified WEB, not yet
  ingested.
- [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]] — Proverbs 24:17–18.

## Analysis

Exodus requires the return of an enemy's wandering animal and assistance with the donkey of someone
who hates the hearer. Need, rather than friendship, determines the duty. The law supplies no
condition that the owner first become friendly. It also offers no account of how the owner responds.

Proverbs 25:21–22 moves from the enemy's animal to the enemy's hunger and thirst, connecting the
action with coals on the enemy's head and Yahweh's reward. Romans 12:20–21 repeats the feeding
instruction and places it under overcoming evil with good. The coals image is not explained in these
verses; this comparison does not make a disputed interpretation of that image its foundation.

Matthew 5:43–48 makes enemy-love and prayer explicit, grounding generosity beyond one's own circle
in the Father's sun and rain for both good and evil. **The instruction to hate an enemy reported in
Matthew 5:43 is not the command of Exodus 23:4–5.** The earlier law already requires help. Matthew's
broader demand should not be described as correcting a Torah rule that all enemies must be hated.

These are related ethical instructions, not evidence that Matthew is quoting Exodus 23. Romans
directly uses the enemy-feeding instruction of Proverbs; Exodus supplies an earlier legal example of
doing good across hostility.

### No gladness at the enemy's fall — Proverbs 24:17–18

Before Proverbs 25:21–22 commands food for an enemy, 24:17–18 forbids the heart's gladness at his
fall: *"Don't rejoice when your enemy falls. Don't let your heart be glad when he is overthrown; lest
Yahweh see it, and it displease him, and he turn away his wrath from him."* Its reason is that the
enemy's fall is Yahweh's affair, not that the enemy has become a friend. Job swears the same
restraint (*"If I have rejoiced at the destruction of him who hated me"*, 31:29), and Proverbs 17:5
has *"He who is glad at calamity shall not be unpunished."* In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the
Masorah* (read 2026-10-26) Job 31:29's *destruction* is *pîḏ*, the word Proverbs 24:22 uses for the
ruin that comes on rebels. 24:29, in the next block, then forbids *"I will do to him as he has done
to me."*

### Bread and water, and Yahweh repays — Proverbs 25:21–22

Now ingested: *"If your enemy is hungry, give him food to eat. If he is thirsty, give him water to
drink: for you will heap coals of fire on his head, and Yahweh will reward you."* In Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26) the enemy is *śōnaʾăḵā*, *the one who hates you* — the
same participle Exodus 23:5 uses of the owner of the fallen donkey, in the WEB *"the donkey of him who
hates you"*. *Reward* is *yəšallem*, *repay*, the verb forbidden to the son at 20:22 (*ʾăšalləmāh-rāʿ*,
*"I will pay back evil"*): what he may not do to his enemy, Yahweh does for him. Romans 12:20 quotes
the first three lines after *"Vengeance belongs to me; I will repay, says the Lord"* (12:19) and
leaves out Proverbs' last clause, *"and Yahweh will reward you"*, ending instead on *"overcome evil
with good"* (12:21). The coals remain unexplained; the passage page lists the usual readings without
adopting one. See [[The Glory of Kings]] and
[[Wait for Yahweh - Proverbs 20.22 and the Refusal of Revenge]].

## Significance

[[Compassion and Mercy]], [[Love]], and [[Strife and Peacemaking]] concern required actions toward
an adversary as well as restored feelings between friends. Hostility remains named while assistance
becomes obligatory. This is an ethical continuity with a broadened formulation, not an asserted
prophecy or fulfillment.
