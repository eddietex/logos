# Help for an Enemy - Exodus 23, Proverbs 25, and Matthew 5

## Passages involved

- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:4–5.
- Proverbs 25:21–22 — food and water for an enemy; verified WEB, not yet ingested.
- Matthew 5:43–48 — love and prayer for enemies; verified WEB, not yet ingested.
- Romans 12:20–21 — enemy-feeding instruction and overcoming evil with good; verified WEB, not yet
  ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus requires the return of an enemy's wandering animal and assistance with the donkey of someone
who hates the hearer. Need, rather than friendship, determines the duty. The law supplies no
condition that the owner first become friendly. It also offers no account of how the owner responds.

Proverbs 25:21–22 moves from the enemy's animal to the enemy's hunger and thirst, connecting the
action with coals on the enemy's head and Yahweh's reward. Romans 12:20–21 repeats the feeding
instruction and places it under overcoming evil with good. The coals image is not explained in these
verses; this comparison does not make a disputed interpretation of that image its foundation.

Matthew 5:43–48 makes enemy-love and prayer explicit, grounding generosity beyond one's own circle
in the Father's sun and rain for both good and evil. **The instruction to hate an enemy reported in
Matthew 5:43 is not the command of Exodus 23:4–5.** The earlier law already requires help. Matthew's
broader demand should not be described as correcting a Torah rule that all enemies must be hated.

These are related ethical instructions, not evidence that Matthew is quoting Exodus 23. Romans
directly uses the enemy-feeding instruction of Proverbs; Exodus supplies an earlier legal example of
doing good across hostility.

**Sources:** Exodus checked in the existing raw WEB. The three later ranges were retrieved from

English wording.

## Significance

[[Compassion and Mercy]], [[Love]], and [[Strife and Peacemaking]] concern required actions toward
an adversary as well as restored feelings between friends. Hostility remains named while assistance
becomes obligatory. This is an ethical continuity with a broadened formulation, not an asserted
prophecy or fulfillment.
