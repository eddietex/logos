# Deceitful Food - The Savory Food of Genesis 27 and the Ruler's Delicacies

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 27:4, 7, 9, 14, 17, 31** — [[The Stolen Blessing]]: Isaac's *"savory food, such as I
  love"* (27:4), made by Rebekah from two young goats (27:9, 14) and handed to Jacob with bread
  (27:17); Esau's made too late (27:31).
- **Proverbs 23:1–3** — [[Thirty Excellent Things]]: *"Don't be desirous of his dainties, since they
  are deceitful food."*
- **Proverbs 23:6–8** — [[Thirty Excellent Things]]: *"Don't eat the food of him who has a stingy eye,
  and don't crave his delicacies"*.
- **Proverbs 20:17** — [[Wine Is a Mocker]]: *"Fraudulent food is sweet to a man, but afterwards his
  mouth is filled with gravel."*

## Analysis

**One noun, two settings.** The WEB's *savory food* in Genesis 27 and its *dainties* (Proverbs
23:3) and *delicacies* (23:6) render the same Hebrew noun, *maṭʿammîm* / *maṭʿammōt*, from the root
*to taste*. In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26) it stands six times in
Genesis 27 (verses 4, 7, 9, 14, 17, 31) and at Proverbs 23:3 and 23:6. [[The Stolen Blessing]] reports
that these two Proverbs verses are its only other occurrences in the Hebrew Bible; that count was not
re-checked against a concordance in this pass.

**In Genesis the food is the instrument of a deception.** Isaac asks for it as the occasion of the
blessing — *"Make me savory food, such as I love, and bring it to me, that I may eat, and that my soul
may bless you before I die"* (27:4). Rebekah turns his request into the means of misleading him: she
makes the dish from goats (27:9), dresses Jacob in Esau's clothes, and puts the food in Jacob's hand
(27:17). Isaac eats and blesses. When Esau arrives with his own dish (27:31), Isaac tells him, *"Your
brother came with deceit, and has taken away your blessing"* (27:35). The meal was real; its purpose
was false.

**In Proverbs the food is called deceitful outright.** *"Don't be desirous of his dainties, since they
are deceitful food"* (23:3) — literally, in the same Hebrew edition, *bread of lies* (*leḥem
kəzābîm*). The proverb does not say what lie the ruler's table tells. The second table answers for its
own case: the stingy host says *"Eat and drink!"* *"but his heart is not with you"* (23:7). There the
deceit is the gap between invitation and intent — the same gap Isaac sat across.

**The two read each other in opposite directions.** In Genesis the one who eats is deceived by the
ones who cook; in Proverbs the one who eats is warned that the host may be using the meal. Isaac's
appetite is the hinge of the Genesis story — the one motive the book gives for his love of Esau is
the venison (25:28) — and Proverbs 23:2 tells the guest with an appetite to *"put a knife to your
throat"*. The verbal link is exact; that the proverb alludes to Genesis 27 is not claimed. The pairing
shows the same noun gathering the same association: a delicacy offered with something other than
generosity behind it.

**Proverbs 20:17 states the general case.** *"Fraudulent food is sweet to a man, but afterwards his
mouth is filled with gravel"* — food obtained by fraud. In Proverbs 23:8 the guest vomits up the morsel
and *"lose[s] your good words"*; in Genesis 27 Jacob's dish wins the blessing and costs him twenty years
in exile from Esau. See [[Sweet Food and Gravel - Proverbs 20.17 and Job 20]].

Sources: Genesis 25:28; 27:4, 7, 9, 14, 17, 31, 35; Proverbs 20:17; 23:1–8 copied from stored WEB.
Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-26.

## Significance

The connection ties [[Hospitality]] to [[Deception]]: a meal is one of the most trusted things one
person can offer another, which is what makes it useful to a deceiver and why Proverbs tells its pupil
to watch the host as well as the plate. It also gives [[Thirty Excellent Things]] a narrative
illustration from the patriarchs, and gives [[The Stolen Blessing]] a wisdom saying that names what its
dish was.
