# Honor Your Father and Mother - Exodus 20 and Ephesians 6

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:12, honoring both parents and long life in the given land.
- Deuteronomy 5:16 — repeats the command with well-being added to long life.
- Ephesians 6:1–3 — applies the command to children; both later texts verified WEB, not yet
  ingested.
- [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] — Proverbs 1:8; *"listen to your father's instruction, and don't forsake your mother's
  teaching"*, repeated at 6:20.
- [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]] — Proverbs 3:2, 9, 12; length of days, *"Honor Yahweh"*, and
  Yahweh *"as a father"*.

## Analysis

Ephesians 6:1 tells children to obey their parents in the Lord, then cites the command to honor
father and mother and the associated promise (6:2–3). Exodus 20:12 speaks of long days in the land
Yahweh gives; Deuteronomy 5:16 includes that it may go well with the hearer. Ephesians 6:3 includes
well-being and speaks of long life on the earth in the WEB. Its wording should therefore be compared
with both Torah versions rather than represented as a verbatim copy of Exodus alone.

The letter calls this the first commandment with a promise (6:2). Exodus 20:6 has already spoken of
loving kindness to those who love God and keep his commandments. A plausible distinction is the
first particular command with an explicit promise directly attached, but Ephesians does not pause to
explain its numbering. The tension should be noted rather than resolved by erasing Exodus 20:6.

The application changes the immediate audience from the assembled people to children in a household
instruction. Honor and the particular obedience called for in Ephesians belong together, but Exodus
itself does not restrict honoring parents to childhood. Nor do these short units explain every case
in which an obedient child dies young.

Proverbs 1:8 is the command's wisdom form. It does not cite Exodus, and it promises ornament
(*"a garland to grace your head"*, 1:9) rather than long days in the land; but it names both parents
as the commandment does, and gives the mother's word the name *tôrâ*, *teaching*. Honor there becomes
*listening* here, at the age when a son is most open to other voices (1:10).

Proverbs 3 carries the commandment's vocabulary further. The father promises *"length of days"* (3:2,
*ʾōreḵ yāmîm*) to the son who keeps his teaching — the reward of Exodus 20:12, *yaʾărīḵûn yāmeḵā* — and
then uses the commandment's own imperative with Yahweh as its object: *"Honor Yahweh with your
substance"* (3:9, *kabbēḏ ʾeṯ-YHWH*, as *kabbēḏ ʾeṯ-ʾāḇîḵā*). Three verses later Yahweh reproves *"even as
a father"* (3:12). The chapter does not cite the commandment; the shared forms are the wiki's evidence.

### The father who first had to listen — Proverbs 4:1–9

The command's household setting gains another dimension in [[Get Wisdom]]. The parent
asks for attention as someone who received teaching in childhood (4:3–4). The mother is
remembered in 4:3, while the father is the quoted teacher. Unlike the land-specific long
life of Exodus 20:12, this instruction promises life, preservation, and honor without
naming a land. It is a thematic development of honoring parents, not an explicit citation
of the commandment. See [[Wisdom Handed On - Proverbs 4 and Exodus 13]]. Source: stored
WEB Proverbs 4:1–9 and Exodus 20:12, checked 2026-10-25.

### Proverbs 20 in context

Proverbs 20:20 states the negative counterpart to honoring father and mother: the person who
curses either parent loses his lamp in darkness. Exodus 21:17 gives a death penalty for that
offense, whereas Exodus 20:12 attaches long life to honor. The wisdom saying names both parents
but supplies no legal procedure, age of the offender, or explicit prediction of childlessness.

## Significance

The parents' place in [[Law and Obedience]] remains attached to a promise of life, with both father
and mother named. The comparison links [[Marriage]], [[Life and Death]], and
[[Land and Sojourning]] without treating every life span as a measurable verdict on filial conduct.
