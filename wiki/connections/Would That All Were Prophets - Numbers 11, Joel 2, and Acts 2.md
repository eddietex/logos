# Would That All Were Prophets - Numbers 11, Joel 2, and Acts 2

## Passages involved

- [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] — Numbers 11:29; *"I wish that all Yahweh's people were prophets, that Yahweh would put his
  Spirit on them!"*
- [[Samuel Anoints Saul]] — 1 Samuel 10:5–6, 10; a band of prophets, and *Yahweh's Spirit will come mightily on
  you, and you will prophesy with them* (stored WEB)
- **Joel 2:28–29** — *"I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh; and your sons and your daughters will prophesy."* (WEB; Joel 3:1–2 in
  Hebrew) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Acts 2:17–18** — Peter quoting Joel at Pentecost (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Neither later text quotes Numbers.** Peter cites Joel, not Moses, and Joel does not name Moses. What the three share is an idea: the
Spirit is on everyone in the group, and it shows as prophecy. *Yittēn yhwh ʾet-rûḥô ʿălêhem* (Numbers 11:29, WLC) is put as a wish.
Joel has it as God's promise, with a different verb, as
[[I Will Pour Out My Spirit - Proverbs 1.23, Joel 2.28, and Acts 2.17]] sets out.

**The widening.** Numbers begins with one man. It goes to seventy at the Tent, then two more in the camp, and then a wish for *all
Yahweh's people*. Joel adds sons, daughters, old, young, and servants. Acts places it at Pentecost. Rahlfs's Numbers and Acts both
speak of *πνεῦμα* given or poured *ἐπ᾿* people, a common enough wording that the wiki does not press it.

**What is not the same.** At Numbers 11 the Spirit on the elders serves a burden, and the sign stops at once. Joel's prophecy has dreams
and visions. Acts has speech others can hear. The wiki treats this as a thread of one hope and does not call Numbers 11 a prophecy of
Pentecost, since the verse is a wish.

**The next chapter.** Numbers 12:2 has Miriam and Aaron claim that Yahweh speaks through them too, directly after Moses' wish. Numbers 12:6 names *a vision* and *a dream* as the prophet's ordinary means, and those are the two things Joel 2:28 spreads across old and young (WEB). So Joel's outpouring is of the ordinary kind in Numbers 12's terms. Nothing there says it reaches the *mouth to mouth* of Moses. See [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]].

## Significance

Moses is the person with the most to lose from a shared Spirit, and he is the one who wishes for it. That is the point Joel and Acts
could not have made. See [[The Spirit of God]], [[Prophecy and Vision]], and
[[Do Not Forbid Them - Joshua in Numbers 11 and John in Mark 9]].

### 1 Samuel 10 — the next group who prophesy

After Numbers 11, the next people to prophesy as a group are the band Saul meets at the hill of God
([[Samuel Anoints Saul]]). In the WLC files searched (Genesis through 2 Samuel, Ruth), the verb *nāḇāʾ* (lemma 5012)
occurs only at Numbers 11:25–27 and in 1 Samuel (10:5–13; 18:10; 19:20–24). In both places the Spirit comes and
people prophesy, the prophesying can be seen, and no words are reported. In Numbers the elders *did so no more*
(11:25). In 1 Samuel Saul *finished prophesying* and went to the high place (10:13). Both texts treat it as an event
with an end. The differences matter too. Moses' wish is that the Spirit would be on *all Yahweh's people*. Saul is
one man, and onlookers find it strange that he is *among the prophets* (10:11). See
[[Band of Prophets (of Samuel's time)]] and
[[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]]. Sources: stored WEB Numbers 11:25–29; 1 Samuel
10:5–13. Hebrew WLC (`Gen` through `2Sam`, `Ruth`), 2026-11-17.

### 1 Samuel 19:18–24 — the third group, and the phrase of the quail

At Naioth a whole *company of the prophets* prophesies with Samuel at its head, and every group Saul sends to them
*also prophesied* (19:20–21). As in Numbers 11, the prophesying spreads beyond those first given it, and no words are
reported. Unlike Eldad and Medad, the messengers are not among those chosen; the Spirit comes on them because they
came. One phrase joins the two chapters: Saul lay naked *all that day and all that night*, *kol-hayyôm hahûʾ
wǝḵol-hallaylâ* (19:24), and the people gathered quail *all that day, and all of that night* (Numbers 11:32). In the
Hebrew files checked the phrase occurs only in those two verses and Exodus 10:13; see
[[All That Day and All That Night - Saul Fallen Before Samuel]]. The verb *nāḇāʾ* is *hitpael* in Numbers 11:25–27 and
for the messengers and Saul; the company at Naioth is *niphal* (19:20, WLC). See
[[Saul Among the Prophets at Naioth]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 11:25–32; Exodus 10:13; 1 Samuel 19:18–24. Hebrew
WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.
