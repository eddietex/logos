# Would That All Were Prophets - Numbers 11, Joel 2, and Acts 2

## Passages involved

- [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] — Numbers 11:29; *"I wish that all Yahweh's people were prophets, that Yahweh would put his
  Spirit on them!"*
- **Joel 2:28–29** — *"I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh; and your sons and your daughters will prophesy."* (WEB; Joel 3:1–2 in
  Hebrew) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Acts 2:17–18** — Peter quoting Joel at Pentecost (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Neither later text quotes Numbers.** Peter cites Joel, not Moses, and Joel does not name Moses. What the three share is an idea: the
Spirit is on everyone in the group, and it shows as prophecy. *Yittēn yhwh ʾet-rûḥô ʿălêhem* (Numbers 11:29, WLC) is put as a wish.
Joel has it as God's promise, with a different verb, as
[[I Will Pour Out My Spirit - Proverbs 1.23, Joel 2.28, and Acts 2.17]] sets out.

**The widening.** Numbers begins with one man. It goes to seventy at the Tent, then two more in the camp, and then a wish for *all
Yahweh's people*. Joel adds sons, daughters, old, young, and servants. Acts places it at Pentecost. Rahlfs's Numbers and Acts both
speak of *πνεῦμα* given or poured *ἐπ᾿* people, a common enough wording that the wiki does not press it.

**What is not the same.** At Numbers 11 the Spirit on the elders serves a burden, and the sign stops at once. Joel's prophecy has dreams
and visions. Acts has speech others can hear. The wiki treats this as a thread of one hope and does not call Numbers 11 a prophecy of
Pentecost, since the verse is a wish.

**The next chapter.** Numbers 12:2 has Miriam and Aaron claim that Yahweh speaks through them too, directly after Moses' wish. Numbers 12:6 names *a vision* and *a dream* as the prophet's ordinary means, and those are the two things Joel 2:28 spreads across old and young (WEB). So Joel's outpouring is of the ordinary kind in Numbers 12's terms. Nothing there says it reaches the *mouth to mouth* of Moses. See [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]].

## Significance

Moses is the person with the most to lose from a shared Spirit, and he is the one who wishes for it. That is the point Joel and Acts
could not have made. See [[The Spirit of God]], [[Prophecy and Vision]], and
[[Do Not Forbid Them - Joshua in Numbers 11 and John in Mark 9]].
