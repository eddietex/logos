# Horns, a Shout, and a Fallen Enemy - Jericho and the Camp of Midian

## Passages involved
- [[The Fall of Jericho]] — Joshua 6:1–27. Priests blow *trumpets of rams' horns*; *all the people shall
  shout with a great shout; and the city wall shall fall down flat* (5); *Shout, for Yahweh has given
  you the city!* (16); *the people shouted with a great shout, and the wall fell down flat, so that the
  people went up into the city, every man straight in front of him* (20) (stored WEB).
- [[The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon]] — Judges 7:16–25. Three hundred men blow the trumpets, break
  the pitchers, and shout *The sword of Yahweh and of Gideon!* (20); *They each stood in his place
  around the camp; and all the army ran* (21); *Yahweh set every man's sword against his fellow* (22)
  (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**The same weapons, and none of the usual ones.** In both battles the instrument is the *šôpār*, the
ram's horn (Joshua 6:4–5; Judges 7:16, 18–20, 22, WLC). In both there is a shout, and in both the enemy's
defence fails without an Israelite blow: the wall *fell down flat*, and the camp *ran*. The battle
shout of Jericho is the verb *rûaʿ* (Joshua 6:5, 10, 16, 20); in Genesis–Judges it occurs otherwise only
in Numbers 10:7, 9 (the trumpet alarm), Judges 7:21, and Judges 15:14 (WLC). At 15:14 the shout is the
Philistines', as they meet the bound Samson at Lehi, and they are the ones who fall; see
[[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]]. See
[[Trumpet and Ram's Horn - Two Instruments, One English Word]].

| | Jericho (Joshua 6) | Midian's camp (Judges 7:16–22) |
|---|---|---|
| Who blows | seven priests (4) | three hundred men in three companies (16, 20) |
| The ark | carried in the procession, the priests with horns before it (4, 6–9) | no ark and no priest |
| The shout | no words given; Joshua's order is *Shout, for Yahweh has given you the city!* (16) | *The sword of Yahweh and of Gideon!* (20) |
| When | by day, the seventh day (15) | by night, *the beginning of the middle watch* (19) |
| What Israel does next | *went up into the city, every man straight in front of him* (20) | *They each stood in his place* (21) |
| What falls | the wall | the camp, by its own swords (22) |

**Where they differ.** At Jericho the people's shout has no recorded words, and Joshua's order to shout
names only Yahweh as the giver. In the valley Gideon writes the words himself, and they add his name. At Jericho Israel goes in
afterwards and the killing is Israel's, *with the edge of the sword* (Joshua 6:21). In the valley Israel
does not go in, and the only swords used in the camp are Midian's. Who shouts in Judges 7:21 is not
certain: the subject of *wayyārîʿû* can be Midian's army, crying out as it runs (see the Notes on
[[The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon]]). If so, the Jericho verb has moved from Israel to the enemy.

**What Judges does not say.** Judges 7 never names Jericho, and it does not use Jericho's distinctive
words: no *yôbēl*, no seven days, no ark. The parallel is in the action — horns, a shout, a defence
that collapses, and a victory given rather than fought for. It is the second time in the book the
pattern of Joshua's first battle in the land is repeated in miniature. The first was the spying of
Bethel (Judges 1:22–26); see [[The Spies at Jericho and at Bethel - Joshua 2 and Judges 1]]. Gideon's
night reconnaissance before this battle also follows Joshua 2; see
[[Spies Who Hear the Enemy's Fear - Joshua 2 and Judges 7]].

## Significance

Both battles are told so that no one can say *My own hand has saved me* (Judges 7:2). Jericho gives
the pattern its purest form: the priests, the ark, and the name of Yahweh alone. Judges 7 keeps the
horns and the shout but puts a man's name into the cry, and the next chapter will show what happens to
that name (8:22–27, not yet ingested). See [[War]] and [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].
