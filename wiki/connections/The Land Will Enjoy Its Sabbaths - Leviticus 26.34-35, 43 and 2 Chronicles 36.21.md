# The Land Will Enjoy Its Sabbaths - Leviticus 26.34-35, 43 and 2 Chronicles 36.21

## Passages involved

- [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]] — Leviticus 25:1–7; the seventh year as the land's Sabbath.
- [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] — Leviticus 26:34–35, 43; *"Then the land will enjoy its Sabbaths as long as it lies desolate"*.
- **2 Chronicles 36:20–21** — *"to fulfill Yahweh's word by Jeremiah's mouth, until the land had
  enjoyed its Sabbaths. As long as it lay desolate, it kept Sabbath, to fulfill seventy years."* (WEB)
- **Jeremiah 25:11–12; 29:10** — seventy years of serving Babylon, then a return (WEB verified;
  neither verse mentions the land's Sabbaths).
- **Daniel 9:2** — Daniel reads the seventy years in the books (WEB verified).

## Analysis

**Chronicles closes on the wording of Leviticus and credits Jeremiah.** The Hebrew of 2 Chronicles
36:21 has *rāṣətâ hāʾāreṣ ʾet-šabbətôtêhā*, *the land enjoyed its Sabbaths*, and *kol-yəmê hāšammâ
šābātâ*, *all the days of desolation it rested* (WLC). Leviticus 26:34–35 has *tirṣeh hāʾāreṣ
ʾet-šabbətōtêhā* and *kol-yəmê hāšammâ tišbōt*. Same subject, verbs, object, and noun of desolation,
with the verbs now in the past.

**The attribution needs care.** Chronicles names *Jeremiah's mouth*, and that fits the seventy years.
The clause about the land's Sabbaths, though, is Leviticus's. The Jeremiah verses checked here say
nothing of it. So the verse joins two texts and names one source, or it attaches the Sabbath clause
as its own comment. The wording does not settle which. **Flagged, not resolved.**

**The number is not computed in the text.** A common later inference is that seventy years repays
seventy missed sabbatical years, or 490 years of neglect. Neither Leviticus nor Chronicles states
that. Leviticus gives no count; Chronicles says only *to fulfill seventy years*.

**From a warning to a report.** Leviticus 26:34–35 is conditional: *if* the stages run their course.
Chronicles narrates that they did. The same clause moves from the imperfect of warning to the perfect
of history.

**The land as a party.** Leviticus 25:2 calls it a *Sabbath to Yahweh*, the land's own rest. Leviticus
26:42 then has God remembering the land itself, after the three patriarchs. Across the three texts,
land law is kept even if Israel will not keep it.

**Isaiah's pairing.** *Rāṣâ* in Leviticus 26 is used both of the land's Sabbaths and of the people's
iniquity (34, 41, 43). Isaiah 40:2 uses the same lemma of Jerusalem, *her iniquity is pardoned* (WEB;
*nirṣâ ʿăwōnāh*, WLC). No dependence on Leviticus is claimed.

## Significance

- It is the clearest case so far of a Torah warning quoted as fulfilled history.
- It shows [[Sabbath]] law is not only a rule for Israel. It is presented as something the land is
  owed. See [[Land and Sojourning]].
- It makes exile an outcome with a purpose beyond punishment, though only for the land. See
  [[Judgment]] and [[Covenant]].
