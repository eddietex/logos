# Forest to Clear and Chariots to Face

## Passages involved

- [[Deborah and Barak]] — Judges 4:3, 13–16; nine hundred chariots of iron confused, and Sisera on foot.
- [[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]] — Judges 1:19; Judah does not take the valley, *because they had chariots of iron*.
- [[The Children of Joseph Ask for More]] — Joshua 17:14–18.
- [[Caleb Claims Hebron]] — Joshua 14:6–15.
- [[Laws for Going to War]] — Deuteronomy 20:1–4.

## Analysis

### Nine hundred chariots — Judges 4

[[Deborah and Barak]] gives the chariots a number and an owner: Jabin *had nine hundred chariots of
iron; and he mightily oppressed the children of Israel for twenty years* (4:3). The obstacle of 1:19
and Joshua 17:16 has become the reason Israel cries. The answer is the one Joshua gave Joseph's tribes
(*though they have chariots of iron*, Joshua 17:18), but carried out by Yahweh: *Yahweh confused
Sisera, all his chariots, and all his army* (4:15). Sisera *abandoned his chariot and fled away on his
feet*. The book does not say why chariots that stopped Judah could not stop Barak; it says only who
acted. See [[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 4:1–16; Joshua 17:16–18.

### The chariots again — Judges 1:19

[[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]] brings the obstacle back: *Yahweh was with Judah; and drove out the inhabitants of the hill
country; for he could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron*.
Joshua's assurance, *you shall drive out the Canaanites, though they have chariots of iron* (Joshua
17:18), was given to Joseph's tribes, not Judah, and the WEB gives the obstacle in the same words. In
Judges, Judah takes the hill country and not the valley. The Hebrew of 1:19 has no verb *could*
(*kî lōʾ ləhôrîš*, WLC), so the verse does not say whether the failure was one of ability. Judges 4:3
gives Jabin *nine hundred chariots of iron*, and that is the army Yahweh routs.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:16–18; Judges 1:19; 4:3; WLC, 2026-11-13.

Caleb asks for hill country despite its fortified cities and Anakim, grounding his hope in
Yahweh’s presence (Joshua 14:12). Joseph’s descendants ask for more because they are numerous,
but name iron chariots as an obstacle (17:14–16). Joshua turns their claimed strength into a
charge to clear forest and drive out the inhabitants. The comparison concerns the different
petitions, not an explicit condemnation of Joseph’s tribes: the text supplies no such verdict.

Deuteronomy 20:1–4 addresses fear when enemies have horses, chariots, and greater numbers.
Joshua’s reassurance fits that setting, though here Joseph’s own numbers become an argument for
action. This is a thematic parallel, not an explicit quotation of the law. Forest clearance
also makes the answer more than encouragement for battle: the people must work the land.

Joshua 17:18 remains an assurance, whereas 17:12–13 reports non-removal. The conversation does
not establish fulfillment or explain that tension away.

Sources: stored WEB Joshua 14:6–15; 17:12–18; Deuteronomy 20:1–4. All comparisons are paraphrases.

## Significance

Land as gift does not eliminate labor or opposition. The comparison joins [[Land and Sojourning]],
[[Work]], [[War]], and [[Faith]] without turning the tribes’ request into an unstated moral verdict.
