# The Song of Moses and the Lamb - Exodus 15 in Revelation 15

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 15:1, 11** — *"Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to Yahweh"*; *"Who is like
  you, Yahweh, among the gods? Who is like you, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?"* —
  [[The Song of the Sea]]
- **Exodus 14:31** — *"they believed in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses"* — [[The Crossing of the Sea]]
- **Revelation 15:2–4** — *"I saw something like a sea of glass mixed with fire, and those who overcame
  the beast … standing on the sea of glass, having harps of God. They sang the song of Moses, the servant
  of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, 'Great and marvelous are your works, Lord God, the Almighty!
  Righteous and true are your ways, you King of the nations. Who wouldn't fear you, Lord, and glorify your
  name? For you only are holy. For all the nations will come and worship before you. For your righteous acts
  have been revealed.'"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**Revelation names the Song but does not quote it.** The words sung in 15:3–4 are not the words of Exodus
15. What it shares is a setting and a set of themes (Greek checked against the SBLGNT; Hebrew against the
WLC):

- **A sea, and singers standing beside or on it** — *"standing on the sea of glass"* (*hestōtas epi tēn
  thalassan*, 15:2), **after a victory over a pursuing power** (*"those who overcame the beast"*).
- **Moses as *"the servant of God"*** — *tou doulou tou theou* (15:3), **the title the prose gave him at the
  sea**: *"his servant Moses"* (*ʿabdô*, Exodus 14:31). **Exodus 14:31 is its first use for Moses in
  Exodus** (as recorded on [[The Crossing of the Sea]]).
- ***"Marvelous are your works"*** — *thaumasta* — beside the Song's *"doing wonders"* (*ʿōśēh peleʾ*, 15:11).
- ***"Who wouldn't fear you, Lord … For you only are holy"*** — a *who* question, fear, and holiness: the
  three elements of *"Who is like you … glorious in holiness, fearful in praises"* (15:11). **The Song's
  question was about the gods; Revelation's is about the nations.**
- ***"King"*** — *ho basileus* — beside the Song's close, *"Yahweh shall reign forever and ever"* (15:18).
  **Text note**: the WEB reads *"King of the nations"*; the SBLGNT prints *tōn aiōnōn*, *"of the ages"*, and
  marks the word as a variation unit. **The manuscripts differ, and the wiki records both readings.**

**The two songs are sung as one** — *"the song of Moses … and the song of the Lamb"*, the article repeated
before each (*tēn ōdēn Mōuseōs … kai tēn ōdēn tou arniou*, SBLGNT). **Whether these are two songs or one
song with two names, the Greek allows either** (reported, not decided).

## Significance

**The first song in Scripture is invoked near the end of Scripture, and in a chapter framed by plagues.**
Revelation 15 opens with *"seven angels having the seven last plagues"* (15:1, WEB; *plēgas*, SBLGNT). The exodus pattern —
a pursuing power, a sea, a people standing on the far side, a song to the God who did it — is read in
Revelation as the shape of final deliverance. **The harps of 15:2 (*kitharas*) and the Song's tambourine
belong to the same pairing Laban first named**: *"with tambourine and with harp"* (Genesis 31:27); **whether
Revelation has that pairing in view is not suggested by the text** and is recorded here only as a shared
image. See [[Praise and Song]] and
[[Baptized into Moses - The Sea in 1 Corinthians 10 and Hebrews 11]].
