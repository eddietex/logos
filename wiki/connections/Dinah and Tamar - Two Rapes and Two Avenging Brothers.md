# Dinah and Tamar - Two Rapes and Two Avenging Brothers

## Passages involved

- Genesis 34:1–31 — Dinah, Shechem son of Hamor, and the massacre by Simeon and Levi
- 2 Samuel 13:1–39 — Tamar, Amnon, and the killing of Amnon by Absalom
- Deuteronomy 22:28–29; Exodus 22:16–17 — the law's remedy, offered in one case and refused in the
  other
- Genesis 49:5–7 — Jacob's verdict on his two sons
- 2 Samuel 12:10–11 — Nathan's sentence on David's house, which 2 Samuel 13 is the first instalment
  of

## Analysis

**A note on the name before anything else.** The Tamar of this page is **David's daughter**, 2 Samuel
13 — **not [[Tamar]], the daughter-in-law of [[Judah]] in Genesis 38**, who has her own page in this
wiki and no connection to this story beyond the name and a sheep-shearing. **The two are different
women four hundred years apart**, and the wiki keeps them apart deliberately: when Samuel is ingested,
the Genesis page is renamed `Tamar (daughter-in-law of Judah)` and David's daughter becomes
`Tamar (daughter of David)`, with the inbound links fixed in the same pass, per `AGENTS.md`.

**These are the Hebrew Bible's two full-length rape narratives, and they are built from the same
parts in a different order.**

| | **Genesis 34** | **2 Samuel 13** |
|---|---|---|
| the woman | **Dinah**, Jacob's only named daughter | **Tamar**, David's daughter |
| the man | **Shechem**, prince of the land, an outsider | **Amnon**, the crown prince, her half-brother |
| the verb | ***wayĕʿannehā***, *he humbled her* (34:2) | ***wayĕʿannehā***, the same verb (13:14) |
| the formula | *"folly in Israel… a thing ought not to be done"* (34:7) | *"don't do this folly… no such thing ought to be done in Israel"* (13:12) |
| the sequence | force, **then** love (34:2–3) | love, **then** hatred *"greater than the love"* (13:15) |
| the father | **Jacob** *"held his peace"* (34:5) | **David** *"was very angry"* — and does nothing (13:21) |
| the woman's voice | **none, at any point** | **an argued refusal, and a lament after** (13:12–13, 16, 19) |
| the avenger | **Simeon and Levi**, her full brothers | **Absalom**, her full brother |
| the delay | **three days** | **two full years** (13:23) |
| the method | a treaty, a covenant sign, and a surprise attack | a sheep-shearing feast and a drunk man |
| the dead | **every male of a city** | **one man** |
| the aftermath | Jacob fears reprisal; the family moves | Absalom flees; the kingdom moves toward revolt |
| the verdict | **withheld fifteen chapters, then a curse** (49:5–7) | **withheld; the narrator reports and does not judge** |

**The shared vocabulary is not a coincidence of subject matter.** Two separate formulas appear in
both and almost nowhere else in the shape they take here: ***nĕbālâ bĕyiśrāʾēl*** and *a thing ought
not to be done* — see [[Folly in Israel - Nebalah from Genesis 34 to 2 Samuel 13]]. **2 Samuel 13
is written in Genesis 34's words**, whichever direction the literary dependence runs.

**What changes between them is who is allowed to speak.** Dinah is named six times and never
quoted. **Tamar is given the most articulate refusal in the Hebrew Bible** — she argues the shame,
the law, and the alternative (*"speak to the king; for he will not withhold me from you"*, 13:13) —
and is overruled by force anyway, then puts ashes on her head and tears the robe of many colours that
13:18 says the king's virgin daughters wore. **Genesis reports what was done to a woman; 2 Samuel lets one
answer back and shows it changing nothing.**

**What does not change is the father.** Jacob hears and *"held his peace"*; David hears and is *"very
angry"* and takes no action. **Both silences are filled by a brother**, and in both the brother's
action is disproportionate to any penalty the law states and catastrophic for the household. **The
law's own remedy is the missing middle in both stories**: Deuteronomy 22:28–29 has the man pay and
marry and never divorce her, and Shechem offers exactly that (34:11–12) while Amnon refuses even to
let Tamar stay (13:15–17). **The one who offers the remedy is killed with a whole city; the one who
refuses it is killed alone, two years later.**

**And in both, the avenger is the full brother.** Genesis specifies *"Simeon and Levi, Dinah's
brothers"* (34:25) in a household of four mothers; 2 Samuel specifies Absalom as Tamar's brother in
a household of many wives. **The claim being asserted is a claim of the maternal sub-family, not of
the father's house** — which is part of why both fathers experience it as an attack on their own
authority.

## Significance

**Neither narrator states a verdict inside the chapter, and both books eventually supply one from
outside it.** Genesis waits until 49:5–7 and curses the avengers' anger, not the act they avenged.
2 Samuel never comments at all, but places the whole episode inside Nathan's sentence — *"the sword
will never depart from your house… I will raise up evil against you out of your own house"*
(12:10–11) — so that Amnon's crime and Absalom's revenge are both the prophecy working itself out.

**The two together are the clearest evidence in the wiki that the Hebrew Bible's narrative ethics
run by juxtaposition rather than by ruling.** The act is condemned in the sharpest term the language
has (*nĕbālâ*); the revenge is condemned too, in another place, by another voice, at another time;
and **the victim's own account is given once out of two chances, and changes nothing either way.**
