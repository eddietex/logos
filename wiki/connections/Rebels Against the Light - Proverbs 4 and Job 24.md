# Rebels Against the Light - Proverbs 4 and Job 24

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 4:16–19** — *"For they don't sleep, unless they do evil. Their sleep is taken away,
  unless they make someone fall. … But the path of the righteous is like the dawning light, that
  shines more and more until the perfect day. The way of the wicked is like darkness. They don't
  know what they stumble over."* — [[The Two Paths]]
- **Job 24:13–17** — *"These are of those who rebel against the light. They don't know its ways,
  nor stay in its paths. … For the morning is to all of them like thick darkness, for they know
  the terrors of the thick darkness."* — [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]
- **Job 24:1, 12** — *"Why aren't times laid up by the Almighty?"*; *"yet God doesn't regard the
  folly."*
- **Job 24:22–24** — *"His eyes are on their ways. They are exalted; yet a little while, and they
  are gone."*
- **Proverbs 2:13** — those *"who forsake the paths of uprightness, to walk in the ways of
  darkness"* — [[If You Seek Her as Silver]]

## Analysis

**The two passages draw the same people.** In both, the wicked are creatures of the night, and
their wrongdoing is not occasional but habitual. Proverbs says they cannot sleep until they have
made someone fall (4:16). Job lists the murderer who is *"like a thief"* in the night, the
adulterer waiting for twilight, and those who *"dig through houses"* in the dark (24:14–16). Both
use the language of the road: Proverbs' *"way of the wicked"* (4:19), Job's rebels who *"don't
know"* the light's ways *"nor stay in its paths"* (24:13). And in both, the wicked do not know
something — in Proverbs, what they stumble over; in Job, the light itself.

**They differ in what the darkness does.** In Proverbs 4:19 the darkness is the consequence: the
wicked walk in it and fall without seeing why. In Job 24:17 the darkness is the wicked's chosen
element, and morning is what they dread. Nothing in 24:13–17 says that they fall. The chapter is
framed by Job's complaint that the Almighty sets no visible times of judgment (24:1) and that God
*"doesn't regard the folly"* of the oppressors (24:12).

**This is a tension, and the wiki records it rather than resolving it.** Proverbs states the end
of the dark road as a certainty to shape a young man's choice. Job, a man the book declares
blameless (Job 1:1), reports that he cannot see that end arriving. Job 24:22–24 does say that the
mighty are exalted *"a little while"* and are gone, and God's speech later says that from the
wicked *"their light is withheld"* (Job 38:15, quoted from the stored WEB); so the book of Job
does not deny that the wicked have an end. What it denies is that the end is visible on a
schedule a sufferer can read. The proverb is guidance for choosing a road; it is not a timetable,
and treating it as one is the error the wiki has recorded in [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]].

## Significance

Read together, the two passages keep each other honest. Proverbs supplies the reason not to
enter the dark path even when Job's observation is true: the road itself is blind. Job supplies
the reason not to turn Proverbs 4:19 into a verdict on whoever has fallen, or a promise that the
wicked will be seen to fall soon. The shared image — evil that cannot bear the light — is later
taken up in John 3:19–20 (cited here by reference only).
