# Day and Night - Joshua 1.8, Deuteronomy 17.18-20, and Psalm 1.2

## Passages involved
- [[After the Death of Moses]] — Joshua 1:7–8 (stored WEB).
- [[The Law for the King]] — Deuteronomy 17:18–20 (stored WEB).
- [[Read the Law to All Israel]] — Deuteronomy 31:9–13 (stored WEB).
- [[The Book Beside the Ark]] — Deuteronomy 31:24–29 (stored WEB).
- Psalm 1:1–3 — WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

**Three readers of one written law.** Deuteronomy 17 gives Israel's future king a copy of *this
law in a book*, which *shall be with him, and he shall read from it all the days of his life …
that he not turn away from the commandment, to the right hand, or to the left* (17:18–20).
Joshua 1 gives Joshua, who is not a king, *this book of the law*, which *shall not depart from
your mouth, but you shall meditate on it day and night*, with the same warning, *Don't turn from
it to the right hand or to the left* (1:7–8). Psalm 1 gives the same discipline to an anonymous
*man*: *his delight is in Yahweh's law. On his law he meditates day and night* (1:2, WEB verified
at bible-api.com).

| | Reader | What he does with the law | Promised result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deuteronomy 17:18–20 | the king | writes a copy and reads it *all the days of his life* | not lifted above his brothers; long reign |
| Joshua 1:7–8 | Israel's leader | keeps it in his mouth, meditates *day and night* | *make your way prosperous … good success* |
| Psalm 1:2–3 | *the man* | delights in it, meditates *day and night* | *Whatever he does shall prosper* |

**The verbal link is exact in Hebrew for Joshua and the Psalm.** Joshua 1:8 has *wəhāgîtā bô
yômām wālaylâ*; Psalm 1:2 has *ûbətôrātô yehgeh yômām wālāylâ* (both MAM via Sefaria): the
same verb *hāgâ* and the same pair *by day and by night*. Both then speak of prospering with the
root *ṣlḥ* — *taṣlîaḥ* in Joshua 1:8 and *yaṣlîaḥ* in Psalm 1:3, *Whatever he does shall prosper*
(both MAM via Sefaria). Deuteronomy 17 shares
the written book, the lifelong reading, and *right hand or left*, but not *hāgâ* or *day and
night* as far as the stored WEB shows; its Hebrew was not checked here.

**Where the law is kept.** Deuteronomy 31 puts the book beside the ark (31:26) and commands it read
aloud to the whole assembly every seventh year (31:10–13). The king's copy and Joshua's mouth bring
the same text into one person's daily life; Psalm 1 extends it to anyone. The movement is from an
office to a person, and the texts do not say whether that is a development or three independent
uses of a common idiom.

**What is not claimed.** None of the three texts cites another. The wiki makes no claim about the
relative dates of Joshua 1 and Psalm 1, or that either depends on the other. The connection is
the shared wording and the shared conviction that a written law, recited, governs success.

## Significance

The courage of Joshua 1 and the happiness of Psalm 1 have the same source: a book kept in the
mouth. Placed together, the texts make the reading of the law a qualification for leadership
in Deuteronomy and Joshua and an ordinary piety in the Psalter. See [[Law and Obedience]],
[[Wisdom]], and [[The Two Ways - Proverbs 4, Psalm 1, and Matthew 7]].
