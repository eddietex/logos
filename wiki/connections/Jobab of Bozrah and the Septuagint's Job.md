# Jobab of Bozrah and the Septuagint's Job

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 36:33** — *"Bela died, and Jobab, the son of Zerah of Bozrah, reigned in his place."* —
  [[The Descendants of Esau]]
- **Genesis 36:4, 10–11** — Eliphaz, Esau's firstborn, and his son **Teman**
- **Genesis 36:28** — *"These are the children of Dishan: **Uz** and Aran"*
- **Job 1:1** — *"There was a man in the land of **Uz**, whose name was **Job**"* — [[Job of Uz]]
- **Job 2:11** — *"**Eliphaz the Temanite**, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite"*
- **Lamentations 4:21** — *"daughter of Edom, that dwells in the land of Uz"*
- **Septuagint, Job 42:17b–e** — the translators' appendix, identifying Job with Jobab

## Analysis

**Four of the book of Job's names sit in one chapter of Genesis, and a Greek translator drew the
obvious conclusion.**

| the name | **Genesis 36** | **the book of Job** |
|---|---|---|
| **Uz** | a Horite, son of Dishan (36:28) | the land Job lives in (1:1) |
| **Eliphaz** | Esau's firstborn (36:4) | the first and senior friend (2:11) |
| **Teman** | Eliphaz's firstborn (36:11, 15) | the first friend's gentilic — *the Temanite* |
| **Jobab** | the second king of Edom (36:33) | — |

**Three of the four are a coincidence of a kind genealogies produce constantly.** The fourth is the
one that got built on.

### The appendix

**The Greek text of Job does not end where the Hebrew ends.** After 42:17 the Septuagint adds several
verses of its own:

> This man is described in the Syriac book as living in the land of Ausitis, on the borders of Idumea
> and Arabia … He was the fifth from Abraham. And these were the kings who reigned in Edom, which
> country he also ruled over: first Balak the son of Beor … and after him Jobab, who is called Job.

**It supplies Job with everything the Hebrew book withholds**: a father (Zerah), a grandfather
(Reuel), a great-grandfather (Esau), a wife, a nationality, a throne, and a date. See [[Jobab]] and
[[Job (person)]].

### Why it is not persuasive

**The book of Job goes out of its way not to give Job any of that.**

- **He is never called a king.** Job 1:3 calls him *"the greatest of all the children of the east"* —
  a man of standing, herds, and servants, who sits *"in the gate"* as an elder and judge (29:7–17).
  **Chapter 1 inventories his losses in obsessive detail** — seven thousand sheep, three thousand
  camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, five hundred donkeys, his servants, his ten children — **and a
  kingdom is not among them**, which it could hardly fail to be if he had one.
- **His father is never named**, in a book that names Bildad's and Zophar's towns and Elihu's father,
  clan, and family (32:2).
- **Edom is never mentioned**, nor Bozrah, nor any Edomite king.
- **The appendix's own arithmetic is strained**: to make Job *fifth from Abraham* and a son of Zerah
  it has to read the Zerah of 36:33 as the Zerah of 36:13, a son of Reuel — a link Genesis never
  makes. **The chapter never connects its chief list to its king list at all.**
- **And the names are different words.** ***ʾIyyôb*** is probably from *ʾyb*, **to be hostile** —
  *the one treated as an enemy*, which the book exploits — or from an Amorite name meaning *where is
  the father?*; ***Yôbāb*** looks like a reduplicated form of another root. **In Greek both come out
  as *Ιωβ-* and the distinction stops being audible**, which is very likely how the identification
  started.

### What the coincidence does support

**A setting, rather than an identification — and that much is well founded.**

**The book of Job is deliberately placed outside Israel.** There is no covenant in it, no law, no
priesthood, no tabernacle, no exodus, no promised land, and no Israelite in the cast. Job sacrifices
for his own children with no authorisation from anywhere (1:5). **The question the book asks —
whether a righteous man's suffering can be accounted for — is set up so that it cannot be answered by
appeal to Israel's particular history with God.** See [[Uz]].

**And the direction it is placed in is south-east.** [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] comes from [[Teman]],
and Teman is Edomite and **proverbial for wisdom**: Jeremiah 49:7 asks *"Is wisdom no more in Teman?
Has counsel perished from the prudent?"*, and Obadiah 8 promises to *"destroy the wise men out of
Edom, and understanding out of the mountain of Esau."* Bildad is a Shuhite and Zophar a Naamathite,
both pointing the same way. **Lamentations 4:21 is the one verse in Scripture that puts Uz on a map,
and it puts it in Edomite hands.**

**So an author staging a wisdom debate outside Israel would reach for exactly these names** — the same
way an author staging one today might reach for Athens. **The Genesis 36 overlap is evidence that the
names are regional, which is what the book needs; it is not evidence that any particular man in
Genesis 36 is the man in Job.**

### The irony the placement creates

**The friend from the town famous for wisdom argues from inherited wisdom kept pure of outsiders** —
*"which wise men have told by their fathers … to whom alone the land was given, and no stranger passed
among them"* (15:18–19) — **and at 42:7 God tells him he has not spoken rightly.** Whether the author
intended it cannot be proven. It is available, and it is sharper if Teman's reputation is assumed
rather than argued. See [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]].

## Significance

- **It is the earliest surviving attempt to locate the book of Job**, and it worked: the Edomite
  reading of Uz dominates later Christian tradition, and Job is still commonly printed and preached
  with a patriarchal, pre-Mosaic setting on the strength of it.
- **It shows a translator doing what the wiki tries not to do** — resolving an obscurity by
  harmonising two texts that never mention each other, and then writing the harmonisation into the
  book.
- **And it is the one place the wiki's two in-progress books touch.** Genesis 36 and the book of Job
  share four names and nothing else, and the connection between them is entirely a matter of what
  later readers made of the coincidence.
