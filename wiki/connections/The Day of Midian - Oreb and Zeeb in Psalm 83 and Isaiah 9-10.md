# The Day of Midian - Oreb and Zeeb in Psalm 83 and Isaiah 9-10

## Passages involved
- [[The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon]] — Judges 7:22–25. *Yahweh set every man's sword against his
  fellow*; *They killed Oreb at Oreb's rock, and Zeeb they killed at Zeeb's wine press* (stored WEB).
- Psalm 83:9–12 — *Do to them as you did to Midian, as to Sisera, as to Jabin, at the river Kishon …
  Make their nobles like Oreb and Zeeb; yes, all their princes like Zebah and Zalmunna* (WEB checked at
  bible-api.com; not yet ingested).
- Isaiah 9:4 — *the rod of his oppressor, you have broken as in the day of Midian* (WEB checked; not yet
  ingested).
- Isaiah 10:26 — *Yahweh of Armies will stir up a scourge against him, as in the slaughter of Midian at
  the rock of Oreb. His rod will be over the sea, and he will lift it up like he did against Egypt* (WEB
  checked; not yet ingested).
- [[Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel]] — Judges 8:10–21. *He took the two kings of Midian, Zebah and
  Zalmunna* (8:12); Gideon kills them (8:21) (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**A battle remembered as a byword.** Later Scripture remembers Gideon's victory not by Gideon's name
but by the enemy's and by the place. Psalm 83 is a prayer against a coalition of nations, and it asks
God to repeat two victories from Judges: Barak's over Sisera and Jabin at the Kishon (Judges 4–5), and
Gideon's over Midian, naming the princes of Judges 7:25 and the kings of Judges 8, [[Zebah]] and
[[Zalmunna]] (now ingested at [[Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel]]). Isaiah 9:4 calls the deliverance he promises *the day of Midian*. Isaiah 10:26
places it exactly, *at the rock of Oreb*, and sets it beside the sea of the exodus.

**The exodus pairing.** Isaiah 10:26 puts *the slaughter of Midian* next to the rod *over the sea*, as
*against Egypt*. Judges 7 has its own link to that night: the noun *watch*, *ʾašmōret*, occurs in
Genesis–Judges only at Judges 7:19 and Exodus 14:24 (WLC), where Yahweh *confused the Egyptian army* *in
the morning watch*. Isaiah does not cite either verse, and the Hebrew of Isaiah was not checked. See
[[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].

**What is remembered.** None of the three texts names Gideon. The psalm remembers what God *did to
Midian*; Isaiah remembers *the day* and *the slaughter*. That matches the battle report itself, where the
decisive act is Yahweh's (Judges 7:22), whatever the shout said about Gideon (7:20).

## Significance

The victory with three hundred men became a standard example of Yahweh saving his people against
odds, used in prayer (Psalm 83) and in prophecy (Isaiah 9–10). See [[Remembrance]], [[War]], and
[[Midian]].
