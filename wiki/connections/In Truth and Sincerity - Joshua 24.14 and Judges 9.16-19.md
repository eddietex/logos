# In Truth and Sincerity - Joshua 24.14 and Judges 9.16-19

## Passages involved

- [[The Covenant at Shechem]] — **Joshua 24:14** — *Now therefore fear Yahweh, and serve him in
  sincerity and in truth. Put away the gods which your fathers served beyond the River, in Egypt; and
  serve Yahweh.* (stored WEB)
- [[Jotham's Fable]] — **Judges 9:16, 19** — *Now therefore, if you have dealt truly and righteously,
  in that you have made Abimelech king …* and *if you then have dealt truly and righteously with
  Jerubbaal and with his house today, then rejoice in Abimelech, and let him also rejoice in you.*
  (stored WEB)

## Analysis

The same two words, in the same town, to the same people, a generation apart. Joshua asks Israel at
Shechem to serve Yahweh *bətāmîm ûbeʾĕmet*, *in sincerity and in truth*. Jotham asks *the men of
Shechem* whether they made Abimelech king *beʾĕmet ûbətāmîm*, *truly and righteously*. The order is
reversed. WEB translates *tāmîm* differently in the two verses, *sincerity* and *righteously*. In the
WLC of Genesis–Judges the two words stand together only in these three verses (Joshua 24:14; Judges
9:16, 19; lemma search of the OpenScriptures morphhb data).

Both speeches open with *Now therefore*, *wəʿattâ*. Both follow a history of what was done for the
hearers. Joshua's has Yahweh's acts from Terah to the conquest (Joshua 24:2–13). Jotham's has his
father's: *my father fought for you, and risked his life, and delivered you out of the hand of Midian*
(Judges 9:17). Both then ask for a choice. Joshua: *choose today whom you will serve* (24:15). Jotham
sets two outcomes, *rejoice*, or *fire*. Joshua asks for faithfulness to Yahweh. Jotham asks about
faithfulness to Jerubbaal's house. The wiki records the shared wording. It does not claim the writer
of Judges intended the echo.

Between the two speeches, Shechem has a house of Baal Berith (Judges 9:4). Joshua's *put away the gods*
was spoken in the same town. See [[The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem]] and
[[Integrity]].

## Significance

Shechem was asked twice for integrity, by the two speakers who stand at its two ends of this history:
Joshua's covenant and Abimelech's crowning. The first time it answered *No, but we will serve Yahweh*
(Joshua 24:21, stored WEB). The second time nobody answers.
