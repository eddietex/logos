# Two Levites from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17 and 19

## Passages involved
- **Judges 17:6–9** — *"There was a young man out of Bethlehem Judah, of the family of Judah, who was a
  Levite; and he lived there. The man departed out of the city, out of Bethlehem Judah, to live where he
  could find a place, and he came to the hill country of Ephraim … Micah said to him, 'Where did you come
  from?'"* (stored WEB). See [[Micah's Idol and Levite]].
- **Judges 19:1, 16–18** — *"there was a certain Levite living on the farther side of the hill country of
  Ephraim, who took for himself a concubine out of Bethlehem Judah"*; the old man asks, *"Where are you
  going? Where did you come from?"* (stored WEB). See [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]].

## Analysis

The two stories of the epilogue (17–18 and 19–21) open on the same three elements:

| | Judges 17–18 | Judges 19 |
|---|---|---|
| Refrain | *no king … right in his own eyes* (17:6); *no king* (18:1) | *no king* (19:1) |
| A Levite | *a young man … who was a Levite* (17:7) | *a certain Levite* (19:1) |
| Sojourning | *wəhûʾ gār šām*, *he lived there* (17:7, WLC) | *ʾîš lēwî gār*, *a Levite living* (19:1, WLC) |
| The road | Bethlehem Judah → hill country of Ephraim (17:8) | hill country of Ephraim → Bethlehem Judah → back (19:1–2, 18) |
| A host's question | *Where did you come from?* (17:9) | *Where are you going? Where did you come from?* (19:17) |
| Hire or bride | ten pieces of silver, clothing, food (17:10) | a concubine from Bethlehem (19:1) |
| Outcome | a tribe's idol at Dan (18:30–31) | a tribe nearly destroyed (20–21) |

In the first story the Levite comes *from* Bethlehem *to* Ephraim and is hired; in the second a Levite of
Ephraim takes a woman *from* Bethlehem and takes her back toward Ephraim. Each story begins with a Levite
crossing the same stretch of hill country and ends with a tribe changed: Dan given a cult, Benjamin brought
almost to extinction. In both, the Levite is the one figure who should hold Israel's worship together and
the one through whom it comes apart.

The two Levites are not the same man. The first is named at 18:30 as [[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]] and is
a *young man* of Bethlehem; the second is unnamed, lives in the far hill country of Ephraim, and is a
husband. The wiki records the parallel of setting and phrasing, not an identity.

**What is verified and what is not.** The shared nouns and the place-pair are verified in the stored WEB
and the WLC. The host's question is close but not identical: *mēʾayin tāḇôʾ* at 17:9 and *ʾānâ tēlēḵ
ûmēʾayin tāḇôʾ* at 19:17 (WLC; the second adds *where are you going*). That the parallel is a deliberate
frame for the epilogue is the usual reading and the wiki's; the text does not state it.

## Significance

Read together, the openings make the epilogue a diptych: one panel about worship without order, one about
violence without order, both framed by *there was no king in Israel*. See
[[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]],
[[The Levite (husband of the concubine)]], and [[Bethlehem]].
