# From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1

## Passages involved

- **Job 36:27–37:24** — Elihu's hymn to God in the weather: rain (36:27–28), cloud and thunder (36:29,
  33; 37:2–5), lightning (36:30, 32; 37:3, 11, 15), snow and rain (37:6), the storm out of its chamber and
  cold out of the north (37:9), ice by the breath of God (37:10), the sky like cast metal (37:18), and the
  golden splendour *"out of the north"* (37:22) — [[Elihu on God's Greatness]]
- **Job 38:1–3** — *"Then Yahweh answered Job out of the whirlwind, 'Who is this who
  darkens counsel by words without knowledge? Brace yourself like a man, for I will question you, then you
  answer me!'"* — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Job 38:22–38** — God's questions about the same subjects: *"Have you entered the
  treasuries of the snow?"* (38:22); *"Who has cut a channel for the flood water, or the path for the
  thunderstorm"* (38:25); *"Out of whose womb came the ice?"* (38:29); *"Do you know the laws of the
  heavens?"* (38:33); *"Can you send out lightnings, that they may go?"* (38:35)
  — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Job 40:6, 9** — the second answer *"out of the whirlwind"*, and *"Can you thunder
  with a voice like him?"* — [[Behemoth and Leviathan]]
- **Job 9:17** — Job: God *"breaks me with a storm"* — *śəʿārâ*, the word of 38:1 — [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]]
- **Job 28:26** — *"When he made a decree for the rain, and a way for the lightning of the thunder"* — the
  same Hebrew as 38:25b — [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]]

## Analysis

**The subjects are the same.** Set Elihu's hymn beside God's first speech and the overlap is close:

| subject | Elihu | God |
|---|---|---|
| rain and its source | *"he draws up the drops of water"* (36:27) | *"Does the rain have a father? Or who fathers the drops of dew?"* (38:28) |
| clouds | *"can any understand the spreading of the clouds?"* (36:29) | *"Who can count the clouds by wisdom?"* (38:37) |
| lightning | *"commands it to strike the mark"* (36:32) | *"Can you send out lightnings, that they may go?"* (38:35) |
| thunder | *"He thunders with the voice of his majesty"* (37:4) | *"Can you thunder with a voice like him?"* (40:9) |
| snow | *"he says to the snow, 'Fall on the earth'"* (37:6) | *"Have you entered the treasuries of the snow?"* (38:22) |
| ice | *"By the breath of God, ice is given"* (37:10) | *"Out of whose womb came the ice?"* (38:29) |
| the sky | *"Can you, with him, spread out the sky?"* (37:18) | *"Do you know the laws of the heavens?"* (38:33) |

**So is the form.** Elihu's examination of Job — *"Do you know how God controls them …? Do you know the
workings of the clouds …? Can you, with him, spread out the sky …?"* (37:15–18) — is the grammar of God's
speeches: the second person, the question, the creature asked whether he made or rules the thing in front
of him.

**And so, perhaps, is the storm.** Elihu says his heart trembles *"at this"* (37:1) and calls on his hearers
to *"Hear, oh, hear"* the thunder (37:2); the light is hidden by cloud and then *"the wind passes, and clears
them"* (37:21); *"Out of the north comes golden splendor. With God is awesome majesty"* (37:22). The next
verse is God speaking *min-hassəʿārâ*, out of the storm-wind (38:1). Many readers, ancient and modern, take
this as staging: Elihu is describing a storm that is coming up as he speaks, and God speaks out of it. The
text never says so. It only puts the two side by side, with no narrative between them.

### Three ways of reading the sequence

- **Elihu as herald.** He prepares the subject, the form, and the setting of God's reply, and his last
  speech is the overture to it. On this reading he is not rebuked in the epilogue (42:7–9) because he did
  his work, and God's first words (*"words without knowledge"*, 38:2) are Elihu's last ones (35:16). See
  [[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]].
- **Elihu overtaken.** God takes Elihu's subject and inverts his conclusion. Elihu describes the storm to
  prove that no one can speak with God — *"Teach us what we shall tell him, for we can't make our case by
  reason of darkness … should a man wish that he were swallowed up?"* (37:19–20) — and that *"We can't reach
  the Almighty"* (37:23, *lōʾ məṣāʾnūhû*, *we have not found him*). Then God, out of that storm, asks Job to
  make his case — *"I will question you, then you answer me!"* (38:3) — and is found. Elihu's hymn proves God
  unapproachable, and the whirlwind approaches. On this reading Elihu's silence afterwards is the book's
  verdict on him.
- **Elihu as later addition.** Scholars who take 32–37 as added to the book can read the overlap as the
  addition's author building a bridge into chapter 38 — writing Elihu toward the speech that was already
  there.

The wiki records all three and reads the book as received.

### What only God says

The overlap makes the differences easy to see. Elihu's storm serves **people**: it judges and feeds
(36:31), stops men's work so they may know their Maker (37:7), and comes *"for correction, or for his land,
or for loving kindness"* (37:13). God's storm, when he describes it, falls **where no one is**: *"To cause it
to rain on a land where no man is; on the wilderness, in which there is no man; to satisfy the waste and
desolate ground"* (38:26–27). Elihu's weather is a lesson for man. God's weather is not about man at all —
which is a harder answer to Job, and a stranger one, than anything Elihu said.

And Job's own word stands at the start of it. *"He breaks me with a storm"* (9:17), Job said, in the speech
where he despaired of ever getting a hearing. *Śəʿārâ* is the storm of 38:1. The storm Job feared would
crush him is the place from which God answers him.

## Significance

- It is the strongest case for Elihu's chapters belonging to the book's design: whatever their origin, they
  carry the reader from the end of the dialogue into the whirlwind by its own subjects and grammar.
- It is also the clearest measure of the distance between Elihu and God: the same storm, opposite
  conclusions — *no one can speak with him* and *answer me*.
- It shows the book's weather moving through three speakers: the *rain and lightning* of the wisdom hymn
  (28:26), Elihu's storm (36–37), and God's (38). See [[Creation]], [[Wisdom]], [[Language and Speech]],
  [[Elihu the Buzite]], and [[The Voice of His Majesty - Job 37.2-5 and Psalm 29]].
