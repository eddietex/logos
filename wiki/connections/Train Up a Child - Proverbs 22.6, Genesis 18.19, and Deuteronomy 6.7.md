# Train Up a Child - Proverbs 22.6, Genesis 18.19, and Deuteronomy 6.7

## Passages involved

- [[A Good Name]] — Proverbs 22:6, 15.
- **Genesis 18:19** — [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]]: *"For I have known him, to
  the end that he may command his children and his household after him, that they may keep the way
  of Yahweh, to do righteousness and justice."*
- **Deuteronomy 6:6–7** *(not yet ingested)* — *"These words, which I command you today, shall be
  on your heart; and you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when
  you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise
  up."*
- **Ephesians 6:4** *(not yet ingested)* — *"You fathers, don’t provoke your children to wrath, but

- **Proverbs 13:1; 17:21, 25; 19:26** — the scoffing, foolish, and shameful sons of the same

  [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]].
- [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] — Genesis 14:14, Abram's *trained men*.

## Analysis

Proverbs 22:6 is the Bible's best-known sentence about raising children:

> Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it.

**The shared picture is a way.** Genesis 18:19 gives Yahweh's reason for choosing Abraham as a
household purpose: that he *"command his children and his household after him"* to keep *"the way of
Yahweh"*. Deuteronomy 6:7 makes the same charge a whole day's conversation — sitting, walking,
lying down, rising. Proverbs 22:6 compresses it into a saying about time: what is set early lasts
until old age. All three speak of a *way* to be kept and of the older generation's responsibility to
set the younger on it. Ephesians 6:4 adds the limit the other three assume and do not state: the
father is told not to provoke his children to wrath, and the *discipline and instruction* belong to
*"the Lord"*.

**A saying, not a guarantee.** The verse is often read as a promise that a well-trained child
cannot finally depart. Proverbs itself resists that reading. The same collection grieves over the
father of a fool (17:21, 25), names the son who robs his father and drives away his mother (19:26),
and opens chapter 13 with a scoffer who will not listen to rebuke. A sentence collection states how
things go; it does not make every parent of a wayward adult guilty of failing to train, and the wiki
does not read 22:6 as a verdict on anyone. This is the same limit the wiki keeps on retribution
sayings generally (see [[Retribution]]).

**What *the way he should go* means is open.** The WEB's phrase can be read as the right way in
general — the way of Yahweh of Genesis 18:19 — or as a way fitted to the particular child. The
English does not decide it, and no claim about the Hebrew is made here.

**Training and the rod.** Nine verses later the same pericope says *"Folly is bound up in the heart
of a child: the rod of discipline drives it far from him"* (22:15). Training and correction appear
side by side, as they do throughout the book; see
[[The Rod of Discipline - Proverbs 13.24 and the Father's Correction]].

**Language.** [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] connects Abram's *trained men* (Genesis 14:14) to
the root of *train up* here. That claim belongs to that page and has not been re-verified against a
Hebrew edition in this pass.

## Significance

The thread shows formation of children as a covenant duty set in the household, from Abraham to
Deuteronomy, and Proverbs stating its long reach without turning it into a guarantee. See
[[Love]], [[Law and Obedience]], [[Wisdom]], and [[Retribution]].
