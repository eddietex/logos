# Birds and Cleansing - Persons and Houses in Leviticus 14

## Passages involved

- [[The Cleansing of the Leper]] — Leviticus 14:1–32, especially 3–7.
- [[Mildew in a House]] — Leviticus 14:33–57, especially 48–53.

## Analysis

Both procedures follow an examination that finds healing: the person in 14:3, the
house after replastering in 14:48. Each uses two birds, cedar wood, scarlet, hyssop,
an earthen vessel, running water, and the slain bird’s blood. Sevenfold sprinkling
and release of the living bird into the open field appear in both (4–7; 49–53).

The shared rite does not erase the distinction between its recipients. The person
washes and shaves, returns to camp but stays outside his tent seven days, and brings
eighth-day offerings with blood and oil applications (8–32). None of these additional
steps is assigned to the house. For the house, the conclusion explicitly names
atonement and cleanness (53); it supplies no confession or personal forgiveness formula.

No explanation of the materials’ symbolism is given in either unit. The released bird
is not expressly described as carrying away sin. The comparison identifies a shared
cleansing procedure without imposing a later typology or a modern diagnosis.

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 14:1–57; comparison is paraphrase.

## Significance

Cleansing reaches both persons and dwellings. Atonement for a house makes it especially
clear that the category cannot be reduced to forgiveness of an individual’s moral
transgression. The separate follow-up requirements preserve the distinction between
restoring a person’s access and cleansing a repaired building.
