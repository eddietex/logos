# The Upright Will Dwell in the Land - Proverbs 2.21-22, Psalm 37, and Matthew 5.5

## Passages involved

- [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] — Proverbs 2:21–22: *"For the upright will dwell in the land. The
  perfect will remain in it. But the wicked will be cut off from the land. The treacherous will be
  rooted out of it."*
- Proverbs 10:30 *(not yet ingested)* — *"The righteous will never be removed, but the wicked will
  not dwell in the land."*
- Psalm 37:9, 11, 22, 29 *(not yet ingested)* — *"evildoers shall be cut off, but those who wait for
  Yahweh shall inherit the land"*; *"the humble shall inherit the land"*; *"such as are blessed by
  him shall inherit the land. Those who are cursed by him shall be cut off"*; *"The righteous shall
  inherit the land, and live in it forever."*
- Deuteronomy 28:63 *(not yet ingested)* — *"You will be plucked from the land that you are going in
  to possess."*
- Genesis 17:14 — [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]]: *"that soul shall be cut off from his
  people. He has broken my covenant."*
- Matthew 5:5 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Blessed are the gentle, for they shall inherit the earth."*
- Job 1:1 — [[Job of Uz]]: *"blameless and upright"*, *tām wəyāšār*.

## Analysis

**Proverbs and Psalm 37 state one rule in shared vocabulary.** Both split humanity into two groups
and give each its relation to the land: one *dwells* (*šāḵan*, Proverbs 2:21; Psalm 37:29) or
*inherits*, the other is *cut off* (*kāraṯ*, Proverbs 2:22; Psalm 37:9, 22). Psalm 37 says it five
times, as a reply to the problem Job raised — the wicked prospering (*"Don't fret because of
evildoers"*, the psalm's opening theme) — and answers it with time: the wicked will be cut off, so
wait. Proverbs 2 says it once, as the end of a lecture, without the problem.

**The verbs are covenant verbs.** *Kāraṯ* is the verb of *cutting* a covenant and of the covenant
penalty, *"cut off from his people"* (Genesis 17:14); *nāsaḥ*, *rooted out* (Proverbs 2:22), is
Deuteronomy 28:63's verb for Israel *plucked* from the land under the covenant curse. **What
Deuteronomy threatens the nation with, Proverbs and the psalm apply to the individual wicked.** The
land is still the covenant land, but the sorting happens person by person.

**Matthew 5:5 widens the land to the earth.** The beatitude takes up Psalm 37:11's *"the humble shall
inherit the land"*, and the Greek *gē* can mean either; English translations of Matthew usually say
*earth*. Hebrew *ʾereṣ* has the same range, so the widening is a matter of reading, not of a changed
word. Whether Matthew's wording follows the Septuagint of the psalm is commonly said but was not
checked here.

**Tension flagged, not resolved.** The rule has two standing exceptions already in the wiki. The
patriarchs, the recipients of the land promise, lived in it as sojourners and owned only a burial
cave ([[Land and Sojourning]]); and Job, introduced in exactly Proverbs 2:21's pair of words
(*yāšār*, *tām*), lost his land and his household (Job 1:13–19) and the book refuses to explain it
by his sin. Psalm 37's answer is *wait*; the restoration of Job (Job 42:10–17) is a narrative form of
the same answer; Matthew 5:5's is eschatological. Proverbs 2 gives no answer because it does not
raise the question.

## Significance

The land is where the wisdom tradition's [[Retribution]] rule becomes concrete — not reward in the
abstract, but staying on one's land or being torn from it. It ties Proverbs to Deuteronomy's
covenant blessings and curses, to Psalm 37's counsel of patience, and through the beatitudes to the
New Testament's hope of an inheritance. See [[Land and Sojourning]], [[Covenant]], and
[[Integrity]].
