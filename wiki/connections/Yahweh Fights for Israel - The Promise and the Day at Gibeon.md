# Yahweh Fights for Israel - The Promise and the Day at Gibeon

## Passages involved

- [[Laws for Going to War]]
- [[After the Death of Moses]]
- [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]]
- Sources: stored WEB Exodus 9:18–26; Deuteronomy 20:1–4; Joshua 1:5; 10:1–15, as relevant below.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 20:1–4 grounds courage in Yahweh’s presence and his fighting for Israel. Joshua 1:5
promises that no man will stand before Joshua. Joshua 10:8 renews that assurance immediately before
the attack; 10:14 interprets the extraordinary day as Yahweh fighting for Israel. The night march
and armed pursuit remain real human actions, while the hail and delayed sunset make divine help
decisive.

## Significance

The battle gives narrative form to the earlier assurances. These texts address Israel’s narrated
campaign; applying them to any later nation’s wars would require an argument these passages do not
supply.
