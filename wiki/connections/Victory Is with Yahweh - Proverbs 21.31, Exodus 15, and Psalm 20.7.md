# Victory Is with Yahweh - Proverbs 21.31, Exodus 15, and Psalm 20.7

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 21:30–31** — *"There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against Yahweh. The
  horse is prepared for the day of battle; but victory is with Yahweh."* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Proverbs 21:22** — *"A wise man scales the city of the mighty, and brings down the strength of
  its confidence."* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Exodus 14:9, 14, 28** — Pharaoh's horses and chariots overtake Israel; *"Yahweh will fight for
  you, and you shall be still"*; *"The waters returned, and covered the chariots and the horsemen"*
  — [[The Crossing of the Sea]]
- **Exodus 15:1, 21** — *"The horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea."* —
  [[The Song of the Sea]]
- **Psalm 20:7** — *"Some trust in chariots, and some in horses, but we trust the name of Yahweh our
  God."* *(WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- **Psalm 33:17** — *"A horse is a vain thing for safety, neither does he deliver any by his great
  power."* *(WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Preparation is assumed; the outcome is not owned.** Proverbs 21:31 does not mock the cavalry. The
horse *is* prepared, and verse 22 has just praised the wise man who takes a strong city. The saying
places the decision of the battle with Yahweh, and verse 30 adds that no counsel stands against
him.

**Exodus is the narrative case.** The horse was the military strength Pharaoh brought to the sea
(14:9); Israel was told to be still while Yahweh fought (14:14); the chariots and horsemen were
covered (14:28); and the first song after the crossing celebrates horse and rider thrown into the
sea (15:1, 21). In Exodus Israel did not prepare a horse at all. Proverbs speaks to a settled
kingdom that does, and adds the same limit.

**The Psalms turn the observation into confession.** Psalm 20:7 contrasts trust in chariots and
horses with trust in Yahweh's name; Psalm 33:17 calls the horse *"a vain thing for safety."*
Proverbs gives the wisdom form; the Psalms give the worship form.
