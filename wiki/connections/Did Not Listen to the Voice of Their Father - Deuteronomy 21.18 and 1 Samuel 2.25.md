# Did Not Listen to the Voice of Their Father - Deuteronomy 21.18 and 1 Samuel 2.25

## Passages involved

- [[Captives, Firstborn Sons, and Public Shame]] — Deuteronomy 21:18–21: *If a man has a stubborn and
  rebellious son, who will not obey the voice of his father or the voice of his mother, and though they
  chasten him, will not listen to them* (18) … *All the men of his city shall stone him to death with
  stones* (21).
- [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] — 1 Samuel 2:22–25: Eli rebukes his sons; *Notwithstanding, they didn't
  listen to the voice of their father, because Yahweh intended to kill them* (25).
- **1 Samuel 3:13** *(not yet ingested)* — *his sons brought a curse on themselves, and he didn't restrain
  them.*

## Analysis

**The phrase.** Deuteronomy 21:18 has *ʾênennû šōmēaʿ bəqôl ʾāḇîw*; 1 Samuel 2:25 has *wəlōʾ yišməʿû
ləqôl ʾăḇîhem* (WLC). The verb *šāmaʿ*, the noun *qôl*, and *father* are shared; the preposition differs
(*bə-* in Deuteronomy, *lə-* in Samuel). In the WLC files of Genesis through 2 Samuel, a verse with
*šāmaʿ*, *qôl*, and *ʾāḇ* in which the voice is a father's occurs only in these two (lemma search; the
other verses with all three lemmas speak of *the fathers* in another clause, and the voice heard is
Yahweh's or Israel's). 1 Samuel does not cite the law, and the
parallel is a reader's.

**The process the law prescribes.** In Deuteronomy, parents who have *chastened* the son and been
ignored bring him to the elders at the gate, and the city carries out the sentence (21:19–21). Eli's
rebuke in 1 Samuel 2:23–25 is a father's warning, and it is ignored. Eli takes no further step. The man of
God's charge in the next pericope is that Eli honours his sons above Yahweh (2:29), and 3:13 that *he
didn't restrain them*.

**Who executes the sentence.** In Deuteronomy the community executes it. In 1 Samuel, the narrator
says the sons' refusal to listen is itself because *Yahweh intended to kill them* (2:25), and the sentence
is carried out by Yahweh through the Philistines (4:11). Where the human court did not act, the text
places the action with God. That reading is offered, not stated by either text.

## Significance

- It gives the law of the rebellious son a narrative case at the priestly centre of Israel, where the
  father is himself the priest and judge (1 Samuel 4:18 says Eli *had judged Israel forty years*; stored
  WEB, not yet ingested).
- It connects [[Law and Obedience]], [[Judgment]], and [[Hardening of the Heart]] through one family. See
  [[Eli]], [[Hophni]], and [[Phinehas (son of Eli)]].
