# The Two Ways - Proverbs 4, Psalm 1, and Matthew 7

## Passages involved

- **Deuteronomy 30:15, 19** — *"Behold, I have set before you today life and prosperity, and death
  and evil. … I have set before you life and death, the blessing and the curse. Therefore choose
  life, that you may live, you and your descendants"* (not yet ingested)
- **Psalm 1:1, 6** — *"Blessed is the man who doesn't walk in the counsel of the wicked, nor stand
  on the path of sinners … For Yahweh knows the way of the righteous, but the way of the wicked
  shall perish."* (not yet ingested)
- **Proverbs 1:15** — *"My son, don't walk on the path with them."* —
  [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]]
- **Proverbs 2:12–20** — the ways of darkness against *"the way of good men"* —
  [[If You Seek Her as Silver]]
- **Proverbs 4:10–19** — *"I have taught you in the way of wisdom. … Don't enter into the path of
  the wicked. … But the path of the righteous is like the dawning light … The way of the wicked is
  like darkness."* — [[The Two Paths]]
- **Proverbs 4:26–27** — *"Make the path of your feet level. … Don't turn to the right hand nor to
  the left. Remove your foot from evil."* — [[Keep Your Heart]]; compare Deuteronomy 5:32–33 and
  Joshua 1:7, *"to the right hand or to the left"* (WEB, verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)
- **Jeremiah 21:8** — *"Behold, I set before you the way of life and the way of death."* (not yet
  ingested)
- **Matthew 7:13–14** — *"Enter in by the narrow gate; for wide is the gate and broad is the way
  that leads to destruction, and many are those who enter in by it. How narrow is the gate, and
  restricted is the way that leads to life! Few are those who find it."* (not yet ingested)

## Analysis

**The image is the same; the setting changes.** In Deuteronomy the two ways are a covenant
choice put to a whole people before the land: life and blessing, death and curse, with the
command *"choose life"*. Psalm 1 makes it the portrait of one person, whose way Yahweh *"knows"*,
against the wicked whose way *"shall perish"*. Proverbs 4 makes it a father's lecture. Jeremiah
21:8 turns it into a choice inside a siege. Matthew 7 puts it in a teaching of Jesus, with a gate
at the head of each road and a count of who walks them.

**Proverbs 4 contributes the image of growth and of blindness.** The other passages name where the
roads end — life or perishing, life or destruction. Proverbs 4:18–19 describes what it is like to
walk them: the righteous path brightens like dawn until full day, and the wicked way is so dark
that its walkers *"don't know what they stumble over"*. And it adds a strategy of avoidance — six
commands in two verses (4:14–15) to stay off the wrong road altogether.

**Matthew adds what the others do not.** Proverbs and Psalm 1 do not say how many walk each road.
Matthew 7:13–14 says the broad way has many and the narrow way few, and that the way to life must
be *found*. The earlier texts assume the right way is visible to anyone taught; Matthew assumes it
is hard to find. That is a difference of emphasis between the passages, recorded here rather than
harmonized.

## Significance

The two-ways teaching is one of the canon's simplest frames for moral life: there are two roads,
not a spectrum, and every step is on one of them. Proverbs gives the frame its most personal form,
a parent speaking to a child, and its most visual one, the brightening path. Read after Job, the
frame is about direction and destination, not about the visible prosperity of those on each road;
see [[Rebels Against the Light - Proverbs 4 and Job 24]] and [[Retribution]].

The next lecture keeps the one road and adds its discipline: turn neither right nor left, the
idiom Deuteronomy uses of the law (5:32) — see [[Keep Your Heart]].
