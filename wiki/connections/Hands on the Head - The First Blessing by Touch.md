# Hands on the Head - The First Blessing by Touch

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 48:13–14, 17–18** — *"Israel stretched out his right hand, and laid it on Ephraim's head …
  guiding his hands knowingly"* — [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]]
- **Genesis 27:21–27** — [[Isaac]] *feels* his son to identify him, and blesses him with a kiss, **not
  with a hand on the head** — [[The Stolen Blessing]]
- **Leviticus 1:4; 4:4; 16:21** — the hand laid on the head of an offering, and Aaron's two hands on
  the scapegoat
- **Leviticus 24:14; Numbers 8:10** — witnesses' hands on a blasphemer's head; Israel's hands on the
  Levites
- **Numbers 27:18–23; Deuteronomy 34:9** — Moses lays his hands on Joshua, *"and he was full of
  wisdom"*
- **Mark 10:16** — *"He took them in his arms and blessed them, laying his hands on them"*
- **Acts 6:6; 13:3; 1 Timothy 4:14; 5:22; 2 Timothy 1:6** — appointment, commissioning, and *"the gift
  of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands"*
- **Hebrews 6:2** — *"the laying on of hands"* listed among the elementary doctrines

## Analysis

**Genesis 48:14 is the first time in the Bible that a hand is put on a head, and everything the gesture
later means is absent from it.**

**No office is conferred, nothing is transferred, and nothing is identified.** **The hands are laid to
bless, and the only thing the narrator says about them is which head each one is on and that the
placement was deliberate.**

### The gesture is not in Genesis 27

**This matters, because the two blessing scenes are otherwise near-identical.** See
[[Two Blind Fathers - Genesis 27 and Genesis 48]].

| | Genesis 27 | Genesis 48 |
|---|---|---|
| **what the hands do** | **Isaac's are felt**: *"Come near … that I may feel you"* (27:21) — and **Jacob's hands are the disguise** (27:22–23) | **Jacob's are laid**: *"stretched out his right hand, and laid it on Ephraim's head"* (48:14) |
| **the physical act of blessing** | **a kiss**: *"Come near now, and kiss me, my son"* (27:26) | **a touch on the head** (48:14) |
| **whose hands are the problem** | **the son's** — goatskin on a smooth arm | **the father's** — crossed on purpose |

**In chapter 27 the hands are the instrument of a deception; in chapter 48 they are the instrument of
the blessing itself.** **Genesis invents the gesture in the scene where the blind man is not deceived.**

### *Right* and *left*

**48:13–14 is the earliest text in Scripture that distinguishes the two hands by value.** **[[Joseph]]
stages the boys so that his father's right hand falls on the elder, and Jacob crosses his arms**; the
word *hand* occurs eleven times in the chapter and ***right hand*** **five of them.**

**Nothing before this verse in the Bible ranks the hands.** **Everything after it assumes the ranking** —
*"Yahweh's right hand is exalted"* (Psalm 118:16), *"at the right hand of the Majesty on high"*
(Hebrews 1:3), the sheep and the goats (Matthew 25:33). **The wiki records Genesis 48:14 as the
convention's first appearance and not as its origin**, since the text argues for nothing and simply
assumes the reader will see why Joseph objects.

### The three later uses, and which one this is

**Scripture's hand-on-head does three distinct jobs, and they should not be run together.**

1. **Identification and transfer of liability** — **the sacrificial hand** (Leviticus 1:4; 4:4), **the
   scapegoat** (16:21), **the witnesses against a blasphemer** (24:14). **Something is put onto the
   head**: ownership, or guilt. See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
2. **Appointment to office** — **Moses on Joshua** (Numbers 27:18–23; Deuteronomy 34:9), **Israel on the
   Levites** (Numbers 8:10), **the apostles on the seven** (Acts 6:6) **and on Paul and Barnabas**
   (13:3). **Authority is conveyed.** See [[Priesthood]].
3. **Blessing** — **Genesis 48:14 and Mark 10:16, and almost nothing else.** **Nothing is transferred and
   no office is given; a hand is put on a head and good is asked for the person under it.**

**Genesis 48 belongs to the third**, and it is the only Old Testament instance of it. **Which makes
Mark 10:16 its nearest parallel in the whole Bible**: *"He took them in his arms and blessed them,
laying his hands on them"* — **children, hands, blessing, and no office.** **The wiki notes the
parallel and claims no citation**; Mark does not mention Jacob.

### What Genesis draws attention to instead

**The chapter's interest is not in the gesture but in its aim.** ***Śikkēl ʾet-yādāyw*** — *guiding his
hands knowingly* (48:14) — **is a verb about insight in a verse about placement**, and it is followed
immediately by the clause the placement contradicts: *"for Manasseh was the firstborn."* **Joseph then
does the one thing nobody does in any other hand-laying passage in Scripture: he takes hold of the hand
and tries to move it** (48:17). **He is refused** (48:19).

**The gesture's first appearance is therefore also the only one that is contested while it is
happening**, and the wiki files that as the point: **Genesis introduces the laying on of hands in a
scene about whose head the hand is on.**

## Significance

**Hebrews 6:2 lists *"the laying on of hands"* among the foundations of the faith, alongside baptism and
the resurrection of the dead.** **Its first occurrence in Scripture is a dying man in [[Egypt]] blessing
two half-foreign grandchildren, with his arms crossed, over their father's objection.** **No institution
is founded in the verse and none is claimed.**

**And the fact that Genesis 27 does not use the gesture is worth keeping.** **The blessing that runs the
whole Jacob narrative is given with a kiss and a plate of venison**, and the hand on the head arrives
only when the same man, two generations on, gives a blessing he means. See [[Blessing]].
