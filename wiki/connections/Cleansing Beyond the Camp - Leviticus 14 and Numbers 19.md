# Cleansing Beyond the Camp - Leviticus 14 and Numbers 19

## Passages involved
- [[The Cleansing of the Leper]] — Leviticus 14:1–32
- [[The Red Heifer]] — Numbers 19:1–22
- [[The Unclean Sent Out and Wrongs Repaid]] — Numbers 5:1–10
- [[The Passover in the Second Year]] — Numbers 9:1–14

## Analysis

Leviticus 14:3–9 and Numbers 19:3–9 begin purification work outside the camp.
Both employ cedar wood, scarlet, hyssop, water, and sprinkling, but in different ways.
Leviticus has two birds, one released alive, and blood-and-water sprinkling on the
healed person. Numbers burns the heifer with cedar, hyssop, and scarlet, then stores
ashes for later use. Its sevenfold blood sprinkling is toward the Tent (19:4);
its ash-water sprinkling treats contaminated people and objects (19:18–19).
Shared materials do not make these interchangeable rites.

Numbers 5:2–3 excludes those unclean by the dead to protect the inhabited camp.
Numbers 9:6–12 provides a later Passover date for corpse-contaminated people.
Numbers 19 gives the purification procedure itself, requiring third- and seventh-day
sprinkling. It does not say the earlier petitioners were guilty for encountering death,
nor supply a date proving when the first heifer rite occurred.

The tension within Numbers 19 is explicit: a rite that cleanses others leaves its
priest, burner, and ash-gatherer temporarily unclean (7–10). That is prescribed service,
not rebellion. The text provides no explanation of the mechanism behind this reversal.
Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 14:1–20; Numbers 5:1–4; 9:1–14; 19:1–22.

## Significance

Exclusion, accommodation, and restoration belong together in [[Clean and Unclean]].
The camp boundary can be a place where restoration begins, not only where impurity is sent.
