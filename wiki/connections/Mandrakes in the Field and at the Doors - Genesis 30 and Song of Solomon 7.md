# Mandrakes in the Field and at the Doors - Genesis 30 and Song of Solomon 7

## Passages involved

- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 30:14–24.
- [[Come into the Fields, My Beloved]] — Song of Solomon 7:12–13.

## Analysis

Reuben finds mandrakes in the field; Rachel asks Leah for them, and the sisters
bargain over Jacob's company for the night (Genesis 30:14–16). In Song 7:13,
mandrakes give fragrance beside fruits the woman has stored for her beloved.
Both passages place them near sexual relations, but the Song has no rival wife,
bargain, or narrated conception. The contrast is between negotiated access and
a lover's offered abundance.

Genesis credits God with hearing Leah and opening Rachel's womb (30:17, 22);
neither passage establishes a medicinal effect of mandrakes. The comparison
requires no botanical identification or claim about their efficacy.

Source: stored WEB Genesis 30:14–24; Song of Solomon 7:12–13.

## Significance

The recurring plant image connects desire across genres without collapsing the
rivalry in Jacob's household into the Song's mutual invitation. See [[Love]].
