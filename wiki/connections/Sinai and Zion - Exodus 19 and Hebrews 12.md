# Sinai and Zion - Exodus 19 and Hebrews 12

## Passages involved

- [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] — Exodus 19:12–24: fire, trumpet, trembling, and the forbidden boundary.
- Exodus 20:18–21 — the people's request for Moses to speak; ahead in the queue, checked in raw.
- Hebrews 12:18–29 — the contrast with Zion, Jesus' mediation, and the final warning; not yet
  ingested.

## Analysis

Hebrews recalls a mountain that burns, darkness and storm, a trumpet, and a voice its hearers
cannot endure (12:18–19). Its prohibition concerning even an animal touching the mountain (12:20)
recalls Exodus 19:12–13, where both animal and human trespass incur death. The plea not to hear
more corresponds to Exodus 20:19, rather than to an event already narrated in chapter 19.
Hebrews draws together the Sinai encounter; it is not quoting one continuous Exodus paragraph.

The address then turns:

> **Hebrews 12:22** But you have come to Mount Zion, and to the city of the living God, the heavenly
> Jerusalem, and to innumerable multitudes of angels,

The following assembly includes God as judge and Jesus as mediator of a new covenant (12:23–24).
The contrast concerns the readers' approach and covenant relationship, not the replacement of
a dangerous deity by a harmless one. Verse 25 warns against refusing the speaker, and 12:28–29
calls for reverence and awe because God is a consuming fire.

**Two cautions preserve the sources.** First, the terrified words attributed to Moses in Hebrews
12:21 are not a quotation of Moses' speech in Exodus 19. The present connection does not supply
an unverified source for them. Second, Exodus 19 itself combines rescue and divine danger: the
God who carries the people to himself (19:4) is the one who sets the boundary (19:12, 21–24).
Hebrews accentuates the contrast of access, but the source narrative already contains gracious
approach and serious warning together.
