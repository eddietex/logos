# On Eagles Wings - Exodus 19 and Deuteronomy 32

## Passages involved

- [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] — Exodus 19:4–6.
- Deuteronomy 32:9–12 — the Song of Moses recalls Jacob as Yahweh's portion and pictures an eagle
  carrying its young; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus 19:4 interprets the completed rescue through an image:

> “how I bore you on eagles’ wings, and brought you to myself.”

The destination is God himself. The next verses explain the relationship as possession,
priestly vocation, and holiness, against the background of God's ownership of the whole earth.
Deuteronomy 32:9–12 joins the same two ideas of belonging and carrying: Jacob is Yahweh's
inheritance, found and guarded in the wilderness, then compared with an eagle's young.

> **Deuteronomy 32:11** As an eagle that stirs up her nest, that flutters over her young, he spread
> abroad his wings, he took them, he bore them on his feathers.

Deuteronomy expands the picture into protective care and guidance and explicitly excludes a
foreign god from that guidance (32:12). Exodus concentrates on the journey's relational end.
The shared imagery is clear; neither passage requires a zoological claim that eagles literally
transport young in exactly the described manner, and neither establishes a direction of literary
dependence between the two texts.

## Significance

The image combines strength with care. The people are carried before being charged to obey;
being God's possession is presented through rescue and protection, not merely through a claim
of ownership. See [[Redemption]], [[Call and Election]], and [[Covenant]].
