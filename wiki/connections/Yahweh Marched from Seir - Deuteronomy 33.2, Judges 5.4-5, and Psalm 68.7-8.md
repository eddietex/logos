# Yahweh Marched from Seir - Deuteronomy 33.2, Judges 5.4-5, and Psalm 68.7-8

## Passages involved

- **Deuteronomy 33:2** — [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]]: *"Yahweh came from Sinai, and rose from Seir to
  them. He shone from Mount Paran. He came from the ten thousands of holy ones. At his right hand was a
  fiery law for them."*
- **Judges 5:4–5** — [[The Song of Deborah]]: *"Yahweh, when you went out of Seir, when you marched out
  of the field of Edom, the earth trembled, the sky also dropped. Yes, the clouds dropped water. The
  mountains quaked Yahweh's presence, even Sinai at the presence of Yahweh, the God of Israel."*
- **Psalm 68:7–8** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"God, when you went out
  before your people, when you marched through the wilderness... Selah. The earth trembled. The sky also
  poured down rain at the presence of the God of Sinai— at the presence of God, the God of Israel."*
- Compare **Habakkuk 3:3** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked)* — *"God came from Teman, the Holy One
  from Mount Paran. Selah. His glory covered the heavens, and his praise filled the earth."*

## Analysis

**One picture in four poems.** Each describes Yahweh coming toward his people from the south — Sinai,
Seir, Edom, Paran, Teman — and the world shaking as he comes. None of them is a travel report. Each
uses the coming as the start of something else: Moses' blessing of the tribes, Deborah's victory song,
the psalm's procession, Habakkuk's prayer.

**Judges 5 and Psalm 68 share their wording.** In the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures
morphhb), Judges 5:4–5 and Psalm 68:8–9 (Hebrew numbering; WEB 68:7–8) have in common:

| | Judges 5:4–5 | Psalm 68:8–9 (Heb.) |
|---|---|---|
| *when you went out* | *bəṣēʾtəkā miśśēʿîr*, *out of Seir* | *bəṣēʾtəkā lipnê ʿammekā*, *before your people* |
| *when you marched* | *bəṣaʿdəkā miśśədēh ʾĕdôm*, *out of the field of Edom* | *bəṣaʿdəkā bîšîmôn*, *through the wilderness* |
| *the earth trembled* | *ʾereṣ rāʿāšâ* | *ʾereṣ rāʿāšâ* |
| *the sky dropped* | *gam šāmayim nāṭāpû* | *ʾap šāmayim nāṭəpû* |
| *this Sinai* | *zeh sînay mippənê yhwh* | *mippənê ʾĕlōhîm zeh sînay* |
| the divine name | *yhwh* | *ʾĕlōhîm* |

The psalm keeps the verbs and changes the places: not Seir and Edom, but *before your people* and *the
wilderness*. Judges names the place Yahweh came from; the psalm names the people he went before. Which
text is older, or whether both draw on a common hymn, the texts do not say; the wiki does not decide.

**Deuteronomy 33:2 shares the places, not the verbs.** Moses' blessing has *missînay bāʾ wəzāraḥ
miśśēʿîr*, *came from Sinai, and rose from Seir*, and *hôpîaʿ mēhar pāʾrān*, *shone from Mount Paran*
(WLC). Its verbs are of light: *rose*, *shone*. Judges' are of marching and storm. Both pair Sinai and
Seir.

**Why this matters in Judges.** The battle of Judges 4–5 was fought in the north, at Taanach and the
Kishon. The Song starts it in the far south, at Seir, with Yahweh setting out. The rain that *dropped
water* (5:4) may be the water that filled the Kishon (5:21); the Song does not join them, but it puts
the storm first and the swollen river after. The god of Sinai comes to the valley of Jezreel. See
[[Kishon]].

**The places.** Seir and *the field of Edom* are the same region (see [[Edom]], whose page records the
names). Sinai is filed under [[Horeb]]. Paran and Teman have their own pages, [[Paran]] and [[Teman]].

## Significance

Israel's poetry remembered Yahweh as a god who comes from the south, from the mountain of the law, to
fight for his people elsewhere. In Judges 5 that memory is the Song's first image of the battle, before
any tribe is named. In Deuteronomy 33 it is the start of a blessing. See [[The Glory of God]],
[[The Song of Deborah]], and [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]].
