# Grew Fat and Kicked - Deuteronomy 32.15 and 1 Samuel 2.29

## Passages involved

- [[The Song of Moses]] — Deuteronomy 32:15; *But Jeshurun grew fat, and kicked. You have grown fat. You
  have grown thick. You have become sleek. Then he abandoned God who made him, and rejected the Rock of
  his salvation.*
- [[A Man of God Against the House of Eli]] — 1 Samuel 2:29; *Why do you kick at my sacrifice and at my
  offering, which I have commanded in my habitation, and honor your sons above me, to make yourselves fat
  with the best of all the offerings of Israel my people?*

(Both stored WEB.)

## Analysis

**One verb, two verses.** *Bāʿaṭ*, *kick*, occurs in the Westminster Leningrad Codex files of Genesis
through 1 Kings only at Deuteronomy 32:15 (*wayyiḇʿāṭ*) and 1 Samuel 2:29 (*tiḇʿăṭû*) (WLC, OpenScriptures
morphhb, searched 2026-11-17). In both, the kick comes with fat. The Song of Moses heaps up the fattening —
*šāmēn*, *ʿāḇâ*, *kāśâ*, *grew fat … thick … sleek* — and then the kick and the abandonment. The man of
God's charge sets the kick first and the fattening after: *to make yourselves fat*, *ləhaḇrîʾăḵem*, the
Hiphil of *bārāʾ*, whose lemma occurs in those files only in this verse (WLC).

**From a nation to a priesthood.** Deuteronomy's *Jeshurun* is Israel, fed by Yahweh in the land and
turning on him. In 1 Samuel the ones who grow fat are the priests, and what they fatten on is *the best
of all the offerings of Israel my people*: the food Israel brings to Yahweh. The nation's sin in the song
is relocated to the altar.

**Why the echo is likely heard.** Hannah's song, one pericope earlier, already borrowed from the Song of
Moses: the Rock (Deuteronomy 32:4; 1 Samuel 2:2) and *Yahweh kills, and makes alive* (32:39; 2:6). See
[[No Rock Like Our God - The Song of Moses and the Song of Hannah]]. The kick at 2:29 is a third contact
with the same poem in the same chapter. 1 Samuel does not cite Deuteronomy, and the link is a reader's,
made on the rarity of the verb.

## Significance

- The image explains the charge in [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]]: fat that should have been burned for
  Yahweh first (2:15–16) ends up on the priests. Eli himself dies *an old man, and heavy* (4:18, stored WEB;
  not yet ingested); the text does not draw the connection.
