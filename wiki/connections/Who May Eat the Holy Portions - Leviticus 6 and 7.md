# Who May Eat the Holy Portions - Leviticus 6 and 7

- [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]] — Leviticus 6:24–7:10.
- [[Thanksgiving, Vows, and the Peace Offering]] — Leviticus 7:11–21.

- [[Fat, Blood, and the Priests’ Share]] — Leviticus 7:22–38.
- [[The Uneaten Sin Offering]] — Leviticus 10:12–20.
- [[Eating the Holy Things]] — Leviticus 22:1–16.

## Analysis

Leviticus 6:26, 29 and 7:6 reserve sin- and trespass-offering meat for priests in a
holy place. Leviticus 6:30 excludes eating altogether when sin-offering blood enters
the tent for atonement. Leviticus 7:19, by contrast, permits everyone clean to eat the
peace-offering meat, subject to the timing and contamination rules of 7:15–21.

**The apparent tension is a difference of offerings:** the broader permission does
not make priestly portions available to everyone. Equally, the sanctifying contact
statement about sin-offering flesh in 6:27 cannot be made a universal claim that sacred
meat removes impurity. Peace-offering flesh that touches an unclean thing must burn;
an unclean person who eats it is subject to being cut off (7:19–21). The text distinguishes
consecrated contact, contaminated food, and an eater's eligibility without explaining
a single mechanism for all three.

Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 2; 6–7. No source-language assertion is made.

### Reserved portions within the shared meal

Leviticus 7:31–34 assigns the breast to Aaron and his sons and the right thigh to the
son who offers blood and fat. The permission for every clean participant in 7:19 does
not cancel these allocations. **The apparent tension is resolved by distinguishing
eligibility to eat from entitlement to a particular portion.** Fat and blood remain
excluded from food even where other parts are lawfully shared (23–27).

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 7:19–38.

### Daughters and the clean place — Leviticus 10:12–15

Leviticus 10:14 names Aaron's daughters with his sons as eaters of the waved breast and heaved
thigh, "in a clean place". This adds eaters to the "Aaron's and his sons'" of 7:31 rather than
contradicting it, and it does not extend to the most holy meal offering, which is assigned to
Aaron and his sons in a holy place (10:12–13), nor to the sin and trespass flesh reserved for
"every male among the priests" (6:29; 7:6). The grading by place and eater is stated; the reason
is not. Leviticus 10:16–20 then shows the sin-offering rule enforced; see
[[Eaten or Burned - The Sin Offering in Leviticus 6, 9, and 10]].

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:29; 7:6, 31–34; 10:12–20.

### The priest's household and the unclean eater — Leviticus 22:1–16

Leviticus 22:4–7 applies to priests the rule 7:20–21 set for peace-offering eaters: an unclean
descendant of Aaron may not eat the holy things until he is clean, and the lesser cases end at
sunset after bathing. Verses 10–13 then define the priestly table by household. A slave the
priest has bought and those born in his house eat; a stranger, a foreigner living with the
priests, and a hired servant do not. A daughter married to an outsider may not eat the heave
offering, but a childless widowed or divorced daughter returned to her father's house may.

**This narrows 10:14 rather than contradicting it.** Leviticus 10:14 includes Aaron's daughters
at the breast and thigh; 22:12–13 says marriage outside the priestly household ends that
eligibility and specifies when it returns. Neither chapter extends the most holy portions
reserved for "every male among the priests" (6:29; 7:6) to slaves or daughters, and 22:10–13
does not say which grade of holy food it covers beyond "the holy thing" and "the heave offering".
That gap is left open.

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:29; 7:6, 20–21; 10:14; 22:1–16.

## Significance

Sacrificial sharing remains governed by the kind of offering and the condition of
its participants. Sacred ownership is compatible with human eating, but does not
make all eating acceptable.
