# From the Well Song to the Heshbon Poem

## Passages involved
- [[Sihon and Og]] — Numbers 21:21–35
- [[The Journey to Moab]] — Numbers 21:10–20

## Analysis

The well song follows Yahweh’s promise of water and is explicitly introduced as Israel’s singing
(21:16–18). The Heshbon poem is introduced as what speakers of proverbs say (21:27), after the
narrator explains Sihon’s earlier conquest of Moab (26). Its celebration of Heshbon and lament for
Moab is now embedded in a report that Heshbon belongs to Israel. The first-person plural in verse 30
is not assigned a named speaker in the stored WEB; an exact reconstruction of poetic layers would
require evidence beyond this account.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers, the references above. No source-language claim is made.

## Significance

The two neighboring poems preserve different kinds of speech. Poetry cited by the narrator is not
automatically worship addressed to Yahweh, and the poem’s claims about Chemosh must retain their
quoted setting.
