# No City Made Peace - Deuteronomy 20 and Joshua 11.19-20

## Passages involved
- [[Laws for Going to War]] — Deuteronomy 20:10–18.
- [[A People Chosen in Love]] — Deuteronomy 7:1–2.
- [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]] — Deuteronomy 2:30.
- [[The Gibeonite Deception]] — Joshua 9:3–15.
- [[The Whole Land Taken]] — Joshua 11:19–20.

## Analysis

**The law has two categories.** Deuteronomy 20:10–11 commands Israel to *proclaim peace* to a
city before fighting it; a city that answers with peace becomes forced labor. Verses 15–17 limit
that to *the cities which are very far off from you, which are not of the cities of these
nations*. For the cities of the land, *you shall save alive nothing that breathes*, and the list
of peoples includes the Hivite (17). Deuteronomy 7:2 says the same of the seven nations: *You shall
make no covenant with them, nor show mercy to them.*

**Gibeon crossed the line by pretending to be far away.** The Gibeonites' worn sacks and moldy
bread in Joshua 9 make sense only against this law. Israel's own question at 9:7 — *What if you
live among us? How could we make a covenant with you?* — shows the category was understood. The
oath stood once sworn. See [[No Covenant Yet a Binding Oath - Deuteronomy and Gibeon]] and
[[Rahab and Gibeon - Two Oaths Preserving Life]].

**Joshua 11:19–20 reports the result.** *There was not a city that made peace with the children
of Israel, except the Hivites, the inhabitants of Gibeon.* The narrator does not say that the
cities were offered peace and refused, nor that a city of the land could have received it.
Instead verse 20 gives a theological cause: *it was of Yahweh to harden their hearts, to come
against Israel in battle, that he might utterly destroy them, that they might have no favor*.
The cities' choice to fight and Yahweh's hardening are both asserted.

**Two readings, both left open.** One reading takes verse 20 as explaining why the law's
second category was carried out without exception: the cities came out to battle, and so fell
under Deuteronomy 20:16–17. Another notices that verse 19 frames the outcome in terms of *making
peace*, which may imply that a city of the land seeking peace was conceivable — as Rahab's
household and Gibeon, by different routes, did survive. The text does not decide between these,
and the wiki does not.

**Sihon as precedent.** Deuteronomy 2:30 already joins a king's refusal with divine hardening:
*Yahweh your God hardened his spirit, and made his heart obstinate, that he might deliver him into
your hand*. Sihon too was first offered *words of peace* (2:26), which places him on the law's
"far off" side. Joshua 11:20 extends the pattern to the kings west of the Jordan. See
[[Pharaoh and Sihon - Hardened Hearts and Defeated Kings]].

Whether Joshua 11:20's *no favor* and Deuteronomy 7:2's *nor show mercy* share a Hebrew root was
not checked in this pass; the link drawn here is thematic, on the stored WEB.

## Significance

The summary in Joshua 11 is the narrative counterpart of the war law in Deuteronomy 20: the one
exception it records is the one city that obtained peace by impersonating the law's other
category. It also sharpens the ethical question rather than resolving it, since the text names
Yahweh's hardening as the reason the cities fought and were destroyed without favor. See
[[Hardening of the Heart]], [[War]], and [[The Nations]].
