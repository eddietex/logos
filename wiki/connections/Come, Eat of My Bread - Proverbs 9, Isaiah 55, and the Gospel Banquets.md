# Come, Eat of My Bread - Proverbs 9, Isaiah 55, and the Gospel Banquets

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 9:1–6** — Wisdom builds her house, prepares meat and mixed wine, sends out her maidens,
  and calls the simple: *"Come, eat some of my bread, Drink some of the wine which I have mixed! Leave
  your simple ways, and live."* — [[The Two Feasts]]
- **Proverbs 9:13–18** — the foolish woman's counter-invitation, *"Stolen water is sweet. Food eaten in
  secret is pleasant"*, and her guests in Sheol — [[The Two Feasts]]
- **Proverbs 4:17** — the wicked *"eat the bread of wickedness, and drink the wine of violence"* —
  [[The Two Paths]]
- **Isaiah 25:6** — *"Yahweh of Armies will make all peoples a feast of choice meat, a feast of choice
  wines"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Isaiah 55:1–3** — *"Come, everyone who thirsts, to the waters! Come, he who has no money, buy, and
  eat! Yes, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price … Hear, and your soul will live"*
  (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Luke 14:16–24** — the great supper: *"He sent out his servant at supper time to tell those who
  were invited, 'Come, for everything is ready now'"*; then *"Go out quickly into the streets and lanes
  of the city"* (WEB, 14:17, 21; *not yet ingested*)
- **Matthew 22:1–14** — the wedding feast: the king *"sent out his servants"*; *"Behold, I have prepared
  my dinner. My cattle and my fatlings are killed, and all things are ready"* (WEB, 22:3–4; *not yet
  ingested*)
- **John 6:35** — *"I am the bread of life. He who comes to me will not be hungry"* (WEB; *not yet
  ingested*)
- **Matthew 11:19** — *"The Son of Man came eating and drinking … But wisdom is justified by her
  children"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**One scene, recurring: a host who has prepared a meal sends out an invitation, and the meal means
life.** Proverbs 9 gives it its fullest Old Testament form, and the later texts repeat its elements.
None of them quotes Proverbs 9, and the wiki does not claim that any depends on it.

| | Proverbs 9:1–6 | Isaiah 55:1–3 | Luke 14 / Matthew 22 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the meal** | *"her meat … her wine … her table"* (9:2) | *"wine and milk"*, *"that which is good"* (55:1–2) | *"a great supper"* (Luke 14:16); *"my cattle and my fatlings are killed"* (Matthew 22:4) |
| **ready** | four perfects: slaughtered, mixed, set (9:2) | — | *"everything is ready now"* (Luke 14:17); *"all things are ready"* (Matthew 22:4) |
| **messengers** | *"She has sent out her maidens"* (9:3) | the prophet's own voice | *"He sent out his servant"* (Luke 14:17); *"sent out his servants"* (Matthew 22:3) |
| **where** | *"the highest places of the city"* (9:3) | — | *"the streets and lanes of the city"* (Luke 14:21) |
| **guests** | the simple, *"void of understanding"* (9:4) | *"he who has no money"* (55:1) | *"the poor, maimed, blind, and lame"* (Luke 14:21) |
| **the call** | *"Come, eat"* (9:5) | *"Come, buy, and eat"* (55:1) | *"Come"* (Luke 14:17; Matthew 22:4) |
| **the gift** | *"and live"* (9:6) | *"your soul will live"* (55:3) | a place at the feast |

**The guests are those who lack something.** Wisdom calls the simple and the one *lacking heart*;
Isaiah calls the thirsty and those without money; the parables end with the poor and the lame. The
meal is offered to the people who cannot provide it for themselves.

**A counterfeit invitation stands beside the true one.** Proverbs alone sets a second hostess beside
the first, with the same words and a different table (9:16–17), and the Hebrew of 9:16 repeats 9:4
(Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*). Proverbs 4:17 already had the wicked eating *"the bread
of wickedness"*. Isaiah 55:2 asks the same question in its own terms: *"Why do you spend money for that
which is not bread?"* In both, people are hungry, and what matters is which bread they take.

**The New Testament puts the invitation in Jesus' mouth.** In John 6:35 he is himself the bread; in
Matthew 11:19 his eating and drinking with sinners is defended by *"wisdom is justified by her
children"*, a saying that names wisdom as the one vindicated. The wiki has traced elsewhere how the
Gospels give Jesus the words of personified Wisdom (see
[[Wisdom Sends Prophets - Proverbs 1.20-33, Luke 11.49, and Matthew 23.34-37]] and
[[Possessed in the Beginning - Proverbs 8.22-31, John 1, and Colossians 1]]). Whether Proverbs 9 lies
behind the banquet parables is a question of interpretation; the resemblance is in the shape of the
scene, not in quoted words.

## Significance

Proverbs 9 closes the book's prologue by turning everything the father has taught into a meal one
accepts or refuses. The later texts keep the shape — prepared food, a sent invitation, guests who have
nothing, life as the result — and extend it to the whole people (Isaiah 25:6) and to the kingdom (the
parables). Read together, they make the invitation to wisdom an invitation to eat. See [[Wisdom]],
[[Hospitality]], [[Life and Death]], and [[Bread from Heaven - The Manna and John 6]].
