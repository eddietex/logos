# God Do So to Me - The Self-Curse Oath in Ruth and 1 Samuel

## Passages involved

- **Ruth 1:17** — [[Ruth Clings to Naomi]]: *"May Yahweh do so to me, and more also, if anything but death
  parts you and me."* (*kōh yaʿăśeh YHWH lî wəḵōh yōsîp*)
- **1 Samuel 3:17** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — Eli to the boy Samuel: *"God do
  so to you, and more also, if you hide anything from me."*
- [[Saul's Rash Oath]] — **1 Samuel 14:44** (stored WEB) — Saul: *"God do so and more also; for you shall
  surely die, Jonathan."*
- [[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]] — **1 Samuel 20:13** (stored WEB) — Jonathan to David: *"Yahweh do so to
  Jonathan, and more also, should it please my father to do you evil, if I don't disclose it to you."*
- **1 Samuel 25:22** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked)* — David, of Nabal's house: *"God do so to the
  enemies of David, and more also, if I leave of all that belongs to him by the morning light …"*

## Analysis

**The form.** *Kōh yaʿăśeh … wəḵōh yōsîp*, *so may he do … and so may he add*. The speaker calls on God to
act against someone, usually the speaker, if the condition that follows is broken or not met. The penalty
is never named; *so* stands in for it. It is a self-curse, a stronger form than a simple *As Yahweh lives*.
In the Westminster Leningrad Codex files the wiki has read (Genesis, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1
Samuel), the full formula with *wəḵōh yōsîp* occurs in these five verses only. Ruth 1:17 is the earliest of
them in canonical order.

| | Speaker | Divine name | Against whom | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ruth 1:17 | Ruth | *YHWH* | herself (*lî*, *to me*) | if anything but death parts her from Naomi |
| 1 Samuel 3:17 | Eli | *ʾĕlōhîm* | Samuel (*ləḵā*, *to you*) | if Samuel hides Yahweh's word |
| 1 Samuel 14:44 | Saul | *ʾĕlōhîm* | (no object) | Jonathan *shall surely die* |
| 1 Samuel 20:13 | Jonathan | *YHWH* | himself, by name | if he does not warn David |
| 1 Samuel 25:22 | David | *ʾĕlōhîm* | *the enemies of David* | if he leaves one male of Nabal's house |

**Ruth is the only woman.** The other four speakers are a priest, a king, a king's son, and a future king.
Ruth is a Moabite widow, and she takes the form in Israel's God's personal name. Only she and Jonathan use
*YHWH*; the others use *ʾĕlōhîm*. Both use the oath to bind themselves to someone they love: 1 Samuel 20:17 says Jonathan loved David *as
he loved his own soul* (WEB checked at bible-api.com).

**Kept, met, and overruled.** Ruth keeps hers (1:18; the rest of the book). Eli's condition is met: *Samuel
told him every bit, and hid nothing from him* (1 Samuel 3:18). Saul's sentence is overruled by the people:
*So the people rescued Jonathan, that he didn't die* (14:45). David's vow is stopped by Abigail, whom he
blesses for keeping him *from blood guiltiness* (25:32–33). Jonathan's is bound up with his covenant with
David (20:12–17; [[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]]), and he keeps it at the cost of his father's spear (20:33)
before sending David away in peace (20:35–42). The five oaths are not one story; the table only sets the form side by
side.

**Wording.** David's form in 25:22, *to the enemies of David*, is generally read as a scribal softening of
*to David* (a common explanation, reported, not verified). The wiki notes it and does not build on it.

## Significance

The self-curse oath in these books is a speech of commitment under pressure. Ruth's is the first, the only
one spoken by a woman or a foreigner, and the only one whose condition is death itself: she names the one
thing that may part her from Naomi. See [[Oaths and Vows]], [[Ruth (person)]], and [[The Nations]].
