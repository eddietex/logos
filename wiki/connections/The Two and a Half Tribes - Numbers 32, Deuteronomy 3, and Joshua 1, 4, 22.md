## Analysis

### The altar dispute now ingested — Joshua 22:10–34

The agreement's first test comes after it is discharged. The eastern tribes build an altar by
the Jordan, and the western tribes muster at Shiloh for war (10–12). A delegation under Phinehas
calls the altar rebellion and offers the eastern tribes land *among us* if theirs is *unclean*
(19). The eastern tribes answer that the altar is a witness and not for sacrifice. They built it
so their children would not be told *You have no portion in Yahweh*, with the Jordan as the
border (24–28). The answer is accepted and war is called off (30–33). The question Moses raised
in Numbers 32, whether two tribes who stay east are still with their brothers, comes back as a
question about worship rather than military service. In this unit the eastern tribes' loyalty is
affirmed. Joshua 22:2–3's commendation therefore stands, and the altar episode does not overturn
it.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:10–34.

### Discharge now ingested — Joshua 22:1–9

Joshua explicitly commends the tribes’ kept obligation (2–3), then makes the brothers’
rest the reason for return (4). Love and service remain binding (5), and plunder is to be
shared (8). The recipients are called brothers without a stated fraction or an explicit
identification of those who stayed home. Verse 7 distinguishes Manasseh’s two grants.
The following altar dispute does not overturn the praise given in this unit.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:1–9.

### The eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

Joshua 13:8–33 now supplies the detailed eastern register. Moses’ grants are recalled by
families: Reuben (15–23), Gad (24–28), and half of Manasseh (29–31). This is an allocation
record, not the discharge of the warriors promised in Numbers 32. The register acknowledges
Geshur and Maacath remaining within Israel (13) and brackets the tribal lists with Levi’s
different inheritance (14, 33).

Dibon is built by Gad in Numbers 32:34 but included with Reuben in Joshua 13:17. The two
activities are distinguishable, yet a later transfer is not narrated. The same issue cannot
simply be asserted for Aroer: Joshua names an Arnon-valley Aroer and one near Rabbah, whereas
Numbers 32:34 does not specify which Gad built. The Ammon wording has its own unresolved
territorial tension; see [[Ammon Protected and Ammonite Land Allotted]].

Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33 and the Torah passages cited.

### The completed crossing — Joshua 4

Joshua 4:12–13 is now ingested: Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh cross armed as Moses commanded, about forty thousand ready for battle. Joshua 1’s undertaking becomes action. Crossing begins fulfillment; [[The Eastern Tribes Sent Home]] now records the discharge in Joshua 22:1–9.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 3–4.

Joshua 1:10–18 is now ingested. Joshua's reminder reproduces Deuteronomy 3:18–20 rather than
Numbers 32: verse 15's *until Yahweh has given your brothers rest, as he has given you, and they
have also possessed the land* matches Deuteronomy 3:20 word for word apart from a longer pronoun
(MAM via Sefaria). He opens with rest rather than land (13), uses *ḥămušîm* for *armed* where
Deuteronomy 3:18 has *ḥălûṣîm* and Numbers 32:17 *ḥušîm* (MAM via Sefaria), and adds *shall help
them* (14). The reply (16–18) goes beyond the terms: obedience to Joshua *as … to Moses*, and death
for anyone who rebels against his word. The text does not name the speakers of 16–18; the wiki
reads them as the eastern tribes. See [[The Eastern Tribes Pledge to Cross]] and
[[Rest from All Your Enemies - Deuteronomy 12, Joshua 1, 21-23, and Hebrews 4]].

Deuteronomy 3:12–22 is now ingested. The grant includes half of Manasseh before the command to
cross (13–18); the retelling omits Numbers 32’s dispute and negotiated proposal. Verse 20 makes
the brothers’ rest the endpoint of service. Verses 21–22 then ground Joshua’s confidence in the
two kings’ defeat. This supplements, rather than duplicates, the earlier negotiation.

Numbers 34:13–15 makes the eastern grant the explicit reason that only nine and a half tribes
receive this Canaan allotment. The western officials include Hanniel for Manasseh (23), but
none for Reuben or Gad. This recognizes the grant; it does not report the military obligation
of Numbers 32 already discharged.

**One agreement, followed through four books.** Numbers 32 makes a conditional grant: Reuben and Gad,
and in the end half of Manasseh, may settle east of the Jordan if their armed men cross *before Yahweh*
and stay *until the children of Israel have each inherited their inheritance* (32:18). Few arrangements in
the Torah are tracked so explicitly to their discharge.

| Stage | Text | What is said |
|---|---|---|
| Request and condition | Numbers 32:1–32 | Moses: *Shall your brothers go to the war, and shall you sit here?* (6); the tribes promise to go armed, and Moses binds it *before Yahweh* (20–24). |
| Witnesses | Numbers 32:28–30 | Moses commands Eleazar, Joshua, and the heads of the tribes, because he will not cross. |
| Grant | Numbers 32:33 | Sihon's and Og's kingdoms to Gad, Reuben, *and to the half-tribe of Manasseh*. |
| Retelling | Deuteronomy 3:18–20 | *You shall pass over armed before your brothers … until Yahweh gives rest to your brothers, as to you.* |
| Reminder | Joshua 1:12–15 | *Remember the word which Moses the servant of Yahweh commanded you*; the reply, *All that you have commanded us we will do* (1:16). |
| Crossing | Joshua 4:12–13 | They *crossed over armed before the children of Israel, as Moses spoke to them* — *about forty thousand men*. |
| Discharge | Joshua 22:1–9 | *You have not left your brothers these many days to this day*; Joshua blesses them and sends them home from Shiloh with plunder to share. |

**The vocabulary shifts from Numbers to Deuteronomy and Joshua.** Numbers 32 speaks of the land being
*subdued before Yahweh* (22, 29). Deuteronomy 3:20 and Joshua 1:13–15 speak instead of *rest*: *until
Yahweh gives rest to your brothers, as to you*. Joshua 22:4 picks that word up: *Now Yahweh your God has
given rest to your brothers*. The east-bank tribes are described as already at rest, and their service
lasts until the others share it. The rest language is not in Numbers 32.

**What Numbers 32 does not say, and the later texts do.** Joshua 1:14 adds *all the mighty men of valor*
and *shall help them*; Joshua 4:13 gives the figure of forty thousand; Joshua 22:7 says that Moses had given
half of Manasseh *inheritance in Bashan* and Joshua gave the other half land west of the Jordan. Numbers 32
gives none of these details, and it never says the half-tribe of Manasseh agreed to the condition. The
later texts treat all two and a half tribes as bound by it.

**The tribes kept their word; the outcome was mixed.** Joshua 22:2–3 is an unqualified commendation. What
follows in Joshua 22:10–34 (now ingested; see [[The Altar of Witness by the Jordan]]) is the altar by the Jordan and a near
war between the eastern and western tribes, ended by inquiry rather than battle. Deborah's song asks *Why did you sit among the sheepfolds?* of Reuben and says
*Gilead lived beyond the Jordan* (Judges 5:16–17) — *sit*, the verb of Moses' question at Numbers 32:6
(English *sit* in both, WEB; the Hebrew of Judges 5 was not checked in this pass). 1 Chronicles 5:25–26
names the two and a half tribes as those carried away by Pul and Tilgath Pilneser *to this day*.
Different books, written for different purposes, give different verdicts on the east-bank tribes; the wiki
keeps them side by side.

## Significance

The thread shows a Torah promise followed to its fulfilment inside the narrative books, with Joshua
quoting Moses' terms back. It also answers the question Moses raised at 32:6–15: the two tribes did not
discourage Israel, and the generation that crossed is not left in the wilderness again. Their land, outside
what Numbers 34 calls Canaan, raises a question the later books keep returning to: whether a tribe on the
other bank is fully part of Israel. Joshua 22 is the first time that question is fought over. See
[[Land and Sojourning]] and [[Brotherhood]].
