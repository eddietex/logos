# The Reproach of Israel - Joshua 5.9, 1 Samuel 11.2, and 17.26

## Passages involved
- [[The Second Circumcision at Gilgal]] — **Joshua 5:9** — *Yahweh said to Joshua, "Today I have rolled away the
  reproach of Egypt from you." Therefore the name of that place was called Gilgal, to this day.* Stored WEB.
- [[Saul Rescues Jabesh Gilead]] — **1 Samuel 11:2** — *On this condition I will make it with you, that all your
  right eyes be gouged out. I will make this dishonor all Israel.* Stored WEB. The story ends at Gilgal (11:14–15).
- **1 Samuel 17:26** — *What shall be done to the man who kills this Philistine, and takes away the reproach from
  Israel?* Stored WEB; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

**The word.** *Ḥerpâ*, *reproach, disgrace* (lemma 2781). In the WLC files of Genesis through 2 Samuel it occurs
seven times: Rachel's *God has taken away my reproach* (Genesis 30:23); Jacob's sons on giving their sister to an
uncircumcised man, *for that is a reproach to us* (Genesis 34:14); Joshua 5:9; 1 Samuel 11:2; 17:26; David's
*the cause of my reproach* against Nabal (1 Samuel 25:39); and Tamar's plea to Amnon (2 Samuel 13:13).
Only three of these speak of a reproach on Israel as a people: Joshua 5:9, 1 Samuel 11:2, and 17:26.

**The three.** At Gilgal the reproach is *of Egypt*, *ḥerpaṯ miṣrayim* (WLC), and Yahweh removes it after the
circumcision of the generation born in the wilderness; the place is named for the act, *gallôṯî*, *I have rolled
away*. Nahash promises to *put*, *śîm*, a reproach on all Israel (WLC). David asks about the man who *takes away*,
*hēsîr*, the reproach from Israel (WLC), and the reproach is Goliath's defiance. In two of the three the reproach
is tied to the uncircumcised: Israel's own wilderness generation at Gilgal (Joshua 5:7), and *this
uncircumcised Philistine* (1 Samuel 17:26). Nahash's threat is tied to the body in another way, a mutilation.

**Gilgal.** The 1 Samuel 11 episode begins with the threat of reproach and ends at Gilgal, where *they made Saul
king before Yahweh* (11:15). 1 Samuel does not mention Joshua 5:9 or the meaning of the name. The wiki records
the shared word and the shared place and does not claim the narrator intends the echo.

**Who removes it.** At Gilgal Yahweh rolls the reproach away. In 1 Samuel 11 the reproach never falls, and Saul
gives the rescue to Yahweh (11:13). In 1 Samuel 17 David asks what will be done for *the man* who takes it
away. The three texts place the removal differently, and the wiki leaves the comparison there.

## Significance

The threat Israel's first king turns back is named with the same word as the disgrace removed at Israel's first
camp in the land and the defiance that brings David forward. *Reproach* on the people as a whole is in each case
answered by Yahweh or by the one he raises. See [[Gilgal]], [[Saul]], [[Nahash (king of Ammon)]], and
[[Kingship and Empire]].
