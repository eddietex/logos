# Fire and Sulfur from the Sky

## Passages involved

- Genesis 19:24–25 — the first occurrence, and the source of everything below
- Deuteronomy 29:23 — *"brimstone, salt, and burning"*, the covenant curse
- Job 18:15; Psalm 11:6; Isaiah 30:33; 34:9–10; Ezekiel 38:22 — the prophetic and wisdom uses
- Luke 17:29 — the one New Testament narrative use, in the discourse on the day of the Son of Man
- Revelation 9:17–18; 14:10; 19:20; 20:10; 21:8 — five uses, all eschatological

## Analysis

> **24** Then Yahweh rained on Sodom and on Gomorrah sulfur and fire from Yahweh out of the sky.

***Gopriṯ wā-ʾēš*, sulfur and fire, occurs here for the first time in Scripture**, and after this it
is never a neutral description. It becomes a fixed pair, and the pair carries Sodom with it wherever
it goes: every subsequent user of the phrase is invoking this chapter, whether or not the city is
named.

**The elements are the region's own.** *Gopriṯ* is sulfur or bitumen — the Dead Sea rift has bitumen
seeps that Genesis 14:10 already mentioned as *"tar pits"*, and sulfur deposits in the marl. The
image is not arbitrary; it is what the place is made of, set on fire and falling from above rather
than lying in the ground. That is the whole grammar of the verse: **the ordinary materials of the
plain, delivered from the wrong direction.**

### The chain

| Text | Use |
|---|---|
| **Genesis 19:24** | narrative: what fell on Sodom |
| **Deuteronomy 29:23** | covenant curse — the land *"is sulfur, salt, and burning … like the overthrow of Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboiim"*, held up as what Israel's own land could become |
| **Job 18:15** | Bildad on the wicked man's tent: *"sulfur is scattered on his habitation"* — see [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]] |
| **Psalm 11:6** | *"On the wicked he will rain blazing coals; fire, sulfur, and scorching wind"* — the verb *himṭîr*, rain, is Genesis 19:24's |
| **Isaiah 30:33** | Topheth prepared, kindled by *"Yahweh's breath, like a stream of sulfur"* |
| **Isaiah 34:9–10** | Edom's streams turned to pitch and its dust to sulfur, *"its smoke will go up forever"* |
| **Ezekiel 38:22** | Gog judged with *"rain, great hailstones, fire, and sulfur"* |
| **Luke 17:29** | *"it rained fire and sulfur from the sky and destroyed them all"* — the Gospels' one retelling, and it is Genesis 19:24 nearly word for word |
| **Revelation 9:17–18** | the plague from the horses' mouths: fire, smoke, and sulfur |
| **Revelation 14:10; 19:20; 20:10; 21:8** | the lake of fire *"burning with sulfur"*, four times |

**Two verbs travel with the pair.** *Himṭîr*, to rain — which is the flood's verb too (Genesis 7:4)
and makes fire the counterpart of water — and *hāpak*, to overthrow, which produces *mahpēkâ*, the
noun the prophets use as this event's proper name (Deuteronomy 29:23; Isaiah 1:7; 13:19; Jeremiah
49:18; 50:40; Amos 4:11).

### Where the smoke goes

Genesis 19:28 has Abraham see *"the smoke of the land went up as the smoke of a furnace"*.
*Kibšān*, furnace, is a rare word: it appears at Exodus 9:8, 10 (the ashes Moses throws up for the
boils), Exodus 19:18 (Sinai smoking *"like the smoke of a furnace"*), and here. Isaiah 34:10 and
Revelation 14:11 both pick up the going-up smoke and make it perpetual — *"the smoke of their
torment goes up forever and ever"* — so the image outlives the chemistry.

## Significance

**This is the clearest case in Genesis of a narrative detail becoming a permanent vocabulary.** The
flood supplied a story that later texts retell; Sodom supplied a *phrase*, and the phrase does work
in books that never mention the city. When Revelation says *"the lake that burns with fire and
sulfur"*, it is not describing a chemical composition — it is citing Genesis 19:24, and every reader
in its tradition would hear the citation.

The wiki notes one asymmetry worth keeping. **In Genesis the fire is temporal and local**: it falls
on two named towns in a valley, on one morning, and Zoar four miles away is untouched. In
Revelation the same pair is unbounded and unending. The trajectory from the one to the other runs
through Isaiah 34:10's *"forever"* and Jude 7's *"eternal fire"*, and the wiki records it as a
development in the tradition rather than as something latent in Genesis 19, which says only that the
smoke went up like a furnace's and that Abraham then walked home.
