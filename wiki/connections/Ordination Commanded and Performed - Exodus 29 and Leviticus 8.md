## Analysis

**Leviticus 8 follows Exodus 29 step by step.** Both take a bull, two rams, and a basket of
unleavened bread (Exodus 29:1–3; Leviticus 8:2). Both have Moses wash Aaron and his sons, clothe and
anoint Aaron, and clothe the sons (Exodus 29:4–9; Leviticus 8:6–13). Both then offer the bull as a
sin offering with its remains burned outside the camp, the first ram as a whole burnt offering,
and the second ram as the ram of consecration (Exodus 29:10–25; Leviticus 8:14–28). Blood on the
right ear, thumb, and big toe, the wave offering placed in the priests' hands, the breast as Moses'
portion, the meal at the tent's door, and the seven days all reappear (Leviticus 8:23–35).

**Leviticus marks the obedience repeatedly.** The phrase *"as Yahweh commanded Moses"* closes
several steps (Leviticus 8:9, 13, 17, 21, 29), and the chapter ends: *"Aaron and his sons did all the
things which Yahweh commanded by Moses"* (8:36). See
[[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

**The performance adds what the command leaves elsewhere.** Leviticus 8:3–5 assembles *"all the
congregation"* at the tent's door; Exodus 29 names no audience. Leviticus 8:8 puts the Urim and
the Thummim in the breastplate, and 8:10–11 has Moses anoint the tabernacle, the altar seven times,
and the basin. Exodus commands those anointings in 30:26–29 and 40:9–11 rather than in chapter 29.
Leviticus 8:33–35 adds that the priests must stay at the tent's door day and night for the seven
days *"that you don't die"*.

**The two accounts differ in small orders and labels.** Exodus 29:5 lists Aaron's coat, robe,
ephod, breastplate, and band, and 29:9 then speaks of belts for Aaron and his sons. Leviticus 8:7
ties the sash on Aaron while dressing him. The WEB's *"belts"* at Exodus 29:9 renders the same
Hebrew word (*ʾabnēṭ*, MAM) that it renders *"sash"* in Exodus 28. The ordinations are the same
rite reported for different purposes; no contradiction is claimed.

**Exodus' daily offering is absent from Leviticus 8.** The lambs of Exodus 29:38–42 belong to the
continuing service, and Leviticus 8 narrates only the installation. See
[[The Continual Burnt Offering - Exodus 29, Numbers 28, and Daniel 8]].

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40

Exodus 40:9–15 repeats the consecration commands for the sanctuary, its equipment,
and Aaron’s family. Verse 16 generally affirms Moses’ obedience. Yet 40:17–33 does not
retell the full seven-day rite prescribed in chapter 29. It names Moses as burning
incense and offering at the altar (40:27, 29), and explicitly reports hand-and-foot
washing by Moses, Aaron, and the sons (40:31–32). Thus the full ordination narrative
remains in Leviticus 8, as documented above, while an absolute claim that Exodus reports
no compliance would ignore 40:16. This is a difference in narrative detail, not grounds
to invent a contradiction or a precise unreported chronology.

### The consecration now ingested — Leviticus 8

The full chapter is now ingested in [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]], from stored WEB.
The broad correspondence described above does not mean an identical sequence in every detail.
**Narrative-order tension:** Exodus 29:21 commands sprinkling blood and oil on the people
and garments before the fat, thigh, bread, and breast directions (22–28). Leviticus 8:30
reports the sprinkling after those portions (25–29). Neither account explains this ordering
difference. The summary affirmation of obedience must not erase it or produce an invented
second sprinkling. The earlier phrase “step by step” describes the broad ritual progression.

Leviticus 8:15 expressly calls the bull’s blood a purification and atonement for the altar;
8:34 names atonement for the priests. The seven-day charge is commanded in 33–35 and obedience
summarized in 36, without seven separate daily narratives. Exodus 29:35–37 supplies explicit
daily altar-atonement instructions. Chapter 9 remains a separate, unchecked pericope.

## Significance

**The pattern of command and performance governs the tabernacle material.** Exodus 25–31 gives
instructions; Exodus 35–40 and Leviticus 8 report them carried out. The full seven-day rite prescribed in Exodus 29 is narrated in Leviticus 8.
Exodus 40:16 already affirms general compliance;
the detailed ordination narrative makes the priesthood a bridge between the two books.

**The ordination ends in the glory it was commanded for.** Exodus 29:43 promised that *"the place
shall be sanctified by my glory"*. Leviticus 9:23–24 narrates *"Yahweh's glory appeared to all the
people"* and fire coming out *"from before Yahweh"*. See [[Priesthood]], [[Aaron]], [[Moses]], and
[[The Glory of God]].
