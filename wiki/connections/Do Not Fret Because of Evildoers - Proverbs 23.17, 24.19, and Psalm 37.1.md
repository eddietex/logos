# Do Not Fret Because of Evildoers - Proverbs 23.17, 24.19, and Psalm 37.1

## Passages involved

- [[Do Not Withhold Good]] — **Proverbs 3:31** — *"Don't envy the man of violence. Choose none of
  his ways."*
- [[Do Not Envy Sinners]] — **Proverbs 23:17–18** — *"Don't let your heart envy sinners; but rather
  fear Yahweh all day long. Indeed surely there is a future hope, and your hope will not be cut
  off."*
- [[Do Not Envy Sinners]] — **Proverbs 24:1–2** — *"Don't be envious of evil men; neither desire to
  be with them: for their hearts plot violence, and their lips talk about mischief."*
- **Proverbs 24:19–20** — *"Don't fret yourself because of evildoers; neither be envious of the
  wicked: for there will be no reward to the evil man; and the lamp of the wicked shall be snuffed
  out."* — [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]]
- **Psalm 37:1–2** — *"Don't fret because of evildoers, neither be envious against those who work
  unrighteousness. For they shall soon be cut down like the grass, and wither like the green
  herb."* (WEB verified at bible-api.com; Psalms not yet in `raw/`)
- **Psalm 73:2–3, 16–17** — *"But as for me, my feet were almost gone. My steps had nearly slipped.
  For I was envious of the arrogant, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked."* … *"When I tried to
  understand this, it was too painful for me; Until I entered God's sanctuary, and considered their
  latter end."* (WEB verified at bible-api.com) — see [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]]
- [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]] — Proverbs 24:19–20.

## Analysis

**Four prohibitions in one book, and one in a psalm, all of the same desire.** Proverbs forbids
envy of the wicked four times (3:31; 23:17; 24:1; 24:19), and each time it supposes what Job and
Psalm 73 say outright: that the wicked can look worth envying. None of the four denies that they
prosper. What changes from one to the next is the reason given.

- **3:31–32** gives Yahweh's judgment: *"the perverse is an abomination to Yahweh"*. The danger it
  names is imitation — *"Choose none of his ways"*.
- **23:17–18** gives a future: *"there is a future hope"*. The danger it names is the heart's
  direction, and it supplies the alternative direction, *"fear Yahweh all day long"*.
- **24:1–2** gives the evil men's present character: their hearts plot violence. The danger it names
  is company — *"neither desire to be with them"*.
- **24:19–20** gives their end: *"no reward"*, the lamp snuffed out.

**24:19 and Psalm 37:1 share their opening words.** In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*
(read 2026-10-26), both verses begin *ʾal-titḥar bammərēʿîm ʾal-təqannēʾ* — *don't fret because of
evildoers, don't envy* — and then name the object of envy differently (Proverbs *bārəšāʿîm*, *the
wicked*; the Psalm *bəʿōśê ʿawlāh*, *those who work unrighteousness*). The WEB obscures the identity
slightly by rendering the shared clause *"Don't fret yourself"* in Proverbs and *"Don't fret"* in the
Psalm. The wiki does not decide which text, if either, borrowed from the other; Psalm 37 is headed
*lədāwid*, *of David* (same edition), and 24:19 stands in *"the words of the wise"*, which do not name an author.

**The reasons converge on the end.** Psalm 37:2 answers envy with time: *"they shall soon be cut
down like the grass"*. Proverbs 23:18 and 24:20 answer it with *ʾaḥărît*, the *latter end* (*"a
future"*, *"no reward"* in the WEB — one Hebrew noun, per the same edition). Psalm 73 arrives at the
same word by a harder road: the psalmist confesses the envy, finds the problem *"too painful"*, and is
released from it only when he *"considered their latter end"* (*ʾaḥărîtām*, 73:17). Proverbs states
as a premise what Psalm 73 reaches as a discovery.

**What stays in tension.** Job 21:7–13 has the wicked live out their days in prosperity and go down
to Sheol *"In an instant"* (21:13), untroubled — an end that is no worse than anyone else's (see
[[Why Do the Wicked Live]] and [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]]). Proverbs' appeal to the latter end
is therefore not a claim the wisdom literature leaves unexamined; the wiki records both, and does not
reconcile them here.

### 24:19–20 in its block

Proverbs 24:19–20 is now ingested. It follows directly on 24:17–18, the ban on rejoicing when an
enemy falls, so the block holds the two attitudes to the wicked side by side: no envy while they
stand, no gladness when they fall. Both are left to Yahweh.

## Significance

The prohibition is constant and the reasons accumulate. Taken together they make envy of the wicked
a question about what a person looks at: the visible present, or the end. That is why Proverbs sets
the fear of Yahweh, not contentment, as envy's replacement (23:17) — it is a way of looking at the
world that includes what has not happened yet.
