## Analysis

### Duties and portions — Numbers 18:1–32

Numbers 18:15–18 maintains firstborn redemption alongside the established service of
Levi. The chapter does not say that the earlier substitution abolished the household
obligation; neither does it narrate the transition between these arrangements.
The chronological reconciliation above remains an inference.

See [[The Duties and Portions of Priests and Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 18.

**The claim comes first, then the replacement.** Exodus 13:2 says of every firstborn, *It is mine*,
and Moses gives the reason at 13:15. Numbers 3:13 gives the same reason in God's own voice: *On the
day that I struck down all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I made holy to me all the firstborn in
Israel, both man and animal.* The claim was never only ritual. It rests on a night when Egypt's
eldest died and Israel's did not.

**The word is *taḥat*.** Numbers 3:12 and 8:16 say *instead of* with *taḥat* (WLC), the word of
Genesis 22:13, where the ram goes up *instead of* Isaac. See
[[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]]. Peṭer, *that which opens*, keeps its narrow
range: its lemma stands at Exodus 13:2, 12, 13, 15; 34:19, 20; Numbers 3:12; 8:16; 18:15; and Ezekiel
20:26, where the claim is turned to judgment (WLC).

**Two arrangements, and no statement of how they fit.** Exodus 13:13 makes redemption a rule for
every family. Numbers 3 makes a single exchange of one tribe for one generation, with money only for
the surplus (3:46–51). Numbers 18:15–16 returns to redemption at a fixed price. The obvious way to
hold them together is that the Levites answered for the generation counted at Sinai and the price
applied after it. That is a reading, and no verse says it.

**The Levites are living, not sacrificed.** Unlike the donkey's lamb or the ram at Moriah, nothing
dies in this exchange. The firstborn's place is filled by men who go on serving, and the claim on
them is paid in labor at the Tent. Numbers 8:19 adds that they do this *to make atonement for the
children of Israel*, and the result it names is *no plague* when Israel comes near. It does not say how.
See [[The Lamps and the Levites Cleansed]]; the passage is the only one here where the Levites are also
offered, as a wave offering. See
[[The Levites as a Wave Offering - Tenufah from the Breast to the Tribe]].

**The exchange as carried out.** Numbers 3:40–51 does what 3:11–13 announced. Moses counts 22,273
firstborn against 22,000 Levites and takes five shekels for each of the 273 (46–47). Levi's livestock
goes in with no count at all (41, 45). The two arrangements above stand in one unit, and the money marks
only where the first ran out. See [[Five Shekels a Head - Numbers 3, Numbers 18, and Leviticus 27]] and [[Twenty-Two Thousand - The Levite Sums in Numbers 3, 4, and 26]].

## Significance

- The claim of [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] becomes an institution here: an entire tribe's life
  is the visible form of what every family owes. See also [[Priesthood]] and [[Redemption]].
- See [[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]] for why the handing back matters.
