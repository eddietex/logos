# The City of a Foreigner - Jebus Passed By for Gibeah

## Passages involved
- **Joshua 15:63** — *"As for the Jebusites, the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the children of Judah couldn't
  drive them out"* (stored WEB). See [[The Cities of Judah]].
- **Joshua 18:28** — *"the Jebusite (also called Jerusalem)"* in Benjamin's list (stored WEB). See
  [[The Inheritance of Benjamin]].
- **Judges 1:21** — *"The children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites who inhabited Jerusalem,
  but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem to this day"* (stored WEB). See
  [[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]].
- **Judges 19:10–15** — *"We won't enter into the city of a foreigner that is not of the children of
  Israel; but we will pass over to Gibeah"* (stored WEB). See [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]].
- **2 Samuel 5:6** — David's men go *"to Jerusalem against the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land"*
  (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not in raw). The capture that follows is paraphrased here, not quoted.

## Analysis

**The choice.** Near Jebus at evening the servant asks to lodge there. The Levite refuses: *lōʾ nāsûr
ʾel-ʿîr nāḵrî ʾăšer lōʾ-mibbənê yiśrāʾēl hēnnâ* (Judges 19:12, WLC). *Nāḵrî*, *foreigner*, is the word of
Deuteronomy 17:15, *You may not put a foreigner over you, who is not your brother* (stored WEB; WLC). He
trusts an Israelite town of Benjamin over a city of Canaanites. The narrator does not say what Jebus would
have done; what Gibeah does is the rest of the chapter.

**Benjamin's failure, twice.** Jebus is a foreign city in the middle of the land because Benjamin did
not drive the Jebusites out (Judges 1:21; Joshua 18:28 allots it to Benjamin; Joshua 15:63 reports Judah's
failure). The Levite avoids the city Benjamin left foreign and is betrayed by the city Benjamin made its
own. The name *Yəḇûs* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges only at Judges 19:10–11, with the
gloss *hîʾ yərûšālāim*, *that is Jerusalem*.

**What the wiki claims.** The verified facts are the refusal, the word *nāḵrî*, and Benjamin's failure to
take Jebus. That the chapter means the reader to weigh Jebus against Gibeah — the foreigner presumed
dangerous, the brother proved so — is the wiki's reading, and a common one; the text gives no comment.

**Afterward.** Jebus stays Jebusite until David takes it (2 Samuel 5:6–9, paraphrased; only 5:6 checked),
and Gibeah becomes the town of Saul (1 Samuel 10:26, WEB checked). The two places the Levite chose between
become the seats of Israel's first two kings. The text of Judges does not look ahead to either.

## Significance

The scene sets Israel's own town below the foreign one in the one test the chapter cares about, the
treatment of a stranger at night. It is the same verdict [[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]]
reaches by wording, reached here by geography. See [[Jerusalem]], [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], and
[[The Nations]].
