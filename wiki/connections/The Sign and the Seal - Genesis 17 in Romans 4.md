# The Sign and the Seal - Genesis 17 in Romans 4

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 15:6** — *"He believed in Yahweh, who credited it to him for righteousness."* See
  [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]].
- **Genesis 17:10–11, 24** — circumcision commanded and performed, with Abraham ninety-nine. See
  [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]].
- **Genesis 17:5** — *"I have made you the father of a multitude of nations."*
- **Romans 4:9–12** — the argument: *"How then was it counted? When he was in circumcision, or in
  uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision."*
- **Romans 4:16–18** — *"that the promise may be sure to all the offspring … who is the father of us
  all"*, quoting Genesis 17:5.
- **Galatians 3:6–9, 15–18** — the same order of events used for a different conclusion.
- **James 2:21–24** — the counter-reading of Genesis 15:6, which dates it forward to Genesis 22
  rather than back from Genesis 17.

## Analysis

**Paul's argument in Romans 4:9–12 is an argument about the order of two chapters, and nothing else.**

Genesis 15:6 credits Abraham with righteousness for believing. Genesis 17:10–11 commands
circumcision, and 17:24 records it performed when he was ninety-nine. Whatever the interval — Genesis
does not date chapter 15, but Abram is under eighty-six in it (16:16) and ninety-nine here, so the
gap is at least thirteen years — the verdict comes first and the sign comes second. Paul asks a
one-sentence question about that gap and builds a doctrine of the Gentile mission on the answer:

> *"He received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while
> he was in uncircumcision, that he might be the father of all those who believe, though they might be
> in uncircumcision … and the father of circumcision to those who not only are of the circumcision,
> but who also walk in the steps of that faith of our father Abraham."*

Two words do the work.

- ***Sign*** — *sēmeion*, which is the Septuagint's word at Genesis 17:11 for *ʾôt*, and Genesis's
  own term. Paul is not introducing a category; he is quoting one.
- ***Seal*** — *sphragis*, which is not in Genesis. A seal authenticates something that already
  exists: it does not create the document it is pressed into. That is the whole argument in one noun.

### Why the order matters so much to him

Because Genesis 17:5 says *"a multitude of nations"*, and Paul reads that plural as a promise with
Gentiles in it (Romans 4:17–18). If righteousness were attached to the sign, the promise would reach
only the circumcised, and Genesis 17:5 would contradict Genesis 17:10. Reading the verdict as prior
lets both stand: Abraham is *"the father of all those who believe"* in his uncircumcised state, and
father of the circumcised who believe as they do.

The same move, with a different lever, runs Galatians 3:15–18 — there the interval is between
Abraham and Sinai, four hundred thirty years, and the conclusion is that the law cannot annul a
prior covenant. In both letters the argument is chronological rather than conceptual: Paul is reading
Genesis as a sequence and treating the sequence as binding.

### What the argument does not say

- **It does not call circumcision worthless.** Romans 3:1–2 has already asked *"What advantage then
  has the Jew?"* and answered *"Much in every way"*, and 4:12 keeps Abraham as *"the father of
  circumcision"* for those who believe.
- **It does not read Genesis 17 as conditional.** The covenant there is *everlasting* three times and
  is established with Isaac before Isaac exists. Paul's *seal* fits that shape rather than fighting
  it.
- **And it does not settle what Genesis 15:6 meant on its own terms.** James 2:21–24 takes the same
  verse and dates it *forward* — *"Wasn't Abraham our father justified by works, in that he offered up
  Isaac his son on the altar?"* — reading Genesis 22 as the fulfilment of Genesis 15:6 rather than
  Genesis 17 as its seal. Both readings are arguments about which later chapter the verse points at.
  See [[Credited to Him for Righteousness - Genesis 15.6 in Paul and James]].

### The rabbinic count

Later Jewish tradition also worked the interval, and reached the opposite conclusion from the same
data: *Seder Olam* and the midrashim compute twenty-nine years between the covenant of Genesis 15 and
the circumcision, and read chapter 17 as the covenant's completion rather than as evidence that it
was already complete. Nothing in Genesis states an interval, and neither reading can be checked
against the text; what the text supplies is only the order, which is why the order is all Paul uses.

## Significance

- **It is the clearest case in the New Testament of a doctrine built on narrative sequence.** No
  allegory, no typology, no appeal to a hidden meaning — just which chapter comes first.
- **It gives the Reformation its standard grammar for sacraments.** *Sign* and *seal* as a pair, with
  the thing signified prior to the sign, is Romans 4:11 read as a definition; the Westminster and
  Heidelberg formulations quote it directly, and so does every argument since about what baptism does.
- **And it makes Genesis 17:5's plural load-bearing.** *A multitude of nations* is the clause that
  forces the question, and it is in the same chapter as the command that seems to exclude them.
