# Heart, Eyes, and Feet - Proverbs 4 and Job 31

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 4:21–27** — *"Let them not depart from your eyes. Keep them in the center of your
  heart. … Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring of life. … Let your
  eyes look straight ahead. … Make the path of your feet level. … Don't turn to the right hand nor
  to the left. Remove your foot from evil."* — [[Keep Your Heart]]
- **Proverbs 4:22** — *"For they are life to those who find them, and health to their whole
  body."*
- **Job 31:1, 7, 9** — *"I made a covenant with my eyes, how then should I look lustfully at a
  young woman? … if my step has turned out of the way, if my heart walked after my eyes, if any
  defilement has stuck to my hands … If my heart has been enticed to a woman"* —
  [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]
- **Job 23:11** — *"My foot has held fast to his steps. I have kept his way, and not turned
  away."* — [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]
- **Job 2:7** — *"struck Job with painful sores from the sole of his foot to his head."*

## Analysis

**The same inventory of the body.** Proverbs 4:20–27 tells the son to guard his heart and then
lists where the heart comes out: mouth, eyes, feet. Job 31 is a man swearing, under curse, that he
guarded exactly these. His eyes are under covenant (31:1); his step has not turned out of the way,
and his heart has not *"walked after"* his eyes (31:7); his heart has not been enticed to a woman
(31:9). Job's order — eyes leading the heart astray, the heart leading the step — is the order of
temptation; Proverbs' order — heart first, then mouth, eyes, feet — is the order of keeping. Read
together they describe the same circuit from opposite ends.

**The turn that did not happen.** Proverbs 4:27 commands, *"Don't turn to the right hand nor to the
left"*. Job 23:11 claims, *"I have kept his way, and not turned away"*. The book of Job vouches for
the claim in its narrator's voice: Job was *"blameless and upright"* and turned away from evil
(Job 1:1, stored WEB).

**What follows is in tension, and the wiki records it rather than resolving it.** Proverbs gives
the reason for keeping the heart: the father's words are *"life"* and *"health to their whole
body"* (4:22). Job kept heart, eyes, and feet, and his whole body was struck *"from the sole of his
foot to his head"* (2:7). Proverbs states the ordinary fruit of a guarded life in order to form a
young man's choices; Job is the case where the body's health did not follow and the reason lay in
a heavenly wager the sufferer never learned. Neither book retracts the other. The lecture remains
the right instruction; Job forbids reading a sick body as evidence that the heart was not kept. See
[[Rebels Against the Light - Proverbs 4 and Job 24]] for the same pattern one lecture earlier.

## Significance

Job 31 shows what Proverbs 4's instruction looks like lived — not as a promise collected but as a
record sworn to. It is the strongest available evidence that the lecture is achievable, and the
strongest evidence that its reward is not mechanical.
