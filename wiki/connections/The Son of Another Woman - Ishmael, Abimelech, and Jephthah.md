## Analysis

**Driven out from the inheritance.** Sarah and Jephthah's brothers say the same two things: put him out,
because he will not inherit. Both use *gāraš*, *to drive out* (Sarah's *gārēš*, Genesis 21:10; the
brothers' *wayəgārəšû*, Judges 11:2; WLC). The verbs of inheriting differ. Sarah says *lōʾ yîraš*,
*will not be heir* (*yāraš*); the brothers say *lōʾ-tinḥal*, *you will not inherit* (*nāḥal*; WLC). In
both, the reason is the mother: *the son of this servant*, *the son of another woman*. Abraham's other
concubines' sons are *sent away* with gifts, *from Isaac his son* (Genesis 25:6). The same pattern, the
heir kept and the other sons removed, appears there without the verb *gāraš*.

**Two sons of a second woman in Judges.** Gideon's *seventy sons* came from *many wives* (8:30), and the
next verse sets one apart: *His concubine who was in Shechem also bore him a son* (8:31). Abimelech goes
to his mother's family and argues from her side, *I am your bone and your flesh* (9:2). Two chapters
later Jephthah is *the son of a prostitute* (11:1) and *the son of another woman* (11:2). Both men are
marked by their mothers. One kills his brothers to rule (9:5). The other is driven out by his brothers
and is later asked to rule (11:2, 8).

**Empty men.** Each of them gathers the same followers. Abimelech *hired vain and light fellows*,
*ʾănāšîm rêqîm ûpōḥăzîm* (9:4); *outlaws*, *ʾănāšîm rêqîm*, gathered to Jephthah (11:3; both WLC). In the
WLC of Genesis, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, and Judges the phrase occurs only in these two verses. The
WEB translates it differently each time. Abimelech pays his men with silver from Baal Berith's house.
Jephthah's men come to him, and the text does not say why.

**Where the threads part.** Ishmael is cast out and does not come back; God is *with the boy*
(Genesis 21:20) and he becomes a nation elsewhere. Abimelech takes what he was not given and dies under
a millstone. Jephthah is the excluded son who is called back, and he makes his return conditional:
*will I be your head?* (11:9).

Joseph is a near relative of this thread. He is Rachel's son, not a concubine's, but his brothers *hated
him* (Genesis 37:4, *śānēʾ*, WLC) and asked *Will you indeed reign over us?* (37:8). Jephthah uses the
same verb of the elders, *Didn't you hate me* (Judges 11:7, WLC), and does come to rule over them. See
[[Joseph Sold into Slavery]].

## Significance

In Genesis the son of the second woman is removed so that the heir can inherit, and the narrative goes
with the heir. In Judges the same exclusion produces the period's leaders. Abimelech and Jephthah are
both sons whose standing in their fathers' houses was in doubt, and both build their power from *empty
men* outside the house. The book does not comment on the pattern. It does put one verb in two places. Yahweh
said he would *not drive out* the nations (2:3), and Gilead's sons *drove out* their brother (11:2;
both *gāraš*, WLC). Then Gilead had to go back to the brother for help. See [[Brotherhood]], [[Birthright and the Firstborn]], [[Ishmael]],
[[Abimelech (son of Gideon)]], and [[Jephthah]].
