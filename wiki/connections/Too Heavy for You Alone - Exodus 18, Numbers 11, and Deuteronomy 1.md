# Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 18:13–27** — Jethro: *"the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to perform it yourself alone"*
  (18:18); *"able men which fear God"* as rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens; *"they shall share the
  load with you"* (18:22) — [[Jethro's Counsel]]
- **Numbers 11:14–17** — Moses to God: *"I am not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too heavy for
  me"*; God: *"Gather to me seventy men of the elders of Israel … I will take of the Spirit which is on you, and will
  put it on them; and they shall bear the burden of the people with you, that you don't bear it yourself alone"*
  (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Deuteronomy 1:9–18** — Moses to Israel: *"I am not able to bear you myself alone"* (1:9); *"Take wise men of
  understanding and well known according to your tribes, and I will make them heads over you"* (1:13); the people:
  *"The thing which you have spoken is good to do"* (1:14); *"The case that is too hard for you, you shall bring to
  me"* (1:17) (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Acts 6:2–4** — the twelve: *"It is not appropriate for us to forsake the word of God and serve tables. Therefore
  select from among you, brothers, seven men of good report … But we will continue steadfastly in prayer and in the
  ministry of the word"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**Three books tell of Moses unable to carry Israel alone, and each tells it differently.** The shared sentence is
nearly identical in Hebrew (WLC):

| | Exodus 18:18 | Numbers 11:14 | Deuteronomy 1:9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| speaker | Jethro, to Moses | Moses, to God | Moses, to Israel, remembering |
| *not able* | *lōʾ-tûkal* | *lōʾ-ʾûkal ʾānōkî* | *lōʾ-ʾûkal* |
| *alone* | *ləbaddekā* | *ləbaddî* | *ləbaddî* |
| *heavy* | *kî-kābēd mimməkā haddābār* | *kî kābēd mimmennî* | — |
| what is carried | *haddābār*, *the thing* (judging) | *kol-hāʿām hazzeh*, *all this people* | *ʾetkem*, *you* |

**Then they diverge on everything else.**

| | Exodus 18 | Numbers 11 | Deuteronomy 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| when | before the arrival at Sinai in Exodus's order (19:1), though *"at the Mountain of God"* (18:5) | after the departure from Sinai (Numbers 10:11–12, by reference), at the complaint about meat | *"at that time"* (1:9), in a speech that begins with the command to leave Horeb (1:6–8, by reference) |
| whose idea | **Jethro's**, a Midianite priest's; conditional on *"and God commands you so"* | **God's**, in answer to Moses' complaint | **Moses'**, approved by the people |
| who chooses | Moses (*"you shall provide"*, *teḥĕzeh*; *"Moses chose"*, 18:25) | God names the pool (*"whom you know to be the elders"*); Moses gathers them | the people supply (*hābû lākem*, *"Take"*, 1:13), Moses appoints |
| qualifications | *"able men which fear God: men of truth, hating unjust gain"* | elders and officers | *"wise men of understanding and well known"* |
| how many | rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens | seventy | *"captains of thousands … hundreds … fifties … tens, and officers"* |
| endowment | none mentioned | ***the Spirit*** taken from Moses and put on them | a charge to judge without partiality |
| what they do | judge the small cases; bring the hard ones to Moses | *"bear the burden of the people with you"* | hear cases; bring the *"too hard"* to Moses |

**Exodus 18 and Deuteronomy 1 describe the same institution** — the decimal ranks, the small cases kept below and
the hard ones sent up — **and disagree on its origin**: Exodus credits Jethro and never reports God's command,
Deuteronomy credits Moses and never mentions Jethro. **Numbers 11 describes a different body** — seventy elders,
endowed with the Spirit, who prophesy once (Numbers 11:25, by reference) — **but in the language of Exodus 18**:
*"they shall bear … with you"* (*wənāśəʾû … ʾittəkā*) is Jethro's *wənāśəʾû ʾittāk* (Exodus 18:22), and *"that you
don't bear it yourself alone"* ends on Jethro's *ləbaddekā* (WLC).

**Readings reported, not adopted:**

- **One event, retold.** Deuteronomy is Moses' sermon to the next generation, and a preacher need not name the
  in-law who first proposed the plan; Exodus 18:24 says Moses *"did all that he had said"*, so the plan became his.
  On this reading Exodus 18 is placed early by subject (the friendly foreigner beside the hostile Amalek), and the
  institution was set up at the departure from Horeb, where Deuteronomy puts it and where Numbers 10:29–32 has
  Moses' Midianite in-law take his leave.
- **Two or three events.** Judges were appointed before Sinai (Exodus 18), the seventy elders later (Numbers 11);
  Deuteronomy compresses.
- **Source criticism** assigns the three to different traditions about the origin of Israel's judiciary, one
  crediting Midian, one the Spirit, one Moses.

**The wiki records the agreements and the differences and does not harmonize them.**

**Acts 6 is the same shape, unquoted.** The twelve are overloaded, the solution is to appoint qualified men for one
part of the work, and the leaders keep the part only they can do — *"prayer and … the ministry of the word"*, as
Jethro kept Moses *"before God"* and teaching the statutes (Exodus 18:19–20). **Luke cites no Old Testament text
and names no precedent**; the likeness is structural, and the wiki notes it without claiming an allusion.

## Significance

- **Moses' insufficiency is a thread, not an episode.** His *"heavy"* mouth (Exodus 4:10), his *"heavy"* hands
  (17:12), and the *"too heavy"* work (18:18) use one adjective, *kābēd* (WLC), and each time others carry what he
  cannot: Aaron, Aaron and Hur, the judges, the seventy. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].
- **The first judiciary is a foreigner's design** in Exodus, and the text lets that stand — the law that follows in
  Exodus 21–23 is God's, but the courts that will apply it were proposed by the priest of Midian. See [[Jethro]] and
  [[The Nations]].
- **What qualifies a judge differs by book**: piety and incorruptibility (Exodus), the Spirit (Numbers), wisdom and
  reputation (Deuteronomy). See [[Judgment]], [[Wisdom]], and [[Integrity]].
