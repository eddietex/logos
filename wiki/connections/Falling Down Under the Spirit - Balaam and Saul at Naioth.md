# Falling Down Under the Spirit - Balaam and Saul at Naioth

## Passages involved

- [[Balak Sends for Balaam]] — **Numbers 22:5, 15** — *He sent messengers to Balaam the son of Beor* (22:5); *Balak
  again sent princes, more, and more honorable than they* (22:15). Stored WEB.
- [[Balaam's Last Oracles]] — **Numbers 24:2–4, 10, 16** — *the Spirit of God came on him. He took up his parable,
  and said, "… who sees the vision of the Almighty, falling down, and having his eyes open"* (24:2–4); *I called you to
  curse my enemies, and, behold, you have altogether blessed them these three times* (24:10). Stored WEB.
- [[Saul Among the Prophets at Naioth]] — **1 Samuel 19:20–24** — *Saul sent messengers to seize David … God's Spirit
  came on Saul's messengers, and they also prophesied. … Saul sent messengers again the third time, and they also
  prophesied. … Then God's Spirit came on him also … and lay down naked all that day and all that night.* Stored WEB.

## Analysis

In both stories a man who wants harm done to someone sends for it more than once. The people he sends, or sends
for, are taken over by the Spirit of God, and the harm turns into prophecy.

| | Numbers 22–24 | 1 Samuel 19:18–24 |
|---|---|---|
| the sender | Balak, king of Moab | Saul, king of Israel |
| the aim | *curse this people for me* (22:17) | *to seize David* (19:20) |
| the sending | *wayyišlaḥ malʾāḵîm* (22:5); *wayyōsep̄ ʿôḏ bālāq šǝlōaḥ* (22:15) | *wayyišlaḥ … malʾāḵîm* (19:20, 21); *wayyōsep̄ šāʾûl wayyišlaḥ* (19:21) |
| three times | *you have altogether blessed them these three times* (24:10) | three groups of messengers *also prophesied* (19:20–21) |
| the Spirit | *wattǝhî ʿālāyw rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* (24:2) | *wattǝhî ʿal-malʾăḵê šāʾûl rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* (19:20); *wattǝhî ʿālāyw gam-hûʾ rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* (19:23) |
| falling | *nōp̄ēl ûḡlûy ʿênāyim*, *falling down, and having his eyes open* (24:4, 16) | *wayyippōl ʿārōm*, *he fell naked* (19:24) |

(Hebrew WLC; English stored WEB.)

**The Spirit clause.** In the Hebrew files checked (Genesis through 2 Samuel, Ruth), *wattǝhî ʿal … rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm*, *the
Spirit of God came on*, occurs only at Numbers 24:2 and 1 Samuel 19:20, 23. With *rûaḥ YHWH* the same clause is used
of Othniel and Jephthah (Judges 3:10; 11:29); see
[[The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah]]. Saul's earlier endowments used other verbs: the Spirit
*came mightily*, *ṣālaḥ*, at 10:10 and 11:6 (WLC); see [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].

**The sending again.** *Yāsap̄* with *šālaḥ*, *sent again*, occurs in those files of Noah's dove (Genesis 8:10), of
Balak (Numbers 22:15), of Jephthah's messengers to Ammon (Judges 11:14), and of Saul (19:21). Balak and Saul are the
two who send again for the same purpose after the first sending has failed.

**The falling.** Balaam's *falling down* (*nōp̄ēl*, the *qal* participle of *nāp̄al*) is his own description in the
oracle, joined to *having his eyes open*: he falls and sees. Saul's *wayyippōl* is the narrator's, joined to
*naked*, and the text says nothing of what he saw. The two uses of *nāp̄al* are not the same image, and the wiki does
not make them one.

**The differences.** Balaam is a seer who comes and speaks; his words are given at length. Neither the messengers nor
Saul is given any words of prophecy. Balaam blesses the one he was sent to curse. Saul's messengers do not bless
David; they simply do not reach him. Balak sends for one man three times; Saul sends three groups, and then goes
himself and becomes the fourth.

## Significance

- **The Spirit as a defense.** In both stories the one under threat does nothing. Israel does not know Balak's plan,
  and David says and does nothing after reaching Samuel. The text gives the protection entirely to the Spirit, and in
  both the instrument is prophecy, not force.
- **A king against the prophet's word.** Balak tries to buy a word against Israel and cannot; Saul tries to take the
  man Samuel anointed out of Samuel's company and cannot. The wiki records the parallel in vocabulary and pattern and
  claims no dependence; the text of 1 Samuel does not name Balaam.
- **The Spirit is not approval.** Balaam is later killed among the Midianites (Numbers 31:8) and remembered for his
  counsel (31:16). Saul has been rejected (15:23, 26). In neither story does the Spirit's coming show that the man was
  faithful. See [[The Counsel of Balaam - Numbers 31.16 and Its Later Readings]] and [[The Spirit of God]].
