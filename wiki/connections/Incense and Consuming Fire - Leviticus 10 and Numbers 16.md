# Incense and Consuming Fire - Leviticus 10 and Numbers 16

## Passages involved
- [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]]
- [[The Rebellion of Korah]]

## Analysis

Leviticus 10:1–3 and Numbers 16:35 describe fire from Yahweh consuming incense offerers. The earlier
victims are Aaron’s sons offering fire not commanded; the later 250 men participate in the test of
disputed priestly access. Numbers 16:40 turns their censers into a warning for anyone outside
Aaron’s offspring.

The accounts therefore guard different boundaries: priestly descent does not excuse unauthorized
worship in Leviticus, while belonging to the holy congregation does not grant Aaron’s office in
Numbers. Neither passage warrants reconstructing a hidden technical cause of the fire. Eleazar, a
surviving son named in Leviticus 10:6, receives the task of recovering the censers in Numbers
16:36–39.

## Significance

The same judgment motif both limits priests’ conduct and guards access to priestly service.
