# Bethel from Jacob's Pillar to Jeroboam's Calf

## Passages involved

- Genesis 12:8; 13:3–4 — Abram's altar on the ridge, at a site the narrator calls Bethel before it is
  named
- Genesis 28:10–22 — the stairway, the pillar, and the naming
- Genesis 35:1–15 — sent back; the foreign gods buried, the altar built, the pillar raised again
- Judges 20:18, 26–28 — the ark at Bethel, and Israel enquiring of God there
- 1 Samuel 7:16 — Bethel on Samuel's circuit
- 1 Kings 12:26–33 — Jeroboam's two calves, and the one at Bethel
- 1 Kings 13:1–34 — the man of God out of Judah, and the altar torn
- 2 Kings 10:29; 17:7–23 — the calves as the sin the northern kingdom never leaves
- Amos 3:14; 4:4; 5:5–6; 7:10–17 — the altars of Bethel cut off, and Amaziah's expulsion of the
  prophet
- Hosea 4:15; 10:5, 15; 12:4 — *Beth Aven*, and the one direct prophetic citation of Jacob's night
- 2 Kings 23:15–20 — Josiah burns bones on the altar
- Ezra 2:28; Nehemiah 11:31 — Bethel resettled after the exile, unremarkable

## Analysis

**No other site in Scripture has this range: God appears there twice, and the prophets want it torn
down.**

**The arc has four stages and each one is built on the last.**

### 1. A real appearance (Genesis 28; 35)

Everything later depends on this being genuine, and the wiki's passage page argues that it is: God
speaks, the promise is given, the name is *house of God*, and God himself later says *"I am the God
of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar"* (Genesis 31:13). **Nothing in the northern kingdom's use of
Bethel is a fabrication about the past.** The claim *God appeared here* is true.

**And Genesis 28 authorises nothing beyond it.** There is no altar in the passage, no offering, no
image, no priest, and no instruction to worship there. There is a stone, some oil, and a vow — and
even the altar of chapter 35 is built on an explicit command to build one, that once.

### 2. A legitimate sanctuary (Judges; 1 Samuel)

The ark stands at Bethel in the judges' period and Israel enquires of God there (Judges 20:26–28);
Samuel judges there annually (1 Samuel 7:16). **For centuries it is simply one of the places where
Israel worships**, alongside Shiloh, Gilgal, and Mizpah, and Scripture passes no comment.

### 3. A state cult (1 Kings 12)

**Jeroboam's problem is political and his solution is geographical.** *"If this people goes up to
offer sacrifices in Yahweh's house at Jerusalem, then the heart of this people will turn again to
their lord"* (12:27). He makes two calves and puts them at the two ends of his kingdom — Dan in the
north, **Bethel in the south, on the Judah road** — and says *"Behold your gods, Israel, which
brought you up out of the land of Egypt"* (12:28).

**That sentence is Exodus 32:4, verbatim.** The narrator of Kings is not describing the calves; he is
quoting the golden calf at Sinai and letting the reader do the rest.

**And every element of the choice leans on Genesis 28.** The site has a patriarchal appearance
attached to it, a name that means *house of God*, a stone Jacob himself set up, and a claim — *the
gate of heaven* — that is a direct rival to Zion's. **Jeroboam does not need to invent a
justification. He needs only to point at Genesis.**

### 4. The prophets' verdict

| | the town | what is said |
|---|---|---|
| **Amos 5:5** | *Bethel* | *"Bethel will come to nothing"* — ***Bêt-ʾĒl … lə-ʾāwen***, a pun: **house of God** becomes **nothingness** |
| **Amos 7:13** | *Bethel* | Amaziah the priest: *"it is the king's sanctuary, and it is a royal house"* — **not God's house; the king's** |
| **Hosea 4:15; 10:5** | ***Beth Aven*** | Amos's pun made into the town's name: **house of trouble** |
| **Amos 3:14** | the altars | *"the horns of the altar will be cut off"* |
| **2 Kings 23:15–16** | the altar | Josiah burns human bones on it — the maximum defilement available |

**Amaziah's line is the whole indictment in one sentence.** Told to leave, he calls Bethel *the
king's sanctuary and a royal house* — ***bêt mamlākâ***. **The house of God has become the house of
the kingdom**, and the priest defending it says so without apparently noticing.

**And Hosea 12:4 is the only prophetic text that goes back to the night itself.** In a passage
indicting Israel for deceit and merchant's scales, Hosea rehearses Jacob — the heel-grabbing, the
wrestling, the weeping — and lands on *"He found him at Bethel, and there he spoke with us."*
***Spoke with us***, present tense, plural: **the prophet uses Bethel against the people who own
Bethel**, by insisting that what God said there was said to them.

## Significance

**The site is Scripture's standing demonstration that a genuine revelation is not a permanent
license.**

Everything that goes wrong at Bethel goes wrong *because* Genesis 28 is true. A place with no history
could not have been used as Jeroboam used it. The calf needs the stairway; the royal sanctuary needs
the *house of God*; Amaziah's confidence in throwing out a prophet rests on eight hundred years of
Bethel being exactly what Jacob said it was.

**And the town keeps the name the whole way down.** Amos has to make a pun to get *nothingness* out of
it, and Hosea has to rename it, because the actual name never stops meaning **house of God** — through
the calf, through Amaziah, through Josiah's bones. **The name is the argument on both sides.**

**The postscript is that it simply ends.** Ezra 2:28 lists two hundred and twenty-three men of Bethel
and Ai among the returnees; Nehemiah 11:31 has Benjaminites living there. **No sanctuary, no altar,
no comment.** The gate of heaven finishes the Old Testament as a small town on a ridge with a census
number.
