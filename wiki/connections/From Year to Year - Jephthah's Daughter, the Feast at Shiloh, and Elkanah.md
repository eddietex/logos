# From Year to Year - Jephthah's Daughter, the Feast at Shiloh, and Elkanah

  season from year to year.* Stored WEB.
- [[Jephthah's Vow]] — Judges 11:40: *the daughters of Israel went yearly to celebrate the daughter of
  Jephthah the Gileadite four days in a year.* Stored WEB.
- [[Wives for Benjamin]] — Judges 21:19: *Behold, there is a feast of Yahweh from year to year in
  Shiloh.* Stored WEB.
- [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] — **1 Samuel 1:3** *(stored WEB)* — *This man went up out of his city

## Analysis

**The phrase.** *Miyyāmîm yāmîmâ*, literally *from days to days*, is how Hebrew says *yearly*. In the WLC
files of Genesis through 1 Samuel searched it occurs at Exodus 13:10, Judges 11:40, Judges 21:19, 1 Samuel
1:3, and 1 Samuel 2:19, and nowhere else (consonantal search). The WEB renders it *from year to year* in
Exodus 13:10, Judges 21:19, and 1 Samuel 1:3, and *yearly* in Judges 11:40.

**Daughters, and a yearly observance.** Both occurrences in Judges are about daughters and a recurring
custom. In 11:40 the daughters of Israel go out every year to *celebrate*, *lətannôṯ*, Jephthah's daughter,
who had come out to meet him *with tambourines and with dances* (11:34). In 21:19–21 the daughters of
Shiloh come out to *dance in the dances* at the yearly feast, and are seized. Both chapters turn on an
oath or vow made by men (11:30–31; 21:1) that is paid with daughters. The wiki notes the pairing; Judges
does not cross-refer the two. See
[[With Tambourines and with Dances - Miriam, Jephthah's Daughter, and the Daughters of Shiloh]].

**Shiloh, yearly, in 1 Samuel.** The next time the phrase appears, the place is again Shiloh: Elkanah goes
up *from year to year* to sacrifice there (1 Samuel 1:3), and Hannah's yearly robe follows (2:19). The
feast Judges 21 uses as cover for a raid becomes the setting for Hannah's prayer and Samuel's birth. That
the two describe the same annual festival is likely and is not stated.

**Exodus 13.** The first use is the Passover ordinance, kept *in its season*. Which feast Judges 21:19
means, *a feast of Yahweh*, it does not say; *ḥaḡ YHWH* is what Leviticus 23:39 calls the autumn feast of
ingathering, and some readers take the vineyards of 21:20 as a sign of that season. Reported, not decided.
See [[Festivals and First Fruits]] and [[Shiloh]].

### 1 Samuel 1 now ingested

The chapter marks the yearly rhythm three ways. 1:3 has *miyyāmîm yāmîmâ*; 1:7 has a different phrase,
*šānâ ḇəšānâ*, *year by year*, for the rival's taunting; and 1:21 calls the offering *zeḇaḥ hayyāmîm*,
literally *the sacrifice of the days*, which the WEB renders *the yearly sacrifice* (WLC). The feast that
Judges 21 used as cover for a raid is, in 1 Samuel 1, the occasion of a family's grief and a woman's
prayer. Whether it is the same annual feast remains unstated. See [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 1:3, 7,
21; 2:19; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

## Significance

A small phrase marks the rhythm of worship at the sanctuary across the end of Judges and the start of 1
Samuel. In Judges it frames two stories of daughters lost to men's oaths; in 1 Samuel it frames a mother's
vow and a son given. See [[A Person Vowed to Yahweh - Leviticus 27.1-8, Judges 11, and 1 Samuel 1]] and
[[Oaths and Vows]].
