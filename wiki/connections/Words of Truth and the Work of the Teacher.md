# Words of Truth and the Work of the Teacher

## Passages involved

- [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]] — Proverbs 1:1–7.
- [[Thirty Excellent Things]] — Proverbs 22:17–21.
- [[The End of the Matter]] — Ecclesiastes 12:9–12.

## Analysis

Proverbs 1:2–6 presents learning as a task for both beginners and the already wise. It
includes understanding sayings, receiving instruction in just conduct, and increasing
knowledge. Ecclesiastes 12:9–10 looks at the other side of that exchange: the teacher
ponders, searches, arranges proverbs, and seeks words that are acceptable and true.
One book announces a curriculum; the other assesses a teacher after his inquiry.

Proverbs 22:17–21 explicitly concerns written instruction, reliable words, and the
pupil's ability to give sound answers. Ecclesiastes 12:10–11 likewise joins written
truth to words that affect their hearer, figured as goads and firmly fixed nails.
The likeness concerns the purpose and craft of wisdom teaching. It does not establish
that the Preacher compiled the biblical book of Proverbs or that either passage
quotes the other; Ecclesiastes does not name that book or Solomon here.

**Learning's cost remains.** Proverbs 1:5 invites increased learning; Ecclesiastes
12:12 warns that much study wearies the flesh and bookmaking has no end. These
emphases remain distinct. The warning immediately follows praise for teaching,
so it qualifies the pursuit rather than simply declaring all learning worthless.
Neither passage specifies a maximum number of books or a closed canon.

## Significance

[[Wisdom]] includes the work of selecting, arranging, and transmitting instruction.
[[Language and Speech]] concerns the truth and effect of words as well as their number;
[[Work]] includes the teacher's effort and the student's bodily limit.
