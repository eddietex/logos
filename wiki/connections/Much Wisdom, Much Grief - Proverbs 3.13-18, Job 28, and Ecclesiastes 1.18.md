# Much Wisdom, Much Grief - Proverbs 3.13-18, Job 28, and Ecclesiastes 1.18

## Passages involved

- [[The Burden of Wisdom]] — Ecclesiastes 1:13, 16–18.
- [[Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth]] — Proverbs 3:13–18.
- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:12, 20–21, 28.
- [[The Words of Agur]] — Proverbs 30:2–3.

## Analysis

**Happiness and grief.** Proverbs 3:13 says, *"Happy is the man who finds wisdom, the man who gets
understanding."* Its paths are *"ways of pleasantness"* and *"peace"* (3:17), and she is a tree of
life to those who hold her (3:18). Ecclesiastes 1:18 says, *"For in much wisdom is much grief; and he
who increases knowledge increases sorrow."* The two statements are not about different subjects.
Both concern acquiring wisdom and understanding, and their claims about its emotional result are
opposite. This wiki records them side by side and does not choose between them.

**A burden God gives.** Ecclesiastes 1:13 calls the search by wisdom *"a heavy burden that God has
given to the sons of men to be afflicted with."* Proverbs 2:6, recorded on
[[If You Seek Her as Silver]], says Yahweh gives wisdom. The same act of giving is described as a
gift in one book and an affliction in the other. Ecclesiastes 1:13 is speaking about the task of
searching out all that is done, not about wisdom as a possession. That difference matters, but
it does not remove the contrast with Proverbs 3.

**Searching and not finding.** Job 28 asks *"where shall wisdom be found?"* (28:12, 20) and answers
that it is *"hidden from the eyes of all living"* (28:21), before ending with the fear of the
Lord (28:28). Agur confesses, *"I have not learned wisdom"* (Proverbs 30:3). The Preacher claims
the opposite starting point: *"I have obtained for myself great wisdom above all who were before
me in Jerusalem"* (Ecclesiastes 1:16). Job's hymn and Agur's words reach their limit by not
finding wisdom. The Preacher reaches his by finding a great deal of it and discovering what it
costs. These are three distinct ways of describing the limit, not one claim repeated.

**Limits on the comparison.** Proverbs 3 also promises length of days, riches, and honor
(3:16); Ecclesiastes 1:12–18 says nothing about those benefits either way, and its later units
will test them separately. Ecclesiastes 2:13, not yet ingested, still says that wisdom excels
folly as light excels darkness. So 1:18 records the cost of wisdom, not a judgment that it is
worthless. One possible harmonization is that Proverbs speaks of the life wisdom directs and
Ecclesiastes of the clarity it brings about a world that cannot be straightened (1:15). Neither
text states that distinction; it is an interpretation.

Sources: all quotations checked in stored WEB Proverbs 3:13–18; 30:3; Job 28:12, 20–21, 28;
Ecclesiastes 1:13, 16–18; 2:13, on 2026-10-27. This comparison makes no claim about shared
Hebrew vocabulary or literary dependence among the three books.

## Significance

[[Wisdom]] in the Hebrew Bible is not given a single emotional profile. Proverbs' invitation,
Job's search, Agur's confession, and the Preacher's report each hold part of it. Ecclesiastes
adds [[Suffering]] to the experience of the wise rather than only to the life of the fool, and
places that suffering within what God has given ([[Providence]]).
