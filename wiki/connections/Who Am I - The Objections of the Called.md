# Who Am I - The Objections of the Called

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 3:11–12** — Moses: *"Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh, and that I should bring the
  children of Israel out of Egypt?"* God: *"Certainly I will be with you."* — [[The Burning Bush]]
- **Exodus 4:10–12** — Moses: *"O Lord, I am not eloquent … I am slow of speech, and of a slow
  tongue."* God: *"Who made man's mouth? … I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall
  speak."*
- **Judges 6:15–16** — Gideon: *"O Lord, how shall I save Israel? Behold, my family is the poorest in
  Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house."* Yahweh: *"Surely I will be with you, and you
  shall strike the Midianites as one man."*
- **1 Samuel 9:21** — Saul: *"Am I not a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel? And my
  family the least of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? Why then do you speak to me like
  this?"*
- **Isaiah 6:5, 8** — *"Woe is me! For I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips"* — and, after
  the coal, *"Here I am. Send me!"*
- **Jeremiah 1:6–9** — *"Ah, Lord Yahweh! Behold, I don't know how to speak; for I am a child."* —
  *"Don't be afraid because of them, for I am with you to rescue you … Behold, I have put my words in
  your mouth."*

## Analysis

**When God calls someone to deliver or to speak for him, the first answer in Scripture is usually a
reason why not — and the reason is usually the person's smallness.** Moses' *"Who am I?"* is the first
instance, and the later ones share its shape closely enough that the pattern is unmistakable.

| | the objection | about | the answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moses | *"Who am I?"*; *"not eloquent"* | standing; speech | *"I will be with you"*; *"I will be with your mouth"* |
| Gideon | *"the poorest … the least"* | family | *"Surely I will be with you"* |
| Saul | *"the smallest of the tribes … the least"* | tribe and family | *(Samuel anoints him regardless, 10:1)* |
| Isaiah | *"a man of unclean lips"* | purity of speech | a coal on the lips; then *"Send me!"* |
| Jeremiah | *"I don't know how to speak; for I am a child"* | age; speech | *"I am with you"*; *"I have put my words in your mouth"* |

**Two things recur.** The objection is about **speech** three times out of five — Moses, Isaiah,
Jeremiah — because the calling is to speak. And **the answer is never a reassurance about the
objector.** God does not tell Moses he is eloquent, Gideon that his clan is strong, or Jeremiah that
he is old enough. **He answers with his own presence** — *"I will be with you"*, to Moses and Gideon in
nearly the same words (*ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*, Exodus 3:12; *ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*, Judges 6:16) — **and, for the
speakers, with his own words put in their mouths** (Exodus 4:12, 15; Jeremiah 1:9).

**Moses is the outlier in persistence.** The others object once. Moses objects five times, and the last
objection is not a reason at all: *"Please send someone else"* (4:13). **It is the only one in the
series that makes God angry** (4:14), and it does not end the call. **Isaiah is the outlier the other
way**: the only one who, once cleansed, volunteers — with Abraham's and Moses' own word, *hinnēnî*,
*Here I am*.

**Saul is the cautionary one.** His objection is the most like Gideon's, and his reign the one that
fails; the pattern describes how calls begin, not whether they end well.

## Significance

- **It makes Exodus 3–4 the template of the prophetic call**, which Judges, Samuel, Isaiah, and
  Jeremiah follow, whether by literary dependence or by a shared form.
- **It shows the theology of the pattern**: in each case the qualification is God's presence, not the
  person's capacity — *"I will be with you"* is the reply to *"Who am I?"*
- **It reads Exodus 2:14 forward.** *"Who made you a prince and a judge?"* asked who Moses was to act;
  *"Who am I?"* asks it of himself; and the answer to both is that God makes him. See
  [[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].
