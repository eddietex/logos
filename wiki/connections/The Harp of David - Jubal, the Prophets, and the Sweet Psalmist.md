## Analysis

**The word.** *Kinnôr* (morphhb lemma 3658) occurs six times in the Hebrew files checked (Genesis through 2 Samuel,
and Ruth): Genesis 4:21; 31:27; 1 Samuel 10:5; 16:16, 23; and 2 Samuel 6:5 (WLC). The WEB renders it *harp*
throughout; it is usually described as a lyre (recalled, not checked).

**Four settings.** The harp is first an invention of Cain's line, beside the herdsmen and the smiths (Genesis
4:20–22). Then it is for parting: Laban says he would have sent Jacob away *with mirth and with songs* (Genesis
31:27). In 1 Samuel 10:5 it goes before prophets *prophesying*, the scene in which the Spirit came mightily on Saul
(10:10). In 1 Samuel 16 it calms Saul after that Spirit has left him. In 2 Samuel 6 it is played *before Yahweh* as
the ark goes up.

**Saul and the harp, twice.** Saul meets the harp first among the prophets, where *the Spirit of God came mightily on
him* (10:10, stored WEB). He meets it again in his own house, with *an evil spirit from Yahweh* on him (16:14–23).
The instrument is the same; the spirit is not. In 18:10 the two scenes meet: *an evil spirit from God came mightily
on Saul, and he prophesied in the middle of the house. David played with his hand* (stored WEB). The narrator does
not comment on the likeness.

**The verb.** *Nāḡan*, *play a stringed instrument* (lemma 5059), occurs in these files only at 1 Samuel 16:16, 17,
18, 23; 18:10; 19:9 (WLC). Every use is David playing for Saul. The *played* of 2 Samuel 6:5 is a different verb,
*śāḥaq*, *make merry*, *məśaḥăqîm* (WLC, lemma 7832).

**The sweet psalmist.** David's last words call him *the sweet psalmist of Israel* (2 Samuel 23:1). The Psalms
headed *of David* are outside the stored books and are not read here. Within the narrative, 1 Samuel 16 is the first
time David makes music, and he does it for a king who does not yet know he has been anointed.

## Significance

David enters the royal house as a musician before he enters it as a warrior. The thread links the first harp in
Scripture to the first king's torment and to the ark's procession, and it keeps in view that the same playing that
refreshed Saul in 16:23 is the setting for the spear in 18:10–11. See [[Praise and Song]], [[Jubal]], [[David]], and
[[An Evil Spirit from God - Abimelech, Saul, and Ahab's Prophets]].
