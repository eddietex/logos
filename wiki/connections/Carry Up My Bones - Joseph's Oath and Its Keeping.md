# Carry Up My Bones - Joseph's Oath and Its Keeping

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 50:24–26** — *"God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up my bones from here"* —
  [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]
- **Genesis 47:29–31** — [[Jacob]]'s oath, which Joseph's deliberately does not copy —
  [[Joseph and the Famine]]
- **Genesis 48:22** — *"one portion above your brothers"* at [[Shechem (city)]] —
  [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]]
- **Exodus 13:19** — *"Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, for he had made the children of
  Israel swear"*
- **Joshua 24:32** — *"They buried the bones of Joseph … in Shechem, in the parcel of ground which
  Jacob bought"*
- **Hebrews 11:22** — the one act of faith the New Testament credits to Joseph

## Analysis

**Joseph could have had his father's funeral and refuses it.**

**Eighteen verses earlier the machinery existed and worked**: a cortege, Pharaoh's court, chariots,
an escort, a week of mourning at [[Abel Mizraim]], and a grave already paid for at [[Machpelah]].
**Jacob was dead in Egypt and in Canaan within the year.** **Joseph, who arranged all of it and who
had more standing than his father ever had, asks for something else.**

> **25** Joseph took an oath from the children of Israel, saying, "God will surely visit you, and you
> shall carry up my bones from here."

**He asks to stay.** **Not to be buried in Egypt permanently — to be kept there, unburied, until the
people go.** **He ties his own body to a departure he has been given no date for, by a God who has
never spoken to him.**

### The two oaths, side by side

| | **[[Jacob]], 47:29–31 / 49:29–32** | **[[Joseph]], 50:24–25** |
|---|---|---|
| **sworn by** | [[Joseph]] alone, hand under the thigh | **the children of Israel**, collectively |
| **timing** | immediate — *"carry me out of Egypt"* | **deferred — whenever God visits** |
| **destination named** | Machpelah, in the deed, by name | ***"from here"*** — no destination given |
| **kept** | within the year, Genesis 50:13 | **four hundred years later, Exodus 13:19** |
| **what it costs the swearer** | a journey | **nothing; the cost is Joseph's** |

**Jacob's oath is a man arranging his burial. Joseph's is a man declining one.** **And where Jacob
swore his son by the thigh-oath of [[Abraham]]'s servant** (24:2–9; see
[[Put Your Hand Under My Thigh]]), **Joseph swears the whole people, because the whole people is
what the oath depends on.** See [[Oaths and Vows]].

### *God will surely visit you*

***Pāqōd yipqōd ʾĕlōhîm ʾetḵem*** — **infinitive absolute, said twice, at 50:24 and 50:25.**
***Pāqad*** **is the verb of 21:1, *"Yahweh visited Sarah as he had said"***, and **it becomes the
password of the exodus**: Moses is to tell the elders *"I have surely visited you, and seen that
which is done to you in Egypt"* (Exodus 3:16), **and at Exodus 4:31 the people hear that God *had
visited* them and bow their heads and worship.** **The elders recognise a phrase, and the phrase is
in Genesis because Joseph said it twice on his deathbed.** See
[[Yahweh Visited Sarah - Paqad and the Visitations of God]].

**Joseph does not say when, does not say through whom, and does not say that he will see it.** **He
names the three men the land was sworn to — [[Abraham]], [[Isaac]], [[Jacob]] — and is not one of
them.**

### The keeping

**Exodus 13:19 is a parenthesis on the night of the departure.**

> *"Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, for he had made the children of Israel swear, saying,
> 'God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up my bones away from here with you.'"*

**The verse quotes Genesis 50:25 nearly verbatim** — **the only time Exodus quotes a Genesis oath
back** — **and it is inserted into the most hurried passage in the Torah**, between the plunder of
the Egyptians and the pillar of cloud. **A nation leaving in the middle of the night, told to eat
standing up with its sandals on, stops to collect a coffin.**

**Joshua 24:32 closes it.**

> *"They buried the bones of Joseph, which the children of Israel brought up out of Egypt, in
> Shechem, in the parcel of ground which Jacob bought of the sons of Hamor the father of Shechem for
> a hundred pieces of money. They became the inheritance of the children of Joseph."*

**Not at Machpelah with his fathers — at [[Shechem (city)]]**, **in the field Jacob gave him at
48:22 as *"one portion above your brothers"*, and which Jacob had bought at 33:19.** **The bones go
to the land the giving of them was attached to.** See
[[Bought, Sacked, or Taken by the Sword - How Jacob Got Shechem]].

**The sequence spans three books and four hundred years**: **sworn in the last chapter of Genesis,
carried in the middle of Exodus, buried in the last chapter of Joshua.** **It is the longest
unbroken transaction in the Hebrew Bible**, and each of the three books closes a section with it.

### What Hebrews makes of it

> *"By faith Joseph, when his end was near, made mention of the departure of the children of Israel,
> and gave instructions concerning his bones."* (Hebrews 11:22)

**Hebrews 11 has a verse each for Abraham's calling, Isaac's blessing, Jacob's crossed hands, and
Moses' refusal of Egypt, and it gives Joseph one line — for this.** **Not the dreams, not the
interpretation, not thirteen years of imprisonment, not the famine administration, not the
forgiveness.** **The coffin.** **The New Testament's verdict is that the most faithful thing Joseph
ever did was decline a grave.** See [[Faith]] and [[Hope]].

## Significance

**Genesis ends on an unburied body, and that is a deliberate ending.**

**Every other patriarch in the book is put in the ground and the narrator tells you where.** **Sarah,
Abraham, Isaac, Rebekah, Leah and Jacob are all at Machpelah and the deed is quoted twice**
([[Buried at Machpelah - The Six Graves of Genesis]]); **[[Rachel]] has a pillar on the road to
Bethlehem** (35:20). **Joseph gets a coffin and a promise.** **The book's last image is a piece of
unfinished business, and the unfinished business is the whole argument for the next three books.**

**Notice what the oath assumes.** **It assumes the people will still be a people; it assumes they
will still be in Egypt long enough for this to be a problem; it assumes somebody will still know
where the box is; and it assumes God will do something nobody has been told the timing of.** **It is
faith in the most literal available form: a man betting his remains on a future he has only heard
about secondhand.**

**And it is the last recorded act of the patriarchal age.** **The next thing that happens in the
Bible is a king who does not know Joseph** (Exodus 1:8).
