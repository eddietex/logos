# Lending Without Interest - Exodus 22.25 and the Poor Brother

## Passages involved

- [[A Good Name]] — Proverbs 22:7.

- **Exodus 22:25** — *"If you lend money to any of my people with you who is poor, you shall not be
  to him as a creditor. You shall not charge him interest."* — [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]]
- **Leviticus 25:35–37** — *"If your brother has become poor … Take no interest from him or profit,
  but fear your God; that your brother may live among you. You shall not lend him your money at
  interest, nor give him your food for profit."* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Deuteronomy 23:19–20** — *"You shall not lend on interest to your brother … You may lend on
  interest to a foreigner; but to your brother you shall not lend on interest"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Psalm 15:5** — the one who may dwell on God's hill is *"he who doesn't lend out his money for
  usury"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Ezekiel 18:8** — the righteous man *"hasn't lent to them with interest, hasn't taken any
  increase from them"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Nehemiah 5:7** — *"You exact usury, everyone of his brother"* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Exodus 22:25 is the first form of the rule, and it is the narrowest.** It concerns a loan of
*money* to one of *"my people"* who is *poor*, and it says two things: do not act *kənōšeh*, *as a
creditor*, and do not put *nešek*, *interest*, on him (MAM 22:24). **The borrower is named first as
God's people — *ʾet-ʿammî* — and only then as poor.** The verse gives no penalty; the next two verses,
on the pledged cloak, supply the reason in God's voice: *"I am gracious"* (22:27).

**The later forms widen the rule in three directions.**

| | Exodus 22:25 | Leviticus 25:35–37 | Deuteronomy 23:19–20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| the borrower | *"my people … who is poor"* | *"your brother"* who *"has become poor"* | *"your brother"* (poverty not mentioned) |
| what is lent | money | money and food | money, food, *"anything that is lent on interest"* |
| the reason | God hears the poor (22:27) | *"fear your God; that your brother may live among you"* | *"that Yahweh your God may bless you"* |
| the foreigner | not mentioned | the poor brother lives *"like an alien"* | *"You may lend on interest to a foreigner"* |

**Deuteronomy is the only one of the three that names an exception**, and it does so by distinguishing
*brother* from *foreigner*. Exodus 22:25 neither allows nor forbids interest from a foreigner; the
wiki does not read Deuteronomy's permission back into it, or Exodus's silence forward. Leviticus
compares the poor brother to *"an alien and a temporary resident"* even while forbidding interest
from him — **the poor Israelite is to be treated with at least the protection the law gives the
alien** in Exodus 22:21.

**The Psalms and prophets turn the rule into a mark of character.** Psalm 15:5 lists not lending at
usury among the qualities of the one who may dwell with God; Ezekiel 18:8 includes it in the
portrait of the righteous man. **Nehemiah 5:7 shows it broken** after the return, by Israelites
against their own brothers.

**The noun *nešek* is often connected with a verb *to bite*** — interest as what bites the borrower.
That derivation is a common one; it was not checked against a lexicon in this pass.

### Proverbs 22 in context

Proverbs 22:7 observes the power this law limits: *"The rich rule over the poor. The borrower is
servant to the lender."* The saying describes what debt does; it does not recommend it. Exodus
22:25–27 forbids the creditor's posture toward a poor neighbor and returns his pledged cloak by
sunset, which is the law's answer to the dependence the proverb names. The next pericope continues
with surety and the bed taken from under the debtor (22:26–27, not yet ingested).

## Significance

- **It is the clearest case of a single Sinai ordinance becoming a thread**: one sentence in the
  Book of the Covenant, restated twice in later law, and quoted as a test of righteousness by a psalm
  and a prophet.
- It belongs with the theme [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] and with
  [[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]], which
  traces the pledge law beside it.
