# The Feast Offerings Numbered - Leviticus 23, Numbers 28-29, Ezra 3, and Ezekiel 45

## Passages involved

- **Leviticus 23:4–44** — the dates, convocations, and rest of the appointed feasts; most days require
  *"an offering made by fire to Yahweh"* without numbering it (23:8, 25, 27, 36) —
  [[Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread]], [[First Fruits and the Feast of Weeks]],
  [[Trumpets and the Day of Atonement]], [[The Feast of Booths]].
- **Leviticus 23:18–19** — at the first fruits, *"seven lambs without defect a year old, one young
  bull, and two rams"*, a goat, and two lambs for peace offerings — [[First Fruits and the Feast of Weeks]].
- **Numbers 28:16–29:39** — the animals for every feast day, each set *besides the continual burnt
  offering* — [[The Offerings of the Appointed Feasts]].
- **Numbers 28:27** — at the first fruits, *"two young bulls, one ram, seven male lambs a year old"* —
  [[The Offerings of the Appointed Feasts]].
- **2 Chronicles 8:12–13** — Solomon offers *"according to the commandment of Moses, on the Sabbaths, on
  the new moons, and on the set feasts, three times per year"* (not yet ingested).
- **Ezra 3:4–5** — the returned exiles *"kept the feast of tents, as it is written, and offered the
  daily burnt offerings by number, according to the ordinance, as the duty of every day required; and
  afterward the continual burnt offering, the offerings of the new moons, of all the set feasts of
  Yahweh"* (not yet ingested).
- **Nehemiah 8:18** — *"They kept the feast seven days; and on the eighth day was a solemn assembly,
  according to the ordinance"* (not yet ingested).
- **Ezekiel 45:21–25** — at Passover the prince provides *"seven bulls and seven rams without defect
  daily the seven days; and a male goat daily for a sin offering"*, and does the same at the
  seventh-month feast (not yet ingested).

(Leviticus and Numbers from stored WEB; the others verified at bible-api.com, `translation=web`,
2026-11-05.)

## Analysis

**Leviticus dates the feasts; Numbers counts them.** Leviticus 23 is organized around the calendar
and the people: when each day falls, that it is a holy convocation, what work stops, what Israel does
— eat unleavened bread, wave the sheaf, afflict their souls, live in shelters. For the offerings it
mostly says only *an offering made by fire*. Numbers 28–29 is organized around the altar: it repeats
the dates briefly and gives, for each, the bulls, rams, lambs, and goat, with the meal offering by
measure. The two read as complementary for most of the calendar. Neither refers to the other.

**One day where they disagree.** On the day of the first fruits, Leviticus 23:18 has one bull and two
rams; Numbers 28:27 has two bulls and one ram. Both have seven lambs and a goat; only Leviticus adds
two lambs as peace offerings. The Hebrew numbers agree with WEB in each verse (MAM via Sefaria). Neither
text says the sets are separate or that one supersedes the other. The wiki records the disagreement
and does not resolve it.

**Kept by number.** Ezra 3:4 says the returned community *offered the daily burnt offerings by number,
according to the ordinance* at the feast of tents. The phrase fits the day-by-day counts of Numbers
29:12–34, where each day of that feast has its own number of bulls and the meal offerings are
*according to their number, after the ordinance* (29:18). Ezra does not cite Numbers by name; *as it is
written* points to the law of Moses (Ezra 3:2). Nehemiah 8:18 records the eighth-day solemn assembly
*according to the ordinance*, and 2 Chronicles 8:13 places the Sabbaths, new moons, and set feasts
together under *the commandment of Moses* — the same layering Numbers 28–29 sets out.

**Ezekiel prescribes other numbers.** Ezekiel 45:21–25 covers the same two seven-day feasts but with
seven bulls and seven rams each day, a goat, and an ephah of meal per bull and ram, offered by the
prince. Numbers has two bulls, one ram, and seven lambs for Unleavened Bread and a descending series of
bulls for the feast of tents. Ezekiel does not mention Numbers. As with Ezekiel 46 and the Sabbath and
new moon (see [[Sabbath and New Moon - Numbers 28, Ezekiel 46, Isaiah 66, and Colossians 2]]), the
difference is flagged here and not harmonized.

## Significance

The calendar of Israel's worship is given in two complementary texts and remembered in later ones by
its numbers. That the post-exilic books stress doing it *by number* and *according to the ordinance*
treats the counts of Numbers 28–29 as something to be kept exactly. Ezekiel's different counts for the
same feasts leave open how his vision relates to the Mosaic prescription; the texts do not say. See
[[Festivals and First Fruits]] and [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
