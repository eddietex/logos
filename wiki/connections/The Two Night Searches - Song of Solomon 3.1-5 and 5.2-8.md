# The Two Night Searches - Song of Solomon 3.1-5 and 5.2-8

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 3:1–5** — [[Seeking Him by Night]]: on her bed at night she seeks him, goes
  about the city, is found by the watchmen, and finds him.
- **Song of Solomon 5:2–8** *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)* — asleep with her heart awake, she
  hears him knock, opens too late, seeks him, is found and beaten by the watchmen, and does not find
  him.

Hebrew of both read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

## Analysis

**The same scene, told twice.** The Song has two night searches by the woman, and they share whole
phrases.

| | Song 3:1–5 | Song 5:2–8 |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | *"By night on my bed"* (3:1) | *"I was asleep, but my heart was awake"* (5:2) |
| Why he is missing | not said | he knocks, she delays, and when she opens *"my beloved left; and had gone away"* (5:6) |
| The search | *"I sought him, but I didn't find him"* (3:1, 2) | *"I looked for him, but I didn't find him. I called him, but he didn't answer"* (5:6) |
| The watchmen | *"The watchmen who go about the city found me"* (3:3) | *"The watchmen who go about the city found me. They beat me. They bruised me"* (5:7) |
| What she says to them | *"Have you seen him whom my soul loves?"* (3:3) | nothing recorded |
| The end | *"I found him … I held him, and would not let him go"* (3:4) | she does not find him |
| The adjuration | not to awaken love *"until it so desires"* (3:5) | *"If you find my beloved, that you tell him that I am faint with love"* (5:8) |

The Hebrew shares more than the English shows. *Biqqaštîw wǝlōʾ mǝṣāʾtîw*, *I sought him and did
not find him*, at 3:1–2 becomes *biqqaštîhû wǝlōʾ mǝṣāʾtîhû* at 5:6, the same verbs with a longer
pronoun suffix. The watchmen's line is word for word: *mǝṣāʾûnî haššōmǝrîm hassōḇǝḇîm bāʿîr* (3:3;
5:7, with a different vowel in the first verb). The WEB's *"I sought him"* (3:1) and *"I looked for
him"* (5:6) are the same Hebrew verb, *b-q-š*, in different English.

**What changes.** In the first search the watchmen only find her, she finds him almost at once,
and she brings him home. In the second, she let him go herself, the watchmen beat her, the keepers
of the walls take her cloak, and the search ends with a request to the daughters of Jerusalem, not
with him. The first ends with *do not awaken love*; the second with *tell him I am faint with
love*, the words she used of herself in 2:5.

**Reading the pair.** Readers often see the second search as a darker repetition of the first, or
the first as the hope and the second as the experience. Some read both as dreams, because both begin
in bed and the second explicitly with sleep. The wiki records the parallels and the differences. It
does not claim the two scenes form one story in sequence, because the Song is not plainly a single
narrative. Song 5:2–8 is not yet ingested; this page will be revised when it is.

## Significance

Both searches belong to the woman. The Song gives her the initiative not only in desire (1:2) but
in looking for the one she loves at night, through a city, at some risk. The second search shows
the risk realized. Both are part of the Song's pattern of absence and presence: the beloved comes
and goes, is called and sent, is sought and found or not. See [[Love]] and
[[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]].
