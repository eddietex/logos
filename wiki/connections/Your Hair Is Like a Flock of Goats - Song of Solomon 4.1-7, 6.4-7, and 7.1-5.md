# Your Hair Is Like a Flock of Goats - Song of Solomon 4.1-7, 6.4-7, and 7.1-5

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 4:1–7** — [[You Are All Beautiful]]: the man describes the woman from her eyes
  down to her breasts, framed by *"Behold, you are beautiful"* and *"You are all beautiful"*.
- **Song of Solomon 6:4–7** — [[My Dove Is Unique]] — *"Your hair is like a flock of goats,
  that lie along the side of Gilead. Your teeth are like a flock of ewes, which have come up from the
  washing"* (6:5–6).
- **Song of Solomon 7:1–5** — [[Return, Return, Shulammite]] — a description from the
  feet up, which repeats *"Your two breasts are like two fawns, that are twins of a roe"* (7:3).
- **Song of Solomon 5:10–16** — [[Altogether Lovely]] — the woman's description of the man,
  ending *"he is altogether lovely"* (5:16).
- **Song of Solomon 1:15** — [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]]: *"Behold, you are beautiful, my
  love. Behold, you are beautiful. Your eyes are doves."*

Hebrew of Song 1:15; 4:1–7; 6:5–7; and 7:4 (MT) read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the
Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

- [[Return, Return, Shulammite]] — Song of Solomon 6:11–7:9.

## Analysis

**Four descriptions of a body.** The Song describes a body part by part four times. Three describe
the woman (4:1–7; 6:4–7; 7:1–5), and one describes the man (5:10–16). Scholars often call the form a
*wasf*, an Arabic word for *description*, by comparison with modern Arabic wedding songs; the wiki
uses the term as a label only.

**The second description repeats the first.** 6:5b–7 takes over three lines of 4:1–3 almost word for
word (Sefaria):

| | Song 4:1–3 | Song 6:5–7 |
|---|---|---|
| Hair | *šeggālǝšû mēhar gilʿāḏ*, *that descend from Mount Gilead* | *šeggālǝšû min-haggilʿāḏ*, *that descend from Gilead* |
| Teeth | *kǝʿēḏer haqqǝṣûḇôṯ*, *a flock of shorn ones* | *kǝʿēḏer hārǝḥēlîm*, *a flock of ewes* |
| Twins | *šekkullām maṯʾîmôṯ wǝšakkulâ ʾên bāhem* | the same words |
| Temples | *kǝp̄elaḥ hārimmôn raqqāṯēḵ mibbaʿaḏ lǝṣammāṯēḵ* | the same words |

What changes is the frame. In chapter 4 the man begins with *"Behold, you are beautiful"*. In
chapter 6 he asks *"Turn away your eyes from me, for they have overcome me"* (6:5), after comparing
her to Tirzah, Jerusalem, and *"an army with banners"* (6:4). Her eyes were doves in 4:1; in 6:5 they
overpower him. The lips and the neck of 4:3–4 are not repeated.

**The third description repeats one line.** At 7:3 (7:4 MT), *šǝnê šāḏayiḵ kišnê ʿŏp̄ārîm tāʾŏmê
ṣǝḇiyyâ* is 4:5 without its last words, *hārôʿîm baššôšannîm*, *feeding among the lilies*. In 7:2 the
lilies appear elsewhere, around *"a heap of wheat"*. Like 4:4, 7:4 compares her neck to a tower, but an
ivory tower, not David's tower hung with shields. The description in chapter 7 begins from the feet,
the reverse of chapter 4. 7:1–5 is now ingested; the passage page distinguishes likely voices from explicit labels.

**His description and hers.** The woman's description of the man (5:10–16) ends with *"he is
altogether lovely"*. That is a different summary from the man's *"You are all beautiful"* (4:7), but
both sum up the whole person. That unit is now ingested in [[Altogether Lovely]].

**The woman's answer to a question.** Now ingested in [[Altogether Lovely]], 5:10–16 responds
to the audience's question about what makes her beloved exceptional (5:9). He is praised
through a sequence of body parts just as she was in 4:1–7. His eyes are like doves (5:12),
and the description ends with the whole person and the word *friend* (5:16). She praises
him to others rather than addressing him directly. The recurrence makes praise reciprocal
without making the two descriptions identical. Source: stored WEB Song 4:1–7; 5:9–16.

**The second description in its whole unit.** Now ingested in [[My Dove Is Unique]], the repeated
hair, teeth, and temples imagery belongs to a frame of cities, overpowering eyes, the
unique dove, and other women's admiration (6:4–10). The army-with-banners comparison at
6:4 returns at 6:10. The description does not restore an earlier scene unchanged: praise
moves outward to public recognition. The eye's effect also recalls 4:9 in
[[A Garden Locked and Opened]]. Source for this addition: stored WEB Song 4:1–9; 6:4–10;
no new source-language claim.

The third description is now ingested in [[Return, Return, Shulammite]]. It rises
from feet to head (7:1–5), then moves into palm, fruit, fragrance, and wine (7:6–9).
Twin fawns and a tower recur from chapter 4; the eyes are pools and the hair holds a
king captive. The admiration affects the observer, as her glance did at 4:9 and 6:5.
The WEB's Lover cue at 6:13 supports the lover's voice, but does not conclusively label
every subsequent line; the turn to my beloved in 7:9 may mark the woman's response.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Song 6:11–7:9 and the Song comparisons cited above.
No new source-language claim is made.

## Significance

The repetitions show that the Song uses fixed phrases. A description, once made, can be said again with
a new frame, as the refrain of 2:7 is said again at 3:5 and 8:4. The wiki does not take the repetitions
as evidence of one author or of several. They do show that the man's praise of the woman is not a
single moment in a story. It returns, changed, at the start of the Song's second half. See
[[Seeing and Being Seen]], [[Love]], and [[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]].
