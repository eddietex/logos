# Swallowed Up - Pharaoh's Dream, Aaron's Staff, and the Sea

- **Exodus 7:12** — *"Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods"* — [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]]
- **Exodus 15:12** — *"You stretched out your right hand. The earth swallowed them."* —
  [[The Song of the Sea]]
- **Hebrews 11:29** — *"When the Egyptians tried to do so, they were swallowed up"* (WEB) —
  [[Baptized into Moses - The Sea in 1 Corinthians 10 and Hebrews 11]] *(not yet ingested)*
- **Numbers 16:30–34; 26:10** — the earth swallows Korah's company: *"The earth opened its mouth and
  swallowed them up, with their households, all of Korah's men, and all their goods"* (16:32; WEB,

## Analysis

**The verb is *bālaʿ*, *to swallow*, and in Genesis and Exodus it occurs at only four verses**:
Genesis 41:7 and 24, Exodus 7:12, and Exodus 15:12 (WLC lemma search). **All four are about Egypt.**

**In Genesis it is the verb of a Pharaoh's dream.** The seven thin ears *swallow* the seven full
ones, and Pharaoh repeats the word when he tells the dream to Joseph (41:24). The dream is the one
*"all of Egypt's magicians and wise men"* could not interpret (41:8). **What is swallowed is Egypt's
plenty**, and Joseph reads it as famine consuming the good years.

**In Exodus 7 the swallowing happens in front of the same guild.** *"The magicians of Egypt"* —
*ḥarṭummê miṣrayim*, the phrase of Genesis 41:8 — match Aaron's sign, and *"Aaron's rod swallowed up
their rods"* (7:12). **The subject and object are both *rods*, not serpents**: the contest is told
as one of staffs, the sign of authority. **What is swallowed is the court's power to match.**

**In Exodus 15 it is the end of the army.** *"You stretched out your right hand. The earth swallowed
them"* (15:12) — the Song of the Sea, after the chariots go down. **The first sign before Pharaoh and
the last act against him use the same verb.** And the *"stretched out … hand"* of the song is the
*nāṭâ yād* promised at 7:5, *"when I stretch out my hand on Egypt"*. (Forms checked in the WLC.)

**Numbers turns the verb on Israel.** When Korah, Aaron's cousin from
[[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]], rises against Moses and Aaron, the earth *swallows* his company
(Numbers 16:30, 32, 34; 26:10; WLC). **The verb that ended Egypt's opposition ends the first
rebellion against Aaron's priesthood** — and that rebellion is decided, in Numbers 17, by another
sign of Aaron's rod (by reference; not yet ingested).

## Significance

**The thread is lexical, and the text never draws it.** No verse says that Aaron's swallowing rod
answers Pharaoh's swallowing ears. But the verb is rare, the setting is the same court, the same
guild is present, and the same root ends the story at the sea. **Read in order, *bālaʿ* tracks Egypt
from the dream that saved it to the water that closed over it**, with Aaron's rod at the turn.

**The prose of the crossing does not use the verb.** Exodus 14 drowns the army with *kāsâ*, *covered*
(14:28), and *nāʿar*, *shook off* (14:27); *bālaʿ* waits for the Song (15:12) (WLC). **Hebrews 11:29 keeps the
Song's image for the prose event** — *katepothēsan*, *they were swallowed up* (SBLGNT), from *katapinō*.
Which Greek verb the Septuagint uses at Exodus 15:12 is not checked in this pass. See
[[The Crossing of the Sea]].
