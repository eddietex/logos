# A False Witness Shall Not Be Unpunished - Proverbs 19 and Exodus 23

## Passages involved

- [[The Glory of Kings]] — Proverbs 25:18.
- [[The Field of the Sluggard]] — Proverbs 24:28–29.
- [[The King's Heart]] — Proverbs 21:28.

- [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] — Proverbs 19:5, 9, 28–29.
- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:16.
- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:1–8.

## Analysis

Verses 5 and 9 of Proverbs 19 share the same first sentence in the stored WEB:

> A false witness shall not be unpunished.

Their second sentences differ: verse 5 denies escape to the one pouring out lies, while verse 9 says
the liar will perish. Verse 28 brings the judicial stakes into view: corrupt witness mocks justice.
Verse 29 ends the chapter with penalties for scoffers and beatings for fools. The chapter supplies
no named case, tribunal, or schedule specifying how the witness's punishment is carried out.

**Exodus supplies the legal setting.** Exodus 20:16 prohibits false testimony against one's
neighbor. Exodus 23:1–2 forbids false reports, malicious witness, and testimony that follows the
crowd into injustice. Verses 3 and 6 refuse both favoritism toward the poor and denial of their
justice; verses 7–8 forbid false charges and bribes. Truthful testimony protects a person's case
from both social pressure and purchased advantage.

**The genres do different work.** Exodus commands conduct and names particular distortions of
justice. Proverbs characterizes the liar and insists that false witness has consequences. Agreement
in moral concern does not demonstrate direct quotation, nor does it mean Proverbs has imported every
legal procedure from the Torah. The comparison is based on the verified WEB English only.

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 19:5, 9, 28–29; Exodus 20:16; 23:1–8. The repeated sentence is an
English-text observation, not a claim about the form of a Hebrew edition.

### Proverbs 21 in context

Proverbs 21:28 restates the warning: *"A false witness will perish, and a man who listens speaks to
eternity."* The listener's lasting word is set against the liar's end; the saying gives no court
procedure.

See [[The King's Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 21; no new source-language claim.

### The witness without cause — Proverbs 24:28–29

*"Don't be a witness against your neighbor without cause. Don't deceive with your lips. Don't say,
'I will do to him as he has done to me; I will render to the man according to his work.'"* The saying
names the false witness's likeliest motive: retaliation. *Without cause* is *ḥinnām* (also 1:11;
3:30), and *deceive* is *pāṯāh*, the sinners' *enticing* of 1:10. Deuteronomy 19:19 turned retaliation
*against* the lying witness — *"you shall do to him as he had thought to do to his brother"* (WEB
verified at bible-api.com) — and Proverbs forbids the retaliator to become a witness in the first
place. See [[As He Has Done to Me - Proverbs 24.29 and the Law of Retaliation]].

See [[The Field of the Sluggard]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 24:28–29; Hebrew of Proverbs 1:10;
24:28–29 read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-26.

### The ninth commandment as a simile — Proverbs 25:18

*"A man who gives false testimony against his neighbor is like a club, a sword, or a sharp arrow."*
In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26) its second line, *ʾîš ʿōneh
ḇərēʿēhû ʿēḏ šāqer*, is Exodus 20:16's *lōʾ-ṯaʿăneh ḇərēʿăḵā ʿēḏ šāqer* (20:13 in that edition's
numbering) turned from command into description: the same verb *ʿānāh*, *to answer* or *testify*, the
same *against your neighbour*, the same *false witness*. Proverbs 19 and 21 promised the false witness
punishment; 25:18 says instead what his testimony does to its victim — it strikes like weapons at
three ranges. See [[The Glory of Kings]] and [[The Ten Commandments]].

Source: stored WEB Exodus 20:16; Proverbs 25:18.

## Significance

[[Language and Speech]] and [[Deception]] belong to [[Judgment]] because testimony changes what
happens to other people. Proverbs' insistence on accountability reinforces the legal demand for
truthful witness without erasing the wiki's separate account of courts that actually behave
unjustly.
