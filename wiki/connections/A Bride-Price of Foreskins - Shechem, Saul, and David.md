# A Bride-Price of Foreskins - Shechem, Saul, and David

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 34:8–19, 25** — [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] — Hamor: *Make marriages with us*; Shechem: *Ask me a
  great amount for a dowry*; Jacob's sons answer *with deceit*: *We can't do this thing, to give our sister to one who is
  uncircumcised*; *he had delight in Jacob's daughter*; *on the third day, when they were sore*, the males are killed
  (stored WEB)
- **Exodus 22:16–17** — [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — the seducer *shall surely pay a dowry for her to be his
  wife*, or *money according to the dowry of virgins* (stored WEB)
- **1 Samuel 18:20–27** — [[David Marries Michal]] — *that she may be a snare to him*; *the king has delight in you*;
  *The king desires no dowry except one hundred foreskins of the Philistines*; *Saul thought he would make David fall by
  the hand of the Philistines*; two hundred brought (stored WEB)
- **2 Samuel 3:14** — David to Ish-bosheth: *my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to me for a hundred foreskins of the
  Philistines* (WLC; paraphrased; not yet ingested)

## Analysis

**Twice in the books stored, a father's household answers a request for marriage with a condition of foreskins, and
both times the condition is a trap the narrator exposes.** The words cluster. *Mōhar*, *dowry* (morphhb lemma 4119),
occurs in the Hebrew files checked only at Genesis 34:12, Exodus 22:16–17, and 1 Samuel 18:25. The *hitpael* of
*ḥāṯan*, *make marriage, become son-in-law* (2859 b), occurs only at Genesis 34:9, Deuteronomy 7:3, Joshua 23:12, and
five times in 1 Samuel 18:21–27. *ʿOrlâ*, *foreskin* (6190), is in both chapters (Genesis 34:14; 1 Samuel 18:25, 27).
And *ḥāp̄ēṣ*, *delight* (2654 a), names the suitor's desire at Genesis 34:19 and the king's pretended delight at
1 Samuel 18:22, while its noun says what the king does *not* desire, a dowry (18:25) (WLC).

| | **Genesis 34** | **1 Samuel 18** |
|---|---|---|
| **who asks** | Shechem and Hamor, for Dinah | no one; Saul offers, through servants |
| **the bride** | Dinah, silent; *defiled* (34:13) | Michal, who *loved David* (18:20) |
| **the dowry** | *whatever you ask* (34:12) | *no dowry except one hundred foreskins* (18:25) |
| **the condition** | *every male of you be circumcised* (34:15) | Philistine foreskins (18:25) |
| **the deceit, named by** | the narrator: *with deceit*, *bǝmirmâ* (34:13) | the narrator: *Saul thought …* (18:25; also 17, 21) |
| **whom the trap is for** | the suitors | the suitor |
| **the outcome** | the trap closes; the men killed while sore (34:25) | the trap fails; the suitor pays it twice (18:27) |

**The roles are reversed.** At Shechem the suitor is a foreigner and the circumcision is asked of him; the trap is
laid by the bride's brothers, and it works. At Saul's court the suitor is an Israelite and the foreskins are asked of
the foreigners, the *uncircumcised* Philistines of Judges 14:3 and 1 Samuel 14:6; 17:26, 36 (stored WEB). The trap is
laid by the bride's father against the groom, and it fails. Jacob's sons meant the circumcision to disable the men who
took it; Saul meant the foreskins to cost David his life, *by the hand of the Philistines* (18:25), and they cost
two hundred Philistines theirs.

**Both are marked as deceit by the narrator, not by a character.** Genesis 34:13 says outright that the brothers
*answered … with deceit*. 1 Samuel never uses *mirmâ* here, but it opens Saul's thoughts three times (18:17, 21, 25),
and the servants are told to speak *secretly* (18:22). In both, the bride's family speaks the language of honor, *that
is a reproach to us* (Genesis 34:14), *to be avenged of the king's enemies* (1 Samuel 18:25), and means a death.

**The law in between.** Exodus 22:16–17 makes the *mōhar* the ordinary obligation of a man who has taken a woman who
was not betrothed: he pays it and marries her, or pays it if her father refuses. That is close to Shechem's own offer
(Genesis 34:12), made after the act, which the law will later require; the brothers refuse it. The law does not speak to
a dowry paid in anything but money (stored WEB).

### What the parallel does not settle

- **Neither text points to the other.** No verse in 1 Samuel recalls Shechem. The link is in the shared vocabulary and
  the shared shape, and the wiki records it as a parallel, not as an allusion.
- **The numbers.** Saul asks a hundred (18:25); David brings two hundred (18:27); David later names a hundred as the
  price of his betrothal (2 Samuel 3:14, WLC). The Greek reads a hundred at 18:27 (recalled, not checked). Both counts
  are kept.
- **Whether David knew.** Genesis tells the reader the Shechemites did not know. 1 Samuel does not say what David
  knew, only that *it pleased David well* (18:26).

## Significance

- **It shows marriage as an instrument of violence in both families.** Dinah is not asked, and Michal's love is the
  thing Saul uses. See [[Marriage]] and [[Deception]].
- **It turns circumcision into a price.** The covenant sign of Genesis 17 is made a pretext at Shechem and a trophy at
  Saul's court, and in neither case is it asked for its own sake. See [[Circumcision]].
- **It sets Saul beside Jacob's sons, not beside Jacob.** Jacob objected afterward, *You have troubled me* (Genesis
  34:30, stored WEB); in 1 Samuel the plotter is the father himself. See [[Saul]] and
  [[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]].
