# Every Word of God Is Flawless - Proverbs 30.5-6, Psalm 18.30, and Deuteronomy 4.2

## Passages involved

- [[The Words of Agur]] — Proverbs 30:5–6.
- [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] — Genesis 15:1, God as Abram’s shield.
- Psalm 18:30 and 2 Samuel 22:31 — God’s tested word and refuge; verified WEB, not yet ingested.
- [[Hear the Statutes and Remember Horeb]] — Deuteronomy 4:2, do not add to or take away from the command.
- [[Do Not Follow Other Gods]] — Deuteronomy 12:32 (WLC 13:1), the same prohibition before the
  cases of enticement to other gods.

## Analysis

Proverbs 30:5 puts God’s flawless word beside his protection of those who take refuge in him.
Psalm 18:30 and its parallel in 2 Samuel 22:31 likewise join the tested word with the shield.
The verified WEB uses “tried” in the psalm and “tested” in Samuel, while Proverbs has “flawless.”
This English comparison establishes a close pairing of ideas, not exact Hebrew identity or a
direction of borrowing. Genesis 15:1 gives the shield image in a personal promise to Abram;
Proverbs speaks generally of those who take refuge.

Verse 6 turns trust in the word into a limit on the interpreter: adding to God’s words risks
reproof and exposure as a liar. Deuteronomy 4:2 similarly forbids addition, and also subtraction,
so that the commandments may be kept. Proverbs 30:6 states the addition warning; its immediate
context does not reproduce both halves of Deuteronomy’s instruction.

Neither verse supplies a list of canonical books or rules out all explanation. Both warn
against altering what is received under divine authority. The wiki’s comparisons and inferences
therefore need to remain distinguishable from the cited wording.

**Explicit tension:** a shield cannot be converted into an empirical guarantee that no trusting
person will suffer. Job 2:3, 7 affirms the sufferer’s integrity and narrates harm; see
[[The Second Test]]. Proverbs 30:5’s protective claim and that counterevidence remain together.

Sources: Proverbs 30:5–6, Genesis 15:1, and Job 2:3, 7 read in raw WEB. Psalm 18:30, 2 Samuel
22:31, and Deuteronomy 4:2 verified directly at bible-api.com with translation=web on 2026-10-26.
Responses identified World English Bible; no whole book fetched or added to raw. No Hebrew
identity is asserted.

**With Deuteronomy 4 now ingested**, the stored WEB confirms the verse: *You shall not add to the word
which I command you, neither shall you take away from it, that you may keep the commandments of Yahweh
your God which I command you.* Its context is Moses' teaching of statutes for life in the land (4:1, 5),
so the prohibition protects obedience to the commanded word; it is not framed, as Proverbs 30:6 is, as a
warning against being found a liar.

**Deuteronomy 12:32 now ingested.** *"Whatever thing I command you, that you shall observe to do.
You shall not add to it, nor take away from it."* In the Hebrew it repeats 4:2's two verbs, *lōʾ
tōsēp* and *lōʾ tigraʿ*, with the same object, the word *that I command you* (WLC Deuteronomy 4:2 and
13:1, morphhb). The setting differs: in 4:2 it introduces the statutes; here it stands between the
ban on the nations' rites (12:29–31) and a prophet whose fulfilled sign urges other gods (13:1–5).
That prophet is the case of someone adding to the command with apparent divine backing, and
Deuteronomy refuses it. This is the wiki's reading of the sequence; the text does not draw the link
in so many words.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:2, 12:32–13:5; WLC (morphhb), retrieved
2026-11-06.

## Significance

[[Faith]] in God’s speech calls for restraint in [[Language and Speech]] about God. The shield
and the prohibition on adding belong together: refuge is located in the trustworthy speaker,
not in the interpreter’s ability to improve his words.
