# Amen to the Curse - Numbers 5 and Deuteronomy 27

## Passages involved

- [[The Suspected Wife]] — Numbers 5:19–22.
- [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]] — Deuteronomy 27:14–26.

## Analysis

Numbers 5:22 directs the woman to answer Amen twice to a conditional curse in an ordeal
concerning suspected unfaithfulness. Deuteronomy 27 directs all the people to answer Amen
after each of twelve curses. In both, the respondent acknowledges a sanction spoken by a
religious minister; neither response is a simple declaration that the wrongdoing occurred.

The differences matter: an individual suspected wife undergoes an ordeal involving water in
Numbers 5, whereas an entire assembly acknowledges a range of obligations in Deuteronomy 27.
No water ordeal, test of guilt, or confession by each participant is prescribed here.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers 5:19–22 and Deuteronomy 27:14–26. This compares the English texts
and their ritual functions without asserting a source-language derivation.

## Significance

The recurring response connects individual and communal accountability while preserving the distinct rites.
