# Favor with God and Man - Proverbs 3.4, 1 Samuel 2.26, and Luke 2.52

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 3:3–4** — *"Don't let kindness and truth forsake you … So you will find favor, and good
  understanding in the sight of God and man"* (*ûməṣāʾ-ḥēn wəśēḵel-ṭôḇ bəʿênê ʾĕlōhîm wəʾāḏām*) —
  [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]]
- **Genesis 39:4, 21** — *"Joseph found favor in his sight"*; *"Yahweh was with Joseph, and showed
  kindness to him, and gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison"* —
  [[Joseph in Potiphar's House]]
- **1 Samuel 2:26** — *"The child Samuel grew on, and increased in favor both with Yahweh, and also
  with men."* (WEB, verified; not yet ingested)
- **Luke 2:40, 52** — *"The child was growing, and was becoming strong in spirit, being filled with
  wisdom, and the grace of God was upon him"*; *"And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favor
  with God and men."* (WEB, verified; not yet ingested)

## Analysis

**Proverbs states as a promise what the narratives report as a fact.** Proverbs 3:4 promises the son
who keeps kindness and truth that he will *find favour* (*ḥēn*) *in the sight of God and man*. Three
narratives describe a young man of whom exactly this was true.

- **Joseph** is the Genesis case, and the closest in vocabulary. *Find favour in the sight of* is the
  idiom of Genesis 39:4 (*wayyimṣāʾ yôsēp̄ ḥēn bəʿênāyw*), and Genesis 39:21 puts *ḥeseḏ* and *ḥēn*
  together — *"showed kindness to him, and gave him favor"* — as Proverbs 3:3–4 puts kindness and
  truth beside favour. The difference is the direction: in Genesis *Yahweh* shows *ḥeseḏ* to Joseph
  and gives him favour; in Proverbs the son keeps *ḥeseḏ* and so finds it. Joseph's favour with men is
  also not a reward for keeping anything: it is how he ends up in prison, and it is what he finds
  there.
- **Samuel** grows *"both with Yahweh, and also with men"* (1 Samuel 2:26). The WEB's *favor* does not
  translate *ḥēn* here: the Hebrew says the boy went on growing *wāṭôḇ*, *and good*, *gam ʿim-YHWH
  wəḡam ʿim-ʾănāšîm*. The pairing *Yahweh and men* is the point of contact, not the noun.
- **Jesus** *"increased in wisdom and stature, and in favor with God and men"* (Luke 2:52, *χάριτι παρὰ
  θεῷ καὶ ἀνθρώποις*, SBLGNT). Luke's verse is usually read as modelled on 1 Samuel 2:26, the growth
  of another boy dedicated in the sanctuary, and its pairing of *God and men* matches Proverbs 3:4's
  *ʾĕlōhîm wəʾāḏām* as well. Which text Luke had in mind — or whether both — is not something the
  verse settles; the wiki reports both parallels. Luke adds *wisdom*, which is Proverbs' subject, and
  Luke 2:40 has already said *"the grace of God was upon him"*.

**Septuagint not checked.** The Greek of Proverbs 3:4 is reported to differ from the Hebrew (to speak
of *providing good things* before the Lord and men); if so, Luke's *χάριτι* would be closer to the
Hebrew *ḥēn* than to the Greek Proverbs. That has not been checked against a Septuagint edition and is
recorded as unverified.

**A tension the narratives raise, not resolved.** Proverbs promises favour with God *and man*. Joseph's
favour with men was intermittent — sold by his brothers, imprisoned on a false charge — and the
Gospels end Jesus' story with the rejection of the one who grew *"in favor with God and men"*. The
promise is true of each boy's growth; it is not a promise of favour without opposition. See
[[Retribution]].

## Significance

- It gives [[Favor and Grace]] a line from wisdom instruction to the boyhood narratives of Samuel and
  Jesus.
- It shows Proverbs' sentences functioning in later narrative as description: Luke describes Jesus in
  terms a reader of Proverbs would recognise as the wise son's.
- It joins [[Joseph]], already the wiki's chief example of *favour in the sight of*, to the wisdom
  tradition that later describes the wise man in the same terms.
