# The Valley of Achor - Trouble Turned to a Door of Hope

## Passages involved

- [[The Sin of Achan]] — Joshua 7:24–26; the valley named for Achan's trouble.
- [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] — Genesis 34:30: *"You have troubled me, to make me odious to
  the inhabitants of the land"* (stored WEB). The same verb, *ʿāḵar*.
- Joshua 15:7 — the valley of Achor on Judah's northern border *(stored WEB; not yet ingested)*.
- 1 Chronicles 2:7 — *"The son of Carmi: Achar, the troubler of Israel, who committed a trespass in
  the devoted thing."* *(WEB, verified at bible-api.com, 2026-11-10; not yet ingested)*
- Hosea 2:15 — *"I will give her vineyards from there, and the valley of Achor for a door of hope; and
  she will respond there, as in the days of her youth, and as in the day when she came up out of the
  land of Egypt."* *(same)*
- Isaiah 65:10 — *"Sharon will be a fold of flocks, and the valley of Achor a place for herds to lie
  down in, for my people who have sought me."* *(same)*

## Analysis

**A name made by a verdict.** Joshua's sentence on Achan is a wordplay: *"Why have you troubled us?
Yahweh will trouble you today"* — *meh ʿăḵartānû yaʿkārǝḵā* (WLC Joshua 7:25). The place is then
*ʿēmeq ʿāḵôr* (7:24, 26). The name records the sin and the judgment together, *to this day*.

**The verb before Achor.** Jacob's rebuke of Simeon and Levi uses the same verb: their massacre at
Shechem has *troubled* him by making him *odious to the inhabitants of the land* (Genesis 34:30).
In both texts, a violent or greedy act by some Israelites endangers the whole household before the
Canaanites. That parallel is the wiki's observation from the shared verb and situation; neither text
cites the other.

**The Chronicler's spelling.** 1 Chronicles 2:7 writes the man's name as *Achar* and glosses it
*the troubler of Israel*, so the pun of Joshua 7:25 becomes the name itself. The text does not say
whether the spelling is a variant or a deliberate reshaping.

**The reversal in Hosea.** Hosea 2:15 sets the valley inside a new exodus: Yahweh will lead Israel
into the wilderness, speak tenderly, and give back vineyards, with *the valley of Achor for a door of
hope*, and Israel will answer *as in the day when she came up out of the land of Egypt*. The place
where Israel's entry into the land first stalled becomes the entrance again. Isaiah 65:10 makes the
same valley pasture for *my people who have sought me*. Neither prophet mentions Achan; the reversal
works on the name and the place, and the reader supplies Joshua 7.

## Significance

The valley carries one of Scripture's clearest movements from judgment to restoration attached to a
single place name. A memorial of trouble is not erased — the stones stay *to this day* — but the
prophets promise that the same ground will open onto hope. See [[Valley of Achor]], [[Achan]],
[[Names and Naming]], and [[Remembrance]].
