# Put Away the Foreign Gods - Genesis 35.2-4 and Joshua 24.14-23

## Passages involved

- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:2–4: *"Put away the foreign
  gods that are among you, purify yourselves, change your garments."* The household hands over
  its gods and earrings, and *"Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem."*
- [[The Covenant at Shechem]] — Joshua 24:14: *"Put away the gods which your fathers served beyond
  the River, in Egypt; and serve Yahweh."* Joshua 24:23: *"Now therefore put away the foreign gods
  which are among you, and incline your heart to Yahweh, the God of Israel."*

## Analysis

**Same words, same place.** In the WEB, Jacob's order and Joshua's second order share their key
phrase: *put away the foreign gods … among you*. Both are spoken in connection with Shechem, and
both involve an oak there. Jacob buries the household's gods *under the oak which was by Shechem*
(Genesis 35:4). Joshua sets up the witness stone *under the oak that was by the sanctuary of
Yahweh* (Joshua 24:26). The texts do not say that the two trees are the same tree. The wiki
records the resemblance and does not make the identification; see
[[The Trees of the Patriarchs]] and [[The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem]].

| | Genesis 35:2–4 | Joshua 24:14–27 |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker | Jacob, to his household | Joshua, to all the tribes |
| Command | put away foreign gods, purify, change garments | put away the fathers' gods; put away foreign gods; incline your heart |
| Response | gods and earrings handed over | three pledges: *we will serve Yahweh* |
| What goes under the oak | the gods, hidden | a stone, set up as witness |
| What follows | the journey to Bethel and an altar | a covenant, a written record, dismissal to the inheritances |

**What is handed over.** In Genesis the household acts: *They gave to Jacob all the foreign gods
which were in their hands* (35:4). In Joshua nothing is reported handed over. The people answer in
words, and Joshua's second order comes *after* their pledge. Joshua 24 therefore leaves open
whether the people held images at all, or whether *put away* names a turning of loyalty. Verse
23's *incline your heart* supports the second reading without ruling out the first.

**Where the gods came from.** Genesis does not say where the household's gods came from, though
Rachel had stolen her father's teraphim four chapters earlier (31:19); see
[[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]]. Joshua names three sources: the fathers
*beyond the River*, Egypt, and the Amorites of the land (24:14–15). Jacob's command concerns
gods carried by a family on the move. Joshua's concerns gods a nation might take up now that it
is settled.

## Significance

- Shechem is where the patriarchal family first renounces other gods and where the nation does
  the same. The renunciation is an act of [[Covenant]] preparation in both: Jacob goes on to build
  an altar, and Joshua makes a covenant.
- The thread shows [[Idolatry]] as something already present inside Israel, not only a danger from
  outside. Both speakers say *among you*.
- Neither text narrates destruction. The gods are hidden in Genesis and nothing is said of their
  fate in Joshua. Contrast the commands to break and burn the nations' images (Deuteronomy 7:5,
  stored WEB).
