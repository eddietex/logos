# Jacob Blessed Pharaoh - The Lesser Blessed by the Greater

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 47:7, 10** — [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]]. *"Joseph brought in Jacob, his father,
  and set him before Pharaoh, and Jacob blessed Pharaoh… Jacob blessed Pharaoh, and went out from the
  presence of Pharaoh."*
- **Genesis 12:2–3** — [[The Call of Abram]]. *"I will bless those who bless you… All the families of
  the earth will be blessed through you."*
- **Genesis 14:18–20** — [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]. The priest-king blesses Abram, and
  Abram gives him a tenth.
- **Hebrews 7:7** — without any dispute, the lesser is blessed by the greater.

## Analysis

**The scene is four verses long and the narrator reports the blessing twice without saying a word of
it.**

> **7** Joseph brought in Jacob, his father, and set him before Pharaoh, and Jacob blessed Pharaoh.
> **8** Pharaoh said to Jacob, "How many are the days of the years of your life?" **9** Jacob said to
> Pharaoh… **10** Jacob blessed Pharaoh, and went out from the presence of Pharaoh.

***Wayəbārek*** brackets the audience. **It is the first thing he does in the room and the last thing
he does before leaving it**, and Genesis, which has given the words of every significant blessing in
the book — Melchizedek's, Isaac's, the angel's at Peniel — **gives none of these.**

### The asymmetry, stated plainly

| | [[Jacob]] | [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] |
|---|---|---|
| **status** | a resident alien on famine relief | the king of Egypt |
| **property** | a grave in Canaan, and livestock | the whole land, and by 47:20 rather more of it |
| **age** | a hundred and thirty, and he says so | not stated |
| **what he does in the scene** | blesses, twice | asks one question |

**Nothing in the situation licenses the gesture and Genesis performs it anyway.** **The only thing
Jacob has that Pharaoh does not is the promise of 12:2–3**, and the promise is precisely a promise
about this: *"I will bless those who bless you… All the families of the earth will be blessed through
you."* **[[Joseph]] has already been the mechanism of that for seven years — Egypt is alive because a
son of Abraham read a dream — and here the promise is exercised directly, by the patriarch, in a
throne room.** See [[Blessing]] and
[[All the Families of the Earth - Genesis 12.3 in the New Testament]].

### Hebrews supplies the rule the scene runs on

**Hebrews 7:7 states it as a principle nobody disputes: the lesser is blessed by the greater.** **The
argument there is about Melchizedek and Abraham** — the priest-king who blessed the patriarch and
received his tithe, and is therefore, by the rule, the greater of the two. See
[[Melchizedek in Psalm 110 and Hebrews]].

**Genesis 47:7 is the same rule with the parties reversed.** **Abram, who received a blessing from a
king at 14:19, has a grandson who gives one to a king at 47:7**, and the two scenes are the only
royal audiences in Genesis. **The first has the king with bread and wine and the patriarch with a
tithe; the second has the king with grain and land and the patriarch with nothing at all.** **The
direction of the blessing reverses and the direction of the goods does not.**

### What Genesis does not do with it

**It does not report Pharaoh's reaction.** **It does not say the blessing took effect.** **It does not
even say what was said.** **Genesis 30:27 had [[Laban]] state the principle out loud** — *"I have
divined that Yahweh has blessed me for your sake"* — and **39:5 said it of [[Potiphar]]'s house in the
narrator's own voice**: *"Yahweh blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake."* **Here, where the
house is the whole of Egypt, the narrator says only that the old man did it.** See
[[Blessed for His Sake - Laban's House and Potiphar's]].

**The restraint is the wiki's reason for taking the scene seriously rather than as a courtesy
formula.** **A Hebrew greeting at parting can be a blessing in the weak sense**, and 47:10's *blessed
him and went out* would fit that reading on its own. **47:7's *blessed* does not**, because it comes
before any exchange at all — **it is the first act of the audience, performed by a man who has just
been walked in, and it is what he does instead of the obeisance the reader expects.** **Everyone else
who comes before this Pharaoh in Genesis bows, runs, is summoned, or answers questions.** **Jacob
blesses him and is asked how old he is.**

## Significance

**It is the plainest demonstration in Genesis of what the Abrahamic promise actually claims.** **The
promise is not that the family will be powerful; it is that the family will be the channel** — **and
the scene stages that with the power entirely on the other side of the room.** **Egypt feeds Israel
and Israel blesses Egypt**, and the pericope records both transactions in the same four verses.

**It is also the last friendly moment between the two nations in the Bible's telling.**
**[[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] gives land, offers employment, and takes a blessing from a
foreigner.** **The king of Exodus 1:8 *"didn't know Joseph"*, and the next thing a Hebrew says to a
throne in Egypt is *let my people go*.** See [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[The Nations]].
