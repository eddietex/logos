# Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 1:8** — *"a new king over Egypt, who didn't know Joseph"* — [[The Oppression in Egypt]]
- **Exodus 2:25; 3:7** — *"God knew"*; *"I know their sorrows"* — [[Moses Flees to Midian]],
  [[The Burning Bush]]
- **Exodus 5:2** — *"Who is Yahweh, that I should listen to his voice to let Israel go? I don't know
  Yahweh"* — [[Bricks Without Straw]]
- **Exodus 6:3, 7** — *"by my name Yahweh I was not known to them"*; *"you shall know that I am Yahweh
  your God"*. The refrain is said first to Israel, and in the next verses Israel does not listen
  (6:9) — [[I Am Yahweh]]
- **Exodus 7:5** — *"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* — the refrain turned toward Egypt
  for the first time, as the stated purpose of the plagues — [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]]
- **Exodus 7:17; 8:10, 22; 9:14, 29; 10:2; 11:7** — the refrain of the plagues *(not yet ingested)*
- **Exodus 14:4, 18** — *"the Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Exodus 15:11** — *"Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Exodus 18:11** — Jethro: *"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"* *(not yet ingested)*
- Compare **Job 21:14–15** — *"we don't want to know about your ways. What is the Almighty, that we
  should serve him?"*
- Compare **Proverbs 30:9** — *"lest I be full, deny you, and say, 'Who is Yahweh?'"*

## Analysis

**Pharaoh's first words to Moses are a question, and Exodus spends ten chapters answering it.**

> Pharaoh said, "Who is Yahweh, that I should listen to his voice to let Israel go? I don't know
> Yahweh, and moreover I will not let Israel go." (Exodus 5:2)

The question is not atheism. ***Mî YHWH*** asks *who*, not *whether*, and the next clause says what is
wrong: ***lōʾ yādaʿtî ʾet-YHWH***, *I don't know Yahweh*. **The refusal rests on the not-knowing.** And
*know*, ***yādaʿ***, is a verb Exodus has been setting up from its first chapter.

### Before the question

| | verse | who knows, or does not |
|---|---|---|
| the new king | 1:8 | *"who didn't know Joseph"* — *lōʾ-yādaʿ ʾet-yôsēp* |
| God | 2:25 | *"God saw the children of Israel, and God knew"* — *wayyēdaʿ ʾĕlōhîm* |
| God | 3:7 | *"I know their sorrows"* — *yādaʿtî* |
| Pharaoh | 5:2 | *"I don't know Yahweh"* — *lōʾ yādaʿtî ʾet-YHWH* |

**A king who did not know Joseph is succeeded by a king who does not know Yahweh**, and between them
the God who *knows*. The construction of 1:8 and 5:2 is the same — *lōʾ* + *yādaʿ* + *ʾet* + a name —
and the wiki records the parallel as one of construction; Exodus does not point it out.

### After the question

**From the next pericope on, *know that I am Yahweh* becomes the stated purpose of what God does to
Egypt**, addressed in turn to Israel, to the Egyptians, and to Pharaoh:

- To Israel: *"you shall know that I am Yahweh your God, who brings you out"* (6:7); *"that you may
  tell in the hearing of your son … that you may know that I am Yahweh"* (10:2).
- To the Egyptians: *"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh, when I stretch out my hand on Egypt"*
  (7:5); and at the sea, twice (14:4, 18).
- To Pharaoh: *"In this you shall know that I am Yahweh"* (7:17), before the Nile is struck; *"that you
  may know that there is no one like Yahweh our God"* (8:10); *"that you may know that I am Yahweh on the
  earth"* (8:22); *"that you may know that there is no one like me in all the earth"* (9:14); *"that you
  may know that the earth is Yahweh's"* (9:29); *"that you may know that Yahweh makes a distinction"*
  (11:7).

**The plagues are therefore, on Exodus's own account, revelation as well as judgment** — what is
being made known is the name Pharaoh said he did not know. (The verses are quoted from the WEB in
`raw/bible/Exodus.md`; the Hebrew *yādaʿ* in each was checked in the Westminster Leningrad Codex, whose
chapter 8 runs four verses ahead of the English, so 8:10 and 8:22 are WLC 8:6 and 8:18.)

**And the first person in Exodus to say that he now knows who Yahweh is, is not Pharaoh, an Egyptian,
or an Israelite.**
It is the priest of Midian: *"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"* (18:11, *ʿattâ
yādaʿtî*). **Pharaoh's *lōʾ yādaʿtî* is answered, in the end, by a foreigner's *ʿattâ yādaʿtî*.** See
[[Jethro]].

### The heavy and the glorified

**The thread runs through a second root as well.** In the same chapter as the question, Pharaoh
orders *"Let heavier work be laid on the men"* — *tikbad hāʿăbōdâ*, *let the service be heavy*
(5:9). The root ***kbd*** then describes his heart: *"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn"*, *kābēd* (7:14).
And at the sea it is God's: *"I will get honor over Pharaoh"* — ***wəʾikkābdâ***, *I will be made
heavy*, *I will be glorified* — *"and the Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* (14:4). **The king who
made the service heavy, and whose heart was heavy, is the one over whom God is made heavy — and the
same verse closes with *know*.** (All three checked in the WLC: *kābēd* the verb at 5:9 and 14:4, the
adjective at 7:14.) See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

### The same question elsewhere

**The Wisdom literature knows the question as the voice of the comfortable.** Job's wicked say to God,
*"Depart from us, for we don't want to know about your ways. What is the Almighty, that we should serve
him?"* (Job 21:14–15). The Hebrew of 21:15 is *mah-šadday kî-naʿabdennû* — *what is Shaddai, that we
should serve him* — and it couples, as Exodus 5 does, **not knowing God with refusing to serve him.**
(Checked in the WLC.) And the sage of Proverbs prays to be kept from wealth *"lest I be full, deny you,
and say, 'Who is Yahweh?'"* (Proverbs 30:9) — ***mî YHWH***, Pharaoh's two words exactly (checked in
the WLC). **Neither text names Pharaoh**, and the wiki does not claim either alludes to Exodus 5:2; the
question is recorded as one Scripture puts in more than one mouth, and always in the mouth of someone
who has enough not to need God.

## Significance

- **It gives the plagues their purpose in Exodus's own words.** They are not only punishment for the
  refusal or leverage for the release; they are the answer to *"Who is Yahweh?"*, given to the man who
  asked it, to his people, and to Israel.
- **It ties the name to the narrative.** At the bush Moses asked the name for Israel's sake (3:13); in
  [[Bricks Without Straw]] Pharaoh asks it in contempt; and in the next pericope God answers Moses
  first, *"I am Yahweh"* (6:2), and says that by this name he *"was not known"* to the patriarchs (6:3,
  *lōʾ nôdaʿtî*). **What Exodus adds to Genesis is not the name, which Genesis already uses, but its
  being *known* — through what the bearer of it does.** See [[The Names of God]] and
  [[Calling on the Name of Yahweh]].
- **It ends with an outsider's confession**, Jethro's, which makes the knowledge of Yahweh something the
  nations can come to and not only something done to them. See [[The Nations]].
