# Rejoice Before Yahweh - The Feast Meals of Deuteronomy

## Passages involved

- [[Stones and an Altar on Mount Ebal]] — Deuteronomy 27:1–10.

- [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]] — Deuteronomy 12:7, 12, 18: *"you shall rejoice in all that you
  put your hand to, you and your households"* (12:7); *"You shall rejoice before Yahweh your God: you,
  and your sons, your daughters, your male servants, your female servants, and the Levite who is
  within your gates"* (12:12).
- [[Tithes for Feasting and Provision]] — Deuteronomy 14:26: the tithe turned to money and spent at
  the place *"for whatever your soul desires"*, and *"you shall eat there before Yahweh your God, and
  you shall rejoice, you and your household"*.
- [[Passover, Weeks, and Booths]] — Deuteronomy 16:11, 14–15: the feast of weeks and the feast of
  tents, with the Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow added to the household; *"and
  you shall be altogether joyful"* (16:15).
- [[First Fruits and the Tithe Declaration]] — Deuteronomy 26:11: the first fruits brought to the place: *"You shall rejoice in all the good which
  Yahweh your God has given to you, and to your house, you, and the Levite, and the foreigner who is
  among you"*.
- Deuteronomy 27:7 — on Mount Ebal: *"You shall sacrifice peace offerings, and shall eat there. You
  shall rejoice before Yahweh your God"*; see [[Stones and an Altar on Mount Ebal]].

## Analysis

The verb is *śāmaḥ*, *rejoice*. It occurs ten times in Deuteronomy (WLC lemma search, morphhb). Eight
of them are commands to eat and rejoice before Yahweh: 12:7, 12, 18; 14:26; 16:11, 14; 26:11; 27:7.
The other two are 24:5, the newly married man who stays at home to *"cheer his wife"*, and the
blessing of Zebulun in 33:18. Deuteronomy 16:15 adds the adjective, *ʾak śāmēaḥ*, *"altogether
joyful"*, which occurs in the Torah only there (WLC lemma search across Genesis–Deuteronomy,
morphhb).

**The joy is commanded, and it is a meal.** Each occurrence comes with eating — the offerings and
tithes at the place (chapter 12), the tithe bought as food and drink (14:26), the feasts (16), the
first fruits (26), the peace offerings at Ebal (27). None is a command to feel something apart from
the table. Each is tied to *before Yahweh your God* or to the place he chooses, except 26:11, which
follows the basket set *"before Yahweh your God's altar"* (26:4) in the same unit. (This page
earlier quoted 26:4 as *"before the altar of Yahweh your God"*; corrected to the WEB 2026-11-10.)

**The guest list grows.**

| Text | Who rejoices |
|---|---|
| 12:7 | *"you and your households"* |
| 12:12, 18 | sons, daughters, male and female servants, and the Levite |
| 14:26 | *"you and your household"* (the Levite is added in 14:27) |
| 16:11, 14 | son, daughter, male and female servant, the Levite, **the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow** |
| 26:11 | *"you, and the Levite, and the foreigner"* |
| 27:7 | no list |

In chapter 14 the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow eat the third-year tithe *"within your
gates"* (14:29), in the towns. Chapter 16 is the first place they are named among those who eat
*before Yahweh* at the place. See
[[Within Your Gates - The Levite, the Foreigner, the Fatherless, and the Widow]].

**The measure is blessing.** The tithe is eaten *"in which Yahweh your God has blessed you"* (12:7).
The feast of weeks is kept *"according as Yahweh your God blesses you"* (16:10). The feast of tents
is joyful *"because Yahweh your God will bless you in all your increase"* (16:15). Rejoicing answers
a harvest already given, and the size of the gift follows the size of the harvest (16:17).

**Elsewhere in the Torah, the feasts are rarely called joyful.** Outside Deuteronomy the verb
*śāmaḥ* occurs in the Torah only twice: Aaron *"will be glad in his heart"* to see Moses (Exodus 4:14),
and Leviticus 23:40, where Israel *"shall rejoice before Yahweh your God seven days"* at the feast of
booths (WLC lemma search across Genesis–Deuteronomy, morphhb). The noun *śimḥâ* appears once in the
cult outside Deuteronomy: *"in the day of your gladness, and in your set feasts"* (Numbers 10:10).
Exodus 23 and 34 and Numbers 28–29 give dates, appearances, and offerings, without a command to
rejoice. The claim is limited to the Torah. How the theme continues in the historical books
and the Psalms is left until those are ingested.

### The meal at Ebal — Deuteronomy 27:1–10

Deuteronomy 27:7 is now ingested with its setting in 27:1–10. Peace offerings, eating, and
rejoicing are commanded beside the inscribed law and the altar of uncut stones. Unlike the
harvest-feast provisions, the unit supplies no calendar date for annual recurrence and no
detailed guest list. Its occasion is entry after the Jordan crossing. The connection is the
command to rejoice before Yahweh, not a claim that this is another annual harvest festival.

## Significance

- It makes joy part of the law at the place. In Deuteronomy, worshipping at the one place is a feast
  shared with the household and the poor, not only a set of offerings.
- It explains why the feast lists keep adding people. The rule is that no one within Israel's gates
  is left out of the meal.
- It ties [[Joy and Enjoyment]] to [[Festivals and First Fruits]], [[Blessing]], and
  [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]].
