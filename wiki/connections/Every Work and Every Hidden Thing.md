# Every Work and Every Hidden Thing

## Passages involved

- [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]] — Proverbs 24:11–12.
- [[From Dust to Dust]] — Ecclesiastes 3:16–17.
- [[The Wicked Buried and the Work Beyond Finding]] — Ecclesiastes 8:10–14.
- [[Remember Your Creator]] — Ecclesiastes 11:9.
- [[The End of the Matter]] — Ecclesiastes 12:13–14.

## Analysis

Proverbs 24:12 refuses the excuse of ignorance after the call to rescue people being
led to death. The one who weighs hearts knows and renders to each person according
to work. Ecclesiastes 12:14 extends its concluding charge to every work and every
hidden thing, explicitly including good and evil. Public appearance does not exhaust
what is accountable. This is a thematic comparison, not a claim of direct quotation.

Within Ecclesiastes, the conclusion gathers earlier affirmations. God judges righteous
and wicked despite corruption in the place of justice (3:16–17); the young person's
choices fall under judgment within the invitation to rejoice (11:9). The closing
statement broadens the explicit scope to hidden things as well (12:14). It does not
make enjoyment inherently sinful or restrict judgment to evil actions.

**The observed contradiction is not erased.** Ecclesiastes 8:11–14 describes delayed
sentence, long-lived wrongdoing, and outcomes reversed between righteous and wicked.
12:14 affirms judgment without narrating those cases being corrected or dating a
future settlement. It therefore must not be used to infer a person's guilt from
present suffering, or to claim that the inquiry has explained each injustice.

The final judgment may be read as extending beyond what the observer sees, but the
text does not explicitly describe a resurrection, court scene, or afterlife timetable.
Its affirmation remains beside 9:5–10's statements about the dead, recorded in
[[One Event Comes to All]]. Neither should be silently rewritten to harmonize the other.

## Significance

[[Judgment]] encompasses what is hidden and what is good, not only public wrongdoing.
[[Retribution]] as a diagnosis from present circumstances remains challenged by
Ecclesiastes' own observations. [[The Fear of God]] and [[Law and Obedience]] are
called for without requiring the reader to possess a complete account of outcomes.
