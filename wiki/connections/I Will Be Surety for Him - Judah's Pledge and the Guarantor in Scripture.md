# I Will Be Surety for Him — Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 43:8–10** — [[The Second Journey]]; [[Judah]]: *"I'll be collateral for him. From my hand
  will you require him."*
- **Genesis 44:32–33** — [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]; the pledge called in
- **Genesis 42:37** — [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]]; [[Reuben]]'s rejected offer
- **Genesis 38:17–18, 20** — [[Judah and Tamar]]; **Judah's other *ʿārab***
- **Job 17:3; Psalm 119:122; Proverbs 6:1–5; 11:15; 17:18; 20:16; 22:26–27**
- **Isaiah 38:14; Hebrews 7:22**

## Analysis

### The verb

***ʿārab*** is a commercial term: **to stand as guarantor, to go bail, to pledge oneself for another
man's obligation.** The noun ***ʿărābôn*** is the deposit that makes the guarantee real — **and it is
the word [[Judah]] uses in the one other place Genesis puts this root in his mouth.**

> **17** … She said, "Will you give me a pledge, until you send it?" **18** He said, "What pledge
> will I give you?" She said, "Your signet and your cord, and your staff that is in your hand."
> (38:17–18)

***ʿĒrābôn*** — *pledge* — **three times in that transaction** (38:17, 18, 20). **The first time
Judah gives a pledge he hands over his seal, his cord and his staff to a woman at a crossroads, to
secure a goat he never delivers**; [[Tamar]] produces the tokens at 38:25 and he says *"She is more
righteous than I."* See [[Judah and Tamar]].

**The second time he gives a pledge, the collateral is himself.**

### Reuben's offer and Judah's

Jacob is asked twice, eleven verses apart, to let [[Benjamin]] go.

| | [[Reuben]], 42:37 | [[Judah]], 43:9 |
|---|---|---|
| **the pledge** | *"Kill my two sons, if I don't bring him to you"* | *"I'll be **collateral** for him"* |
| **who bears it** | **two of Jacob's grandsons**, unnamed here (46:9 gives Reuben four sons) | Judah |
| **the verb** | no *ʿārab*; a bare conditional | ***ʾānōkî ʾeʿerbennû*** — **the pronoun supplied** |
| **the term** | until Benjamin is returned | *"let me bear the blame **forever**"* |
| **Jacob's reply** | **none recorded** | *"If it must be so, then do this"* (43:11) |

**Reuben offers a transaction in lives he does not own.** His instinct is sound and his instrument
never is: at 37:21 he does say *"Let's not take his life"*, **but what he proposes instead is the pit**
(37:22), and the narrator has to add *"that he might deliver him out of their hand, to restore him to
his father"* because the speech itself does not show it. **Here he proposes to cure his father's
bereavement by promising him more of it, and Jacob does not answer him at all.**

**Judah offers a status.** *"Let me bear the blame forever"* — ***wəḥāṭāʾtî ləkā kol-hayyāmîm***,
*I shall have sinned against you all the days* — **is not a penalty he will pay but a thing he will
permanently be.** And *"from my hand will you require him"* — ***mîyādî təbaqšennû*** — **uses
*bāqaš*, to seek out, rather than the *dāraš* Reuben reached for at 42:22** when he invoked the
Noahic *"his blood is required"* (9:5). **The difference is exact: Reuben appeals to a cosmic
reckoning that will happen to somebody; Judah names the hand it comes out of.**

### The pledge is called in

**Genesis makes him keep it, and quotes him doing it.**

> **32** For your servant became collateral for the boy to my father, saying, 'If I don't bring him
> to you, then I will bear the blame to my father forever.' **33** Now therefore, please let your
> servant stay instead of the boy, my lord's slave; and let the boy go up with his brothers.
> (44:32–33)

**He repeats 43:9 almost verbatim and then draws the conclusion it entails** — *let your servant stay
instead of the boy* (see [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]). **Nobody in Egypt can enforce the
pledge**: it was made to his father in a tent, to win an argument, with no oath, no witness and no
jurisdiction that reaches across a border. **He performs it anyway, in front of a court that has just
declared him *blameless* and offered him the road home** (44:10, 17). **The man who said *"what profit is it if we kill our brother"* and sold him for
twenty pieces (37:26–28) offers to take a favoured younger son's sentence.** Whether or not Joseph
designed the cup as a test, **this is the answer to it**, and 45:1 follows immediately: the disguise
does not survive the speech. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]] and [[Testing]].

### What the wisdom literature makes of it

**Proverbs is against this, four times, without qualification.**

- *"My son, if you have become collateral for your neighbor … you are snared with the words of your
  mouth"* (6:1–2), and the advice is to go and get out of it immediately
- *"He who is collateral for a stranger will suffer for it"* (11:15)
- *"A man void of understanding strikes hands, and becomes collateral in the presence of his
  neighbor"* (17:18)
- *"Take a garment from him who puts up collateral for a stranger"* (20:16; cf. 27:13)

**The warnings are about a stranger.** Judah's is for a brother, and it is not offered for profit or
out of vanity — **it is the price of a household's survival**, and he names the three generations it
buys (43:8). **Proverbs is describing a man who does not know what he is signing; Judah states the
term as *forever* before he signs it.**

**And the Bible's other guarantors are people with nothing left.** [[Job (person)]], at the end of
his patience with his friends: *"Now give a pledge, be collateral for me with yourself. Who is there
who will strike hands with me?"* (Job 17:3) — **asking God to stand surety for him against God.**
Hezekiah, sick: *"Yahweh, I am oppressed. Be my security"* (Isaiah 38:14). The psalmist: *"Be
collateral for your servant for good"* (Psalm 119:122). **Three men who cannot cover their own
obligation, asking God to do what Judah did.**

### The Greek

**Hebrews 7:22 answers all of them with one word.** *"By so much, Jesus has become the guarantee of a
better covenant"* — ***enguos***, **the Septuagint's standard rendering of *ʿārab*, and the only
occurrence of the noun in the New Testament.** The argument of Hebrews 7 is that the guarantee holds
because the guarantor does not die.

**The tribal irony is not incidental**: Hebrews 7:14 has just observed that *"our Lord has sprung out
of Judah"*, **and the epistle's word for what he is, is the verb Judah used at 43:9.** Genesis does
not know this and does not need to; **what Genesis knows is that the man who sold a brother later
offered to be sold in one's place**, and that this is the turn on which the family is saved.

## Significance

**Genesis 43:9 is the point at which [[Judah]] takes over the cycle from [[Reuben]]**, and the book
never gives the firstborn the role back — 49:3–4 strips it formally, and 49:8–10 hands the sceptre to
Judah in so many words. **The mechanism of the transfer is one verb.**

**It also establishes the pattern Scripture will use for substitution that is not sacrifice.** The
ram at Moriah dies instead of [[Isaac]] (22:13); **the guarantor does not die instead of anybody —
he binds himself to the outcome in advance and then presents himself when it goes wrong.**
[[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]] is the other half of the idea, and the two
run in parallel through the rest of the canon.

**And it is the Bible's first voluntary suretyship.** Everything before it is compelled — [[Reuben]]
offering what is not his, Judah himself handing over a seal to a woman he took for a prostitute.
**The difference between a pledge you leave behind and a pledge you are is the difference between
Genesis 38 and Genesis 44**, and Judah is the only man in Scripture who does both.
