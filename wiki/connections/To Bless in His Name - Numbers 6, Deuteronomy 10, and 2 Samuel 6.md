# To Bless in His Name - Numbers 6, Deuteronomy 10, and 2 Samuel 6

## Passages involved

- [[Yahweh Bless You and Keep You]] — Numbers 6:23, 27.
- [[The Eighth Day and the Appearing Glory]] — Leviticus 9:22–23.
- Deuteronomy 10:8 — Levi set apart *"to minister to him, and to bless in his name, to this day."* *(WEB,
  bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- Deuteronomy 21:5 — the priests chosen *"to minister to him, and to bless in Yahweh’s name."* *(same)*
- 1 Chronicles 23:13 — Aaron and his sons *"to bless in his name, forever."* *(same)*
- 2 Samuel 6:18 — David *"blessed the people in the name of Yahweh of Armies."* *(same)*
- 1 Kings 8:55 — Solomon *"stood, and blessed all the assembly of Israel with a loud voice."* *(same)*
- 2 Chronicles 30:27 — *"the priests the Levites arose and blessed the people. Their voice was heard."*
  *(same)*
- Psalm 118:26; 129:8 — *"We have blessed you out of Yahweh’s house"*; *"We bless you in Yahweh’s
  name."* *(same)*
- Malachi 2:1–2 — *"I will curse your blessings."* *(same)*
- Luke 24:50–51 — Jesus *"lifted up his hands, and blessed them."* *(same)*

## Analysis

Numbers 6 gives a task to Aaron and his sons. The later texts treat blessing as part of the job
description. Deuteronomy names it with ministering (10:8; 21:5), and 1 Chronicles 23:13 lists it among
Aaron's duties, *forever*. Deuteronomy 10:8 assigns it to *the tribe of Levi*, and 21:5 to *the priests
the sons of Levi*, while Numbers addresses only Aaron and his sons. The wider wording may follow
Deuteronomy's habit of speaking of the Levitical priests, but this pass did not settle that. The
difference is left visible.

The office does not exhaust the act. David blesses the people *in the name of Yahweh* at the ark (2 Samuel
6:18), and Solomon blesses the assembly at the temple (1 Kings 8:55), neither being a priest in the
narrative's terms. So a king could do this, and neither text objects. Numbers 6 does not say that only
priests may bless, though it gives them these words. In those two scenes the words are not these words:
1 Kings 8:56 goes on with its own (*Blessed be Yahweh, who has given rest to his people Israel*), and
2 Samuel gives none.

Leviticus 9:22 supplies a gesture with no words: Aaron *lifted up his hands toward the people, and blessed
them*. Numbers 6 supplies words with no gesture. Joining them, as later practice may have done, goes
beyond either text. Later Jewish rules for the blessing were not checked for this page. Luke 24:50 has
Jesus lift up his hands and bless at the end of the Gospel. Its likeness to Leviticus 9:22 is a likeness
of gesture, and Luke does not name it, and no blessing words are given there.

2 Chronicles 30:27 connects the priests' blessing with prayer heard in heaven, which suits the logic of
Numbers 6:27, where Yahweh does the blessing. Malachi 2:2 shows that the blessing was not automatic. Priests
who do not honor Yahweh's name have their blessings cursed.

2 Corinthians 13:14 is sometimes set beside Numbers 6 as a threefold benediction. Its three members are
*the Lord Jesus Christ*, *God*, and *the Holy Spirit*, and Numbers repeats one name three times. The verse
does not cite Numbers, so any link is only one of form and is not asserted here.

## Significance

Blessing starts in Numbers 6 as an act with assigned words and a clear limit: the priest speaks, and
Yahweh blesses. Later texts widen who speaks but keep that limit. Hebrew from WLC (morphhb); WEB of 1
Kings 8:56 from bible-api.com; no commentary consulted.
