# The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah

## Passages involved

- [[Balaam's Last Oracles]] — **Numbers 24:2** — *Balaam lifted up his eyes, and he saw Israel
  dwelling according to their tribes; and the Spirit of God came on him.* (stored WEB)
- [[Othniel the First Judge]] — **Judges 3:10** — *Yahweh's Spirit came on him, and he judged Israel;
  and he went out to war* (stored WEB)
- [[Jephthah's Vow]] — **Judges 11:29** — *Then Yahweh's Spirit came on Jephthah, and he passed over Gilead and Manasseh*
  (stored WEB)
- [[The Fleece]] — **Judges 6:34** — *But Yahweh's Spirit came on Gideon, and he blew a trumpet* (stored WEB). A
  different verb, kept here for contrast.
- [[Samson's Riddle]] — **Judges 14:6, 19** — *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him* (stored WEB); the verb *ṣālaḥ*, kept here for contrast.

## Analysis

The three verses share one clause (all WLC):

| Verse | Hebrew | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers 24:2 | *wattəhî ʿālāyw rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* | an oracle, *Balaam the son of Beor says* (24:3) |
| Judges 3:10 | *wattəhî ʿālāyw rûaḥ YHWH* | *he judged Israel; and he went out to war* |
| Judges 11:29 | *wattəhî ʿal-yiptāḥ rûaḥ YHWH* | a march through Gilead and Manasseh to Ammon |

The verb is *hāyâ*, *to be, come to be*, feminine to agree with *rûaḥ*, followed by *ʿal*, *on*. The
Spirit is *of God* for Balaam and *of Yahweh* for both judges.

Judges uses other verbs for the other Spirit-endowed judges. Gideon: *Yahweh's Spirit came on
Gideon* in the WEB (6:34), but the Hebrew is *wərûaḥ YHWH lābəšâ ʾet-gidʿôn*, *clothed Gideon* (WLC).
Samson: *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him* (14:6, stored WEB), *wattiṣlaḥ ʿālāyw rûaḥ YHWH*, from
*ṣālaḥ*, *to rush, prosper* (WLC). The WEB's *came on* covers three different Hebrew verbs. Only
Othniel and Jephthah share Balaam's clause exactly.

### Gideon's verse now ingested

[[The Fleece]] confirms the contrast drawn above. *Wərûaḥ YHWH lābəšâ ʾet-gidʿôn* (Judges 6:34, WLC): *rûaḥ* is
the subject, *lābaš*, *to put on, wear*, the verb, and Gideon the object. In the WLC of Judges *lābaš*
occurs only in this verse. Like Othniel's, the endowment leads straight to war, the horn and the muster
(6:34–35). Unlike either Othniel or Jephthah, the next thing Gideon does is ask for a sign, twice
(6:36–40). The clause does not prevent doubt, any more than Jephthah's prevents his vow. Source: stored
WEB Judges 6:33–40; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

## Significance

- **The same gift, different results.** On Balaam the Spirit brought blessing in words he had been
  hired to make curses. On Othniel it brought judging and war. On Jephthah it is followed directly by
  his vow (11:30–31, stored WEB), which ends with his daughter. The clause does not by itself say what
  the person will do with it, or whether he will do it well.
- **None of the three is said to have sought it.** Balaam had stopped going *to meet with
  enchantments* (Numbers 24:1, stored WEB), but the verse does not say he asked for the Spirit. The
  two judges are given no prayer for it.
- **It is said to come, not to stay.** None of the verses says how long it remained. The wiki's earlier
  case of a lasting Spirit, Joshua as *a man in whom is the Spirit* (Numbers 27:18, stored WEB), uses a
  different construction. See [[The Spirit of God]].
- **Jephthah's verse, now ingested.** The clause matches Othniel's exactly, with the name in place of
  the pronoun (WLC). What follows is a march with three uses of *ʿābar*, *passed over*, and then a vow
  (11:29–31). Othniel's endowment was followed by judging and war. Jephthah's is followed by a bargain
  he did not need to make, and the Spirit does not stop it. See [[Jephthah's Vow]].

### 1 Samuel 19:20, 23 — the clause on Saul's messengers and on Saul

The clause returns twice in one pericope: *wattǝhî ʿal-malʾăḵê šāʾûl rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* (19:20) and *wattǝhî ʿālāyw
gam-hûʾ rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* (19:23) (WLC). With *ʾĕlōhîm*, as for Balaam and not as for the two judges, it occurs in the
Hebrew files checked only at Numbers 24:2 and these two verses. What follows is neither judging nor war but
prophesying, by men sent *to seize David* and then by the king who sent them. As with Balaam, the Spirit turns a
hostile errand into prophecy; the further parallels are on
[[Falling Down Under the Spirit - Balaam and Saul at Naioth]]. The clause again shows nothing about the man's
standing: Saul has been rejected (15:23) and an evil spirit was on him fifteen verses earlier (19:9). See
[[Saul Among the Prophets at Naioth]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 15:23; 19:9, 18–24; Numbers 24:2. Hebrew WLC
(OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.
