## Analysis

### Allocation while possession remains incomplete

Joshua 13:1–7 identifies extensive remaining territory without giving a reason for the delay or accusing Joshua of failure. Yahweh promises removal and commands allocation now (6–7). This belongs beside the gradual removal of Exodus 23:29–30 and Deuteronomy 7:22, but it does not repeat their explanation about wild animals. The inventory alone should not be turned into a judgment of disobedience.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:1–7; comparison references as stated.

### Possession without merit — Deuteronomy 9:1–6

Explicit tension: the stored WEB of Deuteronomy 9:3 commands Israel to make the nations perish quickly, while 7:22 says their removal is little by little and forbids consuming them at once. Swift victories within a gradual occupation could reconcile the statements, but neither passage articulates that distinction. Preserve both temporal descriptions rather than silently replacing one.

See [[Not Because of Your Righteousness]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 9:1–6.


### Deuteronomy 7:12–26 now ingested

Deuteronomy 7:16–26 is now ingested, so the quotations above can be checked against the stored WEB.
All three motifs are present. The hornet is sent *until those who are left, and hide themselves,
perish* (20). The nations are cast out *little by little*, *lest the animals of the field increase
on you* (22), the same reason Exodus 23:29 gives. The snare is serving their gods (16), and is then
extended to coveting the silver and gold on their images (25), a development Exodus 23 does not
make. Deuteronomy also sets the gradual pace inside a promise of completeness. The confusion and the
delivery of kings continue *until they are destroyed* (23–24), so the slow conquest is not presented
as a partial one. That makes the contrast with Judges 2:1–3, cited above, sharper, and the wiki
still states both explanations without merging them.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 7:12–26; Exodus 23:27–33.

**Three motifs from Exodus 23:27–33 recur together, and the later texts reread them.**

**1. The hornet.** Exodus 23:28 promises it; Deuteronomy 7:20 repeats the promise; Joshua 24:12 looks
back and says it happened, *"not with your sword, nor with your bow"*. What the hornet is remains
unexplained in all three. Readings include a literal insect, a figure of panic, and a symbol for a
foreign military power. The wiki reports these and adopts none.

**2. "Little by little."** Exodus 23:29–30 gives the reason for a slow conquest: so the land does not
become *"desolate"* and the wild animals multiply. Deuteronomy 7:22 repeats both the phrase and the
reason. Neither verse presents the delay as a judgment.

**3. The snare.** Exodus 23:33 warns that serving the peoples' gods *"will surely be a snare"*
(*môqēš*, MAM). Exodus 34:12 applies the snare to covenant with the land's people, and Joshua 23:13
calls the nations themselves *"a snare and a trap"*. Judges 2:3 then records it as fulfilled: *"their
gods will be a snare to you."*

**Two explanations for the peoples who remain.** This is a tension inside Scripture, and the wiki
states it without merging it:

| Text | Why peoples remain in the land |
|---|---|
| Exodus 23:29–30; Deuteronomy 7:22 | A gradual conquest planned for the land's sake: *"lest the land become desolate"* |
| Judges 2:1–3 | A consequence of disobedience: *"you have not listened to my voice … Therefore I also said, 'I will not drive them out'"* |
| Numbers 33:55; Joshua 23:13 | A warning: if Israel does not drive them out, or turns to them, the peoples will become thorns, traps, and a snare |

The usual reading is that these answer different questions: the pace God planned, and the result of
what Israel then did. The texts do not say so outright. They put the two side by side, as Joshua
21:43–45 and Judges 1 do for the extent of the conquest (see
[[From the River of Egypt to the Euphrates]]).

**The angel frames both ends.** Exodus 23:20 sends an angel to bring Israel in, warning that he must
be heeded (*"listen to his voice"*, 23:21–22). In Judges 2:1–2, *"Yahweh's angel"* comes up to Bochim
and charges Israel: *"you have not listened to my voice."* The condition of Exodus 23:22 returns, as
an accusation, in the mouth of the same kind of messenger. See [[The Angel of Yahweh]].

### Bochim — Judges 2:1–5

Now ingested. The verb matters here. Judges 1 says the tribes *did not drive out*, using *yāraš*. At
Bochim Yahweh says *lōʾ ʾăgārēš*, *I will not drive out*, using *gāraš*, the verb of this
connection's Exodus texts: the hornet *will drive out* (Exodus 23:28) and *I will not drive them out
from before you in one year … little by little I will drive them out* (23:29–30; all WLC). Judges 2:3
negates the same first-person piel, without *in one year*. So the slow removal of Exodus 23 and the
withdrawn removal of Judges 2 are stated in the same words, and the difference between them is the
condition: *you have not listened to my voice* (2:2). Exodus 23:22 had set that condition. Judges
2:22 and 3:1–4 will give a third reason, testing (stored WEB). The wiki still states each reason
separately.

Source: stored WEB Exodus 23:22, 28–30; Judges 2:1–5, 22; 3:1–4. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-13.

### The renewal in its narrative setting — Exodus 34

[[The Covenant Renewed]] extends the snare warning through treaties, sacrificial meals, and marriage into
other worship (34:12–16). The six peoples are again named (34:11), and the removal promise is
joined to security for Israel's land during annual appearances (34:24). Neither the hornet nor a
timetable for gradual removal is stated in this unit. Those details remain features of the
earlier speech, not quotations to supply here.

Exodus 34:1–28 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-24.

### The warning at the border — Numbers 33:50–56

[[Drive Out the Inhabitants]] joins the destruction of cult objects to removal of the inhabitants (52),
then adds possession, dwelling, and proportional allotment by lot (53–54). The inhabitants
left behind will harass Israel (55); Yahweh threatens Israel with their intended fate (56).
The danger here is therefore military as well as religious, and culminates in divine judgment.
The earlier Exodus account’s planned gradual removal and this warning against failure remain
in tension; Numbers gives no deadline that would settle their practical relationship.

Numbers 33:50–56 checked in stored WEB. See also [[The Same Judgment on Israel - Leviticus 18 and Numbers 33]].

### Joshua's farewell — Joshua 23

Now ingested. Joshua holds both explanations together in one speech. Yahweh *will thrust them out
from before you* (5), and Joshua has already allotted the land of *these nations that remain* (4).
If Israel *hold[s] fast to the remnant of these nations* and marries into them, however, Yahweh
*will no longer drive these nations from out of your sight* (12–13). Expulsion is promised, and it
can be stopped. Compared with Numbers 33:55 (*pricks in your eyes … thorns in your sides*),
Joshua's image rearranges the body parts (*a scourge in your sides, and thorns in your eyes*) and
changes the condition. Numbers speaks of failing to drive the inhabitants out. Joshua speaks of
clinging to them. The hornet is not mentioned here; it comes in 24:12.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 23:4–5, 12–13 and Numbers 33:55. No source-language claim is added.

### To test Israel — Judges 2:20–23

Now ingested. [[Another Generation Arose]] repeats Bochim's sentence, *I also will no longer drive out
any of the nations that Joshua left when he died* (2:21), and adds a purpose that neither Exodus nor
Bochim gave: *that by them I may test Israel, to see if they will keep Yahweh's way … or not* (2:22).
Verse 23 then says Yahweh *left those nations, without driving them out hastily*. *Hastily* is
*mahēr*, the word of Deuteronomy 7:22, *you may not consume them at once* (*mahēr*; WLC). So 2:23
gathers more than one reason into one verse: not quickly, as Exodus and Deuteronomy said; and not
into Joshua's hand. The book now holds three explanations side by side: for the land's sake (Exodus
23:29–30; Deuteronomy 7:22), as punishment (Judges 2:3, 21), and as a test (Judges 2:22; 3:1–4). The
wiki does not merge them.

Source: stored WEB Judges 2:20–23; Deuteronomy 7:22. Hebrew of Judges 2:21–23 and Deuteronomy 7:22
from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-13.

### To teach them war — Judges 3:1–6

Now ingested. [[The Nations Left to Test Israel]] restates the test of 2:22 (3:1, 4) and adds a purpose
the wiki has not met before: *that the generations of the children of Israel might know, to teach them
war, at least those who knew nothing of it before* (3:2). *Ləlammədām milḥāmâ* (WLC). The earlier
reasons concern the land (kept from desolation), Israel's sin (a punishment), or Israel's obedience (a
test). This one concerns Israel's skill in war. Verse 2 opens with *raq*, *only*, as if this were the
whole reason, while verse 4 gives the test again. The text sets them side by side and does not rank
them; the wiki does not merge them either. The verb *left* in 3:1 is *hinnîaḥ*, the verb of 2:23
(*wayyannaḥ*, Yahweh left), not the *ʿāzab* of 2:21 (Joshua left), so the leaving is again Yahweh's.

Source: stored WEB Judges 2:21–23; 3:1–4. Hebrew of Judges 2:21–23 and 3:1–4 from the Westminster
Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-13.

## Significance

- **The conquest is described as God's work in its promise and in its retrospect.** Exodus 23 and
  Joshua 24:12 agree on that, and the hornet is the image that carries it.
- **The danger Exodus names is religious, not military.** The snare is the peoples' gods, and the
  covenant forbidden is the one that would draw Israel to them (23:32–33; 34:12–16).
- **The later books do not rewrite the promise; they test Israel by it.** Judges reads its own
  history against Exodus 23's condition and finds the condition broken.
