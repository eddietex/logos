# Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 23:27–33** — terror, confusion, the hornet, *"little by little"*, and the peoples' gods
  as *"a snare"* — [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]
- **Exodus 34:11–13** — *"I drive out before you"*; *"lest it be for a snare among you"*; break their
  altars and pillars (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Numbers 33:55** — those left *"will be as pricks in your eyes and as thorns in your sides"*
  (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Deuteronomy 7:1–5, 20–24** — seven nations; no covenant, no marriages; the hornet; *"little by
  little … lest the animals of the field increase on you"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Joshua 23:13** — *"they shall be a snare and a trap to you"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Joshua 24:12** — *"I sent the hornet before you … not with your sword, nor with your bow"* (WEB;
  *not yet ingested*)
- **Judges 2:1–3** — Yahweh's angel at Bochim: *"I will not drive them out from before you; but they
  shall be in your sides, and their gods will be a snare to you"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**Three motifs from Exodus 23:27–33 recur together, and the later texts reread them.**

**1. The hornet.** Exodus 23:28 promises it; Deuteronomy 7:20 repeats the promise; Joshua 24:12 looks
back and says it happened, *"not with your sword, nor with your bow"*. What the hornet is remains
unexplained in all three. Readings include a literal insect, a figure of panic, and a symbol for a
foreign military power. The wiki reports these and adopts none.

**2. "Little by little."** Exodus 23:29–30 gives the reason for a slow conquest: so the land does not
become *"desolate"* and the wild animals multiply. Deuteronomy 7:22 repeats both the phrase and the
reason. Neither verse presents the delay as a judgment.

**3. The snare.** Exodus 23:33 warns that serving the peoples' gods *"will surely be a snare"*
(*môqēš*, MAM). Exodus 34:12 applies the snare to covenant with the land's people, and Joshua 23:13
calls the nations themselves *"a snare and a trap"*. Judges 2:3 then records it as fulfilled: *"their
gods will be a snare to you."*

**Two explanations for the peoples who remain.** This is a tension inside Scripture, and the wiki
states it without merging it:

| Text | Why peoples remain in the land |
|---|---|
| Exodus 23:29–30; Deuteronomy 7:22 | A gradual conquest planned for the land's sake: *"lest the land become desolate"* |
| Judges 2:1–3 | A consequence of disobedience: *"you have not listened to my voice … Therefore I also said, 'I will not drive them out'"* |
| Numbers 33:55; Joshua 23:13 | A warning: if Israel does not drive them out, or turns to them, the peoples will become thorns, traps, and a snare |

The usual reading is that these answer different questions: the pace God planned, and the result of
what Israel then did. The texts do not say so outright. They put the two side by side, as Joshua
21:43–45 and Judges 1 do for the extent of the conquest (see
[[From the River of Egypt to the Euphrates]]).

**The angel frames both ends.** Exodus 23:20 sends an angel to bring Israel in, warning that he must
be heeded (*"listen to his voice"*, 23:21–22). In Judges 2:1–2, *"Yahweh's angel"* comes up to Bochim
and charges Israel: *"you have not listened to my voice."* The condition of Exodus 23:22 returns, as
an accusation, in the mouth of the same kind of messenger. See [[The Angel of Yahweh]].

## Significance

- **The conquest is described as God's work in its promise and in its retrospect.** Exodus 23 and
  Joshua 24:12 agree on that, and the hornet is the image that carries it.
- **The danger Exodus names is religious, not military.** The snare is the peoples' gods, and the
  covenant forbidden is the one that would draw Israel to them (23:32–33; 34:12–16).
- **The later books do not rewrite the promise; they test Israel by it.** Judges reads its own
  history against Exodus 23's condition and finds the condition broken.
