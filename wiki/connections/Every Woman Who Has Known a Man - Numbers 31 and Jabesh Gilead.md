# Every Woman Who Has Known a Man - Numbers 31 and Jabesh Gilead

## Passages involved

- [[The War Against Midian]] — Numbers 31:17–18, 35: *Now therefore kill every male among the little
  ones, and kill every woman who has known man by lying with him. But all the girls, who have not known
  man by lying with him, keep alive for yourselves* (17–18); the count of *the women who had not known man
  by lying with him* (35). Stored WEB.
- [[Wives for Benjamin]] — Judges 21:10–12: *strike the inhabitants of Jabesh Gilead with the edge of the
  sword, with the women and the little ones. This is the thing that you shall do: you shall utterly
  destroy every male, and every woman who has lain with a man* (10–11); *four hundred young virgins, who
  had not known man by lying with him* (12). Stored WEB.

## Analysis

**The phrase.** Numbers 31:17 has *wəḵol-ʾiššâ yōḏaʿaṯ ʾîš ləmiškaḇ zāḵār*; 31:18 and 35, *ʾăšer
lōʾ-yāḏəʿû miškaḇ zāḵār*. Judges 21:11 has *wəḵol-ʾiššâ yōḏaʿaṯ miškaḇ-zāḵār*; 21:12, *ʾăšer lōʾ-yāḏəʿâ ʾîš
ləmiškaḇ zāḵār* (all WLC). In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel searched, *miškaḇ* with *zāḵār*
and *yāḏaʿ* occurs in these five verses only (lemma 4904; the noun
*miškaḇ* also occurs in Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13, but in a different phrase, *miškəḇê ʾiššâ*). Judges 21:12 matches Numbers 31:17 in having *ʾîš* and
*lə-*; 21:11 matches 31:18 in lacking them. The WEB renders the two passages slightly differently, *known
man by lying with him* and *lain with a man*, but the Hebrew is the same formula.

**The same sorting, a different war.** In both chapters the women of a defeated people are divided into
those who have known a man, who are killed, and girls who have not, who are kept alive. In Numbers the
command is Moses's, after a war Yahweh ordered against Midian for Peor (31:1–3, 15–16). In Judges it is the
congregation's, under its own oath (21:5), against an Israelite town whose offence was absence (21:8–9),
and its purpose is stated: wives for Benjamin. Judges 21:11 adds the ban verb, *taḥărîmû*, *you shall
utterly destroy*, which Numbers 31 does not use.

**What the parallel shows.** If the Judges narrator is borrowing the language of Numbers 31 — the wiki
cannot show that he is, only that the formula is shared and rare — then a procedure written for a war
against a foreign people is here applied by Israel to Israel. That is the pattern of Judges 19–21 already
traced on other pages: Sodom's night at Gibeah, Ai's ambush at Gibeah, Deuteronomy 13's condemned city at
Gibeah. See [[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]],
[[Drawn Away from the City - Ai and Gibeah]], and
[[The Whole City Went Up - Deuteronomy 13 and Gibeah]].

## Significance

Numbers 31 is itself one of the hardest chapters in the Torah, and the wiki does not resolve it here. The
connection is narrower: a sorting of captive women that Israel's law-book records once, against Midian,
Israel's history records once more, against Jabesh Gilead, and in the second case no word from Yahweh
commands it. See [[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]],
[[War]], [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], and [[Jabesh Gilead]].
