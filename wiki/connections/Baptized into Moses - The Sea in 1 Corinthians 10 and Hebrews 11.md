# Baptized into Moses - The Sea in 1 Corinthians 10 and Hebrews 11

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 13:21–22; 14:19–22, 29** — the pillar of cloud; the people *"into the middle of the sea on the
  dry ground"* — [[The Crossing of the Sea]]
- **1 Corinthians 10:1–2** — *"Now I would not have you ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under
  the cloud, and all passed through the sea; and were all baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the
  sea"* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Hebrews 11:29** — *"By faith, they passed through the Red Sea as on dry land. When the Egyptians tried
  to do so, they were swallowed up."* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The New Testament reads the crossing twice, and in two different directions.**

**Paul reads it as baptism.** *"Our fathers were all under the cloud (hypo tēn nephelēn), and all passed
through the sea (dia tēs thalassēs diēlthon); and were all baptized into Moses (eis ton Mōysēn
ebaptisanto) in the cloud and in the sea"* (1 Corinthians 10:1–2; SBLGNT). **Two details of Exodus are
taken over exactly**: the cloud, which in Exodus 14:19–20 stood over and behind the camp, and the sea,
passed *through*. **Paul adds what Exodus does not say — that the crossing was a baptism *"into Moses"*** —
on the model of being baptized *into Christ* (Romans 6:3; *not yet ingested*). **And Exodus 14:31 supplies
the nearest thing to it in the text: *"they believed in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses"*.** The people are
joined to Moses at the sea, in Exodus by belief, in Paul by a word for initiation.

- **The verb form.** The SBLGNT prints ***ebaptisanto***, an aorist middle (*had themselves baptized*, or
  simply *were baptized*), and marks the word as having a textual variant. The variant reading is not checked
  in this pass and the wiki draws nothing from the voice.
- **What Paul does with it.** The verses that follow say that *"with most of them, God was not well
  pleased"* and that *"these things were our examples"* (1 Corinthians 10:5–6; WEB, SBLGNT checked;
  *not yet ingested*) — *katestrōthēsan*, *they were laid low*. **The baptism at the sea is cited as a warning: all were baptized, and most fell.** The
  typology is not a guarantee.

**Hebrews reads it as faith.** *"By faith (pistei), they passed through (diebēsan) the Red Sea (tēn
Erythran Thalassan) as on dry land (hōs dia xērās gēs)"* (Hebrews 11:29; SBLGNT). **The verse keeps three
things from Exodus 14**: the passing through, the *dry land* (*yabbāšâ*, 14:16, 22, 29), and the
Egyptians' attempt. ***"When the Egyptians tried to do so, they were swallowed up"*** — ***katepothēsan***,
from *katapinō*, *swallow down*. **The Hebrew of Exodus 14 does not use *swallow*; the Song does** —
*"You stretched out your right hand. The earth swallowed (bālaʿ) them"* (15:12; WLC). What Greek word the
Septuagint uses at 15:12 is not checked in this pass. See
[[Swallowed Up - Pharaoh's Dream, Aaron's Staff, and the Sea]].

**The two readings differ on who acts.** In Exodus the people are told to *"stand still"* and to *"go
forward"*, and the act is Yahweh's (14:13–14, 30). **Hebrews credits the crossing to the people's faith;
Paul to their being joined to Moses.** Exodus 14:31 places belief *after* the crossing — *"Israel saw … and
they believed"* — and Hebrews places it before, as what carried them through. **The wiki records the order
in each text and does not reconcile them.** See [[Faith]].

## Significance

- **It is the New Testament's own reading of the sea**, and it is double: a sacrament that did not save the
  unfaithful (Paul) and an act of faith that did (Hebrews).
- **It extends the water typology the wiki has tracked from the flood.** 1 Peter 3 reads the ark as
  baptism; Paul reads the sea. See [[The Flood and Baptism]] and
  [[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]].
- **It makes Exodus 14:31's *"and in his servant Moses"* the hinge.** Belief in Moses beside belief in
  Yahweh is new in the text, and Paul's *"into Moses"* gives that bond a sacramental name.
- **Paul's paragraph goes on from the sea to the food and the water.** *"and all ate the same spiritual food;
  and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank of a spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock
  was Christ"* (1 Corinthians 10:3–4; WEB). Paul does not name the episodes; the food is usually read as the
  manna, and the rock as the water of Exodus 17 and Numbers 20.
  See [[Bread from Heaven - The Manna and John 6]] and
  [[The Rock That Followed Them - Exodus 17, Numbers 20, and 1 Corinthians 10]].
