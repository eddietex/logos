# Who Has Ascended - Proverbs 30.4, Job 38, and John 3.13

## Passages involved

- [[The Words of Agur]] — Proverbs 30:2–4.
- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:20–28.
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:4–18.
- [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]] — Job 42:1–6.
- John 3:12–13 — heavenly knowledge and the Son of Man; verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Agur’s confession of inadequate understanding leads into questions about ascent and descent,
wind held in fists, waters in a garment, and the ends of the earth. Job 28:23–27 locates wisdom
with God and links it to wind and water; Job 38:4–18 questions human knowledge of the earth’s
foundations, sea, and breadth. Job finally acknowledges speaking beyond his understanding
(42:3). These are parallels in subject and rhetorical form, not proof of literary dependence.

The voices differ. Agur asks questions after confessing his limits. In Job 38 God questions Job;
in Job 42 Job answers God. Neither comparison warrants assigning Agur a revelation experience
like Job’s whirlwind or identifying the two speakers.

Proverbs 30:4 ends by asking for the name of the cosmic governor and his son. It does not supply
the son’s name. John 3:12–13 moves from earthly and heavenly understanding to the claim that the
one who descended from heaven, the Son of Man, has ascended there. The verified WEB includes its
closing description of the Son of Man as being in heaven. No claim about Greek wording or
textual variants is made here.

**Interpretive boundary:** a Christian reading can place John’s identified Son of Man beside
Agur’s unanswered questions. That is a canonical comparison, not a claim that Proverbs explicitly
names Jesus or that John formally quotes this verse. Nor does the question establish an otherwise
unknown divine genealogy. The identity of the son remains unresolved within Proverbs 30 itself.

## Significance

[[Wisdom]], [[Creation]], and [[The Names of God]] meet at the boundary between human limits
and knowledge received from God. The comparison preserves both Agur’s unanswered question and
John’s particular claim, making the later Christian reading visible without substituting it for
the earlier passage’s wording.
