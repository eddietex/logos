## Analysis

**What the text says.** Judges 21 joins Jabesh Gilead to Benjamin by marriage: four hundred of the town's
daughters become wives of the six hundred surviving Benjamites, who *returned to their inheritance, built
the cities, and lived in them* (21:14, 23). Saul is a Benjamite, and his town is Gibeah, the town whose
crime began the war (Judges 19–20; 1 Samuel 11:4, *Gibeah of Saul*). In 1 Samuel the first deliverance of
Saul's reign is of Jabesh Gilead, and at his death Jabesh Gilead alone goes for his body.

**What it does not say.** 1 Samuel never mentions Judges 21, and never gives a reason for Jabesh's loyalty
beyond Saul's rescue of the town (11:9–11). Many readers infer a bond of kinship: the Benjamites of Saul's
generation would descend in part from Jabesh's daughters. The inference is natural; it is not stated. The
wiki records it as a reading.

**The pattern either way.** Read in canonical order, the three episodes reverse one another. In Judges 21
Israel comes *as one man* against Benjamin (20:1, 8, 11) and then sends twelve thousand against Jabesh to
supply Benjamin's wives. In 1 Samuel 11 a man of Gibeah of Benjamin cuts a body of oxen in pieces, as the
Levite cut the concubine (Judges 19:29), and Israel comes out *as one man* to save Jabesh. In 1 Samuel 31
Jabesh repays him. See [[Cut Limb by Limb - The Burnt Offering, the Concubine, and Saul's Oxen]] and
[[Seven Days and Seven Nights - The Week of Mourning]].

## Significance

The town that Israel destroyed for not coming to Mizpah is the town Israel's first king comes to save and
the town that keeps faith with him when he falls. Judges ends by saying there was no king; 1 Samuel's first
royal victory is at the place Judges' last chapter left in ruins. See [[Jabesh Gilead]], [[Benjamin]],
[[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], [[Kingship and Empire]], and
[[Those Who Did Not Come - Meroz, Succoth and Penuel, and Jabesh Gilead]].
