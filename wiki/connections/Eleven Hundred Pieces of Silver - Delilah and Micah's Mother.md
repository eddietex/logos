# Eleven Hundred Pieces of Silver - Delilah and Micah's Mother

## Passages involved

- [[Samson and Delilah]] — **Judges 16:5, 18** — *Entice him … and we will each give you eleven hundred
  pieces of silver* (5); *the lords of the Philistines came up to her, and brought the money in their
  hand* (18). Stored WEB.
- **Judges 17:2–3** — *The eleven hundred pieces of silver that were taken from you, about which you
  uttered a curse, and also spoke it in my ears, behold, the silver is with me. I took it* (2); *He
  restored the eleven hundred pieces of silver to his mother, then his mother said, "I most certainly
  dedicate the silver to Yahweh from my hand for my son, to make a carved image and a molten image"* (3).
  Stored WEB; not yet ingested, see *Micah's Idol and Levite* on [[Judges]].

## Analysis

**The same sum, back to back.** *ʾElep ûmēʾâ kesep*, *a thousand and a hundred of silver*, occurs in Judges
at 16:5 and 17:2, 3, and nowhere else in the book (WLC; the other *thousand and … hundred* of Judges,
8:26, is Gideon's gold). The two stories are adjacent: Samson's ends at 16:31 and Micah's begins at 17:1.
Nothing in the text links them except the figure.

| | Judges 16 | Judges 17 |
|---|---|---|
| whose silver | each lord of the Philistines | Micah's mother |
| how it moves | paid to a woman for a betrayal | stolen by a son from his mother, then returned |
| what it buys | Samson's capture, blinding, and the mill | *a carved image and a molten image* (17:3) |
| who is wronged | Israel's judge | Yahweh, in whose name the idol is dedicated |

**Inside and outside.** In chapter 16 the silver comes from Israel's enemies. In chapter 17 it is entirely
Israelite: an Israelite son steals it, an Israelite mother curses the thief and then blesses him, *May
Yahweh bless my son!* (17:2), and dedicates it *to Yahweh* to make an idol. The figure crosses the chapter
break and the corruption moves from the Philistines' hands into an Israelite household. This is a reading;
the text does not comment.

**Why the same number?** Interpreters have suggested that the repetition is a deliberate seam between the
Samson cycle and the epilogue (chapters 17–21), or that *eleven hundred* was a conventional large sum, or
even that Micah's mother is Delilah. None of these is in the text; the last is speculation the text gives
no ground for, since Micah is *a man of the hill country of Ephraim* (17:1, stored WEB) and his mother is
with him there, while Delilah lives in the valley of Sorek. The wiki records the shared figure and does not adopt an explanation.

## Significance

- **Silver as the solvent.** The last story of a judge ends with Israel's deliverer sold for silver, and the
  book's epilogue opens with Israel's worship corrupted by the same sum. The refrain that follows, *In
  those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes* (17:6, stored
  WEB), is the book's verdict on the second; the first has no verdict. See
  [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].
- **No one in either story asks Yahweh.** Delilah, the lords, and Micah's mother all act on their own
  account; the mother names Yahweh only to bless and to dedicate an idol. The wiki notes it; Judges does
  not draw it out.
