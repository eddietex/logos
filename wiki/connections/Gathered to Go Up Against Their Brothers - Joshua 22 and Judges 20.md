# Gathered to Go Up Against Their Brothers - Joshua 22 and Judges 20

## Passages involved
- [[The Altar of Witness by the Jordan]] — Joshua 22:10–34; the congregation gathers at Shiloh to go to war
  against the eastern tribes, sends Phinehas first, and does not fight.
- [[The War Against Benjamin]] — Judges 20:1–48; the congregation gathers at Mizpah against Benjamin, and
  Phinehas stands before the ark while it fights.
- [[Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas]] — Numbers 25:7–13; Phinehas's first act and the *covenant of
  peace*.

## Analysis

The only two times in Joshua and Judges that all Israel assembles to make war on part of itself, the
priest is the same man, *Phinehas the son of Eleazar*, and the assembly is told in the same verb and noun:

| | Joshua 22 | Judges 20 |
|---|---|---|
| the gathering | *wayyiqqāhălû kol-ʿăḏaṯ bənê-yiśrāʾēl šilōh*, *the whole congregation … gathered themselves together at Shiloh* (12) | *wattiqqāhēl hāʿēḏâ kəʾîš ʾeḥāḏ … hammiṣpâ*, *the congregation was assembled as one man … at Mizpah* (1) |
| the purpose | *laʿălôṯ ʿălêhem laṣṣāḇāʾ*, *to go up against them to war* (12) | *ʿālêhā bəḡôrāl*, *we will go up against it by lot* (9) |
| the question sent | *mâ hammaʿal hazzeh*, *What trespass is this …?* (16) | *mâ hārāʿâ hazzōʾṯ*, *What wickedness is this …?* (12) |
| who carries it | Phinehas and ten princes, one for each tribe (13–14) | *men through all the tribe of Benjamin*, unnamed (12) |
| the precedent cited | Peor, and Achan: *That man didn't perish alone in his iniquity* (17, 20) | none |
| the reply | the eastern tribes explain the altar (21–29) | *Benjamin would not listen* (13) |
| Phinehas's word | *Today we know that Yahweh is among us* (31) | he stands before the ark while Israel asks *or shall I cease?* (28) |
| the end | *spoke no more of going up against them to war* (33) | Benjamin reduced to six hundred men (47) |

(Hebrew WLC; English stored WEB.)

**The difference is the answer.** In Joshua 22 the accused tribes speak, and what they say ends the war
before it starts. In Judges 20 the accused tribe will not hand over the men, and the assembly had already
sworn not to go home until it was done (20:8). The procedure is the same in both places: the congregation
gathers and sends a question before any fighting. In Judges it fails.

**Phinehas between the two.** At Peor Phinehas *turned my wrath away from the children of Israel* by
killing, and received *my covenant of peace* (Numbers 25:11–12). In Joshua 22 he goes as a mediator, and no
one dies. In Judges 20 he does not speak to Benjamin at all; he appears in a parenthesis, standing before the
ark at Bethel, and asks Yahweh whether to go on. The narrator gives him no other words.

**What the texts do not say.** Joshua 22 is not cited in Judges 20. The verb *qāhal* with *ʿēḏâ* is common
for assemblies of Israel, so the shared gathering formula alone would not make a connection. What joins the
passages is the situation — the whole congregation against part of itself — and the same priest in both.
Whether the narrator of Judges meant the reader to remember Joshua 22 is the wiki's question, not the text's.

## Significance

Joshua 22 shows the assembly avoiding a war among the tribes by sending first and listening. Judges 20 uses
the same procedure, and Benjamin refuses. The wiki's other page on the war,
[[Who Shall Go Up First - Judges 1.1-2 and 20.18]], traces the book's opening question turned against a
brother tribe; this page traces the same congregation, with the same priest, at two outcomes. The
grandson of Aaron who mediated at the Jordan is still serving when the tribes go to war at Gibeah, and Judges
dates the scene by him: *in those days* (20:27–28). See [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]] and [[Brotherhood]].
