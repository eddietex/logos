# In the Beginning — Genesis 1 and John 1

## Passages involved

- [[Genesis 1.1-2.3 - The Creation of the World]] — Genesis 1:1–5
- John 1:1–5 *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

John opens his Gospel with Genesis's first three words — *"In the beginning"* — and then holds
the reader inside Genesis 1's imagery for four more verses. The parallels are point-for-point,
and in the same order:

| Genesis 1 | John 1 |
|---|---|
| *"In the beginning, God created…"* (1:1) | *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."* (1:1) |
| God creates by speaking: *"God said…"* (1:3) | The speech is a person: *"All things were made through him."* (1:3) |
| *"Let there be light," and there was light* (1:3) | *"In him was life, and the life was the light of men."* (1:4) |
| Light divided from darkness (1:4) | *"The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness hasn't overcome it."* (1:5) |

The move John makes is to take the mechanism of Genesis 1 — creation accomplished by God's
spoken word — and identify that word as someone. Genesis says *God said*; John says the saying
was with God and was God, and later that it *"became flesh and lived among us"* (1:14). The
Genesis text on its own supports no such reading; John is not exegeting a hidden meaning but
claiming that the one who has now appeared is the agent already at work in verse 1.

Colossians 1:16 and Hebrews 1:2 make the same identification without the Genesis vocabulary.

## Significance

This is the New Testament's most direct reach back to the Bible's opening line, and it sets the
pattern for reading creation christologically. It also frames John's Gospel as a new beginning:
having started where Genesis starts, John will go on to a new birth (3:3), a new creation on the
first day of the week (20:1), and — in 20:22 — a breathing on the disciples that echoes the
breath of Genesis 2:7.

Revisit this page when John 1 is ingested, and again for Genesis 2:4–25 (the breath) and
Genesis 3 (the darkness that follows the light).
