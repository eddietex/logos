# A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt

## Passages involved

- [[Saul Comes to the Seer]] — 1 Samuel 9:16; *I have looked upon my people, because their cry has come to me*, the words of Exodus 3:7, 9.
- [[The Ark Among the Philistines]] — 1 Samuel 5:12; *the cry of the city went up to heaven*, *šawʿâ*, the noun of Exodus 2:23.
- [[The Tears of the Oppressed]] — Ecclesiastes 4:1–3; tears without comfort, compared with the heard cry in Exodus.

- **Genesis 18:20–21** — *"Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very
  grievous, I will go down now, and see …"* — the first *ṣəʿāqâ* in Scripture —
  [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]]
- **Genesis 19:13** — *"the outcry against them has grown so great before Yahweh that Yahweh has sent
  us to destroy it"* — [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]]
- **Genesis 27:34** — *"When Esau heard the words of his father, he cried with an exceeding great and
  bitter cry"* — the firstborn's blessing lost — [[The Stolen Blessing]]
- **Exodus 2:23** — *"they cried, and their cry came up to God because of the bondage"* —
  [[Moses Flees to Midian]]. The Hebrew here is different — the verb *zāʿaq* and the noun *šawʿâ*
  (WLC) — so the verse belongs to the motif but not to the word
- **Exodus 3:7, 9** — *"I … have heard their cry because of their taskmasters"*; *"the cry of the
  children of Israel has come to me"* — [[The Burning Bush]]
- **Exodus 11:6** — *"There shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there has not
  been, nor shall be any more"* — [[The Last Plague Announced]]
- **Exodus 12:30** — *"there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one
  dead"* — [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]]. **The forecast of 11:6 kept**, with the same
  noun and adjective, *ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ* (WLC)
- **Exodus 22:23–24** — *"If you take advantage of them at all, and they cry at all to me, I will surely
  hear their cry; and my wrath will grow hot, and I will kill you with the sword; and your wives shall be
  widows, and your children fatherless"* —
  [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]]
- **Genesis 19 and Exodus 12** — the two nights of destruction, compared word by word at
  [[The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night]]

## Analysis

**In the Torah *ṣəʿāqâ*, *cry* or *outcry*, is first the cry of the wronged that reaches God.** Its
first use is the *outcry* of Sodom (Genesis 18:20–21), which God *"goes down"* to see; at 19:13 it *"has
grown so great"* that the city is destroyed. **The cry is a legal word as much as a sound: an
accusation that comes before a judge.** At the bush God takes Israel's cry in the same way — *"I have
heard their cry because of their taskmasters … I have come down to deliver them"* (3:7–8), with the same
*come down* as Genesis 18:21.

**In Exodus 11 and 12 the cry changes sides.** The oppressor now cries: *"a great cry throughout all the
land of Egypt"* (11:6), *"a great cry in Egypt"* (12:30), the noun with the adjective *gədōlâ* both
times. **The book's first cry is Israel's under Egypt; its loudest is Egypt's over its dead.**

**The only other *ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ* in the Torah is Esau's** (Genesis 27:34, *wayyiṣʿaq ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ
ûmārâ ʿad-məʾōd*; WLC lemma search of Genesis–Deuteronomy). **So both great cries in the Torah are over
a firstborn**: Esau's for the firstborn's blessing taken by the younger brother, Egypt's for its
firstborn sons, struck because Egypt would not release the people God calls *"my son, my firstborn"*
(Exodus 4:22). Whether the writer meant the echo cannot be shown; the shared phrase and the shared
subject are both in the text. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**The law then writes the pattern down.** In the Book of the Covenant, the widow and the fatherless who
*cry* to God will be heard, and the oppressor will lose his own: *"your wives shall be widows, and your
children fatherless"* (Exodus 22:23–24). **The exodus's measure — the oppressed cry, God hears, the
oppressor's house is made to cry — becomes the sanction of Israel's own law against Israel.**

**With Exodus 22 ingested, two details sharpen the law's use of the word.** Verse 23 is built on
three doubled verbs — *ʿannēh təʿanneh … ṣāʿōq yiṣʿaq … šāmōaʿ ʾešmaʿ ṣaʿăqātô* (MAM 22:22) — and in
the Hebrew it is singular: *if you afflict **him** … **his** cry*, where the WEB has *"them … their
cry"*. **One wronged person's cry is enough.** And the cry recurs four verses later, for the poor man
whose cloak was kept overnight: *"when he cries to me … I will hear, for I am gracious"* (22:27).
See [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].

### Tears without a narrated rescue — Ecclesiastes 4:1–3

[[The Tears of the Oppressed]] describes the victims' tears and the oppressors' power, but no
intervention. Exodus 2:23–25 reports that God hears, remembers, sees, and is concerned;
3:7–9 announces deliverance, and 22:21–27 promises a hearing when vulnerable people cry.
These affirmations stand in tension with Ecclesiastes' unresolved observation. They do not
supply a rescue that Ecclesiastes 4:1–3 itself narrates, and that observation does not state
that God never hears. This is a thematic contrast, not a claim that Ecclesiastes uses the
Hebrew cry vocabulary analyzed above. Sources: stored WEB texts at these references.

### The pledged woman's cry — Deuteronomy 22:23–27

[[Marriage Accusations and Sexual Offenses]] makes *ṣāʿaq* a matter of evidence. The pledged woman in the city is stoned *"because she
didn't cry"* (22:24); the woman in the field is spared because *"the pledged to be married lady cried, and
there was no one to save her"* (22:27) — *ṣāʿăqâ … wǝʾên môšîaʿ lāh* (WLC). The law presumes a cry in the
field where no one could have heard it. Here the cry goes unanswered at the time, and the law's
judgment answers it afterward. *"No one to save"* returns in the curses of 28:29, 31.

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 22:23–27; 28:29, 31. Hebrew from WLC (morphhb), retrieved 2026-11-09.

## Significance

- **It ties the plagues to the Sodom narrative** by the cry that comes up to God and the God who comes
  down (Genesis 18:21; Exodus 3:7–8; 11:4).
- **It sharpens the firstborn theme**: the two great cries in the Torah are both over the loss of a
  firstborn's portion.
- **It sets up the law's warning** that Israel can become the Egypt of its own widows and orphans.

### Judges 6:6–7 — the cry under Midian

[[Midian's Oppression and the Prophet]]: *Israel was brought very low because of Midian; and the children of Israel cried to Yahweh. When
the children of Israel cried to Yahweh because of Midian, Yahweh sent a prophet* (6:6–8). The verb is
*zāʿaq* (WLC), the verb of Exodus 2:23. The prophet then recalls the exodus that answered that first cry
(*I brought you up from Egypt*, 6:8), and names the oppressors with *lāḥaṣ*, the word of Exodus 3:9
(WLC). The cry in Judges is told in the words of the cry in Egypt, but here the first answer is a
rebuke. Sources: stored WEB Exodus 2:23; 3:9; Judges 6:6–9; Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex
(OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

### The Philistine cry — 1 Samuel 5:12

Exodus 2:23 is the motif's *šawʿâ* outlier. The noun's only other occurrence in the WLC files of Genesis
through 1 Samuel is 1 Samuel 5:12, *the cry of the city went up to heaven*, with the same verb, *ʿālâ*
(lemma search). It is the cry of Ekron under Yahweh's hand: the oppressor in Israel's old place. See
[[Their Cry Went Up - Exodus 2.23 and 1 Samuel 5.12]]. Sources: stored WEB Exodus 2:23; 1 Samuel 5:12;
Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The cry answered with a king — 1 Samuel 9:16

*I have looked upon my people, because their cry has come to me*, *kî rāʾîṯî ʾeṯ-ʿammî kî bāʾâ ṣaʿăqāṯô ʾēlāy* (1
Samuel 9:16, WLC). It is Exodus 3 compressed: *I have surely seen the affliction of my people* (3:7) and *the cry of
the children of Israel has come to me*, *ṣaʿăqaṯ bənê-yiśrāʾēl bāʾâ ʾēlāy* (3:9, WLC). The noun *ṣəʿāqâ*, the verb
*bôʾ*, and the preposition *ʾēlāy* are the same. At the bush the answer was Moses; here it is Saul, *to save my
people out of the hand of the Philistines*. A chapter earlier Samuel had warned, *You will cry out in that day because
of your king … and Yahweh will not answer you* (8:18, *zāʿaq*). The book now sets the cry the king answers beside the
cry the king will cause. See [[Saul Comes to the Seer]]. Sources: stored WEB Exodus 3:7, 9; 1 Samuel 8:18; 9:16;
Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.
