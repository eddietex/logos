# A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 18:20–21** — *"Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very
  grievous, I will go down now, and see …"* — the first *ṣəʿāqâ* in Scripture —
  [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]]
- **Genesis 19:13** — *"the outcry against them has grown so great before Yahweh that Yahweh has sent
  us to destroy it"* — [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]]
- **Genesis 27:34** — *"When Esau heard the words of his father, he cried with an exceeding great and
  bitter cry"* — the firstborn's blessing lost — [[The Stolen Blessing]]
- **Exodus 2:23** — *"they cried, and their cry came up to God because of the bondage"* —
  [[Moses Flees to Midian]]. The Hebrew here is different — the verb *zāʿaq* and the noun *šawʿâ*
  (WLC) — so the verse belongs to the motif but not to the word
- **Exodus 3:7, 9** — *"I … have heard their cry because of their taskmasters"*; *"the cry of the
  children of Israel has come to me"* — [[The Burning Bush]]
- **Exodus 11:6** — *"There shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there has not
  been, nor shall be any more"* — [[The Last Plague Announced]]
- **Exodus 12:30** — *"there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one
  dead"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Exodus 22:23–24** — *"If you take advantage of them at all, and they cry at all to me, I will surely
  hear their cry; and my wrath will grow hot, and I will kill you with the sword; and your wives shall be
  widows, and your children fatherless"* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**In the Torah *ṣəʿāqâ*, *cry* or *outcry*, is first the cry of the wronged that reaches God.** Its
first use is the *outcry* of Sodom (Genesis 18:20–21), which God *"goes down"* to see; at 19:13 it *"has
grown so great"* that the city is destroyed. **The cry is a legal word as much as a sound: an
accusation that comes before a judge.** At the bush God takes Israel's cry in the same way — *"I have
heard their cry because of their taskmasters … I have come down to deliver them"* (3:7–8), with the same
*come down* as Genesis 18:21.

**In Exodus 11 and 12 the cry changes sides.** The oppressor now cries: *"a great cry throughout all the
land of Egypt"* (11:6), *"a great cry in Egypt"* (12:30), the noun with the adjective *gədōlâ* both
times. **The book's first cry is Israel's under Egypt; its loudest is Egypt's over its dead.**

**The only other *ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ* in the Torah is Esau's** (Genesis 27:34, *wayyiṣʿaq ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ
ûmārâ ʿad-məʾōd*; WLC lemma search of Genesis–Deuteronomy). **So both great cries in the Torah are over
a firstborn**: Esau's for the firstborn's blessing taken by the younger brother, Egypt's for its
firstborn sons, struck because Egypt would not release the people God calls *"my son, my firstborn"*
(Exodus 4:22). Whether the writer meant the echo cannot be shown; the shared phrase and the shared
subject are both in the text. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**The law then writes the pattern down.** In the Book of the Covenant, the widow and the fatherless who
*cry* to God will be heard, and the oppressor will lose his own: *"your wives shall be widows, and your
children fatherless"* (Exodus 22:23–24). **The exodus's measure — the oppressed cry, God hears, the
oppressor's house is made to cry — becomes the sanction of Israel's own law against Israel.**

## Significance

- **It ties the plagues to the Sodom narrative** by the cry that comes up to God and the God who comes
  down (Genesis 18:21; Exodus 3:7–8; 11:4).
- **It sharpens the firstborn theme**: the two great cries in the Torah are both over the loss of a
  firstborn's portion.
- **It sets up the law's warning** that Israel can become the Egypt of its own widows and orphans.
