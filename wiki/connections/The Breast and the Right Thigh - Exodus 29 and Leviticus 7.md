# The Breast and the Right Thigh - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 7

## Passages involved

- [[The Eighth Day and the Appearing Glory]] — Leviticus 9:1–24.

- [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]] — Leviticus 8:1–36.

- [[The Ordination of the Priests]] — Exodus 29:22–28.
- [[Fat, Blood, and the Priests’ Share]] — Leviticus 7:28–36.
- [[The Portion of the Priests and Levites]] — Deuteronomy 18:3–4.

## Analysis

Exodus 29:22–25 places the consecration ram's right thigh with the fat and bread in
Aaron's and his sons' hands, then orders these portions burned. Its breast belongs to
Moses (26). Verses 27–28 then establish the breast and thigh as continuing portions
for Aaron and his sons from Israel's peace offerings.

Leviticus 7:30–34 specifies the continuing distribution: the breast is waved and belongs
to Aaron and his sons, while the right thigh belongs to the son who offers the blood
and fat. The fat goes to the altar. Verses 35–36 associate the priestly grant with
anointing and service through the generations.

**Apparent contradiction, distinguished by rite:** the right thigh burned in Exodus
29:22–25 is from the ram of consecration; the thigh given to the officiant in Leviticus
7:32–33 is from a peace offering. Exodus itself moves from the exceptional ordination
portions to the continuing grant. Reading all these instructions as a single undifferentiated
meal would erase the distinction between installation and subsequent service.

The passages connect consecration to material support. They do not authorize the priest
to consume everything brought to the altar. Nor does the breast's communal designation
in Leviticus specify an equal division formula for every priest on every occasion.

### The consecration now ingested — Leviticus 8

Leviticus 8:25–29 now performs the ordination allocation: right thigh, fat, and bread are
waved in the priests’ hands and burned, while Moses receives the breast. This confirms
the rite-specific distinction already drawn between Exodus 29 and Leviticus 7. Aaron and
his sons are commanded to eat the remaining consecration meat and bread at the door
(8:31–32). This meal is neither the whole burnt ram nor permission to consume every portion.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Exodus 28–29 and Leviticus 6–8, checked 2026-10-29.

### The inaugural service — Leviticus 9

Aaron waves the breasts and right thigh of the people's peace offerings (9:21),
while the fat burns on the altar (20). The narrative applies the portion distinctions
of 7:28–34 without narrating a meal or explicitly restating the recipients. It does not
report burning the thigh as in the installation ram of 8:25–28. The service's category
matters when comparing the portions.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Leviticus 4:1–12; 7:28–38; 8:33–36; 9:1–24
and Exodus 40:34–38, checked 2026-10-29.

### Different cuts — Deuteronomy 18:3

Deuteronomy 18:3 names a different priestly share: *"from those who offer a sacrifice, whether it be
ox or sheep, … the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the inner parts"*. In the WLC these are *zərōaʿ*,
*ləḥāyayim*, and *qēbâ*. The last two occur nowhere else in Genesis–Deuteronomy, and *zərōaʿ* as a
sacrificial cut only at Numbers 6:19, where the Nazirite's boiled shoulder is waved *"together with
the breast that is waved and the thigh that is offered"* (6:20). Leviticus 7:34 names *ḥăzê
hattənûpâ* and *šôq hattərûmâ*.

**Apparent contradiction, recorded and not resolved.** Leviticus gives the breast and right thigh of
the peace offering; Deuteronomy gives the shoulder, cheeks, and stomach of a sacrifice of ox or sheep,
and does not mention breast or thigh. Proposed harmonizations: Deuteronomy's portions come from
animals slaughtered for food in the towns (12:15, 21); they are an addition to Leviticus' portions;
or the two laws reflect different practice. Numbers 6:19–20 shows a shoulder given alongside breast
and thigh in one rite, so the cuts are not mutually exclusive in principle; it does not show that
Deuteronomy 18:3 means the same. The distinction-by-rite used above for Exodus 29 and Leviticus 7 does
not obviously apply here, since both laws describe continuing provision.

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 7:31–34; Numbers 6:19–20; Deuteronomy 12:15, 21; 18:3. Hebrew from WLC
(morphhb), lemma searches for 2220, 3895, 6896 across Genesis–Deuteronomy, retrieved 2026-11-07.

## Significance

The priesthood's support is built into the offering instructions. Divine ownership,
the donor's participation, and the priest's portion are compatible because the text
assigns their respective roles. See [[Priesthood]] and [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
