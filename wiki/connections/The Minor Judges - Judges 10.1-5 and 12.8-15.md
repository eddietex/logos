# The Minor Judges - Judges 10.1-5 and 12.8-15

## Passages involved

- [[Tola and Jair]] — **Judges 10:1–5** — Tola of Issachar (23 years) and Jair the Gileadite (22
  years). (stored WEB)
- [[Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon]] — **Judges 12:8–15** — Ibzan of Bethlehem (7 years), Elon the
  Zebulunite (10 years), and Abdon the son of Hillel the Pirathonite (8 years). (stored WEB)
- [[Shamgar and the Ox Goad]] — **Judges 3:31** — the earlier one-verse savior, sometimes counted
  with them; see Analysis.

## Analysis

Five judges get short notices with no story. Scholars call them the *minor judges*, a modern
label, not a term in the text. The two groups sit on either side of Jephthah's story (10:6–12:7).
Each notice is built from the same parts, in the same order:

| | Tola (10:1–2) | Jair (10:3–5) | Ibzan (12:8–10) | Elon (12:11–12) | Abdon (12:13–15) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Link | *after Abimelech* | *after him* | *after him* | *after him* | *after him* |
| Origin | *a man of Issachar*, in Shamir | *the Gileadite* | *of Bethlehem* | *the Zebulunite* | *the Pirathonite* |
| Years judged | 23 | 22 | 7 | 10 | 8 |
| Family | — | 30 sons on 30 donkey colts, 30 cities | 30 sons; 30 daughters sent out, 30 brought in | — | 40 sons and 30 grandsons on 70 donkey colts |
| Burial | Shamir | Kamon | Bethlehem | Aijalon in Zebulun | Pirathon in Ephraim |

The key verbs are the same throughout. In the WLC each notice uses *wayyišpōṭ ʾet-yiśrāʾēl*, *he
judged Israel*, and closes with *wayyāmot … wayyiqqābēr*, *and he died … and was buried* (10:2–5;
12:8–15). Tola and Jair are introduced with *wayyāqom*, *arose* (10:1, 3). The three in chapter 12
are introduced with the judging verb itself, *wayyišpōṭ ʾaḥărāyw*, *and after him judged* (12:8, 11,
13).

Only Tola is said to *save*. *Arose to save Israel*, *ləhôšîaʿ* (10:1), uses the cycle's verb (2:16).
None of the five has an enemy, an outcry, the Spirit, or a rest notice.

Two of the five have sons who ride *ʿăyārîm*, *donkey colts* (10:4; 12:14; lemma 5895). For Jair the
same word is used again for his sons' *cities*. Ibzan's sixty daughters and daughters-in-law, married
*outside his clan*, are the other family note. Read together, the notes list large households,
riders, and marriages, the signs of wealth and standing. The Song calls on *you who ride on white
donkeys* (5:10).

The same closing, *died and was buried in*, ends Jephthah's notice in the middle of the two groups:
*Then Jephthah the Gileadite died, and was buried in the cities of Gilead* (12:7). His six years are
given in the same form (12:7). The form that frames the minor judges also closes the major judge
between them. See [[Shibboleth]].

## Significance

- **The cycle without its parts.** The narrative judges are told through the cycle of 2:11–19: sin,
  oppression, cry, a savior, rest, relapse. The minor judges have only the judge, the years, and the
  grave. The book has two ways of telling a judge's life, and it alternates them.
- **Burial in every notice.** Every minor judge has a named grave. Among the cycle judges so far, only
  Gideon has one, *in the tomb of Joash his father* (8:32); Jephthah follows (12:7) and Samson later
  (16:31). See [[Burial and the Grave]].
- **Clan names.** Tola and Puah are the names of Issachar's clans (Genesis 46:13; Numbers 26:23), and
  Elon is the name of a Zebulunite clan (Genesis 46:14; Numbers 26:26). Two of the five judges carry
  their own tribe's clan names. See
  [[Clan Names Among the Minor Judges - Genesis 46, Numbers 26, and Judges 10 and 12]].
- **Seventy years.** 23 + 22 + 7 + 10 + 8 = 70. The text gives no total, and the wiki records the sum as
  arithmetic, not as a claim about design.
- **The tribes spread across the land.** Issachar, Gilead, Bethlehem, Zebulun, and Ephraim. That
  the spread is deliberate is a reading, not a statement of the text.
- **Which Bethlehem — a revision.** This page first read Ibzan's Bethlehem as *Judah's town*, which
  would give the five a southern judge. The ingest of [[Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon]] found that Judges
  names *Bethlehem Judah* at every other occurrence (17:7–9; 19:1–2, 18; WLC *bêt leḥem yəhûdâ*) but
  leaves 12:8, 10 bare, and Joshua 19:15 lists a Bethlehem in Zebulun, the next judge's tribe. The
  earlier reading rested on the better-known town; the text supports either. If Zebulun's is meant,
  the five come from north, east, north, north, and centre, with no judge from the south. The wiki
  now leaves the question open. See [[Bethlehem]] and [[Bethlehem (Zebulun)]].
- **A frame around Jephthah.** The Gileadite Jair comes just before the oppression of Gilead
  (10:8), and Jephthah the Gileadite is the deliverer. The quiet notices on both sides set off the
  only story of this section.
- **Shamgar.** Shamgar's verse (3:31) is also short and storyless, and opens *after him*. But it has
  a deed, six hundred Philistines with an ox goad, and no years, no *judged*, and no burial. Whether
  he belongs with the five is a matter of definition. The text does not group them.
