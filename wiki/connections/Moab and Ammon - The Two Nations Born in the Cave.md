# Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave

## Passages involved

- [[Lot and His Daughters]] — Genesis 19:30–38, the origin: *"He is the father of the Moabites to
  this day"*, *"He is the father of the children of Ammon to this day"*
- [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] — Genesis 14:5, the Emim and Zuzim already in the country
  the two nations will hold
- Deuteronomy 2:9, 19 — *"I have given Ar to the children of Lot for a possession"*, and the same
  for Ammon
- Deuteronomy 23:3–6 — both nations barred from the assembly *"to the tenth generation"*
- Numbers 22–24; 25:1–3 — Balak of Moab and Balaam; Baal Peor
- Judges 3:12–30; 11:4–33 — Eglon of Moab; Jephthah against Ammon
- 1 Samuel 11:1–11; 22:3–4; 2 Samuel 10:1–14; 12:26–31 — Ammon besieging Jabesh Gilead; David
  lodging his parents with the king of Moab; the Ammonite war
- 1 Kings 11:5, 7, 33; 14:21, 31; 2 Kings 23:13 — Chemosh and Milcom on the hill east of Jerusalem,
  and Naamah the Ammonitess, mother of Rehoboam
- Isaiah 15–16; Jeremiah 48; 49:1–6; Ezekiel 25:1–11; Amos 1:13–2:3 — the oracles
- Zephaniah 2:8–11 — *"Moab will be as Sodom, and the children of Ammon as Gomorrah"*
- Ruth 1:1–4; 4:13–22 — Ruth the Moabitess, and the line to David
- Nehemiah 13:1–3, 23–27 — Deuteronomy 23 read out and enforced
- Matthew 1:5 — Ruth in the genealogy of Jesus

## Analysis

Genesis explains most of the peoples around Israel in a list. Moab and Ammon it explains in a
scene, and the scene is the ugliest in the Abraham cycle. **What happens to those two nations
afterwards is the reason the wiki keeps this as a thread rather than filing it under the
passage.** The canon does four different things with them, and they do not add up.

### 1. They are given their land by God, by name, as Lot's children

The single most surprising verse in the thread is in Deuteronomy, and it is spoken on the march to
the conquest:

> Don't bother Moab, neither contend with them in battle; for I will not give you his land for a
> possession, because I have given Ar to the children of Lot for a possession. *(Deuteronomy 2:9)*

And nineteen verses later, the same for Ammon (2:19). **In the book whose entire subject is the
taking of Canaan, Israel is forbidden to take Moab or Ammon, and the ground of the prohibition is a
divine land grant to the offspring of the cave.** The formula is the one used of Israel's own
inheritance — *given for a possession* — and the recipients are identified not by their own names
but as *bənê Lôṭ*, the children of Lot.

Deuteronomy 2 goes further and supplies a small ethnography: the **Emim** held Moab before Moab did,
the **Zamzummim** held Ammon, both were Rephaim, and God dispossessed them for Lot's descendants
exactly as he did the Horites for Esau and will do the Canaanites for Israel. **The conquest
theology is applied to Moab and Ammon in their own favour.** See
[[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]].

### 2. They are barred from the assembly, on the Sodom charge

> An Ammonite or a Moabite shall not enter into Yahweh's assembly; even to the tenth generation
> shall no one belonging to them enter into Yahweh's assembly forever, because they didn't meet you
> with bread and water on the way when you came out of Egypt. *(Deuteronomy 23:3–4)*

**The stated reason is inhospitality**, and it is worth pausing on how exactly that lands. The
grandfather of both nations spent his last night in Sodom shutting a door against a town that would
not leave his guests alone, and the thing his descendants are excluded for is refusing bread and
water to travellers. The charge Ezekiel brings against Sodom — *"she and her daughters had pride,
fullness of bread, and prosperous ease… neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy"*
(Ezekiel 16:49) — and the charge Deuteronomy brings against Sodom's survivor's children are the
same charge. See [[Hospitality]].

Deuteronomy 23:4–5 then adds the second count, Balaam, and closes it with the reversal that
governs the whole thread: *"Nevertheless Yahweh your God wouldn't listen to Balaam, but Yahweh your
God turned the curse into a blessing to you, because Yahweh your God loved you."*

### 3. They are Israel's standing enemies for six hundred years

Eglon of Moab holds Israel eighteen years and is killed on his own toilet (Judges 3:12–30).
Ammon's siege of Jabesh Gilead, with its demand for the right eye of every man in the town, is what
makes Saul king (1 Samuel 11). Ammon shaves David's envoys' beards and cuts their robes off at the
buttocks, and the war that follows is the one Uriah dies in (2 Samuel 10; 11:1; 12:26–31). Chemosh
and Milcom get shrines on the hill east of Jerusalem from Solomon and stand there until Josiah
(1 Kings 11:7; 2 Kings 23:13). The prophets' oracles against the two are among the longest they
write — Jeremiah 48 runs to forty-seven verses on Moab alone.

And Zephaniah closes the circle out loud: ***"Moab will be as Sodom, and the children of Ammon as
Gomorrah"*** (2:9). **The two nations that came out of the escape from Sodom are sentenced to
Sodom's fate.** No prophet says why the comparison is apt, and none of them has to.

### 4. And one of them is David's great-grandmother

> Ruth said, "Don't urge me to leave you, and to return from following you, for where you go, I
> will go… your people shall be my people, and your God my God." *(Ruth 1:16)*

**The book of Ruth is four chapters long, calls its heroine *the Moabitess* five times, and ends
with a genealogy that runs to David** (4:17–22). Matthew puts her in the ancestry of Jesus (1:5),
one of four women named there. The other Moabite detail in David's story is quiet and never
explained: hunted by Saul, he takes his father and mother to **the king of Moab** and asks him to
keep them (1 Samuel 22:3–4). The text gives no reason. His great-grandmother was Moabite.

Ammon has its own smaller version. **Naamah the Ammonitess** is named twice as the mother of
Rehoboam (1 Kings 14:21, 31), which makes an Ammonite woman the mother of the first king of Judah
after the split and an ancestor of every Davidic king thereafter.

### Holding the four together

The wiki records the tension rather than resolving it, and notes what the usual resolutions cost.

- **That Deuteronomy 23 concerns the male line, or public office, and not descent**, is the oldest
  Jewish answer — the Mishnah's ruling that *Ammonite and Moabite* is said of men and not of women
  (*Yevamot* 8:3) is what makes Ruth legally intelligible. It is a real reading of the Hebrew and it
  is also visibly a solution to a problem the text created.
- **That Ruth is deliberate polemic against the exclusion** is the standard modern account, and it
  has the advantage of explaining why the book insists on *the Moabitess* five times when once would
  do. It has to date Ruth late to work as a rebuttal, and that dating is contested.
- **That the canon simply holds both** is the least tidy and the most defensible: Deuteronomy 2
  protects them, Deuteronomy 23 excludes them, the prophets sentence them, and Ruth marries into the
  royal line, and no biblical writer anywhere reconciles the four. Nehemiah 13:1–3 has the community
  reading Deuteronomy 23 aloud and expelling foreigners on the strength of it — in a book that shows
  no sign of finding Ruth awkward.

## Significance

**Genesis 19:30–38 is the reason a reader can be told, four hundred pages later, that Moab is a
Sodom and that Moab is in the line of the Messiah, and hear both as the same story.** The etiology
does not fix the nations' standing; it gives them a starting point disreputable enough that
everything afterwards reads as a verdict on it.

That is what the thread is for. Taken as polemic alone, the cave explains the oracles and Zephaniah
2:9 and nothing else — not the land grant, not Ruth, not the fact that Deuteronomy calls them *the
children of Lot* in the act of protecting them. Taken as origin alone it explains nothing at all,
since Genesis attaches no evaluation to it. **What it actually functions as is a fixed point that
the rest of the canon keeps re-measuring from**, and the measurements disagree.

The last of them is the one the New Testament makes. Matthew's genealogy has forty-two generations
and names four women before Mary: Tamar, who was taken for a prostitute by her father-in-law; Rahab,
who was one; **Ruth the Moabitess**; and *"her who had been the wife of Uriah"*. Three of the four
are foreign and all four are attached to a scandal. **The list is not embarrassed, and the cave is
in it.** See [[Lot and His Daughters]] and
[[Righteous Lot - 2 Peter's Verdict and Genesis's Silence]].
