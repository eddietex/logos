# An Altar Called by a Name - El Elohe Israel, Yahweh Our Banner, and Yahweh Is Peace

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 33:20** — *"He erected an altar there, and called it El Elohe Israel."* — Jacob at
  Shechem, after Peniel and the meeting with Esau.
- **Genesis 35:7** — *"He built an altar there, and called the place El Beth El; because there God was
  revealed to him"* — the name given to the *place*, not the altar.
- **Exodus 17:15** — *"Moses built an altar, and called its name Yahweh our Banner."* — after the
  battle with Amalek.
- **Joshua 22:34** — *"The children of Reuben and the children of Gad named the altar 'A Witness
  Between Us that Yahweh is God.'"* — the altar by the Jordan; see
  [[A Witness Between Us - Galeed, the Jordan Altar, and the Shechem Stone]].
- **Judges 6:24** — *"Then Gideon built an altar there to Yahweh, and called it 'Yahweh is Peace.' To
  this day it is still in Ophrah of the Abiezrites."* — [[The Call of Gideon]]

All quotations from stored WEB.

## Analysis

**Most altars in Scripture are not named.** Noah's, Abram's at Shechem, Bethel, and Hebron, Isaac's at
Beersheba, and the altars of Sinai and Ebal are built and used without a name. The few that are named
form a small group, and in each case the name records what has just happened.

| Altar | Builder | Name | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis 33:20 | Jacob | *El Elohe Israel*, *God, the God of Israel* | the new name *Israel* given at Peniel (32:28) |
| Exodus 17:15 | Moses | *Yahweh our Banner* | Moses' raised hands and the victory over Amalek (17:11–13) |
| Joshua 22:34 | Reuben and Gad | *A Witness Between Us that Yahweh is God* | the accusation of rebellion answered (22:21–29) |
| Judges 6:24 | Gideon | *Yahweh is Peace* | *Peace be to you … You shall not die* (6:23) |

**The formula is the same.** Jacob's and Gideon's altars are named with *wayyiqrāʾ lô*, *and he called
it*, and Moses' with *wayyiqrāʾ šәmô*, *and he called its name* (WLC, Genesis 33:20; Exodus 17:15;
Judges 6:24). Genesis 35:7 uses the same verb for the place. Hagar names the God who spoke to her with
the same verb (Genesis 16:13), and Jacob names Peniel with it (32:30). Altars, places, and God himself
are named in the same way after an encounter.

**Gideon's name repeats what he was just told.** The other names are a confession (*God, the God of
Israel*), a battle cry (*our Banner*), or a defense (*a Witness*). Gideon's repeats the sentence he was
just given, *Šālôm lәkā*, *Peace be to you*, as *YHWH šālôm* (6:23–24, WLC). It was spoken to a man who
expected to die for having seen God, and the altar keeps it. It is also the only one of the four
built in a time of oppression and before the deliverance, not after a victory or reconciliation. *To
this day* (6:24) says the altar still stood when the narrator wrote.

**The next altar in Ophrah is torn down.** That same night Gideon is sent to throw down his father's
altar to Baal and build another to Yahweh on the stronghold (6:25–26). So the story has two altars to
Yahweh in one town within a day, the first named and the second not. The text does not say how the
two are related. The second is built *on the top of this stronghold, in an orderly way*, and used at
once for the second bull, burned with the wood of the Asherah (6:26, 28). It is not named. The name
given that day goes to Gideon instead: *Jerub-Baal*, *Let Baal contend against him* (6:32). See
[[Gideon Throws Down Baal's Altar]] and
[[Break Down Their Altars - Exodus 34.13, Deuteronomy 7.5, Judges 2.2, and Judges 6.25-32]].

## Significance

- **It groups the few named altars** and shows that the name records the event the altar marks.
- **It places Gideon's altar with the patriarchs' and Moses'.** The deliverer who doubts that Yahweh
  is still with Israel builds the same kind of memorial as Jacob and Moses.
- **It gives the divine-name compounds a setting.** *Yahweh is Peace* and *Yahweh our Banner* are
  altar names in their texts, not titles in a list; see [[The Names of God]].
