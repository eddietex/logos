# Stored for the Righteous - Proverbs 13.22 and Job 27.16-17

## Passages involved

- [[The Labor Left to Another]] — Ecclesiastes 2:18–26.

- **Proverbs 13:22** — *"A good man leaves an inheritance to his children’s children, but the
  wealth of the sinner is stored for the righteous."* — [[Hope Deferred]]
- **Job 27:16–17** — *"Though he heap up silver as the dust, and prepare clothing as the clay; he may
  prepare it, but the just shall put it on, and the innocent shall divide the silver."* —
  [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]
- **Job 27:13** — the heading of that portrait, *"This is the portion of a wicked man with God"* —
  see [[The Portion of a Wicked Man - Job 20.29 and Job 27.13]]
- **Job 21:7–8** — *"Why do the wicked live, become old, yes, and grow mighty in power? Their child
  is established with them in their sight"* — [[Why Do the Wicked Live]]
- **Proverbs 13:23** — *"An abundance of food is in poor people’s fields, but injustice sweeps it
  away."* — [[Hope Deferred]]

## Analysis

Proverbs 13:22 makes two claims about where wealth goes after its owner. The good man's passes down
his own line, two generations deep; the sinner's does not stay in his line at all but is *stored*
for someone else, the righteous. Job 27:16–17 tells the same transfer as a scene: the wicked man
heaps up silver *"as the dust"* and clothing *"as the clay"*, and the just wear the clothes and the
innocent divide the silver. The two passages agree on the outcome and differ in form — a two-line
sentence of instruction against a portrait inside a disputed speech.

**The speaker of Job 27:13–23 is uncertain**, and the comparison depends on it. As the text stands
the verses are in Job's mouth; on a widely held reconstruction they are a misplaced speech of
Zophar. The wiki records the readings on [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]] and on
[[The Portion of a Wicked Man - Job 20.29 and Job 27.13]] and does not choose between them here. On
either reading, Job 21:7–8 has already denied that the wicked man's goods and children reliably
leave him, which places Proverbs 13:22 in the same tension the wiki records for 13:9 and 13:21.

**Proverbs supplies its own counterweight in the next verse.** 13:23 describes wealth moving the
other way: a poor man's productive field emptied by *injustice*. Side by side, 13:22–23 say that
wealth does change hands unjustly, and that the sinner's wealth will in the end be stored for the
righteous. Neither verse says when, and the chapter does not reconcile them.

No literary dependence between Proverbs 13:22 and Job 27:16–17 is claimed; both state a conviction
of the wisdom tradition. Sources: quotations copied from stored WEB Proverbs 13:22–23 and Job
21:7–8; 27:13, 16–17. No source-language identity is asserted.

**Ecclesiastes gives the transfer an agent, and a tension.** In
[[The Labor Left to Another]], Ecclesiastes 2:26 says that God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy
to the person who pleases him, and gives the sinner the task of gathering for that person.
Proverbs 13:22 names the transfer without explicitly naming its divine agent; Ecclesiastes
names God. Job 27:16–17 depicts the innocent dividing what the wicked man accumulated.
This is a thematic parallel, not a claim of quotation or shared source-language wording.

The same Ecclesiastes unit first describes a worker who must surrender the fruit of wisdom,
knowledge, and skillfulness to a person who has not labored for it (2:18–21). The successor
might be wise or foolish. **Contrasting evaluations:** Proverbs 13:22 commends leaving an
inheritance to grandchildren; Ecclesiastes 2:21 calls this skilled worker's transfer a great
evil. Different descriptions of the recipient keep the two from being identical cases, but
the wiki does not let the positive proverb erase the Preacher's grievance.

Verse 26's morally ordered transfer does not identify the earlier worker as a sinner, and
its final vanity verdict remains. Job 21:7–8's wicked people established with their children
still contradict an exceptionless reading of the transfer rule. Source for this addition:
stored WEB Ecclesiastes 2:18–26; Proverbs 13:22–23; Job 21:7–8; 27:13, 16–17.

## Significance

The parallel is a second instance, beside [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]], of a sentence from
Proverbs 13 already at work in Job's dialogue. It belongs with [[Retribution]],
[[Wealth and Possessions]], and [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], and with Job's empirical answer in
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].
