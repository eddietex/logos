# Jacob, Jacob - The Doubled Name and Hinneni

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 22:11** — [[The Binding of Isaac]]. *"Abraham, Abraham!" He said, "Here I am."*
- **Genesis 46:2** — [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]]. *"Jacob, Jacob!" He said, "Here I am."*
- **Exodus 3:4** — the bush; *Moses, Moses*.
- **1 Samuel 3:10** — Shiloh; *Samuel, Samuel*.
- **Luke 10:41** — *Martha, Martha*.
- **Luke 22:31** — *Simon, Simon*.
- **Acts 9:4** — the Damascus road; *Saul, Saul*.

## Analysis

**Scripture doubles a personal name in direct address seven times, and the occasions are not
ordinary.**

| | who is called | what is at stake |
|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 22:11** | [[Abraham]], with the knife raised | the son, and the promise in him |
| **Genesis 46:2** | [[Jacob]], at the last town in the land | whether the family leaves Canaan |
| **Exodus 3:4** | Moses, at the bush | the commission that produces the Exodus |
| **1 Samuel 3:10** | Samuel, as a boy | the transfer of the word from Eli's house |
| **Luke 10:41** | Martha, in her own kitchen | a rebuke, gently delivered |
| **Luke 22:31** | Simon Peter, at the table | *Satan asked to have you* |
| **Acts 9:4** | Saul, on the ground | the turning of the persecutor |

**Four of the seven are Old Testament, and all four are answered with the same word.** ***Hinnēnî*** —
***"Here I am"*** — **is not a statement of location.** It is the Hebrew for placing oneself at
someone's disposal, and Genesis has used it of [[Abraham]] three times in one chapter (22:1, 7, 11),
of [[Jacob]] here, and of [[Joseph]] at 37:13 when his father sends him to Shechem to look for his
brothers. **It is what a servant says, and what a son says.**

**Genesis puts the word in seven mouths and the distribution is telling.** [[Abraham]] says it three
times in the Akedah (22:1, 7, 11); **[[Esau]] and [[Jacob]] each say it to a blind [[Isaac]] in the
same chapter** (27:1, 18), **one of them truthfully**; **[[Jacob]] reports having said it to the angel
of God in a dream** — *"the angel of God said to me in the dream, 'Jacob,' and I said, 'Here I am'"*
(31:11) — **with his name called once, not twice**; [[Joseph]] says it at 37:13, **on the errand that
ends in the pit**; and Jacob says it here. **The word is answered to God three times in the book and
to a human father three times, and one of the three to a father is a lie.**

### The shape is identical in the two Genesis cases

**Both come at night or at a moment of suspension. Both interrupt an action already under way** — the
knife is up, the household is packed. **Both are followed by a reversal of the thing the man was
braced for.**

- **At 22:11 Abraham is stopped**, and the substitute is provided.
- **At 46:2 Jacob is not stopped**; **he is released.** **He has sacrificed at [[Beersheba]] and is
  waiting to be told whether he may go**, and the doubled name introduces four verses of permission.

**The difference is worth stating plainly**: **the doubled vocative is not a warning formula.** **It
marks the moment a life's direction is settled**, and the settling can go either way.

### What the doubling does

**The wiki does not treat it as a technical device with a fixed meaning.** **Hebrew and Greek both
double a vocative for urgency and for affection, and the seven cases divide about evenly between
the two.** *Martha, Martha* and *Simon, Simon* are tender; *Abraham, Abraham* is a man being stopped
at speed. **What the seven have in common is not tone but consequence: nothing after any of them is
the same as what came before.**

**One further detail belongs to Genesis 46 alone.** **God calls him *Jacob* twice, in a verse that
opens *"God spoke to Israel"*** — **the narrator uses the new name and the voice uses the old one.**
**The man who was renamed at Peniel is addressed by the name he was born with at the moment he leaves
the land the new name was given in.** See
[[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

## Significance

**The motif ties the Joseph cycle back to the Akedah across twenty-four chapters.** **Genesis 46:2 is
the only divine speech in chapters 37–50**, and it opens with the formula the book last used when God
spoke to [[Abraham]] on the mountain. **Whatever else the narrative has been doing in the intervening
chapters — and it has been doing it without God speaking at all — the reader is told by two words that
this is the same voice.**

**It also supplies the pattern the calls of the Old Testament are built on**: **a name said twice, one
word in reply, and an instruction.** **Moses and Samuel both receive their commissions in that form**,
and in both cases the reply is Jacob's. See [[Call and Election]] and [[Prophecy and Vision]].
