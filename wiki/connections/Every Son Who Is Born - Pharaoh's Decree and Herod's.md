# Every Son Who Is Born - Pharaoh's Decree and Herod's

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 1:16, 22** — *"if it is a son, then you shall kill him"*; *"You shall cast every son who
  is born into the river"* — [[The Oppression in Egypt]]
- **Exodus 2:1–10** — the son hidden three months, set in the river in an ark, and drawn out by
  Pharaoh's own daughter — [[The Birth of Moses]]
- **Exodus 2:15; 4:19** — *"he sought to kill Moses"*; *"Go, return into Egypt; for all the men who
  sought your life are dead"* — [[Moses Flees to Midian]], [[Moses Returns to Egypt]]
- **Matthew 2:13–18** — the flight into Egypt; Herod *"killed all the male children who were in
  Bethlehem and in all the surrounding countryside, from two years old and under"* (2:16); Rachel
  weeping (2:18)
- **Matthew 2:19–20** — *"Arise and take the young child and his mother, and go into the land of
  Israel, for those who sought the young child's life are dead"*
- **Acts 7:19** — *"forced them to throw out their babies, so that they wouldn't stay alive"*
- **Hebrews 11:23** — Moses' parents *"were not afraid of the king's commandment"*

## Analysis

**Matthew tells the birth of Jesus in the shape of the birth of Moses.** A king fears for his
throne; he orders the death of the boys; the one child the order is meant to catch escapes it; and
the child is called back only when *"those who sought the young child's life are dead"* (Matthew
2:20) — **Exodus 4:19's words to Moses in Midian**, *"all the men who sought your life are dead"*,
with the plural kept though only Herod has died.

| | Exodus 1–2 | Matthew 2 |
|---|---|---|
| the king's fear | a people *"more and mightier than we"* (1:9) | *"he who is born King of the Jews"* (2:2) |
| the order | every son cast into the river (1:22) | every boy in Bethlehem two years and under killed (2:16) |
| the rescue | hidden three months, then a basket on the Nile (2:2–3) | warned in a dream, carried by night (2:13–14) |
| the refuge | Midian (2:15) | **Egypt** (2:14) |

**The inversion is the point.** For Moses, Egypt is the place of the massacre; for Jesus, Egypt is
the refuge from one, and *"Out of Egypt I called my son"* (Matthew 2:15) makes the child's return
the Exodus. **The massacre has moved into the land of Israel, and the king who orders it is the king
of the Jews.**

**Exodus 2:1–10 sharpens the inversion.** Moses is saved *inside* the king's household — by his
daughter, at his expense, with a Hebrew mother on the royal payroll (2:9); Jesus is saved by leaving
the king's country altogether. **Both rescues are worked by people the king does not suspect**, and
neither involves a confrontation with him. See [[The Birth of Moses]].

**Stephen's retelling compresses Exodus 1 toward the same point**: the three measures become one —
*"forced them to throw out their babies, so that they wouldn't stay alive"* (Acts 7:19) — and the
next verse is *"At that time Moses was born"* (7:20).

**Exodus 4 adds the other half of the pattern.** The verb of 4:19, *bāqaš*, is the verb of 2:15
(*"he sought to kill Moses"*), and it comes back five verses later with God as its subject (*"wanted to
kill him"*, 4:24). And in the same speech that sends Moses home, God names Israel *"my son, my
firstborn"* and announces the death of Pharaoh's firstborn (4:22–23): **the king who killed Israel's
sons will lose his own.** Matthew's *"Out of Egypt I called my son"* (2:15) takes up that sonship
through Hosea 11:1. See [[Israel My Firstborn - Exodus 4.22, Hosea 11.1, and Matthew 2.15]].

## Significance

Exodus 1's decree is the template for the persecuting king who tries to kill a promise by killing
children. Matthew uses it to say that Jesus recapitulates Israel's story from its beginning — and,
with Rachel weeping (Matthew 2:18), that Israel's mothers mourn under their own king as they did
under Pharaoh. See [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Life and Death]].
