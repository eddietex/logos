# Rise Up, Yahweh - The Ark's Cry in Numbers 10, Psalm 68, and Psalm 132

## Passages involved

- [[The March from Sinai]] — Numbers 10:33–36; the ark seeks a *resting place*, and Moses cries out at its moves
- Psalm 68:1, 7 (WLC 68:2, 8) — *Let God arise!* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Psalm 132:8, 14 — *Arise, Yahweh, into your resting place* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- 2 Chronicles 6:41 — Solomon's prayer ends with the same line *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- 1 Chronicles 28:2 — *a house of rest for the ark* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Psalms 3:7; 7:6 — *Arise, Yahweh*, in personal danger *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Deuteronomy 12:9 — *the rest and … the inheritance* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Psalm 95:11 and Hebrews 4:9 — the rest refused and the rest remaining *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Isaiah 66:1 — *Where will I rest?* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

Numbers 10:35–36 is two lines, and later texts seem to pick up both of them.

**The first line.** Moses says *qûmâ yhwh wəyāpuṣû ʾōyəḇeḵā wəyānusû məśanʾeḵā mippāneḵā* (WLC). Psalm 68:1 has *yāqûm
ʾĕlōhîm yāpûṣû ʾôyəḇāyw wəyānûsû məśanʾāyw mippānāyw* (68:2 in the WLC). The verbs, the nouns, and the closing *from
before him* are the same in the same order. The psalm changes *Yahweh* to *God*, and the address to the third person.
Psalm 68:7 later has *when you marched through the wilderness*. The psalm does not name Numbers, and the wiki takes the
link from the wording alone.

**The cry alone.** *Qûmâ yhwh* becomes a prayer of the psalms on its own, with no ark in sight. By the tags the Psalter
has it at 3:7, 7:6, 9:19, 10:12, 17:13, and 132:8 (WEB numbering; WLC verse numbers run higher in some of these). In
Psalms 3 and 7 the person is in danger, and asks what Moses asked for Israel's march.

**The rest.** In Numbers the ark goes ahead *to seek out a resting place for them*, and *mənûḥâ* is Israel's. Psalm 132:8
turns it around: *Arise, Yahweh, into your resting place; you, and the ark of your strength*. Now it is God's *mənûḥâ*,
and the ark is going in and not out. Verse 14 has God say *This is my resting place forever*. 2 Chronicles 6:41 puts the
line at the end of Solomon's prayer, with *qûmâ yhwh*, and 1 Chronicles 28:2 gives David's aim as *a house of rest for the
ark*. So the ark that set out at Sinai to find a rest for Israel ends by entering a rest of its own.

**The rest not reached.** Deuteronomy 12:9 says Israel has *not yet come to the rest*, and Psalm 95:11 has God swearing they
*won't enter into my rest*, both with *mənûḥâ* (WLC). Isaiah 66:1 asks of a built house, *Where will I rest?*, again with
the same noun, so the temple is not the end of the word's story. Hebrews 4:9 then says *There remains therefore a
Sabbath rest for the people of God*, though the Greek there was not checked for this page and it is arguing from Psalm 95,
not Numbers.

## Significance

The texts do not cite one another as a chain, and the sequence is the wiki's. What the wording supports is smaller and
firm: a line spoken as the ark set out became language for God's going before his people, and the noun for the ark's
goal stayed in use for what Israel still lacked. The two can be read together because Psalm 132 joins them, with
*qûmâ yhwh* and *mənûḥâ* in one verse. See [[The Dwelling of God]], [[Prayer]], and [[Walking with God]].
