# Buy Us and Our Land - Egypt Sold into Servitude and Israel After It

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 47:18–26** — the Egyptians sell themselves and their land to Pharaoh —
  [[Joseph and the Famine]]
- **Genesis 47:27** — and in the next verse Israel takes possession and multiplies —
  [[Joseph and the Famine]]
- **Exodus 1:8–14** — *"there arose a new king over Egypt, who didn't know Joseph"*; Israel put under
  taskmasters
- **Exodus 5:6–19** — the straw withheld and the quota kept: the bondage administered in detail
- Background: **Genesis 37:28, 36; 39:1** — Joseph himself sold into Egypt as an *ʿebed* —
  [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]
- Background: **Genesis 44:9–33** — eleven Hebrews offering to be *"my lord's slaves"* and being
  refused — [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]
- Compare **Genesis 15:13–14** — *"they will serve them, and they will afflict them four hundred
  years"* — [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]
- Compare **Deuteronomy 6:21; 15:15; 24:18** — *"you were a slave in Egypt"*, the stated ground of
  Israel's own law

## Analysis

***ʿEbed*** **— servant, slave — is the hinge word of the whole Joseph narrative, and Genesis 47 is
where it changes hands.**

**The book has used it in one direction for ten chapters.** A seventeen-year-old is sold to
Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver (37:28) and becomes *"an Egyptian's slave"* (39:1). His
brothers, in Egypt, call themselves *"your servants"* a dozen times out of court courtesy (42:10, 11,
13; 43:18, 28; 44:7), **and then offer the real thing** — *"behold, we are my lord's slaves"*
(44:16) — **and are turned down.**

**At 47:19 the word is spoken by Egyptians, about Egyptians, and it is accepted.**

> **47:19** Why should we die before your eyes, both we and our land? Buy us and our land for bread,
> and we and our land will be servants to Pharaoh.
> **47:25** They said, "You have saved our lives! Let us find favor in the sight of my lord, and we
> will be Pharaoh's servants."

**The man who was bought buys.** **Genesis does not say this, and the shape of the sentence is
unmistakable once the chapters are read together.**

### The two verses that sit next to each other

**The structural fact of the pericope is that 47:26 and 47:27 are adjacent.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **47:25–26** | *"we will be Pharaoh's servants" … "Joseph made it a statute … that Pharaoh should have the fifth"* |
| **47:27** | *"Israel lived in the land of Egypt … and they got themselves possessions therein, and were fruitful, and multiplied exceedingly"* |

**One nation loses its land and its liberty; the household living inside it acquires a freehold and
grows.** **The same administration produced both outcomes and the text reports them in consecutive
sentences with no connective at all.** See [[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].

### And then it reverses

**Exodus 1 is Genesis 47 run backwards, and it takes four verses.**

| | **Genesis 47** | **Exodus 1** |
|---|---|---|
| **who multiplies** | *"Israel … were fruitful, and multiplied exceedingly"* (47:27) | *"The children of Israel were fruitful, and increased abundantly, and multiplied"* (1:7) |
| **who administers** | Joseph, for Pharaoh | *"a new king over Egypt, who didn't know Joseph"* (1:8) |
| **the instrument** | purchase, by consent, for bread | *"taskmasters over them to afflict them with their burdens"* (1:11) |
| **the outcome** | *"we will be Pharaoh's servants"* (47:25) | *"made their lives bitter with hard service"* (1:14) |
| **the response** | *"You have saved our lives!"* (47:25) | *"their cry came up to God"* (2:23) |

***"Who didn't know Joseph"*** **is Exodus's own explanation and it is worth taking literally.** **The
arrangement Joseph built required Joseph** — a vizier whose family was the exempt population.
**Remove the man and the machinery remains**, a crown owning the land and the labour of everyone on
it, **with an unassimilated foreign population in [[Goshen]] that is now the only group in Egypt not
already accounted for.** **Exodus 1:10's *"let's deal wisely with them"* is that machinery being
pointed at them.**

### The word that Israel is given to remember

**The Torah's humane legislation is grounded, over and over, in a single sentence about this
period.**

- *"We were Pharaoh's slaves in Egypt, and Yahweh brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand"*
  (Deuteronomy 6:21).
- *"You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and Yahweh your God redeemed you;
  therefore I command you this thing today"* (Deuteronomy 15:15) — **the reason given for releasing a
  Hebrew servant in the seventh year and sending him away furnished.**
- *"You shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt"* (Deuteronomy 24:18) — **the reason given for
  not perverting the justice due to a foreigner or a fatherless child.**

***"Pharaoh's slaves"*** — ***ʿabdê parʿōh*** — **is the phrase the Egyptians of Genesis 47:25 used
about themselves first.** **Israel's law is built on the memory of a status that Genesis records a
whole nation volunteering for, in gratitude, one chapter earlier.** See [[Remembrance]].

## Significance

**Genesis 15:13 had already told [[Abraham]] what was coming** — *"your offspring will live as
foreigners in a land that is not theirs, and will serve them. They will afflict them four hundred
years"* — **and Genesis 47 shows the country acquiring the capacity.** **A land where every field
belongs to the crown and every farmer is the crown's tenant is a country that can put two million
people to work on store-cities by administrative decision.** **That capacity is built in this
chapter, by a Hebrew, with the consent of the governed.**

**The wiki does not read this as Joseph's fault**, and notes that the text offers no such reading:
his brothers' sale of him is the crime Genesis names (37:28; 42:21; 50:17), **not his land policy**,
and Exodus blames a king who did not know him rather than the office he held. **What the connection
records is the mechanism** — that the same institution can be salvation for the generation that
consents to it and bondage for the one that inherits it — **and that Scripture lays the two side by
side without a word of transition.** See [[Providence]] and [[Kingship and Empire]].
