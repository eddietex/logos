# The Half Shekel - Census, Ransom, and the Temple Tax

## Passages involved

- [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]] — Exodus 38:1–31; Census rate, arithmetic, sockets, and fittings (38:25–28).

- **Exodus 30:11–16** — at a census each man pays *"a ransom for his soul to Yahweh … that there be
  no plague among them"*: half a shekel, the same for rich and poor, *"atonement money"* for *"the
  service of the Tent of Meeting"* ([[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]])
- **Exodus 38:25–28** — *"a beka a head, that is, half a shekel"* from 603,550 men; the silver cast
  into the sockets of the sanctuary and the veil, and the hooks and fillets of the pillars ([[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]])
- **Numbers 1:2–3** — *"Take a census of all the congregation … from twenty years old and upward,
  all who are able to go out to war in Israel"* ([[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]])
- **2 Samuel 24:1–2, 10, 15** — David orders Joab to *"count the people, that I may know the sum of
  the people"*; afterwards *"David's heart struck him"*, and *"Yahweh sent a pestilence on
  Israel"*
- **Matthew 17:24–27** — *"Doesn't your teacher pay the didrachma?"*; *"Therefore the children are
  exempt. But, lest we cause them to stumble …"*; a stater from a fish's mouth, *"for me and you"*

## Analysis

**In Exodus the payment has three names.** It is *kōper napšô*, *a ransom for his soul* (30:12); a
*tərûmâ*, *"an offering to Yahweh"* (30:13), the word of the sanctuary offering of 25:2; and *kesep
hakkippurîm*, *"atonement money"* (30:16, MAM). The danger it answers is *negep*, *"plague"*, *"when
you count them"* (30:12). Exodus does not say why counting endangers Israel. It says only that a
counted life must be ransomed, and at a fixed price.

**Exodus 38 shows where the silver went.** The census total of 603,550 men yields one hundred
talents and 1,775 shekels (38:25–26). The talents become the hundred sockets under the frames and
the veil; the rest the hooks and bands of the pillars (38:27–28). The ransom literally became the
foundations of the tabernacle.

**2 Samuel 24 is a census followed by plague.** David orders a count, his heart strikes him
afterward, he confesses *"I have sinned greatly"*, and seventy thousand die in a pestilence
(24:10, 15). The chapter does not mention a half shekel or Exodus 30, and it gives its own account of
why the count happened (24:1). Many interpreters read it in light of Exodus 30:12, as a census
taken without the ransom. That reading is an inference: the text of 2 Samuel 24 does not state it.

**Matthew 17 is about a temple tax of two drachmas.** The collectors ask whether Jesus pays *"the
didrachma"* (17:24). The usual identification of this tax with the half shekel of Exodus 30 depends
on the Greek coin's value and on Second Temple practice outside the Bible, neither of which was
checked in this pass; the identification is common but is reported here, not verified. Jesus' answer
— kings take tribute *"from strangers"*, *"therefore the children are exempt"* (17:25–26) — treats
him and Peter as sons of the house. He pays anyway, *"lest we cause them to stumble"*, with one coin
*"for me and you"* (17:27).

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38

The inventory now supplies both the rate and the allocation: 603,550 men at half a shekel give
301,775 shekels, stated as 100 talents and 1,775 shekels (38:25–26). This implies 3,000 shekels per
talent within the account. The hundred talents become a hundred sockets, and the balance becomes
hooks, capitals’ overlay, and fillets (38:27–28). This calculation checks the narrative’s arithmetic
without supplying modern weights or independently verifying the census historically.

Exodus 35:24 also mentions voluntary silver gifts, whereas 38:25 expressly identifies its silver
total with those counted. The inventory does not separately reconcile the voluntary silver with the
census total. The fixed payment of 30:11–16 and the willing gifts of chapter 35 must both be
retained.

### The census — Numbers 1

Numbers 1 commands a count and names no half shekel, no ransom, and no plague. It reaches the same
603,550, and it is dated a month after the point where Exodus places the silver account. See
[[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]] and
[[Six Hundred Three Thousand Five Hundred Fifty - Exodus 38.26 and Numbers 1.46]].

## Significance

- The half shekel joins three of the wiki's threads: the census idiom *lift up the head* (see
  [[Lift Up Your Head - One Idiom and Two Verdicts]]), the *kōper* of a forfeit life (see
  [[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]]), and the root *kpr* traced in
  [[Atonement]].
- *"The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less"* (30:15) makes every counted
  life equal before God at the census. See [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]].
- If Matthew 17 is read with Exodus 30, the son of the house pays a ransom he says he does not
  owe. The text of Matthew does not draw that conclusion, and the wiki records it as a reading.
