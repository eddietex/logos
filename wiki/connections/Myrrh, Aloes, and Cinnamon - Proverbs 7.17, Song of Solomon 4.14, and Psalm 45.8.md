# Myrrh, Aloes, and Cinnamon - Proverbs 7.17, Song of Solomon 4.14, and Psalm 45.8

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 7:16–17** — [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]: *"I have spread my couch with
  carpets of tapestry, with striped cloths of the yarn of Egypt. I have perfumed my bed with myrrh,
  aloes, and cinnamon"* (*mōr ʾăhālîm wəqinnāmôn*)
- **Song of Solomon 1:13** — [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]]: *"My beloved is to me a sachet of
  myrrh, that lies between my breasts"* (*ṣərôr hammōr*)
- **Song of Solomon 3:6** — [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]]: *"perfumed with myrrh and
  frankincense, with all spices of the merchant"* (*mǝquṭṭereṯ mōr ûlǝḇônâ*)
- **Song of Solomon 4:6** — [[You Are All Beautiful]]: *"I will go to the mountain of myrrh, to the
  hill of frankincense"* (*har hammôr … giḇʿaṯ hallǝḇônâ*)
- **Song of Solomon 4:14** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"spikenard and saffron, calamus and
  cinnamon, with every kind of incense tree; myrrh and aloes, with all the best spices"* (*qāneh
  wəqinnāmôn … mōr waʾăhālôṯ*)
- **Psalm 45:8** *(45:9 MT; not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"All your garments smell like myrrh,
  aloes, and cassia"* (*mōr-waʾăhālôṯ qəṣîʿôṯ*)
- **Exodus 30:23, 32** — [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]]: *"liquid myrrh … fragrant
  cinnamon"* (*mor-dərôr … qinnəmān-beśem*) in the holy anointing oil, which *"shall not be poured
  on man's flesh"*

## Analysis

**The same three spices, in love and in kingship.** Proverbs' bed is perfumed with *mōr*,
*ʾăhālîm*, and *qinnāmôn*. The Song of Solomon's garden, which is the bride (4:12), has all three;
the king of Psalm 45, on his wedding day, wears myrrh and aloes. The Hebrew of all four texts is read
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*. These are imported luxuries, the perfumes of a
royal or bridal chamber, and Proverbs gives them to an adulteress's bed.

**And in the sanctuary.** Myrrh and cinnamon are also the first two spices of the holy anointing
oil (Exodus 30:23), forbidden for ordinary use. The cinnamon of Exodus is written *qinnəmān*,
Proverbs' and the Song's *qinnāmôn*; the noun is the same. Proverbs makes no reference to the
sanctuary oil, and the wiki claims no allusion. The overlap shows how costly these spices were —
fit for a king, a bride, and the tabernacle.

**What the parallel shows.** The strange woman's invitation borrows the setting of a wedding night:
fine linen, spices, and *ḏōḏîm*, the Song's word for love (Proverbs 7:18; Song of Solomon 4:10). What
the Song celebrates between bride and bridegroom, and the psalm at a royal wedding, Proverbs shows
counterfeited. The wiki notes that this matches Proverbs 5:15–19, where the same vocabulary of
delight was given to the son for his own wife; see
[[Your Own Well - Proverbs 5.15-19, Song of Solomon 4.12-15, and Ecclesiastes 9.9]].

**The Song's first myrrh.** Before the garden of chapter 4, myrrh enters the Song at 1:13, in the
woman's mouth. Her beloved is a sachet of myrrh worn between her breasts. Here the spice is
worn on the body, not spread on a bed, and the woman speaks of the man rather than a man of
the woman. The Hebrew *mōr* there is read from the same Sefaria edition (2026-10-28).

**Myrrh on a king's carriage.** At 3:6 myrrh and frankincense perfume Solomon's carriage as it
comes up from the wilderness. The participle *mǝquṭṭereṯ* is from *q-ṭ-r*, the root of the word for
incense, so the perfume is a fragrant smoke. Frankincense, *lǝḇônâ*, joins the Song's spices here
for the first time. Psalm 45:8 puts myrrh on the king's own garments at his wedding; see
[[A Royal Wedding Song - Psalm 45 and Song of Solomon 3.6-11]]. Hebrew from Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

**A mountain of myrrh.** At 4:6 the man says he will go *"to the mountain of myrrh, to the hill of
frankincense"*, the same two spices as 3:6 (*mōr*, *lǝḇônâ*; Sefaria). The verse follows his
description of her breasts (4:5), and the woman called her beloved a sachet of myrrh between her
breasts at 1:13. Many readers therefore take the mountain as a picture of the woman herself. The
verse does not say so, and the wiki leaves it as a reading. See [[You Are All Beautiful]].

## Significance

The spices give [[Love]] and [[Marriage]] a shared vocabulary across the wisdom and poetic books,
and they show the strange woman of [[Sexual Sin]] offering marriage's pleasures without the
marriage. The genre is not one of prohibition of pleasure: the same perfumes are praised in the Song.
