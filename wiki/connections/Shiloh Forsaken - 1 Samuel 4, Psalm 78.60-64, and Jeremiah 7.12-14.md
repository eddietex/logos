# Shiloh Forsaken - 1 Samuel 4, Psalm 78.60-64, and Jeremiah 7.12-14

## Passages involved

- [[The Ark Is Captured]] — 1 Samuel 4:1b–22
- Psalm 78:60–64 *(WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-17; not yet ingested)*
- Jeremiah 7:12–14 *(WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-17; not yet ingested)*; compare Jeremiah 26:6,
  cited by reference only

## Analysis

1 Samuel 4 narrates the loss of the ark and the death of the priests, and says nothing of what happened to
[[Shiloh]] itself. The sanctuary simply stops: the ark returns to Beth Shemesh and Kiriath Jearim, not to
Shiloh (6:12–7:2, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and Ahijah is later called *the priest of Yahweh in Shiloh*
only in his genealogy (14:3, stored WEB). Two later texts read the day as Yahweh's abandonment of the place.

**Psalm 78.** The psalm's survey of Israel's history reaches Shiloh after the conquest and its idolatry:
*So that he abandoned the tent of Shiloh, the tent which he placed among men; and delivered his strength
into captivity, his glory into the adversary's hand. He also gave his people over to the sword … Their
priests fell by the sword, and their widows couldn't weep* (78:60–62, 64, WEB). The details match 1 Samuel
4 point by point: the people given to the sword (4:10), the ark captured (4:11), the priests Hophni and
Phinehas killed (4:11), and a widow who does not mourn, because she dies (4:19–20). That last match is a
reader's; the psalm's plural *widows* need not mean her. The psalm's *glory* is *tip̄ʾartô* (78:61, WLC),
not the *kāḇôḏ* of Ichabod's name, and *abandoned*, *wayyiṭṭōš* (78:60, WLC), is the same verb the WLC uses
at 1 Samuel 4:2 for the battle *spreading*, *wattiṭṭōš* (lemma 5203) — the word is shared, the sense is not.

**Jeremiah 7.** Jeremiah, preaching in the temple gate, makes Shiloh the precedent for Jerusalem: *But go now
to my place which was in Shiloh, where I caused my name to dwell at the first, and see what I did to it for
the wickedness of my people Israel … therefore I will do to the house which is called by my name, in which
you trust, and to the place which I gave to you and to your fathers, as I did to Shiloh* (7:12, 14, WEB).
*In which you trust*, *bōṭəḥîm* (7:14, WLC), is not a word 1 Samuel 4 uses, but it names what the elders
did at 4:3 when they sent for the ark to *save us*: Jeremiah's hearers trust the temple as the elders
trusted the ark. The comparison is a reader's. *Where I caused my name to dwell*, *šikkantî šəmî
šām* (7:12, WLC), is the language of Deuteronomy's chosen place; see [[The Dwelling of God]].

**What is not said.** Neither 1 Samuel nor the two later texts describe Shiloh being destroyed by the
Philistines. That Shiloh was destroyed about this time is commonly argued from the excavations at Khirbet
Seilun (recalled from secondary literature, not checked), and the archaeological dating is disputed. The
wiki records the biblical texts and leaves the event's physical form open.

## Significance

- 1 Samuel 4 is the narrative; Psalm 78 is its liturgical memory; Jeremiah 7 is its use as a warning. Each
  reads the loss of the ark as Yahweh's own act, which 1 Samuel 4 implies but never states.
- The same pattern, a sanctuary trusted as a guarantee and then abandoned, links this pericope with
  [[Both His Ears Will Tingle - 1 Samuel 3.11, 2 Kings 21.12, and Jeremiah 19.3]] and
  [[Is Yahweh Among Us - Exodus 17, Numbers 14, 1 Samuel 4, and Micah 3]].
