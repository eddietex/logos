# Whoever Sheds Man's Blood

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 4:10–15** — Abel's blood crying from the ground, and God alone requiring it —
  [[Cain and Abel]]
- **Genesis 4:23–24** — Lamech's seventy-sevenfold boast, retribution with no ceiling —
  [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]]
- **Genesis 9:5–6** — *"Whoever sheds man's blood, his blood will be shed by man, for God made man
  in his own image"* — [[The Covenant with Noah]]
- **Exodus 20:13; 21:12–14** — *"You shall not murder"*, and the distinction between lying in wait
  and an act of God
- **Leviticus 24:17–21** — life for life, and the *lex talionis* stated as a limit
- **Numbers 35:9–34** — the cities of refuge, the two-witness rule, and the ban on ransom for a
  murderer
- **Deuteronomy 19:1–13; 21:1–9** — the same institutions restated, and the ritual for an unsolved
  killing
- **Matthew 26:52** — *"all those who take the sword will die by the sword"*
- **Romans 13:4** — the magistrate who *"doesn't bear the sword in vain"*
- **James 3:9** — cursing *"men who are made in the image of God"*
- **Revelation 13:10** — *"If anyone is to be killed with the sword, he must be killed"*

## Analysis

**The line is a chiasm, and it is the Bible's first legal sentence.**

```
šōpēk    dam    hā-ʾādām        ba-ʾādām   dāmô      yiššāpēk
shedder  blood  of-the-man      by-the-man his-blood shall-be-shed
   A       B        C               C'        B'         A'
```

Six words, folding back on themselves, with *ʾādām* on both sides of the pivot. The victim and the
executor are named by the same noun, and the penalty is described in the same verb as the crime.
That is not decoration: the form is the argument. What is done to a man is done back, in kind and in
measure, and nothing else.

**It closes a gap Genesis has kept open for five chapters.** After Cain kills Abel, the blood cries
and God answers it — but he answers it by *protecting* the murderer (4:15), and the protection is
sevenfold and comes with a sign. There is no rule; there is one case, handled personally. Five
generations later Lamech takes that same sevenfold and inflates it to seventy-seven, over a wound
(4:23–24), and nobody restrains him. By 6:11 *"the earth was filled with violence"*, and the flood
is the answer.

Genesis 9:6 is what the world gets instead of another flood: a rule, exactly proportionate, with no
multiplier at all. Cain's seven and Lamech's seventy-seven are both cancelled by *one*. This is *lex
talionis* four books before Exodus states it, and — like Exodus 21:23–25 — it is best read as a
**ceiling** rather than a licence. See [[Vengeance]].

**And it delegates.** In Genesis 4 the requiring of blood is God's own act. In Genesis 9:5 it still
is — *"I will surely require"*, three times — but 9:6 says the shedding will be *by man*. Something
moves from God's hands to human ones between the two verses, and the Torah then spends four chapters
building the apparatus Genesis conspicuously lacks: who may kill, on whose testimony, after what
finding of intent, and where a killer may run in the meantime. Numbers 35 is the fullest of them,
and its two most striking provisions both restrain the rule rather than extend it — no conviction on
one witness (35:30), and no ransom accepted for a murderer's life (35:31), so that a rich killer
cannot buy his way out and an accused man cannot be lynched.

**Is 9:6 command or observation?** The Hebrew has neither an imperative nor an addressee — a
participle and an imperfect, *the one shedding… his blood shall be shed*. Both readings have a
serious history:

- As **institution**, it is the foundation text of judicial capital punishment, and the Torah
  proceeds on that basis (Exodus 21:12, Leviticus 24:17, Numbers 35:16–21). Its universality matters
  here: the rule is given to Noah, before Israel exists, which is why the Christian tradition has
  treated it as binding on the state as such rather than on a covenant nation, and why Romans 13:4
  is read as its New Testament counterpart.
- As **prediction**, it is a statement about how violence propagates, and Jesus reads it that way at
  Gethsemane, in the plainest words available: *"all those who take the sword will die by the
  sword"* (Matthew 26:52). Revelation 13:10 makes the same line into a call for the endurance of the
  saints — not an instruction to anyone, but a description of where the sword ends up.

The readings are not exclusive, and Genesis 9 does not adjudicate between them: it supplies no
court, no penalty scale, no procedure, and no exceptions. A text can found a legal tradition without
settling one question about how to run it, and this one does.

**The reason clause is what the line is actually for.** *"For God made man in his own image."*
Genesis 9:6 is the only verse in the Torah that gives the image of God as the *ground* of a command,
and the command it grounds is the prohibition of homicide. Everything the theme has established
survives here: the image was granted at 1:26–27, was transmitted from Adam to Seth at 5:3, was never
revoked in Genesis 3, and after a flood it is still the reason a human death cannot be treated like
an animal's — twelve verses after animals were handed over as food. James 3:9 makes the same
argument about a lesser act: if people are made in God's likeness, then cursing them is incoherent
in the mouth that blesses God. See [[Image of God]].

## Significance

This is the point at which the Bible stops narrating violence and starts regulating it. The three
statements Genesis makes about returning harm are, in order, a protection for a murderer (4:15), a
boast with no limit (4:23–24), and a rule with an exact one (9:6) — and only the third is addressed
to everyone.

Its ground is also the reason it cannot be turned into a general licence. Blood is required because
the person killed carried God's image; the same premise that makes the killer liable makes the
killer an image-bearer too, which is why the Torah surrounds the rule with witnesses, intent, and
refuge, and why Numbers 35:33 frames the whole thing as a matter of the land's pollution rather than
the victim's family's satisfaction. The line runs from Abel's blood in the ground to the martyrs
under the altar still asking *"How long?"* (Revelation 6:10), and it is answered in the New
Testament not by a correction of Genesis 9 but by a blood *"that speaks better than that of Abel"*
(Hebrews 12:24).
