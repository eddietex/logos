# Two Tests of a Prophet - Deuteronomy 13.1-5 and 18.20-22

## Passages involved

- [[Do Not Follow Other Gods]] — Deuteronomy 13:1–5; the prophet whose sign comes to pass.
- [[A Prophet Like Moses]] — Deuteronomy 18:20–22; the prophet whose word does not happen.
- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:6–8; Yahweh speaks to a prophet in a vision or
  a dream, and to Moses *"mouth to mouth"*.
- [[Water Turned to Blood]] — Exodus 7:22; Pharaoh's magicians reproduce a sign.
- Matthew 24:24 — false prophets who *"show great signs and wonders"* (WEB, verified from
  bible-api.com).
- 1 John 4:1 — *"test the spirits, whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone
  out into the world"* (WEB, verified from bible-api.com).

## Analysis

**Deuteronomy gives two tests, and they run in opposite directions.**

| | Deuteronomy 13:1–5 | Deuteronomy 18:20–22 |
|---|---|---|
| The prophet speaks | *"Let us go after other gods"* (13:2) | *"presumptuously in my name"*, or *"in the name of other gods"* (18:20) |
| The sign or word | *"comes to pass"* (13:2) | *"doesn't follow, nor happen"* (18:22) |
| What it shows | *"Yahweh your God is testing you"* (13:3) | *"that is the thing which Yahweh has not spoken"* (18:22) |
| Israel's response | *"you shall not listen"* (13:3) | *"You shall not be afraid of him"* (18:22) |
| The prophet | *"shall be put to death"* (13:5) | *"shall die"* (18:20) |

The first test is about content. A word that leads away from Yahweh is false whatever its sign
achieves. The second is about outcome. A word in Yahweh's name that fails was not Yahweh's.

**Together they set a one-way rule.** A failed prediction disqualifies a prophet; a fulfilled one
does not qualify a prophet. Deuteronomy 13 says so in the strongest possible form by imagining a
sign that really happens and still has to be refused. 18:20 also covers both cases in a single
verse — the presumptuous word in Yahweh's name and the word *"in the name of other gods"* — so
the two chapters overlap on the second case.

**Neither test is sufficient on its own.** 18:22 can only be applied after the event, and 13:2
admits that events can mislead. Deuteronomy does not resolve the gap with a third test; 13:4 gives
instead a list of loyalties — walk after Yahweh, fear him, keep his commandments, obey his voice,
serve him, cling to him — against which any message is to be measured.

**The New Testament keeps the first test's warning.** Matthew 24:24 expects false christs and false
prophets whose great signs and wonders could *"lead astray, if possible, even the chosen ones"*,
and 1 John 4:1 tells believers to *"test the spirits"*. Both assume, as Deuteronomy 13 does, that
signs alone do not settle the question. Galatians 1:8 applies a similar logic to messengers: even
*"an angel from heaven"* who preaches another gospel is to be cursed (WEB, verified from
bible-api.com).

**Earlier in the Torah, a matched sign settles nothing either.** Pharaoh's magicians match Aaron's
staff and then the blood and the frogs (Exodus 7:11–12, 22; 8:7). The point there is different —
the magicians fail at the lice — but Exodus already shows a sign performed on both sides. See
[[Signs and Wonders]].

## Significance

These two passages give the Torah's explicit criteria for recognizing a false prophet. They
anticipate Jeremiah's conflicts with prophets of peace and 1 Kings 22's four hundred prophets, which
are not yet ingested and will need to revisit this page. See [[Prophecy and Vision]] and
[[Testing]].
