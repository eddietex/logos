# Sold for a Servant - Joseph's Sale in Psalm 105 and Acts 7

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:18–36** — the conspiracy, the pit, the sale, and the coat. **No divine name occurs in the
  chapter**, and the narration passes no verdict.
- **Psalm 105:16–22** — *"He called for a famine on the land. He destroyed the food supplies. **He sent a
  man before them. Joseph was sold for a servant.** They bruised his feet with shackles. He was laid in
  irons, until the time that his word happened, and Yahweh's word proved him true. The king sent and freed
  him … He made him lord of his house, and ruler of all of his possessions."*
- **Acts 7:9–10** — *"The patriarchs, moved with jealousy against Joseph, sold him into Egypt. God was
  with him, and delivered him out of all his oppression, and gave him favor and wisdom before Pharaoh."*
- **Genesis 45:4–8** — *"I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt. … it was not you that sent
  me here, but God."*
- **Genesis 50:20** — *"you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good."*

## Analysis

**Two later texts narrate Genesis 37, and both of them put God in it.**

**Genesis 37 does not.** Neither *Yahweh* nor *ʾĕlōhîm* occurs in the chapter; the dreams are not
attributed; nobody prays; and the narration passes no judgement on the sale. **What the chapter reports
is a family, a coat, a cistern, and a price.** The two summaries below are how the rest of Scripture
reads it, and **what each of them adds is the thing Genesis 37 leaves out.**

### Psalm 105 — the sale as a dispatch

***"He sent a man before them. Joseph was sold for a servant."*** **Two clauses, and the first one
reassigns the agency of the second.** The psalm is a recital of Yahweh's acts for Israel, and it puts the
sale **inside the sequence of divine acts rather than beside it**: God calls a famine, God sends a man,
and *"Joseph was sold"* — **a passive with no agent named.**

**The brothers are not mentioned.** Not their hatred, not their conspiracy, not their profit. **The psalm
has no room for them because it is not telling the story of a crime**; it is listing the stages by which
a household reached Egypt and survived. **The verse before names the famine; the verse after names the
shackles.** In between, a man is sent.

***"They bruised his feet with shackles. He was laid in irons."*** **And this is an addition, not a
summary.** Genesis says Joseph was imprisoned (39:20) and says nothing about irons. **The psalm supplies a
physical detail the narrative withholds**, in the same register in which Genesis 42:21 supplies the
begging at the pit — **the tradition filling in what the story would not show.**

***"Until the time that his word happened, and Yahweh's word proved him true."*** ***Ṣərāpātəhû***, *tested
him*, *refined him as metal*. **The psalm reads the prison as an assay on the dreams** — which is exactly
what the brothers said they wanted to see the outcome of (*"we will see what will become of his dreams"*,
37:20) — **and gives the dreams the divine attribution Genesis 37 declined to give them.** See
[[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]] and [[Testing]].

### Acts 7 — the sale as the patriarchs' act

**Stephen's speech goes the other way, and it is the only text in Scripture that names the motive.**

***"The patriarchs, moved with jealousy against Joseph, sold him into Egypt."*** ***Zēlōsantes*** —
**envy, and the Greek is the Septuagint's word for the *qānāʾ* of Genesis 37:11**, *"his brothers envied
him."* **Stephen takes the chapter's own vocabulary and makes it the cause of the sale**, which Genesis
implies and never asserts. See [[Envy]].

***"The patriarchs."*** **The word is doing work.** Stephen is on trial before the Sanhedrin, and his
speech is a catalogue of Israel's leaders rejecting the men God sent them: **Joseph sold by the
patriarchs, Moses refused by the people — *"this Moses whom they refused"* (7:35) — and then
*"you always resist the Holy Spirit"* (7:51).** **Calling the twelve *the patriarchs* rather than *his
brothers* is the point**: the founders of the tribes are the first instance of the pattern the speech is
accusing his judges of.

***"God was with him."*** **Stephen supplies the sentence Genesis 37 withholds and Genesis 39:2
supplies** — *"Yahweh was with Joseph"* — **and puts it immediately after the sale**, so that the
deliverance and the crime are one clause apart, as they are in the book.

### The two summaries are not in competition

| | **Psalm 105:17** | **Acts 7:9** |
|---|---|---|
| **who acts** | God — *"he sent a man"* | the brothers — *"moved with jealousy"* |
| **the sale** | a passive with no agent | an act with named agents and a motive |
| **the brothers** | absent | called *the patriarchs* |
| **what it is for** | rehearsing God's faithfulness | indicting a court that repeats the pattern |

**Both readings are already in Genesis, and both are in Joseph's mouth.** *"I am Joseph, your brother,
whom you sold into Egypt"* and *"it was not you that sent me here, but God"* are **five verses apart in
the same speech** (45:4, 8), and *"you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good"* (50:20) puts
them in one sentence. **The psalm develops the second half and Stephen the first, and Genesis holds both
without explaining how.**

## Significance

- **It is the clearest case in the wiki of later Scripture supplying a chapter's missing verdict.** The
  wiki's practice with Genesis 37 is to report that the narration blames nobody; **Acts 7:9 blames
  somebody and Psalm 105:17 credits somebody, and neither is reading against the grain** — one takes the
  chapter's *envied* (37:11) and the other takes the book's own retrospect (45:8; 50:20).
- **It is a corrective to reading Genesis 37's silence as a theological statement.** The chapter says
  nothing about God; **the canon does not conclude from that that God was absent**, and neither does the
  wiki. See [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]].
- **Neither text mentions the price.** No sum in Psalm 105, none in Acts 7 — **and no New Testament text
  cites Genesis 37:28 at all.** The thirty pieces of Matthew 26:15 come from Zechariah 11:12, not from
  here; see [[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]].
- **Neither text mentions the coat, the dreams as dreams, the pit, or [[Reuben]].** **What survives into
  the summaries is: he was sold, God was with him, and he ended up governing Egypt.** The wiki notes the
  compression as a fact about how the event was remembered, and keeps the detail on the passage page.
- **Stephen's speech gets one detail from somewhere other than Genesis.** At Acts 7:16 he has Jacob and
  the patriarchs buried *"in the tomb that Abraham bought … from the sons of Hamor in Shechem"*, which
  **merges Abraham's purchase at Machpelah (Genesis 23) with Jacob's at Shechem (33:19)** and the burial
  of Joseph's bones there (Joshua 24:32). **The wiki records the conflation without resolving it**, and
  notes that it does not affect what the speech says about the sale. See [[Machpelah]] and
  [[Shechem (city)]].
- **It is also where the Joseph story becomes a pattern rather than an episode.** Stephen uses it as the
  first term in a series; **the New Testament's interest in Joseph is almost entirely in the
  rejected-and-exalted shape**, and never in the typological detail later Christian reading has found in
  the coat, the pit, and the silver. See [[The Nations]] and [[Suffering]].
