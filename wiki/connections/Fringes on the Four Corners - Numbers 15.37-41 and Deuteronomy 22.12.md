# Fringes on the Four Corners - Numbers 15.37-41 and Deuteronomy 22.12

## Passages involved

- [[The Tassels]] — Numbers 15:37–41; fringes with a blue cord, to look on and remember.
- [[Care for the Neighbor and Ordered Distinctions]] — Deuteronomy 22:12; fringes on the four corners
  of the cloak.
- Matthew 9:20; 14:36; 23:5 — the fringe of Jesus' garment, and the fringes enlarged; WEB checked at
  bible-api.com, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Numbers 15:38 tells Israel *"that they should make themselves fringes in the borders of their garments
throughout their generations, and that they put on the fringe of each border a cord of blue"*, and
gives the reason: *"that you may look on it, and remember all Yahweh's commandments, and do them"*
(15:39, stored WEB). The speech ends *"I am Yahweh your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt,
to be your God"* (15:41). Deuteronomy 22:12 has one sentence: *"You shall make yourselves fringes on
the four corners of your cloak with which you cover yourself"* (stored WEB).

**What Deuteronomy adds and leaves out.** It adds *"four"*: Numbers speaks of the *kanpê bigdêhem*, the
*corners* or *borders of their garments*, without a number. It names the garment as the *kǝsût*, the
covering one sleeps in, rather than Numbers' general *beged*. It leaves out the blue cord, the looking,
the remembering, and the reason. And it uses a different noun: *gǝdilîm*, *twisted threads*, occurring
in the Torah only here, where Numbers uses *ṣîṣit*, occurring only in Numbers 15:38–39 (WLC lemmas 1434,
6734; morphhb, retrieved 2026-11-08). Both laws use *kānāp*, *wing*, *corner*, *skirt*. That *gǝdilîm*
and *ṣîṣit* name one object is the common reading and is assumed in Jewish practice; the two texts do
not themselves say so.

**Placement.** In Numbers the fringes follow the Sabbath-breaker's execution, and their purpose is to
keep Israel from following *"your own heart and your own eyes"* (15:39). In Deuteronomy they follow
three laws against mixing kinds (22:9–11). In both, the fringe is a mark worn on ordinary clothing, and
in both it closes a sequence.

**The Gospels.** Matthew records a woman who *"touched the fringe of his garment"* (9:20) and the sick
who begged *"that they might just touch the fringe of his garment"* (14:36), and Jesus' charge that the
scribes and Pharisees *"enlarge the fringes of their garments"* to be seen (23:5) (WEB, bible-api.com,
2026-11-08). WEB's *fringe* in these verses is commonly taken to render the Septuagint's word for the
fringes of Numbers 15; the Greek of Matthew and of the Septuagint was not checked here, so that
identification is reported, not verified. If it holds, Jesus wore the fringes the law requires, and his
criticism in 23:5 is of their display, not of the law.

## Significance

The fringe is the Torah's one law of dress whose stated purpose is memory. See [[Remembrance]],
[[Law and Obedience]], [[Holiness]], and [[Nakedness and Covering]].
