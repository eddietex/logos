## Analysis

### Judges 1:17 ingested

[[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]] now holds the verse: *Judah went with Simeon his brother, and they struck the Canaanites who
inhabited Zephath, and utterly destroyed it. The name of the city was called Hormah.* Judges supplies
the reason Judah is there: Hormah lies in Simeon's lot (Joshua 19:4), and Judah had promised Simeon in
1:3, *I likewise will go with you into your lot*. In Judges the naming is part of a promise between
brother tribes kept, where in Numbers 21 it followed a vow to Yahweh.

Source: stored WEB Judges 1:3, 17; Joshua 19:4; Numbers 21:1–3.

### Deuteronomy’s sentence and ascent

Deuteronomy 1:44–46 is now ingested: Amorites pursue Israel like bees, beat them down in Seir as far
as Hormah, and the people return to weep without being heard. Numbers 14:45 gives Amalekites and
Canaanites as the enemies. The accounts share the defeat and endpoint but differ in the named
opponents; this update does not infer an exact route or harmonize the labels.

Source for this update: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 14 and 20.

**Now ingested: [[Arad and Hormah]].** Numbers 21:1–3 begins with an attack and captives,
then reports Israel's conditional vow, Yahweh's hearing, and destruction of the people
and cities. Victory is attributed to Yahweh, but a divine command initiating the vow
is not reported. The earlier rout at Hormah is a narrative contrast, not evidence
that every detail of the two battles or their sites is identical.

Source for this addition: stored WEB Numbers 14:39–45; 21:1–3.

By the tags the name occurs nine times in the Hebrew Bible, in the verses above. The first use
assumes the reader knows it.

**Three uses, two namings.** Numbers 14:45 uses the name in passing, with the article. Numbers 21:3
says Israel vowed, Yahweh gave up the Canaanite, and they destroyed them and their cities: *The name of
the place was called Hormah*. Judges 1:17 says Judah and Simeon struck the Canaanites of Zephath and
destroyed it: *The name of the city was called Hormah*. Numbers places this before the land is entered
and Judges after Joshua's death, and the people who do it differ. Joshua 12:14 already lists a king of
Hormah among those defeated, which fits neither account easily. All are kept as written.

**The root.** The verb in both namings is *ḥāram* (WLC: *wayyaḥărēm* at 21:3, *wayyaḥărîmû* at Judges
1:17), so each narrator explains the name as *destruction*. See
[[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]]. A
place-name need not originate that way, and this is a wordplay the text itself makes, not an
etymology the wiki can establish.

**The reversal.** The place where the condemned generation is beaten down is the place Israel next
beats the Canaanites, a reversal that comes in the same book. In chapter 14 Yahweh is absent and the
people go up against his word. In chapter 21 Israel vows first and *Yahweh listened to the voice of
Israel*. Deuteronomy 1:45 had just said of the first defeat, *Yahweh didn't listen to your voice*. The
pairing of these two sentences is the wiki's.

**Now ingested: [[The Kings Struck Down]].** Joshua 12:14 counts *the king of Hormah, one; the
king of Arad, one* among the kings whom *Joshua and the children of Israel struck* west of the
Jordan. The list gives no battle, no date, and no naming. It therefore adds a third setting —
Joshua's war — to Numbers 21 (Moses' time) and Judges 1:17 (after Joshua's death), and does not
explain how the three relate. The wiki links the king of Arad to [[King of Arad (of Moses' time)]]
only as a pointer to the earlier account. Source: stored WEB Joshua 12:14; Numbers 21:1–3;
Judges 1:17 checked at bible-api.com (`translation=web`).

## Significance

- A name that means loss in Numbers 14 will mean victory by Numbers 21.
- Numbers 14:45 reads as written from a later standpoint, since it uses a name the book explains only
  later. That is an observation about the text, and the wiki assigns no date from it.
