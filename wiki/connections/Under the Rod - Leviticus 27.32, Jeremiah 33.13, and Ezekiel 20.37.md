# Under the Rod - Leviticus 27.32, Jeremiah 33.13, and Ezekiel 20.37

## Passages involved

- [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]] — Leviticus 27:32: *"whatever passes under the rod, the
  tenth shall be holy to Yahweh."*
- Jeremiah 33:13 — *"the flocks will again pass under the hands of him who counts them."* *(WEB,
  bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- Ezekiel 20:37 — *"I will cause you to pass under the rod, and I will bring you into the bond of the
  covenant."* *(same)*

## Analysis

**Leviticus describes counting.** Animals pass one by one, *taḥat haššābeṭ*, under the rod, and the
tenth is holy. Verse 33 forbids any sorting. It is the one use of *šēbeṭ* in Leviticus (WLC lemma
search).

**Ezekiel takes the same words and turns them on Israel.** *Wəhaʿăbartî ʾetkem taḥat haššābeṭ*: the
preposition and noun match, and the verb is the causative of Leviticus's *pass* (WLC). There Yahweh is
the shepherd and the people are the flock. Ezekiel does not mention a tithe, so it may be drawing on
ordinary shepherding, not on Leviticus. The next verse says *"I will purge out from among you the
rebels"* (20:38; WEB, bible-api.com). Its passing under the rod is at least a sorting. Leviticus's is
expressly not one, and that difference is the most striking thing here.

**Jeremiah shares the image and not the wording.** Flocks pass *ʿal-yədê mōneh*, under the hands of a
counter, with no rod (WLC). It is a promise that the empty land will again have shepherds counting
animals. No tithe is in view.

**Not the rod of discipline.** *Šēbeṭ* is also the noun of [[The Rod of Discipline - Proverbs 13.24 and the Father's Correction]],
but the counting rod in these verses does not strike anyone.

## Significance

A shepherd's routine becomes a picture of God counting his people. The link from Leviticus to
Ezekiel is verbal, and the wiki does not claim dependence. Revisit at Ezekiel and Jeremiah.
