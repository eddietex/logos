# The Hand Held Out - Moses at Rephidim and Joshua at Ai

## Passages involved

- [[The Battle with Amalek]] — Exodus 17:11–12: *"When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed.
  When he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed. But Moses' hands were heavy … Aaron and Hur held up
  his hands … His hands were steady until sunset."* (stored WEB)
- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 14:16, 26: *"stretch out your hand over the sea"* (stored WEB).
- [[The Capture of Ai]] — Joshua 8:18, 26: *"Stretch out the javelin that is in your hand toward
  Ai, for I will give it into your hand"*; *"Joshua didn't draw back his hand, with which he
  stretched out the javelin, until he had utterly destroyed all the inhabitants of Ai."* (stored WEB)

## Analysis

At Rephidim Joshua fought in the valley while Moses held the rod on the hill, and the battle went the
way the hand went. At Ai Joshua holds the outstretched weapon himself, and keeps it out until the end.
The servant who fought under the raised hand now raises it.

The parallel is real but not verbal at every point, and the differences are worth stating:

- **The verbs differ.** Moses *raises* (*yārîm*) and *lets down* (*yānîaḥ*) his hand at Exodus 17:11
  (WLC). Joshua *stretches out* (*nāṭâ*) the *kîḏôn* at Joshua 8:18, 26 (WLC). *Nāṭâ* with the hand
  is the verb of Moses over the sea (Exodus 14:16, 26, WLC) and of the plague signs; Joshua 8 stands
  closer in wording to the sea than to Rephidim.
- **The duration links them.** Rephidim's hands are steady *until sunset* (*ʿaḏ-bōʾ haššāmeš*, WLC
  17:12); Joshua *didn't draw back his hand … until* Ai was destroyed (8:26). Both narratives make the
  holding-out last as long as the battle.
- **The function differs.** At Ai the javelin is first a signal: the ambush rises *as soon as he had
  stretched out his hand* (8:19). Verse 26 then adds what the plan never mentioned, and the holding
  becomes more than a signal. At Rephidim there is no signal to anyone; the hand's effect is
  unexplained.
- **The command differs.** At Ai Yahweh orders the gesture and promises the result in the same
  sentence (8:18). At Rephidim Moses decides it himself (17:9).

Exodus 17:14 told Moses to *rehearse it in the ears of Joshua*. Joshua 8 never cites Rephidim, and
the wiki claims a resemblance the reader is equipped to see, not a citation.

## Significance

Joshua 1:5 promised *As I was with Moses, so I will be with you*, and Joshua 3–4 showed it at the
Jordan, where the waters stood as at the sea. Ai extends the pattern from the crossing to the
battlefield: the outstretched hand of Moses' great acts is now Joshua's, and the man who once fought
below it holds it out. The hand is never treated as magic in either text — at Ai it is ordered and
explained as a signal, at Rephidim it is God's rod — but in both it ties the outcome of human
fighting to something visible that is not fighting.
