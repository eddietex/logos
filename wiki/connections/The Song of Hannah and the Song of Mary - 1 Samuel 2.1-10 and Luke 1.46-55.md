# The Song of Hannah and the Song of Mary - 1 Samuel 2.1-10 and Luke 1.46-55

## Passages involved

- [[The Song of Hannah]] — 1 Samuel 2:1–10 *(stored WEB)*
- [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] — 1 Samuel 1:11 *(stored WEB)*
- Luke 1:46–55 — Mary's song, traditionally the *Magnificat* *(WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet
  ingested)*

## Analysis

Two women sing after a child is given by God, and the two songs run on the same reversals. The wiki has
compared them in English only (WEB); no Greek text was consulted, so nothing here claims shared Greek
wording.

| Theme | Hannah (1 Samuel) | Mary (Luke 1, WEB) |
|---|---|---|
| Opening joy | *My heart exults in Yahweh … I rejoice in your salvation* (2:1) | *My soul magnifies the Lord. My spirit has rejoiced in God my Savior* (46–47) |
| The servant's low state seen | *look at the affliction of your servant* (1:11) | *he has looked at the humble state of his servant* (48) |
| Holiness | *There is no one as holy as Yahweh* (2:2) | *Holy is his name* (49) |
| The proud | *Don't keep talking so exceedingly proudly* (2:3) | *He has scattered the proud in the imagination of their hearts* (51) |
| The mighty and the lowly | *The bows of the mighty men are broken* (2:4); *He brings low, he also lifts up* (2:7) | *He has put down princes from their thrones. And has exalted the lowly* (52) |
| Hunger and fullness | *Those who were full have hired themselves out for bread. Those who were hungry are satisfied* (2:5) | *He has filled the hungry with good things. He has sent the rich away empty* (53) |
| Thrones | the poor made to *inherit the throne of glory* (2:8) | princes put down *from their thrones* (52) |
| The end in view | *his king … his anointed* (2:10) | *Israel, his servant … Abraham and his offspring forever* (54–55) |

**The pattern is the same; the circumstances are opposite.** Hannah was barren and is given a child after
long asking. Mary is a virgin (Luke 1:27, by reference) and is given a child without asking. Hannah's
song comes after she has given the child away (1:28); Mary's comes before her child is born. Elizabeth,
whom Mary is visiting, is the barren woman of Luke's story (Luke 1:7, 36, not yet ingested); see
[[The Barren Matriarchs]].

**Where they differ.** Hannah ends on a king and his anointed. Mary ends on Israel and *Abraham and his
offspring forever*, the promise to the fathers. Hannah's song has death and Sheol (2:6) and the pillars of
the earth (2:8); Mary's has neither. Mary's has *mercy* twice (50, 54); Hannah's has God's *holy ones*,
*ḥăsîḏāyw* (2:9, WLC), from the word for steadfast love.

**Dependence.** That Luke's song is modeled on Hannah's is the usual reading, and the table shows why. The
text of Luke does not cite 1 Samuel, and the wiki records the dependence as a common reading, not as a
claim of the text.

## Significance

- The reversals of 1 Samuel 2 — the proud scattered, the hungry fed, the lowly lifted — become the frame
  for Luke's account of Jesus's birth. Hannah's song introduces the book that brings Israel its anointed
  king; Mary's introduces a gospel whose child is called Christ (Luke 2:11, by reference), the Greek for
  *anointed*.
