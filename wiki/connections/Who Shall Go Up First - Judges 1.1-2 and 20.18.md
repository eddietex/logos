# Who Shall Go Up First - Judges 1.1-2 and 20.18

## Passages involved

- [[Judah Goes Up First]] — **Judges 1:1–2** — *"Who should go up for us first against the
  Canaanites, to fight against them?" Yahweh said, "Judah shall go up. Behold, I have delivered the
  land into his hand."*
- **Judges 20:18** *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)* — *"Who shall go up for us first to battle against
  the children of Benjamin?" Yahweh said, "Judah first."*
- **Judges 20:23, 27–28** *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)* — two further inquiries in the same war,
  the second with Phinehas standing before the ark.

## Analysis

Judges opens and nearly closes with the same question, put to God by all Israel before a war, and
the same answer. The Hebrew question is nearly word for word the same (WLC, OpenScriptures morphhb):

| | Judges 1:1–2 | Judges 20:18 |
|---|---|---|
| asked of | *baYHWH*, *of Yahweh* | *bēʾlōhîm*, *of God* (WEB *asked counsel of God*) |
| where | not stated | Bethel |
| the question | *mî yaʿăleh-lānû … battəḥillāh*, *who will go up for us … first* | *mî yaʿăleh-lānû battəḥillāh*, the same words |
| the enemy | *the Canaanites* | *the children of Benjamin* |
| the answer | *yəhûdāh yaʿăleh*, *Judah shall go up* | *yəhûdāh battəḥillāh*, *Judah first* |
| the promise | *hinnēh nāṯattî ʾeṯ-hāʾāreṣ bəyāḏô*, *I have delivered the land into his hand* | none |

Three things change between the two.

**The enemy.** In chapter 1, Israel fights the peoples of the land. In chapter 20 it fights one of
its own tribes, and verses 23 and 28 call that tribe *Benjamin my brother*. Chapter 1 begins with
Judah calling Simeon *his brother* to fight beside him (1:3); in chapter 20 Judah is sent first
against a brother.

**The promise.** In chapter 1 the answer comes with a promise, and the battle is won in the next
verses. In chapter 20 the answer is two words, with no promise, and Israel loses twenty-two thousand
men that day (20:21, stored WEB). They ask again and are told *Go up against him* (20:23), and lose
again. Only at the third inquiry, with Phinehas at the ark, does Yahweh say *tomorrow I will deliver
him into your hand* (20:28; Hebrew *ʾettənennû bəyāḏeḵā*, the verb *nāṯan* of 1:2). The promise of
1:2 returns only after the losses of chapter 20.

**The name.** The same question is asked *of Yahweh* in 1:1 and *of God* in 20:18, and in both
places *Yahweh* answers. The page records the difference and draws no conclusion from it.

## Significance

The question frames the book. In chapter 1 it asks who will lead Israel against the Canaanites, now
that Joshua is dead. In chapter 20 the same question is asked against Benjamin, and the book's last
war is a war among the tribes. Read together, the two inquiries show how far the book has moved. The
tribe chosen first is the same at both ends. Its enemy is now a tribe of Israel.

The repetition also bears on the book's closing refrain, *In those days there was no king in Israel*
(21:25). The man who will be Israel's first king, Saul, is a Benjamite of Gibeah (1 Samuel 9:1–2;
10:26, WEB checked at bible-api.com: *Saul also went to his house to Gibeah*), and the war of
chapter 20 is fought against Gibeah. That connection is often drawn, and the wiki notes it as a lead
for 1 Samuel rather than a claim of Judges.
