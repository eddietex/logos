## Analysis

### Rest as the setting for a warning

Joshua 23:1 dates the farewell to *when Yahweh had given rest to Israel from their enemies all
around*. The rest here is not the speech's conclusion; it is its setting. The speech warns that
the land can still be lost (13, 15–16). Rest is given, and it is not presented as irreversible.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 23:1–16.

### Rest authorizes return

In Joshua 22:4 the promise becomes a practical decision: the eastern warriors may return
because their brothers now have rest. Verse 5 immediately renews the call to obedience;
rest does not mean covenant obligations have ended. This is military release, not a
seventh-day observance. The remaining-land tension recorded above remains.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:1–9.

### The allotment’s closing verdict

The three-verse conclusion is now ingested. Verse 43 includes actual possession and dwelling, not only distribution; verse 44 attributes victory and rest to Yahweh; verse 45 assesses his good word as wholly fulfilled. Joshua 23:14–16 will repeat that assurance beside a warning of land loss. Thus fulfillment and continuing covenant responsibility coexist, as do the summary and the non-removal notices. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:43–45 and 23:14–16.

### Caleb’s grant at Gilgal

Joshua 14:15 now supplies an ingested rest notice immediately after Caleb anticipates an expulsion (12). Rest from war therefore coexists in the narrative with a remaining local task. This addition concerns the stored WEB’s narrative sequence; the Hebrew of 14:15 is not independently verified here.


**A promise named before the crossing.** Deuteronomy pairs two nouns for what lies across the
Jordan: *you haven't yet come to the rest and to the inheritance* (12:9). *Rest* there is
*mənûḥâ*, from the root *nwḥ*; the next verse defines it as Yahweh giving *rest from all your
enemies around you, so that you dwell in safety* (12:10, stored WEB; *wəhēnîaḥ lākem mikkol-
ʾōyəbêkem missābîb*, MAM via Sefaria). The same formula — rest *from all your enemies all around* —
returns at 25:19 as the moment the command about Amalek takes effect (MAM via Sefaria). Rest in
Deuteronomy is not idleness but security from attack, and it is the condition for central worship
(12:10–11) and for the Amalek command alike.

**The eastern tribes have it first.** In Deuteronomy 3:20 and Joshua 1:13–15 the two and a half
tribes are already given rest east of the Jordan, and their service lasts *until Yahweh has given
your brothers rest, as he has given you* (Joshua 1:15; the clause matches Deuteronomy 3:20, MAM via
Sefaria). Joshua 1:13 opens with it — *Yahweh your God gives you rest* — before it mentions the
land. See [[The Two and a Half Tribes - Numbers 32, Deuteronomy 3, and Joshua 1, 4, 22]].

| Stage | Text | Wording (stored WEB; root checked in MAM via Sefaria) |
|---|---|---|
| Not yet | Deuteronomy 12:9–10 | *you haven't yet come to the rest*; *he gives you rest from all your enemies* (*nwḥ*) |
| Conditional future | Deuteronomy 25:19 | *when Yahweh your God has given you rest from all your enemies all around* (*nwḥ*) |
| Given to some | Joshua 1:13–15 | *gives you rest*; *until Yahweh has given your brothers rest, as he has given you* (*nwḥ*) |
| Reported | Joshua 21:44 | *Yahweh gave them rest all around, according to all that he swore to their fathers* (*nwḥ*) |
| Discharge | Joshua 22:4 | *Now Yahweh your God has given rest to your brothers, as he spoke to them* (*nwḥ*) |
| Retrospect | Joshua 23:1 | *when Yahweh had given rest to Israel from their enemies all around* (*nwḥ*) |

**A second word, kept separate.** Joshua 11:23 and 14:15 say *the land had rest from war*. The
verb at 11:23 is *šāqəṭâ*, *was quiet* (MAM via Sefaria), not *nwḥ*; 14:15 was not checked in the
Hebrew in this pass. English *rest* covers both, and the wiki does not merge them.

**Fulfilled, and not.** Joshua 21:43–45 is the strongest fulfilment statement in the book: rest
all around, no enemy standing, *Nothing failed of any good thing which Yahweh had spoken* (stored
WEB). Joshua 13:1 has already said that *there remains yet very much land to be possessed*, and
the allotment chapters record peoples Israel *didn't drive out* (13:13, 16:10) or *couldn't*
(17:12, the Manassites). Both claims stand in the same book;
the wiki keeps them together rather than harmonising them.

**Hebrews reads the rest as unfinished.** Hebrews 4 quotes Psalm 95:11, *"They won't enter into
my rest"*, and argues from the psalm's *today* that the rest was not exhausted by the entry: *"For
if Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward of another day. There remains
therefore a Sabbath rest for the people of God"* (4:8–9, WEB via bible-api.com). The name in 4:8
is *Iēsous*; see [[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]]. Hebrews does not deny Joshua 21:44; it argues
that a rest spoken of again later must have been more than what the land supplied. Whether the
Greek of Hebrews 4 uses the vocabulary of the Septuagint of Joshua 21–23 was not checked in this
pass, and the wiki makes no claim about it.

## Significance

The thread runs from a promise (Deuteronomy) through a partial possession (two and a half tribes,
Joshua 1) to a reported fulfilment (Joshua 21–23) and on to a reading that leaves it open
(Hebrews 4). The eastern tribes are the hinge: the only Israelites in the Torah and Joshua who are
said to have the rest already, and who are required to give it up for a time for their brothers'
sake. See [[Land and Sojourning]], [[War]], [[Sabbath]], and [[Brotherhood]].
