# Seeking in the Streets and Squares - Song of Solomon 3.1-4 and Proverbs 1, 7, and 8

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 3:1–4** — [[Seeking Him by Night]]: *"in the streets and in the squares I will
  seek him whom my soul loves. I sought him, but I didn't find him"* (3:2); then *"I found him"*
  (3:4).
- **Proverbs 7:8–15** — [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]: the young man *"passing through the
  street near her corner"* (7:8); the woman *"in the streets, now in the squares"* (7:12), who says
  *"I came out to meet you, to diligently seek your face, and I have found you"* (7:15).
- **Proverbs 1:20, 28** — [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]]: Wisdom *"utters her voice in the public
  squares"* (1:20); those who refused her *"will seek me diligently, but they will not find me"*
  (1:28).
- **Proverbs 8:17** — [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]]: *"Those who seek me diligently will find me."*
- Compare **Ecclesiastes 12:5** — [[Remember Your Creator]]: *"the mourners go about the streets"*.

Hebrew of all read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

## Analysis

**The same public places.** The woman of the Song searches *baššǝwāqîm ûḇārǝḥōḇôt*, *in the streets
and in the squares* (Song 3:2). Proverbs puts two women in those places. The forbidden woman is
*baḥûṣ … bārǝḥōḇôt*, *in the streets … in the squares* (Proverbs 7:12), and Wisdom gives her voice
*bārǝḥōḇôt*, *in the squares* (Proverbs 1:20). The *street* word of the Song, *šûq*, is not the one
in Proverbs 7:12, which is *ḥûṣ*, *outside*; but *šûq* is where the young man walks to the forbidden
woman's house four verses earlier (*baššûq*, Proverbs 7:8). So the Song and Proverbs 7 share both
nouns, in different verses. *Šûq* is rare. The standard count, which this page has not verified by
a full concordance search, gives four occurrences: Proverbs 7:8, Ecclesiastes 12:4, 12:5, and Song
3:2.

**Seeking and finding, with different verbs.** Every text here is about a search that succeeds or
fails, but the Song and Proverbs use different Hebrew for *seek*. The Song uses *b-q-š*:
*biqqaštîw*, *ʾăḇaqšâ* (3:1–2). Proverbs uses *š-ḥ-r*, *to seek early* or *diligently*: the
forbidden woman came *lǝšaḥēr pānêḵā*, *to seek your face* (7:15); the scorners *yǝšaḥărunǝnî*,
*will seek me* (1:28); and *mǝšaḥăray*, *those who seek me* (8:17). The verb of *finding*, *m-ṣ-ʾ*,
is shared by all of them: *mǝṣāʾtîw* (Song 3:1, 2, 4), *wāʾemṣāʾekkā*, *and I have found you*
(Proverbs 7:15), *wǝlōʾ yimṣāʾunǝnî*, *and they will not find me* (1:28), *yimṣāʾunǝnî*, *will find
me* (8:17).

**Three women in the streets.** Set side by side:

| | Seeker | Sought | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proverbs 7:12–15 | the forbidden woman | the young man | *"I have found you"*; it leads to death (7:27) |
| Proverbs 1:20–28; 8:17 | the simple and the scorners; those who love her | Wisdom | the scorners do not find her; those who seek her find her |
| Song 3:1–4 | the woman | *him whom my soul loves* | not found, then found and held |

In Proverbs a woman who goes out into the streets and squares to find a man is the picture of
danger: *"Her feet don't stay in her house"* (7:11). In the Song a woman does the same thing, at
night, and the poem neither blames her nor warns. She brings the man she finds to her mother's
house; the forbidden woman of Proverbs 7 brings hers to a house whose husband is away (7:19). Both
houses have a *room*, *ḥeder*. The Song's is *ḥeder hôrātî*, *the room of her who conceived me*
(3:4). The forbidden woman's house goes down *ʾel-ḥaḏrê-māweṯ*, *"to the rooms of death"* (7:27).

**What this does and does not show.** The resemblance is in place and action. Nothing in the Song
quotes Proverbs, and the wiki does not claim either text answers the other. The page sets the scenes
side by side because both books are associated with [[Solomon]] by their headings, both use the
city's public spaces as the setting for a woman's seeking, and their judgments of that seeking
differ completely. The contrast between the two women of Proverbs is laid out on
[[Two Voices in the Street - The Strange Woman and Wisdom in Proverbs 7-9]]; this page adds the
Song's woman beside them.

## Significance

The comparison shows the Song's freedom with a scene that Proverbs treats only as a warning. It also
shows the wisdom texts' own contrast: in Proverbs 1 and 8, Wisdom is a woman in the streets who
is sought, and in Proverbs 7 the forbidden woman is a woman in the streets who seeks. The Song's
woman is a seeker in the same streets, and her search is for love, not for prey. See [[Love]],
[[Wisdom]], [[Sexual Sin]], and [[City and Civilization]].

Ecclesiastes 12:5 is included for the word only. There the mourners *go about the streets*,
*wǝsāḇǝḇû baššûq*, with the same verb and noun the Song uses of the woman (*ʾăsôḇǝḇâ … baššǝwāqîm*,
3:2). The scenes have nothing else in common.
