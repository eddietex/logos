# An Evil Report - Dibbah in Genesis 37, Numbers 13, and Ezekiel 36

## Passages involved

- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:2; *Joseph brought an evil report of them to their
  father*
- [[The Twelve Spies]] — Numbers 13:32
- [[The People Refuse the Land]] — Numbers 14:36–37; the men who brought it die, and verse 37 adds *rāʿâ*
- Psalm 31:13 (31:14 Hebrew) *(WLC, not yet ingested)*
- Proverbs 10:18 — *He who utters a slander is a fool* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Proverbs 25:10 — *your bad reputation never depart* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Jeremiah 20:10 — *I have heard the defaming of many* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Ezekiel 36:3 — *the evil report of the people* *(WEB and WLC checked, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

***Dibbâ* occurs in nine verses by the tags**, listed above. WEB gives *evil report*, *slander*,
*bad reputation*, and *defaming*. It is a report of a damaging kind, and whether it is true depends
on the case.

**Joseph and the spies.** Genesis 37:2 has *wayyābēʾ yôsēp ʾet-dibbātām
rāʿâ*. Numbers 13:32 has *wayyôṣîʾû dibbat hāʾāreṣ*, and 14:37 has *môṣīʾê dibbat-hāʾāreṣ rāʿâ*. The
adjective *rāʿâ* is on the Genesis and Numbers 14 phrases, and it is needed since the noun alone may
not decide. In Numbers 13 the word itself is enough. Note that Joseph brings it to a father about
brothers, and the spies bring it to the people about a land. The verse does not say Joseph's report
was false, and Numbers 13 does not say every clause was false.

**Proverbs 10:18 sets the moral.** The man who *utters a slander*, *môṣîʾ dibbâ*, is a fool, with
the same verb as Numbers 13:32. It is the closest wording to the spies by the tags.

**Ezekiel 36:3 applies it to the land again.** See
[[A Land That Eats Up Its Inhabitants - Numbers 13.32, Leviticus 18 and 26, and Ezekiel 36]].

## Significance

- The chapter marks the turn by changing a noun: *dābār* (13:26) to *dibbâ* (13:32).
- The first man in the Bible to carry a *dibbâ* is a favored son, and the next carriers are the
  spies. The word judges the act of carrying, whatever the facts were.
