# Two Priests Stop the Plague - Aaron and Phinehas

## Passages involved

- [[Aaron Stands Between the Dead and the Living]]
- [[Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas]]

## Analysis

Numbers 16:41–50 describes Aaron running with incense among the congregation; Numbers 25:7–13
describes his grandson killing Zimri and Cozbi. Both accounts say the plague stops and call the
intervention atonement. Their means differ: incense in the first, a spear in the second. Neither
reports the priest dying in place of Israel, and neither should be recast as an animal sacrifice.
Aaron preserves his accusers; Phinehas kills the pair while preserving the wider congregation. The
repeated pattern establishes priestly mediation without making every act of atonement identical.

## Significance

The comparison preserves the distinct claims of each passage while tracing judgment, intervention,
and the survival of the congregation.
