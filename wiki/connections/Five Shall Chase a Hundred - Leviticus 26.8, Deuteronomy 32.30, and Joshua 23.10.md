# Five Shall Chase a Hundred - Leviticus 26.8, Deuteronomy 32.30, and Joshua 23.10

## Passages involved

- [[Blessings for Obedience]] — Leviticus 26:7–8; *"Five of you shall chase a hundred, and a hundred
  of you shall chase ten thousand"*.
- **Leviticus 26:17, 36–37** — the reversal in the same chapter: *"you will flee when no one pursues
  you"*; *"when no one pursues"* (stored WEB; pericope not yet ingested).
- **Deuteronomy 32:30** — *"How could one chase a thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight, unless
  their Rock had sold them, and Yahweh had delivered them up?"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Joshua 23:10** — *"One man of you shall chase a thousand; for it is Yahweh your God who fights for
  you, as he spoke to you."* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The same picture, seen from opposite ends.** All three use *rādap*, *chase*, with a tiny number
against a large one (MAM). Leviticus promises that Israel's few will chase many. Joshua restates it
with the reason attached: Yahweh fights for you. **Deuteronomy 32:30 turns the picture
round.** The one chasing a thousand is now Israel's enemy, and the thousand are Israel. The only
explanation offered is that *their Rock had sold them*. The odds were never about the numbers on
the field, in either direction.

**The reversal is already in Leviticus.** [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] has Israel fleeing with no pursuer at all
(26:17, 36–37). The lesson of 26:8 is finished by that point: without Yahweh, no enemy need even be
present. See [[Bold as a Lion - Proverbs 28.1, Leviticus 26.36, and Job 3.25]].

**The figures are not a formula.** Leviticus's ratio shifts from one to twenty to one to a hundred;
Deuteronomy has one to a thousand and two to ten thousand; Joshua has one to a thousand. The texts share
a pattern of disproportion, not a stated exchange rate. Deuteronomy's second verb is *yānîsû*, *put to
flight*, and not *rādap* again.

## Significance

- It keeps [[War]] from reading as an achievement of Israel's. Leviticus 26:8 credits no skill and
  Joshua 23:10 says plainly who does the fighting.
- It shows a covenant blessing being used as a charge. Deuteronomy's question has force only if the
  promise is remembered.
- It links the conditional structure of [[Blessings for Obedience]] with narrative history still ahead.
