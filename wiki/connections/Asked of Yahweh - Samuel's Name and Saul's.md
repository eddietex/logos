# Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's

## Passages involved

- [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] — 1 Samuel 1:17, 20, 27–28: *may the God of Israel grant your petition
  that you have asked of him* (17); *she named him Samuel, saying, "Because I have asked him of Yahweh"*
  (20); *Yahweh has given me my petition which I asked of him. Therefore I have also given him to Yahweh. As
  long as he lives he is given to Yahweh* (27–28). Stored WEB.
- [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] — 1 Samuel 2:20 — Eli blesses Elkanah and Hannah *for the petition which was asked of
  Yahweh*. Stored WEB.
- [[Saul Comes to the Seer]] — 1 Samuel 9:2 — *He had a son, whose name was Saul*. Stored WEB.
- [[Samuel Anoints Saul]] — 1 Samuel 10:4 — *They will greet you*, literally *ask you of peace*. Stored WEB.
- [[Israel Asks for a King]] — 1 Samuel 8:10 — *Samuel told all Yahweh's words to the people who asked him for a king*. Stored
  WEB.
- [[The Song of Hannah]] — 1 Samuel 2:10, *his king … his anointed*, sung before any king is asked for.
  Stored WEB.

## Analysis

**The verb of chapter 1.** *Šāʾal*, *ask*, and its noun *šəʾēlâ*, *petition*, carry Hannah's story (WLC):

| Verse | Hebrew (WLC) | WEB |
|---|---|---|
| 1:17 | *šēlāṯēḵ ʾăšer šāʾalt* | *your petition that you have asked* |
| 1:20 | *kî mēYHWH šəʾiltîw* | *Because I have asked him of Yahweh* |
| 1:27 | *šəʾēlāṯî ʾăšer šāʾaltî* | *my petition which I asked* |
| 1:28 | *hišʾiltihû* | *I have also given him* |
| 1:28 | *hûʾ šāʾûl laYHWH* | *he is given to Yahweh* |
| 2:20 | *haššəʾēlâ ʾăšer šāʾal* | *the petition which was asked* |

In 1:28 the verb turns: the causative *hišʾiltihû*, *I have caused him to be asked*, means *lent* or
*granted*, and the passive participle *šāʾûl* means *asked*, here *given*. The child who was asked for is
the child given back.

In 2:20 the WEB's passive *was asked* renders a form the WLC parses as active, third person masculine
singular, *šāʾal*, *he asked* (morph *Vqp3ms*). The verse does not name the subject; Elkanah, an
impersonal *one*, and an original *she asked* have all been proposed. The wiki reports the form and does
not choose. See {P}.

**The name does not fit the explanation.** *Šəmûʾēl* (1:20, WLC) does not derive from *šāʾal* by any
obvious rule; the name is more often glossed *name of God* or *heard of God* (conventional glosses, not
verified here). Biblical name explanations are often sound-plays rather than etymologies — Moses, *because
I drew him out of the water* (Exodus 2:10), is another — so the mismatch is not by itself a problem.

**The word that does fit is Saul's name.** The participle *šāʾûl* in 1:28 is written שאול, exactly the
consonants of *Šāʾûl*, Saul (9:2, WLC; Strong's numbers 7592 and 7586, distinguished only by the
lexicon). Some interpreters have therefore proposed that the birth story was first told of Saul and later
attached to Samuel; others read the wordplay as deliberate foreshadowing, the narrator pointing past
Samuel to the king Israel will *ask for* (1 Samuel 8:10; 12:13, the latter not yet ingested). Both proposals are
reported, not verified, and the wiki takes neither as fact. What the text itself shows is narrower: the
book's opening chapter says *asked* five times and ends on the participle that is the next main
character's name.

## Significance

The book's first child is *asked of Yahweh* and given back to him; its first king will be asked of Samuel
by the people, against Samuel's will (8:6). The same verb serves a mother's prayer and
a nation's demand.

**What 1 Samuel 8 shows, now ingested.** In the request chapter itself *šāʾal* occurs once: *the people who
asked him for a king*, *hāʿām haššōʾălîm mēʾittô meleḵ* (8:10, WLC). The elders' own words use other verbs,
*śîmâ-lānû*, *set for us*, and *tənâ-lānû*, *give us* (8:5–6, WLC), and Samuel's displeasure is tied to the
second. So the narrative does not put the two askings side by side in so many words; the narrator supplies
the verb once, in his own voice. The pairing becomes explicit only in the farewell, where *šāʾal* names the
request three times: *the king whom you have chosen, and whom you have asked for* (12:13); *your wickedness is
great … in asking for a king* (12:17); *we have added to all our sins this evil, to ask for a king* (12:19)
(stored WEB; WLC; not yet ingested). In the WLC of 1 Samuel the verb's other uses before chapter 8 are all in
the Hannah story (1:17, 20, 27, 28; 2:20). See [[Israel Asks for a King]].

Between the two askings stands Hannah's song, which names *his king* and *his anointed* (2:10) before the
people ask for anyone. The king there is given by Yahweh, not demanded. See [[The Song of Hannah]] and
[[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].

**What 1 Samuel 9 shows, now ingested.** The name *šāʾûl* first appears at 9:2, *ûšəmô šāʾûl* (WLC), with no
gloss: the narrator, who explained Samuel's name at 1:20, does not explain Saul's or play on it, and *šāʾal* does not
occur in chapter 9 at all (WLC). The asking is left to the reader to hear. What chapter 9 adds instead is the other
side of the request: Yahweh *sends* the man (9:16). See [[Saul Comes to the Seer]].

### 1 Samuel 10:4 — asked of peace

The first *šāʾal* after Saul is named comes in [[Samuel Anoints Saul]]. The three pilgrims at the oak of Tabor *will
greet you*, *wəšāʾălû ləḵā ləšālôm* (10:4, WLC), literally *they will ask you of peace*. It is the ordinary idiom of
greeting, and the narrative makes nothing of it. The wiki notes that the root occurs and claims no wordplay. *Šāʾal*
does not occur in 10:14–16, where the uncle *said … Where did you go?* and *Please tell me* (WLC). Sources: stored
WEB 1 Samuel 10:4, 14–16; Hebrew WLC (`1Sam.xml`), 2026-11-17.
