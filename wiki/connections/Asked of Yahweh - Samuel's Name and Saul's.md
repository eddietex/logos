# Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's

## Passages involved

- [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] — 1 Samuel 1:17, 20, 27–28: *may the God of Israel grant your petition
  that you have asked of him* (17); *she named him Samuel, saying, "Because I have asked him of Yahweh"*
  (20); *Yahweh has given me my petition which I asked of him. Therefore I have also given him to Yahweh. As
  long as he lives he is given to Yahweh* (27–28). Stored WEB.
- 1 Samuel 2:20 — Eli blesses Elkanah and Hannah *for the petition which was asked of Yahweh*. Stored WEB;
  not yet ingested.
- 1 Samuel 9:2 — *He had a son, whose name was Saul*. Stored WEB; not yet ingested.
- [[The Song of Hannah]] — 1 Samuel 2:10, *his king … his anointed*, sung before any king is asked for.
  Stored WEB.

## Analysis

**The verb of chapter 1.** *Šāʾal*, *ask*, and its noun *šəʾēlâ*, *petition*, carry Hannah's story (WLC):

| Verse | Hebrew (WLC) | WEB |
|---|---|---|
| 1:17 | *šēlāṯēḵ ʾăšer šāʾalt* | *your petition that you have asked* |
| 1:20 | *kî mēYHWH šəʾiltîw* | *Because I have asked him of Yahweh* |
| 1:27 | *šəʾēlāṯî ʾăšer šāʾaltî* | *my petition which I asked* |
| 1:28 | *hišʾiltihû* | *I have also given him* |
| 1:28 | *hûʾ šāʾûl laYHWH* | *he is given to Yahweh* |
| 2:20 | *haššəʾēlâ ʾăšer šāʾal* | *the petition which was asked* |

In 1:28 the verb turns: the causative *hišʾiltihû*, *I have caused him to be asked*, means *lent* or
*granted*, and the passive participle *šāʾûl* means *asked*, here *given*. The child who was asked for is
the child given back.

**The name does not fit the explanation.** *Šəmûʾēl* (1:20, WLC) does not derive from *šāʾal* by any
obvious rule; the name is more often glossed *name of God* or *heard of God* (conventional glosses, not
verified here). Biblical name explanations are often sound-plays rather than etymologies — Moses, *because
I drew him out of the water* (Exodus 2:10), is another — so the mismatch is not by itself a problem.

**The word that does fit is Saul's name.** The participle *šāʾûl* in 1:28 is written שאול, exactly the
consonants of *Šāʾûl*, Saul (9:2, WLC; Strong's numbers 7592 and 7586, distinguished only by the
lexicon). Some interpreters have therefore proposed that the birth story was first told of Saul and later
attached to Samuel; others read the wordplay as deliberate foreshadowing, the narrator pointing past
Samuel to the king Israel will *ask for* (1 Samuel 8:10; 12:13, not yet ingested). Both proposals are
reported, not verified, and the wiki takes neither as fact. What the text itself shows is narrower: the
book's opening chapter says *asked* five times and ends on the participle that is the next main
character's name.

## Significance

The book's first child is *asked of Yahweh* and given back to him; its first king will be asked of Samuel
by the people, against Samuel's will (8:6, not yet ingested). The same verb serves a mother's prayer and
a nation's demand. When the people's request is ingested, this page should record whether the narrative
itself sets the two askings side by side.

Between the two askings stands Hannah's song, which names *his king* and *his anointed* (2:10) before the
people ask for anyone. The king there is given by Yahweh, not demanded. See [[The Song of Hannah]] and
[[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].
