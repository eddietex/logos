# Willing Hearts - The Sanctuary, the Temple, and the Collection

## Passages involved

- [[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]] — Exodus 35:30–36:7; willing gifts reach a reported surplus
  and are stopped.

- [[The Offering for the Sanctuary]] — Exodus 25:1–9; the invitation to willing contributors.
- [[The Freewill Offering]] — Exodus 35:4–29; the willing people actually bring materials and work.
- 1 Chronicles 29:1–17 — gifts for the temple and David's prayer; verified WEB, not yet ingested.
- 2 Corinthians 9:1–15 — the collection for the saints; verified WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Exodus moves from the request to receive gifts from willing hearts (25:2) to the response of
men and women whose hearts move them to bring (35:21–29). The work is commanded, but this
collection sets no fixed personal payment. Skill contributes alongside possessions: women spin,
and rulers bring stones, spice, and oil. The equal half-shekel requirement of 30:11–16 belongs
to a distinct contribution and must not be substituted for this scene.

1 Chronicles 29 gathers metal, timber, and stones for God's house (29:1–8). Leaders give willingly;
the people and David rejoice (29:9). David then says the wealth and ability to give have come
from God, and describes the people's willing gifts with joy (29:11–17). This makes explicit a
theology of received goods given back which Exodus 35 does not itself put into the donors' mouths.
The temple setting and royal initiative differ from the wilderness congregation and its spinners.

Paul's collection concerns service to the saints and their needs, not construction of a sacred
building (2 Corinthians 9:1, 12). He arranges advance preparation for a promised gift (9:3–5),
then calls for giving determined in the heart rather than grudgingly or under compulsion (9:7).
Its provision produces thanksgiving and glory to God (9:11–13). Willingness therefore coexists
with organized, accountable giving; it does not mean there is no public appeal or preparation.

These are parallels in the disposition and purpose of giving. This page does not assert that
Paul quotes Exodus 35, that identical Hebrew or Greek words underlie the English parallels, or
that a temple construction collection and relief for people are interchangeable.

### The collection has an endpoint — Exodus 36:3–7

[[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]] adds a practical limit to the willingness already recorded
in chapter 35. The offerings keep arriving after the workers receive the accumulated gifts
(36:3). The makers themselves report the excess; Moses stops the collection, and the narrator
confirms their assessment (36:4–7). Organized giving therefore includes recognizing when a
particular task is supplied. This scene neither establishes an endless building appeal nor
commands every later relief collection to follow the same procedure. The temple and Pauline
comparisons above remain parallels, not identified quotations of this surplus account.

## Significance

[[Sacrifice and Offering]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]] can include a freely directed heart,
shared preparation, and a purpose beyond the donor. The comparison preserves the different
beneficiaries and settings instead of making every gift a building contribution.
