# May Yahweh Establish His Word - Numbers 30 and 1 Samuel 1

## Passages involved

- [[Vows of Men and Women]] — Numbers 30:10–14, a wife's vow made in her husband's house:
  *"Every vow, and every binding oath to afflict the soul, her husband may establish it, or her husband
  may make it void"* (13). *(stored WEB)*
- **1 Samuel 1:11** — *"She vowed a vow, and said, “Yahweh of Armies, if you will indeed look at the
  affliction of your servant, and remember me, and not forget your servant, but will give to your
  servant a boy, then I will give him to Yahweh all the days of his life, and no razor shall come on
  his head.”"* *(WEB verified at bible-api.com, 2026-11-05; not yet ingested)*
- **1 Samuel 1:21–23** — Elkanah goes up *"to offer to Yahweh the yearly sacrifice, and his vow"*;
  Hannah stays until the child is weaned; Elkanah says, *"Do what seems good to you. Wait until you
  have weaned him; only may Yahweh establish his word."* *(same source)*

## Analysis

**A wife's vow, on the terms Numbers 30 sets.** Hannah is married and vows at Shiloh (1 Samuel 1:9–11):
the case of Numbers 30:10, a vow made *in her husband's house*. Under that law Elkanah could confirm it
by silence or void it on the day he heard (30:11–12). 1 Samuel does not say when or whether he heard the
vow, and it never cites Numbers 30.

**The same verb.** Elkanah's words are *ʾak yāqēm YHWH ʾet-dəbārô*, *only may Yahweh establish his word*
(1:23, MAM via Sefaria). *Yāqēm* is the causative of *qûm*, the verb Numbers 30:13–14 uses for a husband
establishing his wife's vow (*yəqîmennû*, *hēqîm*, MAM), and the vows that are not voided *stand*
(*yāqûm*). In Numbers the husband establishes the vow; in 1 Samuel the husband asks Yahweh to establish
*his word*. The verbal overlap is real; a deliberate allusion is not claimed.

**Whose word.** *His word* in 1:23 has no clear antecedent: no word of Yahweh to Hannah is narrated, only
Eli's *may the God of Israel grant your petition* (1:17, not quoted here). The phrase may mean Yahweh's
fulfilling of what was asked, or the keeping of the vow. The wiki leaves the reference open.

**The husband's own vow.** Elkanah goes up with *his vow* (1:21). The household has two vows in it, his and
hers. Numbers 30:2 governs his: *he shall not break his word*. Numbers 30 gives him power over hers.

**What Elkanah does not do.** He does not void the vow, and his *Do what seems good to you* gives Hannah the
timing of its payment. Read beside Numbers 30, his words have the effect of a husband's confirmation; that is
a reading of the two texts together, not something 1 Samuel states.

## Significance

Numbers 30 is a law of household authority over a woman's word to God. 1 Samuel 1 is the fullest narrative
of a wife's vow, and the husband in it uses the law's verb of confirming, applied to Yahweh. See
[[Oaths and Vows]], [[Marriage]], [[A Person Vowed to Yahweh - Leviticus 27.1-8, Judges 11, and 1 Samuel 1]],
and [[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]].
