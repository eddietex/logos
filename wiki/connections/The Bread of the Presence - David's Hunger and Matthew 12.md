## Analysis

**Exodus names the bread and says almost nothing else.** ***Leḥem pānîm ləpānay tāmîd*** — *bread of
face, before my face, always* (25:30; MAM). The bread is named for where it is: in God's presence. It is
to be there *tāmîd*, *continually*. Exodus gives no number, no recipe, and no rule for who eats it.

**Leviticus supplies the rite.** Twelve cakes in two rows on *"the pure gold table"* (*haššulḥān
haṭṭāhōr*, Leviticus 24:6; MAM), with frankincense *"for a memorial"*. They are replaced every Sabbath,
called *"an everlasting covenant"*, and eaten by the priests *"in a holy place"* because they are *"most
holy"* (24:7–9). The number twelve is usually connected with the twelve tribes; Leviticus does not say
so.

**David eats it.** Fleeing Saul, David asks the priest at Nob for bread. The priest has *"no common
bread, but there is holy bread"*, and gives it on condition that the young men have kept themselves
from women (1 Samuel 21:4–5). The Hebrew calls it ***leḥem happānîm*** (21:7 MAM = 21:6 WEB), the
Exodus name. It is *"taken from before Yahweh, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken away"* —
so the bread David eats is the week's old bread, just replaced. **The law of Leviticus is bent, not
broken in secret**: a priest decides, and the narrator does not condemn him.

**Jesus cites it.** When the Pharisees object to his disciples picking grain on the Sabbath, Jesus
answers with David: he *"ate the show bread, which was not lawful for him to eat … but only for the
priests"* (Matthew 12:4). The Greek is ***tous artous tēs protheseōs*** (SBLGNT), *the loaves of the
setting-out*; Hebrews 9:2 has *hē prothesis tōn artōn*. Matthew adds two more arguments: priests
*"profane the Sabbath, and are guiltless"*, and *"one greater than the temple is here"* (12:5–6). Then
it quotes Hosea: *"I desire mercy, and not sacrifice"* (12:7).

**A known difficulty.** Mark's version says David entered *"at the time of Abiathar the high priest"*
(Mark 2:26; *epi Abiathar archiereōs*, SBLGNT). 1 Samuel 21:1 names the priest at Nob as *Ahimelech*.
Abiathar is Ahimelech's son, who escapes the massacre at Nob in the next chapter *(1 Samuel 22:20; not
fetched in this pass)*. Readers explain the difference in several ways: *in the passage about
Abiathar*, a loose dating by the better-known name, or an error. Matthew and Luke do not have the
phrase. The wiki records the difficulty and does not resolve it.

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The table and its gold vessels are made in Exodus 37:10–16, following 25:23–29. The
making account does not repeat 25:30's bread direction or report placing bread on the table.
That action is narrated in 40:23. The furnishing is therefore prepared for the bread service
before that service begins. This stage adds no rule about the number, replacement, or eating
of loaves, and does not settle the later dispute about authorized eating.

## Significance

- **Holy bread in human hands.** The bread is the one thing on the table that is eaten, and the one thing
  in the holy place that leaves it. Its first recorded eater outside the priesthood is a hungry fugitive.
- **It becomes a precedent.** Jesus uses it to argue that need, and the presence of *"one greater than
  the temple"*, can take precedence over a holy rule. Whether that applies to the Sabbath dispute by
  analogy or by a stronger claim is the question Matthew 12 leaves the reader with.
- **It joins Exodus 25 to the Sabbath.** The bread is renewed every Sabbath (Leviticus 24:8), and the
  Gospel dispute about it is a Sabbath dispute. See [[Sabbath]].
