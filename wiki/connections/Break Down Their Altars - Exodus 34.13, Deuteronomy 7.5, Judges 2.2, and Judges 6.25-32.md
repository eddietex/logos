# Break Down Their Altars - Exodus 34.13, Deuteronomy 7.5, Judges 2.2, and Judges 6.25-32

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 34:13** — *"but you shall break down their altars, and dash in pieces their pillars, and you
  shall cut down their Asherah poles"* — the covenant renewed after the golden calf;
  [[The Covenant Renewed]].
- **Deuteronomy 7:5** — *"You shall break down their altars, dash their pillars in pieces, cut down
  their Asherah poles, and burn their engraved images with fire."* — [[A People Chosen in Love]].
- **Deuteronomy 12:3** — *"You shall break down their altars, and dash their pillars in pieces, and
  burn their Asherah poles with fire."* — [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]].
- **Judges 2:2** — *"You shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land. You shall break down
  their altars.' But you have not listened to my voice."* — [[The Angel at Bochim]].
- **Judges 6:25, 28** — *"throw down the altar of Baal that your father has, and cut down the Asherah
  that is by it"*; *"behold, the altar of Baal was broken down, and the Asherah was cut down"* —
  [[Gideon Throws Down Baal's Altar]].

All quotations from stored WEB.

## Analysis

**One command, given three times and quoted once.** The law gives the order three times, and each time
the altars belong to the nations: *their altars*. The angel at Bochim quotes it as something Israel
had not done (2:2). In the WLC the verb is the same throughout: *nātaṣ*, *break down*, in the same form,
*tittōṣûn*, in Exodus 34:13, Deuteronomy 7:5, and Judges 2:2, and as *wənittaṣtem* in Deuteronomy 12:3.

| Text | Altars | Asherah | Who is told |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exodus 34:13 | *nātaṣ* | *kārat*, cut down | Israel, plural |
| Deuteronomy 7:5 | *nātaṣ* | *gādaʿ*, hew down | Israel, plural |
| Deuteronomy 12:3 | *nātaṣ* | *śārap*, burn | Israel, plural |
| Judges 2:2 | *nātaṣ* | — | Israel, plural, accused |
| Judges 6:25–32 | *hāras* (command), *nātaṣ* (report) | *kārat* | Gideon, singular |

(Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex, OpenScriptures morphhb.)

**Judges 6 is the first time anyone in Judges obeys it.** The altar is no longer the nations'. It is
*the altar of Baal that your father has* (6:25), in an Israelite town, kept by an Israelite family and
defended by its neighbors. The command was addressed to all Israel in the plural; now it is given to one
man, in the singular, and he carries it out at night with ten servants. In the report the narrator uses
*nātaṣ*, the law's verb, though Yahweh's command used *hāras*. With Exodus 34:13 Judges 6 shares the
verb for the Asherah, *kārat*.

**The new altar is built where the old one stood.** Deuteronomy 12:3–5 follows the destruction with
the place Yahweh will choose. Judges 6:26 follows it with an altar *to Yahweh your God on the top of this
stronghold*, at once, burning the Asherah's wood. The text does not discuss how this altar relates to
the law of the one place, and neither did the angel's altar on the rock (6:20–21) or Gideon's *Yahweh is
Peace* (6:24). See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].

**The verb comes back.** In the WLC of Judges, *nātaṣ* is used again when Gideon breaks down the tower
of Penuel (8:9, 17) and when Abimelech breaks down Shechem (9:45). Those pericopes are not yet ingested.

## Significance

- **It links the command to the first act of obedience in Judges.** Bochim said Israel had not broken
  down the altars. Gideon does it, and the altar he breaks belongs to his own father.
- **It shows how far the apostasy has gone.** The law aimed the command at the nations' shrines. By
  Gideon's day the shrine is Israelite, and the town wants the man who destroyed it put to death. See
  [[Bring Him Out - Genesis 19.5, Deuteronomy 17.5, Judges 6.30, and Judges 19.22]].
- **It frames Gideon's later failure.** The man who broke down Ophrah's Baal altar later sets up an ephod
  in Ophrah, and *all Israel played the prostitute with it there* (8:27, stored WEB).
