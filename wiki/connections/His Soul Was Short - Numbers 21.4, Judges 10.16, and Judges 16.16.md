# His Soul Was Short - Numbers 21.4, Judges 10.16, and Judges 16.16

## Passages involved
- [[The Brass Serpent]] — Numbers 21:4. *The soul of the people was very discouraged because of the
  journey* (stored WEB). Hebrew *wattiqṣar nepeš-hāʿām baddārek* (WLC).
- [[Israel's Repentance Under Ammon]] — Judges 10:16. *His soul was grieved for the misery of Israel*
  (stored WEB). Hebrew *wattiqṣar napšô baʿămal yiśrāʾēl* (WLC).
- [[Samson and Delilah]] — **Judges 16:16** — *When she pressed him daily with her words, and urged him, his soul was troubled
  to death* (stored WEB). Hebrew
  *wattiqṣar napšô lāmût* (WLC).

## Analysis

**One idiom, three subjects.** The verb is *qāṣar*, *to be short* (lemma 7114), with *nepeš*, *soul*,
as its subject. In the five WLC files read for this ingest (Genesis, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua,
Judges), the pair occurs in these three verses and no others. The WEB renders it three ways:
*discouraged*, *grieved*, *troubled*.

| | Whose soul | Short with what |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers 21:4 | the people's | *the way*, the journey around Edom |
| Judges 10:16 | Yahweh's | *the misery of Israel* |
| Judges 16:16 | Samson's | Delilah's daily pressing, *to death* |

In the two human cases the idiom describes someone who has endured too long and gives way. The
people speak against God and Moses (Numbers 21:5); Samson tells Delilah *all his heart* (Judges
16:17). In Judges 10:16 the same words are used of Yahweh.

**How to read it of Yahweh.** The WEB and many readers take it as grief or compassion, *grieved for
the misery of Israel*, in line with Judges 2:18, *it grieved Yahweh because of their groaning*. The
two human uses suggest exhaustion, not pity. So some interpreters read 10:16 as Yahweh's patience
running out, either with Israel's suffering or with its repeated turning. That reading is reported,
not checked. The text says only that his soul became short, and the next chapter gives Gilead a
deliverer.

## Significance
- The idiom gives Yahweh the language of human endurance reaching its limit.
- In Judges it frames the Jephthah and Samson stories. It is used of Yahweh before the one and of
  Samson inside the other.
- See [[Divine Grief and Relenting]] and [[Compassion and Mercy]].
