## Analysis

**Three nouns in the same order, four buildings.** In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*
(read 2026-10-26) each passage names the same three gifts in the same order — *ḥoḵmāh*,
*təḇûnāh*, *daʿaṯ*:

- Proverbs 3:19–20 — *bəḥoḵmāh … biṯḇûnāh … bəḏaʿtô*: the world.
- Exodus 31:3 — *bəḥoḵmāh ûḇiṯḇûnāh ûḇəḏaʿaṯ*: the tabernacle, given to Bezalel with the Spirit of
  God.
- 1 Kings 7:14 — *ʾeṯ-haḥoḵmāh wəʾeṯ-hattəḇûnāh wəʾeṯ-haddaʿaṯ*: the temple's bronze work, given to
  Hiram. The WEB renders the third noun *"skill"*, which hides the match in English.
- Proverbs 24:3–4 — *bəḥoḵmāh … ûḇiṯḇûnāh … ûḇəḏaʿaṯ*: a house.

Proverbs 3:19 and 24:3 share a second word as well, the verb *kûn*, *to establish* (*kônēn* of the
heavens, *yiṯkônān* of the house). The Exodus 35:31 repetition was read in the stored WEB only; its
Hebrew was not checked in this pass.

**What the pattern says, and what it does not.** The match is in the vocabulary, and the vocabulary
is common in wisdom writing, so the wiki does not claim that Proverbs 24:3 alludes to Exodus 31 or
to 1 Kings 7. What the series shows is a single idea of building that runs from the largest scale to
the smallest: the same gifts that founded earth and heavens equipped the makers of God's dwelling
places, and the same gifts build an ordinary household. Proverbs 9:1 personifies it — Wisdom
herself *"has built her house"* — and 14:1 gives it to *"every wise woman"*.

### The worthy woman — Proverbs 31:13, 19, 24, 26–27

The closing poem brings household skill and wise teaching into one portrait: hands spin and
produce saleable garments, while the mouth speaks wisdom (31:13, 19, 24, 26–27). Exodus
35:25–26 likewise names women’s skilled spinning among the sanctuary contributions; see
[[The Freewill Offering]]. Proverbs 31 does not repeat the threefold formula traced above,
and neither passage identifies these workers with one another. The parallel concerns skill
expressed through textile work, without making household manufacture a sanctuary rite.

## Significance

Read together, the passages make household wisdom continuous with creation and with the sanctuary:
the tabernacle and temple are built with the gifts by which Yahweh built the world, and so is a
family that prospers. That is one reason Proverbs can treat everyday prudence as religious — the
fear of Yahweh is the beginning of the same wisdom (1:7; 9:10). See [[Wisdom]], [[Creation]], and
[[The Dwelling of God]]; for Bezalel, see [[Bezalel]].
