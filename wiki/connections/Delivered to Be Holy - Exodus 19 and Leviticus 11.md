# Delivered to Be Holy - Exodus 19 and Leviticus 11

## Passages involved

- [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] — Exodus 19:1–6.
- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:1–3.
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:31.
- [[Clean and Unclean Animals]] — Leviticus 11:44–47.

## Analysis

Exodus 19:4–6 moves from rescue out of Egypt to belonging to Yahweh as a holy nation.
Exodus 20:2 identifies the lawgiver as the deliverer before the commandments.
Exodus 22:31 joins holiness to refusing meat torn by animals. Leviticus 11:44–45 similarly
grounds Israel's holiness in Yahweh's holiness and his bringing them out of Egypt.

This is a thematic and legal continuity, not a prophecy-and-fulfillment claim.
Leviticus extends food distinctions through an entire classification and through carcass
handling. Exodus 22:31 forbids torn meat; Leviticus 11:39–40 specifies impurity, washing,
and waiting after contact with or eating the carcass of an edible animal. These are
different formulations and cases, not interchangeable restatements.

Sources: stored WEB passages listed above; comparison is paraphrase.

## Significance

Deliverance supplies the reason for belonging and obedience, including ordinary meals.
Holiness cannot be confined to the sanctuary or to the priests alone.
