# Because Abraham Obeyed My Voice - Law Before Sinai

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 26:5** — *"because Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my requirements, my commandments, my
  statutes, and my laws"* — [[Isaac in Gerar]]
- **Genesis 22:16–18** — *"because you have done this thing … because you have obeyed my voice"*, the
  clause 26:5 quotes and then expands — [[The Binding of Isaac]]
- **Genesis 15:6, 17** — *"He believed in Yahweh, who credited it to him for righteousness"*, and God
  passing between the pieces **alone** — [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]
- **Genesis 17:1** — *"Walk before me, and be blameless"*, the one demand the covenant chapter makes
  — [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]]
- **Genesis 18:19** — *"he will command his children and his household after him, that they may keep
  the way of Yahweh, to do righteousness and justice"* —
  [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]]
- **Deuteronomy 11:1** — *"keep his instruction, his statutes, his ordinances, and his commandments,
  always"*, the idiom 26:5 uses of a man four hundred years early
- **Romans 4:1–16; Galatians 3:15–18** — Paul's argument that the promise came by faith and before
  law, made from this family and not from this verse

## Analysis

### The verse

> **4** I will multiply your offspring as the stars of the sky, and will give all these lands to your
> offspring. In your offspring will all the nations of the earth be blessed,
> **5** because Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my requirements, my commandments, my statutes, and
> my laws.”

**Four Hebrew nouns do all the work**: ***mišmartî*** (my charge or requirement), ***miṣwōtay*** (my
commandments), ***ḥuqqôtay*** (my statutes), and ***wətôrōtāy*** (my laws — the plural of *tôrâ*).
**Together they are the standard Deuteronomic formula for keeping the Sinai covenant**, and they
appear here about a man who lived before Sinai, before the tabernacle, before a priesthood, and
before any of it was given.

**The first half of the clause is a quotation.** *ʿĒqeb ʾăšer šāmaʿ ʾAbrāhām bəqōlî* — *because
Abraham listened to my voice* — is the wording of 22:18, where the angel speaks it at the end of the
binding and the referent is unambiguous: **one act, on one mountain.** What 26:5 does is take that
clause and append a legal vocabulary to it, so that the obedience of one command becomes the keeping
of a code.

### Three readings, and the wiki adopts none

1. **A later hand, writing in a later idiom.** The four-term formula is characteristic of Deuteronomy
   and of the historical books shaped by it, and its appearance in a patriarchal narrative is one of
   the standard exhibits for a Deuteronomic layer in Genesis. This is the majority critical reading
   and it rests on the vocabulary itself, which is genuinely distinctive and genuinely late in its
   other occurrences.
2. **Retrospective idiom rather than anachronism.** Genesis has already said that Abraham was told to
   *"walk before me, and be blameless"* (17:1) and that God chose him so that *"he will command his
   children and his household after him, that they may keep the way of Yahweh"* (18:19). On this
   reading 26:5 is a summary of a life the book has narrated, given in the only language the
   tradition had for obedience — the way a modern writer might say a man *kept the rules* about
   someone who lived before the rules were written down.
3. **The maximal reading**, that Abraham kept the whole Torah before it was given. It takes the verse
   at full strength and generates a large rabbinic literature; it also has to explain 12:13, 16:4,
   and 20:2.

### The theological problem, which is not the dating problem

**Everywhere else in Genesis the promise is unilateral.** At 15:17 a smoking furnace and a flaming
torch pass between the pieces alone: **God swears the self-curse the rite exists to enact, and Abram
undertakes nothing.** At 15:6 the thing credited to him is belief. At 17 the sign is imposed on a
household including the men who were bought. **Genesis 26:5 gives the promise to the second
generation *because* the first obeyed**, and that is a different kind of sentence.

**The book contains both and reconciles neither.** The wiki keeps them side by side rather than
subordinating one to the other, on the same principle it applies to the two creation accounts and to
the two Beersheba etiologies.

### And Paul argues around it

Romans 4 and Galatians 3 build the case that the promise to Abraham was prior to law, independent of
law, and received by faith — and **both arguments are made from Genesis 15:6 and from the four
hundred thirty years of Galatians 3:17, not from Genesis 26:5.** Paul does not quote this verse, and
the wiki does not read that as either a concession or an oversight; it records that the verse is in
the book Paul is arguing from and does not appear in the argument. See
[[Credited to Him for Righteousness - Genesis 15.6 in Paul and James]].

**James, arguing the other way** — *"was not Abraham our father justified by works, in that he
offered up Isaac his son on the altar?"* (2:21) — reaches for the binding, which is where 26:5's
first clause comes from, and does not quote 26:5 either.

### What Isaac gets, and what he did

**Nothing in 26:2–5 is grounded in anything Isaac has done**, and the chapter that follows has him
telling the men of Gerar that his wife is his sister. **The covenant arrives on his father's record**
and is confirmed nineteen verses later on the same basis: *"I will bless you, and multiply your
offspring **for my servant Abraham's sake**"* (26:24). See [[Call and Election]] and [[Isaac]].

## Significance

- **It is the only verse in Genesis that makes the promise's continuation depend on obedience**, and
  it does it in the vocabulary of a code that does not exist yet. Both halves of that sentence are
  why the verse matters.
- **It shows the book's own habit of summarizing.** Genesis narrates Abraham's obedience once, on a
  mountain, and then refers back to it here as though it were a career. Whether that is a later
  editor or the narrator's ordinary compression is precisely what is at issue.
- **And it puts the conditional and unconditional statements of the covenant three chapters apart in
  the same document.** Genesis 15 has God bind himself alone; Genesis 26 has him give the same
  promise on the strength of a man's keeping. **The Bible's later arguments about faith and law are
  arguments between two verses that were already in Genesis.**
