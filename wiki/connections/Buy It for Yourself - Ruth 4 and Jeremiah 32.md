# Buy It for Yourself - Ruth 4 and Jeremiah 32

## Passages involved

- [[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]] — Ruth 4:1–12; the nearer kinsman gives up his right: *Take my right of
  redemption for yourself … Buy it for yourself* (4:6, 8).
- **Jeremiah 32:6–15** — Hanamel offers his field in [[Anathoth]] to his cousin Jeremiah: *Buy my field
  that is in Anathoth; for the right of redemption is yours to buy it* (32:7); *for the right of
  inheritance is yours, and the redemption is yours. Buy it for yourself* (32:8). Not yet ingested.
- **Leviticus 25:25** — *his kinsman who is next to him shall come, and redeem that which his brother has
  sold* — [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]]

## Analysis

**The two narrative scenes of a kinsman redeeming a field.** Leviticus 25 sets out the law of the
*gōʾēl* who buys back a poor relative's land; Ruth 4 and Jeremiah 32 are the two places where a story
shows it being done. They share the key words. *Gəʾullâ*, *the right of redemption*, occurs in the WLC
files read (Genesis–Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel, Jeremiah) only in Leviticus 25, Ruth 4:6–7, and Jeremiah
32:7–8; and *qənēh-lāḵ*, *buy it for yourself*, occurs in those files only at Ruth 4:8 and Jeremiah 32:7,
8, 25 (WLC; at 32:25 Jeremiah recalls God's word to him, *Buy the field for money, and call
witnesses*).

| | **Ruth 4** | **Jeremiah 32** |
|---|---|---|
| who has the right first | the nearer kinsman | Jeremiah (*the right of redemption is yours*, 32:7) |
| who says *Buy it for yourself* | the kinsman who declines (4:8) | the cousin who sells (32:8) |
| the buyer | Boaz, the second in line | Jeremiah |
| the form | the shoe, and *You are witnesses today* (4:7–10) | *I signed the deed, sealed it, called witnesses, and weighed the money* (32:10) |
| what is at stake | a dead man's name on his inheritance | the future of the land under siege |

**The purpose differs.** Boaz buys to keep a family's land and name in Israel. Jeremiah buys, at God's
word, while Jerusalem is under siege, as a sign that fields will again be bought in the land (Jeremiah
32:15, by reference; not yet ingested, and the summary here is a paraphrase, not a quotation). In both
the purchase is an act of hope for a future that has not yet come: Ruth's child is not yet born, and
Jeremiah's return is not yet in sight.

## Significance

The connection is lexical and legal; neither book mentions the other. Taken together they show the
redemption law of Leviticus 25 in two very different settings — a village gate in the days of the judges
and a prison court in the last days of Judah — and the same phrase, *Buy it for yourself*, spoken in
both. See [[Redemption]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].
