## Analysis

Moses grounds courage for Israel and Joshua in Yahweh’s continued presence (Deuteronomy 31:6, 8).
Joshua 1:5–9 repeats the assurance and the charge to courage after Moses’ death, connecting the
inheritance task to careful obedience to Moses’ law. This is continuity of commission across
the death of a leader, not Joshua’s independence from instruction.

Hebrews 13:5 uses the assurance in a different setting:

> I will in no way leave you, neither will I in any way forsake you.

The surrounding instruction concerns freedom from the love of money and contentment, followed
by confidence in the Lord’s help (13:5–6). The promise associated with Israel’s entry and Joshua’s
leadership supports the readers’ daily trust. Hebrews does not identify a single source verse;
Deuteronomy 31:6, 8 and Joshua 1:5 belong to the relevant promise tradition. No claim about exact
Greek agreement or an exclusive source is made here.

**Tension retained:** Deuteronomy 31:16–18 also threatens divine forsaking in response to
Israel’s apostasy. The later appeal to assurance must not be used to remove that warning from
the original chapter.

The now-ingested Deuteronomy 31:14–23 places the warning between the summons to commission
Joshua and his final charge. Israel forsakes Yahweh (16), and Yahweh threatens to forsake Israel
and hide his face (17–18); nevertheless, Joshua receives the promise of presence (23). The
tension is internal to the chapter, not merely a difference between earlier and later biblical
books.

## Significance

The promise sustains a people through succession and later readers through material insecurity.
Its reuse extends the application of divine faithfulness without making every recipient a
conquest leader. See [[Faith]], [[Covenant]], and [[Joshua (son of Nun)]].
