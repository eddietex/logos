# He Pours Contempt on Princes - Job 12.21-24 and Psalm 107.40

## Passages involved

- **Job 12:17–25** — *"He leads counselors away stripped. He makes judges fools. He loosens the bond
  of kings… He pours contempt on princes, and loosens the belt of the strong. He uncovers deep things
  out of darkness, and brings out to light the shadow of death. He increases the nations, and he
  destroys them… He takes away understanding from the chiefs of the people of the earth, and causes
  them to wander in a wilderness where there is no way. They grope in the dark without light. He
  makes them stagger like a drunken man."* — [[Though He Slay Me]]
- **Psalm 107:40–41** — *"He pours contempt on princes, and causes them to wander in a trackless
  waste. Yet he lifts the needy out of their affliction, and increases their families like a flock."*
- **Psalm 107:42** — *"The upright will see it, and be glad. All the wicked will shut their mouths."*
- **Job 22:19, 29** — Eliphaz: *"The righteous see it, and are glad"*, and *"When they cast down,
  you shall say, 'be lifted up.' He will save the humble person."* — [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]]
- **Job 5:11–16** — Eliphaz: *"He frustrates the devices of the crafty… He takes the wise in their
  own craftiness… They meet with darkness in the day time, and grope at noonday as in the night. But
  he saves… the needy… So the poor has hope."* — [[Eliphaz's First Speech]]
- **Deuteronomy 28:28–29** — *"Yahweh will strike you with madness, with blindness… You will grope
  at noonday, as the blind gropes in darkness."*
- **1 Samuel 2:6–8** — Hannah: *"Yahweh kills and makes alive… He raises up the poor out of the
  dust… to make them sit with princes."*
- **Isaiah 40:23–24** — *"who brings princes to nothing, who makes the judges of the earth
  meaningless. They are planted scarcely. They are sown scarcely. Their stock has scarcely taken root
  in the ground."*
- **Isaiah 44:25** — *"who frustrates the signs of the liars, and makes diviners mad; who turns wise
  men backward, and makes their knowledge foolish."*
- **Daniel 2:21** — *"He removes kings, and sets up kings. He gives wisdom to the wise."*
- **Luke 1:51–53** — *"He has scattered the proud… He has put down princes from their thrones, and
  has exalted the lowly."*
- **1 Corinthians 1:19–20** — *"I will destroy the wisdom of the wise… Hasn't God made foolish the
  wisdom of this world?"*
- **Genesis 1:2** — *tōhû*, *formless*, the word of Job 12:24's *wilderness*

## Analysis

**A line quoted verbatim.** Psalm 107:40 is *šōpēk bûz ʿal-nədîbîm wayyatʿēm bətōhû lōʾ-dārek*.
Job 12:21a is *šôpēk bûz ʿal-nədîbîm*, and Job 12:24b is *wayyatʿēm bətōhû lōʾ-dārek*. **The psalm
line is two half-verses of Job's hymn joined together**, or Job's hymn is built around a known line
split in half. Which way the borrowing ran is not recoverable; both texts are drawing on a hymnic
stock of praise for the God who overturns the mighty, the stock also visible in Hannah's song, in
Isaiah 40, and in Mary's.

**The difference is the next verse.** In the psalm the line is followed at once by its purpose:
*"Yet he lifts the needy out of their affliction"* (107:41). The great are humbled *so that* the
lowly may be raised; it is the reversal of fortunes, and its logic is justice. Every other text in
the tradition has the same second half — Hannah's *"He raises up the poor out of the dust"*, Mary's
*"has exalted the lowly"*, and Eliphaz's own version: *"But he saves… the needy… So the poor has
hope"* (Job 5:15–16). **Job's hymn has no second half.** It runs for nine verses on the humbling of
counselors, judges, kings, priests, the trusted, elders, princes, the strong, and the chiefs of the
nations, and never says that anyone is raised, or that any of them deserved it. It is the
reversal-of-fortunes hymn with the reversal removed.

**And it runs creation backward.** The wilderness where the chiefs wander is *tōhû*, the word of
Genesis 1:2 — *the earth was formless (tōhû)*. The hymn's last verses move from light to darkness:
God *"brings out to light the shadow of death"* (12:22), and the rulers *"grope in the dark without
light"* (12:25). The groping is the covenant curse of Deuteronomy 28:29, which Eliphaz had used of
the crafty (5:14). Job uses it of everyone in charge. Compare chapter 3, where Job asked for his own
birthday to be returned to the darkness before creation; here he describes God doing the same to the
world's government. See [[Let That Day Be Darkness - Job 3 as Counter-Creation]].

**The point is not that God is unjust.** Job never says so in the hymn. The point is aimed at the
friends: the God they praise as the guarantor of moral order is, on their own hymnic evidence, the God
who unmakes the wise and the judges — the kind of people the friends are — without an explanation.
*"With God is wisdom and might"* (12:13), and the wisdom is not theirs.

**Eliphaz supplies the psalm's ending twice.** Psalm 107 follows the humbling of princes with the
lifting of the needy (107:41) and then with the spectators: *"The upright will see it, and be glad"*
(107:42), *yirʾû yəšārîm wəyiśmāḥû*. Job 22:19 has *yirʾû ṣaddîqîm wəyiśmāḥû*, *"The righteous see
it, and are glad"* — the same verbs, one synonym changed — and 22:29 has the lifting: *"When they
cast down, you shall say, 'be lifted up.' He will save the humble person."* The second half of the
reversal-of-fortunes hymn, which Job's version in chapter 12 left out, is restored by the friend
who uses it against him. In Eliphaz's mouth Job is on the wrong side of both halves: among the
cast-down whom the righteous laugh at, and, if he repents, the one who will speak the word that
lifts others up.

## Significance

- The clearest case in Job of a line shared verbatim with the Psalter. The book's poetry draws on
  Israel's hymns even though no Israelite appears in it.
- Read beside Psalm 107, Job 12 shows how much of the friends' theology rests on a half of a
  tradition that has two halves; Job declines to quote the second.
- 1 Corinthians 1:19–20 makes God's frustrating of the wise into a gospel theme; Paul's one quotation
  of the friends (Job 5:13, at 1 Corinthians 3:19) sits in the same argument. See
  [[He Takes the Wise in Their Craftiness - Job 5.13 in 1 Corinthians 3.19]].
