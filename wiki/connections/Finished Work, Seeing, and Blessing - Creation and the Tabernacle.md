# Finished Work, Seeing, and Blessing - Creation and the Tabernacle

## Passages involved

- [[The Creation of the World]] — Genesis 1:31–2:3; seeing, finished work, rest, and blessing.
- [[The Work Completed]] — Exodus 39:32–43; finished work, inspection, and blessing.
- [[The Freewill Offering]] — Exodus 35:1–3; the Sabbath rule before the construction appeal.
- Exodus 40:17–35 — erection, completion, and filling; checked in stored WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Genesis 1:31 describes God seeing everything he has made and finding it very good.
Genesis 2:1–3 then speaks of the heavens and earth finished, God finishing and resting from
his work, and his blessing and sanctifying the seventh day. Exodus 39:32 declares the work
of the tabernacle finished; after the inventory, Moses sees the work, recognizes obedience
to Yahweh’s command, and blesses the people (39:43).

**The correspondence is a literary parallel visible in the stored WEB.** Both accounts
connect making, completion, seeing, and blessing. They do not put every action in the same
order: Genesis’ comprehensive seeing precedes its completion statements, while Exodus’s
completion statement precedes Moses’ inspection. God’s verdict concerns the goodness of
creation; Moses’ verdict concerns conformity to the command. The recipients of blessing
also differ: the seventh day in Genesis 2:3, the people in Exodus 39:43.

**The sanctuary can be read as an ordered dwelling within God’s creation.** That is a
literary and theological inference from the parallel, not an explicit declaration in Exodus
that the tabernacle is a miniature cosmos. The unit does not assign a part of the sanctuary
to each creation day. Nor does it give the duration of construction or narrate Sabbath
observance at the inspection. The work was introduced under the Sabbath command in 35:1–3;
see [[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]].

**Completion has stages.** Exodus 39:32 announces finished manufacture, and 39:33–41
lists the articles brought to Moses. Chapter 40 separately reports their erection and
placement, concluding with Moses finishing the work (40:33) before the filling with glory
(40:34–35). These statements should not be collapsed into a single event or treated as
contradictory without regard to their different tasks. See
[[That I May Dwell Among Them - Tabernacle, Temple, Flesh, and New Creation]].

**Limits:** this comparison makes no claim about identical Hebrew words or syntax. No
source-language edition was used for a lexical argument. Moses’ blessing is reported but
not transcribed; its wording cannot be supplied from Genesis or a later blessing text.

## Significance

The end of the making account links faithful human work to the world’s ordered beginning,
while retaining the distinction between Creator and craftspeople. Moses recognizes the
community’s obedience and blesses it; God’s indwelling remains an event beyond the review.
[[Creation]], [[Work]], [[Seeing and Being Seen]], [[Blessing]], and [[The Dwelling of God]]
meet here without requiring an allegory for every object.
