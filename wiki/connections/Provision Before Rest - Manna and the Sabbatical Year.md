# Provision Before Rest - Manna and the Sabbatical Year

## Passages involved

- [[Manna and Quail]] — Exodus 16:19–30.
- [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]] — Leviticus 25:18–22.

## Analysis

In Exodus 16, the normal daily gathering rule does not authorize hoarding overnight:
what people keep against the command spoils (19–20). Yet on the sixth day they gather
twice as much, and the portion reserved at Moses' direction remains good for the Sabbath
(22–26). Yahweh's gift of rest includes food before the day without gathering (29).

Leviticus 25 anticipates an analogous question on the scale of years. With sowing and
harvesting interrupted, what will people eat (20)? Yahweh promises a sixth-year blessing
for three years; the old store lasts through eighth-year sowing until ninth-year produce
arrives (21–22). Both passages place provision before commanded rest.

**Storage differs by command and setting.** Exodus forbids ordinary overnight retention
but permits Sabbath retention; Leviticus explicitly relies on stored agricultural food.
The manna rule cannot be generalized into a timeless ban on storing provisions. Nor does
the comparison turn the land law into a report of a miracle already performed: Exodus
narrates collection and preservation, while Leviticus gives a promise for settled life.

The six-day and six-year patterns are a thematic parallel. Neither passage explicitly
quotes the other. The three-year provision is not used here to resolve the alignment of
Jubilee with the next sabbatical cycle, which Leviticus 25:20–22 does not spell out.

## Significance

[[Sabbath]] and [[Work]] are framed by [[Blessing]] and [[Faith]]: rest rests on promised
provision, while the particular commands distinguish permitted storage from disobedience.
[[Famine and Plenty]] includes both wilderness food and agricultural reserves without
collapsing their different settings.
