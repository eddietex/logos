# Seventy Souls - The Count of Jacob's House in Genesis, Exodus and Acts

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 46:8–27** — [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]]. The register, its four subtotals, and the
  two grand totals of 46:26–27.
- **Exodus 1:5** — the same figure, opening the book of the bondage.
- **Deuteronomy 10:22** — seventy went down; the God of Israel has since made them as the stars of the
  sky for multitude.
- **Acts 7:14** — Stephen, before the Sanhedrin: **seventy-five souls.**

## Analysis

### The arithmetic of Genesis 46

**Four subtotals, given by mother, and they add to seventy.**

| verse | line | names printed (living) | subtotal given |
|---|---|---|---|
| **46:9–15** | [[Leah]] | **thirty-two** | **thirty-three** |
| **46:16–18** | [[Zilpah]] | **sixteen** | **sixteen** |
| **46:19–22** | [[Rachel]] | **fourteen** | **fourteen** |
| **46:23–25** | [[Bilhah]] | **seven** | **seven** |
| | | | **seventy** |

**Three of the four reproduce exactly. [[Leah]]'s is one short**, once [[Er]] and [[Onan]] are removed
as 46:12 directs — *"but Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan."*

**The missing one is [[Jacob]], and the register says so in its own opening line**: *"These are the
names of the children of Israel, who came into Egypt, **Jacob and his sons**"* (46:8). **He is inside
the scope of the count**, and Leah's is the line he heads. **The two totals that follow confirm it and
cannot be made to work any other way:**

- **46:26 — sixty-six.** *"All the souls who came with Jacob into Egypt, who were his direct
  offspring, besides Jacob's sons' wives."* **Seventy, minus Jacob himself** (who did not come *with*
  Jacob), **minus [[Joseph]], [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]]** (who did not come at all, being already
  there). **70 − 4 = 66.**
- **46:27 — seventy.** *"The sons of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt, were two souls. All the
  souls of the house of Jacob, who came into Egypt, were seventy."* **66 + 2 + Joseph + Jacob = 70.**

**The register is therefore internally exact, and its exactness depends on counting Jacob once.** The
alternative explanation — that a thirty-third name has dropped out of Leah's list in transmission —
**is possible and leaves 46:26–27 unaccounted for**, so the wiki does not adopt it.

**Note also what the seventy excludes.** ***"Besides Jacob's sons' wives"*** (46:26). **Eleven or more
women are on the road and none of them is in the number.** **The only women counted are [[Dinah]]
(46:15) and [[Serah]] (46:17)** — a daughter and a granddaughter, both in by name, both with no
recorded husband.

### Seventy-five

**The Septuagint reads seventy-five at Genesis 46:27 and at Exodus 1:5**, and **a Hebrew copy of
Exodus from Qumran (4QExodb) agrees with it.** **Stephen follows that text at Acts 7:14**, where
Joseph summons his father and all his relatives, seventy-five souls — **speaking Greek, to a
Greek-speaking Diaspora audience, quoting the Bible they used.**

**The five extra are supplied by the Septuagint's longer version of 46:20**, which gives [[Joseph]]
grandsons and great-grandsons in Egypt — [[Machir]], Gilead, and the sons of Ephraim — **where the
Hebrew gives him only [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]].** **The Hebrew does name Machir, but four
chapters later and outside any count** — at 50:23, where his children are born on Joseph's knees
([[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]) — **so the Septuagint is not inventing a person, it is moving
one into the register.**

| | Genesis 46:27 | Exodus 1:5 | Deuteronomy 10:22 | Acts 7:14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Masoretic Hebrew** | seventy | seventy | seventy | — |
| **Septuagint** | seventy-five | seventy-five | seventy | — |
| **Qumran** | — | seventy-five | — | — |
| **New Testament** | — | — | — | **seventy-five** |

**This is a textual variant, not an error in Acts.** **Stephen is quoting accurately from the text in
front of him**, and the wiki records the difference rather than harmonising it. **The point Stephen is
making does not turn on the number**: his speech is about a people who were always outside the land
and always rescued into it, **and either figure serves it.**

### Why seventy

**[[The Table of Nations]] at Genesis 10 counts the peoples of the world at seventy.** **Genesis 46
counts a household at seventy and walks it into one of them.** **Deuteronomy 32:8 puts the two numbers
in a single verse** — the nations divided *according to the number of the children of Israel* — and
**the Septuagint reads that line differently again.** See
[[Seventy Nations - Genesis 10 in Deuteronomy 32 and Luke 10]].

**The number recurs wherever Israel is being constituted or represented**: seventy elders go up the
mountain with Moses (Exodus 24:1, 9), seventy are appointed to bear the burden with him (Numbers
11:16–25), **and Luke sends out seventy** (Luke 10:1, where the manuscripts themselves divide between
seventy and seventy-two). **Genesis 46 is where the number first attaches to Israel**, and it attaches
at the moment Israel is smallest.

## Significance

**The register is doing two jobs at once and the wiki reads both.**

**It is a legal document**: a list of exactly who entered, taken at the border, with the dead struck
off and the wives excluded — **the kind of thing a family keeps when it expects to be asked, later,
who it is and where it came from.** **Exodus 1:1–5 reprints it**, and the four hundred years between
the two printings are the whole point of the reprint.

**And it is a claim about scale.** **Deuteronomy 10:22 is the verse that says so out loud**: seventy
went down, and look at you now. **The Bible's first census of Israel counts a number small enough to
fit in a courtyard**, and it is taken on the road into the country that will try to destroy them. See
[[Covenant]] and [[Providence]].
