# The Beloved at the Door - Song of Solomon 5.2-6 and Revelation 3.20

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 5:2–6** — [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]]: *"It is the voice of my beloved who
  knocks: 'Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled'"* (5:2); she delays, then *"I opened
  to my beloved; but my beloved left; and had gone away"* (5:6).
- **Song of Solomon 4:16–5:1** — [[A Garden Locked and Opened]]: *"Let my beloved come into his
  garden"*; *"I have come into my garden … I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey"*.
- **Revelation 3:14–22** *(not yet fetched; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — to the assembly in
  Laodicea: *"Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, then
  I will come in to him, and will dine with him, and he with me"* (3:20).

Hebrew of the Song read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28. No Greek
wording of Revelation is claimed on this page.

## Analysis

**What the two scenes share.** Both have someone outside a closed door, a knock, a voice, and a
request to be let in:

| | Song 5:2–6 | Revelation 3:20 |
|---|---|---|
| Who is outside | *"my beloved"* (5:2) | the speaker of the letter, *"the Amen, the Faithful and True Witness"* (3:14) |
| The knock | *"the voice of my beloved who knocks"* (5:2) | *"I stand at the door and knock"* |
| The voice | *"Open to me"* (5:2) | *"If anyone hears my voice"* |
| Opening | she delays (5:3), then opens (5:5–6) | *"and opens the door"* |
| What follows | he has gone; she seeks and calls him (5:6) | *"I will come in to him, and will dine with him, and he with me"* |

The meal of Revelation 3:20 has its counterpart in the Song not in 5:2–6 but in the unit before,
where he comes into the garden at her invitation and eats and drinks (5:1). Set beside both Song
scenes, Revelation 3:20 promises what 5:1 shows and what 5:6 loses: the one who knocks comes in, if
the door is opened.

**What differs.** In the Song the one inside is a woman who loves him and hesitates for reasons she
names, the robe taken off and the feet washed (5:3); the delay is not called a sin, and he says
nothing when she calls. In Revelation the one inside is an assembly judged *"lukewarm"* (3:16), rich
in its own estimate and *"poor, blind, and naked"* in his (3:17), and the knock comes after *"As many
as I love, I reprove and chasten. Be zealous therefore, and repent"* (3:19). The Song's door is a
lover's; Revelation's is a call to repentance. The Song's knocker leaves; Revelation's stands and
waits, as far as the verse goes.

Revelation 3:18 also offers *"white garments, that you may clothe yourself, and that the shame of
your nakedness may not be revealed"*. In Song 5:3 and 5:7 the woman's garments are taken off and taken
away. The wiki notes the shared images of clothing and does not treat them as a link.

**Allusion or not.** Revelation does not cite the Song, and the wording shared in English, *voice*,
*knock*, *open*, is ordinary language for anyone at a door. Whether Revelation's author had Song 5:2 in mind is a
judgment that this page has not checked against the Greek or against commentary. The pairing is
older than modern scholarship: Christian allegorical reading of the Song as Christ and the church
or the soul has long set 5:2 beside Revelation 3:20, and Jewish allegorical reading of the Song as
God and Israel reads the knock at the door as God's call to his people. These are recorded here as
readings, without a specific source verified for either.

## Significance

The connection is typological, and it is made by readers rather than by either text. What it
captures is a shape that both texts share: love that comes to a closed door and asks to be let in,
and a meal that follows an opened door. The Song shows the opening too late, and the absence that
follows (5:6–8). Revelation, speaking to a community, turns the same shape into an offer and a
warning. See [[Love]] and [[The Man (Song of Solomon)]].
