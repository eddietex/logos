# Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8

  Jonathan; but Saul and Jonathan his son had them.* Stored WEB.

## Analysis

**The pair.** *Ḥereḇ* and *ḥănîṯ*, sword and spear, are named together in the Philistine ban (13:19, *ḥereḇ ʾô
ḥănîṯ*), in the count of Israel's arms on the day of battle (13:22, *ḥereḇ waḥănîṯ*), in David's confession before
Goliath (17:47, *lōʾ bəḥereḇ ûḇaḥănîṯ yəhôšîaʿ YHWH*), and in David's request at Nob (21:9 WLC, *ḥănîṯ ʾô
ḥāreḇ*; WEB 21:8). All WLC. The pair recurs inside 1 Samuel as a measure of who is armed.

**Israel without weapons, twice.** The Song of Deborah asks whether *a shield or spear* was seen *among forty
thousand in Israel* (Judges 5:8), the same condition in an earlier oppression, with a different word for spear,
*rōmaḥ* (WLC). 1 Samuel 13 explains the condition as a policy and counts it: two armed men in the whole army.

**The victories that follow are not won by the missing weapons.** Shamgar had saved Israel from the Philistines
*with an ox goad* (Judges 3:31); in 13:21 Israel pays the Philistines to sharpen its goads. At Michmash in the next
chapter the rout is begun by Jonathan and one armour-bearer, and *the earth quaked* (14:15, stored WEB; not yet
ingested). In the valley of Elah the one man who had no sword wins, and David draws the sword he kills Goliath with
from Goliath's own sheath (17:50–51, stored WEB; see [[David Kills Goliath]]). David's sentence states the principle the
narrative has been illustrating since 13:22: *Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear; for the battle is
Yahweh's.*

**Saul and Jonathan are the exception, and their swords go elsewhere.** Only *Saul and Jonathan his son* had them
(13:22). Jonathan gives his sword to David with his robe (18:4; [[Jonathan Loves David]]); before the fight with Goliath Saul dresses David
in his clothing, a helmet, and a coat of mail, and David straps on *his sword* and cannot go in them (17:38–39;
whose sword *his* means the verse does not make explicit); and at Nob the only sword available is Goliath's
(21:9). (All stored WEB; not yet ingested; the sequence is read from the stored text, not checked against later
ingests.)

## Significance

- **The thread runs through the Philistine wars of 1 Samuel and states its moral at 17:47.** The verse in 13:22 is
  the setup; the Goliath scene is where the principle is spoken.
- **Hannah's song had already said it**: *for no man shall prevail by strength* (1 Samuel 2:9, stored WEB). See
  [[The Song of Hannah]], which links this verse forward to the boy with a sling.
- **What the wiki does not claim.** The text never says the Philistine ban was providential, or that Israel's lack
  of weapons was meant to teach dependence. It records the ban, the lack, and later victories won without the
  missing weapons, and it gives the principle in David's mouth. Whether the narrator means the connection is a
  matter of reading.

### 1 Samuel 17:41–54 ingested

David's list at 17:45 has three weapons, *ḥereḇ*, *ḥănîṯ*, and *kîḏôn*, the last being Goliath's javelin of 17:6
(WLC). The principle at 17:47 keeps only the pair, *sword and spear*, the two the Philistines had banned at 13:19. In
the Hebrew files checked, *yāšaʿ*, *save*, and *ḥereḇ* stand in one verse otherwise only at Deuteronomy 33:29, where
Yahweh himself is *the sword of your excellency* for *a people saved by Yahweh* (stored WEB; WLC). The narrator then
says the thing twice: *there was no sword in the hand of David* (17:50), and David takes Goliath's (17:51). See
[[David Kills Goliath]] and [[The Head and the Armor - Goliath at Elah and Saul at Gilboa]]. Source: stored WEB
Deuteronomy 33:29; 1 Samuel 13:19; 17:6, 45–51. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### 1 Samuel 18:10–11 — Saul's spear

A chapter after *Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear* (17:47), Saul has *his spear in his hand* and throws it at
David (18:10–11). The verb, *wayyāṭel* (*ṭûl*, lemma 2904, *hiphil*), occurs in the files checked only here and at
20:33, where he throws it at Jonathan ([[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]]). The spear of 13:22, one of two in Israel, is now
turned on the man who won without one. See [[Saul's Jealousy of David]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 13:22; 17:47;
18:10–11; 20:33. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### The third spear — 1 Samuel 19:9–10

*As he sat in his house with his spear in his hand* (19:9): Saul's spear is now a fixture of the scene, and at 22:6 he
will sit under the tamarisk *with his spear in his hand* again (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The throw of 19:10 uses
Saul's own sentence from 18:11, *to strike … David and the wall*, *bǝḏāwiḏ ûḇaqqîr* (WLC), and the spear goes into the
wall. In 1 Samuel *ḥănîṯ* belongs first to an Israel without one (13:19, 22) and to Goliath (17:7, 45, 47), and from
18:10 to Saul (18:10–11; 19:9–10; 20:33; 22:6; 26:7–22) (WLC). At 26:7 it is *stuck in the ground at his head*
while he sleeps, and David takes it. See [[Michal Helps David Escape]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 13:19, 22; 17:7,
45, 47; 18:10–11; 19:9–10; 22:6; 26:7–22. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.

### No weapon at Naioth — 1 Samuel 19:18–24

The pericope after the third spear has no weapon in it at all. Saul's messengers come *to seize David*, and the
Spirit of God stops them; Saul comes himself, without the spear, and is stopped the same way (19:20–24). David is
delivered without fighting and without a ruse. The verse David spoke to Goliath, *Yahweh doesn't save with sword and
spear* (17:47), is not quoted, but no one in the pericope uses either. See [[Saul Among the Prophets at Naioth]].
Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 17:47; 19:18–24.

### The fourth spear — 1 Samuel 20:33

At the new-moon table, Saul sits *on the seat by the wall* (20:25) — the wall of 18:11 and 19:10 — and when Jonathan
defends David (*Why should he be put to death? What has he done?*), *Saul cast his spear at him to strike him* (20:33).
The spear that in 13:22 belonged solely to *Saul and Jonathan his son* against the Philistines has now been thrown
three times at David and a fourth time by the father at the son himself. Meanwhile Jonathan's arrows — the weapons he
carries into the field (20:20, 36–40) — become instruments of warning and peace rather than war. See
[[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 13:22; 18:11; 19:10; 20:20, 25, 32–40.
