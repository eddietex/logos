# The Drawn Sword Before Balaam and Joshua

## Passages involved

- [[Balaam's Donkey]] — Numbers 22:22–35.
- [[The Commander of Yahweh's Army]] — Joshua 5:13–15.

## Analysis

Numbers 22:23, 31 describes Yahweh's angel with a drawn sword. When Balaam finally sees
him, he falls on his face; the angel rebukes him and restricts what he may say (32–35).
Joshua 5:13–15 also presents a drawn sword, a human falling face down, and an authoritative
word. Joshua responds by asking for instruction and obeying.

The differences matter. Balaam fails to see until Yahweh opens his eyes, and his donkey
has saved his life. Joshua sees the man, approaches, and asks his allegiance. The commander
does not accuse Joshua of a perverse journey or threaten to kill him. Numbers expressly
calls its figure Yahweh's angel; Joshua calls its figure commander of Yahweh's army.
These parallels do not prove their identity or transfer Balaam's guilt to Joshua.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers 22:22–35 and Joshua 5:13–15; comparisons in paraphrase.

## Significance

A drawn sword need not signify a weapon at the human observer's disposal. Both scenes
place the observer under a word he must receive, while preserving the contrast between
Balaam's rebuke and Joshua's obedient approach.
