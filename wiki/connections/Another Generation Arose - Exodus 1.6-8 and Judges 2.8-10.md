# Another Generation Arose - Exodus 1.6-8 and Judges 2.8-10

- **Genesis 50:22, 26** — *"Joseph lived one hundred ten years"*; *"So Joseph died, being one hundred
  ten years old"* — [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]
- **Exodus 1:6, 8** — *"Joseph died, as did all his brothers, and all that generation"*; *"Now there
  arose a new king over Egypt, who didn't know Joseph"* — [[The Oppression in Egypt]]
- **Joshua 24:29; Judges 2:8** — *"Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of Yahweh, died, being one

- **Judges 2:10** — *"After all that generation were gathered to their fathers, another generation

## Analysis

**The same three steps open the bondage and the book of Judges**: a great leader dies at a hundred
and ten; his whole generation dies with him; and someone *arises* who does not *know*.

| | Exodus 1 | Judges 2 |
|---|---|---|
| the leader | Joseph, 110 (Genesis 50:26) | Joshua, 110 (Judges 2:8) |
| the generation | *"and all that generation"* (1:6) | *"all that generation were gathered to their fathers"* (2:10) |
| the arising | *"there arose* (*wayyāqom*) *a new king"* (1:8) | *"another generation arose* (*wayyāqom*) *after them"* (2:10) |
| the not-knowing | *"who didn't know Joseph"* | *"who didn't know Yahweh, nor the work which he had done"* |
| what follows | Israel enslaved by Egypt | Israel serving the Baals (2:11) and given into the hand of its enemies |

**The ages are exact and both are given twice** (Genesis 50:22, 26; Joshua 24:29, Judges 2:8). A
hundred and ten years was the Egyptian conventional ideal lifespan, which is often noted as a
reason the figure suits Joseph; that it is then given to Joshua binds the two men together.

**The difference is who forgets.** In Exodus it is the foreign king who does not know Israel's
benefactor; in Judges it is Israel that does not know its God. **The first forgetting enslaves
Israel; the second is Israel enslaving itself** — and the cycle of Judges is a cycle of oppressions
by foreign kings, each one a small Egypt.

## Significance

**Memory is generational, and Scripture marks its failure at the same seam twice.** Exodus answers
the king's not-knowing with a God who will make Egypt *"know that I am Yahweh"* (7:5); Deuteronomy
answers the danger of Judges in advance, with commands to teach the children what God did in Egypt
(see [[Remembrance]]). The pattern will be traced further when Joshua and Judges are reached.

**Joshua 24 now ingested.** Joshua 24:31 says Israel served Yahweh in the days of the elders who
*had known all the work of Yahweh*; Judges 2:7 has *had seen all the great work*, and 2:10 the
generation that *didn't know*. Joshua 24 ends on that faithful generation and three burials;
Judges uses the same notice to introduce the forgetting. The Joshua 24:29 and Judges 2:8, 2:10
quotations above have been checked against stored WEB and bible-api.com (`translation=web`),
2026-11-12. The *wayyāqom* identifications in the table and the Egyptian-lifespan remark were not
checked in this pass. See [[Joshua's Death Told Twice - Joshua 24.28-31 and Judges 2.6-9]].
