## Analysis

### The song now delivered

In Deuteronomy 32:26–27 Yahweh himself restrains threatened erasure lest enemies claim victory for their own hand. Unlike the earlier intercessions, no prayer by Moses intervenes within this part of the song. Concern for what enemies would conclude is voiced as divine deliberation.

Twice Yahweh offers to destroy Israel and start again with Moses, and both times Moses refuses to
discuss it.

**Deuteronomy 9 borrows from Numbers 14.** *Mibbəlî yəkōlet YHWH lahăbîʾām* (Deuteronomy 9:28, WLC)
is nearly the same as *mibbiltî yəkōlet YHWH ləhābîʾ* (Numbers 14:16, WLC), yet Deuteronomy 9 is
telling the story of the calf, and Exodus 32 has no such words. So the retelling of one intercession
carries the language of the other. Whether that reflects two prayers in one memory cannot be said
from the text.

**The offer is Abraham's.** *Gôy gādôl* is the promise of Genesis 12:2. Each offer would restart
that promise in Moses, and so would keep it. Moses' refusal is not an argument that Yahweh would be
breaking his word. His argument is about what the watching world would conclude.

**Numbers 14 goes further than Exodus 32 in one way.** Exodus has him ask Yahweh to remember a sworn
promise. Numbers has him quote Yahweh's account of himself, including the clause against him.
Between the two, Moses has heard Exodus 34:6–7, and in the wiki's sequence he uses it at the first
opportunity.

**The result differs.** Exodus 32 has relenting (32:14), and later Yahweh strikes the people (32:35). Numbers 14 ends
with pardon and a sentence. In both, Moses gets what he asked for, the survival of the people, and
not everything else.

**Later voices.** Joshua makes the same move at Ai (Joshua 7:9), with *the Canaanites and all the
inhabitants of the land will hear of it*. Ezekiel 20:14 has Yahweh saying he did what Moses asked,
*for my name's sake*, *that it should not be profaned in the sight of the nations*, and does not
mention Moses at all. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

## Significance

- Moses' strongest prayers are not made on Israel's behalf as deserving people. They rest on
  Yahweh's name.
- The two offers show that the promise did not depend on any one generation, and Moses still would
  not accept that as a reason to lose this one.
