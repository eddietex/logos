# The Unpruned Vine and the Unshaved Head - Leviticus 25 and Numbers 6

## Passages involved

- [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]] — Leviticus 25:4–5, 11.
- [[The Nazirite Vow]] — Numbers 6:3–5.

## Analysis

By Strong's tags (WLC), *nāzîr* is used twice of a plant: *ʿinnəbê nəzîreḵā*, the grapes of *your
undressed vine* (Leviticus 25:5), and *nəzīreyhā*, *the undressed vines* (25:11). Verse 4 supplies the
sense, since it forbids pruning the vineyard in that year. All the other uses of the noun are of
people, including the Nazirite of Numbers 6 and Joseph in Genesis 49:26.

The two laws then line up in a way neither one states. In the Sabbath year, the vine is not cut and its
grapes are not gathered. For the Nazirite, the head is not cut and grapes are not taken. Both last for a
set time, and both are given over to Yahweh, since Leviticus 25:4 calls it *a Sabbath to Yahweh* and
Numbers 6:2 says *to Yahweh*.

This should not be stretched. Leviticus does not mention the Nazirite, and Numbers does not mention the
vine's rest, and the word may simply describe anything left apart. Which use came first cannot be
told from these passages. That the Nazirite's head was compared with a vine is the wiki's inference.

## Significance

In one word, the land's rest and a person's vow are both something left uncut for Yahweh. See
[[Holiness]], [[Sabbath]], and [[The Vine and the Cup]].
