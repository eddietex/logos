# Three Tithe Laws - Leviticus 27, Numbers 18, and Deuteronomy 14

## Passages involved
- [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]] — Leviticus 27:30–33
- [[The Duties and Portions of Priests and Levites]] — Numbers 18:21–32
- [[Tithes for Feasting and Provision]] — Deuteronomy 14:22–29
- [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]] — Deuteronomy 12:6, 11, 17–18
- Deuteronomy 26:12–15 — the third-year tithe declared; not yet ingested

## Analysis

The Torah commands a tithe three times, and each time it goes somewhere different.

| | Leviticus 27:30–33 | Numbers 18:21–32 | Deuteronomy 14:22–29 |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is tithed | seed of the land, fruit of the trees, herds and flocks | *"all the tithe in Israel"* | the increase of the seed: grain, new wine, oil |
| Whose it is | *"Yahweh's. It is holy to Yahweh"* (30) | the Levites', *"for an inheritance"* (21, 24) | eaten by the household before Yahweh (23) |
| Who eats it | not said | the Levites and their households, *"anywhere"* (31), after a tenth of it goes to Aaron (26–28) | the giver's household and the Levite, at the chosen place; every third year, the Levite, foreigner, fatherless, and widow in the towns (26–29) |
| Redemption or exchange | produce may be redeemed with a fifth added; herd and flock may not (31–33) | — | produce may be turned into money for the journey, with no fifth (24–26) |

All three use *maʿăśēr* (WLC Leviticus 27:30; Numbers 18:21; Deuteronomy 14:23, 28, morphhb).
None refers to the others.

**The differences are recorded, not resolved.** Three ways of relating them are commonly given:

1. **More than one tithe.** Later Jewish practice counted a first tithe for the Levites (Numbers
   18), a second tithe eaten at the sanctuary (Deuteronomy 14:22–27), and in certain years a tithe
   for the poor (Deuteronomy 14:28–29; 26:12). The wiki has not checked the rabbinic or other
   ancient sources for this here and states it only as a widely reported tradition.
2. **Successive legislation.** Deuteronomy revises an earlier law for a people with one distant
   sanctuary. This is a common modern reading; the texts themselves do not say so.
3. **One tithe seen from different sides.** Leviticus names the owner (Yahweh), Numbers the
   standing recipient (the Levite), Deuteronomy the meal. This leaves Deuteronomy's household eating
   what Numbers gives to the Levites, which the texts do not explain.

Two points stand whatever the reconciliation. The Levite is a recipient in Numbers and in
Deuteronomy, both times because he has no inheritance (Numbers 18:24; Deuteronomy 14:27, 29). And
Deuteronomy drops Leviticus' fifth: its money rule is not a redemption from holiness but a way to
carry the value to the place.

A related tension, outside the tithe: Deuteronomy 14:23 sets the firstborn of herd and flock on the
household's table, while Numbers 18:17–18 gives the meat of the clean firstborn to Aaron. See the
Notes of [[Tithes for Feasting and Provision]].

## Significance

[[Tithing]] in the Torah is not one rule but a cluster, and the cluster serves three ends at once:
holiness to Yahweh, support for the landless Levite ([[Priesthood]]), and feasting with provision
for [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]]. Any later appeal to "the tithe" has to choose among them.
See also [[The Tithe Before the Law]].
