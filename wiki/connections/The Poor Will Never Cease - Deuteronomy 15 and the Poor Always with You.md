## Analysis

**Deuteronomy 15 makes two statements about the poor, six verses apart.** Verse 4: *"However there
shall be no poor with you"*, because Yahweh will bless Israel in the land, *"if only you diligently
listen"* (4–5). Verse 11: *"For the poor will never cease out of the land. Therefore I command you
to surely open your hand."* Both use *ʾebyôn* (WLC 15:4, 11, morphhb). Verse 4 is a promise under a
condition; verse 11 is a realistic statement that grounds a command. The chapter does not reconcile
them, and the wiki does not either.

**The Gospels' saying is commonly read as an echo of 15:11.** In each, by the usual summary of the
setting, Jesus answers an objection that ointment poured on him could have been sold for the poor;
only the four verses quoted here were fetched, so that setting is reported, not checked. The
wording is close to Deuteronomy in sense — the poor will always be there — and **Mark alone adds *"whenever you want
to, you can do them good"***, which keeps something of Deuteronomy's command to open the hand. None
of the three cites Deuteronomy or marks a quotation. Whether the saying alludes to 15:11 is an
interpretive judgment; the Greek of the Gospels and of the Septuagint was not compared in this pass.

**Acts 4:34 is sometimes read as Deuteronomy 15:4 fulfilled**: in the community it describes *"neither
was there among them any who lacked"*, because owners of lands or houses sold them and brought the
proceeds. In the WEB the resemblance is one of sense, not of wording. A verbal link between Acts
4:34 and the Greek of Deuteronomy 15:4 has been proposed by commentators; it was not checked against
a Septuagint edition here, and is reported, not asserted.

**Read together, the two New Testament passages take up both sides of Deuteronomy 15.** The Gospels
repeat verse 11's realism. Acts describes a community where verse 4's promise held. Neither text says
it is resolving the tension in Deuteronomy, and the wiki does not present them as doing so.

## Significance

- A rare case where an inner tension in one Old Testament law is answered, in sense, by two separate
  New Testament passages.
- The Gospel saying is often quoted to excuse inaction toward the poor. In Deuteronomy, the poor
  always being there is the reason for the command to give: *"Therefore I command you to surely open
  your hand"* (15:11).
