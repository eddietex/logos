# Meal Offerings Eaten and Wholly Burned

## Passages involved

- [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]] — Leviticus 6:24–7:10.

- [[The Meal Offering]] — Leviticus 2:1–16, especially 3 and 10.
- [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]] — Leviticus 6:14–23.

## Analysis

Leviticus 2 repeatedly assigns the meal offering's remainder to Aaron and his sons.
Leviticus 6:14–18 develops that rule: the memorial is burned, and male Aaronides eat the
remainder without yeast in the court of the Tent of Meeting. The portion is God's gift
and most holy; eating it is an authorized use of holy food.

Leviticus 6:19–23 then distinguishes the priest's own offering. Aaron's anointing, morning
and evening portions, and the anointed successor frame the prescription, whose closing
rule requires every priest's meal offering to be wholly burned and never eaten. A statement
that all meal offerings leave a priestly remainder would conflict with 6:23. The earlier
rule must retain this explicit qualification; the two destinations are not interchangeable.

The text supplies no explanation that priests must not benefit from their own gifts, even
if readers find that rationale plausible. Nor does it settle every scheduling question
raised by the combination of anointing-day and perpetual language in 6:20. The contrast
between eating and whole burning is explicit even where the schedule invites interpretation.

### Priestly portions — Leviticus 6:24–7:10

Leviticus 7:9 assigns oven, pan, and griddle offerings to the officiating priest, while
7:10 assigns every meal offering mixed with oil or dry to all Aaron’s sons equally.
There is a wording tension: Leviticus 2:4–7 also puts oil in prepared offerings. Reading
7:10 as unprepared grain fits the contrast but adds a qualification not explicit in the
WEB. Preserve both allocation clauses without pretending the distribution is fully
explained. Neither clause cancels the prohibition on eating a priest’s own offering (6:23).

Source for this addition: stored WEB Leviticus 2; 4; 6:24–7:10.

## Significance

[[Sacrifice and Offering]] depends on the offerer as well as the material. [[Priesthood]]
involves both receiving holy provision and making offerings without an edible remainder.
[[Holiness]] does not mean that food can never be eaten; it determines its authorized use.
