# Hanged on a Tree - Genesis 40, Deuteronomy 21, and Galatians 3

## Analysis

### The five kings at Makkedah — Joshua 10:16–27

Joshua 10:26 explicitly places killing before hanging in the stored WEB. Verse 27 removes
the bodies at sunset and returns them to the cave under great stones. This is firmer evidence
for postmortem display here than Joshua 8:29 alone provides; neither verse specifies a device
or permits certainty about impalement. The sunset removal parallels the king of Ai and accords
with Deuteronomy 21:22–23. The hiding place has become the burial place.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:16–27; Joshua 8:29; Deuteronomy 21:22–23. This addition makes
no claim about exact Hebrew wording.

See [[The Five Kings in the Cave at Makkedah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:16–27.


***Tālâ ʿal-ʿēṣ***, *hang on a tree*, enters the Bible in an Egyptian prison, as one clause of a dream
interpretation, and it is **almost certainly not what an English reader pictures.** Hanging by the neck
as a method of execution is barely attested in the ancient Near East; what the Old Testament describes
throughout is **impalement, or the public display of a body already dead.** Joshua 8:29 hangs the king
of Ai and then takes the corpse down; 2 Samuel 4:12 hangs severed hands and feet. **Genesis 40:19 makes
the point itself** — *"and the birds will eat your flesh from off you"* — **which requires an exposed
body and not a suspended living man.**

**What the idiom is for, everywhere it occurs, is the denial of burial.** That is why the birds are in
the sentence, and why the threat is legible without explanation to a reader who knows Deuteronomy 28:26,
Goliath's boast at 1 Samuel 17:44, or Jeremiah 7:33. See [[Burial and the Grave]]. **The baker's
sentence is not merely that he dies; it is that nothing will be left to bury.**

**Then the Torah legislates the practice it inherited.**

> **22** If a man has committed a sin worthy of death, and he is put to death, and you hang him on a
> tree, **23** his body shall not remain all night on the tree, but you shall surely bury him the same
> day; for he who is hanged is accursed of God; that you don't defile your land which Yahweh your God
> gives you for an inheritance. (Deuteronomy 21:22–23)

**Three things in the law are worth noticing against Genesis 40.**

1. **The hanging follows the execution.** *"He is put to death, and you hang him on a tree"* — **the
   tree is the display, not the killing.** Which is exactly the order Genesis 40:19 implies: the head is
   lifted off first.
2. **The law does not forbid the practice; it time-limits it.** One day, and then burial. **Joshua obeys
   this twice, at sunset both times** (8:29; 10:26–27) — which is the Deuteronomic historian showing his
   working.
3. **The curse is not on the crime; it is on the state of the body.** ***Qilelat ʾĕlōhîm tālûy*** — a
   compressed phrase, and the ambiguity of the Hebrew genitive is real: *the curse of God* is either
   *God's curse resting on him* or *an affront to God*. **Both readings are ancient and the wiki adopts
   neither**; what both agree on is that a body left up overnight defiles the land.

**Esther turns the thread into a plot.** The gallows Haman builds fifty cubits high (5:14) is the same
verb; **he is hanged on it himself (7:10)**, and the reversal is the book's central mechanism. **Esther
is also the other biblical book set in a foreign court in which two royal officers offend a king and a
Jewish outsider's reading of events decides who survives** — 2:21–23 has two doorkeepers hanged for a
plot Mordecai reports, **and then the king cannot sleep, has the chronicles read to him, and remembers
the man he forgot to reward** (6:1–3). **The resemblance to Genesis 40:23 and 41:9 is close enough to
notice and the wiki notes it without arguing a dependence.**

**And then Paul picks up Deuteronomy 21:23 and puts it at the centre of the gospel.**

> **13** Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us. For it is written,
> "Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree." (Galatians 3:13)

**Crucifixion is not the practice Deuteronomy 21 legislates** — it is a Roman method of killing, not
a Jewish method of displaying the already-dead — **but the verse's language fits it exactly**, and
readers in the first century applied it that way. **The apostolic preaching in Acts uses *hanging on
a tree* as its standing shorthand for the crucifixion** (5:30; 10:39; 13:29), which is a deliberate
choice: **it is the one phrase that makes a Roman execution audible in Torah vocabulary.** And John
19:31, with the bodies taken down before the Sabbath, **is Deuteronomy 21:23 being obeyed at the
foot of the cross.**

## Significance

**The thread runs from a baker's dream to the centre of Paul's argument about the law, and every link in
it is the same three Hebrew words.**

Its usefulness is that it keeps two things from being confused. **Deuteronomy 21:23 does not say that
being executed makes a man accursed** — it says that a body left hanging is a defilement, and requires
it to be taken down. **Paul's argument depends on the verse meaning something stronger**, and he states
the stronger sense without softening it: *"having become a curse for us."* **The wiki records that the
step from the law's plain sense to Galatians 3:13 is a step, and records that Paul takes it openly.**

The Genesis end of the thread contributes something the later texts assume: **the horror in the idiom is
about burial, not about pain.** Genesis 40:19 spends half its clause on the birds. The law's remedy is
burial. Rizpah's vigil (2 Samuel 21:10) is a mother keeping birds and beasts off seven bodies for
months, **and it is the most direct commentary on Genesis 40:19 in Scripture** — the same threat, the
same scavengers, from the other side.

**Nothing in Genesis 40 anticipates any of this.** The baker is a minor Egyptian official in a chapter
about whether an interpreter is reliable, **and the wiki claims no typology.** What it claims is
lexical: the phrase the New Testament uses of the cross enters Scripture here.


### Peor now ingested

Numbers 25:4 commands exposure of the chiefs before Yahweh and the sun; verse 5 addresses
the judges and men attached to Baal Peor. No tree is specified here, and the chapter does
not report completion of these commands. This must not become an asserted method of
execution or a narrated hanging of every chief.

### Deuteronomy 21 now ingested

See [[Captives, Firstborn Sons, and Public Shame]]. Two points above are now checked against WLC (morphhb). The order of 21:22 is
*wǝhûmāt wǝtālîtā ʾōtô ʿal-ʿēṣ*, *he is put to death, and you hang him on a tree* — death first, then
display. And *tālâ* occurs in the Torah only at Genesis 40:19, 22; 41:13 and Deuteronomy 21:22–23 (lemma
8518), so the thread's Torah links are complete. The body is called *nǝbēlâ*, otherwise the Torah's word
for a carcass. Galatians 3:13's *"Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree"* differs in English from the
WEB of 21:23, *"accursed of God"*; the Greek of Galatians and of the Septuagint was not checked, so the
source of Paul's wording is left open.

### Joshua 8 now ingested

See [[The Capture of Ai]]. The wording is checked against WLC (morphhb): the king *tālâ ʿal-hāʿēṣ ʿaḏ
ʿēṯ hāʿāreḇ*, *hanged on the tree until the evening*, and at sundown (*ûḵǝḇôʾ haššemeš*) Joshua has
*niḇlātô*, *his carcass*, taken down — the *nǝḇēlâ* of Deuteronomy 21:23. The verse does not say how
the king died, and the order of Deuteronomy 21:22 (death, then display) makes display the natural
reading. The body is then thrown at the city gate and covered with a heap of stones, so the burial
the law requires is a cairn, not a tomb. Source: stored WEB Joshua 8:29; WLC retrieved 2026-11-10.
