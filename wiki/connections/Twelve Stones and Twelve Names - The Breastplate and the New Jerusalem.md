# Twelve Stones and Twelve Names - The Breastplate and the New Jerusalem

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 28:9–12, 17–21** — two onyx stones with six names each on the ephod's shoulders, and
  twelve stones *"according to the names of the children of Israel … for the twelve tribes"* on
  the breastplate — [[The Priestly Garments]].
- **Genesis 2:12** — *"Bdellium and onyx stone are also there"*, in the land of Havilah —
  [[The Garden of Eden]].
- **Ezekiel 28:13** — *"You were in Eden, the garden of God. Every precious stone adorned you"*,
  followed by a list of nine stones *(verified WEB, not yet ingested)*.
- **Revelation 21:12–14, 19–20** — twelve gates bearing *"the names of the twelve tribes of the
  children of Israel"*, twelve foundations bearing *"twelve names of the twelve Apostles of the
  Lamb"*, and the foundations adorned with twelve precious stones *(verified WEB, not yet
  ingested)*.
- [[The Priestly Garments Made]] — Exodus 39:1–31; Named shoulder and breastplate stones made; English gem list matches chapter 28.

## Analysis

**The English lists overlap heavily.** In the WEB, Ezekiel 28:13 names ruby, topaz, emerald,
chrysolite, onyx, jasper, sapphire, turquoise, and beryl. All nine also appear in the WEB of
Exodus 28:17–20, which adds the third row — jacinth, agate, amethyst — for twelve. Revelation
21:19–20 shares eight English names with Exodus 28: jasper, sapphire, emerald, chrysolite,
beryl, topaz, jacinth, and amethyst; its other four are chalcedony, sardonyx, sardius, and
chrysoprasus.

**That overlap is a translation-level observation, not a mineral identification.** Ancient gem
names are uncertain, translators render them differently, and Revelation's list is Greek while
Exodus and Ezekiel are Hebrew. This page does not claim that the same Hebrew or Greek term sits
behind each shared English name; no source-language edition was checked in this pass. Genesis
2:12 and Exodus 28:9, 20 likewise share only the English label *onyx* here.

**The stronger link is structural: stones joined to names.** In Exodus the twelve tribal names
are engraved on stones and carried by the high priest into the holy place. In Revelation the
twelve tribal names stand on the city's gates, the apostles' names on its foundations, and the
foundations are themselves precious stones. Revelation does not cite Exodus 28, and it puts the
tribes' names on the gates, not on the stones. The correspondence is an allusion a reader may
draw, not a stated fulfilment.

**Ezekiel sets stones in Eden on a figure addressed in a lament.** Ezekiel 28:13 places a
covering of precious stones on the one *"in Eden, the garden of God"*. It does not mention the
priesthood or the breastplate. The resemblance between that list and Aaron's breastplate is
noted here and left open until Ezekiel is ingested.

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

The stones specified in chapter 28 are now worked, set, and engraved (39:6–14). The stored
WEB’s twelve gem labels in 39:10–13 match 28:17–20 in the same order; the engraved names again
refer to the twelve tribes without enumerating them. Manufacture strengthens the local connection
between names and stones without changing the later comparison’s limits: it does not establish
mineral identities or add a claim that Revelation directly quotes this passage.

## Significance

Exodus 28 puts Israel's names on precious stones twice — once on the shoulders, once on the
heart — and makes the priest the one who brings them *"before Yahweh"*. Read alongside the
garden of Genesis 2 and the city of Revelation 21, the same materials mark a place where God
dwells. The wiki records the lists and the shared English labels and leaves their significance
to be tested as Ezekiel and Revelation are ingested. See also
