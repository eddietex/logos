# Show Yourself to the Priest - Leviticus 14 and Mark 1

## Passages involved

- [[The Cleansing of the Leper]] — Leviticus 14:1–32.
- Mark 1:40–45 — verified WEB; Gospel passage not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Mark narrates a man with leprosy asking Jesus for cleansing. Jesus touches him and the
leprosy departs (1:40–42). Jesus then directs him to show himself to the priest and offer
what Moses commanded for cleansing (44). Leviticus 14 supplies the law for that priestly
examination and offering after healing; Mark does not enumerate the animals or report
completion of the prescribed rites.

The sequence matters: Leviticus 14:3 presupposes healing when the priest examines, and in
Mark the healing precedes the instruction to visit the priest. Jesus’ act and the priest’s
examination thus have different narrative functions. The man’s subsequent publicity keeps
Jesus from entering a city openly (45); Mark explains that circumstance by the spreading
report, not by pronouncing Jesus ritually unclean.

## Significance

The Gospel’s reference to Moses gives the cleansing law a specific later reception.
It does not warrant treating Leviticus as a procedure that itself heals disease, or treating
the offering as unnecessary in Jesus’ instruction. See [[Clean and Unclean]] and [[Priesthood]].
