# My Beloved Is Mine - Song of Solomon 2.16, 6.3, and 7.10

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 2:16** — [[Rise Up, My Love]]: *"My beloved is mine, and I am his. He browses
  among the lilies."* (*dôḏî lî waʾănî lô*)
- **Song of Solomon 6:3** *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)* — *"I am my beloved's, and my beloved is
  mine. He browses among the lilies,"* (*ʾănî ləḏôḏî wəḏôḏî lî*)
- **Song of Solomon 7:10** *(stored WEB, not yet ingested; Hebrew 7:11)* — *"I am my beloved's. His
  desire is toward me."* (*ʾănî ləḏôḏî wəʿālay təšûqāṯô*)
- **Genesis 3:16** — [[The Fall]]: *"Your desire will be for your husband, and he will rule over
  you."* (*wəʾel-ʾîšēḵ təšûqāṯēḵ*)
- **Genesis 4:7** — [[Cain and Abel]]: *"Its desire is for you, but you are to rule over it."*
  (*wəʾēleyḵā təšûqāṯô*)

Hebrew of all five read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

## Analysis

**One statement, said three times, changing each time.** The woman says the belonging formula
three times in the Song, and each version differs.

| | Song 2:16 | Song 6:3 | Song 7:10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| First half | *my beloved is mine* | *I am my beloved's* | *I am my beloved's* |
| Second half | *and I am his* | *and my beloved is mine* | *his desire is toward me* |
| Addition | *He browses among the lilies* | *He browses among the lilies* | — |

At 2:16 she begins with what she has: he is hers. At 6:3 the order is reversed, and she begins with
whom she belongs to. At 7:10 the second half is no longer *he is mine*. It is *his desire is toward
me*. The wiki records the sequence; it does not claim a development of the relationship from the
order of the words alone, since the Song is not plainly a single story.

**The desire word.** *Təšûqâ*, *desire*, occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible by the usual count:
Genesis 3:16, Genesis 4:7, and Song 7:10 (Hebrew 7:11). The count is a standard observation that
this page has not verified by a full concordance search; the three verses themselves were read in
Sefaria. In Genesis 3:16 the woman's desire is for her husband, and *he will rule over you*
(*yimšāl-bāḵ*). In Genesis 4:7 sin's desire is for Cain, and *you are to rule over it* (*timšāl-bô*).
In both, *desire* is paired with *rule*. In Song 7:10 the desire is the man's, it is toward the
woman, and no one rules.

Many readers take Song 7:10 as a deliberate reversal of Genesis 3:16. The direction of desire is
reversed and the ruling is gone. The wiki records the reading as an observation about a rare word.
It does not claim the Song quotes Genesis, because the Song names neither Eden nor the curse. The
Genesis pair is laid out on [[Desire and Rule - Genesis 3.16 and 4.7]], which already notes that the
Song's occurrence is ordinary desire, with no contest and no *māšal*. Song 7 is not yet ingested, so
this page sets out the three verses and leaves the reading of 7:10 itself for that ingest.

## Significance

The formula puts mutual possession in the woman's mouth. In Genesis 3:16 the relation between
the man and the woman is described from outside, as part of a sentence. In the Song the woman says it
herself, three times, about her own beloved. See [[Love]], [[Marriage]], and
[[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]].

The formula's shape — *mine* and *his* — is close in form to the covenant formula, *"I will take
you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God"* (Exodus 6:7); see
[[I Will Take You as My People - The Covenant Formula]]. That comparison is common in readings of
the Song as a picture of God and Israel. It is noted here and not developed. The Song itself does
not name God in 2:16, and the wiki does not read the formula allegorically on the strength of its
shape alone.
