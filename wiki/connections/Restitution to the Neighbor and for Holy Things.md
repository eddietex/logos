# Restitution to the Neighbor and for Holy Things

## Passages involved

- [[Laws of Property and Restitution]] — Exodus 22:1–15.
- [[The Trespass Offering for Holy Things and Unknown Guilt]] — Leviticus 5:14–19.

- [[Restitution for Fraud and False Oaths]] — Leviticus 6:1–7.
- [[Eating the Holy Things]] — Leviticus 22:14.

## Analysis

Exodus 22 distinguishes deliberate theft from damage, entrusted property, and loans.
The sold or slaughtered ox and sheep bring fivefold and fourfold repayments; live stolen
livestock bring double. Grazing damage and fire call for compensation, while some animal
losses exempt the keeper. Restitution is therefore not a single uniform penalty.

Leviticus 5:15–16 addresses unwitting wrong in Yahweh’s holy things. The wrong must be
made good with an additional fifth given to the priest; an unblemished ram is also
required, and priestly atonement concludes with forgiveness. Material repair and the
sacrificial remedy belong together. Neither can be substituted for the other in this case.

The Exodus theft cases and Leviticus 5 holy-things case address different offenses.
Exodus 22 does not prescribe this ram rite in the compared unit. Conversely, the holy-things
case is not described as a thief selling a stolen animal. Leviticus 5:17–19 retains the ram
for wrongdoing without knowledge but does not repeat the restitution amount.


### The injured neighbor and overlapping penalties

Leviticus 6:1–7 extends the ram-and-restitution pattern to fraud, robbery, oppression,
lost property dishonestly retained, and false swearing. The principal plus a fifth goes
to the owner; the priest receives the ram and makes atonement. The wrong against the
neighbor is explicitly a trespass against Yahweh (2). This joins material repair and
forgiveness without making the priest the recipient of the neighbor’s compensation.

**Unresolved tension:** Exodus 22:1–9 requires multiplied payments in theft and disputed
property cases; Leviticus 6:1–5 requires an added fifth in cases including robbery and
fraud. Some situations overlap. Neither passage explains the interaction of the rates
or explicitly supplies a voluntary-confession versus judicial-conviction distinction.
The WEB’s reference to being found guilty in 6:5 does not by itself settle that question.
This comparison preserves the stated amounts without claiming a complete harmonization.

Exodus 22:10–11 accepts an oath concerning entrusted goods where there is no witness.
Leviticus 6:3, 5 addresses false swearing about goods: the oath can itself become part
of the wrong. That is a legal comparison, not an assertion that the second passage
narrates an actual violation of the first.

### Unwitting eating of holy things — Leviticus 22:14

A man who eats something holy unwittingly adds a fifth of its value and gives the holy thing to
the priest. The fifth matches Leviticus 5:16, but 22:14 does not mention the ram of 5:15 or
priestly atonement. **Unresolved:** the text neither says the ram is assumed nor that it is
waived, and this comparison does not decide between them.

## Significance

Restitution takes the loss seriously; atonement addresses the wrong before Yahweh. This
comparison guards against treating sacrifice as a substitute for repair or treating every
repayment as the same penalty. See [[Theft and Restitution]], [[Holiness]], and [[Atonement]].
