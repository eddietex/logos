# Never Satisfied - Proverbs 27.20, 30.15-16, Ecclesiastes 1.8, and Habakkuk 2.5

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 27:20** — *"Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; and a man's eyes are never
  satisfied."* — [[Do Not Boast About Tomorrow]]
- **Proverbs 27:7** — *"A full soul loathes a honeycomb; but to a hungry soul, every bitter thing is
  sweet."* — [[Do Not Boast About Tomorrow]]
- **Proverbs 1:12** — the gang's *"Let's swallow them up alive like Sheol"* — [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]]
- **Proverbs 30:15–16** *(not yet ingested)* — *"There are three things that are never satisfied; four
  that don't say, 'Enough:' Sheol, the barren womb; the earth that is not satisfied with water; and the
  fire that doesn't say, 'Enough.'"*
- **Ecclesiastes 1:8** *(not yet ingested)* — *"The eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled
  with hearing."*
- **Isaiah 5:14** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Therefore Sheol has enlarged its desire, and opened its mouth
  without measure"*
- **Habakkuk 2:5** *(not yet ingested)* — the haughty man *"who enlarges his desire as Sheol, and he is
  like death, and can't be satisfied"*

## Analysis

**Proverbs 27:20 sets the human eye beside the underworld.** The verb *lōʾ ṯiśbaʿnāh*, *they are not
satisfied*, is repeated for both halves: Sheol and Abaddon, and *ʿênê hāʾāḏām*, the eyes of man (Hebrew
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-26). Death keeps taking, and so does the eye.
The same chapter uses the root *śbʿ* at 27:7 for the *full* soul that loathes honey: satiety is possible
for the stomach, and the saying sets it against an appetite that has no such limit.

**The motif recurs in four other places with the same root or image.** Proverbs 30:15–16 lists Sheol
first among the four things that never say *"Enough"*, and its *earth that is not satisfied* uses *śbʿ*
(*lōʾ-śāḇəʿāh*). Ecclesiastes 1:8 has the eye again, *lōʾ-ṯiśbaʿ ʿayin lirʾôṯ*, *the eye is not satisfied
with seeing* — the noun and verb of Proverbs 27:20, in the book of the restless round. Isaiah 5:14 and
Habakkuk 2:5 picture Sheol's open mouth and enlarged *desire*; Habakkuk applies it to a conqueror who is
*"like death, and can't be satisfied"*. The Hebrew of Isaiah 5:14 and Habakkuk 2:5 was not checked, and
no claim is made that they share the verb.

**Two uses: warning and diagnosis.** In Proverbs 1:12 the gang wants to *be* Sheol, swallowing the
innocent alive; Habakkuk's conqueror is compared to it. There the insatiable appetite is a vice taken
on. In Proverbs 27:20 and Ecclesiastes 1:8 it is a condition: the eye simply does not fill. The wiki
records both without merging them.

The motif ties [[Sheol]] to [[Envy]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]]: the craving that does not stop is
described in the same words as death. See also
