## Analysis

**Proverbs 27:20 sets the human eye beside the underworld.** The verb *lōʾ ṯiśbaʿnāh*, *they are not
satisfied*, is repeated for both halves: Sheol and Abaddon, and *ʿênê hāʾāḏām*, the eyes of man (Hebrew
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-26). Death keeps taking, and so does the eye.
The same chapter uses the root *śbʿ* at 27:7 for the *full* soul that loathes honey: satiety is possible
for the stomach, and the saying sets it against an appetite that has no such limit.

**The motif recurs in four other places with the same root or image.** Proverbs 30:15–16 lists Sheol
first among the four things that never say *"Enough"*, and its *earth that is not satisfied* uses *śbʿ*
(*lōʾ-śāḇəʿāh*). Ecclesiastes 1:8 has the eye again, *lōʾ-ṯiśbaʿ ʿayin lirʾôṯ*, *the eye is not satisfied
with seeing* — the noun and verb of Proverbs 27:20, in the book of the restless round.
The Ecclesiastes wording was independently checked in Mechon-Mamre's Masoretic Hebrew text,
Ecclesiastes 1:8, on 2026-10-27 (source: `https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt3101.htm`);
its English column is not used as WEB. Isaiah 5:14 and
Habakkuk 2:5 picture Sheol's open mouth and enlarged *desire*; Habakkuk applies it to a conqueror who is
*"like death, and can't be satisfied"*. The Hebrew of Isaiah 5:14 and Habakkuk 2:5 was not checked, and
no claim is made that they share the verb.

**Two uses: warning and diagnosis.** In Proverbs 1:12 the gang wants to *be* Sheol, swallowing the
innocent alive; Habakkuk's conqueror is compared to it. There the insatiable appetite is a vice taken
on. In Proverbs 27:20 and Ecclesiastes 1:8 it is a condition: the eye simply does not fill. The wiki
records both without merging them.

### Agur's list — Proverbs 30:15–16

The list is now ingested. It opens with a leech whose *"two daughters"* say only *"Give, give"*, then
counts four things that do not say *"Enough"* (*hôn*). The first two, Sheol and *ʿōṣer rāḥam*, the
barren womb, are given in a single half-line, so death's taking and the womb's longing are set side
by side; the earth and the fire follow. Verse 15's *lōʾ ṯiśbaʿnāh*, *they are not satisfied*, is the
verb of 27:20; within verse 16 only the earth repeats *śbʿ*, the fire repeats *"doesn't say"* *hôn*,
and Sheol and the womb stand without a verb of their own (Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-27).
Unlike 1:12 and 27:20, Agur's list makes no moral point about human appetite. It records the
condition and leaves the reader to recognize it. See [[Four Things]].

### The opening poem now ingested — Ecclesiastes 1:1–11

[[Vanity of Vanities]] places the eye and ear after rivers flowing into a sea that is not full
(7–8). The image belongs to the poem's larger movement from recurring nature to human
weariness and forgetting. This expands the comparison beyond acquisitiveness: 1:8 names no
possessions or particular immoral observer. Proverbs 27:20's eye is a close comparison;
Agur's list adds further examples without making them identical to the Preacher's whole
argument. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:1–11; Proverbs 27:20; 30:15–16.

### The eye and wealth — Ecclesiastes 4:8

[[Two Are Better Than One]] brings the motif back to possessions. The man alone, with neither son
nor brother, labors without end, and his eyes are *"not satisfied with wealth"* (4:8). The Hebrew is
*lōʾ-ṯiśbaʿ ʿōšer*, the verb of 1:8 and Proverbs 27:20, now with *ʿōšer*, *wealth*, as what fails to
fill (Mechon-Mamre's Masoretic text, Ecclesiastes 4, read 2026-10-27; its consonantal text writes
*his eyes*, read as *his eye*, and the WEB follows the plural). Unlike 1:8, this is an acquisitive
case, and it is diagnosed by a question the man asks too late — *"For whom then, do I labor?"* — not
by comparison with Sheol. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:8; 4:8; Proverbs 27:20.

### The lover of silver — Ecclesiastes 5:10–11

[[The Lover of Silver and the Gift of Enjoyment]] makes the acquisitive case explicit: the lover
of silver is not satisfied with silver (10). Increased goods draw increased consumers,
and the owner is left to look at them (11). This extends 4:8's unsatisfied eyes but does
not make every act of seeing a moral fault. The same unit allows enjoyment of wealth
as God's gift (19), so the target is not possession alone. This comparison is drawn from
the stored WEB of Ecclesiastes 1:8; 4:8; 5:10–11, 19 and Proverbs 27:20. The Hebrew of
Ecclesiastes 5 was not checked; no shared source-language verb is claimed for this addition.

### The mouth and wandering desire — Ecclesiastes 6:7–9

[[Riches Without Enjoyment]] describes all labor as for the mouth, yet appetite unfilled
(7). The unit then prefers the sight of the eyes to the wandering of desire (9). This
qualifies an overly simple account of the eye as always the problem: seeing available
good may be preferable to endless craving, even though 1:8 and Proverbs 27:20 deny the
eye lasting satisfaction. The positive reading of available good is an interpretation
of the image; the vanity refrain prevents treating it as a complete remedy. No shared
Hebrew term is asserted for this addition. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:8; 4:8;
5:10–11; 6:1–9; Proverbs 27:20; 30:15–16.

## Significance

The motif ties [[Sheol]] to [[Envy]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]]: the craving that does not stop is
described in the same words as death. See also
[[Abaddon Has No Covering - Job 26.6, Proverbs 15.11, and Revelation 9.11]]. Sources: Proverbs 1:12;
27:7, 20; 30:15–16 copied from stored WEB; Ecclesiastes 1:8, Isaiah 5:14, and Habakkuk 2:5 verified at
bible-api.com with `translation=web` on 2026-10-26.
