# Hail from Heaven - Egypt and Gibeon

## Passages involved

- [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]
- [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]]
- Sources: stored WEB Exodus 9:18–26; Deuteronomy 20:1–4; Joshua 1:5; 10:1–15, as relevant below.

## Analysis

Exodus 9:18–26 and Joshua 10:10–11 both portray Yahweh sending deadly hail. In Egypt the warning
allows those who fear his word to shelter people and livestock, and Goshen is spared. Near Gibeon
hail strikes the retreating coalition and kills more than Israel’s sword. The shared weather image
serves different narratives: pressure on Pharaoh to release Israel, and aid to Israel as it rescues
Gibeon. Joshua does not repeat the advance warning or the shelter provision.

## Significance

The conquest continues the portrayal of Yahweh’s command of the sky seen in the exodus. This is a
thematic parallel, not a claim that Joshua explicitly quotes Exodus.
