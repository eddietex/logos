## Analysis

**The verb.** *Rōʿeh baṣṣōʾn* (1 Samuel 16:11, WLC), *keeping the sheep*, is the participle of *rāʿâ*, *to pasture* or
*shepherd* (morphhb lemma 7462). The same verb is in David's *lirʿôṯ ʾeṯ-ṣōʾn ʾāḇîw*, *to feed his father's sheep*
(17:15), and *rōʿeh hāyâ ʿaḇdəḵā*, *your servant was keeping* (17:34), and in the tribes' memory of Yahweh's word,
*ʾattâ tirʿeh ʾeṯ-ʿammî*, *you will shepherd my people* (2 Samuel 5:2) (WLC). 1 Samuel 16 uses it of sheep, and 2
Samuel 5 of Israel.

**From following.** Yahweh's word through Nathan, *I took you from the sheep pen, from following the sheep*, *mēʾaḥar
haṣṣōʾn* (2 Samuel 7:8, WLC), names the same place the family named in 1 Samuel 16:11. Jesse had not brought the boy
to the sacrifice. Yahweh's account of the choice begins where Jesse left him. Psalm 78 tells it as *from following the
ewes that have their young*, and ties the shepherding to *the integrity of his heart* (WEB checked; the Hebrew of the
Psalm was not checked). That brings back 1 Samuel 16:7, *Yahweh looks at the heart*.

**Earlier shepherds.** In the stored books the participle describes Abel, *a keeper of sheep*, *rōʿēh ṣōʾn* (Genesis
4:2), and Joseph at seventeen, *rōʿeh ʾeṯ-ʾeḥāw baṣṣōʾn* (Genesis 37:2) (WLC; stored WEB). Both are younger sons, and
both suffer at the hands of older brothers (Genesis 4:8; 37:18–28). Moses was *keeping the flock of Jethro* when he was called (Exodus 3:1, stored
WEB). Jacob calls God *the God who has fed me*, the first time God is a shepherd in the text (Genesis 48:15; see
[[The God Who Has Fed Me - The First Time God Is a Shepherd]]). Moses asked for a leader so that the congregation would
not be *as sheep which have no shepherd* (Numbers 27:17; see
[[Sheep Without a Shepherd - Numbers 27.17, 1 Kings 22.17, and the Gospels]]).

## Significance

The thread runs from the boy left with the flock to the king called shepherd of Israel. In 1 Samuel 16 the sheep are
the reason David is absent and overlooked. 2 Samuel and Psalm 78 later treat them as his preparation. 1 Samuel 16
itself does not draw that meaning: it says only where he was. See [[David]], [[Kingship and Empire]], and
[[Call and Election]].
