# In the Multitude of Words - Proverbs 10.19, 17.27-28, and Ecclesiastes 5.2-7

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 10:19** — *"In the multitude of words there is no lack of disobedience, but he who
  restrains his lips does wisely."* — [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]]
- **Proverbs 17:27–28** — *"He who spares his words has knowledge. He who is even tempered is a
  man of understanding. Even a fool, when he keeps silent, is counted wise. When he shuts his
  lips, he is thought to be discerning."* — [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]]
- **Ecclesiastes 3:7** — *"a time to tear, and a time to sew; a time to keep silence, and a time to
  speak;"* — [[A Time for Every Purpose]]
- **Ecclesiastes 5:2–3** — *"Don’t be rash with your mouth, and don’t let your heart be hasty to
  utter anything before God; for God is in heaven, and you on earth. Therefore let your words be
  few. For as a dream comes with a multitude of cares, so a fool’s speech with a multitude of
  words."* — [[Let Your Words Be Few]]
- **Ecclesiastes 5:7** — *"For in the multitude of dreams there are vanities, as well as in many
  words: but you must fear God."* — [[Let Your Words Be Few]]

## Analysis

**One phrase, two settings.** Proverbs 10:19 and Ecclesiastes 5:3 both speak, in the WEB, of *the
multitude of words*. In Proverbs the setting is ordinary life: much talk inevitably includes
wrongdoing, and the restrained lip is wise. Ecclesiastes moves the same observation into the house
of God. The listener there is not a neighbor but God, who *"is in heaven"*, and the mouth that runs
on can lead its owner into sin (5:6) through a vow it cannot keep.

**The fool is known by volume.** Proverbs 17:28 says even a fool who keeps silent is counted wise;
Ecclesiastes 5:3 says a fool's speech is recognized by its many words. Both assume that the amount
of speech reveals the speaker. Ecclesiastes adds a comparison neither proverb has: many words are
like a dream produced by many cares — abundant, and with nothing solid behind it.

**Silence has its time.** Ecclesiastes 3:7 had already said there is a time to keep silence and a
time to speak; see [[A Time to Keep Silence - Proverbs 15.23, 25.11, and Ecclesiastes 3.7]]. Chapter
5 names one setting where few words are the rule. It does not forbid speech before God; it asks
that speech there be deliberate.

**Where the two books differ.** Proverbs grounds restraint in wisdom and reputation. Ecclesiastes 5:7
grounds it in the fear of God and calls the many words *vanities*, using the book's keyword. See
[[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]] for the charge of
multiplied words made against Job by Elihu and then by God — a charge the book does not let stand
as its last word on Job (42:7).

## Significance

The thread links [[Language and Speech]], [[Folly]], and [[The Fear of God]]. Proverbs teaches
restraint of the tongue as a general discipline; Ecclesiastes applies it where the stakes are
highest, in speech addressed to God, and attaches the vanity verdict to speech that is merely
abundant.

**Sources.** Proverbs 10:19; 17:27–28; Ecclesiastes 3:7; 5:2–7 quoted from stored WEB. No claim is
made that the Hebrew phrases behind *multitude of words* are identical; the comparison rests on the
WEB's English and on the sense of each passage.
