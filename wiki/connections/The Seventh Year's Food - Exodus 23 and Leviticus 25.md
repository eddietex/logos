# The Seventh Year's Food - Exodus 23 and Leviticus 25

## Passages involved

- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:10–12.
- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:8–11, the household's seventh-day rest.
- [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]] — Exodus 21:2, a similar six-and-seven service pattern.
- [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]] — Leviticus 25:1–22; land rest, food, Jubilee, and provision.

## Analysis

Exodus 23:10–11 orders six years of cultivation and a seventh in which the land rests and lies
fallow. Its expressed purpose is food for the poor, followed by food for the animals from what
remains. Vineyards and olive groves are included. Verse 12 then gives the six-and-seven pattern in
days, naming refreshment for working animals, the servant's son, and the alien.

Leviticus 25:1–7 expressly calls the land's seventh year a Sabbath to Yahweh, prohibits sowing and
pruning, and limits harvesting of spontaneous produce. It then names the produce as food for the
owner, servants, hired worker, foreign resident, livestock, and wild animals. The prohibition of
reaping cannot simply mean that nobody may eat anything growing that year: the food provision
follows it.

**The beneficiaries are described differently.** Exodus foregrounds the poor and the animals after
them; Leviticus includes the owner and a fuller household and laboring population. The two passages
share the interruption of cultivation and the availability of food, but Leviticus is not a
word-for-word restatement of Exodus. Neither passage here describes ordinary commercial harvesting
or proves how the rule was historically observed.

The servant's release after six years in Exodus 21:2 supplies a related pattern. It is not
identified here with the land cycle, and these verses do not legislate a general remission of debts.
That would require comparison with additional legislation.

## Significance

[[Sabbath]] limits the power to extract labor and produce. [[Work]],
[[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], and [[Wealth and Possessions]] meet in food made available through
suspended cultivation. The comparison preserves the distinctive reasons and beneficiaries in each
formulation.
