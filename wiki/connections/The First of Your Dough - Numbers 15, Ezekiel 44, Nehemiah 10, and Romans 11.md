# The First of Your Dough - Numbers 15, Ezekiel 44, Nehemiah 10, and Romans 11

## Passages involved

- **Numbers 15:20–21** — *"Of the first of your dough you shall offer up a cake"* —
  [[Offerings in the Land and Sins of Ignorance]]
- **Nehemiah 10:37** (10:38 in WLC) — *"the first fruits of our dough"*, promised in a signed covenant
- **Ezekiel 44:30** — *"You shall also give to the priests the first of your dough, to cause a blessing
  to rest on your house"*
- **Romans 11:16** — *"If the first fruit is holy, so is the lump. If the root is holy, so are the
  branches"*

(WEB. Numbers from `raw/bible/`; the others checked at bible-api.com, `translation=web`, 2026-11-02.)

## Analysis

The noun *ʿărîsâ* occurs four times in WLC by the tags, in the three Old Testament texts above, and
the phrase with *rēʾšît* occurs in all of them (Numbers has it twice). Numbers gives the gift to
Yahweh. Nehemiah and Ezekiel say that it reaches the priests.

Ezekiel adds a result that Numbers does not state: a blessing resting on the house. So in Ezekiel the
small part given affects the whole household.

Rahlfs renders Numbers 15:20 as *ἀπαρχὴν φυράματος ὑμῶν*. Romans 11:16 in SBLGNT reads *εἰ δὲ ἡ ἀπαρχὴ
ἁγία, καὶ τὸ φύραμα*. Both Greek nouns are the same. Paul quotes no text here, and the verbal link is
to the Greek of Numbers; that he has this law in mind is a likely inference, not a stated fact. Numbers
says nothing about the rest of the dough becoming holy. That step is Paul's own.

## Significance

The rule is a very small one, a single cake from ordinary bread, and it reaches into the kitchen as
the threshing-floor offering reaches the field. Its later history shows it kept after exile and
turned into an argument about Israel.
