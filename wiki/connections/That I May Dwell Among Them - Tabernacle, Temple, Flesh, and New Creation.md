## Analysis

**One word runs through the thread: Hebrew *šākan*, *to dwell*, and Greek *skēnē*, *tent*.**

**In Exodus the verb moves.** At 24:16 the glory *šākan* on the mountain (MAM). At 25:8 God says
*wəšākantî bətôkām*, *I will dwell in their midst* (MAM), and the next verse names the structure
***miškān***, *dwelling place* (25:9; MAM). At 29:45 the same verb, *wəšākantî bətôk bənê yiśrāʾēl*, is
joined to the covenant formula: *"and will be their God"* (MAM). **The mountain the people could not
touch becomes a tent they camp around.** Its first furniture is a place to speak from: *"There I will
meet with you"* (*wənôʿadtî*, 25:22; MAM), over the ark. The dwelling's first function is speech.

**Leviticus puts the tent and the formula together.** *"I will set my tent (miškānî) among you"*
(26:11; MAM), followed by *"I will walk among you, and will be your God, and you will be my people"*
(26:12). The verb *walk* is the hitpael of *hālak* — *wəhithallaktî* here, and *mithallēk* of God in
the garden at Genesis 3:8 (both MAM). The wiki notes the shared verb and does not build an argument on
it.

**Solomon's temple raises the problem the tabernacle leaves open.** The cloud fills the house as it
filled the tent (1 Kings 8:10–11), and Solomon then asks whether God can live on earth at all:
*"heaven and the heaven of heavens can't contain you; how much less this house"* (8:27). **The temple
prayer holds both claims at once** — God's glory really fills the house, and no house can contain
him.

**John uses the tent word.** *"The Word became flesh, and lived among us"* (John 1:14, WEB). The Greek
verb is ***eskēnōsen*** (SBLGNT), literally *pitched a tent* or *tabernacled*, from *skēnē*, the
Septuagint's word for the tabernacle (Exodus 25:9, *tēs skēnēs*, Rahlfs). The next clause, *"We saw
his glory"*, puts the glory where the tent is, as in Exodus 40:34. The sound-likeness of Greek
*skēnē* and Hebrew *šākan* (*s-k-n* / *š-k-n*) is often noted; whether John intended it cannot be
shown, and the wiki records it only as an observation.

**Revelation ends the thread with the same words.** *"God's dwelling (hē skēnē tou theou) is with
people, and he will dwell (skēnōsei) with them"* (Revelation 21:3; SBLGNT). The verse continues with
the covenant formula, as Exodus 29:45 and Leviticus 26:12 do. **The last dwelling has no
building**: *"I saw no temple in it, for the Lord God, the Almighty, and the Lamb, are its temple"*
(Revelation 21:22; *not yet ingested*).

**A textual note on Revelation 21:3.** The WEB's *"a loud voice out of heaven"* and *"they will be his
people"* follow a text different from the SBLGNT, which reads *ek tou thronou*, *from the throne*, and
*laoi*, *peoples*, plural. The difference does not affect the dwelling language.

**The Septuagint does not carry the verb at Exodus 25:8.** Rahlfs reads *ophthēsomai en hymin*, *I will
be seen among you*. The Greek thread from Exodus to John and Revelation therefore runs through the
noun *skēnē* (25:9), not through the verb of 25:8.

**Residence comes with an internal boundary.** Exodus 26 joins many curtains into one dwelling
and divides the interior into holy and most holy spaces. The ark is within the veil; the table
and lampstand are outside it (26:33–35). The dwelling promise of 25:8 does not itself abolish
distance or regulate everyone's entrance alike. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]] and [[The Veil - Separation in Exodus and Access in Hebrews]].

**The dwelling has an outer boundary and needs recurring care.** The court adds linen
hangings and an east entrance around the tent; the oil rule assigns Israel a supply and
Aaron's family the tending of the light (27:9–21). God's dwelling among the people includes
this ordered service without the text saying that material supplies sustain God's own
existence. See [[The Altar and the Court]].

### Exodus 33 in its narrative setting

The dwelling promise encounters an explicit refusal after the calf: Yahweh will not go among
a stiff-necked people lest he consume them (33:3–5). In [[Show Me Your Glory]], the tent is far
outside the camp, but encounter has not ended: the cloud descends and Moses speaks with God
(33:7–11). Moses’ plea secures the promise of accompaniment (33:12–17). The later filled
tabernacle must be read through this crisis rather than as the automatic completion of a
building plan. See [[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]].

### The enclosure made — Exodus 36:8–38

The enclosure made in Exodus 36:8–38 is a concrete step toward the dwelling promised in 25:8.
The joining of its curtain assemblies is already accomplished work (36:13, 18), but the final
raising and the filling with glory remain distinct in 40:17–35. After the calf and renewed
covenant, the makers again enact the earlier sanctuary design. The text reports construction,
not a mechanism by which human craftsmanship compels God's presence.

See [[The Tabernacle Built]]. Source: Exodus 26 and 36:8–38 checked in local raw WEB,
2026-10-24; later Exodus references checked in the same source.

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

Exodus 37 equips the enclosure with the ark and mercy seat, table, lampstand, incense
altar, oil, and incense. The objects serve the prior dwelling and meeting promises
(25:8, 22). Making them is not itself a narrated meeting or arrival of glory. Chapter 40
separately gives installation and service (40:20–27) and the filling (40:34–35). The
narrative after the calf thus continues toward the promised dwelling without representing
craftsmanship as a way to compel God's presence.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38

The outer altar, basin, and court now equip the approach to the dwelling (38:1–20). Their material
account (38:21–31) follows the work without narrating the arrival of glory. Exodus 40:29–35
separately describes offerings, washing, raising the court, and filling. Costly materials and
faithful making prepare the sanctuary; this account does not present their cost as buying divine
presence.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]]. Source: the cited Exodus passages
checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

Exodus 39:32 declares the work finished, and 39:43 joins Moses’ inspection to blessing.
The inventory is presented to him before he erects the tabernacle in chapter 40. The completion
of manufacture, the completion of installation in 40:33, and the filling in 40:34–35 are distinct
narrative moments. The dwelling promise is approaching fulfillment, but no glory appears in the
inspection itself and no claim is made that workmanship compels God to inhabit the tent.

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40

The promised dwelling is now filled: the cloud covers the tent and glory fills it
(40:34–35). Moses’ inability to enter preserves divine initiative within the outcome of
his obedient work. Verses 36–38 also qualify any simple contrast between residence and
accompaniment: the dwelling’s cloud governs travel and waiting throughout the journeys.
This is the conclusion after the threatened absence in 33:3, not an indwelling detached
from intercession and renewal. The temple and New Testament comparisons above remain
later canonical developments with their own documented sources.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in stored WEB,
2026-10-25.

### Leviticus 26 in context

The tent of 26:11 already stands, so *I will set my tent among you* cannot mean a first arrival. The
promise of residence depends on the condition of 26:3 and follows every material blessing in the list.
The next unit states what happens when that condition fails. See [[Blessings for Obedience]].
Source: stored WEB Leviticus 26, 2026-10-30.

## Significance

- **It names what the exodus was for.** Exodus 29:45–46 says it outright, and 25:8 is the first
  instruction that serves it. The covenant formula and the dwelling formula are joined in Exodus,
  Leviticus, and Revelation. See [[I Will Take You as My People - The Covenant Formula]].
- **Residence and accompaniment meet.** The presence promised to travellers in
  [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]] becomes a dwelling among the people, whose
  cloud also governs their journeys (Exodus 40:36–38).
- **It gives the New Testament its vocabulary for the incarnation and the end.** John 1:14 and
  Revelation 21:3 both use *skēnē* words for God living with people.
