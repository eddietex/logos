# The Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades

## Passages involved

- **Job 9:9** — *"He makes the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades, and the rooms of the south."* —
  [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]]
- **Job 38:31–33** — *"Can you bind the cluster of the Pleiades, or loosen the cords of Orion? Can
  you lead the constellations out in their season? Or can you guide the Bear with her cubs? Do you
  know the laws of the heavens?"* — [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
- **Amos 5:8** — *"Seek him who made the Pleiades and Orion, and turns the shadow of death into the
  morning, and makes the day dark with night."*
- **Isaiah 13:10** — *"For the stars of the sky and its constellations (kəsîlêhem) will not give
  their light."*
- **Genesis 1:16** — *"He also made the stars"*; **Psalm 147:4**; **Isaiah 40:26** — God numbering
  and naming the stars.

## Analysis

**Three named star-groups, and the Hebrew Bible names them almost nowhere else.** *Kîmâ* occurs three
times (Job 9:9; 38:31; Amos 5:8), *kəsîl* as a constellation four (the same three, and the plural of
Isaiah 13:10), *ʿāš/ʿayiš* twice (Job 9:9; 38:32). Two of the three passages are in Job, and they
face each other across the book.

**The identifications.** *Kîmâ*, *cluster* or *heap*, is the Pleiades with near-certainty, and 38:31's
*"can you bind the cluster (maʿădannôt kîmâ)?"* fits a tight knot of stars. *Kəsîl* means *fool* or
*insolent one*; the ancient versions and later Jewish tradition read it as Orion, the giant bound in
the sky for his rebellion — hence 38:31's *"cords of Orion"*, the fetters God could loosen and Job
could not. *ʿĀš* is the least certain: the Great Bear (the WEB, following the Vulgate and the
rabbinic tradition, and 38:32's *"the Bear with her cubs"*), or the Hyades, or Aldebaran. *Ḥadrê
tēmān*, *the chambers of the south*, are usually taken as the southern constellations — the stars of
the far southern sky, below the horizon of the other three for much of the year.

**Each passage uses the same stars for a different argument.**

- **Amos 5:8** is a hymn fragment set in the middle of an indictment for injustice. The God who made
  the Pleiades and Orion *"turns the shadow of death (ṣalmāwet) into the morning"* — the maker of the
  stars is the one who can reverse darkness, and so the one who will judge Israel's courts (5:7, 10–12).
  The stars are evidence for justice.
- **Job 9:9** is a hymn fragment set in the middle of a complaint about justice. The God who made the
  Bear and Orion also *"commands the sun, and it doesn't rise, and seals up the stars"* (9:7). The
  maker of the stars is too great to be summoned to court. The stars are evidence that there will be
  no justice — not because God is unjust, but because no one can make him answer.
- **Job 38:31–33** is God's own reply, and he asks it as a question: *can you bind them? can you lead
  them? do you know the laws (ḥuqqôt) of the heavens?* Job had listed the constellations as things God
  makes. God lists them as things God *governs*, by laws Job does not know. The answer to 9:9 is not
  a correction but an enlargement: the stars are not only made, they are ordered, and the order is
  beyond Job's reach in the same way the order of his own case is.

Amos and Job share a further word. Amos's maker of the Pleiades turns *ṣalmāwet* into morning; Job's
speech in which the Pleiades appear ends in the land of *ṣalmāwet*, *"where the light is as midnight"*
(Job 10:22). **The same God, the same stars, and the opposite direction of travel.**

## Significance

The three passages together are the Hebrew Bible's whole explicit astronomy, and in all three the
point is that the stars belong to God and not to the gods. In a world where the Pleiades and Orion
were deities or omens across Mesopotamia and Egypt, Job 9:9 and Amos 5:8 make them artefacts — *he
makes* (*ʿōśeh*). Job 38 goes one step further and makes them subjects under law. See [[Creation]],
[[Light and Darkness]], and [[Wisdom]].
