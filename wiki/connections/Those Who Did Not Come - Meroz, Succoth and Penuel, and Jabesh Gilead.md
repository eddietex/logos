# Those Who Did Not Come - Meroz, Succoth and Penuel, and Jabesh Gilead

## Passages involved

- [[The Song of Deborah]] — Judges 5:15–17, 23; Reuben, Gilead, Dan, and Asher stay home; Meroz is
  cursed.
- **Judges 8:5–17** *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)* — Succoth and Penuel refuse Gideon bread, and he
  punishes both.
- **Judges 21:5–12** *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)* — Jabesh Gilead does not come to Mizpah, and the
  congregation destroys it.

## Analysis

Three times in Judges a part of Israel is called to stand with the rest and does not, and each time the
book records what it cost. The three grow worse in order.

1. **The Song (5:15–17, 23).** Four tribes are asked *why*: *Why did you sit among the sheepfolds? …
   Why did Dan remain in ships?* They are shamed, not punished. One town is cursed by Yahweh's angel:
   *Curse Meroz … because they didn't come to help Yahweh, to help Yahweh against the mighty* (23). The
   curse is a word; the Song reports no action taken against Meroz.
2. **Succoth and Penuel (8:5–17).** Gideon, pursuing the kings of Midian, asks the men of Succoth for
   bread for *the people who follow me; for they are faint* (8:5). The princes answer, *Are the hands of
   Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand, that we should give bread to your army?* (8:6). Penuel answers
   the same (8:8). Gideon returns and *taught the men of Succoth* with *thorns of the wilderness and
   briers* (8:16), *broke down the tower of Penuel, and killed the men of the city* (8:17). Here the
   refusal is answered by a judge's own hand.
3. **Jabesh Gilead (21:5–12).** After the war against Benjamin, Israel asks *Who is there among all the
   tribes of Israel who didn't come up in the assembly to Yahweh?* (21:5), under *a great oath* that such
   a one *shall surely be put to death*. *No one came from Jabesh Gilead to the camp to the assembly*
   (21:8). Twelve thousand men are sent to *strike the inhabitants of Jabesh Gilead with the edge of the
   sword, with the women and the little ones* (21:10), and four hundred virgins are taken for Benjamin
   (21:12).

**What changes.** In the Song the charge is that they did not come *to help Yahweh*, and the sentence
is Yahweh's angel's. At Succoth and Penuel the charge is refusal of bread to Gideon's men, and the
sentence is Gideon's. At Jabesh Gilead the charge is absence from an assembly, and the sentence is a
vow that kills a city's women and children to supply wives. The book does not say which of these
judgments was right. It sets the first, spoken by Yahweh's angel, at the start, and the last, carried
out by Israel against Israel, at the end.

**Gilead twice.** *Gilead lived beyond the Jordan* (5:17) is among those who stayed in the Song, and
Jabesh Gilead is the city destroyed in 21. Succoth and Penuel are also east of the Jordan. The wiki
notes the geography without reading a pattern into it; Judges does not draw one. See [[Gilead]],
[[Succoth (Transjordan)]], and [[Peniel]].

## Significance

Judges keeps asking whether Israel will act as one people, and keeps recording the price of not
coming. The Song shames and curses; the later chapters punish, and the punishments are worse each time.
See [[Meroz]], [[Curse]], [[Brotherhood]], and
