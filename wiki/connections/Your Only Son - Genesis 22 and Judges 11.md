# Your Only Son - Genesis 22 and Judges 11

## Passages involved
- [[The Binding of Isaac]] — Genesis 22:2. *Now take your son, your only son, whom you love, even Isaac,
  and go into the land of Moriah. Offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains which I
  will tell you of* (stored WEB). Also 22:12 and 22:16, *your son, your only son*.
- [[Jephthah's Vow]] — Judges 11:31, 34. *I will offer it up for a burnt offering … his daughter came out
  to meet him with tambourines and with dances. She was his only child* (stored WEB).

## Analysis

**One word, two children.** *Yāḥîd*, *only* (lemma 3173), occurs in the WLC of Genesis through Judges
four times. Three are in Genesis 22, of Isaac: *yəḥîdəkā* (22:2, 12), *yəḥîdekā* (22:16). The fourth is
Jephthah's daughter, *wəraq hîʾ yəḥîdâ*, *she alone, an only one* (Judges 11:34). Both narrators then say
it again in other words. Genesis says *whom you love*. Judges says *besides her he had neither son nor
daughter*.

**One offering.** God's command is *wəhaʿălēhû šām ləʿōlâ*, *offer him up there as a burnt offering*
(Genesis 22:2). Jephthah's vow is *wəhaʿălîtihû ʿôlâ*, *I will offer him* (or *it*) *up as a burnt
offering* (Judges 11:31). Both use the causative of *ʿālâ* with a masculine suffix and the noun *ʿōlâ*
(all WLC).

**On the mountains.** Isaac is taken to *one of the mountains which I will tell you of* (22:2). Jephthah's
daughter goes *down on the mountains*, *ʿal-hehārîm*, for two months before she returns (Judges 11:37–38,
WLC).

**Where they differ.**

| | Genesis 22 | Judges 11 |
|---|---|---|
| who asks for the offering | God; *God tested Abraham* (22:1) | Jephthah, in his own vow (11:30–31) |
| does the father know who? | yes, *your only son* | no, *whatever comes out* |
| does the child speak? | Isaac asks *where is the lamb for a burnt offering?* (22:7) | she tells her father to keep his word (11:36) |
| what stops it | the angel of Yahweh calls from the sky (22:11–12) | nothing; no voice |
| the end | a ram *instead of his son* (22:13) | *he did with her according to his vow* (11:39) |
| what follows | an oath of blessing, *because you have not withheld your son* (22:16–17) | a lament, four days every year (11:40) |

In Genesis God asks and then stops the father. In Judges God does not ask, and Jephthah is not stopped.
Abraham's offering is called a test of whether he fears God (22:12). Jephthah's is called a vow, and the
text never says God wanted it.

**Whether the daughter died** is disputed. The vow said *burnt offering*; the fulfillment says only *his
vow* and *she was a virgin* (11:39). [[Jephthah's Vow]] sets out both readings. The parallel of word and
offering on this page holds on either reading.

## Significance

The two passages are the only places in the first seven books where a father's *only* child is set apart
as a burnt offering. Judges does not cite Genesis, and the wiki's comparison rests on the shared words.
Read side by side, they make the point drawn on
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]]: Genesis 22 is the story where the angel comes,
and Judges 11 is the story where he does not. See also
[[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]], [[Isaac]], and [[Jephthah's Daughter]].
