# Justice More Than Sacrifice - Proverbs 21.3, 1 Samuel 15.22, and Hosea 6.6

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 21:3** — *"To do righteousness and justice is more acceptable to Yahweh than
  sacrifice."* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Proverbs 21:27** — *"The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination; how much more, when he
  brings it with a wicked mind!"* — [[The King's Heart]]
- **Proverbs 15:8** — *"The sacrifice made by the wicked is an abomination to Yahweh, but the prayer
  of the upright is his delight."* — [[A Gentle Answer]]
- **1 Samuel 15:22** — *"Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of
  rams."* *(WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- **Hosea 6:6** — *"For I desire mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt
  offerings."* *(WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- **Micah 6:8** — *"What does Yahweh require of you, but to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk
  humbly with your God?"* *(WEB verified at bible-api.com; not yet ingested)*
- **Ecclesiastes 5:1** — *"to draw near to listen is better than to give the sacrifice of fools, for
  they don’t know that they do evil."* — [[Let Your Words Be Few]]

## Analysis

**A comparison, not an abolition.** Proverbs 21:3 says righteousness and justice are *more
acceptable* than sacrifice. Twenty-four verses later the chapter assumes sacrifice is still brought
and judges it by the one bringing it (27). The sayings rank conduct over ritual; they do not tell
the reader to stop offering.

**The same ranking appears in narrative and prophecy.** Samuel's rebuke of Saul sets obeying
Yahweh's voice above burnt offerings and the fat of rams (1 Samuel 15:22), answering a king who,
on the chapter's account (not yet ingested; paraphrased here), excused disobedience by appeal to
sacrifice. Hosea's *"mercy, and not sacrifice"* uses a
negation that the parallel line glosses as *more than* (6:6). Micah answers a question about what
offering would suffice with justice, mercy, and humility (6:8). Proverbs states the same
priority as wisdom rather than as prophetic accusation.

**Verse 27 adds intent.** Proverbs 15:8 called the wicked man's sacrifice an abomination; 21:27
adds *"how much more"* when it is brought *"with a wicked mind"*, an offering used as a means to an
end. That fits the situation 15:22 answers, where an offering is invoked as an excuse.

Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 15:8; 21:3, 27. 1 Samuel 15:22, Hosea 6:6, and Micah 6:8 quoted from
the WEB at bible-api.com (2026-10-26). No claim about Hebrew vocabulary is made.

**Ecclesiastes states it as wisdom too.** Ecclesiastes 5:1 ranks drawing near *to listen* above
the sacrifice of fools, as 1 Samuel 15:22 ranks listening above the fat of rams in the WEB. The
fault, again, is in the offerer: fools *"don’t know that they do evil."* No claim is made that
the two verses share Hebrew vocabulary. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 5:1.

## Significance

The thread runs through the law's own sacrificial system and does not oppose it; it tells the
worshipper what the offering cannot replace. See [[Sacrifice and Offering]], [[Righteousness]],
