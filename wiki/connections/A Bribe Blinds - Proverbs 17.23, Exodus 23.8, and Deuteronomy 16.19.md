## Analysis

**The law forbids, Proverbs describes, and the vocabulary is the same.** Exodus 23:8 commands
*wəšōḥad lōʾ tiqqāḥ*, *you shall not take a bribe*; Proverbs 17:23 reports that *šōḥad … rāšāʿ
yiqqāḥ*, *a wicked man takes a bribe*. Exodus 23:6 forbids turning aside justice, *lōʾ taṭṭeh
mišpaṭ*, and Deuteronomy 16:19 repeats it as *lōʾ-taṭṭeh mišpāṭ*; Proverbs 17:23 gives the bribe's
purpose in the same verb and noun, *ləhaṭṭôt ʾorḥôt mišpāṭ*, *to turn aside the paths of
justice*. Deuteronomy 16:19 joins the two clauses in one verse, as Proverbs 17:23 does. (Hebrew of
all four read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.) This is a shared legal
vocabulary, not a citation; no direction of borrowing is claimed.

**Exodus grounds the rule in God; Proverbs grounds it in God's reaction.** *"I will not justify the
wicked"* (Exodus 23:7) is the reason the judge may not either. Proverbs 17:15 uses the same
causative, *maṣdîq rāšāʿ*, and calls the judge who does it, and the judge who condemns the
righteous, *"both of them alike … an abomination to Yahweh."* Deuteronomy 25:1 gives the positive
form — *wəhiṣdîqû ʾet-haṣṣaddîq wəhiršîʿû ʾet-hārāšāʿ* — and Isaiah 5:23 the woe, *maṣdîqê rāšāʿ
ʿēqeb šōḥad*, *who justify the wicked for a bribe*, which the WEB renders *"acquit the guilty"*. The
bribe and the false verdict are one sin in Isaiah, as they are one cluster in Proverbs 17.

**What the bribe does to the receiver, and how it looks to the giver.** Exodus and Deuteronomy say a
bribe *blinds* — *those who have sight* (Exodus 23:8), *the eyes of the wise* (Deuteronomy 16:19)
— so the danger is to the judge's perception, not only his honesty. Proverbs 17:8 looks from the
other side: *in the eyes of* the giver the bribe is *ʾeben-ḥēn*, a *stone of favour*, and
*"wherever he turns, he prospers."* Proverbs 21:14 adds that a gift *in secret* (*bassēter*) and a
bribe *in the cloak* (*baḥēq*) calm anger. These two sayings observe that bribery works; 17:23
supplies the judgment they withhold, and the bosom (*mēḥēq*) where the bribe is hidden at 21:14 is
the bosom it comes out of at 17:23.

**Proverbs 17:26 adds the case the law did not name.** Exodus 23 protects the poor (6) and the
innocent (7). Proverbs 17:26 extends the concern upward: *"nor to flog officials (nədîbîm) for their
integrity."* Corrupted judgment wrongs whoever stands in its way, not only the weak.

**Proverbs 18 continues the court.** *"To be partial to the faces of the wicked is not good, nor to
deprive the innocent of justice"* (18:5): *śəʾēt pənê*, *lifting the face*, is the idiom of Leviticus
19:15's *"You shall not be partial to the poor, nor show favoritism to the great"* (WEB at
bible-api.com), and *ləhaṭṭôt*, *to deprive*, is 17:23's verb for perverting justice (Hebrew from
Sefaria). The next chapter also has a gift that is not called a bribe: *"A man's gift makes room for
him, and brings him before great men"* (18:16). The noun is *mattān*, not *šōḥad*, and the saying only
observes. Read with 17:8 and 17:23, the collection leaves the reader to tell the present that opens a
door from the bribe that buys a verdict. See [[A Strong Tower]].

### Proverbs 21 in context

Now ingested, Proverbs 21:14 sits between sayings on justice (12, 15): doing justice is joy to the
righteous and ruin to evildoers. The bribe that pacifies wrath is reported as effective, not
commended, and the chapter's own frame judges it.

### Recognizing faces — Proverbs 24:23–25

The appendix of *"sayings of the wise"* opens on the court: *"To show partiality in judgment is not
good. He who says to the wicked, 'You are righteous'; peoples will curse him, and nations will abhor
him— but it will go well with those who convict the guilty, and a rich blessing will come on them."*
Its idiom for partiality is not 18:5's *lifting the face* but *hakker-pānîm*, *recognizing faces* —
the words of Deuteronomy's charge to the judges, *lōʾ-takkîrû p̄ānîm bammišpāṭ* (1:17, WEB *"You
shall not show partiality in judgment"*), and of 16:19's *lōʾ ṯakkîr pānîm*, quoted above. Proverbs
28:21 repeats the clause, *hakker-pānîm lōʾ-ṭôḇ*. The partial judge's verdict is quoted as one word,
*ṣaddîq*, *"Righteous!"* — the acquittal that 17:15 called an abomination to Yahweh and Deuteronomy
25:1 reverses. Here the disgust is the peoples': the judge is cursed and abhorred by *ʿammîm* and
*ləʾummîm*, while *those who convict* (*môḵîḥîm*) receive *"a rich blessing"*. Yahweh's verdict and
the nations' are not in competition; together they say an acquittal of the guilty is a public wrong.

### Proverbs 28 in context

Partiality is condemned, yet a person will do wrong for a piece of bread. The small inducement
exposes how little can corrupt conduct. The verse does not name a judge or explicitly call the bread
a bribe in the WEB; the judicial reading is a contextual comparison with 24:23 and Exodus 23:3, 6–8,
not an added detail of the verse.

## Significance

The wisdom collection is sometimes read as describing a world that runs justly. This cluster is
its plainest admission that it does not: the bribe prospers its giver, the guilty go free, the
righteous are flogged. Proverbs does not answer that by promising that it will not happen; it calls
it *an abomination to Yahweh* and leaves the verdict on the court with him. Job's friends assumed
that no court could misjudge Job. Job's own oath includes the court — *"If I have lifted up my hand
against the fatherless, because I saw my help in the gate"* (Job 31:21) — and the Torah and
Proverbs both assume, as he did, that the gate can be bought. See [[Judgment]],
[[Righteousness]], [[Wealth and Possessions]], [[Law and Obedience]], and
[[Truthful Witness and Lying Lips - Proverbs 12 and Exodus 20-23]].
