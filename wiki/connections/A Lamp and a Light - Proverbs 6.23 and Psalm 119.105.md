# A Lamp and a Light - Proverbs 6.23 and Psalm 119.105

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 6:23** — [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]: *"For the commandment is a lamp, and the law
  is light. Reproofs of instruction are the way of life"* (*kî nēr miṣwâ wəṯôrâ ʾôr*)
- **Psalm 119:105** *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)* — *"Your word is a lamp to my feet, and a light
  for my path"* (*nēr-ləraḡlî dəḇāreḵā wəʾôr linṯîḇāṯî*)
- **Proverbs 4:18–19** — [[The Two Paths]]: *"But the path of the righteous is like the dawning light
  … The way of the wicked is like darkness. They don't know what they stumble over."*
- **Job 29:3** — [[The Months of Old]]: *"when his lamp shone on my head, and by his light I walked
  through darkness"*

## Analysis

**The same pair, in the same order.** Both verses set *nēr*, *lamp*, in the first half and *ʾôr*,
*light*, in the second, and both apply them to what God has commanded: *miṣwâ* and *tôrâ* in
Proverbs, *dāḇār*, *word*, in the psalm, whose whole length is a meditation on the law under a
series of names. The Hebrew of both verses is read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.
The wiki records this as a parallel of image and vocabulary. Neither text quotes the other, and the
pair *lamp* and *light* is common enough that no direction of borrowing is claimed.

**The lamp lights the next step, not the whole road.** A *nēr* is a small clay oil lamp, carried in
the hand or set in a niche. Psalm 119:105 makes the point by placement: a lamp *to my feet*, a light
*for my path*. Proverbs 6:22–23 gives the same lamp to a son walking, sleeping, and waking, and then
says what it is for: *"to keep you from the immoral woman"* (6:24) — the danger the next chapter will
set *"in the twilight, in the evening of the day, in the middle of the night and in the darkness"*
(7:9; see [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]). The commandment lights the step where the son would otherwise fall.

**Light carried and light dawning.** [[The Two Paths]] had the righteous path itself become light,
*"like the dawning light, that shines more and more"* (4:18). In 6:23 the light is not a growing
daylight on the road but a lamp the walker holds. The two are compatible — the path shines because
it is walked by the light of the teaching — but they are different images, and the wiki keeps both.

**The lamp put out.** The wiki's thread [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]] follows the other use of
*nēr*: the lamp of a life or a house extinguished (Job 18:6; Proverbs 13:9; 24:20). Job remembered
God's lamp shining on his head as the light by which he once walked through darkness (Job 29:3).
Proverbs 6:23 is the positive form of the same picture: the lamp that does not go out is the
commandment kept. The tabernacle lamp, kept burning *"from evening to morning"* (Exodus 27:21), is a
separate thread, recorded on
[[From Evening to Morning - The Lamp in Exodus 27 and Leviticus 24]]; neither Proverbs nor the psalm
draws on it explicitly.

## Significance

The image gives [[Light and Darkness]] its moral and practical form in the wisdom books: the light
by which a person walks is the instruction given to him. It also joins [[Law and Obedience]] to
[[Walking with God]] — a law that is not only kept but carried, and that does its work at night.
