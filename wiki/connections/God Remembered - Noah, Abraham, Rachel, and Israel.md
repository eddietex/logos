# God Remembered - Noah, Abraham, Rachel, and Israel

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 8:1** — *"God remembered Noah, all the animals, and all the livestock that were with him
  in the ship; and God made a wind to pass over the earth. The waters subsided."* —
  [[The Waters Recede]]
- **Genesis 9:15** — *"I will remember my covenant, which is between me and you and every living
  creature of all flesh."* — [[The Covenant with Noah]]
- **Genesis 19:29** — *"When God destroyed the cities of the plain, God remembered Abraham, and sent
  Lot out of the middle of the overthrow."* — [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]]
- **Genesis 30:22** — *"God remembered Rachel, and God listened to her, and opened her womb."* —
  [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]]
- **Exodus 2:24** — *"God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with
  Isaac, and with Jacob."* — [[Moses Flees to Midian]]
- **Exodus 6:5** — *"Moreover I have heard the groaning of the children of Israel, whom the Egyptians
  keep in bondage, and I have remembered my covenant."* 2:24 in God's own voice, said to Moses after
  the failed audience — [[I Am Yahweh]]
- **Leviticus 26:42, 45** — *"then I will remember my covenant with Jacob; and also my covenant with
  Isaac, and also my covenant with Abraham; and I will remember the land."* The same act, promised in
  advance for exile — [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]]
- **Psalm 105:42** — *"For he remembered his holy word, and Abraham, his servant."*
- **Luke 1:72–73** — *"to show mercy towards our fathers, to remember his holy covenant, the oath
  which he swore to Abraham, our father"*
- **Exodus 12:14** — *"This day shall be to you for a memorial"* — the first *zikkārôn* in Scripture,
  and the turn from God remembering to Israel being given something to remember with —
  [[The Passover]]

## Analysis

**In the Torah's narrative, *God remembered* is never a report about God's memory. It is the sentence
on which a rescue turns.** *Zākar* with God as subject appears at a handful of decisive points, and
each time the next clause is an act:

| | who is remembered | what follows at once |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 8:1 | Noah and every creature in the ark | a wind, and the waters subside |
| Genesis 19:29 | Abraham | Lot sent out of the overthrow |
| Genesis 30:22 | Rachel | her womb opened; Joseph born |
| Exodus 2:24 | the covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob | God sees, knows, and appears at the bush (3:1–10) |

**Each instance comes at the lowest point of a long wait.** Noah has been shut in the ark through the
whole flood; Rachel has watched her sister and two maids bear ten sons; Israel has been in bondage
through the whole life of the king who began it. **The narrator does not imply God had forgotten** —
the Hebrew verb carries no such sense — **but marks the moment of turning from waiting to acting.**
See [[Remembrance]].

**What God remembers is a person, then a covenant.** Noah, Abraham, and Rachel are remembered by
name. In Exodus 2:24 the object is *"his covenant"* with three named men, all of them long dead — and
the covenant meant is the one that put Israel's bondage on a schedule: *"your offspring will live as
foreigners in a land that is not theirs … and afterward they will come out with great wealth"*
(Genesis 15:13–14). **Genesis 9:15–16 is the hinge between the two kinds**: God promises to remember
*a covenant* whenever he sees the bow. Exodus 2:24 is that promise, kept for a different covenant.
See [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

**Abraham is in two of the four.** God *remembered Abraham* and saved Lot because of him (19:29), and
God *remembered his covenant with Abraham* and saved Israel because of it. **The intercessor of
Genesis 18 is still the reason for rescue four centuries later.** Psalm 105 closes its
retelling of the exodus on exactly this: *"For he remembered his holy word, and Abraham, his servant"*
(105:42).

**The sequence of verbs in Exodus 2:24–25 — *heard*, *remembered*, *saw*, *knew* — is the fullest
form.** Genesis 30:22 has two of them (*remembered*, *listened*); Exodus 6:5 repeats *heard* and
*remembered* to Moses in God's own voice. **And the New Testament opens with the same verb**: in
Zechariah's song the purpose of the coming salvation is *"to remember his holy covenant, the oath
which he swore to Abraham"* (Luke 1:72–73). The rescue from Egypt and the rescue Luke announces are
described as the same act of remembering, toward the same man.

**At the bush the verb gets a noun, and the noun is given to Israel.** *"This is my name forever, and
this is my memorial to all generations"* (Exodus 3:15) — ***zikrî***, *my memorial*, from *zākar*.
**God remembered his covenant at 2:24; at 3:15 he gives the people the means to remember him**, and
the password for the elders is Joseph's dying promise kept word for word — *pāqōd pāqadtî*, *"I have
surely visited you"* (3:16; Genesis 50:24–25). See [[The Burning Bush]].

**Leviticus 26 extends the pattern to a disaster that has not happened.** Genesis 9:15 had promised
remembering in advance; Leviticus 26:42, 45 does the same for exile, with the three names reversed
and, for the first time, the land as an object: *wəhāʾāreṣ ʾezkōr*, *and the land I will remember*
(WLC). Unlike the table above, nothing follows at once. No rescue is narrated, and the next clause
has the land still lying desolate (43). See
[[If They Confess Their Iniquity - Leviticus 26.40-45, 1 Kings 8, Daniel 9, and Nehemiah 9]].

## Significance

- **It makes the exodus a kept promise rather than a new decision.** Exodus 2:24 grounds everything
  from the bush to the sea in an obligation God took on in Genesis 15.
- **It defines the verb.** Read across these texts, *God remembered* means *God acted on a commitment*,
  and the commitment can be to a person or to a covenant. That is the sense in which Israel will be
  told to *remember* — the Sabbath, the exodus, the stranger: memory as obligation.
- **It gives the human prayers for remembrance their background.** Job (*"remember me"*, 14:13),
  Joseph to the cupbearer (*"remember me"*, Genesis 40:14), and the psalmists ask for exactly the
  act these narratives report. See [[Remember Me - Genesis 40.14 and the Prayers of the Condemned]].
