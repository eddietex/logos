# The Judgment Is God's - Exodus 23, Leviticus 19.15, and Deuteronomy 1.17

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 23:3, 6** — *"You shall not favor a poor man in his cause."* … *"You shall not deny
  justice to your poor people in their lawsuits."* — [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]]
- **Leviticus 19:15** — *"You shall do no injustice in judgment. You shall not be partial to the
  poor, nor show favoritism to the great; but you shall judge your neighbor in righteousness."* —
  [[Be Holy and Love Your Neighbor]]
- **Deuteronomy 1:16–17** — *"judge righteously between a man and his brother, and the foreigner who
  is living with him. You shall not show partiality in judgment; you shall hear the small and the
  great alike. You shall not be afraid of the face of man, for the judgment is God's."* —
  [[Judges Appointed for the People]]
- **Deuteronomy 10:17** — Yahweh *"doesn't respect persons, nor takes reward"* (WEB; stored, *not
  yet ingested*)
- **Deuteronomy 16:18–19** — judges and officers *"in all your gates"*: *"You shall not pervert
  justice. You shall not show partiality. You shall not take a bribe"* —
  [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]]
- **Job 34:19** — God *"doesn't respect the persons of princes, nor respects the rich more than the
  poor; for they all are the work of his hands"* — [[Elihu on God's Justice]]
- **Proverbs 24:23** — *"To show partiality in judgment is not good."* — [[The Field of the Sluggard]]

## Analysis

**One rule, stated from both sides.** Exodus 23 forbids bending a case in either direction — not
*for* the poor man (23:3), not *against* him (23:6). Leviticus 19:15 puts both halves in one verse:
not partial to the poor, not favoring the great. Deuteronomy 1:17 turns the pair into a procedure:
*hear the small and the great alike*. The direction of the bias does not matter to any of them; the
fault is that the verdict follows the person rather than the case.

**Three reasons given.**

| | ground of the rule |
|---|---|
| Leviticus 19:15 | holiness: the chapter's refrain *"I am Yahweh"*; *judge your neighbor in righteousness* |
| Deuteronomy 1:17 | ownership: *"the judgment is God's"*, so the judge need fear no man |
| Deuteronomy 10:17; Job 34:19 | imitation: God himself does not respect persons; all are *"the work of his hands"* |
| Proverbs 24:23 | observation: *"not good"*, with public curse on the judge who acquits the wicked (24:24) |

**Deuteronomy 1:17 is the only one that names fear.** The other texts assume a judge tempted by
sympathy or by a bribe. Deuteronomy adds the judge who is intimidated — *"You shall not be afraid of
the face of man"* — and answers him by relocating the verdict: it is God's, not his, so the powerful
party has no claim on it. The same verse then gives the judge an outlet for the case he cannot
decide: bring it to Moses. Deuteronomy 17:8–9 later sends such cases to the place Yahweh chooses.

**Deuteronomy says it twice.** The charge is given first as history — to the judges appointed at
Horeb (1:16–17) — and later as law for the land, to judges *"in all your gates"* (16:18–19), where
the bribe is added. Between them, 10:17 supplies the reason in God's own conduct. When 16:18–20 is
ingested, its passage page should be added above.

**Later echoes, not yet stored.** 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 has Jehoshaphat tell the judges he appoints
that they judge not for man but for Yahweh, and that with Yahweh there is no respect of persons
nor taking of bribes. That reference is given from memory as a paraphrase; the WEB text is not
stored and has not been checked. James 2:1–9 applies the rule to the seating of rich and poor in
the assembly — likewise a paraphrase, unchecked.

### Deuteronomy 16:18–17:13 now ingested

[[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]] moves the rule from Moses' wilderness judges to judges in
every town. The partiality clause is the same idiom as 1:17, *hakkîr pānîm*, *to recognize faces*,
but singular, addressed to Israel, instead of plural, addressed to the judges (WLC 1:17; 16:19,
morphhb). Deuteronomy 1:17 kept the hard case for Moses. Deuteronomy 17:8–9 sends it to the chosen
place and to *"the priests who are Levites, and to the judge who shall be in those days"*. See
[[The Case Too Hard - Exodus 18, Deuteronomy 1.17, and Deuteronomy 17.8]].

## Significance

- **Impartiality is theological, not procedural.** Every text that gives a reason grounds it in God
  — his holiness, his ownership of judgment, or his own refusal to respect persons. See [[Judgment]]
  and [[The Fear of God]].
- **The poor are protected without being favored.** Exodus 23:3 and Leviticus 19:15 forbid tilting
  the court toward the poor, in books that elsewhere press hard for their protection. The protection
  is the fair hearing itself. See [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]].
- **The foreigner is inside the court.** Deuteronomy 1:16 names him alongside the brother. See
  [[The Nations]].
