# Visible Reminders and the God Who Brought Israel Out

## Passages involved
- [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] — Exodus 13:3–16, especially 8–9, 16.
- [[Acceptable Victims and the Holy Name]] — Leviticus 22:31–33.
- [[The Tassels]] — Numbers 15:37–41.

## Analysis

Exodus 13 joins remembered departure from Egypt to household instruction and a sign on the
hand and memorial between the eyes (8–9, 16). Numbers 15 gives a concrete garment instruction:
the fringe with its blue cord is to be seen, and seeing is to prompt remembrance and practice
of all the commandments (38–40). Both make deliverance a continuing obligation rather than
only a past event. The resemblance does not establish that Exodus's sign is the same object
as the Numbers fringe.

Leviticus 22:31–33 and Numbers 15:40–41 connect keeping commandments, holiness, and the God
who brought Israel out of Egypt to be their God. Leviticus names Yahweh as the one making
them holy; Numbers calls them to be holy through remembered obedience. These are complementary
emphases within the cited speeches, not permission to delete either divine action or human
responsibility.

## Significance

The reminders direct attention toward a relationship and its demands. Numbers explicitly
commands looking at the fringe while prohibiting following one's own heart and eyes: the
answer to misdirected sight is instructed sight. Neither passage attributes automatic moral
efficacy to a visible sign.
