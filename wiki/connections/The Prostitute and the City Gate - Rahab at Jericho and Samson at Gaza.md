# The Prostitute and the City Gate - Rahab at Jericho and Samson at Gaza

## Passages involved

- **Joshua 2:1–7** — [[Rahab and the Spies]]: *They went and came into the house of a prostitute whose name
  was Rahab, and slept there. The king of Jericho was told, "Behold, men of the children of Israel came in
  here tonight to spy out the land"* (1–2). The gate is shut *when it was dark* (5), and shut again behind
  the pursuers (7).
- **Judges 16:1–3** — [[Samson at Gaza]]: *Samson went to Gaza, and saw there a prostitute, and went in to
  her. The Gazites were told, "Samson is here!"* They wait all night *in the gate of the city* (2), and at
  midnight he carries the gate away (3).

## Analysis

**The same scene, set twice.** Israelite men enter an enemy city at night and go to a prostitute's house.
Word reaches the city's rulers. The city gate is where the city tries to hold them. The Hebrew shares its
key words (WLC):

| | Joshua 2 | Judges 16 |
|---|---|---|
| the woman | *ʾiššâ zônâ*, *a prostitute* (1) | *ʾiššâ zônâ*, *a prostitute* (1) |
| entering | *wayyābōʾû bêt-ʾiššâ zônâ*, *came into the house of* (1) | *wayyābōʾ ʾēlêhā*, *went in to her* (1) |
| lying down | *wayyiškəbû-šām*, *slept there* (1) | *wayyiškab*, *lay until midnight* (3) |
| the report | *wayyēʾāmar … hinnê ʾănāšîm bāʾû hēnnâ hallaylâ*, *men … came in here tonight* (2) | *laʿazzātîm lēʾmōr bāʾ šimšôn hēnnâ*, *Samson is here* (2) |
| the gate | *haššaʿar*, shut at dark and after the pursuers (5, 7) | *bəšaʿar hāʿîr*, *in the gate of the city* (2–3) |

The two reports differ in one place: Joshua 2:2 has a verb, *wayyēʾāmar*, *it was told* (niphal), and
Judges 16:2 has none, only *to the Gazites, saying*. The WEB supplies *were told* in both.

**What differs is everything the woman does.** Rahab hides the spies, lies to the king's men, sends the
pursuers to the fords, confesses Yahweh, and asks for an oath (Joshua 2:3–13). The woman at Gaza does
nothing after verse 1: she is not named, she does not speak, and she is not said to hide, warn, or betray
anyone. The text does not say who told the Gazites.

**What differs is also what the gate does.** At Jericho the gate is shut, and the spies get out over the
wall, *by a cord through the window* (2:15). At Gaza the gate is held by men waiting for morning, and
Samson goes out through it by taking it with him. At Jericho the city falls afterward (Joshua 6). Gaza
does not fall; Samson comes back to it blind, in fetters (Judges 16:21).

**And the Israelites differ.** The spies come to see the land on Joshua's orders (2:1). Samson comes
alone, for no reason the text gives but the woman, and the wiki records that the narrator does not
comment.

## Significance

Judges keeps returning to Joshua's scenes and running them differently: the spies at Bethel spare a man
who shows them the way in, as Rahab was spared (Judges 1:22–26; see
[[The Spies at Jericho and at Bethel - Joshua 2 and Judges 1]]). At Gaza the scene of Joshua 2 is played
by one man, for himself. The parallel is drawn by the wiki from shared vocabulary; neither text refers to
the other.
