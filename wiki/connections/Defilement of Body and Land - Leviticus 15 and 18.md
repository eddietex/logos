# Defilement of Body and Land - Leviticus 15 and 18

## Passages involved

- [[Penalties and a People Set Apart]] — Leviticus 20:1–27.

- [[Bodily Discharges and Cleansing]] — Leviticus 15:16–24, 31.
- [[Forbidden Unions and the Defiled Land]] — Leviticus 18:19–30.
- [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] — Leviticus 26:33–35. Leviticus 18:28 has the land vomit Israel out for defilement; chapter 26
  has Yahweh scatter Israel for breach of covenant, and the land gains rest. The two warnings give
  different pictures of one expulsion, and neither cites the other.

## Analysis

### Leviticus 20 now ingested

Leviticus 20:18 now adds cutting off for intercourse during menstruation. This strengthens the
prohibition in 18:19 without making menstruation itself an offense or turning 15:24 into
permission. Verses 22–26 join the land warning to animal distinctions and Israel’s separation to
God.

Leviticus 15 assigns uncleanness to bodily processes without identifying an offense in
each case. Verse 24 gives seven days of uncleanness in the described intercourse case.
Leviticus 18:19 explicitly prohibits sexual approach during menstrual impurity. A status
consequence and a prohibition are distinct statements; chapter 15 is not permission.

Leviticus 15:31 protects the tabernacle from uncleanness. Leviticus 18:24–30 describes
prohibited acts defiling the land and leading to expulsion. Washing and waiting appear
repeatedly in chapter 15; chapter 18 offers no equivalent remedy for its prohibited acts.

This qualifies the wiki’s earlier categorical description of uncleanness as nonmoral:
that account fits many bodily conditions but cannot cover every use of defilement.
It does not follow that childbirth, menstruation, or all sexual activity is sinful.

## Significance

Bodily status, prohibited conduct, sanctuary protection, and land tenure must be
distinguished. Neither every impurity is an offense nor every defilement a temporary
bodily condition.
