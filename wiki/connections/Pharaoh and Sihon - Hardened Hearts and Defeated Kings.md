# Pharaoh and Sihon - Hardened Hearts and Defeated Kings

## Analysis

In Exodus 4:21 Yahweh announces that he will harden Pharaoh’s heart and that Pharaoh will
not let Israel go. Exodus 14:4 joins further hardening to pursuit and Yahweh’s gaining honor
over Pharaoh and his armies. Deuteronomy 2:30 similarly combines a king’s refusal with
Yahweh’s hardening, here to deliver Sihon into Israel’s hand.

The situations differ: Pharaoh holds Israel in Egypt; Sihon refuses transit through his land.
At the sea Israel escapes an army; at Jahaz Israel fights and goes on to take cities, killing
their inhabitants, including women and little ones (Deuteronomy 2:32–35). A thematic parallel
does not make the events identical or remove the ethical difficulty of hardening followed by
destruction. Neither selected account explains away divine agency as mere foreknowledge.

This comparison concerns the stored WEB’s narrative assertions, not an assertion that the
Hebrew verbs are identical. Sources: stored WEB Exodus 4:21–23; 14:4; Deuteronomy 2:24–37.

**Extended to the kings of Canaan.** Joshua 11:20 applies the same pattern to the kings west of
the Jordan: *it was of Yahweh to harden their hearts, to come against Israel in battle, that he
might utterly destroy them, that they might have no favor*. Here the hardening explains why no
city but Gibeon made peace (11:19). Pharaoh's hardening ends in escape from an army, Sihon's and
the Canaanite kings' in battle and destruction. See
[[No City Made Peace - Deuteronomy 20 and Joshua 11.19-20]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:19–20;
no claim is made about the Hebrew verb in Joshua 11:20.

## Significance

Hardening links the departure from captivity with the approach to possession, while the different
forms of violence remain visible.
