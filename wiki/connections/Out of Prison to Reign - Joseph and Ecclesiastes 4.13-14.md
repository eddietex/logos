# Out of Prison to Reign - Joseph and Ecclesiastes 4.13-14

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 39:20** — *"Joseph’s master took him, and put him into the prison, the place where the
  king’s prisoners were bound, and he was there in custody."* — [[Joseph in Potiphar's House]]
- **Genesis 41:14** — *"Then Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and they brought him hastily out of the
  dungeon."* — [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Genesis 41:39–40** — *"there is no one so discreet and wise as you. You shall be over my house,
  and according to your word will all my people be ruled. Only in the throne I will be greater than
  you."* — [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Ecclesiastes 4:13–14** — *"Better is a poor and wise youth than an old and foolish king who
  doesn’t know how to receive admonition any more. For out of prison he came out to be king; yes,
  even in his kingdom he was born poor."* — [[The Youth and the Old King]]
- **Ecclesiastes 4:16** — *"yet those who come after shall not rejoice in him."* —
  [[The Youth and the Old King]]

## Analysis

**The shared pattern.** In both texts a young man is taken from a prison into rule, and in both
his qualification is wisdom. Pharaoh says *"there is no one so discreet and wise as you"* (Genesis
41:39); the Preacher calls his youth *"poor and wise"* (Ecclesiastes 4:13). Each also has a ruler
set against the young man: Pharaoh, who listens, and the old king, who no longer can.

**The differences.** Joseph does not become king. Pharaoh keeps the throne (*"Only in the throne
I will be greater than you"*, 41:40), and Joseph rules under him as a second. The youth of
Ecclesiastes *"came out to be king"*. Genesis says nothing of Joseph being born poor. He is his
father's favoured son (Genesis 37:3) and becomes a slave, while the youth of 4:14 is
*"born poor"*. And the stories end differently. Joseph's rise is the means by which life is
preserved, and Genesis gives it lasting consequence. The Preacher's youth is surrounded by *"no end
of all the people"*, and then *"those who come after shall not rejoice in him"* (4:16).

**Not an identification.** Ecclesiastes names no one, and the differences above keep the wiki from
reading 4:13–16 as a retelling of Joseph. The connection is a shared motif, wisdom raised from
prison to rule, used to different ends. Genesis uses it to show providence at work. Ecclesiastes
uses it to show that even a deserved rise is forgotten. The Exodus text that follows Genesis
supplies a case of that forgetting in Joseph's own story: *"a new king over Egypt, who didn’t
know Joseph"* (Exodus 1:8; see [[The Oppression in Egypt]]). Ecclesiastes does not cite it, and
the parallel is the wiki's observation, not the text's claim.

**Proverbs 30:22's warning.** Proverbs lists *"a servant when he is king"* among the things under
which the earth trembles (30:21–22; [[Four Things]]). Ecclesiastes 4:13 is not that saying's
contradiction: Proverbs names an unfit ruler's elevation, while the Preacher's youth is *wise*.
Status does not decide either case. Ecclesiastes 10:7's *"servants on horses"*, in
[[Folly in High Places and Everyday Work]], is closer to the Proverbs warning. See
[[Servants on Horses - Proverbs 19.10, 30.21-22, and Ecclesiastes 10.5-7]].

## Significance

The motif links Joseph's story to the Preacher's inquiry into [[Wisdom]] and
[[Kingship and Empire]]. It also shows two uses of one shape: in Genesis, an elevation that God
directs toward the preservation of life, and in Ecclesiastes, an elevation measured against
[[Remembrance]] and judged [[Vanity]]. It concerns [[Joseph]] and
[[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]], and [[The Preacher (Ecclesiastes)]].
