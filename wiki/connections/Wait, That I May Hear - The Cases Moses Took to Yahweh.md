# Wait, That I May Hear - The Cases Moses Took to Yahweh

## Passages involved

- [[The Blasphemer and One Law for All]] — Leviticus 24:10–23; *They put him in custody, until Yahweh's will should be declared to them* (12)
- [[The Passover in the Second Year]] — Numbers 9:6–14; *Wait, that I may hear what Yahweh will command concerning you* (8)
- [[The Sabbath Breaker]] — Numbers 15:32–36; *They put him in custody, because it had not been declared what should be done to him* (34)
- [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]] — Numbers 27:1–11; Moses submits the petition (5).
- Numbers 36:1–12 — the same inheritance, questioned by the heads of Gilead's family *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

### The inheritance petition now ingested

The five daughters appear before Moses, Eleazar, the princes, and the whole congregation
at the Tent entrance (27:1–2). Moses brings their cause to Yahweh (5). The answer first
confirms their claim (7), then lays down a succession rule for Israel (8–11). Like the
Passover request in Numbers 9:6–14, an appeal about exclusion produces a provision for
future cases. Unlike the custody cases, these petitioners are not accused of wrongdoing.

See [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]].


### The Sabbath case now ingested

Numbers 15:33 names Aaron and the congregation alongside Moses; verses 35–36 specify
communal stoning outside the camp. Compare Leviticus 24:12–14, 23: both narratives move from
custody through Yahweh’s ruling to execution. Leviticus also commands the hearers to lay hands
on the offender; Numbers 15 gives no such instruction. Exodus 31:14–15 already prescribes
death for Sabbath work, so the unresolved question in Numbers must not be described as the
absence of any prior law. Whether method or application was pending is not stated.

See [[The Sabbath Breaker]]. Source: stored WEB Exodus 31, Leviticus 24, Numbers 15.

The Torah's law is mostly given as speech from Yahweh with no prompting. In these cases someone brings a
situation requiring a further ruling, Moses does not rule, and Yahweh answers.

| Text | Who brings it | Held in custody? | The answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leviticus 24 | Accused, by the people | Yes | Death, and a law for the future |
| Numbers 9 | The men themselves | No | A second date, and a law for the future |
| Numbers 15 | Accused, by those who found him | Yes | Death |
| Numbers 27 | The daughters themselves | No | *The daughters of Zelophehad speak right* (7), and a law for the future |
| Numbers 36 | The family heads | No | *The tribe of the sons of Joseph speaks right* (5), and a limit on the earlier ruling |

Two of the cases are accusations, and the other three are requests. The wording shows that split. Leviticus
24:12 and Numbers 15:34 both use *wayyannîḥu ... bammišmār*, *they put in custody* (WLC), and both use the verb
*pāraš* for a declaration that was still awaited. Numbers 9 and 27 have no custody, because nobody has done
anything wrong.

Numbers 9:7 and 27:4 share a question. *Lāmmâ* with *gāraʿ*, *why ... be taken away*, is tagged in these two
verses only (WLC). The men fear being kept from the offering, and the daughters fear their father's name
being lost from his family. The second has the wider answer, and Numbers 36 then shows that a ruling can
itself raise a further question.

## Significance

These cases show that the wiki should not read the law as complete at Sinai in every detail. Moses is the
channel and not the source, and he says so at Numbers 9:8. Being without a ruling is treated as normal. The
two petitions also show people arguing for inclusion and getting it, without any suggestion that asking was
wrong. See [[Law and Obedience]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

Why these cases are told when others were not is not stated. Exodus 18:15–16, 26 has Moses inquiring of God
for hard cases in general, so these may be examples of a wider practice. That link is the wiki's.
