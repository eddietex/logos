# Time and Chance and the Prepared Horse

## Passages involved

- [[The King's Heart]] — Proverbs 21:30–31.
- [[One Event Comes to All]] — Ecclesiastes 9:1, 10–12.
- [[A Time for Every Purpose]] — Ecclesiastes 3:11–14.

## Analysis

Proverbs 21:30–31 denies that wisdom, understanding, or counsel can stand against
Yahweh, then distinguishes preparing a horse for battle from the source of victory:
the latter belongs with Yahweh. Preparation is described, not prohibited.

Ecclesiastes 9:11 similarly breaks the expected link between capacity and success:
the swift do not necessarily win the race, the strong do not secure battle, and
wisdom, understanding, and skill do not guarantee bread, riches, and favor. Its
stated common factor is time and chance. Verse 12 shows people taken unawares by
sudden evil like fish or birds caught in a trap.

**Related limit, different explanation.** Proverbs names Yahweh as the source of
victory; Ecclesiastes 9:11 names time and chance. These cannot simply be substituted
for one another. Yet Ecclesiastes 9:1 already places the righteous, wise, and their
works in God's hand, so its observation does not by itself assert a world outside
God's governance. The text leaves that relationship unexplained. Ecclesiastes
3:11–14 likewise affirms God's work and the human inability to find it out.

Neither passage recommends abandoning effort. Ecclesiastes 9:10 expressly commands
work with might immediately before it denies that ability secures the prize. The
comparison therefore limits control rather than supplying an excuse for idleness.
It neither claims that every success is deserved nor explains why a specific person
suffers defeat. No literary borrowing or shared Hebrew expression is asserted.

## Significance

[[Providence]], [[Wisdom]], [[Work]], and [[War]] meet at the limit of preparation.
Skill and effort matter in life without becoming a guarantee; confidence in God
does not make the cause of every outcome known to the observer.
