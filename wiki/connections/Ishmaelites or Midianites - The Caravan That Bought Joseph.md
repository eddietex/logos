# Ishmaelites or Midianites - The Caravan That Bought Joseph

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:25** — *"saw a caravan of **Ishmaelites** was coming from Gilead."*
- **Genesis 37:27** — *"Come, and let's sell him to the **Ishmaelites**."*
- **Genesis 37:28** — *"**Midianites** who were merchants passed by, and they drew and lifted up Joseph out
  of the pit, and sold Joseph to the **Ishmaelites** for twenty pieces of silver."*
- **Genesis 37:36** — *"The **Midianites** sold him into Egypt to Potiphar."*
- **Genesis 39:1** — *"Potiphar … bought him from the hand of the **Ishmaelites** who had brought him
  down there."*
- **Genesis 16:15; 25:12–18** — Ishmael, Abraham's son by Hagar, and his twelve princes.
- **Genesis 25:1–4** — Midian, Abraham's son by Keturah.
- **Judges 8:22–24** — *"for they had golden earrings, because they were **Ishmaelites**"* — said of the
  **Midianite** kings Gideon had just defeated.

## Analysis

**Five verses name the traders and they do not agree, and the disagreement is inside a single verse.**

| verse | the caravan is | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| 37:25 | **Ishmaelite** | comes from Gilead with spices, bound for Egypt |
| 37:27 | **Ishmaelite** | Judah proposes selling to them |
| 37:28a | **Midianite** | draws Joseph out of the pit |
| 37:28b | **Ishmaelite** | is sold Joseph for twenty shekels |
| 37:36 | **Midianite** | sells him in Egypt to Potiphar |
| 39:1 | **Ishmaelite** | is who Potiphar bought him from |

**Read with maximum strictness, 37:28 says that Midianites pulled Joseph out of the cistern and sold him
to Ishmaelites — which would leave the brothers as spectators at the sale they had just agreed on**, and
would make 37:36's *"the Midianites sold him into Egypt"* contradict both 37:28b and 39:1.

### Three readings, and the wiki adopts none

1. **The two names are overlapping designations for the same people, and Genesis uses them
   interchangeably.** *Ishmaelite* functions in some biblical usage as a general term for the
   camel-borne trading peoples of the northern Arabian desert; *Midianite* is one such people. **Judges
   8:24 does exactly this**, calling the defeated **Midianite** kings **Ishmaelites** in a subordinate
   clause with no sense of a correction — **and it is the same Gideon narrative that has been calling them
   Midianites for two chapters.** On this reading there is one caravan, the brothers sell to it, and the
   variation is stylistic. **This is the traditional harmonisation and it has a real proof text.**
2. **Composite sources.** The chapter interleaves two accounts of the sale: one in which the brothers sell
   Joseph to Ishmaelites on [[Judah]]'s initiative, and one in which **Midianites find and remove him from
   the pit** while the brothers — following [[Reuben]]'s plan — had only meant to leave him there, which is
   why **Reuben returns to an empty pit and is genuinely surprised** (37:29–30). On this reading the two
   strands explain more than the names: they explain **why there are two intercessors (Reuben and Judah),
   two disposal plans (the pit and the sale), and a Reuben who does not know what happened.** This is the
   standard critical account.
3. **The narrative is deliberately reporting a chain of hands.** Midianite middlemen pull him out and
   resell him to the larger Ishmaelite caravan heading for Egypt; 37:36's *Midianites* is then loose usage
   for *the traders*, or the Midianites travelled with the caravan as far as Egypt. **This reading keeps
   both names literal and costs a little in 37:36.**

**The wiki records all three and adopts none.** What is worth noting is that **reading 2 explains a
narrative feature and not merely a word**: Reuben's astonishment at the empty pit is hard to place if he
was present when his brothers agreed to a sale, and easy to place if the only plan he knew about was the
cistern.

### They are both Abraham's

**Whatever the traders are called, the wiki notes what Genesis has already established about them.**

- **[[Ishmael]]** is Abraham's firstborn, by Hagar (16:15), and Genesis gave him a *toledot* and twelve
  princes before turning back to Isaac (25:12–18).
- **[[Midian]]** is Abraham's son by **[[Keturah]]** (25:2), in the list Genesis supplies immediately
  before Abraham's death.

**So the men who carry Joseph out of the land of promise are his cousins**, from the two lines the book
wrote out of the inheritance and then recorded carefully anyway. See
[[The Other Line First - The Order of the Toledot in Genesis]] and
[[Abraham's Other Sons in Isaiah 60]].

**And the descendants of both are still on this road later.** The Midianites Gideon defeats are a camel
people with golden earrings (Judges 8:21–26); Moses marries a Midianite priest's daughter (Exodus 2:21);
**Isaiah 60:6 has the camels of Midian and Ephah bringing gold and incense to Zion** — *"the multitude of
camels shall cover you"* — **the same freight as the caravan at 37:25, going the other way.**

## Significance

- **It is the wiki's clearest case of a contradiction the text does not notice and the reader cannot
  resolve.** The two names sit in one verse. **No ancient version smooths it** — the Septuagint, the
  Samaritan Pentateuch, and the Vulgate all keep both — and **the discrepancy is old enough that whoever
  transmitted the chapter was content with it.**
- **It changes who is guilty of what, which is why it is worth a page rather than a footnote.** On reading
  1 the brothers are slave traders. On reading 2 **ten of them agreed to a sale and somebody else made
  it**, and Reuben is innocent of everything except failing. **Genesis 42:21–22 and 45:4–5 both have the
  brothers accept the sale as their own act** — *"I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt"* —
  which the wiki records as the book's own final position, without using it to settle the textual question.
- **It is a good test of the wiki's practice.** The convention is to set out the readings, mark what each
  costs, and adopt none — the same handling given to Benjamin's birthplace at 35:26, Bilhah's three job
  titles, and the three sites for Rachel's grave. **Here the harmonising reading has an explicit proof
  text in Judges 8:24, which is more than most such cases offer**, and it still does not account for
  Reuben's surprise.
- **It is one of the two places the Joseph cycle's seams are visible on the surface**, the other being the
  double intercession of [[Reuben]] and [[Judah]] in the same chapter — **and they are probably the same
  seam.** See [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]].
