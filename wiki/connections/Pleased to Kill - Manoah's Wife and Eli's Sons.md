# Pleased to Kill - Manoah's Wife and Eli's Sons

## Passages involved

- [[The Announcement of Samson's Birth]] — Judges 13:23: *But his wife said to him, "If Yahweh were
  pleased to kill us, he wouldn't have received a burnt offering and a meal offering at our hand, and he
  wouldn't have shown us all these things, nor would he have told us such things as these at this
  time."*
- [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] — 1 Samuel 2:25: *Notwithstanding, they didn't listen to the voice of their
  father, because Yahweh intended to kill them.*

## Analysis

**The same clause, turned.** Judges 13:23 has *lû ḥāp̄ēṣ YHWH lahămîṯēnû*, *if Yahweh had desired to put
us to death*; 1 Samuel 2:25 has *kî-ḥāp̄ēṣ YHWH lahămîṯām*, *because Yahweh desired to put them to death*
(WLC). The verb *ḥāp̄ēṣ*, *desire, be pleased*, with Yahweh as subject and the Hiphil infinitive of
*mûṯ*, occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 2 Samuel only in these two verses (lemma search,
OpenScriptures morphhb XML). The WEB renders the same Hebrew two ways, *were pleased to kill* and
*intended to kill*.

**The offering as evidence.** Manoah's wife reasons from the sacrifice: Yahweh *received a burnt offering
and a meal offering at our hand*, *ʿōlâ ûminḥâ* (WLC), so he does not mean to kill them. Eli's sons
*despised the offering of Yahweh*, *minḥaṯ YHWH* (1 Samuel 2:17, WLC), and of them the narrator says he
does mean to. In both, the offering is the sign of where a family stands with Yahweh: received from one
barren couple's hand, treated with contempt by the priests' sons. The juxtaposition is a reader's; neither
text refers to the other.

**Both stand at the start of a birth story's sequel.** Manoah's wife is the barren mother of Samson; Hannah
is the barren mother of Samuel, and her son is growing beside Eli's sons in the same pericope (2:18–26).
See [[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]] and
[[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].

**The hard clause.** 1 Samuel 2:25 makes the sons' refusal to listen a consequence of Yahweh's intent,
not only its cause. It invites comparison with the hardening of Pharaoh's heart in Exodus. The narrator
does not explain how this sits with the sons' responsibility, which 2:17 and 2:29 assert; the wiki
records both and does not harmonize them. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

## Significance

- It sets the sons of Eli against the one figure in Judges who reasons rightly about Yahweh from an
  accepted offering. See [[Manoah's Wife]], [[Manoah]], [[Hophni]], and [[Phinehas (son of Eli)]].
- It adds 1 Samuel 2:25 to the wiki's material on divine intent behind human refusal. See
  [[Sacrifice and Offering]] and [[Judgment]].
