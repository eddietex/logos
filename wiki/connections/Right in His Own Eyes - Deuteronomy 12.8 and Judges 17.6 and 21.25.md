# Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25

## Passages involved

- [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]] — Deuteronomy 12:8: *"You shall not do all the things that we
  do here today, every man whatever is right in his own eyes"*; set against *"that which is right
  in Yahweh's eyes"* (12:25) and *"good and right in Yahweh your God's eyes"* (12:28).
- Judges 17:6 and 21:25 — *"In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did that which was
  right in his own eyes."* WEB, verified from bible-api.com; Judges not yet ingested.
- [[Whoever Loves Correction]] — Proverbs 12:15: *"The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but
  he who is wise listens to counsel."*
- [[The King's Heart]] — Proverbs 21:2: *"Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but Yahweh
  weighs the hearts."*

## Analysis

The English phrase *right in his own eyes* appears in the stored WEB only in Deuteronomy 12:8 and
Proverbs 12:15 and 21:2. Judges 17:6 and 21:25 repeat it word for word in the WEB. This page
compares the English wording. No claim is made that the Hebrew is identical in every occurrence;
that has not been checked.

**Deuteronomy 12** uses the phrase for a practice that is about to end. Moses includes himself —
*"the things that we do here today"* — and explains why it has been tolerated: *"you haven't yet
come to the rest and to the inheritance"* (12:9). The context is worship: offerings brought *"in
every place that you see"* (12:13) rather than at the place Yahweh chooses. The corrective is
another set of eyes, *"right in Yahweh's eyes"* (12:25, 28).

**Judges** uses the same words as a refrain for the land after the conquest. The verse opens
with *"In those days there was no king in Israel"*, so it explains the disorder by the absence of
a king, not by the absence of rest. Judges 17:6 sits inside the story of Micah's household shrine
and private priest. The wiki has not yet ingested Judges and does not summarize that story beyond
the verse checked here. If the shrine narrative proves to be what it appears to be, worship
outside any chosen place, it would be the very practice Deuteronomy 12 forbids. That reading is
provisional until Judges 17–18 is ingested.

**Proverbs** uses the phrase without a narrative setting: the fool's way (12:15), and every
person's way, weighed by Yahweh (21:2). It is the wisdom literature's version of the same contrast,
human sight against Yahweh's judgment.

Read together, the three settings give three answers to *right in his own eyes*: the chosen place
and Yahweh's eyes (Deuteronomy), a king (Judges, as its refrain implies), and counsel and Yahweh's
weighing of hearts (Proverbs). The wiki does not make any of them the key to the others.

**Deuteronomy 13:18 now ingested.** The next unit closes on the same contrast: obedience is *"to
do that which is right in Yahweh your God's eyes"* (13:18), here set against following other gods
rather than against worship at many places. See [[Do Not Follow Other Gods]].

Source for this addition: stored WEB Deuteronomy 13:18.

## Significance

The phrase ties Deuteronomy's law of the place to the history that follows. Moses describes his
own generation's practice as each man doing what is right in his own eyes, and Judges uses the
same words for Israel in the land. Deuteronomy expected the rest to change it; Judges says it did
not. See also [[Wise in His Own Eyes - Proverbs 3.7, 26.12, Isaiah 5.21, and Romans 12.16]],
[[Law and Obedience]], [[Seeing and Being Seen]], and [[Kingship and Empire]].
