## Analysis

The priest carries names in two directions. In the sanctuary, *Aaron shall bear their names before
Yahweh on his two shoulders for a memorial* (Exodus 28:12), and again *in the breastplate of judgment on
his heart* (28:29). So the tribes' names are brought to Yahweh on Aaron's body. Outside it, in Numbers 6:27,
the priests *put my name on the children of Israel*. Yahweh's name is brought to the tribes in Aaron's
mouth.

The two passages do not cite each other, and the verbs differ: *nāśāʾ*, *bear*, at Exodus 28:12 and *śîm*,
*put*, at Numbers 6:27. The comparison is the wiki's. It is made because of the shape of the office, not
because of shared wording.

Aaron also wears Yahweh's name in a way of its own, on the plate reading *HOLY TO YAHWEH* (Exodus 28:36).
What is engraved on the priest is spoken over the people, but nothing in Numbers says that the people
receive what the plate says. The plate concerns the iniquity of the holy things (28:38), and the
blessing concerns keeping, grace, and peace.

The object of *put my name* is the unusual part. In Deuteronomy and Kings the name is put on a place
(Deuteronomy 12:5; 1 Kings 9:3). Exodus 20:24 joins the name and the blessing at a place: *In every place
where I record my name I will come to you and I will bless you.* Numbers 6:27 has the same two acts, the
name and *I will bless*, but the name is on people. A moving people gets, by speech, what a place will
later get. That last step is the wiki's reading, and Numbers does not say it. Deuteronomy 28:10 says
something close from the outside: other peoples will see that Israel is called by Yahweh's name, though
that verse uses a different verb.

## Significance

The comparison shows the priest as a go-between in both directions, and shows that a name can be placed
on a people as well as on a site. Hebrew from WLC (morphhb); no commentary consulted.
