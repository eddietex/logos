# Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24

## Passages involved

- [[Samuel Anoints Saul]] — **1 Samuel 10:11–12** — *When all who knew him before saw that, behold, he prophesied
  with the prophets, then the people said to one another, "What is this that has come to the son of Kish? Is Saul
  also among the prophets?" One of the same place answered, "Who is their father?" Therefore it became a proverb,
  "Is Saul also among the prophets?"* Stored WEB.
- **1 Samuel 19:23–24** — *Then God's Spirit came on him also, and he went on, and prophesied, until he came to
  Naioth in Ramah. He also stripped off his clothes, and he also prophesied before Samuel, and lay down naked all
  that day and all that night. Therefore they say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"* Stored WEB; not yet
  ingested.
- **1 Samuel 18:10** — *an evil spirit from God came mightily on Saul, and he prophesied in the middle of the
  house*. Stored WEB; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

**One proverb, two origins.** Both passages end on the same four Hebrew words, *hăḡam šāʾûl bannəḇîʾîm* (10:11,
12; 19:24, WLC; 19:24 is written defectively, *bannəḇîʾim*). Each also gives the saying a cause, with *ʿal-kēn*,
*therefore*: *ʿal-kēn hāyəṯâ ləmāšāl*, *therefore it became a proverb* (10:12), and *ʿal-kēn yōʾmərû*, *therefore
they say* (19:24). The book tells where the proverb came from twice, and the two stories differ.

| | 1 Samuel 10:5–13 | 1 Samuel 19:18–24 |
|---|---|---|
| Saul is | newly anointed, going home | king, hunting David |
| the prophets | *a band*, *ḥeḇel*, coming down from the high place with music | *the company*, *lahăqaṯ*, with Samuel *standing as head over them* |
| the Spirit | *came mightily*, *wattiṣlaḥ* (10:10) | *came on him also*, *wattəhî … gam-hûʾ* (19:23) |
| what follows | *turned into another man*; *another heart* (10:6, 9) | strips, and lies naked before Samuel all day and night (19:24) |
| who speaks | *all who knew him before*; *one of the same place* | unnamed: *they say* |

(Hebrew from WLC, lemmas 6743 and 5012; English stored WEB.)

**In chapter 10 the Spirit's coming is a sign of his choosing.** Samuel promised it as the third sign (10:5–7), and
it confirms the anointing. The question *Is Saul also among the prophets?* comes from people who knew the son of
Kish and are surprised to find him there.

**In chapter 19 the same Spirit stops him.** Saul has sent three groups of messengers to seize David, and each
group prophesies instead (19:20–21). Then he goes himself, and *the Spirit of God came on him also* on the road
(19:23). The prophesying keeps him from what he came to do, and he ends up lying before the prophet who had
anointed him. In between, *an evil spirit from God came mightily on Saul, and he prophesied in the middle of the
house* while David played (18:10), and Saul threw the spear (18:11, stored WEB; not yet ingested). By then
*prophesying* can name a frenzy as well as a gift.

**The wiki does not choose between the two origins.** Some read them as two traditions about one saying. Others
read the second as a deliberate echo, showing what the first sign became. Both are recalled readings, not checked,
and the text presents both stories without comment. The question can also be heard two ways. In chapter 10 it
sounds like wonder. In chapter 19 it sounds more like mockery. The text does not settle the tone of either.

## Significance

- **Saul's kingship opens and his time with Samuel closes on the same question.** He is made king in a scene where
  he prophesies. The last scene in which he and the living Samuel are together is also a scene where he prophesies
  (19:24), and Samuel's death is reported at 25:1 (stored WEB). **Recorded, not resolved:** 15:35 says *Samuel came
  no more to see Saul until the day of his death* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). At 19:24 it is Saul who comes to
  Samuel, and whether the two verses conflict depends on how 15:35 is read. The wiki notes this and does not
  decide.
- **The Spirit that comes on him is not shown as approval.** As with Samson
  ([[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]]), the Spirit's coming gives power or ecstasy. It does not
  show that the person is faithful.
- **The proverb is the book's first explained proverb** (*māšāl*, 10:12, WLC). The pattern of a saying with an
  origin story is common in Genesis place names. Here it is applied to a person. See [[Wisdom]] and
  [[Prophecy and Vision]].
