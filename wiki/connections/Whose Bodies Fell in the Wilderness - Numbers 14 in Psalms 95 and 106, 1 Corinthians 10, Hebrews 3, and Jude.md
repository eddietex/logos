# Whose Bodies Fell in the Wilderness - Numbers 14 in Psalms 95 and 106, 1 Corinthians 10, Hebrews 3, and Jude

## Passages involved

- [[The People Refuse the Land]] — Numbers 14:16, 23, 29–35
- [[The Spies and the Refusal to Go Up]] — Deuteronomy 1:32, *in this thing you didn't believe Yahweh your God* *(stored WEB)*
- Psalm 95:10–11 — *They won't enter into my rest* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Psalm 106:24–27 — *he swore to them that he would overthrow them in the wilderness* *(WEB checked,
  not yet ingested)*
- 1 Corinthians 10:5, 10 — *they were overthrown in the wilderness* *(WEB and SBLGNT checked, not
  yet ingested)*
- Hebrews 3:16–19 — *whose bodies fell in the wilderness* *(WEB and SBLGNT checked, not yet
  ingested)*
- Jude 5 — *afterward destroyed those who didn't believe* *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Psalm 106 is the closest reading in the Hebrew.** *Wayyimʾăsû bəʾereṣ ḥemdâ* (24, WLC) has the
verb and preposition of *məʾastem bāh* (Numbers 14:31). *Lōʾ heʾĕmînû* is *lōʾ-yaʾămînû* (14:11).
*Lōʾ šāməʿû bəqôl YHWH* is *lōʾ šāməʿû bəqôlî* (14:22). *Wayyiśśāʾ yādô lāhem ləhappîl ʾôtām
bammidbār* (26) has the raised hand of 14:30 and the causative of the verb of 14:29, *to make them
fall in the wilderness*. WEB's *swore* and *overthrow* hide both. Verse 27 goes beyond Numbers and
adds the scattering among the nations, so the exile is read back into the wilderness oath.

**Psalm 95 keeps the oath and changes the object.** *ʾIm-yəbōʾûn ʾel-mənûḥātî* (11, WLC) is the oath
form of Numbers 14:23, 30, and *forty years* is here. But *the land* has become *my rest*. Psalm 95
also sets this after Massah and Meribah, not at Kadesh. See
[[Do Not Test Yahweh - Massah in Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and Hebrews]].

**Hebrews reads Numbers through the psalm, and its wording comes from the Greek of Numbers.** *Ὧν τὰ
κῶλα ἔπεσεν ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ* (3:17, SBLGNT) matches Rahlfs at Numbers 14:29 and 32, *πεσεῖται τὰ κῶλα
ὑμῶν* with *ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ ταύτῃ*. *Κῶλα* is an unusual word, and in the Greek New Testament this seems
to be its only use, though that is recalled and was not searched. It is the plainest verbal link to
this chapter in the New Testament material the wiki has checked.

**Not able.** Hebrews 3:19 ends with *οὐκ ἠδυνήθησαν εἰσελθεῖν δι᾿ ἀπιστίαν*, *they were not able to
enter in because of unbelief* (SBLGNT). In Numbers the ten said *we are not able* (13:31), and Moses
feared the nations would say Yahweh was not able (14:16; Rahlfs has *δύνασθαι*). Hebrews settles the
question: the inability was theirs, and its cause is the one Yahweh named at 14:11. That chain is
the wiki's reading, and Hebrews does not cite those verses.

**Paul uses the nations' verb.** *Κατεστρώθησαν γὰρ ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ* (1 Corinthians 10:5, SBLGNT) has
the verb and phrase of Rahlfs at Numbers 14:16, *κατέστρωσεν αὐτοὺς ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ*. In Numbers that is
the slander Moses dreads, and Paul states it as what happened. He does not say Yahweh was unable.
*Most of them* is his qualification, and it leaves room for Caleb and Joshua. 10:10 has *γογγύζετε*
and *ἐγόγγυσαν*, Rahlfs's word at Numbers 14:27 and 29, though Paul may also have Numbers 16 in
view.

**Jude sets the rescue against the end.** *Having saved a people out of the land of Egypt, afterward
destroyed those who didn't believe* (Jude 5). The same people experienced both. Jude does not name
Numbers.

(morphhb). Greek of Numbers from Rahlfs 1935 (eliranwong LXX-Rahlfs-1935), and of 1 Corinthians and

## Significance

- This is the Old Testament's standing warning: the people who came out did not necessarily get in.
- The New Testament writers address it to people who have already been rescued, which is the
  situation of Numbers 14.
