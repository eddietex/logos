# Go Back or Cling - Joshua 23.12 and Ruth 1.14-16

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 2:24** — [[The Garden of Eden]]: *"Therefore a man will leave his father and his mother, and
  will join with his wife, and they will be one flesh."* (*wədāḇaq*)
- **Deuteronomy 10:20; 30:20** — *"you shall cling to him"*; *"to love Yahweh your God, to obey his voice,
  and to cling to him"* — [[The Word Is Near and the Choice Is Life]]
- **Joshua 23:8, 12** — [[Joshua's Farewell to the Leaders]]: *"but hold fast to Yahweh your God, as you
  have done to this day"*; *"But if you do at all go back, and hold fast to the remnant of these nations,
  even these who remain among you, and make marriages with them …"*
- **Ruth 1:14–16** — [[Ruth Clings to Naomi]]: *"Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth joined with
  her."* Naomi: *"your sister-in-law has gone back to her people, and to her god."* Ruth: *"Don't urge me
  to leave you, and to return from following you."*

## Analysis

**Two verbs.** *Šûb*, *to go back* or *return*, and *dābaq*, *to cling* or *hold fast*. Joshua 23:12 sets
them in one sentence: *kî ʾim-šôḇ tāšûḇû ûḏəḇaqtem bəyeṯer haggôyim*, *if you do at all go back, and hold
fast to the remnant of these nations*, and the next words are about marriage. Ruth 1:14–16 sets them in
one scene: Orpah *goes back* (*šāḇâ*, 15) to her people and her god, and Ruth *clings* (*dāḇəqâ*, 14) to
Naomi, refusing *to return from following you* (*lāšûḇ mēʾaḥărāyiḵ*, 16). Both verbs are confirmed in the
WLC at Joshua 23:12 and Ruth 1:14–16 (lemmas 7725 and 1692).

| | Who goes back | Who clings | To whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joshua 23:12 | Israel (warned against it) | Israel | *the remnant of these nations*, by marriage |
| Ruth 1:14–16 | Orpah, *to her people, and to her god* | Ruth, a Moabite | Naomi, and through her Israel's people and God |

**The direction is reversed.** Joshua warns Israel not to turn back and cling to the nations through
marriage. In Ruth, a woman of the nations, already married into Israel and widowed, turns away from her own
people and clings to Israel. The verbs are the same; the movement runs the other way.

**And the verb is the marriage verb.** Genesis 2:24 says a man *will leave his father and his mother, and
will join with his wife* (*dābaq*). Boaz will say of Ruth that she *left your father and your mother, and
the land of your birth* (2:11). In the same chapter the verb comes back three times for Ruth staying
close to Boaz's young women (*tiḏbāqîn*, 2:8, 21; *wattiḏbaq*, 2:23; see [[Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz]]). The leaving of Genesis 2:24 is a man's leaving for his
wife; Ruth leaves for her mother-in-law. Deuteronomy uses the same verb for Israel's loyalty to Yahweh
(10:20; 11:22; 13:4; 30:20), and so does Joshua 23:8. In Ruth 1 the verb of marriage and the verb of
covenant loyalty describe one widow's attachment to another.

**Limits.** Ruth 1 does not quote or mention Joshua 23, and *šûb* and *dābaq* are common verbs. The wiki
records the shared pair and the reversal; it does not claim the narrator of Ruth wrote with Joshua 23 in
view. In the WLC files read (Genesis, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel), *dābaq* occurs 23 times;
Joshua 23:12 is the one verse that joins it both to *going back* and to marriage with the nations, and Ruth
1:14–16 is the one scene that joins it to a foreign woman leaving her people. (The two verbs also
share a verse at Deuteronomy 13:17 and 28:60, English numbering, where *cling* is used of the devoted
thing and of diseases, not of people.) Proverbs 18:24's friend who
*sticks closer than a brother* uses the same verb (see
[[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]]).

## Significance

Israel was told to cling to Yahweh and warned that clinging to the nations would mean going back. Ruth
reverses both halves. She leaves her own people, and she clings, in words that bind people and God together
(*Your people will be my people, and your God my God*, 1:16). The book of [[Ruth]] ends with her in David's
line (4:17–22). See [[The Nations]], [[Marriage]], and [[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].
