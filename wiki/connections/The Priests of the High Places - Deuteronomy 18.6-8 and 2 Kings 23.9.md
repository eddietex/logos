# The Priests of the High Places - Deuteronomy 18.6-8 and 2 Kings 23.9

## Passages involved

- [[The Portion of the Priests and Levites]] — Deuteronomy 18:6–8, the Levite who comes from the towns
  to the chosen place.
- [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]] — Deuteronomy 12, worship at one place and not on every high hill.
- **2 Kings 23:8–9** *(not yet ingested)* — Josiah and the priests of the cities of Judah.

## Analysis

Deuteronomy 18:6–8 provides for a Levite who lives *"as a foreigner"* in one of Israel's towns and
comes *"with all the desire of his soul"* to the chosen place: *"he shall minister in the name of
Yahweh his God, as all his brothers the Levites do, who stand there before Yahweh. They shall have
like portions to eat"*.

2 Kings 23:8 reports that Josiah *"brought all the priests out of the cities of Judah, and defiled the
high places where the priests had burned incense, from Geba to Beersheba"*. Verse 9: *"Nevertheless the
priests of the high places didn't come up to Yahweh's altar in Jerusalem, but they ate unleavened bread
among their brothers"* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-07). *Bətôk ʾăḥêhem*, *among their
brothers* (WLC), sets them with the same *brothers* as Deuteronomy 18:7, *kəkol-ʾeḥāyw halwiyyim*.
They eat with them; they do not serve at the altar.

**Tension, recorded and not resolved.** Read together, the texts can be taken in at least three
ways:

- *2 Kings 23:9 records a law not applied.* The country priests were brought in but not given the
  altar right Deuteronomy 18:7 grants. This reading is common where Deuteronomy is linked to Josiah's
  reform, and it is a reading of the history, not something either text says.
- *The cases are different.* Deuteronomy's Levite comes from a town where he sojourns, to serve
  Yahweh at the chosen place; the priests of 2 Kings 23 had served at the high places. Deuteronomy
  12:2–3 orders the destruction of *"all the places in which the nations … served their gods, on the
  high mountains, and on the hills"*, though it does not use the word *bāmôt*, *high places*, which
  occurs in Deuteronomy only at 32:13 and 33:29 (WLC lemma 1116). Whether Judah's high places, where
  2 Kings says these priests burned incense, fall under that law is itself an inference. On this
  reading their exclusion is consistent with Deuteronomy, not a departure from it.
- *They received the livelihood but not the office.* *"They ate unleavened bread among their
  brothers"* matches Deuteronomy 18:8's *"like portions to eat"* without 18:7's ministry.

2 Kings does not cite Deuteronomy 18, and does not say whether these priests were Levites. The
reasons for their exclusion are not stated.

## Significance

Deuteronomy's law of one place raises a practical question its own text answers: what becomes of the
Levite of the towns. 2 Kings shows the question arising in history, among men who had served at other
altars. See [[Priesthood]] and [[The Dwelling of God]].
