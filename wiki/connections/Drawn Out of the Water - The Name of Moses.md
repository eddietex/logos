# Drawn Out of the Water - The Name of Moses

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 2:10** — *"She named him Moses, and said, 'Because I drew him out of the water'"* —
  [[The Birth of Moses]]
- **Exodus 1:11** — *"Pithom and Raamses"*, the store-cities — [[The Oppression in Egypt]]
- **Exodus 14–15** — Israel brought through the sea *(not yet ingested)*
- **2 Samuel 22:17; Psalm 18:16** — *"He drew me out of many waters"*
- **Isaiah 63:11** — *"Then he remembered the days of old, Moses and his people, saying, 'Where is
  he who brought them up out of the sea with the shepherds of his flock?'"*

## Analysis

**The princess's explanation and the shape of the name point in two directions.** She says
*məšîtihû*, *I drew him out*, from the verb *māšâ*. A name meaning *drawn out* would be passive,
*māšûy*. **But *Mōšeh* is the active participle: *the one who draws out*.** She names him for what
was done to him; the grammar names him for what he will do — draw Israel out through the sea.

***Māšâ* occurs only three times in the Hebrew Bible**: here, and in the twin texts of David's song —
*"He sent from on high and he took me. He drew me out of many waters"* (2 Samuel 22:17; Psalm
18:16). **The only other person *drawn out* is the king delivered from his enemies, pictured as
floodwaters.** Whether David's song is alluding to Moses or simply using a rare word for rescue from
water, the wiki does not decide.

**Isaiah 63:11 is the clearest echo.** In the prophet's lament God *"remembered the days of old,
Moses and his people"*, and asks *"Where is he who brought them up out of the sea"*. The Hebrew of
63:11 sets *mōšeh* beside the language of bringing up out of water, and the passage is usually read
as playing on the name. The wiki records it as a probable wordplay rather than a certain one.

**The Egyptian derivation runs alongside.** The name is usually connected with Egyptian *msy*, *to
bear*, *-mose* or *-messu*, *son*, *born*, familiar from Ahmose, Thutmose, and **Ramesses**, *Ra has
borne him* — the royal name behind *Raamses*, the city the Hebrews were building (1:11). **On that
derivation the princess gave her son the second half of a royal name**, the god's name left off,
and the narrator heard in it a Hebrew word. Both explanations can be true at once; the Egyptian one
is the standard scholarly proposal and not settled. See [[Rameses]].

## Significance

The name is the first of Exodus's many reversals told through a single word: the child put into the
water to die is drawn out of it, and will draw a people out of the sea in which Pharaoh's army
drowns. It also places Moses beside David as the Bible's two figures *drawn out of waters*. See
[[Names and Naming]], [[Moses]], and [[Providence]].
