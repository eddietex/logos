# Plucking a Neighbor's Grain - Deuteronomy 23.24-25 and the Sabbath Grain Fields

  into your neighbor's vineyard, then you may eat of grapes your fill at your own pleasure; but you
  shall not put any in your container. When you come into your neighbor's standing grain, then you may

## Analysis

**What the law allows.** Deuteronomy 23:24–25 lets anyone who *"comes into"* a neighbor's vineyard or
grain eat there. It draws the line at the tool. A hand that plucks and a mouth that eats are allowed.
A container (*kǝlî*) and a sickle (*ḥermēš*) are not. In the Torah the sickle occurs only here and at
16:9, where it marks the start of the owner's harvest (WLC, morphhb). The passer-by may eat; only the
owner may harvest.

**How it differs from the gleaning laws.** Leviticus 19:9–10 and Deuteronomy 24:19–21 name who benefits:
the poor, the foreigner, the fatherless, the widow. Those laws work at the owner's harvest, when he
leaves the edges and the forgotten. 23:24–25 names no class of beneficiary. It addresses any Israelite
who passes through, and it operates before the harvest, on standing grain. The two kinds of law
overlap in effect but differ in who they are written for.

**The Gospel scene presupposes it.** All three Synoptic accounts have the disciples plucking grain
while walking through fields, and all three record an objection about the Sabbath. Neither Matthew 12:2
nor Mark 2:24 records any charge of theft. The scene fits the permission of 23:25: a hand, no sickle,
eaten on the spot. On that reading the only dispute is the day. Luke 6:1's *"rubbing them in their
hands"* keeps the act to the hand. The Gospels do not cite Deuteronomy, and the wiki does not claim
that they allude to it. The connection is that the law explains why the objection is about the Sabbath
alone. The Greek of the Gospel texts was not checked.

## Significance

A small permission in Deuteronomy shapes a dispute in the Gospels. It also shows the law dividing
property and hunger by the hand. The neighbor's crop is still his, and the hungry passer-by still
eats. See [[Theft and Restitution]], [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]], and [[Sabbath]].
