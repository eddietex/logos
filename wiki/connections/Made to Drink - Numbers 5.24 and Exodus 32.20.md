# Made to Drink - Numbers 5.24 and Exodus 32.20

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 32:20** — [[The Golden Calf]]: *"He took the calf which they had made, and burned it with fire, ground it to powder, and scattered it on the water, and made the children of Israel drink it."*
- **Numbers 5:24, 26–27** — [[The Suspected Wife]]: *"He shall make the woman drink the water of bitterness that causes the curse"*.

## Analysis

Both verses have the same causative of *šāqâ* (*wayyašq*, Exodus 32:20; *wəhišqâ*, Numbers 5:24;
WLC). In Exodus 2 and Genesis 29 that verb means watering flocks, and it is an ordinary word. What
the two passages share is its use for a person forced to swallow something, in water, after a
suspected or known breach. Each water carries a residue: the powdered calf, or dust and washed-off
curses.

The differences are plain. Moses acts in anger and the guilt is known. The priest acts by rule and
the guilt is unknown. Exodus gives no reason for the drinking, and no result of it, while Numbers
states both results. The people who drink in Exodus are also not said to fall ill from the water:
the plague at 32:35 is attributed to Yahweh, because they made the calf.

**A reported reading, not adopted.** Some readers take the calf episode as a jealousy rite on a
national scale, with Israel as the unfaithful wife. That reading was not checked against a source in
this pass. Exodus 34:14–16 does put *Jealous* and prostitution after other gods side by side, so the
materials are present. But Exodus 32 does not say so, and the later prophetic marriage image is not
in view at this point in the wiki.

## Significance

The link is verbal and thematic only. It is worth keeping because it sets [[Envy]]'s divine sense
and its marital sense on one page, without claiming the Torah makes that connection here. See also
[[Curse]], [[Idolatry]], and [[Sin]].
