# You Have Fashioned Me as Clay - Job 10.8-12, Psalm 139, and the Potter

## Passages involved

- **Job 10:8–13** — *"Your hands have framed me and fashioned me altogether, yet you destroy me.
  Remember, I beg you, that you have fashioned me as clay… You have clothed me with skin and flesh,
  and knit me together with bones and sinews… Yet you hid these things in your heart."* —
  [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]]
- **Job 9:12** — *"Who will ask him, 'What are you doing?'"* — same passage
- **Genesis 2:7; 3:19** — man formed of dust; *"to dust you shall return."* —
  [[The Garden of Eden]], [[The Fall]]
- **Psalm 119:73** — *"Your hands have made me and formed me. Give me understanding, that I may learn
  your commandments."*
- **Psalm 139:13–16** — *"For you formed my inmost being. You knit me together in my mother's womb…
  My frame wasn't hidden from you, when I was made in secret, woven together in the depths of the
  earth."*
- **Isaiah 29:16; 45:9–10; 64:8** — the clay and the potter: *"Shall the clay ask him who fashions it,
  'What are you making?'"*; *"we are the clay, and you our potter."*
- **Jeremiah 18:1–6** — the potter's house: the vessel spoiled and reworked.
- **Romans 9:20–21** — *"Will the thing formed ask him who formed it, 'Why did you make me like
  this?'"*
- **Job 14:15; 31:15; 33:6** — *"the work of your hands"* (14:15, in [[Though He Slay Me]]: the potter
  imagined, beyond death, *desiring* what he made); *"Didn't he who made me in the womb make
  him?"*; Elihu: *"I am also formed out of the clay."*
- **Ecclesiastes 11:5; Ezekiel 37:6–8** — the forming of the body as mystery, and its re-forming, in
  sinews, flesh, and skin.
- **Job 23:8–10** — the other half of Job's use of Psalm 139: the God who cannot be escaped, turned
  into the God who cannot be found —
  [[Where Can I Go from Your Spirit - Job 23.8-10 and Psalm 139.7-12]]

## Analysis

**Job 10:8–12 and Psalm 139:13–16 are the Bible's two great accounts of being made in the womb, and
they share a verb.** Job: *ʿôr ûbāśār talbîšēnî, ûbəʿăṣāmôt wəgîdîm təśōkəkēnî* — *you clothed me
with skin and flesh, and wove me with bones and sinews* (10:11). The psalmist: *təsukkēnî bəbeṭen
ʾimmî* — *you wove me in my mother's womb* (139:13). The same verb in a variant spelling, used for the
same act, and both speakers say *you*. Psalm 119:73 shares Job's opening clause: *yādêkā ʿāśûnî*,
*your hands made me*.

**What the three speakers draw from it is the whole difference.** The psalmist draws praise and
trust: *"I will give thanks to you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made"* (139:14), and the God
who saw him unformed is the God who searches him and leads him (139:23–24). Psalm 119 draws a
petition: you made me, *so* give me understanding. Job draws an accusation: you made me, *yet* you
destroy me (10:8). And then a suspicion the psalm could not imagine: *"Yet you hid these things in
your heart"* (10:13). **The psalmist's God made him in secret and then searched him with love; Job's
God made him with care and searches him for sin** (10:6, 14). Psalm 139's *"search me, God"* is
Job's complaint word for word in the opposite mood — Job's *tidrôš*, *you search after my sin*
(10:6), against the psalmist's *ḥoqrēnî*, *search me* (139:23), the two ordinary Hebrew verbs of
searching.

**The potter's question.** Job 9:12 states the rule: *"Who will ask him, 'What are you doing?'"* —
*mah-taʿăśeh*. Isaiah 45:9 uses the identical two words as the question the clay may not put to the
potter: *"Shall the clay ask him who fashions it, 'What are you making (mah-taʿăśeh)?'"* Ecclesiastes
8:4 uses them of a king. **Job knows the rule, says it aloud, and then in chapter 10 asks the potter
exactly that**: you shaped me as clay (*ḥōmer*, Isaiah's word, 10:9) — will you now grind me back to
dust? Isaiah and Paul (Romans 9:20) both close the question as illegitimate. Job opens it, and the
book does not rebuke him for it; at 42:7 he is the one who spoke rightly.

**The dust.** *"Will you bring me into dust again?"* (10:9) — *wəʾel-ʿāpār təšîbēnî* — is Genesis 3:19's
*wəʾel-ʿāpār tāšûb*, *to dust you shall return*, turned from a sentence into a question. Genesis
pronounces the return to dust as the consequence of a sin. Job, who has asked to be shown his sin,
asks why it is being carried out.

**The later echoes in Job.** 14:15 imagines the hope this passage denies: *"You would call, and I
would answer you. You would have a desire to the work of your hands."* 31:15 turns the shared making
into ethics — *"Didn't he who made me in the womb make him?"* — the servant and the master formed by
the same hands. And Elihu, at 33:6, claims the clay for himself as a credential: *"I am toward God
even as you are. I am also formed out of the clay"* — offering to be the man-sized party Job said
God could not be (9:32).

## Significance

Every text in this thread affirms that God forms each person deliberately, in the womb, by hand. The
disagreement is over what that implies. For the psalmist it implies intimacy; for Isaiah and Paul,
the maker's absolute right; for Jeremiah, the freedom to rework a spoiled vessel; for Job it implies
an obligation — *you made me; why destroy what you made?* — and that is the form of the argument God
does not reject. See [[Creation]], [[Image of God]], and [[Life and Death]].
