# Into My Mother's House - Song of Solomon 3.4, 8.2, Genesis 24, and Ruth 1.8

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 3:4** — [[Seeking Him by Night]]: *"until I had brought him into my mother's
  house, into the room of her who conceived me."* (*ʾel-bêt ʾimmî wǝʾel-ḥeder hôrātî*)
- **Song of Solomon 8:2** *(stored WEB, not yet ingested)* — *"I would lead you, bringing you into
  my mother's house, who would instruct me."* (*ʾăḇîʾăḵā ʾel-bêt ʾimmî*)
- **Genesis 24:28** — [[A Wife for Isaac]]: *"The young lady ran, and told her mother's house about
  these words."* (*lǝḇêt ʾimmāh*)
- **Genesis 24:67** — [[A Wife for Isaac]]: *"Isaac brought her into his mother Sarah's tent, and
  took Rebekah, and she became his wife. He loved her."* (*wayǝḇiʾehā yiṣḥāq hāʾōhĕlâ śārâ ʾimmô*)
- **Ruth 1:8** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — Naomi: *"Go, return each of you
  to her mother's house."* (*lǝḇêt ʾimmāh*)

Hebrew of all read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

## Analysis

**Father's house, mother's house.** The ordinary Hebrew term for a family household is *bêt ʾāḇ*,
*a father's house*. Abraham's servant asks Rebekah exactly that: *"Is there room in your father's
house for us to stay?"* (*bêt-ʾāḇîḵ*, Genesis 24:23). Five verses later she runs and tells *her
mother's house* (24:28). The phrase *bêt ʾēm*, *mother's house*, is rare. The usual list is Genesis
24:28, Ruth 1:8, Song 3:4, and Song 8:2; this page has not verified that list by a full concordance
search. In each case the house is named in connection with a woman. In Genesis and Ruth a marriage
is near; in the Song none is stated.

| | Whose mother | Who goes in, or is sent | What marriage is near |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis 24:28 | Rebekah's | Rebekah runs there with the news | the betrothal to Isaac |
| Genesis 24:67 | Isaac's (*his mother Sarah's tent*) | Isaac brings Rebekah in | *"she became his wife. He loved her."* |
| Ruth 1:8 | each daughter-in-law's | Orpah and Ruth are sent back | Naomi prays that each will find rest *"in the house of her husband"* (1:9) |
| Song 3:4 | the woman's | she brings the beloved in | not stated |
| Song 8:2 | the woman's | she wishes she could bring him in | not stated |

**The Song and Genesis 24:67.** The closest action is Isaac's. *Wayǝḇiʾehā*, *he brought her*, into
his mother's tent; in the Song, *šeheḇêʾtîw*, *I brought him*, into my mother's house (3:4); and
*ʾăḇîʾăḵā*, *I would bring you* (8:2). The verb is the same, *b-w-ʾ* in the causative. In Genesis
the man brings the woman; in the Song the woman brings the man. The wiki notes the reversal. It
does not claim the Song alludes to Isaac, because the Song names no patriarch and the verb is
common.

**What the house might mean.** Readers have suggested that the mother's house was where marriages
were arranged or celebrated, or simply that a daughter's world is centred on her mother's
household, or, in the Song, that the phrase *"the room of her who conceived me"* (3:4) points to
the place of her own beginning. Song 8:5 adds that the man's mother conceived and bore him under the
apple tree (with a different verb, *ḥibbǝlaṯḵā*). None of these readings is stated in the texts. The
page sets the uses side by side and leaves the meaning open.

## Significance

The phrase belongs to the women of Israel's stories: Rebekah, Ruth and Orpah, and the woman of the
Song. Where it appears, the woman is the one who moves — she runs, she is sent home, she brings him
in. In the Song, the house is the place she brings the one she has found, and the place she wants
to bring him again. See [[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]], [[Rebekah]], [[Marriage]], and
[[The Betrothal at the Well]], where Rebekah's run to her mother's house is one step of the
type-scene.
