# Not in Riddles - Numbers 12.8 and 1 Corinthians 13.12

## Passages involved

- [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]] — Numbers 12:8; *mouth to mouth, even plainly, and not in riddles*
- **1 Corinthians 13:12** — *For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]] — Proverbs 1:6; riddles as the wise man's study
- **Psalm 78:2** — *I will utter dark sayings of old* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The shared word.** Rahlfs at Numbers 12:8 has *ἐν εἴδει καὶ οὐ δι᾿ αἰνιγμάτων*, and 1 Corinthians 13:12 has *διʼ ἐσόπτρου ἐν αἰνίγματι* (SBLGNT). WEB's *dimly* hides the noun, which is the Greek word for riddle. Paul then has *πρόσωπον πρὸς πρόσωπον*. Numbers has *στόμα κατὰ στόμα*, so *face to face* is not that verse's phrase, though it is the phrase of Exodus 33:11 in Hebrew.

**A possible reversal.** If Paul has the verse in mind, then what Numbers says Moses alone had, Paul says all believers will have, and until then even Paul sees *in a riddle*. That would make the Christian present more like the prophets of verse 6 than like Moses. The wiki notes this as a reading. Paul does not cite Numbers, and *αἴνιγμα* is not rare enough to prove a link.

**Riddles are not an insult.** Hebrew *ḥîdâ* is what Samson poses at Judges 14, what the queen of Sheba brings at 1 Kings 10:1, and what the wise study at Proverbs 1:6 (WLC). Numbers 12 does not say riddles are bad, only that Moses did not need them.

## Significance

The verse gives a scale for revelation, from dark to plain. Paul, if he uses it, moves the top of the scale to the end of the age. See [[Prophecy and Vision]] and [[Wisdom]].
