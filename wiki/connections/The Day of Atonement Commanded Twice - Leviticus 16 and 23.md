# The Day of Atonement Commanded Twice - Leviticus 16 and 23

## Passages involved

- [[The Day of Atonement]] — Leviticus 16:1–34.
- **Leviticus 23:26–32** — the same day within the festival calendar *(stored WEB; not yet
  ingested)*.

## Analysis

Both passages fix the tenth day of the seventh month (16:29; 23:27), forbid work (16:29;
23:28, 30–31), and call the day *"a Sabbath of solemn rest"* (16:31; 23:32). Both call it a
statute forever (16:29, 31, 34; 23:31).

They differ in what they emphasize:

- **Leviticus 16** is the rite: the high priest's entry, the two goats, the blood, and the
  cleansing of sanctuary and people. The people's part is to afflict their souls and rest,
  and the resident foreigner is explicitly included in the rest (29). No penalty is stated.
- **Leviticus 23** is the calendar. It names the day *"the day of atonement"*, calls it *"a
  holy convocation"*, and requires *"an offering made by fire to Yahweh"* (27). It adds
  sanctions: whoever does not deny himself *"shall be cut off from his people"* (29), and
  whoever works Yahweh *"will destroy from among his people"* (30). It also defines the span —
  *"In the ninth day of the month at evening, from evening to evening"* (32) — and does not
  mention the foreigner.

The WEB's wording for the people's duty varies: *"afflict your souls"* (16:29, 31), *"afflict
yourselves"* (23:27), *"deny himself"* / *"deny yourselves"* (23:29, 32). Whether these render
one Hebrew expression is **not verified here against a source-language edition**; the
practice they describe is not further specified in either chapter, and identifying it with
fasting is an interpretation.

Source: stored WEB Leviticus 16:29–34 and 23:26–32.

## Significance

The two chapters complement rather than repeat each other: the rite without the calendar's
sanctions, and the calendar without the rite. Neither should be silently supplied from the
other. See [[Festivals and First Fruits]], [[Sabbath]], and [[Atonement]].
