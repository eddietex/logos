# Do Not Forbid Them - Joshua in Numbers 11 and John in Mark 9

## Passages involved

- [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] — Numbers 11:27–29; *"My lord Moses, forbid them!"*
- **Mark 9:38–40** — *"we forbade him, because he doesn't follow us." But Jesus said, "Don't forbid him"* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Luke 9:49–50** — *"Don't forbid him, for he who is not against us is for us."* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**The scenes match in shape.** A follower close to the leader sees someone acting outside the group, reports or objects, and asks for it
to stop. The leader refuses, and the refusal turns on loyalty rather than rank.

**In Greek the verb is shared.** Rahlfs has *κώλυσον αὐτούς* at Numbers 11:28, and SBLGNT has *ἐκωλύομεν* and *μὴ κωλύετε* at Mark 9:38–39.
The Hebrew is *kəlāʾēm* (WLC). Neither Gospel cites Numbers. *Κωλύω* is an ordinary verb, so this is a likeness and not shown to be
an allusion.

**Differences.** Eldad and Medad had the Spirit and were on the list. The man in Mark acts *in your name* and is not one of the group.
Joshua speaks for Moses' place. John speaks for the group, *he doesn't follow us*. Moses gives a wish, and Jesus gives a principle.

## Significance

Both passages put the fault in the follower, not in the outsider. Jealousy for a leader is still jealousy, as Moses names it at
Numbers 11:29. See [[Envy]].
