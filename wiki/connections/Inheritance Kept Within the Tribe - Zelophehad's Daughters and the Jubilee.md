# Inheritance Kept Within the Tribe - Zelophehad's Daughters and the Jubilee

## Passages involved
- [[The Inheritance of Manasseh]] — Joshua 17:1–13.

- [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]] — Numbers 27:1–11.
- [[The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad]] — Numbers 36:1–13.
- [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]] — Leviticus 25:10–16.
- [[Redemption of Land and Houses]] — Leviticus 25:23–34.

## Analysis

### Manasseh’s allotment

Joshua 17:3–6 records the sisters receiving their inheritance under the earlier command. It does not
repeat or rescind the marriage restriction described above. The narrative moves from the legal
provision to allocation.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:1–13.

Numbers 27 protects a sonless man's inheritance through his daughters. Numbers 36 asks
what happens to the tribal allotment when those inheriting daughters marry into another
tribe. Both petitions receive approval (27:7; 36:5), and the second ruling keeps the first
in force while constraining the heiresses' choice of husband.

Leviticus 25:13, 28 requires return of possession at Jubilee, in a context of land sales.
The family heads in Numbers 36:4 say Jubilee will leave the transferred inheritance in
the receiving tribe. These provisions are in tension with an unrestricted claim that
Jubilee reverses every loss of tribal territory, but do not directly contradict one another:
a sale and an owner's change of tribal affiliation are different situations. This is an
interpretive distinction drawn from their contexts, not an additional rule quoted from
either text.

The solution is preventative: every inheriting daughter must marry within her paternal
tribal family (36:8). The five sisters marry their father's brothers' sons (11–12).
Their specific cousin marriages fulfill the wider family requirement; the text does
not require every Israelite marriage to follow that precise relationship.

## Significance

The land gift includes obligations that outlast the original allocation. Individual
inheritance and tribal continuity are protected together, while women's marital choice
is restricted. The ending of Numbers records compliance without reporting the conquest.
