# Who Is Put Out of the Camp - Numbers 5 and the Purity Laws

- Numbers 9:6–10; 12:10–15; 19:11–22; 31:19 — stored WEB, not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Numbers 5:2 sends three kinds of people out of the camp. The other laws do not all say the same thing.

| Condition | Numbers 5 | Law on the condition | What that law says about place |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leprosy | sent out | Leviticus 13:46 | *Outside of the camp shall be his dwelling* |
| Discharge | sent out | Leviticus 15 | names no camp; contact, washing, and waiting |
| Unclean by the dead | sent out | Numbers 19:11–22 | seven days and sprinkling; states no removal |

Only the leper's case agrees plainly. Leviticus 15 assumes people with a discharge live among others,
since it rules on who touches them and what they sit on. Its closing warning, Leviticus 15:31, has the
same concern as Numbers 5:3, the defiling of the tabernacle among them, but it is met by separation from
uncleanness and not by leaving the camp. Numbers 19 sets out the cleansing from contact with the dead
without saying where the unclean person stays. Numbers 31:19 does tell the soldiers, *Encamp outside of
the camp seven days*.

**Unresolved.** The texts do not say whether Numbers 5 widens the earlier rules, applies to a narrower
case, or belongs to the camp at Sinai in particular. Whether *everyone who has an issue* covers the
monthly period of Leviticus 15:19–24 is not stated. Numbers 9:6 has men unclean by a dead body coming
before Moses and Aaron, and it does not say where they lived. This page keeps the difference visible.

### Same three, smaller circle

Leviticus 22:4 names the same three conditions, and a priest with any of them may not eat the holy
things until he is clean. By Strong's tags (WLC), the three terms stand together nowhere else in the
Pentateuch. Leviticus 22 bars a priest from a table, and Numbers 5 bars anyone from the camp.

### Later cases

Miriam is shut outside the camp seven days, and the people do not travel until she is brought back
(Numbers 12:14–15). Deuteronomy 23:10–14 sends out a man unclean from what happens by night, in a
military camp, with a reason close to Numbers 5:3: *for Yahweh your God walks in the middle of your
camp*. After settlement the pattern continues without a camp. Four leprous men sit *at the entrance of
the gate* (2 Kings 7:3); King Uzziah *lived in a separate house* (2 Chronicles 26:21); and ten lepers
meet Jesus and stand *at a distance* (Luke 17:12).

## Significance

Numbers 5 gives the reason that the other laws imply: Yahweh dwells in the camp. The cost falls on
people who in most cases have done nothing wrong, as [[Clean and Unclean]] and
[[Outside the Camp and Back Again - Leviticus 13 and 14]] both record. See also
[[The Dwelling of God]].
