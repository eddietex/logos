# You Shall Not Speak Evil of a Ruler - Exodus 22.28 in Acts 23.5

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 22:28** — *"You shall not blaspheme God, nor curse a ruler of your people."* —
  [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]]
- **Acts 23:1–5** — Paul before the council: struck on the mouth at the high priest Ananias's
  command, he answers, *"God will strike you, you whitewashed wall!"*; told *"Do you malign God's
  high priest?"*, he replies, *"I didn't know, brothers, that he was high priest. For it is written,
  'You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.'"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Genesis 12:3** — the same two Hebrew verbs for *curse* — [[The Call of Abram]]

(WEB throughout; Acts fetched from bible-api.com, `translation=web`, on 2026-10-23.)

## Analysis

**Paul quotes the second half of Exodus 22:28, in the Septuagint's wording.** The texts, as
checked:

| Text | Wording |
|---|---|
| Hebrew (MAM 22:27) | *wənāśîʾ bəʿamməkā lōʾ tāʾōr*, *and a ruler among your people you shall not curse* |
| Septuagint (Rahlfs 1935, Greek 22:27) | *kai archontas tou laou sou ou kakōs ereis*, *and rulers of your people you shall not speak evil of* |
| Acts 23:5 (SBLGNT) | *Archonta tou laou sou ouk ereis kakōs*, *a ruler of your people you shall not speak evil of* |

**The Greek of Acts agrees with the Septuagint word for word except in two places**: the noun is
singular, *archonta*, where the Septuagint has the plural *archontas*, and the adverb comes after
the verb. **The Hebrew's *ʾārar*, *curse*, is rendered in both Greek texts as *speak evil of*,
*kakōs ereis***, so the Greek is milder than the Hebrew verb. Paul's citation formula, *gegraptai
gar hoti*, *for it is written that* (SBLGNT, with a textual variant marked at *hoti*), presents the
sentence as Scripture, not as a proverb.

**The first half of the verse is not quoted, but the scene supplies it.** The bystanders' question,
*"Do you malign God's high priest?"* (*Ton archierea tou theou loidoreis*, SBLGNT), puts *God* and
*ruler* side by side as Exodus 22:28 does. Whether Luke intended the echo of the verse's first clause
cannot be shown.

**The verse's own first clause is disputed.** The Hebrew *ʾĕlōhîm lōʾ təqallēl* can mean *you shall
not blaspheme God* (WEB) or *you shall not revile the gods / the judges* — the KJV has *"the gods"*,
and the Septuagint *theous ou kakologēseis*, *you shall not speak evil of gods*. On the second
reading the verse is about two kinds of human authority, judges and rulers, which is close to Paul's
situation before the council. **The wiki records both readings and does not choose**; the same
question about *ʾĕlōhîm* arose at Exodus 21:6 and 22:8–9 (see [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]]
and [[Laws of Property and Restitution]]).

**The two Hebrew verbs are those of Genesis 12:3.** *Qillēl* (*make light of, curse*) and *ʾārar*
(*curse*) stand together in God's promise to Abram, *ûməqallelkā ʾāʾōr* (MAM), and again here, with
God and the ruler as their objects instead of Abram's enemies. **This is a shared pair of verbs, not
a demonstrated allusion.**

**What Paul's answer means is debated.** He says he did not know Ananias was high priest. Readers
have taken that literally — he did not recognize him — or as irony, that a man who commands a
prisoner to be struck *"contrary to the law"* (23:3) does not act like a high priest. The text
reports the words and does not explain them; the wiki records both readings.

## Significance

- **It is an explicit New Testament citation of the Book of the Covenant** (whether it is the only
  citation of this verse was not searched), and it shows Paul accepting the law of Exodus 22 as a
  rule against himself in the middle of a trial.
- **It shows the Septuagint behind an apostolic quotation** in a case where the Hebrew verb is
  stronger than the Greek.
- **It keeps the *ʾĕlōhîm* question open** for the later uses of the word for judges, which the wiki
  will meet again in Psalm 82 and John 10:34–35.
