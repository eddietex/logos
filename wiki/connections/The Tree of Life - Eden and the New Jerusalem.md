# The Tree of Life — Eden and the New Jerusalem

## Passages involved

- [[The Garden of Eden]] — Genesis 2:9
- Genesis 3:22–24 *(not yet ingested)*
- Revelation 2:7 · 22:2, 14, 19 *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

The tree of life appears in the Bible's second chapter and its second-to-last, and almost nowhere
in between. That distribution is the point: it is planted, lost, and restored, and the arc it
traces is the shape of the whole canon.

**Genesis 2:9** puts it *"in the middle of the garden"*, alongside the tree of the knowledge of
good and evil. It is never forbidden. The prohibition of 2:16–17 names only the other tree, which
means the man and woman were free to eat from the tree of life for as long as they were in the
garden — a detail easy to miss, and one that changes the tone of the expulsion.

**Genesis 3:22–24** is the reversal, and it gives the reason for the exile explicitly: *"lest he
reach out his hand, and also take of the tree of life, and eat, and live forever."* Cherubim and a
flaming sword are stationed at the east of the garden — not to keep them from the tree of
knowledge, which they have already eaten from, but from the tree of life. Endless life in a
fallen condition is treated as a fate to be prevented, which reframes the expulsion as at least
partly mercy.

**Revelation 2:7** reopens it as a promise: *"To him who overcomes I will give to eat from the
tree of life, which is in the Paradise of God."* Note *paradeisos*, the Septuagint's word for the
garden of Genesis 2:8.

**Revelation 22:1–2, 14, 19** restores it in full — a river flowing from the throne, the tree on
both sides of it, twelve kinds of fruit, and leaves *"for the healing of the nations"*. The
guarded gates are open (22:14), and access to the tree is now the reward rather than the danger.

**The pattern, and its one shift.** What is restored is not simply what was lost:

| Genesis 2–3 | Revelation 21–22 |
|---|---|
| a garden | a city, with the garden inside it |
| one river dividing into four | one river from the throne |
| the tree in the middle of the garden | the tree on both sides of the river, bearing twelve crops |
| access barred by cherubim and a sword | gates never shut; access is the promise |
| a man and a woman | the nations, healed by its leaves |
| God walking in the garden | *"the throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it"* |

The end is not a return to Eden but Eden urbanized and multiplied. Between the two ends,
Proverbs keeps the phrase alive as metaphor — wisdom is a tree of life to those who take hold of
her (3:18), and so are the fruit of the righteous, fulfilled desire, and a gentle tongue (11:30,
13:12, 15:4).

## Significance

This is the clearest single instance of the Bible's beginning-and-end symmetry, and the reason
Christian readers speak of redemption as restoration rather than replacement. It also sets up the
tree as an image the New Testament can use in a third way: Acts 5:30, 10:39, 13:29, Galatians
3:13, and 1 Peter 2:24 all call the cross a *tree* (*xylon*) — the same word Revelation 22:2 uses
for the tree of life — so that the way back to the tree runs through one.
