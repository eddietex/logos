# A Carved Image and a Molten Image - Deuteronomy 27.15 and Micah's House

## Passages involved

- [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]] — **Deuteronomy 27:15** — *'Cursed is the man who makes an
  engraved or molten image, an abomination to Yahweh, the work of the hands of the craftsman, and sets it
  up in secret.' All the people shall answer and say, 'Amen.'* Stored WEB.
- [[Micah's Idol and Levite]] — **Judges 17:3–4** — *I most certainly dedicate the silver to Yahweh from my
  hand for my son, to make a carved image and a molten image* (3); she *gave them to a silversmith, who made
  a carved image and a molten image out of it. It was in the house of Micah* (4). Stored WEB.
- [[The Danite Migration]] — Judges 18:14, 17–18, 20, 30–31; the Danites take *the engraved image*, the
  molten image, the ephod, and the teraphim, and set the image up in Dan.
- [[The Ten Commandments]] — **Exodus 20:4** — *You shall not make for yourselves an idol, nor any image*
  (stored WEB); *pesel*, the first noun of the pair (WLC).
- [[The Golden Calf]] — **Exodus 32:4** — *made it a molten calf*, *ʿēgel massēkâ* (stored WEB; WLC), the
  second noun.

## Analysis

**The pair.** *Pesel ûmassēkâ*, *a carved image and a molten image*, stands together in the WLC files of
Genesis through Judges only in Deuteronomy 27:15 and in Micah's story (Judges 17:3, 4; 18:14, 18). Each
noun occurs separately elsewhere: *pesel* in the Decalogue (Exodus 20:4; Deuteronomy 5:8), in Deuteronomy
4:16, 23, 25 and Leviticus 26:1; *massēkâ* for the calf (Exodus 32:4, 8; Deuteronomy 9:12, 16) and in
Exodus 34:17, Leviticus 19:4, and Numbers 33:52 (all WLC). Whether *pesel ûmassēkâ* names two objects or
one (a carved figure overlaid with cast metal) is disputed; Judges 18 lists them separately at 18:14, 17–18
and then speaks of *the engraved image* alone at 18:30–31 (stored WEB). The wiki does not decide.

**The curse and the curse.** Deuteronomy 27:15 is the first of the twelve curses spoken by the Levites at
Ebal, answered with *Amen* (27:14–15, stored WEB). Judges 17 begins with a different curse: Micah's mother's
on the thief of her silver (17:2). Her curse is turned into a blessing, *May Yahweh bless my son!*, and the
silver into the image Deuteronomy curses. The law's curse is never pronounced in the story. This is a
reading of the juxtaposition; the narrator does not cite Deuteronomy.

**In secret, in a house.** Deuteronomy's image is set up *in secret*, *bassāter*. Micah's is not hidden: it
is *in the house of Micah* (17:4), visited by passing Danites (18:2–3), and later set up for a whole tribe
(18:30). What Deuteronomy pictures as a hidden sin becomes a household's open worship and then a tribe's.

**The craftsman.** Deuteronomy calls the image *the work of the hands of the craftsman*, *ḥārāš*; in
Judges 17:4 it is a silversmith, *ṣôrēp* (WLC). The two words differ; the role is the same.

**The Levites.** At Ebal the Levites pronounce the curse (27:14). In Judges 17 a Levite is hired to serve
the image (17:10–13). See [[Filling the Hand - Aaron's Sons, the Levites at the Calf, and Micah's Priests]].

## Significance

Judges 17 is the first narrative after the law in which an Israelite makes the carved-and-molten image
Deuteronomy named. Gideon's ephod (8:27) and the Baals of the cycle formula (2:11) were snares; Micah's
image is the very object of 27:15, made from silver dedicated *to Yahweh*. See [[Idolatry]], [[Curse]], and
[[Micah (of Ephraim)]].

### Set up at Dan — Judges 18

Deuteronomy 27:15 curses the man who *sets it up in secret*, *wəśām bassāter* (WLC). Judges 18:31 says the
Danites *set up for themselves Micah's engraved image*, *wayyāśîmû lāhem ʾet-pesel mîkâ* (WLC) — the same
verb *śîm*, and no secrecy at all: a tribe's image in a named city, with a hereditary priest, *all the
time that God's house was in Shiloh*. The four objects are listed together twice (18:14, 18), then three
(20), then *the engraved image* alone (30–31); the shifting lists leave the one-or-two-objects question
where it was. See [[The Danite Migration]]. Sources: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:15; Judges 18:14–31; Hebrew
WLC, 2026-11-17.
