# Shining on the Mountain - Moses and the Transfiguration

## Passages involved

- [[The Shining Face of Moses]] — Exodus 34:29–35; the mediator returns shining from Sinai.
- [[The Blood of the Covenant]] — Exodus 24:15–18; mountain, cloud, and six days before the call.
- Matthew 17:1–8; Mark 9:2–8; Luke 9:28–36 — the Transfiguration accounts; verified WEB,
  not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Matthew describes Jesus' face shining like the sun and his clothing becoming white as light
(17:2). Moses and Elijah appear, a bright cloud overshadows the group, and a voice identifies
Jesus as the beloved Son and commands attention to him (17:3–5). The disciples fear, and Jesus
reassures them (17:6–8). The mountain, shining face, Moses, divine speech, and frightened
witnesses make comparison with Sinai well grounded, though Matthew does not explicitly cite
Exodus 34 here.

**The parallels do not make the scenes interchangeable.** Exodus places the shining face on
Moses as he comes down and gives Israel the commands; Matthew shows the changed Jesus to
three companions on the mountain. Moses carries tablets in Exodus, but no tablets or face
veil are mentioned in these Gospel passages. Exodus attributes Moses' brightness to speaking
with Yahweh; Matthew does not explain Jesus' radiance by repeating that causal statement.
The voice singles out Jesus while Moses and Elijah are present, and the scene ends with Jesus
alone. This supports a Christian typological reading while preserving the narrative differences.

**Keep the Gospel accounts distinct.** Mark describes the changed form and exceptionally white
clothing (9:2–3), without Matthew's explicit shining-face description. Luke locates the altered
face and dazzling clothing during prayer (9:28–29) and names the conversation's subject as
Jesus' departure to be accomplished at Jerusalem (9:30–31). Luke says that Moses and Elijah
appear in glory and that the companions see Jesus' glory (9:31–32). This page makes no claim
about the Greek word translated departure.

Matthew and Mark introduce the event after six days; Luke says about eight days after the
sayings. These differing time notices are retained without imposing a calendar harmonization.
The six days in Exodus 24:16 offer another Sinai resemblance, distinct from the shining face
in Exodus 34. None of these Gospel verses names the mountain, so identifying it with Sinai or
another particular summit would go beyond the passages.

## Significance

[[The Glory of God]] and [[Light and Darkness]] acquire a later personal focus, while
[[Mediation and Advocacy]] links the commands Moses conveys to the Gospel voice's direction
to hear the Son. This is a comparison across distinct scenes, not a claim that Exodus 34
explicitly predicts the Transfiguration or that all three Gospels use identical details.
