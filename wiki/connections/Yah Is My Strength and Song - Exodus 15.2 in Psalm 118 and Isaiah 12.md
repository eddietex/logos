# Yah Is My Strength and Song - Exodus 15.2 in Psalm 118 and Isaiah 12

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 15:2** — *"Yah is my strength and song. He has become my salvation. This is my God, and I will
  praise him; my father's God, and I will exalt him."* — [[The Song of the Sea]]
- **Psalm 118:14** — *"Yah is my strength and song. He has become my salvation."* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Psalm 118:28** — *"You are my God, and I will give thanks to you. You are my God, I will exalt you."*
  (WEB; *not yet ingested*)
- **Isaiah 12:2** — *"Behold, God is my salvation. I will trust, and will not be afraid; for Yah, Yahweh, is
  my strength and song; and he has become my salvation."* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**The line is repeated, not alluded to.** In the Westminster Leningrad Codex the six Hebrew words of Exodus
15:2a — ***ʿozzî wəzimrāt yāh wayhî-lî lîšûʿâ*** — recur in the same order in Psalm 118:14, and in Isaiah 12:2
with one addition: the name ***YHWH*** after *yāh*, which the WEB renders *"Yah, Yahweh"* (WLC, all three
verses). **Letters and vowels are identical; only the cantillation accents differ** (WLC).

**Psalm 118 carries the Song further than one line.** Its verse 28 — ***ʾēlî ʾattâ wəʾôdekkā ʾĕlōhay
ʾărôməmekkā*** — **takes the two halves of the rest of Exodus 15:2**, *"This is my God (ʾēlî) … my father's
God, and I will exalt him (waʾărōməmenhû)"*, and turns them into address: *"You are my God … I will exalt
you"* (WLC). **The same verb, *rûm* in the polel, and the same title, *ʾēlî*.** Whether the psalm's other
lines echo the Song is not checked in this pass.

**Isaiah 12 places the line after a second exodus.** The chapter closes a prophecy of a road for the
remnant *"like there was for Israel in the day that he came up out of the land of Egypt"* (Isaiah 11:16, WEB),
and the song of 12:2 answers it. **Isaiah sets the Song's words in
the mouth of a future people coming home** (reported from the context, *not yet ingested*).

## Significance

**Exodus 15:2 is the first time a deliverance is put into a formula someone else can sing.** Jacob's
*"I have waited for your salvation, Yahweh"* (Genesis 49:18) was a cry with no object; Moses' *"see the
salvation of Yahweh"* (14:13) a promise for *"today"*; the Song's *"He has become my salvation"* a report.
**The Psalm and Isaiah take the report and make it portable** — sung by a worshipper at *"the gate of Yahweh"*
(Psalm 118:20, WEB) and by a returning people (Isaiah 12). **The singular *"my"* of a crowd at
the sea becomes the singular of every later singer.** See
[[I Have Waited for Your Salvation - The First Yeshuah in Scripture]] and [[Praise and Song]].
