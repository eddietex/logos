# One Event Happens to Them All - Job 21.23-26 and Ecclesiastes 2.14-16

## Passages involved

- [[From Dust to Dust]] — Ecclesiastes 3:16–22.

- [[The Wise and the Fool Share One End]] — Ecclesiastes 2:14–16.
- [[Why Do the Wicked Live]] — Job 21:23–26.
- [[The Two Paths]] — Proverbs 4:18–19.
- Ecclesiastes 9:2–5 *(not yet ingested)*.

## Analysis

**The same observation.** Job 21:23–25 sets two deaths side by side: one man *"dies in his full
strength, being wholly at ease and quiet"*, another *"dies in bitterness of soul, and never tastes
of good."* Verse 26 ends both: *"They lie down alike in the dust. The worm covers them."*
Ecclesiastes 2:14 says of the wise man and the fool, *"one event happens to them all,"* and 2:16,
*"the wise man must die just like the fool!"* Both texts observe that death does not distinguish
between people whom life distinguished sharply.

**Different pairs.** Job's pair is distinguished by fortune — ease and bitterness. The Preacher's is
distinguished by wisdom — eyes in the head and a walk in darkness (Ecclesiastes 2:14). Ecclesiastes
9:2 (not yet ingested) extends the leveller to moral standing: *"There is one event to the righteous
and to the wicked"* (stored WEB), and 3:19–20 to humans and animals: *"All are from the dust, and
all turn to dust again"* (stored WEB). Each text names a different distinction that death erases.

**Different uses.** In Job 21 the common end is an argument. Job is answering friends who claim
that the wicked are visibly punished; if the prosperous and the bitter lie down alike, then death
cannot be the proof of a just distinction that the friends need. In Ecclesiastes 2:15–17 the common
end is a personal discovery — *"why was I then more wise?"* — and its result is grief: *"So I hated
life."* Job turns the observation outward against a theology; the Preacher turns it inward against
his own investment in wisdom.

**Against Proverbs.** Proverbs 4:18–19 compares the righteous path to *"the dawning light, that
shines more and more until the perfect day,"* and the wicked way to darkness where they do not know
what they stumble over. Ecclesiastes 2:13–14 keeps the light-and-darkness contrast — *"wisdom excels
folly, as far as light excels darkness"* — but does not keep the destination. The tension is
explicit: Proverbs presents the two ways as leading to different ends, while Ecclesiastes 2:14–16,
as stated, gives the wise and the fool the same one. Proverbs' pair is righteous and wicked, the
Preacher's is wise and fool, so the texts are not about identical categories; but Proverbs
consistently aligns those pairs, and the difference cannot be removed by that observation alone.

**What neither text says.** Neither Job 21 nor Ecclesiastes 2 says what follows death beyond the
dust and forgetting. Both are observations made *under the sun*, in the Preacher's phrase. Later
material on judgment (Ecclesiastes 12:14, not yet ingested) and on resurrection elsewhere in
Scripture belongs to other pages; it is not read back into these verses here.

### The comparison reaches animals — Ecclesiastes 3:16–22

[[From Dust to Dust]] now places shared mortality inside a unit that first affirms God's
judgment (17). The paired deaths of humans and animals (19–20) make it impossible to treat
death by itself as a verdict identifying personal guilt. The WEB's question about spirit
(21) is not a settled denial of any further existence. Verse 22 responds with enjoyment in
works, unlike 2:17's hatred of life; both responses remain in the book. Sources: stored WEB
Ecclesiastes 2:14–17; 3:16–22; Job 21:23–26.

## Significance

[[Life and Death]] gains a recurring wisdom-tradition observation: death as the leveller. Job uses
it to dispute retribution ([[Retribution]]); Ecclesiastes uses it to question the lasting advantage
of [[Wisdom]] over [[Folly]], while still ranking one above the other. [[Remembrance]] is the second
casualty in Ecclesiastes: the wise are forgotten as the fool is. [[Vanity]] is the Preacher's name
for what the observation leaves him with.
