# Washing and Life Before the Sanctuary - Exodus 30 and Leviticus 15

## Passages involved

- [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] — Exodus 30:17–21.
- [[Bodily Discharges and Cleansing]] — Leviticus 15:1–33.


## Analysis

Exodus 30:17–21 places a basin between the tent and altar and requires Aaron and his
sons to wash hands and feet before entering or ministering, with a warning against
death. Leviticus 15 regulates washing, contact, and waiting throughout the community,
then grounds separation from uncleanness in the danger of defiling the tabernacle
among Israel (31).

These are related protections of approach, not identical washing rites. The priestly
basin instruction names hands and feet and repeated ministerial entry. The discharge
laws prescribe bodily bathing, washing clothes, waiting periods, and, in specified
cases, eighth-day offerings. No claim that every bath uses the sanctuary basin is
warranted. Water does not make the other conditions unnecessary.

## Significance

Divine dwelling gives communal purity rules their stated gravity. The connection
links priestly service and household conduct while preserving their different duties.
