# The Priests' Trumpets - Numbers 10 from the Camp to the Second Temple

## Passages involved

- [[The Two Silver Trumpets]] — Numbers 10:1–10
- [[The War Against Midian]] — Numbers 31:6, *the trumpets for the alarm* with Phinehas
- 2 Kings 11:14 — trumpets at the crowning of Joash *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- 2 Kings 12:13 — trumpets among the temple vessels *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- 1 Chronicles 15:24; 16:6 — priests blow before the ark *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- 2 Chronicles 5:12–13 — 120 priests, and the cloud fills the house *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- 2 Chronicles 13:12–15 — Abijah's priests in battle *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- 2 Chronicles 29:26–28 — Hezekiah's burnt offering *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Ezra 3:10 — the foundation of the second temple *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*
- Nehemiah 12:35, 41 — the dedication of the wall *(WEB checked, not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

Numbers 10 names three uses for the *ḥăṣōṣərâ*: signals for the camp, war in the land, and worship. By the tags the
word then appears 23 more verses after Numbers 10, and nearly all of them are later narrative, mostly Chronicles. None of
these texts cites Numbers 10, and only the shared word and the shared roles link them to it. The tracing is the wiki's.

**The camp use does not continue.** The march signal has no later narrative in these texts. Once Israel is settled, there
is no moving camp to signal, which fits the shape of Numbers 10:9–10: the unit itself looks beyond the march.

**War.** Numbers 31:6 and 2 Chronicles 13:12 are the only places the tags give the phrase *ḥăṣōṣərôt hattərûʿâ*,
*trumpets of alarm* (WLC). Abijah's speech says *his priests with the trumpets of alarm to sound an alarm against
you*, and 13:10 has just claimed *the sons of Aaron* for Judah. Then the story follows the order of Numbers 10:9
closely: *they cried to Yahweh, and the priests sounded with the trumpets*, the men shouted, and *God struck Jeroboam*
(13:14–15, WEB). Alarm, then remembering, then rescue. The Chronicler does not say *remembered*, so this is a match
of pattern and not a quotation. The adversary here is also the northern kingdom, other Israelites, which verse 9 does
not have in view as worded.

**Worship.** 2 Chronicles 29:27 is the closest to Numbers 10:10: *When the burnt offering began, Yahweh's song also
began, along with the trumpets*, and verse 28 keeps it going *until the burnt offering was finished* (WEB). The
trumpets are there *over your burnt offerings*, now joined to singing and to David's instruments, which Numbers does
not have. Numbers 10 has no song in it. Chronicles and Ezra have added that, and Ezra 3:10 attributes the arrangement to
*the directions of David*, not to Moses.

**Cloud and trumpet again.** At 2 Chronicles 5:12–13, one hundred twenty priests sound, the singers join *to make one
sound*, and *then the house was filled with a cloud*. Numbers 9–10 set the cloud and the trumpets side by side at the
start of the journey, and here they come together at its end, in the house where the ark is brought. That pairing is the wiki's
observation. The Chronicler does not draw it. See [[The Cloud over the Tabernacle]].

**The second temple keeps it.** Ezra 3:10 has priests *in their clothing with trumpets* at the laying of the foundation,
and Nehemiah 12:35, 41 has *the priests' sons* and named priests with trumpets at the wall. So the statute of verse 8,
*forever throughout your generations*, is shown still in force after the exile, in that the instrument remains in
priestly hands.

**A temple vessel.** 2 Kings 12:13 lists trumpets among the temple vessels that were not made from the money brought
in under Joash. Numbers 10 commanded two, and by this time the instrument is listed as a temple furnishing.

**Who blows.** Numbers 10:8 gives the trumpets to the sons of Aaron, and most of these texts agree. Others do not fit
cleanly. At 2 Kings 11:14 and 2 Chronicles 23:13, *all the people of the land rejoiced, and blew trumpets*; there the
verb is *tāqaʿ* with *ḥăṣōṣərôt* (WLC). 1 Chronicles 13:8 has David and all Israel playing with trumpets, and 1 Chronicles
16:42 sets them with Heman and Jeduthun. Numbers 10:8 could be read as covering only the sacred signals, or the
later texts could describe a practice wider than the statute. The text does not resolve this, and the wiki leaves it open.

## Significance

The trumpets start as a tool of a nomad camp, and the statute survives the end of that camp. The war use and the worship
use are what carry on, as verses 9–10 anticipated. Priests are present at battles and at sacrifices with the same object
in their hands, which makes Israel's fighting and its worship two occasions for the same act, on the wiki's reading: making a sound before God.
