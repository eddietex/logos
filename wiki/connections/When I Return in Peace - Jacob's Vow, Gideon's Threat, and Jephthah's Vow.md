# When I Return in Peace - Jacob's Vow, Gideon's Threat, and Jephthah's Vow

## Passages involved
- [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]] — Genesis 28:20–21. *If God will be with me … so that I come again to my
  father's house in peace, and Yahweh will be my God* (stored WEB).
- [[Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel]] — Judges 8:9. *When I come again in peace, I will break down this
  tower* (stored WEB).
- **Judges 11:30–31** *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)* — *whatever comes out of the doors of my house to
  meet me, when I return in peace from the children of Ammon, it shall be Yahweh's, and I will offer it
  up for a burnt offering.*

## Analysis

**One phrase, three times.** The Hebrew is *bəšûbî bəšālôm* in Judges 8:9 and 11:31, *when I return in
peace*, and *wəšabtî bəšālôm* in Genesis 28:21 (WLC). A search of the WLC of Genesis, Exodus, Numbers,
Deuteronomy, Joshua, and Judges for *šûb* followed immediately by *šālôm* finds these three verses and no
others. Leviticus was not searched.

**Two vows and a threat.** Jacob and Jephthah both make conditional vows to God before a journey or a
war. Jacob's condition is that God keep him and bring him back; his promise is that *Yahweh will be my
God*, a stone that will be *God's house*, and a tenth (28:21–22). Jephthah's condition is victory; his
promise is a burnt offering of *whatever comes out of the doors of my house*. Gideon's sentence in
between is not addressed to God at all. It is a promise to a town of what he will do to it, and he keeps
it (8:17).

**What each return brings.** Jacob comes back, and God sends him up to Bethel to make an altar *to God, who
answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me on the way which I went* (Genesis 35:1, 3; see
[[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]]). Gideon comes back and breaks down the
tower and kills the men of Penuel. Jephthah comes back and his daughter comes out to meet him (11:34,
stored WEB; not yet ingested). In Judges, the safe return of the man who speaks the phrase is followed
both times by death at home: Israelite men in 8:17, and his own daughter in 11:34–39.

## Significance

The phrase belongs to a man going out who expects to come back. Genesis uses it in a vow of trust, and
Judges uses it twice for promises that end in killing. The wiki records the verbal thread; Judges does
not cite Genesis. See [[Oaths and Vows]], [[Jacob]], and [[Gideon]].
