# A Child of His Old Age — Ben-Zequnim from Joseph to Benjamin

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 37:3** — [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]; *"Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his
  children, because he was the son of his old age, and he made him a coat of many colors"* —
  ***ben-zəqūnîm***
- **Genesis 44:20** — [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]; [[Judah]] to [[Joseph]] about
  [[Benjamin]]: *"a child of his old age, a little one; and his brother is dead, and he alone is left
  of his mother; and his father loves him"* — ***yeled zəqūnîm***
- **Genesis 21:2, 7** — [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]]; *"Sarah … bore Abraham a son
  in his old age"*
- **Genesis 37:4** — *"His brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, and
  they hated him"*
- **Genesis 43:34** — the fivefold portion at the table — [[The Second Journey]]
- **Genesis 45:22** — five changes of clothing and three hundred pieces of silver —
  [[Joseph Reveals Himself]]; see [[Five Changes of Clothing - The Coat Given Again]]

## Analysis

***Zəqūnîm*** is **a plural abstract of *zāqēn*, old age**, and Genesis uses it of a son exactly
twice. **Both times it is the reason a father loves one boy more than the others, and both times the
boy is [[Rachel]]'s.**

| | Genesis 37:3 | Genesis 44:20 |
|---|---|---|
| **who says it** | **the narrator**, explaining [[Jacob]] | **[[Judah]]**, quoting the family's account to [[Joseph]] |
| **the phrase** | ***ben-zəqūnîm*** — *son of old age* | ***yeled zəqūnîm qāṭān*** — *a child of old age, a little one* |
| **who it describes** | [[Joseph]] | [[Benjamin]] |
| **what follows in the verse** | *"and he made him a coat of many colors"* | *"and his father loves him"* |
| **what it produces** | *"they hated him, and couldn't speak peaceably to him"* (37:4) | **a plea for clemency** |

**The same clause, about two different sons, explains the crime in one chapter and argues against its
repetition in the other.**

### The arithmetic does not work and Genesis does not fix it

**Benjamin is younger than Joseph.** He is born at 35:16–19, on the road to Ephrath, in the labour
that kills their mother — **three chapters and some years after Joseph's birth at 30:22–24.** So when
37:3 says Jacob loved Joseph *because he was the son of his old age*, **the son of his older age is
alive and in the house.**

**The wiki takes 37:3 as the narrator reporting Jacob's own account of himself rather than as a
calculation** — the same way it takes *"my wife bore me two sons"* (44:27) as Jacob's arithmetic
rather than the book's. **What 44:20 establishes is that the household used the phrase of Benjamin
too.** Either it was always a figure for *the late-born favourite* rather than a statement about
birth order, **or the family simply transferred it, intact, from one son of Rachel to the next when
the first one was gone.**

**Genesis offers no ruling.** It lets Judah say the words to Joseph and does not tell the reader
whether Joseph notices.

### Judah is saying it to the man it was said about

**This is the point of the parallel, and it is entirely unspoken.**

[[Judah]] is describing Benjamin to an Egyptian governor in order to explain why the boy cannot be
kept. **Every clause of 44:20 is a fact about the speaker's own crime**:

- ***"a child of his old age"*** — **the narrator's stated cause of the hatred in 37:4**
- ***"his brother is dead"*** — **he is not, and the reason the family believes he is, is a coat
  Judah's brothers dipped in goat's blood** (37:31–33)
- ***"he alone is left of his mother"*** — **true only if Joseph is dead**; the man being addressed is
  the counter-example
- ***"and his father loves him"*** — ***wəʾābîw ʾăhēbô***, **the verb of 37:3–4**, *"his brothers saw
  that their father loved him more than all his brothers, and they hated him"*

**Judah is offering paternal favouritism as grounds for mercy to a man whose life it destroyed.** He
is not being ironic and he is not being cruel; **he does not know who he is talking to.** **That is
the chapter's whole technique**, and 44:20 is its densest instance. See [[Envy]] and [[Love]].

### The love is not disputed anywhere

**Genesis never says Jacob was wrong to love Joseph more, or Benjamin more.** It says the brothers saw
it and hated him (37:4); it lets [[Joseph]] himself reproduce it at a table, serving Benjamin five
times the others' portion in front of the ten (43:34); **and it has Judah argue from it in court
without a hint that it is a defect.** The book reports the favouritism as the engine of everything
that happens and passes no judgment on it whatever.

**The one thing it does do is repeat it.** Joseph's coat (37:3), Benjamin's five portions (43:34),
and, after the disclosure, **Benjamin's five changes of clothing** while the others get one each
(45:22). **The gift that started it is given again, twice, by the man it was given to.** See
[[Blessing]] and [[Brotherhood]].

### Isaac, and the other late son

**The only other *old age* birth in Genesis is [[Isaac]]** — *"Sarah conceived, and bore Abraham a son
in his old age"* (21:2), *"for I have borne him a son in his old age"* (21:7). **The noun is
different** (*zəqūnāyw*, his old age, rather than the abstract plural used of a child) **and so is the
situation**: Isaac's lateness is the miracle itself, the point of the whole promise, **not a reason
one son is preferred over another.**

**But the pattern it establishes is the one both later cases run on.** **The late-born son is the
loved son; the elder is the one who leaves.** Ishmael is sent out (21:14), Esau sells and is
supplanted, Joseph is sold, **and Benjamin is the one who must not be risked.** See
[[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]] and
[[The Elder Will Serve the Younger - Genesis 25.23 in Malachi and Romans]].

## Significance

**The phrase is a small hinge and it carries the cycle's whole moral argument.**

**In Genesis 37 it is the *cause*.** The narrator gives it as the reason for the love, the love as the
reason for the coat, the coat as the reason for the hatred, and the hatred as the reason for the pit.
**Four clauses and a boy is in Egypt.**

**In Genesis 44 it is the *plea*.** The same phrase, about the next boy, offered by one of the men it
once drove to a sale — **and offered to the man they sold.** **Nothing has changed about the father,
the favouritism, or the family's way of describing it.** What has changed is that [[Judah]] now argues
for the favoured son instead of against him, **and is willing to be enslaved rather than let the old
man lose him** (44:33).

**That is the measure Genesis gives for what twenty-two years have done to these men, and it gives it
as a two-word Hebrew phrase used twice.**
