# The Priests Who Kept Their Land and the Levites Who Got None

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 47:22, 26** — the priests of Egypt exempted from the sale, *"for the priests had a
  portion from Pharaoh"* — [[Joseph and the Famine]]
- **Genesis 41:45; 46:20** — [[Potiphera]], *priest of [[On]]*, Joseph's father-in-law —
  [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]]
- **Numbers 18:20–24** — *"You shall have no inheritance in their land … I am your portion and your
  inheritance"*; the tithe given to Levi instead
- **Deuteronomy 10:9; 18:1–2** — *"Levi has no portion nor inheritance with his brothers. Yahweh is
  his inheritance"*
- **Joshua 13:14, 33; 14:3–4** — the allotment of Canaan, with Levi conspicuously skipped
- **Numbers 35:1–8; Joshua 21** — the forty-eight Levitical cities and their pasture lands: the one
  concession
- Compare **Leviticus 25:32–34** — Levite houses exempted from the walled-city rule, because they
  have no fields — [[The Land Is Mine - Pharaoh's Freehold and the Jubilee]]
- Compare **Genesis 49:5–7** — *"I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel"*, said as a
  curse — [[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]]

## Analysis

**Two priesthoods are treated as exceptions to a national land settlement, and the exceptions run in
opposite directions.**

> **47:22** Only he didn't buy the land of the priests, for the priests had a portion from Pharaoh,
> and ate their portion which Pharaoh gave them. That is why they didn't sell their land.

> **Numbers 18:20** Yahweh said to Aaron, "You shall have no inheritance in their land, neither
> shall you have any portion among them. I am your portion and your inheritance among the children
> of Israel."

**The same noun sits at the centre of both.** ***Ḥōq*** in Genesis 47:22 is **an allotted due, a
ration fixed by authority**, and the Torah uses it repeatedly of the priests' share of the offerings
— *"a portion for ever"* (Leviticus 6:18; 7:34; 10:13–14; Numbers 18:8). **In Egypt the *ḥōq* comes
from Pharaoh and its effect is that the priests never have to sell their fields. In Israel the *ḥōq*
comes from the altar and its effect is that the priests never have any fields to sell.**

| | **Egypt** | **Israel** |
|---|---|---|
| **who feeds the priesthood** | Pharaoh, from the royal stores (47:22) | the people, through the tithe and the offerings (Numbers 18:21, 24) |
| **land held** | **retained in full** — the one class untouched | **none at all** (Numbers 18:20; Joshua 13:14) |
| **relative to the nation** | the exception that keeps what everyone lost | the exception that lacks what everyone got |
| **the stated reason** | they were already provided for | *"I am your portion and your inheritance"* |
| **the effect on the crown** | a priesthood independent of the land reform | a priesthood dependent on the people's obedience |

### Why Israel's arrangement is the harder one

**Egypt's exemption makes its priests secure.** **Israel's makes its priests structurally
vulnerable**, and the Old Testament says so repeatedly: when the tithe fails, the Levites go back to
their fields — *"I perceived that the portions of the Levites had not been given them, so that the
Levites … had fled everyone to his field"* (Nehemiah 13:10). **Malachi 3:8–10 is a prophet arguing
about exactly this revenue.**

**Deuteronomy builds the vulnerability into its law and then protects it.** The Levite is listed
beside the foreigner, the fatherless and the widow as a person with no land and therefore no security
(Deuteronomy 14:27, 29; 16:11, 14; 26:12), **and the triennial tithe is directed to the four of them
together.** **A priesthood that owns nothing is a priesthood that can be starved**, and the Torah's
answer is not to give it land but to make feeding it a commandment.

### The curse that became the arrangement

**Levi's landlessness enters Scripture as a punishment.** **[[Jacob]]'s deathbed word over
[[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] — *"Cursed be their anger… I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in
Israel"* (49:7) — is a sentence of dispersal**, and it is passed on the two brothers who took swords
into [[Shechem (city)]] (34:25–29). **By Numbers 18 and Joshua 21 the dispersal has become the
priesthood**: forty-eight cities scattered through every tribe, no territory, and *Yahweh* as the
inheritance. **The wiki records that Genesis states the curse and the later books state the office,
and that neither text says the second is the first transfigured.** See [[Priesthood]].

### And Joseph's own household

**[[Joseph]] married the daughter of a priest of [[On]]** (41:45), **and the priests are the one
Egyptian class his policy leaves whole** (47:22). **Genesis reports both facts and connects neither,
and the reason it gives for the exemption — a standing royal stipend — is sufficient on its own.**
**The coincidence is recorded here and no motive is alleged.**

**Note also that [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]], the grandsons about to be adopted into the tribal
list** (48:5), **are the sons of that marriage.** **Two of Israel's tribes descend from an Egyptian
priestly house**, and the tribe that will hold Israel's priesthood descends from the brother Jacob
cursed.

## Significance

**The pairing gives a precise measure of how differently the two systems locate religious power.**

**In Egypt the priesthood is an estate: it holds property, is maintained by the crown, and survives
the reduction of the whole population to tenancy because it was never at risk.** **The result is a
priesthood aligned with the throne by interest.**

**In Israel the priesthood is deliberately propertyless, and the sentence that says so gives the
reason in the first person**: *"I am your portion and your inheritance among the children of
Israel."* **The result is a priesthood whose maintenance is a test of the nation's fidelity every
year**, and whose only security is the thing it exists to represent.

**Genesis does not draw the contrast and could not**, since the Levitical law is four books away.
**What Genesis supplies is the photograph of the alternative**, taken by a Hebrew administrator, in
the one chapter of the Bible that describes an ancient land reform from the inside. See
[[Wealth and Possessions]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].
