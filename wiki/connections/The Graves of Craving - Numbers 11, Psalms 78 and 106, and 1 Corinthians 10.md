# The Graves of Craving - Numbers 11, Psalms 78 and 106, and 1 Corinthians 10

## Passages involved

- [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] — Numbers 11:4, 33–34; *hitʾawwû taʾăwâ*, and *the people who lusted*
- **Psalm 78:26–31** — *"He gave them their own desire. They didn't turn from their cravings. Their food was yet in their mouths, when
  the anger of God went up against them"* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **Psalm 106:14–15** — *"but gave in to craving in the desert … He gave them their request, but sent leanness into their soul."*
  (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- **1 Corinthians 10:6** — *"Now these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also
  lusted."* (WEB) *(not yet ingested)*
- [[The Fall]] — Genesis 3:6; *"a delight to the eyes"*, *taʾăwâ*

## Analysis

**One word runs the whole way.** Numbers 11:4 and 34 use the root at the start and the end of the chapter. Psalm 106:14 repeats the
pair of verb and noun, and Psalm 78:29–30 has the noun twice (WLC). Psalm 78:30 has *ʿôd ʾoklām bəpîhem*, *their food was yet in
their mouths*, which is close in sense to Numbers 11:33 without using its words.

**Greek carries it into Paul.** Rahlfs has *ἐπεθύμησαν ἐπιθυμίαν* at Numbers 11:4, and at verse 34 it calls the dead *τὸν ἐπιθυμητήν*.
1 Corinthians 10:6 has *ἐπιθυμητὰς κακῶν, καθὼς κἀκεῖνοι ἐπεθύμησαν* (SBLGNT). Paul does not name Kibroth Hattaavah. *Κακῶν*, *of evil things*, is Paul's addition.

**The psalms differ from Numbers in detail.** Psalm 78:31 says God *killed some of their fattest, and struck down the young men of
Israel*, which Numbers does not. Psalm 78:26 names the east and south winds, and Numbers names no direction. The wiki records these and
does not harmonize them.

**The first craving.** *Taʾăwâ* is what the woman saw in the tree. The link is verbal, and Numbers does not recall Eden, except that the
manna's *bdellium* shares a rare word with Genesis 2:12 (tags). The wiki notes both, and it does not build an argument on them.

## Significance

The graves are remembered by the desire rather than the disease. The plague is not described, and the name that stays on the map is the
wanting itself. The later texts all take up that word. See [[Murmuring]], [[Judgment]], and
[[Taberah Remembered - Numbers 11, Deuteronomy 9, and Psalm 78]].
