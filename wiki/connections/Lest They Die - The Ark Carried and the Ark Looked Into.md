# Lest They Die - The Ark Carried and the Ark Looked Into

## Passages involved

- [[The Service of Kohath, Gershon, and Merari]] — Numbers 4:15, 19–20; do not touch, do not look, *lest they die*
- [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] — Exodus 25:13–15; poles made to carry the ark
- Numbers 7:9 — no wagons for Kohath; *they carried it on their shoulders* *(not yet ingested; stored WEB)*
- Deuteronomy 10:8; 31:9 — the tribe of Levi, and the priests, bearing the ark *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*
- Joshua 3:3 — *the priests the Levites bearing it* *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*
- 1 Samuel 6:13, 19 — the men of Beth Shemesh *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*
- 2 Samuel 6:6–7 — Uzzah *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*
- 1 Chronicles 15:2, 13–15 — David's correction *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*
- 1 Kings 8:3–4 — the ark brought into the temple *(not yet ingested; WEB verified)*

## Analysis

**Two prohibitions.** Numbers 4 forbids two things to the Kohathites: touching (15) and going in to see
(20). Each carries the same result, *lest they die*. Later narrative has one story for each.

- **Looking.** *He struck of the men of Beth Shemesh, because they had looked into Yahweh's ark* (1 Samuel
  6:19). These are not said to be Kohathites, and the verse does not cite Numbers.
- **Touching.** *Uzzah reached for God's ark, and took hold of it; for the cattle stumbled … and he died
  there by God's ark* (2 Samuel 6:6–7). His motive looks good, and the story gives him none of the
  credit. It says that God *struck him there for his error*.

**The carrying rule.** Uzzah's ark was on a cart. Numbers 7:9 gives Kohath no wagon because the sanctuary
is carried on the shoulder. Chronicles draws that conclusion itself. David says, *No one ought to carry
God's ark but the Levites* (1 Chronicles 15:2), and *because you didn't carry it at first, Yahweh our God
broke out in anger against us* (15:13). The Levites then carry it *on their shoulders with its poles, as
Moses commanded according to Yahweh's word* (15:15). So Chronicles reads the death as a breach of the
Mosaic order. 2 Samuel 6 itself does not say so.

**Who carries it?** The texts differ, and the wiki leaves the difference standing.

| Text | Bearers |
|---|---|
| Numbers 4:15; 7:9 | Kohathite Levites; priests only wrap it |
| Deuteronomy 10:8 | *the tribe of Levi* |
| Deuteronomy 31:9 | *the priests the sons of Levi* |
| Joshua 3:3 | *the priests the Levites* |
| 1 Chronicles 15:2, 15 | *the Levites*, on their shoulders |
| 1 Kings 8:3 | *the priests picked up the ark* |

Deuteronomy's language for priests and Levites differs from that of Numbers more broadly, so this may
be part of a larger question, which this page does not settle. A narrower answer would treat the march
as one case and ceremonial occasions as another, but no text says that.

## Significance

The danger in these stories is not a matter of wicked intent. Beth Shemesh *rejoiced to see it* (1 Samuel 6:13), and
Uzzah is trying to save it. That is why the positive side of Numbers 4 matters: *Don't cut off the tribe*,
and *that they may live, and not die* (4:18–19). The law sets the distance in advance so that the people
who come nearest can survive. See [[Holiness]], [[The Dwelling of God]], and
[[The Stranger Who Comes Near - Numbers 1.51, 3.10, 18.7, and Leviticus 10]].
