# Left-Handed Men of Benjamin - Judges 3.15 and 20.16

## Passages involved

- [[Ehud and Eglon]] — **Judges 3:15** — *Ehud the son of Gera, the Benjamite, a left-handed man*
  (stored WEB)
- **Judges 20:16** — *Among all these soldiers there were seven hundred chosen men who were
  left-handed. Every one of them could sling a stone at a hair and not miss.* (stored WEB) *(not yet
  ingested)*
- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — **Genesis 35:18** — *his father named him Benjamin*
  (stored WEB)

## Analysis

The phrase is the same in both Judges verses (WLC):

| Verse | Hebrew (WLC) | WEB |
|---|---|---|
| Judges 3:15 | *ben-hayəmînî ʾîš ʾiṭṭēr yad-yəmînô* | *the Benjamite, a left-handed man* |
| Judges 20:16 | *šəbaʿ mēʾôt ʾîš bāḥûr ʾiṭṭēr yad-yəmînô* | *seven hundred chosen men who were left-handed* |

In the WLC files for Genesis through 1 Samuel, *ʾiṭṭēr* (OpenScriptures lemma 334) occurs in these
two verses only, and both times of Benjamites. Literally the phrase is *restricted* (or *bound*) *as to
his right hand*. The English *left-handed* gives the result and drops the image.

**The pun.** *Binyāmîn*, *son of the right hand* (Genesis 35:18), and in 3:15 *ben-hayəmînî*, *the
son of the right-hander*, sits beside *ʾiṭṭēr yad-yəmînô*, *restricted in his right hand*. Both
*yəmînî* and *yəmînô* are from *yāmîn*. The tribe of the right hand produces men whose right hand is
restricted, and both times the narrator says so in a single breath.

**What it means is not settled.** Three readings are commonly offered, and the two verses alone do not
decide between them:

1. a defect or injury of the right hand;
2. natural left-handedness, described from the right hand's side;
3. trained ambidexterity or left-handed fighting, a Benjamite specialty. 1 Chronicles 12:2 describes
   Benjamite archers who *could use both the right hand and the left in slinging stones and in
   shooting arrows from the bow* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested).

That list is reported, not checked. The narrative point does not depend on it: in 3:15–21 the sword is
on the *right* thigh (16, 21), where a guard would not look for a weapon, and Ehud draws it with his
left hand. In 20:16 the left-handed men are an elite unit of seven hundred *chosen* (*bāḥûr*) men.

**One tribe, two stories.** In chapter 3 a left-handed Benjamite saves Israel from Moab. In chapter 20
seven hundred left-handed Benjamites fight against the rest of Israel. The skill that delivered Israel
is later turned on Israel.

## Significance

The phrase ties Ehud to his tribe's character in the book, and his tribe to its ancestor's name. It is
one of several details that link the first deliverance in Judges with the civil war at its end: *who
shall go up first?* (1:1; 20:18) is another. See [[Benjamin]].
