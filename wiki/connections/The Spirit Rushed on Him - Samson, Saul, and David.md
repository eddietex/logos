# The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David

## Passages involved

- [[Samson's Riddle]] — **Judges 14:6** — *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him, and he tore him as he
  would have torn a young goat* (the lion); **14:19** — *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him, and he
  went down to Ashkelon, and struck thirty men … His anger burned*. Stored WEB.
- [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]] — **Judges 15:14** — *Then Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him, and

- **1 Samuel 10:6, 10** — *Then Yahweh's Spirit will come mightily on you, and you will prophesy with

- **1 Samuel 16:13** — *Then Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on David from that day forward*. WEB at

- **1 Samuel 18:10** — *an evil spirit from God came mightily on Saul, and he prophesied in the middle of

## Analysis

The WEB's *came mightily on* translates one Hebrew verb in every verse above: *ṣālaḥ* (lemma 6743), with
*rûaḥ*, *spirit*, as its subject (WLC). Judges 14:6, 19; 15:14 and 1 Samuel 10:10; 11:6; 16:13; 18:10
have the same form, *wattiṣlaḥ*; 1 Samuel 10:6 has *wəṣāləḥâ*, of a promise. The preposition is *ʿal*,
*on*, everywhere except 1 Samuel 16:13 and 18:10, which have *ʾel*, *to*.

Outside these verses *ṣālaḥ* in the stored books is the verb of *prosper*: Abraham's servant wondering
*whether Yahweh had made his journey prosperous* (Genesis 24:21), *Yahweh was with Joseph, and he was a
prosperous man* (Genesis 39:2), *then you shall make your way prosperous* (Joshua 1:8), and the Danites'
question *whether our way which we go shall be prosperous* (Judges 18:5). All stored WEB. Whether the
Spirit sense and the *prosper* sense are one verb or two homonyms is reported to be debated (not checked
here); the WLC data gives them one lemma number, and the wiki does not decide.

**In the Pentateuch, Joshua, and Judges the Spirit-*ṣālaḥ* is Samson's alone.** Othniel and Jephthah have
*wattəhî ʿālāyw rûaḥ YHWH*, *came on him*, and Gideon *lābəšâ*, *clothed*; see
[[The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah]]. Samson's three are each followed at once by
a feat of the body: a lion torn, thirty men struck, ropes burned off. In the WLC files of Genesis through
Judges checked, the verb with *rûaḥ* as subject occurs nowhere else.

**In 1 Samuel it passes to the kings.** It comes on Saul at his anointing (10:6, 10) and for his first
battle (11:6); then on David at his anointing, *from that day forward* (16:13). Two verses later, *Now
Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul* (16:14, WEB at bible-api.com), and at 18:10 the same verb
brings *an evil spirit from God* on him.

**Judges 14:19 and 1 Samuel 11:6 share a second clause.** Both read *wattiṣlaḥ … rûaḥ …* followed by
*wayyiḥar ʾappô*, *his anger burned* (WLC). Samson's anger follows a lost bet and leads to thirty dead at
Ashkelon. Saul's follows the news of Jabesh Gilead and leads to the muster of Israel (11:7, not checked
for this page). The same two clauses carry a private grievance and a public rescue.

## Significance

- **The verb names the Spirit's force, not its fruit.** Samson tears a lion and kills thirty men for their
  clothes under the same clause. Saul prophesies and then is *turned into another man* (10:6). The text
  does not say the Spirit approved of what Samson did with it.
- **The Spirit that rushes can leave.** Samson's ends with *he didn't know that Yahweh had departed from
  him* (Judges 16:20, stored WEB; [[Samson and Delilah]]); Saul's with *Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul* (1 Samuel 16:14).
  David's is the one said to stay *from that day forward*.
- **Samson is the forerunner of the Philistine wars of the kings.** *He shall begin to save Israel out of
  the hand of the Philistines* (Judges 13:5). The verb that empowers him is the verb that empowers Saul,
  who is promised the same salvation (1 Samuel 9:16), and David, who completes it. See
  [[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].

### Judges 15:14 — the third rush, and a great deliverance

The last *ṣālaḥ* on Samson comes when he is bound and handed over by the men of Judah, and it is followed,
as at 14:6 and 19, by a feat of the body: the ropes melt and a thousand die. For the first time it is
also followed by speech to God. Samson calls the victory *this great deliverance*, *hattəšûʿâ haggədōlâ*
(15:18, WLC). The noun phrase *təšûʿâ gədôlâ* returns in 1 Samuel 19:5, *Yahweh worked a great victory
for all Israel*, of David's killing of the Philistine, and in 2 Samuel 23:10, 12 of David's mighty men
(WEB at bible-api.com; WLC). The *təšûʿâ* of 1 Samuel 11:9, 13 follows Saul's *ṣālaḥ* of 11:6 (WLC). In
the files checked the noun follows the Spirit's rush in Samson's story and Saul's, and names the
Philistine victories of David's. See [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]]. Sources: stored WEB Judges 15:14–18;
1 Samuel 19:5 and 2 Samuel 23:10, 12 checked at bible-api.com (WEB), 2026-11-16; Hebrew WLC
(`Judg.xml`, `1Sam.xml`, `2Sam.xml`), 2026-11-16.
