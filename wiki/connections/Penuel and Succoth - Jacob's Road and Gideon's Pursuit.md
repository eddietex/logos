# Penuel and Succoth - Jacob's Road and Gideon's Pursuit

## Passages involved
- [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]] — Genesis 32:30–31. *Jacob called the name of the place Peniel: for, he
  said, "I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved." The sun rose on him as he passed over
  Peniel* (stored WEB).
- [[The Reconciliation with Esau]] — Genesis 33:17. *Jacob traveled to Succoth, built himself a house,
  and made shelters for his livestock. Therefore the name of the place is called Succoth* (stored WEB).
- [[The Call of Gideon]] — Judges 6:22. *Alas, Lord Yahweh! Because I have seen Yahweh's angel face to
  face!* (stored WEB).
- [[Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel]] — Judges 8:5–9, 13–17. Succoth and Penuel refuse Gideon bread; he
  punishes both (stored WEB).
- 1 Kings 12:25 — *Then Jeroboam built Shechem in the hill country of Ephraim, and lived in it; and he
  went out from there, and built Penuel* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested).

## Analysis

**The same two towns, in the opposite order.** Jacob comes from the north with his family, crosses the
Jabbok, wrestles at Peniel, meets Esau, and then goes on to Succoth, where he builds (Genesis 32–33).
Gideon crosses the Jordan from the west and comes first to Succoth, then *went up there to Penuel*
(Judges 8:8). The two towns are the only two named on both journeys. Jacob names both; Gideon damages
both.

**Face to face.** At Peniel Jacob says *I have seen God face to face*, *pānîm ʾel-pānîm* (Genesis 32:31,
Hebrew numbering; WLC). Gideon says the same of the angel, *pānîm ʾel-pānîm* (Judges 6:22, WLC). Both
men expected death and lived. Judges does not mention Jacob, and the phrase is not confined to these two
(Moses is said to speak with Yahweh *face to face* in Exodus 33:11 with a different construction, not
checked here). The wiki records the match without claiming that Judges 8 alludes to Genesis 32.

**Building and breaking.** Jacob *built himself a house* at Succoth (Genesis 33:17). Gideon *broke down
the tower of Penuel* (Judges 8:17). Jeroboam later *built Penuel* again (1 Kings 12:25). The narrator of
Judges does not draw the contrast.

**Spelling.** The Hebrew has *Pənîʾēl* at the naming (Genesis 32:31 Hebrew) and *Pənûʾēl* in the next
verse and in Judges 8:8, 9, 17 (WLC). See [[Peniel]].

## Significance

The two towns where Jacob, after his night with God, made peace with his brother and settled are the
towns where Gideon, after his victory, turns his anger on fellow Israelites. The thread is geographic and
lexical. Neither text cites the other. See [[Succoth (Transjordan)]], [[Peniel]], [[Jacob]], and
[[Gideon]].
