# Six Things Yahweh Hates - Proverbs 6.16-19 and the Ten Commandments

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 6:16–19** — [[The Surety, the Sluggard, and the Seven Abominations]]: *"There are six
  things which Yahweh hates; yes, seven which are an abomination to him: haughty eyes, a lying
  tongue, hands that shed innocent blood; a heart that devises wicked schemes, feet that are swift
  in running to mischief, a false witness who utters lies, and he who sows discord among
  brothers."*
- **Exodus 20:13–17** — [[The Ten Commandments]]: *"You shall not murder. You shall not commit
  adultery. You shall not steal. You shall not give false testimony against your neighbor"*, and
  the command against coveting.
- **Exodus 23:1, 7** — [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]]: *"You shall not spread a false report.
  Don't join your hand with the wicked to be a malicious witness"*; *"Keep far from a false charge,
  and don't kill the innocent and righteous: for I will not justify the wicked."*
- **Proverbs 6:20–35** — [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]: the lecture on the neighbour's wife
  (6:25, 29, 32) and the thief who must restore sevenfold (6:30–31).

## Analysis

**What the list shares with Sinai.** Proverbs does not cite the Decalogue, and its list is not a
paraphrase of it. But two of its seven items use the commandments' own matter, and one uses their
exact words. *"A false witness"* is *ʿēḏ šāqer* — the phrase of the ninth commandment, *lōʾ-ṯaʿăneh
ḇərēʿăḵā ʿēḏ šāqer* (Exodus 20:16 in English numbering; 20:13 in the Masoretic text, which prints
the four short commandments as one verse). *"Hands that shed innocent blood"* is the matter of the
sixth, and of Exodus 23:7's *"don't kill the innocent"*. Exodus 23:1 joins the two: its *"malicious
witness"* (*ʿēḏ ḥāmās*, *a witness of violence*) is a false witness whose words shed blood. The
Hebrew is read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

**The next lecture completes the table.** Proverbs 6:20–35 turns to adultery with a neighbour's
wife (6:29, 32) and compares it with theft (6:30–31). Read across the chapter, 6:16–35 touches
murder, false witness, adultery, and theft — the four short commandments of Exodus 20:13–16, in a
different order and none of them quoted as law. The wiki records the overlap, and makes no claim
that the editors arranged chapter 6 to match Exodus 20.

**What the list adds.** The commandments name acts; Proverbs' list names the organs and dispositions
the acts come from. **Eyes, tongue, hands, heart, feet**: five members, top to bottom, and the
*"heart that devises wicked schemes"* sits at the centre of the seven. That is the argument of
[[Keep Your Heart]] — *"out of it is the wellspring of life"* (4:23) — applied in reverse: what
Yahweh hates is not only the shed blood but the eyes raised in pride and the heart that ploughed
the scheme. Exodus 20:17 is the Decalogue's own move in that direction, since coveting is the one
commandment about desire, not deed.

**What the list puts last.** The Decalogue ends on the neighbour's goods. Proverbs ends on
*"discord among brothers"*. The climactic position in an *x, x+1* list carries weight (compare
Job 5:19, *"in seven no evil shall touch you"*), and Proverbs gives it to a sin that is not a
breach of any one commandment but the breaking of the community the commandments protect. The
worthless man of 6:12–15 is defined by it — *"always sows discord"* (6:14) — and the seven items
read as his portrait made into a list. See [[Strife and Peacemaking]] and [[Brotherhood]].

**Hate, not only forbid.** Exodus gives commands; Proverbs says what Yahweh *hates* (*śānēʾ*) and
what is *tôʿăḇâ* to him. Proverbs first used *tôʿăḇâ* at 3:32, *"the perverse is an abomination to
Yahweh"*, and will use it of pride (*"Everyone who is proud in heart is an abomination to
Yahweh"*, 16:5), of a false balance (11:1), and of lying lips (12:22). The vocabulary moves from law to the
character of the lawgiver: the seven are not merely illegal, they are what God cannot bear.

## Significance

The list gives Proverbs' moral teaching a fixed shape early, before the sentence collections
begin, and that shape is close to Sinai's second table without being drawn from it. It joins
three lines the wiki already tracks — the body members of [[Keep Your Heart]], the bloodshed of
[[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] and
[[Their Feet Run to Evil - Proverbs 1.16, Isaiah 59.7, and Romans 3.15]], and the commandments of
[[The Ten Commandments]] — and its final item points at a sin Genesis narrates more often than any
other: strife between brothers. See [[Law and Obedience]], [[Deception]], [[Blood]], and
[[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].
