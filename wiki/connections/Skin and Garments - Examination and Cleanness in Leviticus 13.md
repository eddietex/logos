# Skin and Garments - Examination and Cleanness in Leviticus 13

## Passages involved

- [[Examining Diseases of the Skin]] — Leviticus 13:1–46.
- [[Mildew in Garments]] — Leviticus 13:47–59.

## Analysis

Both units place inspection and a clean-or-unclean ruling with the priest. Seven-day
observation, further inspection, change over time, and washing recur. In the skin rules,
a clean ruling may need review when a condition spreads (7–8, 35–36). In the garment
rules, a washed and faded patch can recur and require destruction (56–57).

The criteria cannot simply be transferred between cases. Skin examinations attend to
hair, depth, raw flesh, and particular appearances; garments present greenish or reddish
marks. An unchanged garment stain remains unclean even without spread (55). The human
case concludes with residence outside the camp while the plague remains (45–46); the
material case can end in burning, removal of a patch, or washing and cleanness (52–58).
These are distinct dispositions, not a warrant to apply destruction of objects to people.

Both passages regulate status without identifying a personal offense that caused the
condition. Neither unit prescribes an offering. The garment conclusion (59) makes the
purpose of clean and unclean pronouncements explicit.

## Significance

Reading the adjoining laws together shows the reach of priestly discernment into bodies
and possessions, while preserving the separate criteria and consequences of each case.
The shared framework does not make every visible defect unclean or every impurity a sin.
