# The Wife of Your Youth - Proverbs 2.17, Malachi 2.14, and Jeremiah 3.4

## Passages involved

- [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] — Proverbs 2:17: the strange woman *"forsakes the friend of her
  youth, and forgets the covenant of her God"* (*ʾallûp̄ nəʿûreyhā … bərîṯ ʾĕlōheyhā*); 2:22, *"The
  treacherous (bôḡəḏîm) will be rooted out."*
- Malachi 2:14–16 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Yahweh has been witness between you and the wife of your
  youth, against whom you have dealt treacherously, though she is your companion, and the wife of your
  covenant … let no one deal treacherously against the wife of his youth."*
- Jeremiah 3:4 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Will you not from this time cry to me, 'My Father, you are the
  guide of my youth?'"* (*ʾallûp̄ nəʿuray*).
- Proverbs 5:18 *(not yet ingested)* — the son told to rejoice in *"the wife of your youth"*.
- Hebrews 13:4 *(not yet ingested)* — *"Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the bed be
  undefiled."*

## Analysis

**Three texts, one vocabulary.** Proverbs 2:17 and Malachi 2:14 both describe marriage with the
same cluster of terms: a partner *of one's youth* (*nəʿûrîm*), a *covenant* (*bərîṯ*), and its
breach as *forsaking* or *treachery* (*b-g-d*). The Hebrew of both was checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*. **Malachi accuses the husband who deals treacherously with his
wife; Proverbs 2 warns against the wife who forsakes her husband.** The direction of the betrayal is
reversed, the language is not.

**The covenant is God's, not only the couple's.** Proverbs says *"the covenant of her God"*; Malachi
says *"Yahweh has been witness between you"*. Both make God a party to the marriage — as witness in
Malachi, as the God whose covenant it is in Proverbs. This is the strongest Old Testament evidence
that marriage itself was spoken of as a *bərîṯ*, and it is why the wiki reads the strange woman of
Proverbs 2 as an unfaithful wife rather than only as a foreigner (the alternative is set out on the
passage page).

**Jeremiah turns the phrase onto Israel and God.** *ʾAllûp̄ nəʿuray*, *the friend* (WEB: *guide*) *of
my youth*, is what faithless Israel is invited to call Yahweh, in a chapter about Israel as an
unfaithful wife (Jeremiah 3:1–3, not quoted here). The human marriage of Proverbs 2:17 and the
covenant between Yahweh and Israel are described in the same words, which is the prophets' marriage
metaphor seen from the wisdom side.

## Significance

The thread shows Proverbs' sexual ethic resting on covenant, not on custom: adultery in Proverbs 2
is forgetting a covenant God witnessed, and Malachi uses the same language to forbid treacherous
divorce. See [[Marriage]], [[Covenant]], and [[Sexual Sin]].
