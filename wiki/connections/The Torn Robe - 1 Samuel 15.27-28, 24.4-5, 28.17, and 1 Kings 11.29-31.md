# The Torn Robe - 1 Samuel 15.27-28, 24.4-5, 28.17, and 1 Kings 11.29-31

## Passages involved

- [[Saul Rejected over Amalek]] — 1 Samuel 15:27–28; *Saul grabbed the skirt of his robe, and it tore*.
- 1 Samuel 24:4–5, 11, 20 — David cuts off *the skirt of Saul's robe* at En Gedi (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- 1 Samuel 28:17 — *Yahweh has torn the kingdom out of your hand, and given it to your neighbor, even to David*
  (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- 1 Kings 11:29–31 — Ahijah tears his new garment into twelve pieces (WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-20; not
  stored).
- 1 Samuel 2:19 — Hannah's *little robe* for Samuel (stored WEB).

## Analysis

Three times in 1 Samuel and once in 1 Kings, a garment is torn or cut, and a prophet or a king reads it as the
kingdom passing from one man to another.

| | 1 Samuel 15:27–28 | 1 Samuel 24:4–5, 20 | 1 Samuel 28:17 | 1 Kings 11:29–31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **garment** | *kənap̄ məʿîlô*, the skirt of his robe | *kənap̄ hamməʿîl … ləšāʾûl*, the skirt of Saul's robe | — | *a new garment* |
| **act** | grabbed, and it *tore*, *wayyiqqāraʿ* | *cut off … secretly* | — | *tore it in twelve pieces* |
| **who speaks** | Samuel | Saul | Samuel, from the dead | Ahijah |
| **the kingdom** | *Yahweh has torn*, *qāraʿ*, *the kingdom of Israel from you today* | *the kingdom of Israel will be established in your hand* | *Yahweh has torn*, *wayyiqraʿ*, *the kingdom out of your hand* | *I will tear the kingdom out of the hand of Solomon* |
| **to whom** | *a neighbor of yours who is better than you* | David | *your neighbor, even to David* | Jeroboam, *ten tribes* |

Hebrew for 1 Samuel from the WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20; *məʿîl* is lemma 4598 and *qāraʿ* lemma
7167. 24:4–5 in the WEB is 24:5–6 in the WLC. 1 Kings is quoted from the WEB only; no Hebrew of Kings was
checked.

**The robe in 1 Samuel.** *Məʿîl* is the word for the *little robe* Hannah made for Samuel each year (2:19), for
Jonathan's robe given to David (18:4), for Saul's robe at En Gedi (24:4, 11), and for the robe the medium sees on
the old man coming up at Endor (28:14) (stored WEB; WLC). In 15:27 it is Samuel's, if the WEB's reading of the
pronouns is right. Who grabbed and whose robe tore is not grammatically certain, and some readings have Samuel
tear Saul's robe; the WEB makes it Saul grabbing Samuel's (recalled that alternatives exist, not checked).

**What the likeness shows.** In chapter 15 the tear is an accident that the prophet turns into a sign. In chapter
24 David's cut is deliberate and secret, and his *heart struck him* for it (24:5); he will not stretch out his
hand against *Yahweh's anointed* (24:6), and it is Saul who draws the conclusion, *I know that you will surely be
king* (24:20). At Endor Samuel repeats 15:28 almost word for word and adds the name the earlier sentence withheld.
In 1 Kings the tearing is the prophet's own act, done to his own garment, and the pieces are counted out. The
sequence runs from an accident read as a sign to a sign acted out on purpose. That ordering is the wiki's, drawn
from the stored text; it is not claimed that the later passages allude to the earlier ones, although 28:17 plainly
recalls 15:28.

**The neighbor.** *Ləreʿăḵā*, *to your neighbor* (15:28; 28:17, WLC). Saul had been introduced as the man than
whom there was none *better* in Israel (9:2); the neighbor is *better than you* (15:28).

## Significance

- The kingdom passes in 1 Samuel by a garment before it passes by a battle. Saul's last scene with Samuel alive
  (15:27–31) and his last scene with Samuel dead (28:14–19) both turn on a robe and the word *torn*.
- David's restraint at En Gedi is measured against this sign: he holds the piece of the robe and does not take the
  kingdom.
