# Two Blind Fathers - Genesis 27 and Genesis 48

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 27:1–40** — [[Isaac]]'s eyes are dim, [[Jacob]] takes [[Esau]]'s blessing by impersonation,
  Isaac cannot undo it — [[The Stolen Blessing]]
- **Genesis 48:1–22** — [[Jacob]]'s eyes are dim, he gives the right hand to the younger of
  [[Joseph]]'s sons on purpose, and refuses to move it — [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]]
- **Genesis 25:23** — *"the elder will serve the younger"* — [[Jacob and Esau and the Sold Birthright]]
- Compare **Genesis 37:3–4; 42:38** — Jacob doing to his own sons what was done in his father's tent:
  preferring one
- **Hebrews 11:20–21** — the two blessings named in consecutive verses, **both as acts of faith**

## Analysis

**Genesis stages the same scene twice, seventy years apart, with the same man in both — once as the
beneficiary of a blind father's mistake and once as the blind father who makes no mistake.**

| element | Genesis 27 | Genesis 48 |
|---|---|---|
| **the father** | [[Isaac]], *"his eyes were dim, so that he could not see"* (27:1, *kāhâ*) | [[Jacob]], *"the eyes of Israel were dim for age, so that he couldn't see"* (48:10, *kābed*) |
| **who arranges the scene** | [[Rebekah]], against her husband | [[Joseph]], for his father (48:13) |
| **the question** | *"Who are you, my son?"* (27:18) | *"Who are these?"* (48:8) |
| **the answer** | ***a lie*** — *"I am Esau your firstborn"* (27:19) | ***the truth*** — *"They are my sons, whom God has given me here"* (48:9) |
| **the touch** | hands felt, and misread: *"the hands are the hands of Esau"* (27:22) | hands laid, and aimed: *"guiding his hands knowingly"* (48:14) |
| **who gets the blessing** | the younger | the younger |
| **the father's knowledge** | **none**: *"he didn't recognize him"* (27:23) | **complete**: *"I know, my son, I know"* (48:19) |
| **the protest** | [[Esau]], after the fact: *"Bless me, even me also, my father"* (27:34, 38) | [[Joseph]], during: *"Not so, my father; for this is the firstborn"* (48:18) |
| **the response** | *"Yes, he will be blessed"* (27:33) — **helpless** | *"His father refused"* (48:19) — **deliberate** |
| **the elder's future** | subjection: *"you will serve your brother"* (27:40) | greatness: *"he also will become a people, and he also will be great"* (48:19) |
| **the aftermath** | murderous hatred, twenty years of exile (27:41–45) | **nothing.** The chapter ends with a formula of blessing (48:20) |

**Nine of the eleven rows match. The two that do not are the whole point.**

### What the second scene removes

**The deceit.** **Genesis 27 needs four instruments to get the blessing onto the younger son** — goat
skins, a borrowed garment, a cooked kid, and a direct lie — **and Genesis 48 needs none.** The
narrator's ***śikkēl ʾet-yādāyw***, *guiding his hands knowingly* (48:14), **is inserted before the
clause *"for Manasseh was the firstborn"* precisely so that the reader cannot import chapter 27's
explanation.** **The root is that of *discreet and wise* at 41:33, 39.**

**The victim.** **[[Esau]] ends chapter 27 with a blessing that is a description of hardship and a
plan to kill his brother.** **[[Manasseh]] ends chapter 48 as the ancestor of a tribe settled on both
banks of the Jordan, told in the same sentence that demotes him that he *"also will be great."*** **A
reversal of precedence and a disinheritance are not the same act**, and Genesis 48 is the only one of
the book's four reversals that is unmistakably the first and not the second. See
[[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

### What it keeps

**Irrevocability.** *"Yes, he will be blessed"* (27:33) and *"His father refused"* (48:19) **are the
same doctrine from opposite sides**: once said, a blessing stands, whether the man who said it meant
it or not. See [[Yes, He Will Be Blessed - The Irrevocable Word]].

**The blindness.** **Both fathers lose their sight in the verse that opens their last blessing**, and
**both scenes make the hands do the work the eyes cannot** — Isaac's are felt and deceived, Jacob's are
placed and correct. **Genesis 48 uses *hand* eleven times in twenty-two verses.**

**A parent choosing between brothers.** **Jacob was the younger son his mother preferred** (25:28), **he
preferred [[Joseph]] over ten sons and said so with a coat** (37:3), **and he ends by preferring
[[Joseph]]'s younger son over his elder.** **The book does not condemn the pattern here and does not
excuse it in chapter 37**, where the same preference produces a pit and twenty pieces of silver.

### The one thing Genesis will not say

**Whether Jacob knows he is repeating his father's scene.** **He has every reason to**: he is the son
who exploited a blind man, and he is now a blind man giving a blessing out of birth order. **Genesis
gives him no line about it.** *"I know, my son, I know"* is the closest thing to a comment the chapter
contains, and what he says he knows is which boy is the firstborn, not what he once did in a tent in
[[Canaan]]. **The wiki records the symmetry and refuses the inference**, as it refuses it at
[[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]], where the same question arises about the
coat.

## Significance

**Genesis has a habit of running a scene twice and letting the second reading correct the first** — two
creation accounts, two floods' worth of covenant language, [[The Wife-Sister Episodes]], two
annunciations to [[Hagar]]. **This is the sharpest instance, because the same character occupies both
chairs.**

**And it settles a question chapter 25 opened.** ***"The elder will serve the younger"*** (25:23) **had
been fulfilled so far only by trickery, purchase and force** — a sold birthright, a stolen blessing, a
midwife's scarlet thread. **Genesis 48 shows the principle operating in the open, announced in
advance** (48:5), **enacted deliberately** (48:14), **restated by the narrator** (48:20), **with nobody
cheated.** See
[[The Elder Will Serve the Younger - Genesis 25.23 in Malachi and Romans]].

**Hebrews 11:20–21 names both blessings in consecutive verses and credits both to faith**, which is the
New Testament declining to distinguish between them at all. The wiki notes that Hebrews praises the
blessing Isaac gave in ignorance on the same ground as the one Jacob gave in full knowledge; see
[[On the Bed's Head or on His Staff - Genesis 47.31 in Hebrews 11.21]] and [[Faith]].
