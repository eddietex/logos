# Yahweh Walks in the Camp - Genesis 3.8, Leviticus 26.12, and Deuteronomy 23.14

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 3:8** — [[The Fall]]: *"They heard Yahweh God's voice walking in the garden in the cool of the
  day, and the man and his wife hid themselves from the presence of Yahweh God among the trees of the
  garden."*
- **Leviticus 26:12** — [[Blessings for Obedience (Leviticus)]]: *"I will walk among you, and will be your God, and you
  will be my people."*
- **Deuteronomy 23:14** — [[The Assembly and the Holy Camp]]: *"for Yahweh your God walks in the middle of
  your camp, to deliver you, and to give up your enemies before you. Therefore your camp shall be holy, that
  he may not see an unclean thing in you, and turn away from you."*
- **2 Corinthians 6:16** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"Even as God said, 'I will
  dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they will be my people.'"*

All Old Testament quotations from stored WEB.

## Analysis

**Three verses, one verb form.** In the Torah the hithpael of *hālak*, *to walk about, go to and fro*, has
God as its subject in only these three places (WLC morphology search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). Genesis 3:8 and
Deuteronomy 23:15 (WLC numbering) use the identical participle, *mithallēk*; Leviticus 26:12 has the
first-person perfect with *waw*, *wǝhithallaktî*, *"I will walk"*. Every other hithpael of the verb in the
Torah is human: Enoch and Noah who *"walked with God"* (Genesis 5:22, 24; 6:9), Abram told to *"walk before
me"* (17:1), and others. See [[Walking with God]].

**What the walking is for.** In each text it is presence in a bounded place, and in each the place has to
be fit for it.

- In the garden, the man and the woman hear him and *hide* — the first thing the text reports after they
  know they are naked (3:7, 10). Presence becomes something to flee.
- In Leviticus 26, the walking is promised as covenant blessing, inside the formula *"I will be your God,
  and you will be my people"*, on condition of obedience (26:3). See
  [[I Will Take You as My People - The Covenant Formula]].
- In Deuteronomy 23, the walking is already happening, in an army camp, and its purpose is military: *"to
  deliver you, and to give up your enemies before you"*. The condition is concrete — a man unclean by night
  goes out until sunset, and excrement is buried — and the warning is that he may *"see an unclean thing in
  you, and turn away"*.

**Nakedness and seeing.** Genesis 3 and Deuteronomy 23 both join God's walking to something that must not be
exposed. In Genesis it is the couple's nakedness, *ʿêrōm*, which they cover with fig leaves and still hide.
In Deuteronomy it is *ʿerwat dābār*, *the nakedness of a thing*, WEB *"an unclean thing"*. The nouns differ
(WLC lemmas 5903 and 6172), so the link is in the idea, not the word; the wiki records it as a thematic
parallel and not as an allusion.

**Paul's reading.** 2 Corinthians 6:16 gives *"I will dwell in them, and walk in them"* as something God said,
followed by the covenant formula. That is Leviticus 26:11–12 in substance, with *"dwell"* drawn from 26:11's
*"I will set my tent among you"* (stored WEB); the wording of Paul's Greek against the Septuagint was not
checked in this pass. Paul applies it to the church as *"a temple of the living God"*, and the next verse draws the
conclusion, *"'Come out from among them, and be separate,' says the Lord. 'Touch no unclean thing'"* (6:17) —
the same pairing of presence and separation from uncleanness that Deuteronomy 23 makes for the camp.

## Significance

The thread traces the God who walks among his people from the garden, through the promise of Leviticus,
to the war camp of Deuteronomy, where the presence is concrete enough to make sanitation a matter of
holiness. In each case the presence is the reason for a boundary, and the boundary protects the presence,
not only the people. See [[The Dwelling of God]] and
[[That I May Dwell Among Them - Tabernacle, Temple, Flesh, and New Creation]].
