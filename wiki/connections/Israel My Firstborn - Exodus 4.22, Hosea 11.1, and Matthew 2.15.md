# Israel My Firstborn - Exodus 4.22, Hosea 11.1, and Matthew 2.15

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 4:22–23** — *"Yahweh says, Israel is my son, my firstborn, and I have said to you, 'Let
  my son go, that he may serve me'; and you have refused to let him go. Behold, I will kill your son,
  your firstborn."* — [[Moses Returns to Egypt]]
- **Exodus 11:4–7** — *"About midnight I will go out into the middle of Egypt, and all the firstborn
  in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne, even to the
  firstborn of the female servant who is behind the mill"*; and against Israel *"a dog won't even bark
  or move its tongue"* — [[The Last Plague Announced]]. **The threat of 4:23 announced with an hour and a range**
- **Exodus 12:29** — *"At midnight, Yahweh struck all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the
  firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon"* —
  [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]]. **The threat of 4:23 carried out, in one verse**, and
  Pharaoh's first words afterward are *"go, serve Yahweh"* (12:31) — the *"that he may serve me"* of 4:23
- **Exodus 13:15** — Israel's firstborn redeemed because of it *(not yet ingested)*
- **Deuteronomy 14:1; 32:6** — *"You are the children of Yahweh your God"*; *"Isn't he your father
  who has bought you?"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Psalm 89:26–27** — of David's line: *"He will call to me, 'You are my Father …' I will also
  appoint him my firstborn"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Jeremiah 31:9** — *"I am a father to Israel. Ephraim is my firstborn."* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Hosea 11:1–2** — *"When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt.
  They called to them, so they went from them. They sacrificed to the Baals"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Matthew 2:13–15** — the flight into Egypt, *"that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the
  Lord through the prophet, saying, 'Out of Egypt I called my son'"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Romans 9:4** — *"who are Israelites; whose is the adoption"* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Exodus 4:22 is the first place Scripture calls Israel God's son, and it does so in a message to a
foreign king.** The sentence is not addressed to Israel and is not explained to them; it is the legal
ground of a demand. *Bənî bəkōrî yiśrāʾēl* — *my son, my firstborn, Israel* — and therefore *"Let my
son go"*, and therefore, if not, *"I will kill your son, your firstborn"*. **The sonship is introduced
as the reason one father may claim his child from another's house.** See [[Moses Returns to Egypt]].

**The word *firstborn* carries Genesis with it.** *Israel* is the name given to Jacob, the younger
twin who took the birthright (Genesis 25:31–34) and the blessing (27). Genesis sets aside the
firstborn in generation after generation — Cain, Ishmael, Esau, Reuben, Manasseh — and **Exodus then
gives the firstborn's title to the whole people, under the name of the younger son.** Jeremiah does
the same with Joseph's younger son: *"Ephraim is my firstborn"* (31:9). **In Scripture's usage
*firstborn* is a rank conferred, not a birth order observed**, and Psalm 89:27 says so in a verb:
*"I will also appoint him my firstborn"*. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**Hosea turns the title into a history — and a complaint.** *"When Israel was a child, then I loved
him, and called my son out of Egypt"* (11:1) — in the Hebrew *ûmimmiṣrayim qārāʾtî libnî*, *and out of
Egypt I called to my son*. **The next verse is the son's answer: *"They called to them, so they went
from them. They sacrificed to the Baals"*** (11:2). Hosea reads the Exodus as the moment the father
called, and the rest of Israel's history as the son walking away. The chapter goes on to God teaching
Ephraim to walk and bending down to feed him (11:3–4), and refusing to give him up (11:8–9). See
[[Divine Grief and Relenting]].

**Matthew applies Hosea's sentence to Jesus, and the application is the hard part.** *"Out of Egypt I
called my son"* (Matthew 2:15) — *Ex Aigyptou ekalesa ton huion mou* in the SBL Greek New Testament —
is attached to the flight *into* Egypt, not to the return, and Hosea's sentence is a statement about
the past, not a prediction. Three standard readings, none adopted:

- **Typology**: Jesus is Israel's son-ship embodied — he goes down into Egypt and comes out as the
  nation did, and where Hosea's son *"went from them"*, this son does not. *Fulfilled* means *filled
  full*, not *predicted and come true*.
- **Corporate identity**: the king is the nation's representative (Psalm 89:27 calls David's line
  *firstborn*, as Exodus 4:22 calls Israel), so what was said of Israel can be said of its king.
- **Proof-texting**: Matthew uses a verbal match to a passage whose context he does not intend; the
  critical view, which the wiki records as such.

**The Greek of Matthew matches the Hebrew of Hosea, not, on the commonly reported reading, the
Septuagint**, which is said to have *his children* rather than *my son*; the Septuagint was not
checked for this page. If so, Matthew deliberately keeps the singular *son* that Exodus 4:22 and
Hosea share.

**Matthew's chapter is Exodus 4 read backward.** Exodus 4:19, *"all the men who sought your life are
dead"*, becomes Matthew 2:20, *"those who sought the young child's life are dead"*, with the plural
kept. And **the death of Pharaoh's firstborn, announced at Exodus 4:23, has its shadow in Matthew
2:16**: a king who kills the boys of Bethlehem to reach one child. In Exodus the king who will not
release God's son loses his own; in Matthew the king tries to kill God's son and fails. See
[[Every Son Who Is Born - Pharaoh's Decree and Herod's]].

**The firstborn sonship is claimed in Exodus with a death sentence attached.** God's firstborn is
spared, Pharaoh's is struck, and Israel's own firstborn are thereafter redeemed because of that night
(13:15). See [[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]]. The New Testament carries the
title further: Christ is *"the firstborn of all creation"* (Colossians 1:15), and the church *"the
assembly of the firstborn"* (Hebrews 12:23).

## Significance

Exodus 4:22 gives the Bible its first account of what Israel *is* to God — not a client people or a
subject nation but a son, ranked first — and makes it the ground of the Exodus itself. **Hosea shows
that the title could be held and betrayed at once**; Matthew shows how the New Testament reads the
title as waiting for a son who would keep it. See [[Adoption]] and [[Covenant]].
