# The Seventh-Year Release - Exodus 23, Leviticus 25, and Deuteronomy 15

## Passages involved

- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:10–11; six years of sowing, and the seventh year the
  land is let *"rest and lie fallow, that the poor of your people may eat"*.
- [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]] — Leviticus 25:1–7, 18–22; *"a Sabbath of solemn rest for
  the land"*, its growth food for household, servants, foreigners, and animals, and the sixth-year
  harvest promised for three years.
- [[The Year of Release and the Open Hand]] — Deuteronomy 15:1–11; *"At the end of every seven
  years, you shall cancel debts"*.
- [[Read the Law to All Israel]] — Deuteronomy 31:9–13; the law read to all Israel *"in the set time of the year of release, in
  the feast of tents"*.

(WEB throughout, copied from `raw/bible/`.)

This page extends [[The Seventh Year's Food - Exodus 23 and Leviticus 25]], which compares the first
two laws and notes that neither legislates a remission of debts. Deuteronomy 15 is that legislation.

## Analysis

The now-ingested Deuteronomy 31:9–13 assigns the reading to the release-year feast of tents.
Men, women, little ones, and resident foreigners must hear; hearing aims at learning, fear,
and obedience, including children who have not known. This adds an instructional purpose
to the release calendar without resolving the relation to the land’s fallow cycle.


**Three laws keep a seventh year, and each releases something different.**

| | Exodus 23:10–11 | Leviticus 25:1–7 | Deuteronomy 15:1–11 |
|---|---|---|---|
| what is released | the land, left fallow | the land, kept as a Sabbath | debts owed by a brother |
| the word | *tišməṭennâ*, *let it rest* (23:11) | *šabbat šabbātôn*, *a Sabbath of solemn rest* (25:4) | *šəmiṭṭâ*, *release* (15:1, 2, 9) |
| who benefits | *"the poor of your people"*, then the animals | owner, servants, hired worker, foreigner, livestock, wild animals | the indebted *"neighbor and his brother"* |
| who is excluded | — | — | *"Of a foreigner you may require it"* (15:3) |
| the reason | none stated beyond the poor's food | *"a Sabbath to Yahweh"* (25:4) | *"Yahweh's release has been proclaimed"* (15:2) |
| the fear answered | — | *"What shall we eat the seventh year?"* (25:20) | the lender's *"The seventh year … is at hand"* (15:9) |

Hebrew: WLC Exodus 23:11; Leviticus 25:4; Deuteronomy 15:1–3, 9 (morphhb).

**The link is a verb.** *Šāmaṭ* is used of the land in Exodus 23:11 and of the creditor's hand in
Deuteronomy 15:2–3 (WLC, morphhb, lemma 8058). Leviticus 25 does not use it in 25:1–7; it uses
*šabbāt*. Exodus gives the land's produce to the poor; Deuteronomy gives the poor their debt back. In
both the seventh year is for the poor.

**Deuteronomy 15 does not mention the land's rest at all**, and Leviticus 25:1–7 does not mention
debts. The texts do not say whether the debt release was meant to fall in the same year as the
fallow land, or whether Deuteronomy's *"end of every seven years"* (*miqqēṣ*, WLC 15:1) means the
seventh year itself or its close. Readings that join the two into one sabbatical year are
harmonizations; the wiki reports the parallel and does not assert one calendar.

**Both later laws answer a fear the institution creates.** In Leviticus the fear is hunger:
*"What shall we eat the seventh year?"*, answered by a sixth-year harvest *"for the three years"*
(25:20–21). In Deuteronomy the fear is loss: a lender who sees the year coming and refuses a loan.
That refusal is called *"sin to you"* (15:9), and the answer is again blessing (15:10). See also
[[Provision Before Rest - Manna and the Sabbatical Year]].

**Deuteronomy 31:10 ties the year to the reading of the law**: at the end of every seven years, at
the Feast of Tents, the law is read to men, women, children, and the foreigner. The phrase *šənat
haššəmiṭṭâ*, *the year of release*, is the same as at 15:9 (WLC 15:9; 31:10, morphhb).

## Significance

- The seventh-year law grows from field to purse. Exodus releases the land so the poor may eat;
  Deuteronomy releases the debts the poor have incurred.
- The release of the Hebrew servant in Deuteronomy 15:12–18 follows directly and uses the same
  seven-year count, though with *ḥopšî*, *free*, not *šəmiṭṭâ*; see
  [[The Hebrew Servant Goes Free - Exodus 21, Deuteronomy 15, and Jeremiah 34]].
- Leviticus' fiftieth-year Jubilee, which returns land and people, is a separate institution not
  found in Deuteronomy. See [[The Land Is Mine - Pharaoh's Freehold and the Jubilee]].
