# Prohibitions and Penalties - Leviticus 18 and 20

## Analysis

Leviticus 18 lists forbidden relations and warns of cutting off and expulsion from the
land (18:24–30). Chapter 20 revisits many of those prohibitions while distinguishing
sanctions: death for adultery and several other acts (20:10–16), cutting off for the
sister and menstrual cases (17–18), bearing iniquity for the aunt case (19), and
childlessness for the uncle’s-wife and brother’s-wife cases (20–21). The lists are not
identical, and chapter 20 does not give a single execution procedure for all of them.

The Molech ban of 18:21 becomes a command to stone the offender, joined to Yahweh’s own
opposition even when the people conceal the offense (20:2–5). Consultation of mediums
in 19:31 is forbidden; 20:6 names cutting off for the consulter, while 20:27 prescribes
stoning for the practitioner. These distinctions preserve who does what to whom.

The same land-expulsion warning recurs (18:28; 20:22). The closing animal distinctions
(20:25) also recall 11:43–47: holiness concerns worship, households, and the distinction
of clean from unclean. Being separated to God (20:26) does not cancel Israel’s liability.

## Significance

The chapter adds differentiated penalties and community responsibility to the earlier
prohibitions, while keeping land tenure and belonging to Yahweh together.
