## Analysis

### The survey at Shiloh — Joshua 18:1–10

Joshua 18:4–10 now supplies an order not specified by the earlier commands: representatives traverse
the land, record seven portions by cities in a book, return to Joshua, and then receive allocation
by lot. Judah, Joseph, and the eastern grants are excluded from this new division (5, 7). Numbers
26:52–56 requires shares according to numbers and allocation by lot; Joshua 18 does not state that
the seven surveyed portions are equal in area or explain how population affects this survey.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 18:1–10 and the stored Torah passages cited.

### Judah’s boundary circuit

Judah’s lot now receives its own boundary circuit in Joshua 15:1–12. Its south follows the Salt Sea, Akrabbim, Zin, Kadesh Barnea, Azmon, the brook of Egypt, and the sea, shared with Numbers 34:3–5. Numbers names Hazar Addar; Joshua names Hezron, Addar, and Karka. These are not identical lists, and no name equation is needed to see the common southern line. Numbers describes Canaan’s outer boundary; Joshua describes one tribe’s allotment, whose northern line is therefore different. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34:1–12 and Joshua 15:1–12.

### The western division — Joshua 14:1–5

Joshua 14:1–5 now reports the western distribution under Eleazar, Joshua, and tribal household heads. Its nine-and-a-half tribes and lot correspond to Numbers 34:13–18; Moses’ eastern grants remain the reason for the reduced western count. The command has become narrated compliance (5), though this introduction does not enumerate the individual lots or establish that every assigned district is occupied. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:1–5 and Numbers 34:13–29.


Numbers 26 attaches inheritance to the counted generation, requiring shares in proportion to
numbers and distribution by lot. Numbers 33:54 repeats both instructions after the command
to possess the land. Numbers 34 adds the external boundaries, explicitly excludes the already
settled eastern holdings from this western distribution, and names its administrators.

Eleazar and Joshua, paired in the commissioning of Numbers 27, now lead a named commission
of ten tribal representatives. Ten names fit nine full tribes and the western half of Manasseh;
Reuben and Gad have no official in this roster. Caleb represents Judah, linking the commission
to the faithful spy who survived the wilderness sentence.

**Lot does not eliminate administration.** Numbers 34:13 retains lot while 17–29 appoints
human officers. Neither passage supplies the order of surveying, drawing lots, and adjusting
shares. These commands should not be converted into an invented procedure, nor into a report
that the distribution has already occurred.

Sources: stored WEB Numbers 26, 27, 33, and 34. Comparisons are paraphrases.

## Significance

The promised inheritance becomes an assigned public responsibility. Borders, recipients,
and officers are specified before the narrative reports settlement. See [[Land and Sojourning]],
[[Priesthood]], and [[Call and Election]].
