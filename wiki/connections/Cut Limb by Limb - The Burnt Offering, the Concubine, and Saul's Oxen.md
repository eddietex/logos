# Cut Limb by Limb - The Burnt Offering, the Concubine, and Saul's Oxen

## Passages involved
- **Exodus 29:17; Leviticus 1:6, 12; 8:20** — the burnt offering *cut into its pieces* (stored WEB). See
  [[The Burnt Offering]] (Leviticus 1).
- **Judges 19:29** — *"he took a knife, and cut up his concubine, and divided her, limb by limb, into
  twelve pieces, and sent her throughout all the borders of Israel"* (stored WEB). See
  [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]].
- [[The War Against Benjamin]] — **Judges 20:6** — *"I took my concubine, and cut her in pieces, and sent
  her throughout all the country of the inheritance of Israel"* (stored WEB).
- **1 Samuel 11:7** — *"He took a yoke of oxen, and cut them in pieces, and sent them throughout all the
  borders of Israel by the hand of messengers … The dread of Yahweh fell on the people, and they came out
  as one man"* (WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-17; 1 Samuel not yet in raw).
- **1 Kings 11:30** — Ahijah *"tore it in twelve pieces"*, his new garment (WEB checked at bible-api.com;
  not in raw). Different verb and object; listed for the number only.

## Analysis

**The verb.** *Nittaḥ*, *cut into pieces*, with its noun *nēṯaḥ*, *piece*, occurs in the WLC files of
Genesis through Judges only for the burnt offering and ordination ram (Exodus 29:17; Leviticus 1:6, 12;
8:20; *nəṯāḥîm* also Leviticus 1:8; 9:13) and for the concubine (Judges 19:29; 20:6) (lemmas 5408, 5409).
In 1 Samuel the verb occurs only at 11:7, of Saul's oxen (WLC). The Levite's act is described in the
vocabulary of the altar: *wayənattəḥehā laʿăṣāmehā lišnêm ʿāśār nəṯāḥîm*, *he cut her by her bones into
twelve pieces* (Judges 19:29, WLC); *wənittaḥ ʾōṯāh linṯāḥehā*, *cut it into its pieces* (Leviticus 1:6,
WLC). The wiki records the shared verb; it does not claim the Levite performed a sacrifice, and the text
does not call it one.

**The summons.** Judges 19:29 and 1 Samuel 11:7 share more than the verb: *wayəšalləḥehā bəḵol gəḇûl
yiśrāʾēl* and *wayəšallaḥ bəḵol-gəḇûl yiśrāʾēl*, *sent … throughout all the borders of Israel* (WLC). In
both the pieces are a call to muster, and both musters come out *as one man*, *kəʾîš ʾeḥāḏ* (Judges 20:1;
1 Samuel 11:7, WLC). Saul's message is explicit, *so shall it be done to his oxen*; the Levite's is not
spoken, and the onlookers supply it: *Consider it, take counsel, and speak* (Judges 19:30).

**The town.** Saul sends from Gibeah — *the messengers came to Gibeah of Saul* (1 Samuel 11:4) — the town
where the concubine was abused. Whether 1 Samuel 11 is written to recall Judges 19 the text does not say.
Readers who find a deliberate echo take it as reversing Gibeah's shame: the same town and the same act,
this time to rescue an Israelite city, Jabesh Gilead (1 Samuel 11:1, WEB checked at bible-api.com), rather than to destroy a tribe. Jabesh Gilead is also
the city Israel destroys in Judges 21 ([[Wives for Benjamin]]); see
[[Jabesh Gilead and Benjamin - Judges 21, 1 Samuel 11, and 1 Samuel 31]]. The wiki reports the reading and does
not adopt it beyond the verified wording.

**Twelve.** The concubine is cut into twelve pieces, one for each tribe. Ahijah's twelve torn pieces of
cloth (1 Kings 11:30) divide the kingdom; the word is different and the wiki lists it for comparison only.

## Significance

The altar's verb, used once outside the altar in the books the wiki has stored, measures how far the act
lies from any order the law knows. The same summons in Saul's hands — a king doing deliberately what a
Levite did in outrage, from the same town — is one of the threads that ties the epilogue of Judges, with
its *no king in Israel*, to the first king. See [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]]
and [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]].

### Judges 20 now ingested — *as one man*

The muster the pieces called is told three times *as one man*, *kəʾîš ʾeḥāḏ* (20:1, 8, 11, WLC). In the WLC
files of Genesis through 1 Samuel the phrase occurs otherwise at Numbers 14:15, Judges 6:16, and 1 Samuel
11:7, Saul's muster after the oxen. The Levite's own retelling at 20:6 keeps the verb *nittaḥ*,
*wāʾănattəḥehā*, *and I cut her in pieces*. In Judges the summons brings a war against a tribe of Israel; in 1
Samuel 11 the same summons brings Israel to the rescue of Jabesh Gilead (WEB checked at bible-api.com,
2026-11-17; not stored). See [[The War Against Benjamin]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 14:15; Judges 6:16;
20:1–11; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.
