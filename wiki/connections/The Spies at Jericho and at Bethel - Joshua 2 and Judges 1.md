# The Spies at Jericho and at Bethel - Joshua 2 and Judges 1

## Passages involved
- [[Rahab and the Spies]] — Joshua 2:1–24 (stored WEB).
- [[The Fall of Jericho]] — Joshua 6:22–25; Rahab's household saved (stored WEB).
- [[The Tribes That Did Not Drive Out]] — Judges 1:22–26 (stored WEB, now ingested).
- [[The Twelve Spies]] — Numbers 13:2; the verb of spying in Judges 1:23.

## Analysis

The two stories have the same outline. Spies go to a Canaanite city. A person of the city helps them
in. A promise of kindness is made. The city is struck, and the helper's household is spared.

| | Jericho (Joshua 2; 6) | Bethel (Judges 1:22–26) |
|---|---|---|
| Spies | Joshua *secretly sent two men … as spies* (2:1) | The house of Joseph *sent to spy out Bethel* (23) |
| Verb (WLC) | *məraggəlîm*, from *rāgal* (2:1; 6:25) | *wayyātîrû*, from *tûr*, as in Numbers 13:2 |
| The helper | Rahab hides them and acts first (2:4–6) | An unnamed man, seen *come out of the city*, is asked (24) |
| The promise | *we will deal kindly and truly with you* (2:14) | *we will deal kindly with you* (24) |
| Hebrew (WLC) | *wəʿāśînû ʿimmāḵ ḥesed weʾĕmet* | *wəʿāśînû ʿimməḵā ḥāsed* |
| Oath | Rahab asks them to *swear … by Yahweh* (2:12) | None reported |
| Saved | *Rahab … her father's household, and all that she had* (6:25) | *the man and all his family* (25) |
| Afterward | *She lives in the middle of Israel to this day* (6:25) | He *went into the land of the Hittites, built a city, and called its name Luz … to this day* (26) |

The promise is word for word the same clause (*we will do ḥesed with you*), with Joshua's *and truth*
lacking at Bethel. The verbs of spying differ: Judges uses the word of Numbers 13, not of Joshua 2.

The endings point in opposite directions. Rahab confesses that *Yahweh your God, he is God in heaven
above, and on earth beneath* (Joshua 2:11, stored WEB), and her household ends up *in the middle of
Israel*, *bəqereb yiśrāʾēl* (WLC). The man of Luz makes no confession, and he leaves. What he builds
outside the land is a new Luz, the old name of the city Israel has just taken. Both stories end *to
this day*.

The setting in Judges sharpens the contrast. Rahab was a Canaanite brought inside Israel. Immediately
after the man of Luz goes out, the chapter lists Canaanites staying inside Israel's tribes *among
them* (*bəqirbô*, 1:29–30), not by any oath but because they were not driven out. Judges does not
comment on the man or on the list. The wiki notes the shape and leaves the judgment open.

## Significance

The Bethel story is the only capture narrated in Judges 1 outside Judah's campaign, and it is told in
the shape of Israel's first capture in the land. Set side by side, they show the same act of kindness
leading one Canaanite household into Israel and another out of it. See also
[[Rahab and Gibeon - Two Oaths Preserving Life]] and [[Twelve Spies and Two - Numbers 13 and Joshua 2]].
