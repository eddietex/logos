# Flour for a Sin Offering - Leviticus 2 and 5

## Passages involved

- [[The Meal Offering]] — Leviticus 2:1–3, 8–10.
- [[Confession and Offerings According to Means]] — Leviticus 5:11–13.

## Analysis

Both provisions bring fine flour to a priest, burn a memorial portion on the altar,
and assign the remainder to the priesthood. Leviticus 5:13 itself compares the remainder
to the meal offering, so the relationship is explicit rather than an inferred typology.

The differences are equally explicit. Leviticus 2:1–2 includes oil and frankincense;
5:11 forbids both because this is a sin offering. Chapter 2 does not attach a particular
confessed offense or an explicit forgiveness formula to its flour rite. Chapter 5 places
flour at the end of an affordability sequence and promises atonement and forgiveness.
Shared material does not make the offerings interchangeable.

This limits an absolute reading of [[Blood]]: not every prescribed sin offering brings
its own animal blood. The flour burns on the existing altar offerings (5:12), but the
text gives no explanatory theory about borrowing their blood. Conversely, this exception
does not remove the priest, altar, confession, or prescribed gift.

Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 2 and 5:1–13. No exact Hebrew claim or New Testament quotation
is needed to establish this comparison.

## Significance

[[Sacrifice and Offering]], [[Atonement]], and [[Forgiveness]] must account for the flour
provision alongside animal rites. [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] are included by a
concrete adjustment of cost rather than a different promise of pardon.
