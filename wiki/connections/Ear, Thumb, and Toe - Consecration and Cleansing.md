# Ear, Thumb, and Toe - Consecration and Cleansing

## Passages involved

- [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]] — Leviticus 8:22–30.
- [[The Cleansing of the Leper]] — Leviticus 14:14–18, 25–29.

## Analysis

Moses applies consecration-ram blood to Aaron’s right ear, thumb, and great toe, then does
the same for Aaron’s sons (8:23–24). In the cleansing rite the priest applies trespass-offering
blood to the same three bodily locations of the healed person (14:14). The correspondence
is precise, but the offerings and recipients differ.

In chapter 8, oil and altar blood are sprinkled on priests and garments (30), following
earlier anointing. In chapter 14, the priest sprinkles oil seven times before Yahweh, applies
it at the blood-marked body parts, and puts the remainder on the head (15–18). The poverty
provision repeats these actions (25–29). These are related patterns, not an identical rite.

Neither passage assigns a symbolic meaning to each body part. Hearing, work, and walking
are interpretive associations rather than explanations supplied by these verses.

## Significance

Bodily marking participates both in consecration for [[Priesthood]] and restoration from
uncleanness. Shared [[Blood]] and oil applications do not confer the same office: chapter 14
restores a healed person without appointing him a priest.
