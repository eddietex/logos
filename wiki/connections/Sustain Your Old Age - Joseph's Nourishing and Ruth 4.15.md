# Sustain Your Old Age - Joseph's Nourishing and Ruth 4.15

## Passages involved

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — Genesis 45:11 *(stored WEB)*
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 47:12 *(stored WEB)*
- [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] — Genesis 50:21 *(stored WEB)*
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:38 *(stored WEB)*
- [[The Birth of Obed]] — Ruth 4:15 *(stored WEB)*

## Analysis

The verb *kilkēl* (the pilpel of *kûl*, *to hold, contain*), *to sustain* or *nourish* with food, occurs
four times in the WLC files the wiki has searched (Genesis–Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel): three times of Joseph
and once in Ruth.

| Verse | Who sustains | WEB | Hebrew |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis 45:11 | Joseph, promising | *There I will nourish you; for there are yet five years of famine* | *wəḵilkaltî* |
| Genesis 47:12 | Joseph | *Joseph nourished his father, his brothers, and all of his father's household, with bread* | *wayəḵalkēl* |
| Genesis 50:21 | Joseph, after Jacob's death | *I will nourish you and your little ones* | *ʾăḵalkēl* |
| Ruth 4:15 | the child Obed, as the women promise | *sustain you in your old age* | *ûləḵalkēl ʾeṯ-śêḇāṯēḵ* |

**Famine at both ends.** Joseph's three uses come in the famine that brought Jacob's household down to
Egypt; Ruth's comes at the end of a book that began with *a famine in the land* (Ruth 1:1) and a family that
went abroad to survive it. In both the one who sustains is a younger member of the family providing for the
older generation.

**Old age.** *Śêḇâ*, *gray hairs, old age*, is the object in Ruth. In the Joseph story it is Jacob's word
for what he fears losing Benjamin will do: *you will bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to Sheol*
(Genesis 42:38; *śêḇāṯî*, WLC; also 44:29, 31). Jacob's old age was threatened and then sustained by a son
he had thought dead; Naomi's, after the death of her sons, is promised sustenance by a grandson.

**Limits.** The shared verb is rare and the setting is shared, but Ruth does not mention Joseph, and the
verb is a natural one for feeding dependants. The wiki records the link as a thread of vocabulary and
situation, not as an allusion the narrator intended.

## Significance

- In both stories food and family survival are the same problem, and the answer is a younger kinsman who
  provides.
- In Ruth the provider is a newborn, so the promise is about the future: what Joseph did, Obed will do.
- See [[Famine and Plenty]] and [[Life and Death]].
