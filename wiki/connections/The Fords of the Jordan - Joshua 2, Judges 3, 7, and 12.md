# The Fords of the Jordan - Joshua 2, Judges 3, 7, and 12

## Analysis

The noun is *maʿbārâ*, *crossing-place* (OpenScriptures lemma 4569), from *ʿābar*, *to cross over*,
the verb of Israel's own crossing of the Jordan in Joshua 3–4. In the WLC files for Genesis through
1 Samuel it occurs at Genesis 32:22 (Hebrew 32:23, the ford of the Jabbok, singular *maʿăbar*), Joshua
2:7, Judges 3:28, Judges 12:5 and 6, and 1 Samuel 13:23; 14:4 (a pass, not a ford). Judges 7:24 tells
the same tactic without the word: *take … the waters … even the Jordan*.

| Verse | Who holds the fords | Against whom |
|---|---|---|
| Joshua 2:7 | (Jericho's men search toward them) | Israel's spies |
| Judges 3:28 | Ehud and Israel | Moab |
| Judges 7:24 | Ephraim, at Gideon's call | Midian |
| Judges 12:5–6 | Gilead | Ephraim |

The tactic is the same each time in Judges: hold the crossings, and the enemy on the wrong bank cannot
get home. In 3:28 *they didn't allow any man to pass over* (*wəlōʾ nātənû ʾîš laʿăbōr*, WLC), and *no
man escaped* (29).

**The line moves inward.** At Jericho the fords are where Israel's enemies look for Israel. In Ehud's
day Israel holds them against a foreign king. Under Gideon, Ephraim holds them against Midian. Under
Jephthah, Gilead holds them against Ephraim, and the test at the ford is how a man says *Shibboleth*:
*forty-two thousand of Ephraim fell* (12:6, stored WEB). The same place, and the same tactic, end up
used by one tribe against another. Ephraim, which held the waters in chapter 7, is caught at the fords
in chapter 12.

## Significance

The thread follows the book's arc from deliverance from outsiders to war among the tribes. It also
fits Judges 1 and 20, where Judah goes up first, first against the Canaanites and finally against
Benjamin (1:1–2; 20:18). Compare [[Left-Handed Men of Benjamin - Judges 3.15 and 20.16]].
