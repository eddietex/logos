# Rahab Saved by the Oath and the Walls Fallen by Faith

## Passages involved
- [[Rahab and the Spies]] — Joshua 2:12–21.
- [[The Fall of Jericho]] — Joshua 6:17, 22–25.
- Hebrews 11:30–31 — WEB verified from bible-api.com, 2026-11-10; not yet ingested.

## Analysis

Joshua 2 binds the spies to protect the family gathered in Rahab’s house. Joshua 6:22 expressly
invokes that oath, and the two spies themselves bring the household out. Their former host receives
both survival and an enduring place among Israel (25). The initial placement outside the camp
(23) and later residence within Israel (25) are both reported; no purification ceremony is narrated.

Hebrews pairs the falling walls with Rahab’s survival:

> By faith, the walls of Jericho fell down, after they had been encircled for seven days.
> By faith, Rahab the prostitute, didn’t perish with those who were disobedient, having received the spies in peace.

The quotation is the verified WEB of Hebrews 11:30–31. Hebrews interprets the events as faith;
Joshua describes the obedience, sheltering, oath, and rescue. This comparison does not make
Rahab’s scarlet cord a stated symbol of sacrificial blood, or claim that Joshua supplies Hebrews’
explicit interpretive vocabulary.

## Significance

The fall and rescue belong together: the city’s destruction is not the end of Rahab’s story.
Her incorporation is a named exception to destruction, not a denial that the destruction occurs.
