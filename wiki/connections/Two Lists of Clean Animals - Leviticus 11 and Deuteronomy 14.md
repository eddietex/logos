# Two Lists of Clean Animals - Leviticus 11 and Deuteronomy 14

## Passages involved

- [[Clean and Unclean Animals]] — Leviticus 11:1–47.
- [[A Holy People and Its Food]] — Deuteronomy 14:1–21.

## Analysis

The Torah gives the food law twice. Leviticus 11 is spoken by Yahweh to Moses and Aaron (11:1);
Deuteronomy 14 is Moses addressing the people. The tests are the same in both: land animals must
part the hoof and chew the cud, and water creatures must have fins and scales (Leviticus 11:3, 9;
Deuteronomy 14:6, 9). Most of the forbidden animals are the same, and Leviticus 11:5 and
Deuteronomy 14:7 use the same Hebrew name, *šāpān*, for the animal the WEB calls *cony* in one and
*rabbit* in the other (WLC, morphhb).

The differences:

| | Leviticus 11 | Deuteronomy 14 |
|---|---|---|
| Permitted land animals named | none | ten (14:4–5) |
| Ground given | *"be holy; for I am holy"*, and the exodus (11:44–45) | *"you are a holy people"*, chosen (14:2, 21) |
| Forbidden birds (WEB count) | 20 (11:13–19) | 21 (14:12–18) |
| Winged swarming things | forbidden, but four hopping kinds permitted (11:20–23) | forbidden, with no exception stated (14:19) |
| Carcass rules | long rules on touch, carrying, vessels, and water (11:24–40) | one clause, *"you shall not touch their carcasses"* (14:8) |
| Animal that dies of itself | edible kind: eating it makes unclean until evening (11:39–40) | not to be eaten; may go to the *gēr* or be sold to a foreigner (14:21) |
| Goat in its mother's milk | absent | closes the unit (14:21) |

**The insects are the sharpest difference.** Both chapters use the phrase *šereṣ hāʿôp*, *winged
swarming things* (WLC Leviticus 11:20; Deuteronomy 14:19, morphhb). Leviticus then permits the
locust, katydid, cricket, and grasshopper. Deuteronomy does not. Deuteronomy 14:20, *"Of all clean
birds you may eat"*, uses *ʿôp* where 14:11 used *ṣippôr* (WLC, morphhb), and some readers take
14:20 as allowing the clean winged things of Leviticus. The text does not say so, and the wiki
records the difference without resolving it.

**The bird lists** differ at one point in the Hebrew: Deuteronomy 14:13 reads *rāʾâ*, *ʾayyâ*, and
*dayyâ*, where Leviticus 11:14 reads *dāʾâ* and *ʾayyâ* (WLC, morphhb). The English names in the
WEB do not line up one for one across the two lists, and the wiki does not identify the species.

**The ground for the law is stated differently but not in conflict.** Leviticus derives Israel's
holiness from Yahweh's: *"be holy; for I am holy"* (11:44). Deuteronomy starts from Israel's status:
children of Yahweh, a holy people, chosen (14:1–2). Both treat the diet as following from that
holiness, not producing it.

Neither chapter says which was written first or whether one depends on the other, and the wiki
does not supply a direction of borrowing.

## Significance

The repetition shows the food law addressed to two audiences: the priests who teach the
distinctions (Leviticus 10:10–11; 11:1) and the people who keep them. See [[Clean and Unclean]],
[[Holiness]], and [[Law and Obedience]]. For the animal that dies of itself, see
[[Torn Flesh and Cleansing - Exodus 22 and Leviticus 11 and 17]]; for the New Testament, see
[[All Foods Clean - Leviticus 11, Deuteronomy 14, Mark 7, and Acts 10]].
