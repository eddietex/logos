## Analysis

**The word.** *Sāḵal*, *act foolishly* (lemma 5528), occurs five times in the WLC files of Genesis–2 Samuel:
Genesis 31:28 (*hiskaltā*, Hiphil); 1 Samuel 13:13 (*niskāltā*, Niphal); 1 Samuel 26:21 (*hiskaltî*, Hiphil);
2 Samuel 15:31 (*sakkel*, Piel, David's prayer concerning Ahithophel's counsel); and 2 Samuel 24:10 (*niskaltî*,
Niphal). The Niphal occurs only twice in those files: once spoken to Saul by the prophet, once spoken by David to
Yahweh.

**Two kings, one verdict, two speakers.** At Gilgal Samuel pronounces *You have done foolishly*, and Saul has
already explained himself: *I forced myself* (13:12). He does not confess. At the census David says it of himself,
*I have done very foolishly*, before the prophet Gad arrives, and adds *I have sinned greatly* and a prayer for the
iniquity to be *put away* (2 Samuel 24:10, WEB checked). Saul does reach the word himself later, at the hill of
Hachilah, *I have played the fool*, with the Hiphil and *I have sinned* (26:21, stored WEB); there he says it to
David, not to Yahweh.

**Both are kings' offences in matters the text treats as Yahweh's.** Saul offers the burnt offering before the
prophet comes; David counts the fighting men. In both the narrative leaves the exact wrong partly unstated — the
commandment at Gilgal is not quoted again (the instruction in view is 10:8), and 2 Samuel 24 opens with Yahweh's
anger already *kindled* (recalled, not checked; 2 Samuel is not stored). Both bring a prophet's word of
consequence. The consequence differs: Saul's kingdom *will not continue* (13:14); David's house stands, and the
punishment falls on the people (2 Samuel 24, recalled, not checked).

**Laban's use is the odd one.** Laban says it to the man who ran from him, not as a judgment from God but as a
grievance, *you didn't allow me to kiss my sons and my daughters* (Genesis 31:28, stored WEB). He goes on to say God
told him not to harm Jacob (31:29).

## Significance

The verb gives the two kings a common word for the moment each acted on his own judgment in a matter of Yahweh's,
and it lets the books set their responses side by side: Saul answered with reasons and later with a confession to
the man he was hunting, David with a confession to Yahweh before the prophet came. The wiki does not claim the
narrator built the pair; the verb's rarity in these books makes the comparison easy to see. See
[[Folly]], [[Saul]], and [[David]], and for the census, [[David's Count and Moses' Count - Numbers 1, 2 Samuel 24, and 1 Chronicles 21]].
