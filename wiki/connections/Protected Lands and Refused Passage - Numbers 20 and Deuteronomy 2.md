# Protected Lands and Refused Passage - Numbers 20 and Deuteronomy 2

## Passages involved
- [[Edom Refuses Passage]]
- [[Around Seir and Past Moab and Ammon]]

## Analysis

Numbers 20:14–21 narrates a diplomatic request from Kadesh, an offer to pay for water,
Edom’s armed refusal, and Israel’s withdrawal. Deuteronomy 2:1–8 recalls a long circuit of
Seir, a command to turn north, the brotherhood of Esau’s descendants, and Yahweh’s prohibition
of contention because Seir has already been given to Esau. Food and water are to be purchased.

**Difference retained:** Numbers emphasizes the refusal by Edom; Deuteronomy emphasizes the
divine limit on Israel. Passing by in Deuteronomy 2:8 need not describe the passage through
Edom requested in Numbers, but neither account supplies a detailed route to prove that
harmonization. A command to buy in 2:6 is not itself a report that a sale occurred.

Deuteronomy extends protected possession to Moab and Ammon (2:9, 19), both identified through
Lot. Genesis 36:6–8 records Esau settling in Seir after separation from Jacob; Deuteronomy
interprets that possession as Yahweh’s gift. This is a narrative comparison, not a prophecy
and fulfillment claim.

## Significance

[[Land and Sojourning]] includes respecting the land given to others. [[Brotherhood]] and
[[War]] meet in restraint, without requiring a claim that the neighboring nations welcomed
Israel. Sources: stored WEB Numbers 20:14–21, Deuteronomy 2:1–23, and Genesis 36:6–8.
