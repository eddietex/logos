# The Diseases of Egypt - The Plagues in Deuteronomy 28

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 9:8–11** — the boils, *šəḥîn* — [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]
- **Exodus 10:12–15** — the locusts, *ʾarbeh* — [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- **Exodus 10:21–22** — *"darkness which may be felt"*, *wəyāmēš ḥōšek*; *"thick darkness"*,
  *ḥōšek-ʾăpēlâ* — [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- **Deuteronomy 28:27** — *"Yahweh will strike you with the boils of Egypt, with the tumors, with the
  scurvy, and with the itch, of which you can not be healed."* (WEB)
- **Deuteronomy 28:29** — *"You will grope at noonday, as the blind gropes in darkness, and you shall
  not prosper in your ways."* (WEB)
- **Deuteronomy 28:38** — *"You will carry much seed out into the field, and will gather little in;
  for the locust will consume it."* (WEB)
- **Deuteronomy 28:60** — *"He will bring on you again all the diseases of Egypt, which you were afraid
  of; and they will cling to you."* (WEB)
- **Deuteronomy 28:68** — *"Yahweh will bring you into Egypt again with ships"* (WEB)
- [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] — Leviticus 26:16; *šaḥepet* and *qaddaḥat*, *consumption and fever*, found only there and at
  Deuteronomy 28:22 (WLC lemma search). The Exodus plagues themselves are not named in Leviticus 26.
- **Job 2:7** — the same boil on a blameless man —
  [[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]]

## Analysis

**Deuteronomy 28's curses for breaking the covenant include the plagues of Egypt, by name and by
vocabulary.** None of Deuteronomy is yet ingested, so the verses are quoted from the WEB at
bible-api.com, and the Hebrew links were checked in the WLC.

| Plague in Exodus | The curse in Deuteronomy 28 | Shared Hebrew (WLC) |
|---|---|---|
| boils (9:9–11) | *"the boils of Egypt"* (28:27) | *šəḥîn* |
| darkness that may be *felt*, *thick* (10:21–22) | *"grope at noonday, as the blind gropes in darkness"* (28:29) | *māšaš*; *ʾăpēlâ* |
| locusts (10:12–15) | *"the locust will consume it"* (28:38) | *ʾarbeh* |
| all of them | *"all the diseases of Egypt, which you were afraid of"* (28:60) | — |

**The darkness link is the closest.** *Māšaš*, *feel, grope*, gives the ninth plague its strangest
phrase, *"darkness which may be felt"* (Exodus 10:21). *ʾĂpēlâ*, *thick darkness*, names it in the next
verse. **In the Torah *ʾăpēlâ* occurs only at Exodus 10:22 and Deuteronomy 28:29, and Deuteronomy 28:29
has *māšaš* twice in the same verse** (WLC lemma search). **The curse makes Israel the people who grope
in Egypt's darkness**, and at *noonday*, when there should be light.

**The boils are named outright.** *"The boils of Egypt"* (28:27) can only mean the sixth plague. See
[[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]], which follows the same boil to Job.

**The locusts are named by word, not by place.** *ʾArbeh* in the Torah is found only in Exodus 10,
in the food law (Leviticus 11:22), and here (WLC lemma search). Deuteronomy does not say *the locusts
of Egypt*; the reader who knows Exodus 10 supplies it.

**And then the curses send Israel back.** *"All the diseases of Egypt, which you were afraid of"* (28:60)
— *which you feared*, the plagues Israel watched from Goshen. The chapter ends with Israel taken *"into
Egypt again with ships"* to be sold as slaves (28:68). **The covenant curse is the Exodus run
backwards**: the plagues return, and the people return to Egypt.

## Significance

- **It shows the distinction of the plagues is conditional.** In Exodus the darkness fell on Egypt
  while *"all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings"* (10:23). Deuteronomy says the same
  darkness can fall on Israel. See [[Call and Election]] and [[Covenant]].
- **It makes the plagues the standard language of covenant judgment.** Joel reuses the locusts and the
  darkness in the same way; see [[The Day of the Locust - Exodus 10 and Joel 1-2]] and [[Judgment]].
- **It explains why Exodus 10:2 insists the plagues be told to *"your son, and … your son's son"*.**
  The story of what God did to Egypt is also the warning of what he can do to Israel. See
  [[Remembrance]].
- **It explains the friends' reading of Job**: a man with *"the boils of Egypt"* looks cursed by the
  rules. See [[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]].
