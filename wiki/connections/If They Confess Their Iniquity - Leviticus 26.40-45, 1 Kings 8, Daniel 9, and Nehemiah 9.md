# If They Confess Their Iniquity - Leviticus 26.40-45, 1 Kings 8, Daniel 9, and Nehemiah 9

## Passages involved

- [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]] — Leviticus 26:40–45.
- **Deuteronomy 4:27–31** — scattered among the peoples, then *"He will not fail you, neither destroy
  you, nor forget the covenant of your fathers"* (WEB).
- **Deuteronomy 30:1–3** — return, then *"release you from captivity … gather you"* (WEB).
- **1 Kings 8:46–50** — Solomon's prayer for a people taken captive: *"We have sinned, and have done
  perversely; we have dealt wickedly"* (WEB).
- **Psalm 106:44–45** — *"He remembered for them his covenant"* (WEB).
- **Ezekiel 16:60** — *"Nevertheless I will remember my covenant with you"* (WEB).
- **Nehemiah 1:8–9; 9:2** — scatter and gather recalled; *"confessed their sins, and the iniquities
  of their fathers"* (WEB).
- **Daniel 9:4–16** — *"made confession"*, *"their trespass that they have trespassed against you"*,
  *"the curse and the oath written in the law of Moses"*, *"the iniquities of our fathers"* (WEB).

## Analysis

**Later prayers do what Leviticus 26:40 describes, in its words** (WLC):

| Leviticus 26:40 | Later |
|---|---|
| *wəhitwaddû ʾet-ʿăwōnām wəʾet-ʿăwōn ʾăbōtām* | Nehemiah 9:2 *wayyitwaddû … waʿăwōnôt ʾăbōtêhem* |
| *bəmaʿălām ʾăšer māʿălû bî* | Daniel 9:7 *bəmaʿălām ʾăšer māʿălû bāk* |
| — | Daniel 9:16 *ûbaʿăwōnôt ʾăbōtênû* |

The Daniel 9:7 clause matches, with *against me* turned to *against you*. **The sentence Yahweh
speaks in Leviticus is spoken back to him.** A lemma search finds *yādâ*, *confess*, with *ʿāwōn* in
only four verses: Leviticus 16:21; 26:40; Nehemiah 9:2; Psalm 32:5 (WLC; proximity search,
so Daniel 9, which divides the terms between verses, is not among them). Daniel also names the source
as *the law of Moses* (9:11). No claim is made here that he means this chapter alone rather than
the curse tradition with Deuteronomy.

**1 Kings 8 shares the place, not the verb.** *Bəʾereṣ ʾōyəbêhem*, *in the land of their enemies*, is
at 1 Kings 8:48 and Leviticus 26:41, 44 (WLC). Solomon's verbs are *return* and *plead*, with no
*hitwaddâ*. He also asks for *forgiveness* and for favor from the captors, neither of which Leviticus
26 mentions.

**What differs across the thread.**

| | Human act | God's act | Return stated? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leviticus 26:40–45 | confess, humbled heart, accept punishment | remember; not reject | **no** |
| Deuteronomy 4:29–31 | seek, return, listen | not fail, destroy, or forget | no |
| Deuteronomy 30:1–3 | call to mind, return, obey | release, compassion, gather | **yes** |
| 1 Kings 8:46–50 | return, plead, pray toward the land | hear, forgive, give compassion | no |
| Psalm 106:44–45 | a cry | hear, remember, relent | no |

**Leviticus is the sparest of these.** Its turning point is an act of memory, and its last word is a
refusal to finish Israel off. The later texts add what it leaves out.

**Is it a condition?** WEB's *if* at Leviticus 26:40 is not an *ʾim*. The Hebrew form could equally be
read as *they will confess*, and verse 44 is unconditional. Deuteronomy 30:1 is framed with *when*.
See the note at [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]]. **The tension between a required repentance and an assured remembering is not
resolved in the thread.** Psalm 106:44–45 has God remembering at a cry, with no confession mentioned
in those verses.

## Significance

- It gives [[Prayer]] a pattern: exile prayers quote the covenant's own terms back to God.
- It sets [[Remembrance]] after the worst outcome: see
  [[God Remembered - Noah, Abraham, Rachel, and Israel]]. The same verb that opened the exodus is
  promised for exile.
- It places [[Hope]] where the chapter places it, in God's refusal to end the covenant. It does not
  rest on a date or on Israel's record.
- It marks a limit of [[Guilt and Conscience]]: these texts require saying so, and also keep the
  fathers in the sentence. Compare [[The Ten Commandments]].
