# Don't Go Down to Egypt - The Three Patriarchs and the Same Road

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 12:10–20** — [[Abram in Egypt]]. A famine; Abram goes down without
  consulting anyone; Sarai is taken into Pharaoh's house; the household is expelled with its goods.
- **Genesis 26:1–6** — [[Isaac in Gerar]]. A famine; Yahweh appears and forbids the journey outright;
  Isaac stays and is blessed in the land.
- **Genesis 46:1–7** — [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]]. A famine; Jacob stops at [[Beersheba]] and
  sacrifices; God answers by night and sends him down with a promise.
- **Isaiah 30:1–2; 31:1** and **Jeremiah 42:13–43:7** — going down to Egypt as the standing prophetic
  image of a people trusting the wrong thing.

## Analysis

**Three patriarchs, one famine each, one road, and three different answers — and Genesis arranges them
so that the differences are the point.**

| | the famine | what he did | what God said | how it went |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **[[Abraham]]** (12:10) | *"There was a famine in the land"* | **went down**, unprompted | **nothing** — God is not consulted and does not speak | wife taken, plagues, expulsion |
| **[[Isaac]]** (26:1–2) | *"besides the first famine that was in the days of Abraham"* | **started for Gerar** | ***"Don't go down into Egypt. Live in the land I will tell you about"*** | stayed; sowed and reaped a hundredfold |
| **[[Jacob]]** (46:1–4) | the seven years of 41:30 | **stopped at Beersheba and sacrificed** | ***"Don't be afraid to go down into Egypt"*** | went down with a promise of return |

**The text makes the three deliberately comparable.** **Genesis 26:1 explicitly cross-references
12:10** — *"besides the first famine that was in the days of Abraham"* — **so the second episode is
written as a rerun of the first with a correction inserted.** And **46:1's sacrifice is addressed to
*"the God of his father, Isaac"***: **not the God of Abraham, but the God of the man who was told to
stay.** **Jacob makes his enquiry to the deity on record as having prohibited the journey**, at
Beersheba, the town where that deity appeared to Isaac by night (26:23–24).

### The prohibition is not a rule about Egypt

**If 26:2 were a standing law, 46:3 would break it.** **It is not, and the wiki reads the three
episodes as three occasions rather than one policy.** What distinguishes them is not the destination
but **whether the man asked**:

- **Abram did not ask and was not forbidden.** Genesis reports the descent, the deception and the
  expulsion without a word of divine comment — **the episode's whole judgment is carried by what
  happens in it.**
- **Isaac was about to ask nothing and was stopped anyway**, with the promise re-attached to the land
  he was standing in.
- **Jacob asked, by sacrificing, and was sent.** **The going down that God authorises is the one that
  was submitted for authorisation.**

### The direction of the verbs

***Yārad***, *to go down*, is the verb in all three (12:10; 26:2; 46:3–4). **It is geographically
accurate — Canaan is high country and the delta is not — and it becomes theologically loaded.** **Only
in the third episode does *ʿālâ*, *to go up*, appear alongside it**: *"I will go down with you into
Egypt. I will also surely bring you up again"* (46:4). **Abram came back up without being promised it;
Isaac never went; Jacob is told in advance that the road runs both ways.**

### What the prophets do with it

**By Isaiah and Jeremiah the phrase has hardened into an accusation.** **Isaiah 30:1–2 and 31:1
condemn those who go down to Egypt for help and trust in horses and chariots without asking Yahweh**;
**Jeremiah 42–43 is a whole episode built on it** — the remnant asks Jeremiah for a word, swears to
obey it, is told plainly not to go to Egypt, and goes, **taking the prophet with them.** **That is
Abram's version of the journey, four hundred years of covenant later, with the enquiry made and
ignored.**

**The prophets are not condemning Genesis 46.** **They are condemning the pattern Genesis 12 set and
Genesis 46 broke** — and the thing that breaks it is the sacrifice at Beersheba and the night that
followed.

## Significance

**The wiki's reading is that Genesis is not teaching a geography.** **Egypt is not a forbidden
country; it is the place the family goes when it stops being able to feed itself**, and the book's
interest is in whether the going is submitted to God or merely done. **The one descent God commands is
the one that produces the nation** (46:3), **and the one He says nothing about is the one that costs a
patriarch his wife's safety** (12:15).

**It also sets a limit on how the land promise is read.** **The covenant of 12:7 and 26:3 is about
Canaan, and in 46:3–4 God personally escorts the covenant family out of it.** **The promise turns out
to survive its own suspension**, which is the thing the Exodus will be for and the thing Jacob is told
in advance so that he can leave without fear. See [[Covenant]], [[Land and Sojourning]] and
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].
