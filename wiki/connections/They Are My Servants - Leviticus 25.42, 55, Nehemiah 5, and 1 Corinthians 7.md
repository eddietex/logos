# They Are My Servants - Leviticus 25.42, 55, Nehemiah 5, and 1 Corinthians 7

- **Leviticus 25:42** — *"For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt. They
  shall not be sold as slaves."* — [[The Poor Brother and Redemption from Service]]
- **Leviticus 25:55** — *"For to me the children of Israel are servants; they are my servants whom

## Analysis

**Leviticus turns the exodus into a title deed.** The demand at Exodus 4:23 was never
that Israel should stop serving; it was a change of whom it served. Leviticus 25 applies that
change as law: *ʿăbāday hēm*, *they are my servants* (25:42, 55; Miqra according to the Masorah,
Sefaria). Because the slave-owner's rights are taken already, no Israelite can acquire them.

**The claim is an argument, and the argument has a limit.** Its reason is that Yahweh *brought them
out*. The same chapter lets Israel own foreign slaves forever (25:44–46), so the argument
protects those it names. It does not reason from what a human being is. The wiki elsewhere records
a broader ground, the shared maker of Job 31:13–15; see
[[Contempt for His Maker - Proverbs 14.31 and Job 31.13-15]].

**History breaks the law at the very points it foresaw.** Second Kings 4:1 has a creditor
coming for a widow's children. Nehemiah 5 has Jews mortgaging fields, borrowing, and handing over
children, then being told *You exact usury, everyone of his brother* (5:7). **Nehemiah 5:8 is
close to the case of Leviticus 25:47–48, with an inversion**: the community has bought brothers back
from the nations, and brothers are being sold within it. Leviticus's buyer is a resident alien;
Nehemiah's phrase is *sold to the nations*. Nehemiah does not cite Leviticus in these
verses, so the link is one of situation and subject, not a marked quotation.

**Paul's sentence has the same structure.** *You were bought with a price. Don't become bondservants
of men* (1 Corinthians 7:23) argues from a prior purchase to a refused human mastery, and the
same paragraph tells a slave to take freedom if it comes (7:21). Paul does not cite Leviticus 25,
and the parallel is structural; the Greek was not checked here. Unlike Leviticus, the claim's
scope in that passage is not limited to an ethnic people, though neither text abolishes slavery as
an institution.

**The next verses keep the claim.** [[Blessings for Obedience]] ends at 26:13 on the same exodus, now
stated from the other side: *that you should not be their slaves*. The promises of land and presence
are closed by the release that 25:42, 55 had made the ground of the law. Source: stored WEB Leviticus
25–26, 2026-10-30.

## Significance

The thread gives a theological reason for release that is not sentiment: **a person cannot be sold
by someone who does not own him.** See [[Slavery and Servitude]], [[Redemption]],

[[The Hebrew Servant Goes Free - Exodus 21, Deuteronomy 15, and Jeremiah 34]].
