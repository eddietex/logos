# Two Explanations for One Flock - The Rods and the Dream

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 30:37–43** — peeled rods in the watering-troughs, selective breeding, and *"the man
  increased exceedingly"* — [[Jacob's Flocks]]
- **Genesis 31:4–13** — Jacob to Leah and Rachel: *"God has taken away your father's livestock and
  given them to me"*, and the angel in the dream with the streaked goats *(not yet ingested)*
- **Genesis 31:38–42** — Jacob to Laban: twenty years, ten changes of wages, and *"unless the God of
  my father… had been with me, surely now you would have sent me away empty"* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Genesis 45:5–8; 50:20** — the same double account in the next generation: *"you sold me"* and
  *"God sent me"* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Genesis tells the story of Jacob's flocks twice, and the two tellings have nothing in common
except the flocks.**

**The first is the narrator's, in [[Jacob's Flocks]].** Seven verses of procedure: poplar, almond and
plane rods with the bark peeled to bare the white wood; the rods set in the troughs where the animals
came to drink; the flocks conceiving in front of them; the young separated; the rods used on the
strong animals and withheld from the feeble. **God is not in any of it.** He is named twice in the
pericope and both times inside a human sentence, and **no verb in the whole passage has him as its
subject.** The result is stated as a flat consequence: *"So the feebler were Laban's, and the
stronger Jacob's. The man increased exceedingly."*

**The second is Jacob's, one chapter later.** Speaking to his wives in a field, he says his wages
were changed ten times, that *"God has not allowed him to hurt me"*, that when the flocks conceived
he saw in a dream that the males were streaked and speckled, and that **the angel of God addressed
him by name and said *"I have seen all that Laban does to you"*** — and then, flatly:
***"God has taken away your father's livestock and given them to me"*** (31:9). **Rods are not
mentioned.** Trees are not mentioned. The one time a technique surfaces at all it has become
something he *saw*, not something he did.

**The two accounts are not contradictory in the way two dates or two names would be.** Nothing in
30:37–43 says God was uninvolved, and nothing in 31:9–12 says Jacob cut no rods. **But the book
supplies no sentence joining them**, and it never returns to say which one the reader should carry.

**Three readings are standing, and the wiki adopts none of them.**

1. **Both are true, at different levels.** God's providence works through ordinary means, and the
   rods (or the selective breeding underneath them) are the means. **This is the harmonising reading
   and it fits everything the text says** — but Genesis does not say it, and Jacob's speech in 31
   conspicuously does not mention the means.
2. **Jacob is revising.** The dream is a later gloss he puts on his own cleverness, told to two women
   he is asking to leave home with him, in a speech that is also an argument. **Genesis records the
   speech without endorsing it** — as it records Abram's *"say you are my sister"* and Jacob's *"I am
   Esau your firstborn"* without comment.
3. **The narrator is withholding.** The pericope's silence is deliberate, and 31:9–12 is the payoff:
   the reader is shown six years of husbandry and then told what was underneath it. **On this
   reading the two accounts are one account, delivered in order.**

**What is checkable is the shape, and the shape is this book's habit.** Genesis narrates human
agency in full procedural detail and attributes the outcome to God elsewhere, in a different voice,
without reconciling the two. **It does it with Rachel's mandrakes** — a fertility drug changes hands
in [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and every conception in the chapter is credited to God, seven
verses later, with the drug never mentioned again. **It does it with the birthright and the
blessing**, where the oracle of 25:23 stands over chapters of pure manoeuvre and is never quoted by
anyone doing the manoeuvring. **And it does it, most explicitly of all, with Joseph**: *"you sold me
into Egypt"* and *"God sent me before you to preserve life"* (45:4–5) — **the same event, both
sentences in one breath, by the same speaker.**

## Significance

**This is the wiki's standing example of a place where Genesis declines to arbitrate between
providence and technique**, and it is the reason the passage page for [[Jacob's Flocks]] credits the
increase to neither.

**The tempting move is to let Genesis 31 settle Genesis 30.** It is the last word, it is theological,
and it comes from the man who was there. **The wiki does not make that move**, for two reasons the
text supplies: 31:9–12 is **reported speech inside an argument**, not narration, and the narrator who
gave seven verses to rods had every opportunity to mention an angel and did not.

**What the pair does establish is that the book is not embarrassed by either half.** It reports a
folk breeding technique resting on maternal impression without correcting it, and it reports a dream
of an angel without qualifying it, **and it puts them nineteen verses apart about the same goats.**
The reader is left holding both, which is the same position [[Joseph]] leaves his brothers in at
50:20.
