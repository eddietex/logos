# He Who Answers Before He Hears - Proverbs 18.13, 17 and Elihu's Waiting

## Passages involved

- **Proverbs 18:13** — *"He who gives answer before he hears, that is folly and shame to him."* —
  [[A Strong Tower]]
- **Proverbs 18:17** — *"He who pleads his cause first seems right; until another comes and questions
  him."* — [[A Strong Tower]]
- **Proverbs 18:2** — *"A fool has no delight in understanding, but only in revealing his own
  opinion."* — [[A Strong Tower]]
- **Proverbs 18:15** — *"The ear of the wise seeks knowledge."* — [[A Strong Tower]]
- **Job 32:4** — *"Now Elihu had waited to speak to Job, because they were elder than he."* —
  [[Elihu Speaks in Anger]]
- **Job 32:11–12** — *"Behold, I waited for your words, and I listened for your reasoning, while you

- **Job 13:5** — *"Oh that you would be completely silent! Then you would be wise."* —
  [[Though He Slay Me]]
- **Leviticus 19:15** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"You shall not be partial
  to the poor, nor show favoritism to the great; but you shall judge your neighbor in righteousness."*

## Analysis

**Proverbs 18 gives two rules for hearing a case.** The first is about the hearer: answer only after
hearing (13). The second is about the case itself: the first speaker *seems right* (*ṣaddîq*, the word
for *righteous*) *until another comes and questions him* (17). The verb for questioning is *ḥqr*,
*to search out*, the word for examining evidence. Between them stand the fool, who wants only to air
his opinion (2), and the wise, whose *ear … seeks knowledge* (15). Leviticus 19:15's *"judge your
neighbor in righteousness"*, without partiality to either party, is the legal form of the same rule,
and Proverbs 18:5 uses its idiom, *nśʾ pənê*, *lifting the face* (Hebrew from Sefaria).

**Elihu is introduced as the man who heard first.** He *"had waited to speak"* (Job 32:4), and he
says so twice more: *"I waited for your words, and I listened for your reasoning"* (32:11). He uses
Proverbs 18:17's verb for what the friends did: *ʿad-taḥqərûn millîn*, *"while you searched out what
to say"* (Job 32:11; Hebrew from Sefaria). His complaint is that their search produced no answer to
Job's words (32:12). He presents himself as the *other* of 18:17 who comes and questions both parties.

**The book of Job is a lawsuit run as 18:17 describes it.** Each friend's speech sounds right until
Job answers it; Job's own case, which he presses in legal terms, sounds right until God answers from
the whirlwind. No one in the book has the last word except the one who asks the questions. And the
friends' best moment came before anyone spoke: Job's *"Oh that you would be completely silent! Then
you would be wise"* (13:5) is the complaint of 18:13 made by the man being answered too soon.

**This does not make Elihu the wise man of 18:13.** The wiki's page on him records that he waited
and then spoke for six chapters, angrily, and that God's speeches do not mention him. Whether he
heard Job better than the friends did is disputed by readers, and this page does not settle it. The
thread is about the rule Proverbs states and the way the book of Job stages it, not a verdict on
Elihu.

## Significance

Proverbs 18 makes listening a moral and judicial duty, and Job shows a whole book of people
answering before they have heard. See [[Language and Speech]], [[Judgment]], [[Folly]],
[[Elihu the Buzite]], [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]], and
