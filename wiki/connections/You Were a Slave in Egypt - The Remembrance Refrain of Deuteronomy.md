# You Were a Slave in Egypt - The Remembrance Refrain of Deuteronomy

## Passages involved

- **Deuteronomy 5:15** — the Sabbath: *"You shall remember that you were a servant in the land of
  Egypt, and Yahweh your God brought you out of there by a mighty hand and by an outstretched arm.
  Therefore Yahweh your God commanded you to keep the Sabbath day."* —
  [[The Ten Commandments Recounted]]
- **Deuteronomy 15:15** — the released Hebrew servant: *"You shall remember that you were a slave in
  the land of Egypt, and Yahweh your God redeemed you. Therefore I command you this thing today."* —
  [[Release the Servant with Gifts]]
- **Deuteronomy 16:12** — the feast of weeks: *"You shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt.
  You shall observe and do these statutes."* — [[Passover, Weeks, and Booths]]
- **Deuteronomy 24:18** — justice for the foreigner and the fatherless, and the widow's clothing not
  taken in pledge: *"but you shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and Yahweh your God
  redeemed you there. Therefore I command you to do this thing."* *(not yet ingested)*
- **Deuteronomy 24:22** — the forgotten sheaf, the olive tree, and the vineyard left for the
  foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow: *"You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of
  Egypt. Therefore I command you to do this thing."* *(not yet ingested)*

## Analysis

**Five times Deuteronomy grounds a command in the same sentence.** All five open with *wəzākartā kî
ʿebed hāyîtā*, *and you shall remember that you were a slave*, followed by *bəʾereṣ miṣrayim*, *in
the land of Egypt* (5:15; 15:15; 24:22), or *bəmiṣrayim*, *in Egypt* (16:12; 24:18) (WLC, morphhb).
The WEB has *"servant"* at 5:15 and *"slave"* in the other four; the Hebrew word is *ʿebed* in all
five.

The second half varies:

| | what Yahweh did | the formula that follows | the command it grounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5:15** | *"brought you out of there by a mighty hand and by an outstretched arm"* (*wayyōṣiʾăkā*) | *ʿal-kēn ṣiwwəkā YHWH*, *"Therefore Yahweh your God commanded you"* | the Sabbath rest, *"that your male servant and your female servant may rest as well as you"* (5:14) |
| **15:15** | *"redeemed you"* (*wayyipdəkā*) | *ʿal-kēn ʾānōkî məṣawwəkā*, *"Therefore I command you"* | the Hebrew servant released and furnished |
| **16:12** | — | *"You shall observe and do these statutes"* | the feast of weeks, with servants, Levite, foreigner, fatherless, and widow rejoicing (16:11) |
| **24:18** | *"redeemed you there"* (*wayyipdəkā*) | *ʿal-kēn ʾānōkî məṣawwəkā* | justice for the foreigner and fatherless; no widow's clothing in pledge (24:17) |
| **24:22** | — | *ʿal-kēn ʾānōkî məṣawwəkā* | the gleanings left for the foreigner, fatherless, and widow (24:19–21) |

(WLC 5:15; 15:15; 16:12; 24:18, 22, morphhb.)

**15:15 and 24:18 are nearly the same sentence**, with *pādâ*, *redeem*, and Moses' own *"I command
you"*. 5:15 is the only one with *brought out* and the only one that says *Yahweh* commanded; it is
the one inside the Decalogue, which Yahweh spoke (5:22). Whether the variation is deliberate cannot be
shown from the text.

**What the five commands share is who benefits.** Every one protects someone in a dependent
position: servants who rest, a Hebrew servant who goes free with goods, servants and the poor who
feast, the foreigner, orphan, and widow who get justice and gleanings. The memory is of having been
at the bottom; the command is to treat those now at the bottom differently than Egypt did.

The refrain is related to, but distinct from, the motive *"you were foreigners in the land of
Egypt"* (*gērîm*), which Deuteronomy 10:19 uses for loving the foreigner. That motive goes back to
Exodus 22:21 and 23:9. *Remember you were a slave* is Deuteronomy's own; neither Exodus nor
Leviticus has the clause. Leviticus reaches the same commands by another route — *"they are my
servants"* (25:42, 55). A search of the stored WEB for *"you were a slave"* and *"you were a
servant"*, and of WLC for *ʿebed hāyîtā*, finds the clause only in these five verses of
Genesis–Deuteronomy (morphhb). The books after Deuteronomy were not searched.

## Significance

**Deuteronomy makes Israel's history into a reason for its law.** Exodus 20:11 grounds the Sabbath
in creation; Deuteronomy 5:15 grounds it in the exodus. Exodus 21:2 releases the servant without
saying why; Deuteronomy 15:15 says why. The refrain asks the hearer to remember from below, as the
slave, and then to act as the master Egypt was not.

**It is not an argument against owning servants.** 15:15 grounds a release with gifts, not the end of
service. In the next two verses the servant may still stay *"forever"*. See
[[The Hebrew Servant Goes Free - Exodus 21, Deuteronomy 15, and Jeremiah 34]] and
[[Slavery and Servitude]].
