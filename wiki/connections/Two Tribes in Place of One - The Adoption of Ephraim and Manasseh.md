# Two Tribes in Place of One - The Adoption of Ephraim and Manasseh

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 48:5–6** — *"Now your two sons … are mine; Ephraim and Manasseh, even as Reuben and
  Simeon, will be mine"* — [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]]
- **Genesis 35:22** — [[Reuben]] and [[Bilhah]] — [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]]
- **Genesis 49:3–7** — Reuben *"shall not excel"*; [[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] *divided and scattered*
- **1 Chronicles 5:1–2** — ***"his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph"***, with the reason
  stated
- **Numbers 1:10, 32–35; 2:18–21** — the census and the camp: **Ephraim and Manasseh counted as
  separate tribes, [[Levi]] not numbered**
- **Numbers 26:28–37** — the second census, *"the sons of Joseph after their families: Manasseh and
  Ephraim"*
- **Joshua 14:3–4** — ***"the children of Joseph were two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim"***, and *"they
  gave no portion to the Levites"*
- **Joshua 16:1–17:18** — the two allotments, Manasseh on both banks of the Jordan
- **Deuteronomy 33:13–17** — Moses' blessing on Joseph: *"the ten thousands of Ephraim … the thousands
  of Manasseh"*
- **Ezekiel 47:13** — *"Joseph shall have two portions"*, in the eschatological division
- **Revelation 7:5–8** — the sealed twelve: **[[Joseph]] and Manasseh listed, Ephraim and [[Dan (person)]]
  absent**

## Analysis

**One sentence in Genesis 48 makes every later list of the tribes an arithmetic problem, and Scripture
solves it the same way every time except the last.**

### The problem

**Jacob has twelve sons. Israel has twelve tribes. These are not the same twelve**, because 48:5 turns
[[Joseph]] into two and 48:6 stops it there.

| the list | how it gets to twelve |
|---|---|
| **Genesis 35:22–26** — the sons | twelve sons, Joseph one of them, Levi one of them |
| **Numbers 1–2** — the census and camp | **Joseph split into Ephraim and Manasseh; [[Levi]] removed** |
| **Numbers 26** — the second census | the same |
| **Joshua 13–19** — the allotments | the same; *"they gave no portion to the Levites"* (14:4) |
| **Ezekiel 48** | the same, and *"Joseph shall have two portions"* stated outright (47:13) |
| **Revelation 7:5–8** | **Joseph and Manasseh named; Ephraim and Dan omitted** |

**Levi comes out because Levi is given no land** (Numbers 18:20; Joshua 13:14, 33) — **so wherever the
twelve are counted as territories, the priestly tribe cannot be one of them, and Genesis 48:5 has
already supplied the replacement.** **The adoption and the Levitical landlessness are two halves of one
arithmetic**, and neither book mentions the other. See
[[The Twelve Sons and the Lists That Reorder Them]] and
[[The Priests Who Kept Their Land and the Levites Who Got None]].

### The one text that explains it

> **1** The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel (for he was the firstborn; but, because he defiled
> his father's couch, his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph the son of Israel; and the
> genealogy is not to be listed according to the birthright. **2** For Judah prevailed above his
> brothers, and of him came the prince; but the birthright was Joseph's) — 1 Chronicles 5:1–2

**This is the only verse in Scripture that says what Genesis 48 was.** **It reads the adoption as a
transfer of the *bekōrâ*, the firstborn's double portion**, from [[Reuben]] to [[Joseph]], **on the
ground of 35:22.** **And it splits the firstborn's two privileges between two men**: the double
inheritance to Joseph, the *nāgîd* — the prince, the ruling line — **to [[Judah]].**

**Genesis itself never says any of this.** **48:5 gives a reason for nothing**; it simply names
[[Reuben]] and [[Simeon]] as the measure. **Chronicles supplies the motive, and the wiki keeps the two
apart**: Genesis records an adoption, Chronicles interprets it as a penalty.

### Why Reuben and Simeon

**The comparison in 48:5 names the first and second sons of [[Leah]]**, so the rank conferred on
[[Ephraim]] and [[Manasseh]] is first and second. **Both men named lose that rank one chapter later**:

- **[[Reuben]]** — *"Boiling over like water, you shall not excel; because you went up to your father's
  bed"* (49:4), **for 35:22**. See
  [[Lying with the Father's Concubine - A Claim on the Succession]]
- **[[Simeon]]** — *"Cursed be their anger … I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel"*
  (49:7), **for 34:25–30**. See
  [[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]]

**Jacob measures the new sons against exactly the two positions he is about to vacate**, in the chapter
before he vacates them. **Whether that is deliberate the text does not say**, and the wiki notes the
sequence without asserting the design.

### What the split actually produced

**The two half-tribes of Joseph became the largest bloc in Israel.** **Manasseh alone is settled on both
sides of the Jordan** — Gilead and Bashan in the east (Numbers 32:33; Joshua 13:29–31), the hill
country in the west (Joshua 17:7–10). **Ephraim holds Shiloh, where the tabernacle stood, and Bethel on
its border**; **Joshua is an Ephraimite** (Numbers 13:8, 16), **Gideon a Manassite** (Judges 6:15),
**Samuel raised at Shiloh, Jeroboam an Ephraimite** (1 Kings 11:26). **When the kingdom divides,
*Ephraim* becomes the prophets' ordinary name for the whole north** (Hosea 4:17; 11:8; Isaiah 7:2, 9;
Jeremiah 31:9, where God calls Ephraim *"my firstborn"* — **48:14's verdict repeated by God eight
centuries later**).

**And 48:19's *"his younger brother will be greater than he"* is the thing that came true**: Ephraim
is the leading tribe of the north and Manasseh, though larger in land, is not.

### Revelation 7

**The last tribal list in Scripture breaks the pattern in both directions.** It **restores *Joseph* as
a name** and **keeps *Manasseh*** — so Joseph is counted twice under two names — **omits Ephraim**, and
**omits [[Dan (person)]]**, while **restoring [[Levi]]**. **The result is twelve, and the wiki notes
only that it is twelve**: Revelation gives no reason, and every explanation on offer (Dan's idolatry at
Judges 18; a scribal confusion of *Dan* and *Manasseh* in Greek) is imported.

## Significance

**Genesis 48:5 is the only verse in the Bible that changes the membership of Israel by declaration.**
Every other tribe is in the list because a woman bore a son. **Ephraim and Manasseh are in it because a
dying man said *they are mine* about two boys born in [[Egypt]] to an Egyptian priest's daughter.** See
[[Adoption]] and [[The Nations]].

**And it is the mechanism by which the firstborn's double portion reaches a younger son of a younger
wife.** [[Rachel]] bore two sons where [[Leah]] bore six; **Joseph receives two shares of the land
through his own two sons**, and Genesis 48:7 — the abrupt sentence about Rachel's grave — **sits in the
middle of the adoption for that reason.** See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].
