# The Bread of God - Leviticus 3 and 21, Numbers 28, Psalm 50, and Malachi 1

## Passages involved

- **Leviticus 3:11, 16** — the fat of the peace offering: *"it is the food of the offering made by
  fire to Yahweh"*; *"it is the food of the offering made by fire, for a pleasant aroma"* —
  [[The Peace Offering]].
- **Leviticus 21:6, 8, 17** — the priests *"offer the offerings of Yahweh made by fire, the bread of
  their God"*; *"he offers the bread of your God"*; a priest with a defect may not *"approach to offer
  the bread of his God"* — [[The Holiness of the Priests]].
- **Numbers 28:2** — Yahweh's own voice: *"my offering, my food for my offerings made by fire"* —
  [[Daily, Sabbath, and New Moon Offerings]]; and 28:24, *"the food of the offering made by fire"*
  for each day of Unleavened Bread (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Psalm 50:12–13** — *"If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world is mine, and all that
  is in it. Will I eat the meat of bulls, or drink the blood of goats?"* (not yet ingested).
- **Malachi 1:7** — *"You offer polluted bread on my altar"*, and the people's reply that
  *"Yahweh’s table is contemptible"* (not yet ingested).

(Leviticus and Numbers from stored WEB; Psalm 50 and Malachi 1 verified at bible-api.com,
`translation=web`, 2026-11-05.)

## Analysis

**One word for the altar's portion.** In Leviticus 3:11 the Hebrew is *leḥem ʾiššeh laYHWH*, and in
21:6 *leḥem ʾĕlōhêhem*, *the bread of their God* (Miqra according to the Masorah, via Sefaria).
Numbers 28:2 has *laḥmî*, *my bread* or *my food*, on Yahweh's own lips (same source). WEB renders the
same noun *food* in Leviticus 3 and Numbers 28 and *bread* in Leviticus 21; the English variation is a
translator's choice, not a change in the Hebrew noun. What is burned on the altar is called God's food,
and in Numbers 28 he calls it that himself.

**The texts do not explain the word.** Leviticus attaches the phrase to the fat that is burned, and to
the priests' holiness: those who handle God's bread must be without defect. Numbers attaches it to the
calendar: the bread is to be presented *in its appointed time*. None of these verses says that God
needs to eat, and none says that he does not.

**Psalm 50 says it.** In Psalm 50:12–13 God speaks against the idea directly: he would not report
hunger to Israel, because the world is his, and he asks whether he eats bulls' flesh or drinks goats'
blood. The psalm is not quoting Leviticus or Numbers, and the wiki does not claim it is correcting them.
Read together, the Torah keeps the vocabulary of a meal while the psalm denies the appetite.

**Malachi turns the word against the priests.** *Polluted bread on my altar* (1:7) uses the meal
vocabulary to charge that the offerings are unworthy — the very concern of Leviticus 21–22 that no
defective priest or animal approach. The table is God's, and its contempt is an insult to him.

## Significance

The phrase belongs with [[A Pleasing Aroma]]: both describe accepted offerings in the language of a
host's table, and both are later held up against careless or unfaithful worship. The wiki records the
Torah's own wording and the psalm's denial side by side rather than harmonizing them. See
[[Sacrifice and Offering]] and [[Priesthood]].
