# He Who Gathered Much - Exodus 16.18 and 2 Corinthians 8.15

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 16:16–18** — *"Gather of it everyone according to his eating; an omer a head … The children of
  Israel did so, and gathered some more, some less. When they measured it with an omer, he who gathered much
  had nothing over, and he who gathered little had no lack."* — [[Manna and Quail]]
- **2 Corinthians 8:13–15** — *"For this is not that others may be eased and you distressed, but for equality.
  Your abundance at this present time supplies their lack, that their abundance also may become a supply for
  your lack; that there may be equality. As it is written, 'He who gathered much had nothing left over, and he
  who gathered little had no lack.'"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*)

## Analysis

**Paul quotes the manna rule as Scripture's pattern for a collection of money.** The context is the
offering he is gathering from the Gentile churches for the believers in Jerusalem (2 Corinthians 8–9; by
reference, *not yet ingested*). **The quotation is nearly the WEB's own wording of Exodus 16:18** — *"had
nothing over"* in Exodus, *"had nothing left over"* in Corinthians (WEB of both compared; the Greek of 2
Corinthians and of the Greek Exodus is not checked in this pass).

**What Exodus leaves open, Paul's use settles for his purpose.** Exodus does not say how the equal omer came
about. **One reading takes it as a miracle in the measuring** — every portion came out to an omer whatever had
been gathered; **another as the households sharing out their gathering**, so that each tent ended with what it
needed (readings reported, not decided). **Paul's *"Your abundance … supplies their lack"* reads the verse as
the second** — or at least applies it that way: the equality is achieved by those with more giving to those
with less.

**The vocabulary of the Hebrew is about excess and lack, not about amount.** *Lōʾ heʿdîp*, *had no
surplus*, from *ʿādap*; *lōʾ heḥsîr*, *had no lack*, from *ḥāsēr* (WLC). ***Ḥāsēr* is the verb of *"You have
lacked nothing"* (Deuteronomy 2:7) and *"Yahweh is my shepherd: I shall lack nothing"* (Psalm 23:1)** (WEB; WLC;
*not yet ingested*). **Paul's *"lack"* in 8:14–15 keeps the Exodus pair** in English; whether it does in Greek
is not checked here.

**The rule in Exodus is attached to a test** — *"that I may test them, whether they will walk in my law, or
not"* (16:4) — **and the test is failed by hoarding**: what was kept overnight *"bred worms, and became foul"*
(16:20). **Paul's appeal is the positive form of the same rule**: abundance held back is the thing the manna
would not allow.

## Significance

**The manna is the Torah's first economy, and it is an economy of enough.** It contrasts with the last
economy of Genesis, Joseph's, in which *"Joseph gathered up all the money … and brought the money into
Pharaoh's house"* (Genesis 47:14) — the same verb, *lāqaṭ*, gathering everything to the centre (WLC). See
[[Joseph and the Famine]] and [[Famine and Plenty]]. **Paul's quotation carries the wilderness rule into a
church collection**, and later Christian teaching on sharing goods appeals to it (reported, not checked in
this pass).
