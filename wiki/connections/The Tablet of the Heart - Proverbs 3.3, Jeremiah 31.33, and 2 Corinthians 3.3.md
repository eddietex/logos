# The Tablet of the Heart - Proverbs 3.3, Jeremiah 31.33, and 2 Corinthians 3.3

- **Exodus 24:12; 31:18** — *"the stone tablets with the law and the commands that I have
  written"* (24:12, *luḥōṯ hāʾeḇen wəhattôrâ wəhammiṣwâ*); *"the two tablets of the testimony, stone
  tablets, written with God's finger"* (31:18) — [[The Blood of the Covenant]],
  [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]], [[The Replacement Tablets - Exodus 34 and Deuteronomy 10]]
- **Deuteronomy 6:6, 8** — *"These words, which I command you today, shall be on your heart … You
  shall bind them for a sign on your hand"* (WEB, verified; not yet ingested)
- **Proverbs 3:1, 3** — *"don't forget my teaching; but let your heart keep my commandments"* (*tôrāṯî
  … ûmiṣwōṯay*); *"Bind them around your neck. Write them on the tablet of your heart."* —
  [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]]
- **Proverbs 6:21** — *"Bind them continually on your heart. Tie them around your neck."* The
  father's commandment and the mother's teaching bound on the heart itself (*qošrēm ʿal-libbəḵā*) —
  [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]
- **Proverbs 7:3** — *"Bind them on your fingers. Write them on the tablet of your heart."* The same

- **Jeremiah 17:1** — *"The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron, and with the point of a

- **Jeremiah 31:33** — *"I will put my law in their inward parts, and I will write it in their heart"*

- **Ezekiel 36:26** — *"I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you a heart
  of flesh"* (WEB, verified; not yet ingested)

## Analysis

**One word, *lûaḥ*, runs from Sinai to the heart.** The tablets Moses received are *luḥōṯ*, and the
*tablet of the heart* in Proverbs and Jeremiah is *lûaḥ lēḇ*. Exodus 24:12 calls what is written on
the stone tablets *hattôrâ wəhammiṣwâ*, *the law and the command*; Proverbs 3:1 asks the son to keep
*tôrāṯî … ûmiṣwōṯay*, *my teaching … my commandments*, and two verses later to write them on the
tablet of his heart. The father's teaching is not the Sinai law, and Proverbs never cites it; but its
words for what it teaches, and its picture of where to put it, are Sinai's. Deuteronomy 6:6–8 had
already moved the words *"on your heart"* and bound them *"for a sign on your hand"*; Proverbs 3:3 and
7:3 are its wisdom form — neck and fingers for hand, and the heart as a tablet.

**Four writers on the heart.** The motif has a direction, and the writer changes.

1. **The son writes** (Proverbs 3:3; 7:3). The command is addressed to him: *kāṯḇēm*, *write them*.
   The father assumes the heart can be written on and that the son can do it.
2. **Sin has written already** (Jeremiah 17:1). Judah's sin is *"engraved on the tablet of their
   heart"*, with an iron pen and a diamond point — the same phrase, *ʿal-lûaḥ libbām*, and the
   opposite content. The heart is a tablet, and what is cut into it is not the teaching.
3. **God will write** (Jeremiah 31:33). In the new covenant Yahweh says *"I will write it in their
   heart"*, and *it* is *tôrāṯî*, *my law* — the same form as the father's *"my teaching"* at
   Proverbs 3:1. What the son was told to do, God promises to do. Ezekiel 36:26 gives the same promise
   by replacing the material: a heart of flesh for a heart of stone.
4. **The Spirit has written** (2 Corinthians 3:3). Paul calls the Corinthian church a letter written
   *"not in tablets of stone, but in tablets that are hearts of flesh"*, combining Sinai's stone, the
   tablet of the heart, and Ezekiel's flesh in one sentence. That Paul is alluding to Jeremiah and
   Ezekiel here is the usual reading, and the vocabulary supports it; he does not cite either.

**Development, not contradiction.** Proverbs does not say the son cannot write on his own heart, and
Jeremiah does not say the father's command was wrong. But Jeremiah 17:1 and 31:33, read beside
Proverbs 3:3, imply that the command is not carried out by the son alone: the tablet already bears
an engraving, and the new covenant answer is that God writes. The wiki records the sequence and does
not harmonize it further.

**What *"them"* refers to in Proverbs 3:3 is open.** The nearest antecedent is *"kindness and truth"*
(*ḥeseḏ weʾĕmeṯ*) in the same verse; the farther is the teaching and commandments of 3:1. Proverbs 7:1–3
makes the commandments the antecedent of the same line. If 3:3 means kindness and truth, then the
two qualities Yahweh proclaims of himself at Sinai (Exodus 34:6, *rav-ḥeseḏ weʾĕmeṯ*) are what the son
writes on his heart; if it means the commandments, the parallel with the stone tablets is closer
still.

## Significance

- It gives [[Law and Obedience]] its interior line: from stone tablets, to words *"on your heart"*, to
  a heart written on by God.
- It joins Proverbs' wisdom instruction to the prophets' new covenant through a shared object rather
  than a quotation — a case where a motif, not a citation, carries the connection.
- It sits beside [[Circumcision of the Heart]], the other Old Testament image of a heart that must be
  changed, and beside [[The Shining Face and the Veil - Exodus 34 and 2 Corinthians 3]], where the
  same chapter of 2 Corinthians continues the comparison of ministries.
- Proverbs 4:21–23 keeps the father's words *"in the center of your heart"* and makes that heart the
  wellspring of life — the tablet as source as well as store. See [[Keep Your Heart]] and
  [[Out of the Heart - Proverbs 4.23, Matthew 15, and Luke 6]].
