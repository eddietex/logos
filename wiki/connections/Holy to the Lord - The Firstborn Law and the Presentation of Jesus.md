## Analysis

### The ingested purification law — Leviticus 12

[[Purification After Childbirth]] prescribes the same offerings for a son or daughter,
not only for a firstborn. Luke 2:24 names the two birds corresponding to the provision
for a mother unable to afford a lamb (12:8). This supports the inference of limited means
without establishing a complete account of the household’s finances. The purification
offering and presentation of the firstborn remain distinct legal matters.

Leviticus 12 checked in stored WEB; Luke 2:21–24 rechecked at bible-api.com
(`translation=web`) on 2026-10-29. No new Greek or Hebrew assertion is made.


**Luke's citation is not a quotation of one verse but a summary of the firstborn law in its own words.**
*"Every male who opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord"* — *pan arsen dianoigon mētran hagion tō
kyriō klēthēsetai* (SBLGNT) — gathers three things Exodus 13 says in three places: ***"whatever opens the
womb"*** (*peṭer reḥem*, 13:2), ***"The males"*** (13:12), and ***"Sanctify to me"*** (13:2) turned into *holy
to the Lord*. **No single verse of Exodus 13 reads as Luke does**; the closest wording is a matter for the
Septuagint, which was not checked in this pass. The wiki records the citation as Luke's paraphrase of the
law, not as a verbatim quotation of one verse.

**Luke calls the source *"the law of the Lord"* — *nomos kyriou* — three times in the chapter (2:23, 24,
39; SBLGNT), after calling it *"the law of Moses"* once (2:22).** The Hebrew phrase *tôrat YHWH*, *law of
Yahweh*, first appears in Scripture inside this very pericope: *"that Yahweh's law may be in your mouth"*
(Exodus 13:9; WLC, the only occurrence in Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Whether Luke had 13:9 in mind cannot be
shown**; the phrase recurs in later Hebrew Scripture (not counted in this pass). The match is recorded, not built on.

**Two laws are folded together in Luke 2:22–24.** *"The days of their purification"* and the offering of
*"a pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons"* belong to the mother's purification (Leviticus 12:6–8; [[Purification After Childbirth]]). **The pair of birds is the offering of a mother who *"cannot afford a lamb"*** (12:8) — Luke
does not say so, but the law he cites does. *"To present him to the Lord"* belongs to the firstborn. **Luke does not mention the five-shekel
redemption of Numbers 18:16**; the text does not say whether it was paid. Readers have taken the silence
either as Luke assuming it, or as Luke presenting the child as *given* to God rather than bought back —
Samuel at Shiloh (1 Samuel 1–2) is often adduced. **The wiki records both and adopts neither.**

**What the connection does show is the ground of the law.** Exodus 13:15 gives the reason in the
father's mouth: *"Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt … Therefore I sacrifice to Yahweh
all that opens the womb, being males; but all the firstborn of my sons I redeem."* **Every firstborn son
of Israel was claimed because of the night of 12:29, and bought back because God does not want sons
sacrificed.** Luke places Jesus inside that law as an infant; Matthew, separately, applies *"Out of Egypt I
called my son"* to him (Matthew 2:15) — see
[[Israel My Firstborn - Exodus 4.22, Hosea 11.1, and Matthew 2.15]].

### The renewal in its narrative setting — Exodus 34

[[The Covenant Renewed]] repeats the ownership claim and explicitly requires firstborn sons to be redeemed
(34:19–20). The donkey alone has the alternative of a broken neck. The unit supplies neither a
price for a son nor a narrated presentation rite. It strengthens the Exodus basis of the later
comparison while leaving Luke's account and its silences distinct.

## Significance

- **It is the New Testament's one explicit citation of the firstborn law** among the passages the wiki has
  reached so far; the later texts are tracked on [[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]].
- **It shows the law of Exodus 13 still practised in the form it took in the land** — *"when Yahweh shall
  bring you into the land"* (13:5, 11) — at the Temple in Jerusalem, a place Exodus never names.
- **It keeps open a question the wiki should not close early**: whether Luke presents a firstborn redeemed
  or a firstborn given. When Luke and 1 Samuel are ingested, this page is where that is decided or left
  open.
