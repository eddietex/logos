# Fallen Down Dead - Eglon's Upper Room and Jael's Tent

## Passages involved

- [[Ehud and Eglon]] — Judges 3:16–26
- [[Jael and Sisera]] — Judges 4:17–22

## Analysis

The second and third deliverances of Judges each turn on one killing, and the two are told the same
way. In each, an enemy of Israel is alone with someone he trusts, is killed by a hand weapon driven
into his body, and is found by people coming in from outside, *behold*, dead on the ground.

| | Ehud and Eglon (3:16–26) | Jael and Sisera (4:17–22) |
|---|---|---|
| The victim | Eglon, king of Moab | Sisera, Jabin's captain |
| The place | *the cool upper room*, alone (20) | *the tent*, alone (18) |
| The approach | *I have a message from God to you* (20) | *Turn in, my lord … don't be afraid* (18) |
| The weapon | a sword *from his right thigh* (21) | *a tent peg … a hammer in her hand* (21) |
| The verb | *wayyitqāʿehā*, *thrust it* (21, WLC) | *wattitqaʿ*, *struck* (21, WLC) |
| What is left in | *the handle also went in after the blade* (22) | *the tent peg was in his temples* (22) |
| The finders | his servants, who unlock the doors (25) | Barak, whom Jael brings in (22) |
| The discovery | *wəhinnēh ʾădōnêhem nōpēl ʾarṣâ mēt*, *behold, their lord had fallen down dead on the floor* (25) | *wəhinnēh sîsərāʾ nōpēl mēt*, *behold, Sisera lay dead* (22) |
| After | *Moab was subdued*, *wattikkānaʿ* (30) | *God subdued Jabin*, *wayyaknaʿ* (23) |

**The verb.** *Tāqaʿ* covers both thrusts. In the WLC files for Genesis, Exodus, Numbers, Joshua, and
Judges, it is otherwise used mostly of blowing trumpets (Numbers 10; Joshua 6; Judges 3:27; 6:34; 7:18–22)
and of pitching a tent (Genesis 31:25). Judges 16:14, Delilah fastening Samson's hair with the pin
(*wattitqaʿ bayyātēd*), is the third use for driving something home in Judges, and has the same noun
as 4:21.

**The discovery clause.** *Wəhinnēh … nōpēl … mēt*: *behold*, the participle *fallen*, and *dead*, in
both verses (WLC). WEB renders the same participle *had fallen down* in 3:25 and *lay* in 4:22. Eglon's
servants open the door with a key and find their lord; Barak is invited in by the woman who did it.

**The differences.** Ehud is *raised up* as a *savior* (3:15) and is an Israelite; Jael is not called
either, and she is a Kenite. Ehud deceives with words that are true in a sense (*a message from God*),
and the narrator lets them stand. Jael's words promise safety (*don't be afraid*), and the narrator
again lets them stand. Ehud's killing opens his story and leads to a battle; Jael's ends a battle
already won. Eglon is a king; Sisera is a king's general who has lost his army.

**What the pattern does.** Judges tells its early deliverances through hidden, single-handed killings by
unlikely people: a left-handed man, then a woman in a tent, both of whom go in alone. The book does not
comment on the method in either case; it reports the result, the enemy *subdued* (3:30; 4:23).

## Significance

Reading the two scenes together shows that 4:17–22 is not an appendix to the battle of 4:14–16 but a
second deliverance story of the same shape as Ehud's. It also shows the book telling both with the same
reserve: neither killer is praised or blamed by the narrator. Later in Judges the pattern reverses —
a woman, a pin, and a sleeping man in 16:14 end in Israel's champion captured, not Israel's enemy
killed.
