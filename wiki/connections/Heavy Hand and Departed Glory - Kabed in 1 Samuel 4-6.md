# Heavy Hand and Departed Glory - Kabed in 1 Samuel 4-6

## Analysis

The root *k-b-d* carries three senses that English separates: *be heavy*, *honor* (give weight to), and
*glory* (weight, splendor). Morphhb lemmatizes them as 3513 (the verb), 3515 (the adjective *heavy*), and
3519 (*kāḇôḏ*, *glory*). In 1 Samuel 2–6 the root recurs at every turn of the ark story:

| Verse | Hebrew (WLC) | WEB | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2:29 | *wattəḵabbēḏ* | *honor your sons above me* | Eli |
| 2:30 | *məḵabbəḏay ʾăḵabbēḏ* | *those who honor me I will honor* | Yahweh |
| 4:18 | *wəḵāḇēḏ* | *and heavy* | Eli, at his death |
| 4:21–22 | *ʾî-ḵāḇôḏ*; *gālâ ḵāḇôḏ* | *Ichabod*; *The glory has departed* | Phinehas' wife |
| 5:6 | *wattiḵbaḏ yaḏ-YHWH* | *Yahweh's hand was heavy* | Ashdod |
| 5:11 | *kāḇḏâ məʾōḏ yaḏ hāʾĕlōhîm* | *The hand of God was very heavy* | Ekron |
| 6:5 | *kāḇôḏ* | *give glory to the God of Israel* | the Philistine priests |
| 6:6 | *təḵabbəḏû … kibbəḏû* | *harden your hearts, as … Pharaoh hardened* | the Philistine priests |

Read in sequence, the root traces a single movement. Eli gives weight to his sons rather than to Yahweh; he
dies *heavy*; the *glory* leaves Israel; the glory is next met as a *heavy* hand on the Philistines; and
their own priests tell them to *give glory* rather than make their hearts *heavy* as Pharaoh did. The last
step ties the chain to Exodus, where *kāḇēḏ* is one of the verbs for Pharaoh's hardened heart (Exodus 7:14;
8:15, 32 [WLC 8:11, 28]; 9:7, 34; 10:1; WLC co-occurrence search of *k-b-d* with *lēḇ*).

The pairing of *heavy hand* with *glory* is the narrator's vocabulary, not a pun the narrator points out.
The commentary tradition widely notes the wordplay; the wiki records where the root falls and leaves the
question of intent open.

Psalm 78 tells the same events with different words: Yahweh *delivered his strength into captivity, his
glory into the adversary's hand* (78:61, WEB checked at bible-api.com), where *glory* is *tip̄ʾartô*, not
*kāḇôḏ*, and *hand* is *yaḏ* (WLC). The psalm keeps the pairing of *glory* and *hand*, without the root.

Outside these chapters, the phrase *your hand was heavy on me* (Psalm 32:4, WEB checked at bible-api.com)
uses the same verb with *yād* (WLC); in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings and Psalms the verb
*kāḇēḏ* and the noun *yād* stand in one verse only at Judges 1:35, 1 Samuel 5:6 and 5:11, and Psalm 32:4
(co-occurrence search). Exodus 9:3's *very grievous pestilence*, *deḇer kāḇēḏ məʾōḏ*, uses the adjective
in a verse that opens *Yahweh's hand is on your livestock* (WLC; stored WEB), the nearest Exodus parallel
to *the hand of God was very heavy* (1 Samuel 5:11).

### The light hand — 1 Samuel 6:5, now ingested

The priests' counsel adds the antonym. *Give glory*, *kāḇôḏ*, to the God of Israel, *perhaps he will
lighten his hand from you*, *ʾûlay yāqēl ʾeṯ-yāḏô mēʿălêḵem* (6:5, WLC). The WEB's *release* renders
*qālal*, *be light, slight* (lemma 7043), the standard opposite of *kāḇēḏ*. The same pair stands in Eli's
sentence: *those who honor me I will honor, and those who despise me will be cursed* (2:30), where
*despise* is *yēqallû*, from *qālal* (WLC). The house of Eli made Yahweh *light* and was made light; the
Philistines are told to give him *weight* so that his hand may be made *light*. The verse in which the priests
cite Pharaoh's *heavy* heart (6:6) also uses *hiṯʿallēl*, *worked wonderfully*, the verb of Exodus 10:2
(WLC). See [[The Ark Returns to Israel]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 2:30; 6:5–6; Exodus 10:2. Hebrew WLC
(OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

## Significance

The chain lets the ark narrative say, without stating it, that what Israel lost was not lost to Yahweh. The
*glory* that went into exile works as a *heavy hand* in the enemy's cities, and the enemy's priests draw
the lesson Pharaoh would not. See [[The Glory of God]], [[Hardening of the Heart]], and [[Judgment]].
