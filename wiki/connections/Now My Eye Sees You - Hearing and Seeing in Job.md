# Now My Eye Sees You - Hearing and Seeing in Job

## Passages involved

- **Job 42:5** — *"I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you."* —
  [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]]
- **Job 4:12, 16** — Eliphaz's night-vision: *"My ear received a whisper of it … It stood still, but I
  couldn't discern its appearance. A form was before my eyes."* — [[Eliphaz's First Speech]]
- **Job 9:11** — *"Behold, he goes by me, and I don't see him. He passes on also, but I don't perceive
  him."* — [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]]
- **Job 13:1** — *"Behold, my eye has seen all this. My ear has heard and understood it."* —
  [[Though He Slay Me]]
- **Job 19:26–27** — *"After my skin is destroyed, then in my flesh shall I see God, whom I, even I, shall
  see on my side. My eyes shall see, and not as a stranger."* — [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]]
- **Job 23:8–9** — *"If I go east, he is not there; if west, I can't find him; he works to the north, but
  I can't see him. He turns south, but I can't catch a glimpse of him."* —
  [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]
- **Job 26:14** — *"How small a whisper do we hear of him!"* — [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]
- **Job 28:22** — *"Destruction and Death say, 'We have heard a rumor of it with our ears.'"* —
  [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]]
- **Job 29:11** — *"For when the ear heard me, then it blessed me; and when the eye saw me, it commended
  me"* — [[The Months of Old]]
- **Isaiah 6:5** — *"Woe is me! For I am undone … for my eyes have seen the King, Yahweh of Armies!"*
- **Matthew 5:8** — *"Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God."*
- **James 5:11** — *"You have heard of the perseverance of Job, and have seen the Lord in the outcome, and
  how the Lord is full of compassion and mercy."*
- **1 John 3:2** — *"we know that, when he is revealed, we will be like him; for we will see him just as
  he is."*

## Analysis

**Job's last verse but one turns on a pair the whole book has been using: the ear and the eye.** *"I had
heard of you by the hearing of the ear (ləšēmaʿ-ʾōzen), but now my eye (ʿênî) sees you"* (42:5). The
contrast is not between ignorance and knowledge — Job knew a great deal *about* God, and said much of it
well — but between knowledge by report and knowledge by encounter.

**The ear is the dialogue's organ.** Everyone in it hears about God and no one sees him. Eliphaz's one
claim to revelation is a whisper received by the ear and a form he *"couldn't discern"* (4:12–16). Job,
outbidding Bildad's hymn, says that all of creation is *"the outskirts of his ways"* and *"How small a
whisper do we hear of him!"* (26:14). The hymn of chapter 28 gives Destruction and Death only a *"rumor …
with our ears"* of wisdom (28:22). Even Job's memory of his old life is a pair of ear and eye, but turned
toward himself: *"when the ear heard me … when the eye saw me"* (29:11) — the city looking at Job, not
Job at God.

**And the eye is what Job lacks.** *"He goes by me, and I don't see him"* (9:11). *"He works to the north,
but I can't see him"* (23:9). The friends' tradition is hearsay, and his own experience gives him a God
who is present as an assailant and absent as a face.

**Against that stands a single hope, at 19:26–27** — *"in my flesh shall I see God … My eyes shall
see"*. It is the one place in the dialogue where Job claims the eye for himself, and he places it beyond
the destruction of his skin. **At 42:5 the hope is met, sooner and differently than he said.** Not after
his skin is destroyed but while he is still diseased; not by a redeemer standing on the dust but out of a
storm; and with the same organ and the same verb, *ʿayin* and *rāʾâ*.

**What Job saw is not told.** The theophany in chapters 38–41 is a voice out of a whirlwind; there is no
description of any form. *Now my eye sees you* may mean the storm itself, or it may mean what *seeing*
often means in Hebrew, knowing directly. The wiki leaves both open. Either way, what has changed is not
information: God told Job nothing about the wager or the reason for his suffering. **What changed is that
God spoke to him, and that counts, for Job, as seeing.**

**The prophetic parallel is Isaiah 6.** Isaiah *sees* the King and says at once *"Woe is me! For I am
undone"* (6:5); Job sees God and says *"Therefore I abhor myself"* (42:6). In both, seeing is followed
immediately by a word of self-abasement, and in both the speaker then goes on to speak for God or to others
— Isaiah sent (6:8), Job told to pray for his friends (42:8). The pattern is recorded; neither text refers
to the other.

**The New Testament picks the pair up twice.** James 5:11 uses *heard* and *seen* of the reader of Job:
*"You have heard of the perseverance of Job, and have seen the Lord in the outcome."* Whether James is
deliberately echoing Job 42:5 cannot be established from the wording in English, and the wiki does not
claim it; but the sentence does what Job 42:5 does — hearing about a man, then seeing the Lord. Matthew 5:8
and 1 John 3:2 make seeing God the promise at the end; Job 42:5 is one of the few places in the Old
Testament where a man says it has already happened.

## Significance

- **It is the resolution of the theme the dialogue keeps open.** The friends' failure is that they speak
  about God from hearsay; Job's complaint is that he cannot see God; the book ends with Job seeing. See
  [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **It is the fulfilment of 19:26–27, in a form Job did not expect.** See
  [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]].
- **It explains why the whirlwind's content does not need to answer Job's question.** The speeches answer
  the request for a hearing, not the request for a reason, and Job's reply treats the hearing as enough.
  See [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]].
- **It connects Job to the Bible's line of people who see God and live** — and to the New Testament
  promise that the pure in heart will see him. See [[Walking with God]].
- **The book's last use of the verb is a blessing.** After *"now my eye sees you"* (42:5), the epilogue's *rāʾâ*
  is *"saw his sons, and his sons' sons, to four generations"* (42:16) — the sight Job had said the dead man is
  denied (14:21) and the wicked enjoy (21:8). See [[The Restoration of Job]].
