## Analysis

**The court's sentence in a private mouth.** Leviticus 24:19 states the law of retaliation in the
passive: *kaʾăšer ʿāśāh kēn yēʿāśeh lô*, *as he has done, so shall it be done to him*. Proverbs 24:29
quotes a man saying the same thing in the first person: *kaʾăšer ʿāśāh-lî kēn ʾeʿĕśeh-lô*, *as he
has done to me, so I will do to him*. The structure — *kaʾăšer … kēn*, the verb *ʿāśāh* twice — is
the same; what changes is who acts. In Leviticus the doing is done to the offender, by the community
under a judge; in Proverbs the injured man appoints himself. (Hebrew of both verses read from
Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-26.) Proverbs does not cite Leviticus, and the
wiki does not claim a literary dependence: the formula may simply have been a common way of
speaking of just return. But the overlap shows exactly what 24:29 forbids. It is not the principle
of proportion; it is the private use of it.

**What the son may not say about himself, God says of himself.** The second half of 24:29, *ʾāšîḇ
lāʾîš kəp̄oʿŏlô*, *I will render to the man according to his work*, is the clause of 24:12, where it
belongs to God: *wəhēšîḇ ləʾāḏām kəp̄oʿŏlô*. Twelve verses apart, the same verb and noun are a divine
prerogative and a forbidden human resolve. That is the logic of 20:22, which forbids *"I will pay
back evil"* and points the wronged man to Yahweh's saving instead. See
[[According to His Work - Proverbs 24.12, Psalm 62.12, and Romans 2.6]] and
[[Wait for Yahweh - Proverbs 20.22 and the Refusal of Revenge]].

**The witness stand.** 24:29 follows directly on *"Don't be a witness against your neighbor without
cause"* (24:28), and is best read as giving the motive: the false or needless witness is the
retaliator's instrument. The law already joined the two: when the judges find a false witness, *"then
you shall do to him as he had thought to do to his brother"* (Deuteronomy 19:19, WEB verified at
bible-api.com) — retaliation applied *to* the lying witness, by the court. Proverbs, without that
procedure, forbids the motive at its source. See
[[A False Witness Shall Not Be Unpunished - Proverbs 19 and Exodus 23]].

**Matthew 5.** Jesus quotes the talion — *"An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth"* — and then
forbids resisting the evil man. The text quoted is the law's (Exodus 21:24; Leviticus 24:20), not
Proverbs; but Proverbs 24:29 had already refused the talion as a rule for personal response, and
the wiki notes it as one Old Testament place where that refusal stands within the Hebrew Bible
itself. The Greek of Matthew 5:38–39 was not checked in this pass.

### Deuteronomy 19:19 now ingested

The Hebrew of 19:19, unchecked above, is *waʿăśîtem lô kaʾăšer zāmam laʿăśôt ləʾāḥîw* (WLC): *you shall do
to him as he planned to do to his brother*. It has the *kaʾăšer* and the verb *ʿāśāh* of Leviticus 24:19
and Proverbs 24:29, but no *kēn*. Instead of a completed act (*ʿāśāh*, *he has done*) it measures by an
intention (*zāmam*, *planned*; in Deuteronomy only here). The doer is plural, *you shall do*, the community
acting through its judges (18), so this is the court's retaliation and not the private one Proverbs
forbids. See [[Landmarks, Witnesses, and False Testimony]].

### A defeated king's confession — Judges 1:7

Judges 1:7 gives the formula a third speaker. Adoni-Bezek, his thumbs and big toes cut off, says
*kaʾăšer ʿāśîtî kēn šillam-lî ʾĕlōhîm* (WLC, OpenScriptures morphhb), *as I have done, so God has
repaid me*. The *kaʾăšer … kēn* frame and the verb *ʿāśāh* match Leviticus 24:19 and Proverbs 24:29.
The second verb is not *ʿāśāh* but *šillam*, *repaid*, and the one who repays is *ʾĕlōhîm*. In
Leviticus the court does to the offender what he did; in Proverbs 24:29 a private man resolves to do
it and is forbidden; in Judges 1:7 the offender himself names God as the one who has done it. The
human agents, Judah and Simeon, go unmentioned in his sentence. The narrator does not say whether the
maiming was right; the only verdict is the king's. See [[Judah Goes Up First]] and [[Retribution]].
Source: stored WEB Judges 1:6–7.

## Significance

Proverbs 24:29 is a small saying with a large implication. It does not abolish justice — 24:23–25,
four verses earlier, demands that the guilty be convicted — but it removes the injured party from the
judge's seat. Read with 24:12 and 20:22, it locates the rendering of each according to his work in
God and in a court that refuses to recognize faces, never in the private resolution of the wronged.
That is the same line Joseph walks in Genesis 50:19, *"Don't be afraid, for am I in the place of
God?"* — the scene the *Wait for Yahweh* page sets beside 20:22. See [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]].
