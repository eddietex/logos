# On the Bed's Head or on His Staff - Genesis 47.31 in Hebrews 11.21

## Passages involved

- **Genesis 47:31** — *"Israel bowed himself on the bed's head"* — [[Joseph and the Famine]]
- **Hebrews 11:21** — *"By faith, Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and
  worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff"*
- **Genesis 48:1–2** — *"Israel strengthened himself, and sat on the bed"*, the next scene *(not yet
  ingested)*
- Compare **1 Kings 1:47** — *"The king bowed himself on the bed"*, the same idiom for a dying
  patriarch
- Compare **Genesis 37:7–10** — *šāḥâ*, the verb of the sheaves and the stars —
  [[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]]
- Compare **Genesis 24:26, 48, 52** — *"bowed his head and worshipped Yahweh"*, the same verb for
  worship — [[A Wife for Isaac]]

## Analysis

**The last four words of Genesis 47 are the most quoted textual crux in the Joseph narrative, and it
is not a disagreement between manuscripts.**

**The Hebrew consonants are** ***מטה***. **They can be pointed two ways:**

| pointing | word | meaning | who reads it |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***miṭṭâ*** | מִטָּה | **a bed, a couch** | the Masoretic Text; Jewish tradition; most modern English versions, including the WEB |
| ***maṭṭeh*** | מַטֶּה | **a staff, a rod** | the Septuagint; the Vulgate; **Hebrews 11:21** |

**The consonantal text is identical on both readings.** **Vowel points were not written down until
roughly a thousand years after the Greek translation was made**, so the Septuagint's translators were
reading the same letters and supplying different vowels — **and there is no manuscript evidence that
can decide it, because no manuscript of the relevant age records vowels at all.**

### The two English sentences

> **WEB (Masoretic)** — *Israel bowed himself on the bed's head.*

> **Hebrews 11:21 (quoting the Septuagint)** — *and worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff.*

**The preposition is the other half of the difficulty.** ***ʿal rōʾš*** is *at the head of* or *on
the top of*, **and a bed has a head in Hebrew as it does in English.** **On the Masoretic reading a
dying man turns at the head of his bed and bows; on the Greek reading a dying man props himself on
his stick and bows.** **Both are physically natural for a hundred-and-forty-seven-year-old, and both
are attested elsewhere**: 1 Kings 1:47 has David *"bowed himself on the bed"*, and the staff is
Jacob's own image for his whole life — *"with just my staff I passed over this Jordan, and now I have
become two companies"* (32:10).

### Hebrews has moved the gesture

**The quotation raises a second question that is usually missed.** **Hebrews 11:21 attaches the
bowing to the wrong scene.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Genesis 47:29–31** | the oath about the burial; then *"Israel bowed himself on the bed's head"* |
| **Genesis 48:1–20** | the blessing of [[Ephraim]] and [[Manasseh]], crossed hands and all |
| **Hebrews 11:21** | *"blessed each of the sons of Joseph, **and** worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff"* |

**Hebrews combines the end of chapter 47 with the middle of chapter 48 into one sentence**, which is
ordinary in the epistle's summaries — 11:9 compresses three patriarchs into one tent, 11:22 takes the
whole of Joseph's death down to *"the departure of the children of Israel; and gave instructions
concerning his bones."* **The author is not misreading Genesis; he is abbreviating it**, and the
detail he keeps from chapter 47 is the one the Septuagint gave him.

### What Hebrews is doing with it

**The verse's argument does not depend on which reading is right.** **Hebrews 11's subject is faith
that acts on a promise it will not live to see, and Jacob qualifies on the content of the two
chapters either way**: he blesses the younger over the elder *"by faith"* (11:21), **having already
made his son swear to carry his body out of Egypt to a field in a country he no longer lives in**
(47:29–30). **The immediately following verse makes the point explicit about [[Joseph]] and his
bones** (11:22; Genesis 50:25).

**On either reading the posture is the same and it is the pericope's last image**: **a man who has
just secured a grave in [[Canaan]] bows**, either over a bed he will die in or over a stick he
crossed the Jordan with. See [[Faith]] and [[Burial and the Grave]].

### The verb

***Šāḥâ*** — **bow down, prostrate, worship** — **is the same verb as the sheaves and the stars of
Joseph's dreams** (37:7, 9, 10) **and as Abraham's servant worshipping at the well** (24:26, 48, 52).
**Hebrew does not distinguish obeisance to a person from worship of God by vocabulary**, which is why
the Greek renders it *προσεκύνησεν*, *worshipped*, and the WEB renders it *bowed himself*. **The wiki
notes that 47:31 is the closest Genesis ever comes to staging the second dream — the father bowing in
the presence of the son — and that the text gives no sign of intending it.**

## Significance

**It is the wiki's clearest single case of the New Testament quoting the Septuagint against the
Hebrew, and of the difference being invisible in the letters.** **A reader with an English Bible open
at Genesis 47:31 and another at Hebrews 11:21 sees a contradiction; there is none, only two
vocalisations of one word, neither of which the written text distinguishes.**

**The wiki follows the WEB, records the Greek, and adopts neither as against the other.** **Nothing
in the interpretation of either passage turns on it**, and the case is kept because it is short,
clean, and shows exactly how the New Testament's Old Testament differs from the Masoretic one. See
[[Names and Naming]] for the wiki's other notes on transcription, and
[[Jobab of Bozrah and the Septuagint's Job]] for the Septuagint supplying material the Hebrew does
not have at all.
