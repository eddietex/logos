# Witnesses Against Yourselves - Joshua's Farewell and Samuel's

## Passages involved
- [[Joshua's Farewell to the Leaders]] — **Joshua 23:2, 14** — *I am old and well advanced in years* … *Behold,
  today I am going the way of all the earth.* Stored WEB.
- [[The Covenant at Shechem]] — **Joshua 24:5, 14, 16, 22, 24** — *I sent Moses and Aaron* (5); *Now therefore
  fear Yahweh, and serve him in sincerity and in truth* (14); *Far be it from us that we should forsake Yahweh*
  (16); *"You are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen Yahweh yourselves, to serve him." They said,
  "We are witnesses."* (22); *We will serve Yahweh our God, and we will listen to his voice* (24). Stored WEB.
- [[Samuel's Farewell Address]] — **1 Samuel 12:2, 5, 8, 13–14, 23–24** — *I am old and gray-headed* (2); *"Yahweh
  is witness against you … " They said, "He is witness."* (5); *Yahweh sent Moses and Aaron* (8); *the king whom
  you have chosen* (13); *If you will fear Yahweh, and serve him, and listen to his voice* (14); *far be it from me
  that I should sin against Yahweh* (23); *Only fear Yahweh, and serve him in truth with all your heart* (24).
  Stored WEB.

## Analysis

Two leaders, at the end of an era, gather Israel and speak before they withdraw. The shared elements, all checked
in stored WEB and the WLC:

| Element | Joshua 23–24 | 1 Samuel 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Old age | *zāqantî bāʾṯî bayyāmîm* (23:2) | *zāqantî wāśaḇtî* (12:2) |
| History from Jacob in Egypt | *Jacob and his children went down into Egypt* (24:4) | *When Jacob had come into Egypt* (12:8) |
| *Šālaḥ* Moses and Aaron | *wāʾešlaḥ ʾeṯ-mōšeh wəʾeṯ-ʾahărōn* (24:5) | *wayyišlaḥ YHWH ʾeṯ-mōšeh wəʾeṯ-ʾahărōn* (12:8) |
| Witness formula | *ʿēḏîm ʾattem bāḵem … wayyōʾmərû ʿēḏîm* (24:22) | *ʿēḏ YHWH bāḵem … wayyōʾmer ʿēḏ* (12:5) |
| *Chosen* | *you have chosen Yahweh* (24:22) | *the king whom you have chosen* (12:13) |
| *Ḥālîlâ* | *Far be it from us* (24:16), the people | *far be it from me* (12:23), Samuel |
| Fear and serve in truth | *yərʾû … wəʿiḇḏû … bəṯāmîm ûḇeʾĕmeṯ* (24:14) | *yərʾû … waʿăḇaḏtem … beʾĕmeṯ* (12:24) |
| *Listen to his voice* | the people's promise (24:24) | Samuel's condition (12:14–15) |

**The difference in what is chosen.** At Shechem the people are witnesses that they have *chosen Yahweh*
(24:22). At Gilgal, if the setting is Gilgal (the chapter does not say), Samuel shows them *the king whom you have
chosen* (12:13). The verb is the same, *bāḥar* (WLC). The two speeches mark the two choices Israel made at the
start of two eras.

**The difference in who witnesses.** At Shechem the people witness against themselves, and Joshua sets up a stone
as a witness (24:27). In 1 Samuel 12, Yahweh and *his anointed* witness for Samuel, and against the people (12:5).
The king's first public act in the speech is to testify.

**No, but.** At Shechem the people answer Joshua's warning *No, but we will serve Yahweh*, *lōʾ kî ʾeṯ-YHWH
naʿăḇōḏ* (Joshua 24:21, WLC). Samuel quotes the people's answer to him, *No, but a king shall reign over us*,
*lōʾ kî-meleḵ yimlōḵ ʿālênû* (1 Samuel 12:12, WLC), close to their words at 8:19, *lōʾ kî ʾim-meleḵ yihyeh
ʿālênû* (WLC). The same two particles introduce the opposite resolve.

**Who promises.** At Shechem the people promise, three times, *We will serve Yahweh* (24:18, 21, 24). In 1 Samuel
12 the people make no promise; they confess and ask for prayer (12:19), and the promise is Samuel's: *far be it
from me … in ceasing to pray for you* (12:23). The *ḥālîlâ* passes from the people to the prophet.

### What the page does not claim

It does not claim literary dependence in either direction. Many readers group these speeches with others as
programmatic speeches at the turning points of Joshua–Kings (recalled, not checked); the wiki notes the view and
does not adopt it. The parallels above are what the texts share.

## Significance

- **Samuel's farewell is a covenant assembly with a king added.** The terms are Joshua's, fear, serve, listen, not
  turn aside; the new element is that the king stands under the same *if* (12:14–15, 25). See [[Covenant]] and
  [[Kingship and Empire]].
- **Both leaders end their era by putting the people on record.** Joshua's witnesses are the people and a stone;
  Samuel's are Yahweh and the king. See [[A Witness Between Us - Galeed, the Jordan Altar, and the Shechem Stone]]
  and [[Heaven and Earth as Witnesses - Deuteronomy 4, 30, and 31]].
- **Samuel, unlike Joshua, does not die after his speech.** He keeps praying and teaching (12:23), and he will
  return to rebuke the king (13:13, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Samuel]] and [[Joshua (son of Nun)]].
