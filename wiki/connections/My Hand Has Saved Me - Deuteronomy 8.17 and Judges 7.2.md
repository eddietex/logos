# My Hand Has Saved Me - Deuteronomy 8.17 and Judges 7.2

## Passages involved

- [[Remember the Wilderness and the Giver of Wealth]] — Deuteronomy 8:17–18; *"and lest you say in your
  heart, 'My power and the might of my hand has gotten me this wealth.' But you shall remember Yahweh
  your God, for it is he who gives you power to get wealth"*.
- [[Gideon's Three Hundred]] — Judges 7:2; *"The people who are with you are too many for me to give the
  Midianites into their hand, lest Israel brag against me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me.'"*
- [[The Fleece]] — Judges 6:36–37; *"If you will save Israel by my hand, as you have spoken"*.
- [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]] — 1 Samuel 14:6; *"there is no restraint on Yahweh to save by many or by
  few."*

## Analysis

**Two imagined sentences.** Both passages put words in Israel's mouth that Israel has not yet said.
Deuteronomy imagines Israel in the land, fed and prosperous, saying *in your heart* that its own strength
made its wealth. Judges imagines Israel after a victory, saying aloud that its own hand saved it. In
both, the sentence is quoted to prevent it.

**The shared words.** Deuteronomy 8:17 reads *kōḥî wəʿōṣem yādî ʿāśâ lî ʾet-haḥayil hazzeh*, *my strength
and the might of my hand has made for me this wealth*. Judges 7:2 reads *yādî hôšîʿâ lî*, *my hand has
saved for me* (both WLC). The shared elements are *yādî*, *my hand*, as subject, and *lî*, *for me*. The
verbs differ: *ʿāśâ*, *made*, and *hôšîʿâ*, *saved*. Judges does not cite Deuteronomy, and three common
Hebrew words are not enough to show dependence. The wiki records the agreement as a parallel.

**Two different answers.** Deuteronomy's answer is a reminder: *it is he who gives you power to get
wealth* (8:18). Israel keeps its strength and is told where it came from. Judges' answer is
subtraction. Yahweh does not remind the army. He sends twenty-two thousand men home, and then all
but three hundred of the ten thousand left, so that the sentence cannot be said (7:3–8). In Deuteronomy, the hand is real and has to be
credited to God. In Judges, most of the hand is removed.

**Gideon's "by my hand."** Two verses before Judges 7:2, Gideon asked God twice to *save Israel by my
hand*, *môšîaʿ bəyādî* (6:36–37, WLC). The verb is the same *yāšaʿ*, and *yādî* is again *my hand*. The
request was for Gideon's hand to be God's instrument, which is what 6:14 had promised, and the wiki does
not read it as the boast of 7:2. But the next thing Yahweh says is that no Israelite hand must be able
to claim the saving. In 7:7 the promise is *I will save you, and deliver the Midianites into your
hand*. Gideon's hand still strikes. It is not said to save.

**The verb *boast*.** Judges 7:2 has *yitpāʾēr*, the hitpael of *pāʾar*, *to glorify oneself* (WLC).
Deuteronomy 8 does not use it. Its heart-language is *your heart might be lifted up* (8:14), with *rûm*
(WLC). The two passages describe the same temptation in different words.

**Jonathan's version.** 1 Samuel 14:6 states as a confession what Judges 7 enacted: *there is no restraint on Yahweh
to save by many or by few*, *ləhôšîaʿ bərāḇ ʾô ḇimʿāṭ* (WLC). Judges 7:2 opened with the same adjective, *rab*, *too
many*, and the same verb, *yāšaʿ* (WLC). The difference is who speaks. In Judges Yahweh reduces the army so that
Israel cannot say *my hand*. At Michmash an Israelite, with one companion, says beforehand that the number does not
matter, and the narrator closes *So Yahweh saved Israel that day* (14:23). See [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]].

## Significance

The pair shows that Israel's danger after success, in Deuteronomy's peace or in Judges' war, is to
take credit for what God did. Deuteronomy guards against it by commanding remembrance. Judges guards
against it by making the victory impossible to explain any other way. See
[[Pride and Self-Exaltation]], [[Remembrance]], and [[War]].
