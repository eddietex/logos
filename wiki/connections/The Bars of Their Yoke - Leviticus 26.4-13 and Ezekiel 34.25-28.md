# The Bars of Their Yoke - Leviticus 26.4-13 and Ezekiel 34.25-28

## Passages involved

- [[Blessings for Obedience]] — Leviticus 26:4–6, 13; the conditional blessings of Sinai.
- **Ezekiel 34:25–28** — *"I will make with them a covenant of peace, and will cause evil animals to
  cease out of the land"*; *"The tree of the field will yield its fruit, and the earth will yield its
  increase"*; *"when I have broken the bars of their yoke, and have delivered them out of the hand of
  those who made slaves of them"*; *"no one will make them afraid"* *(not yet ingested; WEB from
  bible-api.com, 2026-10-30)*.

## Analysis

**The two passages share a run of wording, not just a theme.** All Hebrew below is from MAM.

| Leviticus 26 | Ezekiel 34 | Shared wording |
|---|---|---|
| 4, rains in their season | 26, shower in its season | *gešem* with *bəʿēt* |
| 4, land and tree yield | 27, tree and land yield | *ʿēṣ haśśādeh*, *piryô*, *yəbûlāh* |
| 5–6, safely in the land | 25, 27, 28, securely | *lābeṭaḥ* |
| 6, peace in the land | 25, covenant of peace | *šālôm* |
| 6, evil animals removed | 25, evil animals made to cease | *wəhišbattî ḥayyâ rāʿâ min-hāʾāreṣ* |
| 6, no one will make you afraid | 28, no one will make them afraid | *wəʾên maḥărîd* |
| 13, broke the bars of your yoke | 27, broke the bars of their yoke | *š-b-r*, *mōṭôt ʿōl* |
| 13, not to be their slaves | 27, those who made slaves of them | *ʿ-b-d* |

**What changes.** Leviticus 26 opens *If you walk in my statutes* (3). Ezekiel's unit, as quoted here,
opens *I will make with them a covenant*. The conditional sentence has become a promise after the
failure the next unit of Leviticus describes. In Leviticus the broken yoke is already past: *I have
broken*, the proof of who is speaking. In Ezekiel 34:27 it is what will finally make them know him:
*Then they will know that I am Yahweh, when I have broken*. The order also turns round: Leviticus puts
land before tree and animals after harvest, and Ezekiel has tree before land and animals first.

**The dependence cannot be fixed from the texts alone.** The relative date of the two is disputed, and
this page reports shared language without claiming which wrote from which, or whether both draw on a
common stock of covenant phrases. Within the canon's reading order, Ezekiel's promise reads as
Leviticus's blessing restated.

**The animals differ in scale.** Leviticus removes them from *the land*. Ezekiel lets people *sleep
in the woods* (25), their own territory, so the same removal extends to where the wild animals live.

## Significance

- It shows that the covenant blessing was remembered as wording. A restored people is promised the
  very things Leviticus 26 listed, and not generic prosperity.
- It ties the exodus to the return: Ezekiel 34:27 uses the yoke language of Leviticus 26:13 for a release still to come.
  See [[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[Covenant]].
- The formula *no one will make them afraid* is followed further at
  [[None Shall Make You Afraid - The Covenant Blessing of Security]].
