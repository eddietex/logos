# Blessed Among Women - Jael in Judges 5.24 and Mary in Luke 1

## Passages involved

- **Judges 5:24** — [[The Song of Deborah]]: *"Jael shall be blessed above women, the wife of Heber the
  Kenite; blessed shall she be above women in the tent."*
- **Luke 1:28** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked at bible-api.com)* — *"Having come in, the angel said to
  her, 'Rejoice, you highly favored one! The Lord is with you. Blessed are you among women!'"*
- **Luke 1:42** *(not yet ingested; WEB checked)* — Elizabeth to Mary: *"Blessed are you among women,
  and blessed is the fruit of your womb!"*

## Analysis

**The phrase.** Judges 5:24 has *təbōrak minnāšîm*, *she shall be blessed from among women*, twice
(WLC). In the stored WEB books, Genesis through Judges, Job, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of
Solomon, no other woman is called *blessed above* or *among women*; the Song of Solomon's *fairest
among women* (1:8; 5:9; 6:1) is praise of beauty, not a blessing. In Luke the
words come to Mary: from the angel in some manuscripts of 1:28 (WEB includes the clause) and from
Elizabeth in 1:42. The Greek of Luke was not checked for this page, and nothing here depends on it.

**What the two have in common, and what they do not.** Both women are blessed for their part in what
God is doing for Israel. There the likeness ends. Jael is blessed for a killing, at the end of a war
song; Mary for a child, in an annunciation. Luke does not cite Judges, and the wiki does not claim he
alludes to it. The parallel is one of wording: a nearly identical blessing given to two women, one in a
tent with a hammer, the other greeted as the mother of a child to come. WEB has *above women* in Judges
and *among women* in Luke.

**The blessing as verdict.** Judges 4 told Jael's act without a word of judgment. The Song supplies
one: *blessed above women*. Readers troubled by a host who kills her guest have found the blessing hard,
and the wiki records that difficulty on [[Jael]] and [[Hospitality]]. The comparison with Luke does not
resolve it; it shows only that the Song's verdict uses words Luke later uses of Mary.

**Into a woman's hand.** Deborah foretold that *Yahweh will sell Sisera into a woman's hand* (4:9).
The Song blesses the woman. See [[Into a Woman's Hand - Judges 4.9, 4.21, and 9.53-54]].

## Significance

The Song's blessing of Jael is not a passing compliment. Its wording returns in Luke for the mother of
Jesus. Whether the echo is intended is unknown; the close likeness of the English wording is a fact of
the WEB text, and the Greek was not checked. See [[Jael]], [[Blessing]], and [[The Song of Deborah]].
