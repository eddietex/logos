# Call Me Mara - Marah, Naomi, and Job 27.2

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 15:23** — [[Bitter Water at Marah]]: *"When they came to Marah, they couldn't drink from the waters
  of Marah, for they were bitter. Therefore its name was called Marah."* (*mārâ*, lemma 4785)
- **Ruth 1:13** — [[Ruth Clings to Naomi]]: *mar-lî məʾōḏ*, *it grieves me seriously* (WEB), literally *it
  is very bitter for me* (WLC, qal of *mārar*).
- **Ruth 1:20–21** — [[Naomi Returns to Bethlehem]]: *"Don't call me Naomi. Call me Mara; for the Almighty
  has dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and Yahweh has brought me home again empty."* (*mārāʾ*,
  lemma 4755; *hēmar šadday lî*)
- **Job 27:2** — [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]: *"As God lives, who has taken away my right, the
  Almighty, who has made my soul bitter"* (*wəšadday hēmar napšî*)

## Analysis

**One root, three uses.** The root *mrr*, *to be bitter*, names a place in Exodus, a woman in Ruth, and a
complaint in Job. At Marah the bitterness is in the water, and the place is named for it by the narrator:
*Therefore its name was called Marah*. In Ruth the bitterness is in a life, and Naomi names herself for it:
*Call me Mara*. In Job it is in the *nep̄eš*, the soul, and Job names the one he holds responsible.

**The spelling differs.** The place is *mārâ*, with the usual feminine ending (lemma 4785). Naomi's name is
spelled *mārāʾ*, with a final *ʾālep* (lemma 4755), and occurs only at Ruth 1:20 in the WLC files the wiki
has read (Genesis through Ruth, 1 Samuel, Job). The text does not say why. The meaning is the same, and the
page does not claim Ruth is quoting Exodus.

**Shaddai makes bitter.** The verb in Ruth 1:20 and Job 27:2 is the same form: *hēmar*, the hiphil
perfect of *mārar*, *has made bitter*, with *Shaddai*, *the Almighty*, as subject. In the files read, these
are the only two places where *Shaddai* is the subject of that verb. (The WLC tags one other hiphil of the
lemma, Exodus 23:21, *Don't provoke him*, of the angel; its subject is Israel.) Both speakers pair *Shaddai*
with another divine name in the next line: Naomi with *Yahweh* (*Yahweh has testified against me*, Ruth
1:21), Job with *ʾēl* (*God, who has taken away my right*, 27:2). Both use legal language. Naomi says Yahweh
has *testified against* her (*ʿānâ ḇî*, the witness's verb of Exodus 20:16); Job says God has *taken away my
right* (*mišpāṭ*), and swears an oath on it.

**What happens to the bitterness.** At Marah, Yahweh shows Moses a tree, and *the waters were made sweet*
(Exodus 15:25). In Ruth, no one sweetens Naomi's name, and the book never calls her Mara again; the next
time she is named (2:1) she is *Naomi*. The reversal comes through people: Boaz will not send Ruth back
*empty* (3:17), and the women will call the child *a restorer of life*, *mēšîḇ nep̄eš*, one who brings back
the soul (4:15). Job's bitter *nep̄eš* (27:2) and Naomi's restored one (4:15) use the same noun. In Job the
end is Yahweh's speech and the restoration of 42:10–17.

**What the texts do not say.** None of the three cites another. Ruth does not mention Marah, and Job does
not mention Naomi. Later readers often read Naomi as a Job-like figure (reported, not verified here). What
this page records is a shared root, a shared verb with a shared subject, and a shared turn from bitterness
to restoration that each book tells in its own way.

## Significance

- **Bitterness is spoken to God by name.** Naomi and Job do not curse God or abandon him. They name him
  as the cause and say so to his people's faces. The text records the complaint and does not rebuke it.
  See [[Suffering]] and [[Mourning and Lament]].
- **A name can be refused.** Naomi's renaming does not stick. The narrator keeps calling her Naomi, and the
  book's end gives her a grandson instead of a new name. See [[Names and Naming]].
- **Shaddai is the name of the sufferer's God.** Outside Genesis's promises, *Shaddai* belongs mostly to
  Job; Naomi is the only other speaker in the files read to use it of her own suffering. See
  [[God Almighty - El Shaddai in Genesis and Exodus]] and [[The Names of God]].
