# The Lion of Judah - Genesis 49.9 and Revelation 5.5

## Passages involved

- Genesis 49:9 — *"Judah is a lion's cub. From the prey, my son, you have gone up. He stooped down,
  he crouched as a lion, as a lioness. Who will rouse him up?"*
- Numbers 23:24; 24:9 — Balaam uses the same picture of Israel, the second time nearly word for word
- Deuteronomy 33:20, 22 — Moses gives the lion to [[Gad]] and to [[Dan (person)]], not to [[Judah]]
- Hosea 5:14 — *"I will be to Ephraim like a lion"* — God as the lion, and Judah's is the house he
  tears
- Ezekiel 19:1–9 — a lament for the princes of Israel as a lioness and her cubs, taken in a pit
- Amos 3:8; Jeremiah 4:7 — the lion's roar as the prophetic and the invading terror
- Revelation 5:5 — *"the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has overcome"*

## Analysis

**The picture grows inside one verse, and the growth is the point.** ***Gûr ʾaryēh*** is a cub;
***ʾaryēh*** is the adult; ***lābîʾ*** is the lioness, the most dangerous of the three. **Genesis
49:9 moves through all three nouns in nineteen Hebrew words** — **a young animal that has just come
up from a kill, lies down, and becomes something no one will disturb.**

***"Who will rouse him up?"*** is the only rhetorical question in the poem, **and the answer it
assumes is *nobody*.** The verse does not describe an attack; **it describes a predator at rest,
which is a statement about security rather than about violence.**

**Balaam picks the line up twice and moves it from a tribe to a nation.** *"Behold, the people rises
up as a lioness. As a lion he lifts himself up. He shall not lie down until he eats of the prey"*
(Numbers 23:24); then, at 24:9, **almost the words of Genesis**: *"He couched, he lay down as a
lion, as a lioness. Who shall rouse him up?"* — followed immediately by *"Blessed is everyone who
blesses you"*, which is [[Abraham]]'s promise at 12:3. **A Mesopotamian diviner quotes Jacob's
deathbed and Abram's call in consecutive lines, and Numbers does not say he knew he was doing it.**

**Moses' blessing withholds the lion from Judah.** Deuteronomy 33 gives it to [[Gad]] (*"he dwells
as a lioness"*, 33:20) and to [[Dan (person)]] (*"a lion's cub"*, 33:22), **and gives Judah four
words about being heard and helped against his adversaries** (33:7). **The wiki records the
reversal**: the tribe Genesis makes the lion is the one tribe Deuteronomy's blessing does not.
See [[Jacob's Blessing and the Blessing of Moses - Genesis 49 and Deuteronomy 33]].

**The prophets turn the image against its holders.** Hosea makes God the lion and Judah's house the
prey (5:14). **Ezekiel 19 is a lament in which the lioness is the royal house of Judah itself and
both her cubs are taken in pits and led away in hooks** — first to Egypt, then to Babylon. **The
picture Genesis used for untouchable security becomes the picture for a dynasty in a cage**, and
Ezekiel ends *"now she is planted in the wilderness, in a dry and thirsty land"* (19:13).

## Significance

**Revelation 5:5 is the only verse in the New Testament that names the tribe and the animal
together**, and it is a citation of two Old Testament promises in one breath: *"the Lion of the
tribe of Judah, the Root of David"* — **Genesis 49:9 and Isaiah 11:1, 10.** **What follows in the
next verse is the reversal the whole book turns on**: John hears *a Lion* announced and, when he
looks, **sees a Lamb standing as though slain** (5:6). **The title is not withdrawn; the image is
substituted.**

**The wiki records the trail as an image with a history, not as a prophecy with a fulfilment.**
Genesis 49:9 says nothing about a person; it says a tribe will be dangerous and unbothered. **What
Numbers, Hosea, Ezekiel and Revelation each do with the picture is their own**, and the last of them
is the only one to make it a title.
