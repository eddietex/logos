# A Morsel of Bread - Genesis 18.5 and Judges 19.5

## Passages involved
- **Genesis 18:5** — Abraham to the three visitors at Mamre: *"I will get a morsel of bread so you can
  refresh your heart. After that you may go your way"* (stored WEB). See
  [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]].
- **Judges 19:5, 8** — the Levite's father-in-law in Bethlehem: *"Strengthen your heart with a morsel of
  bread, and afterward you shall go your way"*; *"Please strengthen your heart and stay until the day
  declines"* (stored WEB). See [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]].

## Analysis

**The words.** Genesis 18:5 has *wəʾeqḥâ p̄at-leḥem wəsaʿăḏû libbəḵem ʾaḥar taʿăḇōrû*; Judges 19:5 has
*səʿāḏ libbəḵā pat-leḥem wəʾaḥar tēlēḵû* (WLC). Three pieces coincide: *pat leḥem*, *a morsel of bread*;
*sāʿaḏ lēḇ*, *strengthen the heart*; and *ʾaḥar*, *afterward*, with a verb of going on. *Sāʿaḏ* occurs in
the WLC files of Genesis through Judges only in these three verses (Genesis 18:5; Judges 19:5, 8; lemma
5582).

**The scenes.** Both are a host pressing food on travelers who mean to go on. Abraham's meal is the start
of a visit that leads to the promise of a son and then to the road to Sodom (Genesis 18:16). The
father-in-law's meal is the start of a delay that puts the Levite on the road at evening, toward Gibeah,
where the scene of Sodom is replayed (Judges 19:22–25). In Genesis the morsel becomes a feast and the
guests leave in the morning; in Judges the morsel becomes five days, and the guest leaves at night.

**What the wiki claims and what it does not.** The shared phrase and the rare verb are verified; the
shared plot-position — hospitality before the Sodom scene — is the wiki's observation. Whether the writer
of Judges 19 meant the reader to hear Mamre in Bethlehem is not stated in the text. The broader reuse of
Genesis 19 in Judges 19 is documented at [[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]]; this page adds
that the borrowing begins a chapter earlier in Genesis, with Genesis 18.

## Significance

Judges 19 has three hosts: one who gives too much (Bethlehem), one city that gives nothing (Gibeah), and
one outsider who gives what he has and then gives up two women (the old man). The first of the three
speaks Abraham's words, and the third speaks Lot's. Read together, the chapter runs Genesis 18–19 inside
Israel with Abraham's hospitality intact and his intercession missing. See [[Hospitality]] and
[[The Levite's Father-in-Law]].
