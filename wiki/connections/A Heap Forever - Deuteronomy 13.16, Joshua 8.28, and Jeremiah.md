# A Heap Forever - Deuteronomy 13.16, Joshua 8.28, and Jeremiah

## Passages involved

- [[Do Not Follow Other Gods]] — Deuteronomy 13:16 (13:17 in WLC): *"You shall gather all its
  plunder into the middle of its street, and shall burn with fire the city, and all of its plunder,
  to Yahweh your God. It shall be a heap forever. It shall not be built again."* (stored WEB)
- [[The Capture of Ai]] — Joshua 8:28: *"So Joshua burned Ai, and made it a heap forever, even a
  desolation, to this day."* (stored WEB)
- Joshua 11:13 — *"But as for the cities that stood on their mounds, Israel burned none of them,
  except Hazor only."* (stored WEB; not yet ingested)
- Jeremiah 30:18 — *"The city will be built on its own hill"* (WEB, verified at bible-api.com).
- Jeremiah 49:2 — of Rabbah of the Ammonites: *"it will become a desolate heap"* (WEB, verified at
  bible-api.com).

## Analysis

The Hebrew word is *tēl*, the word modern archaeology borrowed (through Arabic) for a city mound.
In the books searched for this page — Genesis through Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Kings, Isaiah,
Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Hosea, Micah, Ezra, and Nehemiah, in the Westminster Leningrad Codex
(OpenScriptures morphhb) — it occurs exactly five times: Deuteronomy 13:17 (WLC), Joshua 8:28,
Joshua 11:13, Jeremiah 30:18, and Jeremiah 49:2. The search did not cover the whole Hebrew Bible.

The first two share the full phrase *tēl ʿôlām*, *a heap forever* (WLC). In Deuteronomy it is the
sentence on an Israelite town that has turned to other gods: everything burned *to Yahweh*, the
site never rebuilt. In Joshua it is what is done to Ai. The burning is the same, and the
permanence; the plunder is not — Deuteronomy 13:16 burns it, Joshua 8:27 gives it to Israel. So the
phrase travels from the law of the apostate city to the conquest without the whole procedure coming
with it.

The other three show the word's range. Joshua 11:13 uses it for the mounds cities stood on — the
ordinary settled *tel* — to explain why Israel did not burn them. Jeremiah 30:18 turns it into a
promise: Jerusalem *built on its own hill* (*ʿal-tillāh*, WLC), the ruin-mound becoming the
foundation again. Jeremiah 49:2 returns it to judgment, on Rabbah: *lǝṯēl šǝmāmâ*, *a desolate
heap* — the same *šǝmāmâ*, *desolation*, that Joshua 8:28 adds to *tēl ʿôlām* (WLC).

## Significance

The name *Ai* already means *the ruin* (see [[Ai]]), and Joshua 8:28 makes the name and the verdict
coincide: the ruin is made a ruin forever, *to this day*. The page records three things the lexical
link shows without settling more: that the conquest narrative applied to a Canaanite city the
language Deuteronomy reserved for an apostate Israelite one; that the word that names a ruin can
also name the ground a city is rebuilt on; and that whether Ai's mound was already a ruin when the
story was told is the archaeological question [[Ai]] states and does not resolve.
