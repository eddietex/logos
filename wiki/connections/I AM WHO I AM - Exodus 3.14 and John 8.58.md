# I AM WHO I AM - Exodus 3.14 and John 8.58

## Passages involved

- **Exodus 3:12** — *"Certainly I will be with you"* — ***kî-ʾehyeh ʿimmāk***
- **Exodus 3:14** — *"God said to Moses, 'I AM WHO I AM,' and he said, 'You shall tell the children of
  Israel this: "I AM has sent me to you."'"* — ***ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh***; Septuagint ***egō eimi ho
  ōn***, *I am the one who is* — [[The Burning Bush]]
- **Exodus 4:12** — *"I will be with your mouth"* — ***ʾehyeh ʿim-pîkā***
- **John 8:58** — *"Most certainly, I tell you, before Abraham came into existence, I AM."*
- **Revelation 1:8** — *"I am the Alpha and the Omega," says the Lord God, "who is and who was and who
  is to come, the Almighty."*

## Analysis

**Exodus gives the name as a sentence, and the sentence is built on the verb *to be*.** *ʾEhyeh* is the
first person imperfect of *hāyâ* — *I am*, or *I will be* — and the four letters *YHWH* given in the
next verse are heard as its third person, *he is*. **The passage uses *ʾehyeh* three times as a
promise of presence before and after it uses it as a name** (3:12; 4:12, 15). The three readings of the
name — self-existence, faithful presence, and reticence — are set out on [[The Burning Bush]]; the
wiki adopts none.

**The Septuagint made one of them standard.** *Egō eimi ho ōn*, *I am the one who is*, turned a verb
of presence into a statement about being, and it is through the Greek that the verse reached the New
Testament.

**John 8:58 reaches for it.** Jesus is arguing about Abraham — *"Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my
day"* (8:56) — and his hearers object that he is not yet fifty. His answer sets two verbs against each
other: Abraham *came into existence* (*genesthai*), and *I am* (*egō eimi*), with no predicate. **A
man who says *before Abraham was, I am* is not claiming to be old; he is using the form of the divine
self-naming.** The response tells us how it was heard: *"they took up stones to throw at him"* (8:59).
John has other absolute *egō eimi* sayings (8:24, 28; 13:19), and the many *I am* sayings with
predicates (*the bread of life*, *the light of the world*) may play on the same form; the wiki records
those as a wider pattern, not as citations of Exodus.

**Revelation expands the name into three tenses.** *"Who is and who was and who is to come"* — *ho ōn
kai ho ēn kai ho erchomenos* — **opens with the Septuagint's *ho ōn***, and is widely read as a
paraphrase of Exodus 3:14 that makes the Hebrew imperfect's openness explicit: the God who is, was,
and will be. **The third member is *who is to come*, not *who will be*** — the name's future turned
into an arrival.

### What the pairing does not claim

**John 8:58 does not quote Exodus 3:14.** It uses *egō eimi*, which the Septuagint has in the verse,
but without *ho ōn*; the link is an allusion, very widely recognized, not a citation. Revelation 1:8 is
likewise a paraphrase. **The wiki records both as allusions.**

## Significance

- **It ties the Gospel's highest claim about Jesus to the Torah's central naming of God**, and shows
  how the claim was received.
- **It shows the Septuagint shaping the reading** — the New Testament inherits Exodus 3:14 in the form
  *egō eimi ho ōn*, not the open Hebrew *ʾehyeh*.
- **It keeps the Hebrew sense in view.** Read through 3:12 and 4:12, *ʾehyeh* is a promise to be
  *with*, and the Gospel that applies the name to Jesus begins by calling him the Word who *"lived among
  us"* — a claim recorded for when John 1 is reached.
