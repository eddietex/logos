# The Thief Breaking In - Exodus 22 and Matthew 24

## Passages involved
- **Exodus 22:2–3, 7–8** — [[Laws of Property and Restitution]]; *"If the thief is found breaking
  in, and is struck so that he dies, there shall be no guilt of bloodshed for him. If the sun has
  risen on him, guilt of bloodshed shall be for him"*; and the householder, *"the master of the
  house"*, suspected when a deposit goes missing
- **Matthew 24:43** — *"But know this, that if the master of the house had known in what watch of the
  night the thief was coming, he would have watched, and would not have allowed his house to be
  broken into."*
- **John 10:10** — *"The thief only comes to steal, kill, and destroy. I came that they may have life,
  and may have it abundantly."*

## Analysis

**The two texts describe the same situation from opposite sides.** Exodus 22:2–3 asks what the
householder is allowed to do *once* a thief has broken in, and answers with the difference between
night and sunrise. Matthew 24:43 asks what he would have done *beforehand* if he had known the
hour: kept watch, and not let the house be broken into. Both put the thief at night and the
householder in the dark about him. In the WEB both use *"breaking in"* / *"broken into"*, and
Exodus 22:8 and Matthew 24:43 both say *"the master of the house"*. **That last agreement is an
English one.** The underlying Hebrew is *baʿal-habbayit* (Exodus 22:7 in Hebrew numbering, MAM via
Sefaria); the Greek of Matthew was not checked in this pass, and the wiki makes no claim that
Matthew is alluding to Exodus.

**What Jesus does with the image is turn the householder's ignorance into a warning.** The point in
Matthew 24 is that the coming is at an hour nobody knows, so watchfulness has to be constant. The
night-thief law shows why the image works: a break-in at night was the case in which the
householder could not tell what he faced, and the law accepted that he might strike first.

**John 10:10 uses the thief differently**, as a figure for those who come to take life rather than
give it. The verse names stealing, killing, and destroying together, the combination the night case
of Exodus 22:2 contemplates.

## Significance

A modest link, recorded so that the night-thief law is not read in isolation. **The Torah's
ordinance about a real break-in supplies the furniture of a later image** — night, a house, a master
who does not know the hour — and the New Testament uses it for vigilance rather than for law.
