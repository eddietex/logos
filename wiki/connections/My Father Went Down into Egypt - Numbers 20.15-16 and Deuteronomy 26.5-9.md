# My Father Went Down into Egypt - Numbers 20.15-16 and Deuteronomy 26.5-9

- [[Edom Refuses Passage]] — Numbers 20:15–16; Moses' message to the king of Edom
- [[Do Not Forget When You Are Full]] — Deuteronomy 6:21–23; the answer to the son's question
- Deuteronomy 10:22 — *"Your fathers went down into Egypt with seventy persons"*; stored, on

- [[First Fruits and the Tithe Declaration]] — Deuteronomy 26:5–9; the confession over the basket

## Analysis

The Torah tells the exodus at length, and also gives Israel several short versions of it to say
aloud. Each short version is spoken to someone, and each is shaped by who is listening.

**To a foreign king (Numbers 20:15–16).** Moses' messengers tell Edom, *"your brother Israel"*:
*"our fathers went down into Egypt, and we lived in Egypt a long time. The Egyptians mistreated us and
our fathers. When we cried to Yahweh, he heard our voice, sent an angel, and brought us out of
Egypt."* The story ends at Kadesh, *"a city in the edge of your border"*, because its point is a
request to pass through.

**To a son (Deuteronomy 6:21–23).** *"We were Pharaoh's slaves in Egypt. Yahweh brought us out of
Egypt with a mighty hand; and Yahweh showed great and awesome signs and wonders … and he brought us
out from there, that he might bring us in, to give us the land which he swore to our fathers."* It
answers *"What do the testimonies … mean?"*, and so ends in the commandments: *"Yahweh commanded us to
do all these statutes"* (6:24).

**To Yahweh, over a basket (Deuteronomy 26:5–9).** *"My father was a Syrian ready to perish. He went
down into Egypt … The Egyptians mistreated us, afflicted us, and imposed hard labor on us. Then we
cried to Yahweh, the God of our fathers. Yahweh heard our voice … Yahweh brought us out of Egypt with
a mighty hand … and he has brought us into this place, and has given us this land."* It ends in the
land, because the speaker is standing in it holding its fruit: *"Now, behold, I have brought the
first of the fruit of the ground"* (26:10).

The verbal overlap between the first and third is close. In the Hebrew, Numbers 20:15–16 and
Deuteronomy 26:6–8 share *wayyārēʿû*, *"mistreated"*; *wanniṣʿaq*, *"we cried"*;
*wayyišmaʿ … qōlēnû*, *"he heard our voice"*; and *wayyōṣiʾēnû … mimmiṣrāyim*, *"brought us out of
Egypt"* (WLC, morphhb). The differences are the point:

| | Numbers 20:15–16 | Deuteronomy 26:5–9 |
|---|---|---|
| Who went down | *"our fathers"* | *"My father … a Syrian ready to perish"* |
| In Egypt | *"a long time"* | *"few in number"* → *"a great, mighty, and populous nation"* |
| The oppression | *"mistreated us and our fathers"* | *"mistreated us, afflicted us, and imposed hard labor on us"* |
| How brought out | *"sent an angel"* | *"a mighty hand … outstretched arm … great terror … signs … wonders"* |
| Where it ends | Kadesh, at Edom's border | *"this land, a land flowing with milk and honey"* |

The account to Edom is told on the way, and stops short of the land; the confession is said in the
land. The Numbers account has an angel as Yahweh's agent; Deuteronomy's has Yahweh act with his own
*"mighty hand"*. The wiki records the difference and does not explain it.

**Deuteronomy 10:22** compresses the growth into one sentence: *"Your fathers went down into Egypt
with seventy persons; and now Yahweh your God has made you as the stars of the sky for multitude."*
**Joshua 24:2–4**, a later and longer retelling, begins further back, with Terah and *"other gods"*
beyond the River, and arrives at the same line: *"Jacob and his children went down into Egypt"* (WEB at
bible-api.com). That reading supports taking Jacob as the *"father"* of Deuteronomy 26:5, though
Deuteronomy itself does not name him.

**What the confession adds.** Only the basket version begins with a single ancestor and calls him
*ʾărammî*, the gentilic of Bethuel and Laban (Genesis 25:20; 31:20). Only it uses the vocabulary of
Exodus 1 and 3: *ʿinnâ* (Exodus 1:11), *ʿăbōdâ qāšâ* (1:14), *ʿŏnî* and *laḥaṣ* (3:7, 9). And only it
turns, at the end, from *"he brought us"* to *"I have brought"*, *hēbēʾtî*, the same hiphil of *bôʾ*.

## Significance

These are the closest the Torah comes to a creed: short, repeatable summaries of what Yahweh did,
put in the mouth of a speaker who says *we* and *us*. Deuteronomy 26:5–9 is the one framed as a
liturgy, to be said every year by every household that brings first fruits, and it is the one that
moves from the third person (*"he went down"*) to the first (*"mistreated us"*). The speaker was not
in Egypt, and says *us* anyway. Whether these summaries are older than the narratives they summarize
is a question in modern scholarship that this page does not take up.
