# A Worthy Woman - Proverbs 12.4, 31.10, and Ruth 3.11

## Passages involved

- [[Whoever Loves Correction]] — Proverbs 12:4.
- [[The Worthy Woman]] — Proverbs 31:10–31, especially 10, 28–31.
- Ruth 3:11 — a verified cross-reference; the book is not yet ingested.

## Analysis

The WEB calls the woman in Proverbs 12:4 worthy and depicts her as her husband’s crown.
Proverbs 31:10 opens its fuller portrait with the same description. The shared Hebrew phrase
*ʾēšet ḥayil* (אֵשֶׁת־חַיִל) is also applied to Ruth in Ruth 3:11. This verbal link was checked
in Mechon-Mamre’s Masoretic Hebrew text of all three verses, not inferred from translation.

In the verified WEB of Ruth 3:11, the speaker tells her that the city’s people know she is
a worthy woman. Proverbs ends by requiring acknowledgment of the woman’s works at the gates
(31:31). The comparison joins recognized character with public esteem; it does not make
commercial prosperity a condition stated by Ruth 3:11.

The phrase connects a short maxim, an ideal praise portrait, and a named narrative figure.
It does not prove that Proverbs 31 describes Ruth, that Ruth quotes Proverbs, or which text
came first. This limited comparison does not ingest Ruth’s narrative or construct a biography
from a single cross-reference. The fuller context remains for that book’s ingestion.

## Significance

The recurring description gives [[Marriage]] and [[Wisdom]] a positive female portrait
alongside the warnings about domestic strife. Praise should follow demonstrated character;
it cannot be reduced to appearance, prosperity, or an invented historical identification.
See [[The Contentious Woman - Proverbs 19.13, 21.9, 19, 25.24, and 27.15]].
