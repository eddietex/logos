# Pillars of Smoke - Song of Solomon 3.6, Joel 2.30, and the Wilderness Pillar

## Passages involved

- **Song of Solomon 3:6** — [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]]: *"Who is this who comes up from the
  wilderness like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense"*.
- **Joel 2:30** *(Joel 3:3 in the Hebrew numbering; not yet fetched or ingested)* — *"I will show
  wonders in the heavens and in the earth: blood, fire, and pillars of smoke."*
- **Exodus 13:21** — [[The Crossing of the Sea]]: *"Yahweh went before them by day in a pillar of
  cloud, to lead them on their way, and by night in a pillar of fire"*.
- **Exodus 19:18** — [[Israel at Mount Sinai]]: *"its smoke ascended like the smoke of a furnace"*.
- **Exodus 30:1** — [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]]: *"an altar to burn incense
  on"*.

Joel 2:30 was checked at bible-api.com (`translation=web`) on 2026-10-28. Exodus is from the stored
WEB. Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-28.

## Analysis

**The same rare phrase.** The Song says *kǝṯîmǝrôṯ ʿāšān*, *like pillars of smoke* (3:6). Joel says
*wǝṯîmǝrôṯ ʿāšān*, *and pillars of smoke* (Joel 3:3 Hebrew). The two phrases are identical except for
the first letter. The word *tîmǝrâ* is commonly said to occur only in these two places in the Hebrew
Bible. That count is a standard observation that this page has not verified by a concordance search.
The usual explanation links it with *tāmār*, *palm tree*: smoke rising in columns like palm trunks.
That is a lexical proposal, also not verified here.

**Different contexts.** In Joel the pillars of smoke are among the signs of the day of Yahweh, with
blood and fire, in heaven and on earth. In the Song they are the sight of a royal procession from far
off, fragrant with spices. Nothing in the Song is a sign or a judgment. The shared phrase is a picture
of smoke rising, used in two very different settings.

**Not the wilderness pillar's word.** Exodus's pillar of cloud and pillar of fire use *ʿammûḏ*:
*bǝʿammûḏ ʿānān*, *in a pillar of cloud* (Exodus 13:21; Sefaria). The Song uses *ʿammûḏ* too, but for
the carriage's silver *"pillars"* (3:10), not for the smoke. So the Song's smoke from the wilderness
is not verbally the Exodus pillar. Readers who hear the wilderness journey in Song 3:6 do so because
of the combination: something coming up *from the wilderness*, seen as rising smoke, and a guard of
the *"mighty men of Israel"* (3:7). That is an allusion some readers propose. The text does not make
it explicit, and the wiki records it as a reading.

**Smoke and incense.** The Song's smoke is perfumed: *mǝquṭṭereṯ mōr ûlǝḇônâ*, *perfumed with myrrh
and frankincense* (3:6; Sefaria). The verb is from *q-ṭ-r*, the root of *qǝṭōreṯ*, *incense*, the word
used of the altar *"to burn incense on"* (Exodus 30:1, *miqṭar qǝṭōreṯ*; Sefaria). Myrrh is one of the
spices of the holy anointing oil (Exodus 30:23), and frankincense is part of the holy incense (30:34).
At Sinai the mountain *"smoked"* and *"its smoke ascended like the smoke of a furnace"* (19:18).
The Hebrew there is *ʿāšan*, *smoked*, and *wayyaʿal ʿăšānô*, *and its smoke went up* (Sefaria): the
ordinary word for smoke, as in the Song, and the verb *ʿ-l-h*, *to go up*, the same verb as the
Song's *ʿōlâ*, *coming up* (3:6). Those are common words, and the wiki does not claim the Song is
pointing to Sinai.

## Significance

The phrase *pillars of smoke* connects a love poem and a prophecy of the day of Yahweh by one rare
image, and the incense vocabulary connects the Song's spices with the tabernacle's. Neither link gives
the Song a cultic or eschatological meaning. Both show how the Song draws on a common stock of
images, which elsewhere are used for worship and judgment, to describe a king's splendor. See
[[Myrrh, Aloes, and Cinnamon - Proverbs 7.17, Song of Solomon 4.14, and Psalm 45.8]] and
[[Cloud and Fire - From the Sea to the Filled Tabernacle]].
