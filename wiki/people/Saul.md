# Saul

## Overview

The first king of Israel, a Benjamite, the son of [[Kish (father of Saul)]]. His name, *šāʾûl*, is the passive
participle of *šāʾal*, *ask* (WLC): he is the king the people *asked* for (8:10; 12:13, stored WEB). Not to be
confused with [[Shaul (son of Simeon)]], whose name is the same in Hebrew. The page grows as 1 Samuel is ingested.

### Looking for donkeys, found by the seer — 1 Samuel 9:1–25

Saul enters as *an impressive young man*, *bāḥûr wāṭôḇ*, with no one in Israel *better* than he, and *taller than
any of the people* from the shoulders up (9:2, WLC). His father's donkeys are lost and he is sent to find them with
a servant (3). He is a dutiful son and not a seeker: his first words are a worry that his father will stop caring
about the donkeys and *be anxious for us* (5); it is the servant who knows of the man of God and carries the silver
(6–8), the young women at the water who give the way (11–13), and Yahweh who had told [[Samuel]] the day before to
expect *a man out of the land of Benjamin*, to anoint him *prince over my people Israel* to save them from the
Philistines (15–16). Saul asks Samuel himself where the seer's house is (18). Told that *all that is desirable in
Israel* is for him and his father's house, he answers in the words of a reluctant deliverer: *Am I not a Benjamite,
of the smallest of the tribes of Israel? And my family the least …?* (21), echoing Gideon's *I am the least in my
father's house* (Judges 6:15). Samuel seats him at the head of the thirty guests and gives him the portion kept for
him (22–24). See [[Saul Comes to the Seer]] and
[[The Least in My Father's House - Judges 6.15 and 1 Samuel 9.21]].

The height and the humility both recur. He will be presented *higher than any of the people* (10:23), found *hidden
among the baggage* (10:22), and later reminded *Though you were little in your own sight, weren't you made the head
of the tribes of Israel?* (15:17); and when the next king is chosen Yahweh will say *Don't look on his face, or on
the height of his stature* (16:7) (all stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**Recorded, not resolved — Saul's grandfather.** 1 Samuel 9:1 makes Kish *the son of Abiel*, and 14:51 is
consistent with it; 1 Chronicles 8:33 and 9:39 read *Ner became the father of Kish. Kish became the father of
Saul* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested). See [[Kish (father of Saul)]].

9:39 checked at bible-api.com (`translation=web`), 2026-11-17. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### Anointed in private, changed, and silent — 1 Samuel 9:26–10:16

At dawn Samuel walks Saul to the edge of the city, sends the servant on ahead, and has Saul *stand still* to hear
*God's message* (9:26–27). He pours *the vial of oil* on his head, kisses him, and asks, *Hasn't Yahweh anointed you
to be prince over his inheritance?* (10:1). Saul is not recorded as answering. Samuel gives him three signs: two men
at [[Rachel's Tomb]] with news that the donkeys are found and his father is worried for him; three pilgrims at
[[The Oak of Tabor]] who give him two loaves; and a band of prophets at [[The Hill of God]], where *Yahweh's Spirit
will come mightily on you, and you will prophesy with them, and will be turned into another man* (10:2–6). He is
told *do what is appropriate for the occasion; for God is with you*, and also to go down to Gilgal and wait seven
days (10:7–8). As he turns to go, *God gave him another heart* (10:9, WLC *lēḇ ʾaḥēr*). The Spirit comes on him with
Samson's verb, *wattiṣlaḥ* (10:10, WLC), and he prophesies, and people who knew him ask *What is this that has come
to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?* (10:11). When his uncle asks what Samuel said, he tells him
about the donkeys and keeps quiet about *the matter of the kingdom* (10:16).

The pericope gives Saul no recorded words to Samuel and no prayer. What it gives is a change done to him, *turned*
and *given another heart*, both with the verb *hāp̄aḵ* (WLC), and a silence. The seven days at Gilgal (10:8) come
due at 13:8, *He stayed seven days, according to the time set by Samuel; but Samuel didn't come* (stored WEB; not
yet ingested). The proverb returns at 19:24, at Naioth, where it is told a second way. See
[[Samuel Anoints Saul]], [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]], and
[[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 9:26–10:16; 13:8; 19:24. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### Chosen by lot, found hiding, and acclaimed — 1 Samuel 10:17–27

At Mizpah Samuel rebukes the people, *you have today rejected your God*, and has them stand before Yahweh by tribes
(10:19). Benjamin is chosen, then the family of the [[Matrites]], then *Saul the son of Kish*, *but when they looked
for him, he could not be found* (10:21). Yahweh tells them *he has hidden himself among the baggage* (10:22). The
verb is *neḥbāʾ*, the same form used of Jotham hiding from Abimelech (Judges 9:5, WLC). Brought out, he stands
taller than everyone *from his shoulders and upward* (10:23, echoing 9:2), and Samuel asks, *Do you see him whom
Yahweh has chosen, that there is no one like him among all the people?* The people shout *Long live the king!*
(10:24). Saul goes home to Gibeah with *the army … whose hearts God had touched* (10:26). When
[[Worthless Fellows (of Saul's time)]] say *How could this man save us?* and bring him no present, *he held his
peace* (10:27).

He says nothing in the whole pericope. In 10:16 he kept the kingdom secret, and now he hides from it and then
stays silent under an insult. The text does not explain any of the three, and *little in your own sight* (15:17,
stored WEB; not yet ingested) is Samuel's later judgment, not the narrator's explanation here. He was chosen by the
same procedure that exposed Achan, and the same procedure will later point to his son Jonathan (14:41–42). See
[[Saul Chosen by Lot at Mizpah]] and [[Taken by Lot - Achan, Saul, and Jonathan]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 9:2; 10:16–27; 14:41–42; 15:17; Judges 9:5. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-17.

### The first rescue, and the kingdom renewed — 1 Samuel 11:1–15

The doubters' question, *How could this man save us?* (10:27), is answered in the next chapter. Messengers from
besieged [[Jabesh Gilead]] reach *Gibeah of Saul* (11:4), and Saul is *coming following the oxen out of the field*
(11:5): the acclaimed king has gone back to his farm. When he hears of [[Nahash (king of Ammon)]]'s demand, *God's
Spirit came mightily on Saul … and his anger burned hot* (11:6), the verb and the clause of Samson at Judges 14:19
(WLC). He cuts a yoke of oxen in pieces and sends them through Israel, as the Levite of Gibeah sent his concubine
(Judges 19:29), with a threat to the oxen of anyone who does not come out *after Saul and after Samuel*; *the dread
of Yahweh* brings Israel out *as one man* (11:7). He counts them at Bezek, divides them into three companies as
Gideon did, and strikes Ammon from the morning watch *until the heat of the day* (11:8–11).

He acts like a judge, and the narrative lets the likeness stand. Two things are his own. When the people demand
the death of those who had said *Shall Saul reign over us?*, he refuses: *No man shall be put to death today; for
today Yahweh has rescued Israel* (11:13). The man who *held his peace* under insult (10:27) now speaks to spare the
men who gave it, and gives the victory to Yahweh. And the victory leads to a third king-making: at Gilgal *they
made Saul king before Yahweh*, with peace offerings, and *Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly*
(11:14–15). Oil, lot, and victory have each made him king, and the text does not say how the three relate. See
[[Saul Rescues Jabesh Gilead]], [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]], and
[[Jabesh Gilead and Benjamin - Judges 21, 1 Samuel 11, and 1 Samuel 31]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 10:27; 11:1–15; Judges 14:19; 19:29. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-18.

### Witness and subject — 1 Samuel 12:1–25

Saul does not speak in Samuel's farewell, but he is in it from the second verse: *now, behold, the king walks
before you* (12:2). Samuel calls him as a witness, *before Yahweh, and before his anointed* (12:3, 5), the first
time a living king is called *Yahweh's anointed* in the narrative. The king's first public act in the speech is to
confirm that the old judge took nothing. Then Samuel presents him as *the king whom you have chosen, and whom you
have asked for*, *šəʾeltem* (12:13, WLC), the root of his name, and in the same verse as the one Yahweh *has set*
over them. The conditions that follow bind him with the people: *both you and also the king who reigns over you*
(12:14), and the speech's last words are *you will be consumed, both you and your king* (12:25). See
[[Samuel's Farewell Address]] and [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].

### The seven days at Gilgal, and the kingdom that will not continue — 1 Samuel 13:1–15

The account of the reign opens with a regnal formula whose numbers the Hebrew lacks: *ben-šānâ šāʾûl bəmolḵô
ûštê šānîm mālaḵ*, *Saul was a son of a year … and two years he reigned* (13:1, WLC); the WEB's *thirty* and
*forty-two* are supplied, and Acts 13:21 gives *forty years* (WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-18; not
stored). Saul keeps three thousand men, two thousand with himself at [[Michmash]], one thousand with
[[Jonathan (son of Saul)]] at Gibeah. Jonathan strikes the Philistine garrison at Geba; Saul has the trumpet
blown, and all Israel hears that *Saul had struck the garrison* (13:2–4). The Philistines come up *as the sand*,
Israel hides or crosses the Jordan, and the men at [[Gilgal]] follow Saul *trembling* (13:5–7).

He waits *seven days, according to the time set by Samuel*, the order of 10:8, and Samuel does not come. With
the people scattering, he offers the burnt offering, and as he finishes Samuel arrives (13:8–10). To *What have
you done?* he gives three reasons and a motive: *I haven't entreated the favor of Yahweh. I forced myself*,
*wāʾeṯʾappaq* (13:11–12, WLC), the verb of Joseph controlling himself (Genesis 43:31; 45:1). Samuel's verdict is
*You have done foolishly*, *niskāltā*: he has not kept *the commandment of Yahweh*; his kingdom would have been
established *forever*, and now *will not continue*; Yahweh has sought *a man after his own heart* as *prince*
(13:13–14). Saul counts six hundred men (13:15). He does not confess here. Whether his fault was impatience
before the prophet or a king's offering of sacrifice, the text names only the commandment. What he loses is the
dynasty; the rejection *from being king* comes over Amalek (15:23, 26, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]],
[[You Have Done Foolishly - Saul at Gilgal and David at the Census]], and
[[A Man After His Own Heart - 1 Samuel 13.14, 16.7, and Acts 13.22]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 10:8; 13:1–15; 15:23, 26; Genesis 43:31; 45:1. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-18.

### Two swords in Israel — 1 Samuel 13:16–23

Saul holds *Geba of Benjamin* with Jonathan and the six hundred, facing the Philistines at [[Michmash]] (13:16). The
raiders go out in three companies, the tactic Saul himself used at Jabesh Gilead (11:11), and nobody opposes them
(13:17–18). The Philistines have forbidden smiths, *Lest the Hebrews make themselves swords or spears* (13:19), and
*in the day of battle* no one in the army is armed *but Saul and Jonathan his son* (13:22). The verb is *māṣāʾ*,
*found*: *wattimmāṣēʾ ləšāʾûl*, literally *it was found with Saul* (WLC), the verb of the donkeys that *have been
found* at the start of his story (9:20). The king commissioned to *save my people out of the hand of the
Philistines* (9:16) faces them with six hundred men and two swords. See [[No Smith in Israel]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 9:16, 20; 11:11; 13:16–23. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### Sitting under the pomegranate, and the inquiry cut off — 1 Samuel 14:1–23

While Jonathan goes over the pass, Saul *stayed in the uttermost part of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree which is in
Migron* with six hundred men and Ahijah the priest *wearing an ephod* (14:2–3). The verb is *yôšēḇ*, *sitting*
(WLC); later he will be *sitting in Gibeah, under the tamarisk tree* with his spear (22:6, stored WEB; not yet
ingested). Jonathan *didn't tell his father* (14:1), *lōʾ higgîḏ* (WLC), the words used when Saul kept *the matter of
the kingdom* from his uncle (10:16).

He learns of the battle from his watchmen (14:16). His first response is to count the people, *piqḏû-nāʾ* (14:17,
WLC), as he counted them at Gilgal (13:15). His second is to ask Ahijah to bring *God's ark* (14:18). His third is to
stop the priest while the tumult grows: *Withdraw your hand!* (14:19). He asks for guidance and does not wait for
it, as at Gilgal he did not wait for Samuel (13:8–12). Then he and his men come to the battle and find the
Philistines killing one another (14:20). The narrator's closing sentence gives the victory neither to Saul nor to
Jonathan: *So Yahweh saved Israel that day* (14:23). The commission at his anointing, *He will save my people out of
the hand of the Philistines* (9:16), is carried out by his son and by Yahweh. See [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 9:16; 10:16; 13:8–15; 14:1–23; 22:6. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### Three oaths in one evening — 1 Samuel 14:24–46

On the day of the rout Saul *had adjured the people*: *Cursed is the man who eats any food until it is evening, and I
am avenged of my enemies* (14:24). The purpose is his own, *wᵊniqqamtî*, *I am avenged* (WLC), the form Samson used of
his private revenge (Judges 15:7). The army goes hungry, and Jonathan, who did not hear the oath, says *My father has
troubled the land* (14:29). At evening the people eat *with the blood*, and Saul acts promptly and lawfully: a great
stone, slaughter at one place, *don't sin against Yahweh* (14:33–34). *This was the first altar that he built to
Yahweh* (14:35).

He proposes a night raid; the priest proposes to ask God; Saul asks, and *he didn't answer him that day* (14:36–37),
the outcome Samuel had foretold for the people's king, *Yahweh will not answer you in that day* (8:18). The name
*šāʾûl*, *asked*, and the verb *wayyišʾal*, *asked*, stand side by side in 14:37 (WLC). Saul swears that the guilty
man will die *though it is in Jonathan my son* (14:39), places himself and Jonathan on one side for the lot (14:40),
and when Jonathan is taken swears again, *God do so and more also; for you shall surely die, Jonathan* (14:44). The
people overrule him with their own oath and *rescued Jonathan* (14:45). Saul goes up from the pursuit (14:46). He
kept the law on blood and his own oath to the letter, and the people did not let him keep the oath. See
[[Saul's Rash Oath]] and [[A Father's Oath and His Child - Jephthah's Daughter and Jonathan]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 15:7; 1 Samuel 8:18; 14:24–46. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### The summary of a reign — 1 Samuel 14:47–52

The narrator steps out of the story to sum Saul up. *When Saul had taken the kingdom over Israel*, *lāḵaḏ
hamməlûḵâ* (WLC), the verb by which the lot had *taken* him (10:20–21; 14:41–42), he fought *on every side*: Moab,
Ammon, Edom, *the kings of Zobah*, the Philistines; *wherever he turned himself, he defeated them*; he *did
valiantly*, *struck the Amalekites, and delivered Israel out of the hands of those who plundered them* (14:47–48).
The last phrase is the language of the judges (Judges 2:16). Then his house: sons Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchishua;
daughters Merab and Michal; his wife Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz; his commander Abner son of Ner; and the
genealogy that makes Ner and Kish sons of Abiel (14:49–51; 9:1). The last verse is a policy: *when Saul saw any
mighty man, or any valiant man, he took him into his service* (14:52), what Samuel had warned the king would do
(8:11), and how David will come to court (16:18–22, not yet ingested).

The summary comes after the sentence of 13:13–14 and before the rejection of chapter 15, and it records the
people's request, *fight our battles* (8:20), as fulfilled. David's summary in 2 Samuel 8 uses the same frame with
Yahweh as the subject: *Yahweh gave victory to David wherever he went* (8:6, 14, WEB at bible-api.com). See
[[Saul's Wars and Household]] and [[Saul's Wars and David's Wars - 1 Samuel 14.47-52 and 2 Samuel 8]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 2:16; 1 Samuel 8:11, 20; 9:1; 10:20–21; 13:13–14; 14:41–52; 16:18–22. 2 Samuel 8 checked
at bible-api.com (`translation=web`), 2026-11-19. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb).

### Rejected over Amalek — 1 Samuel 15

Samuel brings a commission in Yahweh's name: *strike Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have* (15:3). Saul
musters two hundred and ten thousand at [[Telaim]], warns the Kenites away for their old kindness, and strikes Amalek
*from Havilah as you go to Shur* (4–7). He takes King [[Agag]] alive, and *Saul and the people spared Agag, and the
best* of the flocks and herds, *and were not willing to utterly destroy them* (9). Yahweh tells Samuel *It grieves me
that I have set up Saul to be king* (11). Saul sets up *a monument for himself* at Carmel and goes down to Gilgal
(12), and greets Samuel with *I have performed the commandment of Yahweh* (13), the very verb of the charge against
him (11, WLC). Asked about the bleating of the sheep, he says *the people* spared them *to sacrifice to Yahweh your
God* (15, 21); he insists *I have obeyed Yahweh's voice* (20). Samuel answers *to obey is better than sacrifice* and
*Because you have rejected Yahweh's word, he has also rejected you from being king* (22–23).

Then Saul confesses, twice. The first time, *I have sinned … because I feared the people, and obeyed their voice*,
with Pharaoh's words, *please forgive my sin* (24–25; Exodus 10:16–17, WLC), and Samuel refuses to return with him.
Saul grabs the skirt of Samuel's robe and it tears, and Samuel makes it the sign: *Yahweh has torn the kingdom of
Israel from you today, and has given it to a neighbor of yours who is better than you* (27–28). The second time, he
asks *please honor me now before the elders of my people*, and Samuel goes back with him, and Saul worships (30–31).
Samuel kills Agag himself. The two men go to Ramah and Gibeah, and *Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of
his death* (34–35). After chapter 13 Saul had no dynasty; after chapter 15 he has a throne without the kingdom. See
[[Saul Rejected over Amalek]] and
[[The Torn Robe - 1 Samuel 15.27-28, 24.4-5, 28.17, and 1 Kings 11.29-31]].

Source: stored WEB Exodus 10:16–17; 1 Samuel 15:1–35. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.

### *Since I have rejected him* — 1 Samuel 16:1–2

Saul is not in the scene at Bethlehem, but the scene is about him. Yahweh's first words are *How long will you mourn
for Saul, since I have rejected him from being king over Israel?* (16:1). Samuel's first words are *If Saul hears it,
he will kill me* (16:2). The king who was chosen partly for his height (9:2; 10:23–24) is in the background when
Yahweh tells Samuel not to look at *the height of his stature* (16:7). Eliab is *rejected*, *māʾas*, in the word used
of Saul (15:23, 26; 16:1, 7, WLC). Saul is still king when another man is anointed. See [[Samuel Anoints David]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 9:2; 10:23–24; 15:23, 26; 16:1–7. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.

### The Spirit departed, and the harp — 1 Samuel 16:14–23

*Now Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from Yahweh troubled him* (16:14). The verb is *sārâ*
(lemma 5493), and it closes the pericope too, when *the evil spirit departed from him* (16:23, WLC). *Troubled*,
*bāʿaṯ*, is stronger: *terrify*, *overwhelm*. Saul's servants name the spirit and propose music; Saul agrees, sends to
Jesse for David, *loved him greatly*, makes him his armor bearer, and asks to keep him (16:15–22). When David plays,
*Saul was refreshed, and was well* (16:23). *Rāwaḥ*, *refreshed*, occurs in the Hebrew files checked only here (WLC).
The same verb *sûr* returns in 18:12, *Yahweh … had departed from Saul*, and at Endor, *God has departed from me*
(28:15) (stored WEB; 18:12 in [[Saul's Jealousy of David]], 28:15 not yet ingested). The only other man in the stored books troubled by *an evil spirit from God* is
Abimelech (Judges 9:23); both die asking an armor bearer to kill them (Judges 9:54; 1 Samuel 31:4). See [[David Plays the Harp for Saul]] and
[[An Evil Spirit from God - Abimelech, Saul, and Ahab's Prophets]].

**Contradiction flagged.** Saul knows David and Jesse here (16:19–22), and asks Abner *whose son is this youth?* in
17:55–58 (stored WEB). Recorded, not reconciled; see [[David Plays the Harp for Saul]] and
[[Whose Son Is This Youth - 1 Samuel 16.18-22 and 17.55-58]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:14–23; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### Dismayed before the champion — 1 Samuel 17:1–11

Saul and *the men of Israel* camp in the valley of Elah against the Philistines (17:2). Goliath of Gath, six cubits
and a span, comes out and calls Israel *servants to Saul* (17:4, 8), and asks for *a man* (17:8, 10). *When Saul and
all Israel heard those words of the Philistine, they were dismayed, and greatly afraid* (17:11). The people had asked
for a king to *go out before us, and fight our battles* (8:20); Saul was taller than all the people (9:2; 10:23), the
root *g-b-h* that 17:4 uses of Goliath's *height* (WLC). At Jabesh Gilead the Spirit came mightily on Saul *when he
heard those words* (11:6); here he hears *those words* and is afraid (WLC). *Ḥāṯaṯ*, *dismayed*, is in the Hebrew files
checked otherwise only in the commands not to be dismayed in Deuteronomy and Joshua. See [[Goliath Defies Israel]]
and [[Afraid and Dismayed - Deuteronomy 1.21, Joshua 1.9, and 1 Samuel 17.11]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 8:20; 9:2; 10:23; 11:6; 17:1–11; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### The king's reward and the king's armor — 1 Samuel 17:12–40

The king offers what the people expected kings to give and take: *great riches*, *his daughter*, and to *make his
father's house free* (17:25). *Ḥop̄šî*, *free*, is otherwise in the Hebrew files checked the word for the Hebrew
servant who *goes out free* (Exodus 21:2; Deuteronomy 15:12, WLC). When David's words reach him he sends for David
(17:31) and refuses him: *you are but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth* (17:33). The narrator had a
servant call David *a man of war* at 16:18 (WLC), and by 17:33 Saul seems not to know it (see the contradiction note
above). David answers with the lion and the bear and *Yahweh who delivered me*, and Saul says *Go! Yahweh will be
with you* (17:37). It is the first time in chapter 17 Saul names Yahweh, and the words are close to 16:18's *Yahweh is
with him*. He dresses David in his *clothing*, a bronze helmet, and mail, the armor of a man like Goliath, and David
takes it off (17:38–39). The king who was to go out before the people now sends a shepherd out in his armor, and the
armor does not fit the shepherd. See [[David Comes to the Camp]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:18; 17:25, 31–39; Exodus 21:2; Deuteronomy 15:12; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-21.

### Whose son, and the man he keeps — 1 Samuel 17:55–18:5

*When Saul saw David go out against the Philistine*, he asked Abner *whose son is this youth?*, ordered him to find
out, and asked David himself, *Whose son are you, you young man?* (17:55–58). It is always *ben-mî*, *whose son*
(WLC), and the reward he had promised the killer included making *his father's house free in Israel* (17:25); the
text does not say which he is asking about, the man or the father. After 16:19–22, where he sent to Jesse by name, the
question does not fit, and the contradiction stands as flagged above.

Then he *took him that day, and would let him go no more home to his father's house* (18:2). *Lāqaḥ* is the verb of
Samuel's warning, *he will take your sons* (8:11); in 16:22 he had asked Jesse's leave, and here he does not. He sends
David out, and David *behaved himself wisely*, and Saul *set him over the men of war* (18:5). The verse records that
it was good *in the sight of all the people, and also in the sight of Saul's servants*, and says nothing of Saul's own
sight, which the next verses turn to: *Saul watched David from that day and forward* (18:9, stored WEB;
[[Saul's Jealousy of David]]). His son, meanwhile, loves David, makes a covenant with him, and gives him the robe and the sword (18:1–4);
Saul will call it conspiracy, *qāšar*, the verb of 18:1 (22:8, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[Jonathan Loves David]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 8:11; 16:19–22; 17:25, 55–58; 18:1–9; 22:8. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-21.

### Thousands and ten thousands — 1 Samuel 18:6–16

The women come out of all the cities of Israel *to meet king Saul*, and their song gives him thousands and David ten
thousands (18:6–7). He is *very angry*; it is *evil in his eyes*; and he reasons to the end of it, *What can he have
more but the kingdom?* (18:8). He does not know what Samuel told him at 15:28 was already done in Bethlehem (16:1–13),
but he guesses it from a song. From that day he *watched David* (18:9).

The next day the evil spirit *came mightily* on him, the verb of the Spirit's rush at 10:6, 10 and 11:6, and he
*prophesied in the middle of the house* with his spear in his hand, as at the hill of God he had prophesied among the
prophets. He throws the spear, *I will strike David and the wall*, literally (*ʾakkeh*, the *hiphil* of *nāḵâ*, the
women's *hikkâ*, *has slain*, WLC), and David escapes twice (18:10–11). The narrator's reason: *Saul was afraid of
David, because Yahweh was with him, and had departed from Saul* (18:12). Yahweh *departed*, *sār*, and Saul *removed*
David, *wayəsirēhû*, the same verb *sûr* (18:13, WLC), sending him to command a thousand, and David goes out and comes in
before the people, who love him (18:13, 16). When Saul *saw* David's success he *stood in awe of him* (18:15). His fear
grows in three words, *angry*, *afraid*, *in awe*; the text does not say his love of 16:21 ended. See
[[Saul's Jealousy of David]] and [[Saul Has Slain His Thousands - 1 Samuel 18.7, 21.11, and 29.5]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 10:6, 10; 11:6; 15:28; 16:1–13, 21; 18:6–16. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-21.

### Two daughters and the hand of the Philistines — 1 Samuel 18:17–30

Saul stops throwing the spear and starts using his daughters. He offers [[Merab]], *Only be valiant for me, and fight
Yahweh's battles*, and the narrator gives his thought: *Don't let my hand be on him, but let the hand of the
Philistines be on him* (18:17). Merab goes instead to [[Adriel]] (18:19). When [[Michal]] loves David, *the thing
pleased him*: *that she may be a snare to him* (18:20–21). He sends his servants *secretly* to say *the king has
delight in you* (18:22), and names the price, *one hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be avenged of the king's
enemies*, meaning *to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines* (18:25). *To be avenged of … enemies* is his own
oath at Michmash (14:24; the *niphal* of *nāqam*, WLC). David pays it twice over, and Saul gives him Michal (18:27).
Then *Saul saw and knew that Yahweh was with David*, the first time he is said to know it; *Saul was even more afraid of
David; and Saul was David's enemy continually* (18:28–29), *ʾōyēḇ*, the word of the *enemies* the dowry was to avenge
(WLC). See [[David Marries Michal]] and [[A Bride-Price of Foreskins - Shechem, Saul, and David]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 14:24; 18:17–30. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.

### The order spoken, and the oath — 1 Samuel 19:1–7

What chapter 18 kept in his thoughts Saul now says aloud, *to Jonathan his son, and to all his servants, that they
should kill David* (19:1). Jonathan answers with a plea that makes Saul the possible sinner, *Don't let the king sin
against his servant … Why then will you sin against innocent blood?*, and calls him as witness, *You saw it, and
rejoiced* (19:4–5). *Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan* (19:6), the idiom of his claim and confession at 15:20,
24, and swore *As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death*: his second *As Yahweh lives*, and the first sworn for a
life (14:39; 28:10). David is *in his presence, as before* (19:7). The oath holds for three verses (19:10, stored
WEB). See [[Jonathan Speaks for David]] and [[As Yahweh Lives - The Three Oaths of Saul]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 14:39; 15:20, 24; 19:1–10; 28:10. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.

### The third spear, and the messengers — 1 Samuel 19:8–17

The oath of 19:6 does not survive the next victory. After *war again* (19:8), *an evil spirit from Yahweh*, *rûaḥ YHWH
rāʿâ* (WLC), is on him as he *sat in his house with his spear in his hand* (19:9), and he tries *to pin David to the
wall*, his own words of 18:11; the spear goes into the wall (19:10). It is the third throw at David. Then he sends,
three times: messengers to David's house *to watch him, and to kill him in the morning* (19:11), messengers *to take
David* (19:14), and messengers *to see David*, with the order *Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may kill him*
(19:15). The king who swore *he shall not be put to death* asks for a sick man to be carried to him to be killed. He
is deceived by his daughter, and asks her *Why have you deceived me thus, and let my enemy go …?* (19:17), the question
Jacob asked Laban (Genesis 29:25) and that the medium will ask him at Endor (28:12). *My enemy*, *ʾōyǝḇî*, is the first
time he says it himself (18:29 was the narrator). See [[Michal Helps David Escape]],
[[Why Have You Deceived Me - Laban, the Gibeonites, Michal, and the Medium]], and
[[As Yahweh Lives - The Three Oaths of Saul]].

Source: stored WEB Genesis 29:25; 1 Samuel 18:11, 29; 19:6–17; 28:12. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.

### Among the prophets again — 1 Samuel 19:18–24

Told *David is at Naioth in Ramah* (19:19), he sends, as he did the night before: messengers *to seize David*, other
messengers, and a third group, *wayyōsep̄ šāʾûl wayyišlaḥ*, *Saul added and sent* (19:20–21, WLC), Balak's idiom when he
sent again for Balaam (Numbers 22:15). Every group prophesies. Then he goes himself. His only words in the pericope
are a question at the great well in Secu, *Where are Samuel and David?* (19:22). On the road *God's Spirit came on him
also*, *wattǝhî ʿālāyw gam-hûʾ rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* (19:23, WLC), the clause used of Balaam (Numbers 24:2), and he goes on
prophesying until he arrives. He strips off his clothes, prophesies *before Samuel*, and lies naked all that day and
all that night (19:24). The proverb of 10:11–12, *Is Saul also among the prophets?*, is told a second time. Fifteen
verses earlier *an evil spirit from Yahweh* was on him (19:9); here it is *the Spirit of God*, and the text does not
reconcile the two, or either with 16:14, *Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul*. At 15:35 *Samuel came no more to see
Saul*; at 19:24 Saul comes to Samuel, and the text records no word between them. See [[Saul Among the Prophets at Naioth]],
[[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]],
[[Falling Down Under the Spirit - Balaam and Saul at Naioth]], and
[[All That Day and All That Night - Saul Fallen Before Samuel]].

Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:15; 24:2; 1 Samuel 10:10–12; 15:35; 16:14; 19:9–24. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-28.

- Holds Geba facing Michmash; with Jonathan, the only armed man in Israel's army — 13:16, 22.

  and commander; mighty men taken into his service — 14:47–52.
- Sent to devote Amalek to destruction; musters at Telaim; spares the Kenites; strikes Amalek but spares Agag and
  the best of the livestock; sets up a monument at Carmel; claims to have performed the commandment; confesses twice;
  tears Samuel's robe; rejected from being king; Samuel sees him no more — 15:1–35.
- Absent while Samuel, fearing him, anoints David at Bethlehem — 16:1–13.
- Yahweh's Spirit departs; troubled by an evil spirit from Yahweh; sends for David the harpist, loves him, makes him
  his armor bearer, and is refreshed when he plays — 16:14–23.
- Camps in the valley of Elah; hears Goliath's challenge and is *dismayed, and greatly afraid* with all Israel — 17:1–11.
- Offers riches, his daughter, and freedom to Goliath's killer; hears David, refuses and then sends him, *Yahweh will
  be with you*; dresses him in his armor — 17:25, 31–39.
- Asks Abner and then David *whose son* he is; takes David and keeps him from going home; sets him over the men of
  war — 17:55–58; 18:2, 5.
- Met by the singing women; angered by *Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands*; watches David;
  under the evil spirit, prophesies and throws the spear twice; afraid of David and sends him to command a thousand;
  stands in awe of him — 18:6–15.
- Offers Merab and gives her to Adriel; uses Michal's love as *a snare*; asks a hundred Philistine foreskins to make
  David fall; gives Michal to David; *saw and knew that Yahweh was with David*; *David's enemy continually* —
  18:17–29.
- Orders Jonathan and all his servants to kill David; listens to Jonathan; swears *As Yahweh lives, he shall not be
  put to death*; receives David back *as before* — 19:1–7.
- Under *an evil spirit from Yahweh*, throws the spear a third time; sends messengers to kill David in his house;
  deceived by Michal's teraphim; *Why have you deceived me thus?*; calls David *my enemy* — 19:8–17.
- Sends messengers to seize David at Naioth three times, and each group prophesies; goes himself; the Spirit of God
  comes on him; strips, prophesies before Samuel, and lies naked all day and night; *Is Saul also among the
  prophets?* — 19:18–24.
