# Eliphaz (son of Esau)

## Overview

**Esau's firstborn, the father of five clans and of [[Amalek]], and the holder of a name that turns up
again four books later in the mouth of the wrong man.**

> **4** Adah bore to Esau Eliphaz. …
> **11** The sons of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho, and Gatam, and Kenaz.
> **12** Timna was concubine to Eliphaz, Esau’s son; and she bore to Eliphaz Amalek. …

**He does nothing in Genesis.** He is a name at the head of a branch: born in Canaan to
[[Adah (wife of Esau)]] before the household moved to Seir (36:4–5), given five sons and a concubine
in the second register (36:11–12), and then dissolved into seven chiefs (36:15–16). **The branch is
the point, not the man** — see [[The Descendants of Esau]].

***ʾĔlîp̄az*** is transparent Hebrew: ***ʾēl***, **God**, plus *paz*, **fine gold** — *God is fine
gold*, or *my God is gold*. It belongs to the same class of confessional names as *Eliezer*,
*Elimelech*, and *Eliab.*

## The name in the book of Job

**[[Eliphaz the Temanite]] is Job's first and senior friend, and this Eliphaz's firstborn son is
[[Teman]]** (36:11, 36:15). **The overlap is the strongest single reason the cast of Job is read as
deliberately Edomite.** Add the Uz of 36:28 and the Jobab of 36:33 and **four of the book of Job's
names sit in this one chapter** — which is what the Septuagint's appendix to Job is trying to explain
when it makes Job a descendant of Esau. See [[Jobab of Bozrah and the Septuagint's Job]] and [[Uz]].

**They are not the same man**, and the wiki does not treat them as one. This Eliphaz is an Edomite
ancestor in a genealogy; the other is a character in a wisdom dialogue, identified by a gentilic
rather than a patronymic, and the book of Job never names his father. **What the coincidence supports
is a setting, not an identification**: Edom had a reputation for wisdom (Jeremiah 49:7; Obadiah 8), and
an author placing a wisdom debate outside Israel would reach for exactly these names.

## The sons, and where two of them go

- **Teman** — the firstborn, and the district proverbial for counsel; a chief at 36:15 and again at
  36:42. See [[Teman]].
- **Kenaz** — ancestor of the **Kenizzites**, a clan absorbed into Judah. **Caleb is *the son of
  Jephunneh the Kenizzite*** (Numbers 32:12; Joshua 14:6, 14), and Othniel, Israel's first judge, is
  *the son of Kenaz* (Judges 3:9). **An Edomite line produces the one spy besides Joshua who says the
  land can be taken.**
- **Omar, Zepho, Gatam** — names and nothing else; the Chronicler reads *Zephi* at 1 Chronicles 1:36.
- **Amalek** — by [[Timna]], and counted in Adah's line.

**Genesis 36:16 also lists a *chief Korah* among Eliphaz's chiefs**, though Korah is [[Oholibamah]]'s
son at 36:5, 14, and 18. The Samaritan Pentateuch and part of the Greek tradition do not have him
there. The passage page records the doubling rather than choosing between the lists.

## Timeline / key events

- Born in Canaan to Esau and [[Adah (wife of Esau)]], Esau's firstborn — Genesis 36:4, 15
- Father of Teman, Omar, Zepho, Gatam, and Kenaz — Genesis 36:11
- [[Timna]] bears him [[Amalek]] — Genesis 36:12
- His line resolved into seven chiefs in the land of Edom — Genesis 36:15–16
- Copied by the Chronicler — 1 Chronicles 1:35–36

## Appears in

- [[The Descendants of Esau]] — Genesis 36:4, 10–12, 15–16; **the firstborn of the firstborn, twice
  over, and entirely without incident.** His branch supplies the chapter's two consequential names —
  Teman, which carries Edom's reputation for wisdom into the book of Job, and Amalek, which carries
  Edom's reputation for nothing else into the rest of the Bible
