# Doeg

## Overview

*Doeg the Edomite*, *a certain man of the servants of Saul* and *the best of the herdsmen who belonged to Saul* (1 Samuel
21:7, stored WEB). No other person in Scripture bears the name, so the page takes the bare title.

### Detained before Yahweh at Nob — 1 Samuel 21:7

Doeg enters the narrative in a single, silent verse wedged between [[Ahimelech (son of Ahitub)]] giving [[David]] the
show bread and giving him Goliath's sword: *Now a certain man of the servants of Saul was there that day, detained before
Yahweh; and his name was Doeg the Edomite, the best of the herdsmen who belonged to Saul* (21:7). He speaks no word and
takes no action in chapter 21; his mere presence is the shadow over the scene.

Three details stand out in that single sentence:

- **Detained before Yahweh.** The text never explains why Doeg was *detained before Yahweh* at [[Nob]] — whether by a
  vow, ritual uncleanness, a purification period, an offering, or sanctuary business. Whatever held him there kept him at
  the sanctuary on the exact day David arrived.
- **An Edomite in Saul's service and at Yahweh's sanctuary.** Saul had fought [[Edom]] among his enemies *on every side*
  (14:47), yet an Edomite holds high office over Saul's herdsmen and stands *before Yahweh* at Nob. Deuteronomy 23:7–8
  commanded *You shall not abhor an Edomite; for he is your brother*, allowing an Edomite's children into Yahweh's
  assembly in the third generation ([[The Assembly and the Holy Camp]]). The text does not say how Doeg entered Saul's
  household or Israel's worship.
- **Herdsman to Saul.** *The best of the herdsmen who belonged to Saul* (WEB; chief or mightiest of Saul's herdsmen)
  makes Doeg a senior royal official — an ironic parallel to David, the former shepherd of Bethlehem (16:11; 17:15, 34).

In the next chapter (22:9–10, 18–19, stored WEB; not yet ingested), Doeg breaks his silence at Gibeah: he tells Saul he
saw *the son of Jesse* come to Nob, claiming Ahimelech *inquired of Yahweh for him, gave him food, and gave him the sword
of Goliath the Philistine* (adding the inquiry not narrated in 21:1–9). When Saul's own guards refuse to lift a hand
against *the priests of Yahweh* (22:17), Doeg obeys Saul's order alone, killing eighty-five men wearing a linen ephod and
putting Nob — men, women, children, nursing babies, and livestock — to the edge of the sword (22:18–19). David later
confesses to Abiathar, *I knew on that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell Saul* (22:22).
Psalm 52's superscription attaches that psalm to Doeg's report to Saul (recalled, not checked).
