# Canaan (son of Ham)

## Overview

The fourth son of [[Ham]], and the only person in the primeval history who is given a destiny and
never an act.

He is named three times before he does anything — twice as a gloss on his father (*"Ham is the father
of Canaan"*, 9:18 and 9:22) and then three times in three verses as the object of a curse (9:25, 26,
27). In all six occurrences he is a grammatical patient. He is not present at the offence, is not
addressed, and never speaks; Genesis does not record a single thing he did. Everything else the book
says about him is a list of the peoples descended from him (10:15–19).

**The curse.** In [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]], [[Noah]] wakes from his wine, learns what
his youngest son has done to him, and says: *"Canaan is cursed. He will be a servant of servants to
his brothers"* (9:25). *ʿEbed ʿăbādîm* is the Hebrew superlative — the lowest of slaves — and the
line is then repeated inside each of the two blessings that follow, so that the curse is the only
element common to all three verses of the oracle.

Why the curse falls on him rather than on the man who committed the offence is the passage's central
difficulty, and Genesis gives no answer. The four proposals — etiology, punishment measure for
measure in a son, a minority reading that makes Canaan himself the offender at 9:24, and a
source-critical reconstruction — are set out on the passage page. What can be said flatly is that the
text never states an offence of his own.

**The name is a land before it is a man, and that is the point of the episode.** *Kənaʿan* is the
standard designation for the territory between the Jordan and the sea in Egyptian and Akkadian
sources centuries before Israel, and it is the land the rest of the Torah is aimed at. The curse and
the conquest are the same word, and the wiki keeps the man and the territory on separate pages for
that reason: the land is [[Canaan]], and the man keeps this page. See
[[The Curse of Canaan and the Conquest]].

**His eleven sons, and the only border in the Table.** [[The Table of Nations]] derives from him Sidon
(*"his firstborn"*), Heth, and the Jebusites, Amorites, Girgashites, Hivites, Arkites, Sinites,
Arvadites, Zemarites, and Hamathites (10:15–18) — which is to say, the peoples Israel will meet in the
land, six of them recurring in the seven-nation formula of Deuteronomy 7:1 and Joshua 3:10. And it
gives his territory a frontier, the only one drawn anywhere in the chapter: *"The border of the
Canaanites was from Sidon—as you go toward Gerar—to Gaza—as you go toward Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and
Zeboiim—to Lasha"* (10:19).

**The Table does not mention the curse.** He is given a firstborn, eleven descendants, and a surveyed
border, twenty verses after being consigned to servitude three times over, and on exactly the same
footing as everyone else in the chapter. Genesis neither ratifies the oracle here nor withdraws it.

**What Scripture does not do with him.** No conquest narrative, prophet, psalm, or epistle ever cites
Noah's curse. When the Torah explains why the Canaanites are dispossessed it appeals to what they do
(Leviticus 18:24–30; Deuteronomy 9:4–5), and when Joshua 9 puts a Canaanite people into perpetual
service it is the outcome of a broken treaty rather than of Genesis 9. The verse's whole career as a
warrant — including its long use to justify the enslavement of African peoples, which is a misreading
on six separate counts set out on [[Ham]] and on the passage page — happens outside the canon.

## Related people

- [[Ham]] — his father, whose seeing and telling he is cursed for
- [[Noah]] — his grandfather, who curses him in the first words he speaks in Scripture
- [[Shem]] and [[Japheth]] — his uncles, to whom he is consigned as *"a servant of servants"*
- [[God]] — who is spoken about twice in the oracle and says nothing in it, and who is never shown
  acting on the curse
