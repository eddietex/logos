# Cain

## Overview

The firstborn of the human race, and the first person in it to do something the Bible calls sin by
name. He is a farmer — *"a tiller of the ground"* (4:2) — which is his father's trade and his
father's sentence: the ground Adam was sent out to till (3:23) is the ground Cain works, and the
ground that will eventually refuse him.

His mother names him at birth with the Bible's first recorded human thanksgiving: *"I have gotten
a man with Yahweh's help."* The name *Qayin* plays on *qānîtî*, "I have gotten". Readers who hear
in that sentence an expectation that this child is the offspring promised in Genesis 3:15 — and
some ancient readers did — are reading the story's first hope into the story's first murderer.

Everything Cain is remembered for happens in [[Cain and Abel]], and the passage stacks his firsts
without comment. He brings the first offering. He is the first person to be **warned** — God
addresses him before he acts, tells him plainly that *"sin crouches at the door"* and that *"you
are to rule over it"*, which is more direct help than anyone in Genesis 3 received. He commits the
first murder. He tells the first outright lie to God (*"I don't know"*, where his parents at least
admitted the act). He is the first human being on whom the curse formula *ʾārûr* is pronounced.
And he is the first person given a protective sign.

His one famous line is a question: *"Am I my brother's keeper?"* — using *šāmar*, the verb for
what his father was to do to the garden. God does not answer it. The rest of the Bible does.

What happens to him is exile in three widening circles: from the ground (*"it won't yield its
strength to you"*), from settled life (*"a fugitive and a wanderer"*), and from God's presence
(*"I will be hidden from your face"*, and then *"Cain left Yahweh's presence"*). He is the first
person in Scripture to leave.

His reply to the sentence — *"My punishment is greater than I can bear"* — is grammatically also
*"my guilt is too great to be forgiven"*, and the tradition has never decided which it is: the
first self-pity in the Bible or the first confession. What is not ambiguous is God's response,
which is to keep him alive on purpose.

The New Testament makes him an archetype and does so harshly: *"of the evil one"* who *"killed his
brother"* because *"his works were evil, and his brother's righteous"* (1 John 3:12), and one
third of a triad of apostates in Jude 11. Genesis is more restrained, and does not stop the story
when he leaves — 4:17–24 gives his line the city, the tent, the harp, and the forge, so that
civilization's beginnings run through the man who was sentenced to wander. See
[[Cain and Abel in the New Testament]].

## Timeline / key events

- Born to Adam and Eve; becomes a tiller of the ground — [[Cain and Abel]]
- Brings an offering that is not respected; is warned by God that sin crouches at the door; kills
  his brother Abel in the field — [[Cain and Abel]]
- Is questioned, cursed from the ground, sentenced to wander, and given a protective sign; settles
  in the land of Nod, east of Eden — [[Cain and Abel]]
- Fathers Enoch and builds a city; his line runs to Lamech, Jabal, Jubal, and Tubal Cain —
  Genesis 4:17–24 *(not yet ingested)*
