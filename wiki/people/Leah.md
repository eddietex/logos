# Leah

## Overview

Laban's elder daughter, Jacob's first wife by substitution and never his chosen one, and **the mother
of half the tribes of Israel — including the two the nation's future actually runs through.** Levi,
who gets the priesthood, and Judah, who gets the kingship and the Messiah, are both hers. **So is the
grave at Machpelah.** Genesis buries Leah with Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebekah, and eventually Jacob
himself (49:31); [[Rachel]] lies under a pillar beside a road.

**She enters the Bible in half a clause about her eyes**, and leaves the wedding chapter without
having said anything.

### In this pericope — Genesis 29:1–30

***"Leah's eyes were weak, but Rachel was beautiful in form and attractive"*** (29:17). **The verse
gives her one feature and her sister a whole person**, and joins them adversatively. ***Rakkôt*** is
*soft, tender, delicate* — the adjective of tender meat at 18:7 and of a delicate child at Deuteronomy
28:56 — and **what it means here is genuinely open:**

- **A weakness of sight.** The Septuagint has ***ἀσθενεῖς***, *weak*, which the WEB follows, and the
  plot wants a sister who is hard to marry off.
- **A compliment that is immediately outbid.** *Soft eyes* as the one lovely thing about her, set
  against *beautiful in form and appearance* — a reading with a long history in Jewish commentary and
  with the adjective's ordinary sense behind it.

**The wiki records both and adopts neither.** What is not in doubt is that the narrator's own
vocabulary here is *the big one* and *the little one* (*haggədōlâ*, *haqqəṭannâ*, 29:16) — **and that
Laban, refusing Jacob at 29:26, switches to *the firstborn* and *the younger*, which is the oracle's
vocabulary and the birthright's.** Leah is not called a *firstborn* until the sentence that uses her
as one. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**She is married under her sister's name and Genesis gives her no word about it.** She is *taken*,
*brought*, and *given* (29:23–24); a slave, [[Zilpah]], is handed over with her; and in the morning
Jacob's three questions are all about the contract — *what is this you have done to me, didn't I serve
for Rachel, why have you deceived me* — and **none of them is about her.** She does not appear again
until 29:31.

**The book that recorded Rebekah's *"I will go"* records nothing at all here.** Whether she was an
accomplice, an instrument, or a girl with no more say in it than the slave who came with her, Genesis
does not say, and the wiki does not fill the silence. See
[[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]].

*"He loved also Rachel more than Leah"* (29:30). **The narrator says it in his own voice**, and the
very next verse turns the comparative into a flat word: *"Yahweh saw that Leah was hated."* **The
first thing God is said to see in the Jacob cycle is her.** See [[Love]] and
[[Loved and Hated - Genesis 29 and Deuteronomy 21.15-17]].

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

God opens her womb and she names four sons in five verses, and **the names are a diary**: Reuben,
*see, a son* — *"Yahweh has looked at my affliction; for now my husband will love me"*; Simeon,
*heard* — *"Yahweh has heard that I am hated"*; Levi, *joined* — *"now this time will my husband be
joined to me"*; and then Judah — *"this time will I praise Yahweh"*, **the first name in the list that
asks her husband for nothing** (29:32–35). She gives Zilpah to Jacob when she stops bearing, buys a
night with him from her sister for her son's mandrakes, and bears two more sons and Dinah.

She is named second when Jacob divides his household to meet Esau and puts them furthest from danger
after the slaves (33:2). She dies before the descent into Egypt, unrecorded, and Jacob mentions her
once from his deathbed — *"there I buried Leah"* (49:31) — in the only sentence he is given about her.
**Ruth 4:11 blesses a bride to be *"like Rachel and like Leah, which two built the house of
Israel"***, and the child that blessing produces is Obed, David's grandfather, out of Judah:
**Leah's line.** See [[Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel]].

## Appears in

- [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]] — Genesis 29:1–30; introduced in half a clause about her eyes, in an
  adversative sentence whose other half is her sister. She is taken, brought, and given on a night
  the text describes in six words, and the man who wakes beside her asks three questions, none of
  them about her. **She has no line, no consent scene, and no reaction in the entire pericope**, and
  the passage ends by ranking her second

## Related people

- [[Jacob]] — her husband by substitution, who served seven years for her sister and got her
- [[Rachel]] — her younger sister, married to the same man a week later; the rivalry Leviticus 18:18
  names as the reason for its own prohibition
- [[Laban]] — her father, who gave her away without telling either party
- [[Zilpah]] — her slave, given to her at the wedding and given by her to Jacob
- [[Bilhah]] — her sister's slave
- [[Rebekah]] — her husband's mother, whose technique her father repeats on her husband
