# The Rephaim

## Overview

### Joseph asks for more — Joshua 17:14–18

Joshua names their land beside that of the Perizzites as the forest to be cleared (15). This western designation stands beside Og’s eastern remnant in Joshua 13:12. It does not establish that living Rephaim remain here, or that Og’s remnant notice covers every regional use of the name.

See [[The Children of Joseph Ask for More]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:14–18.

### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

Og is recalled as remaining from their remnant (12). The statement belongs to the retrospective account of his defeated kingdom.

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

Bashan is called the land of Rephaim (13), continuing the notice about Og in 3:11. This is a
remembered territorial designation, not a new encounter with surviving Rephaim.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### Og’s defeat recalled — Deuteronomy 3:1–11

Og is identified as the sole remaining member of the remnant in this notice (11). Read beside the
regional histories in Deuteronomy 2, the statement connects Bashan to the earlier peoples; it does
not itself settle the scope of every biblical use of the designation.

See [[The Defeat of Og Recounted]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:1–11.

A collective designation for earlier inhabitants in Deuteronomy 2:10–11, 20–21. The Emim and
Zamzummim are local names associated with this category, and the Anakim are compared with it. This
page treats the peoples in these verses, without equating them with every use of a similar term
elsewhere.

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:1–23.

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** Og is again *of the remnant of the Rephaim* (12:4), as in Deuteronomy 3:11. See [[The Kings Struck Down]].
