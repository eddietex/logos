# Pharaoh (of the oppression)

## Overview

**The king who begins the bondage — and the first man in Scripture defined by what he does not
know.** *"Now there arose a new king over Egypt, who didn't know Joseph"* (Exodus 1:8). He is never
named, here or anywhere in the Bible; *Pharaoh* is a title (Egyptian *pr-ʿȝ*, *the great house*),
so the page is qualified by the narrative he appears in, per the schema's rule for title-only
figures. **He is not the Pharaoh of the plagues**: Exodus 2:23 says *"the king of Egypt died"*
before Moses is sent back, so the king who meets Moses at 5:1 is his successor and gets his own
page when he first appears.

**He reads a blessing as a threat.** His first words — *"Behold, the people of the children of
Israel are more and mightier than we"* (1:9) — are Abimelech's to Isaac (Genesis 26:16), and they
describe in the narrator's own vocabulary what God promised the patriarchs. His plan opens with
Babel's word, *hābâ*, *come* — *"Come, let us deal wisely with them, lest they multiply"* (1:10) —
and he answers the multiplication with Babel's materials, brick and mortar. See
[[Come, Let Us - Babel's Bricks and Pharaoh's]] and
[[Mightier Than We - Isaac in Gerar and Israel in Egypt]].

**He takes three measures, and each one widens the circle of guilt**: taskmasters and forced labour
on the store-cities of [[Pithom]] and [[Rameses]] (1:11–14); a private order to the midwives to kill
the boys at birth (1:15–16); and a public decree to *"all his people"* to cast every son into the
river (1:22). **All three fail in the blessing's own words** — *"the more they afflicted them, the
more they multiplied"* (1:12) — and the second fails because two women fear God more than they fear
him. See [[Shiphrah and Puah]].

**His fear is specific, and specifically wrong.** He fears war, an alliance with Egypt's enemies,
and that Israel will *"escape out of the land"* — *ʿālâ*, *go up*, the verb of Joseph's dying
promise (Genesis 50:24). He fears the men and spares the daughters. **Every defeat he suffers in
Exodus 1–2 comes from a woman**: the midwives, Moses' mother and sister, and his own daughter, who
draws the child out of the river he made a grave.

**He is the antitype of [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]].** The earlier king welcomed Jacob's
household *unprompted*, gave them *"the best of the land"*, and was blessed by Jacob; this one
*"didn't know Joseph"* — *yādaʿ* in its sense of acknowledgment, the debt one house owes another.
The verb returns from his successor as defiance — *"I don't know Yahweh"* (5:2) — and from God as
the plagues' refrain, *"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* (7:5).

**Who he was historically is not recoverable from the text**, and the wiki does not decide it. The
two usual proposals — a native king of the dynasty that expelled the Hyksos, or a Ramesside king
because of the city name *Raamses* — are set out in
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

**Psalm 105:25 says God *"turned their heart to hate his people"***; Exodus 1 gives the king his own
reasons and does not say it. The difference is recorded on [[The Oppression in Egypt]] and not
resolved.

## Timeline / key events

- Arises over Egypt, not knowing Joseph — Exodus 1:8, [[The Oppression in Egypt]]
- Tells his people Israel is *"more and mightier than we"*; proposes to deal wisely with them —
  Exodus 1:9–10
- Sets taskmasters over Israel; Pithom and Raamses built as storage cities — Exodus 1:11–14
- Orders the Hebrew midwives to kill the sons at birth — Exodus 1:15–16
- Summons the midwives and is answered with the *vigorous* Hebrew women — Exodus 1:18–19
- Commands all his people to cast every son into the river — Exodus 1:22
- His daughter draws Moses out of the river, hires his mother as nurse, and adopts him — Exodus
  2:5–10, [[The Birth of Moses]]
- Hears that Moses has killed an Egyptian and seeks to kill him; Moses flees to Midian — Exodus 2:15,
  [[Moses Flees to Midian]]
- Dies *"in the course of those many days"*; the bondage does not end with him — Exodus 2:23

## Appears in

- [[The Oppression in Egypt]] — Exodus 1:8–22; **the three measures**, from forced labour to
  infanticide
- [[The Birth of Moses]] — Exodus 2:1–10; **present only as a title, a decree, and a father.** His
  river decree is obeyed to the letter by a Hebrew mother, defeated by an ark, and broken by his own
  daughter, who pays the mother from his house and raises the deliverer in it
- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — Exodus 2:15, 23; **his last act and his death.** He *"sought to kill
  Moses"* — the second time he has sought the life of the same Hebrew boy, and the second time he
  fails — and then *"the king of Egypt died"*. **Israel's groaning goes on after him**, which is the
  narrator's point in reporting it: the bondage was never one man's policy. Exodus 4:19 confirms it
  to Moses: *"all the men who sought your life are dead"*
