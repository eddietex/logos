# Balaam

## Overview

### Hired to curse, remembered — Deuteronomy 23:4–5

Deuteronomy gives Balaam one sentence and calls the transaction a hire: Ammon and Moab are excluded from
the assembly *"because they hired against you Balaam the son of Beor from Pethor of Mesopotamia, to
curse you"* (4). Numbers 22:7 had the elders bring *"the rewards of divination"* without the verb
*hire*. The verse recalls only the hiring; it does not mention the counsel of Peor (Numbers 31:16).
*"Nevertheless Yahweh your God wouldn't listen to Balaam; but Yahweh your God turned the curse into a
blessing to you, because Yahweh your God loved you"* (5). Nehemiah 13:2 repeats the clause. See
[[The Counsel of Balaam - Numbers 31.16 and Its Later Readings]].

See [[The Assembly and the Holy Camp]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 23:1–14; Numbers 22:7.

### His death, and the counsel of Peor — Numbers 31:1–54

Balaam is killed *with the sword* among the Midianites (8), though 24:25 had him return *to his place*;
the text does not explain how he came to be there. Moses then says the Midianite women caused Israel's
trespass at Peor *through the counsel of Balaam* (16) — the only statement in Numbers that he had a part
in it. Chapter 25 does not name him. The counsel is reported, not narrated, and the wiki keeps this
portrait beside that of the seer who would speak only *the word that God puts in my mouth* (22:38). See
[[The Counsel of Balaam - Numbers 31.16 and Its Later Readings]].

See [[The War Against Midian]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 31.

### Balaam’s last oracles — Numbers 23:27–24:25

He abandons the earlier search for enchantments, sees the tribal camp, and receives the Spirit of
God (24:1–2). His third blessing provokes dismissal, but he continues with future oracles before
departing. This inspiration is not a blanket verdict on his character, nor does the text report
accepted payment.

See [[Balaam's Last Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 23:27–24:25.


### The first two oracles — Numbers 22:36–23:26

His promised restriction becomes narrated action: Yahweh puts the words in his mouth twice (23:5,
16). He blesses Israel despite Balak’s demand, and refuses both reversal and silence (23:20, 25–26).
His wish for a righteous death (23:10) is a wish, not a verdict on his own eventual end.

See [[Balaam's First Two Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:36–23:26.


### Balaam’s donkey — Numbers 22:22–35

Balaam strikes the donkey three times without seeing the angel she sees. Once Yahweh opens his eyes, he bows, confesses sin, and offers to return. Permission to continue is renewed with his speech restricted to the given word (23–35). His confession does not explain away the tension between permission in 22:20 and anger in 22:22.

See [[Balaam's Donkey]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:22–35.

Son of [[Beor (father of Balaam)]] , summoned from [[Pethor]] by [[Balak]] to curse Israel. He
consults Yahweh twice and asserts that even a house full of wealth cannot authorize him to exceed
the divine word (8–20). The narrator reports the rewards brought by the first delegation but does
not yet say that Balaam accepted payment.
