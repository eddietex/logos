# Basemath (daughter of Elon)

## Overview

**Esau's second wife at Genesis 26:34 — and the reason the two lists of his wives cannot both be
read straight.**

> **34** When Esau was forty years old, he took as wife Judith, the daughter of Beeri the Hittite,
> and Basemath, the daughter of Elon the Hittite.
> **35** They grieved Isaac’s and Rebekah’s spirits.

***Bāśəmat*** is from ***bōśem***, **spice** or **balsam** — the aromatic resin the same word names
in the trade lists and in the Song of Songs. It is an ordinary and attractive name, and Genesis makes
nothing of it.

### The problem with her name

**Genesis names Esau's wives twice and the two lists do not agree.**

| | **Genesis 26:34; 28:9** | **Genesis 36:2–3** |
|---|---|---|
| daughter of **Elon the Hittite** | **Basemath** | **Adah** |
| daughter of **Beeri the Hittite** | Judith | — |
| daughter of Anah | — | Oholibamah |
| daughter of **Ishmael** | **Mahalath** (28:9) | **Basemath** |

**The name *Basemath* is attached to Elon's daughter in one list and to Ishmael's daughter in the
other**, and the woman Elon's daughter is called in the second list, *Adah*, appears nowhere in the
first. **This wiki therefore qualifies the page by the father rather than leaving the name bare**,
since a second Basemath is certain to need one, and it flags the divergence rather than resolving
it. See [[The Names of Esau's Wives - Genesis 26 and Genesis 36]].

**Her page is the 26:34 woman**: the second of the two Hittite marriages Esau makes at forty, whose
effect Genesis states in one sentence — *"They grieved Isaac's and Rebekah's spirits"* — and whose
consequence is Rebekah's declared reason for sending Jacob to Haran at 27:46. She does nothing, says
nothing, and is not mentioned again under this name and this father.

**What the marriage means in the book.** Abraham had made his servant swear *"that you shall not take
a wife for my son of the daughters of the Canaanites"* (24:3) and sent him four hundred miles.
**Esau, at exactly the age his father was married (25:20), takes two women of the neighbourhood on
his own initiative**, and Genesis reports it without negotiation, journey, well, prayer, or consent
scene — the whole apparatus of chapter 24 absent in one verse. See
[[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] and [[Marriage]].
