# Haran (son of Terah)

## Overview

The first son in the Bible to die before his father.

He is named three times in four verses and never again. *"Terah became the father of Abram, Nahor,
and Haran. Haran became the father of Lot. Haran died before his father Terah in the land of his
birth, in Ur of the Chaldees"* (Genesis 11:27–28). A verse later he is named once more, as the
father of [[Milcah]] and Iscah (11:29). That is the whole of him: a son, two daughters, a birthplace,
and an early grave.

**"Died before his father"** — *ʿal pənê teraḥ ʾābîw*, literally *in the face of*, or *in the
presence of*, his father Terah. The idiom means predeceased, and it is the first time Genesis has had
occasion to use it. Nine chapters of genealogy have recorded fathers outlived by their sons as the
natural order; this is where the order breaks, and the text marks it by giving the death a place —
*"in the land of his birth, in Ur of the Chaldees"* — which no death in either register receives.
The register that declined to bury nine men buries this one, in a sentence with an address in it.

**He is probably the eldest of the three brothers**, despite being listed last. His daughter is old
enough to marry his brother, and his son [[Lot]] travels with the family as an adult and is a
householder within two chapters. The chronology reinforces it: Terah is 70 when he begins fathering
(11:26) and 130 when Abram is born, if Acts 7:4 is to be squared with Genesis 11:32 and 12:4. On that
reading Haran is the firstborn, Abram is listed first because he is the one the book follows — the
same courtesy Genesis pays [[Shem]] — and the sixty-year spread explains why a nephew and a niece are
of an age with their uncles.

**His name is not the city's.** The son of Terah is *Hārān* (הָרָן, with *he*); the town the family
settles in is *Ḥārān* (חָרָן, with *ḥet*), Akkadian *Ḥarrānu*, "crossroads". English collapses them
and Hebrew never does, so the reading in which a bereaved father settles in a place named for his
dead son is an artefact of translation. It is a common misreading and worth stating plainly. See
[[Haran (city)]].

**What his death does to the story.** It is the reason Lot is in Abram's household. An orphaned
grandson is taken along when the family leaves Ur (11:31), stays with Abram after Terah dies (12:4),
and becomes the nearest thing Abram has to an heir until the promise supplies a real one — which is
why the separation at Genesis 13 and the rescue at Genesis 14 carry the weight they do. One death in
Ur sets up four chapters.
