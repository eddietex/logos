# Bela (son of Beor)

## Overview

**The first king of Edom, and a man whose father's name belongs to somebody far more famous.**

> **31** These are the kings who reigned in the land of Edom, before any king reigned over the children
> of Israel.
> **32** Bela, the son of Beor, reigned in Edom. The name of his city was Dinhabah.

**He heads the list that gives Genesis 36 its point.** Eight kings reign in Edom before Israel has
one, each succeeded by a man from a different town and a different family — **no son follows a father
anywhere in the list.** Bela's city, **Dinhabah**, is named once in Scripture and has never been
located. See [[The Descendants of Esau]] and [[Kingship and Empire]].

## The Beor coincidence

***Balaam the son of Beor*** is the foreign seer hired by Balak of Moab to curse Israel, and the
central figure of Numbers 22–24 — the man whose donkey speaks, and who blesses Israel four times
because he cannot do otherwise. **The patronymic is identical**; the names are close but distinct,
***Belaʿ*** against ***Bilʿām***; and both men belong to the same Transjordanian world in roughly the
same era of the story.

**Nothing in the Bible connects them.** Numbers puts Balaam at Pethor *"by the River"* in Aram, not in
Edom, and never calls him a king. **The similarity is most likely what it looks like — a common name
and a common patronymic in a shared onomastic pool**, of the same kind that puts a Reuel in Esau's
family and another in Midian, an Eliphaz in Edom and another in the book of Job, and a Hadad among
Ishmael's sons (25:15) and another on Edom's throne (36:35).

**It is worth recording because the Deir ʿAlla inscription** — a plaster text of about 800 BC found in
the Jordan valley — **names *Balaam son of Beor, a seer of the gods***, independently of the Bible.
**The name and patronymic were real and regional**, which makes the coincidence in Genesis 36:32 less
surprising and more interesting at once.

## Timeline / key events

- Reigns in Edom, first in the list, *"before any king reigned over the children of Israel"* —
  Genesis 36:31–32
- His city is Dinhabah, named once in Scripture and unlocated — Genesis 36:32
- Dies; [[Jobab]] of [[Bozrah]] reigns in his place — Genesis 36:33
- Copied by the Chronicler — 1 Chronicles 1:43–44
