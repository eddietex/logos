# Moab (person)

## Overview

**A man who exists in Scripture as one clause and a nation.**

> **37** The firstborn bore a son, and named him Moab. He is the father of the Moabites to this day.

That is the whole of him. He is born in a cave above the burned plain to the elder of
[[Lot's Daughters]], he is named by his mother, he is credited with a people, and he is never
mentioned again — no life, no words, no death, no descendants listed. Genesis has done this before,
with [[Canaan (son of Ham)]], who is cursed three times in three verses and is never the subject of
a verb; but Canaan at least gets eleven sons in the Table of Nations (10:15–18). **Moab gets a
sentence.**

### The name

**The Hebrew text supplies no etymology, and this is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly.**
Genesis is a book that explains names constantly — Babel from *bālal*, Zoar from *miṣʿār*, Isaac
from *ṣāḥaq* — and the very next verse explains his cousin's: *Ben ʿAmmî*, *son of my kinsman*,
which is transparent Hebrew needing no gloss. Here the narrator says only that she *"named him
Moab"* and moves on.

The traditional reading takes *Môʾāb* as *mē-ʾāb*, **from father**, which fits the scene exactly and
which the Septuagint states outright: its Genesis 19:37 has the elder daughter name him *saying,
"From my father"*, a clause the Hebrew does not have. **So the pun is either in the Hebrew and left
for the reader to hear, or it is a Greek explanation of a name whose real derivation is unknown.**
Both are held; the wiki records the LXX plus as a plus and does not decide the philology, which is
genuinely uncertain — the name is attested in Moabite and Egyptian sources with no bearing on
Genesis at all.

### What the name becomes

Everything. The word *Moab* occurs some 180 times in the Hebrew Bible, and after Genesis 19 it is
never a person: it is a country, a tableland, a language, a king, a god's people, an oracle, and a
woman from there who marries into the line of David. **The man is a hinge and nothing else** — the
narrative device by which a nation gets a father, so that Deuteronomy can call the Moabites *"the
children of Lot"* (2:9) and mean something by it.
