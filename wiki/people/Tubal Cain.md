# Tubal Cain

## Overview

Lamech's son by Zillah, and the Bible's origin of metallurgy: *"Zillah also gave birth to Tubal
Cain, the forger of every cutting instrument of brass and iron. Tubal Cain's sister was Naamah"*
(4:22).

He is described differently from his half-brothers. Jabal and Jubal are each *"the father of"* a
class of people; Tubal Cain is *"the forger of"* a class of **things**. The Hebrew is *lōṭēš*, a
sharpener or hammerer, and what he sharpens is *every cutting instrument* — which in the ancient
world means the plough point, the sickle, the axe, the chisel, and the sword, all out of the same
forge. Genesis names the capability and lets the reader supply the applications. Coming two verses
before his father's boast about killing a man, the placement is at least suggestive; the text still
says nothing.

**The metals.** WEB's *"brass and iron"* renders *neḥōšet* (copper, or the bronze alloy) and
*barzel* (iron). Historically these belong to two eras roughly a millennium apart, so 4:22 has both
technologies present in the world's seventh generation — one of several places where the
chronology of Genesis 4 does not line up with an archaeological sequence, and one that readers who
take these chapters as a differently-shaped kind of history and readers who take them strictly have
both had to address.

**The name.** *Tûbal-qayin* contains *Cain*, and *qayin* in several cognate Semitic languages is
the word for *smith* or *metalworker*. Read that way, the ancestor's name was the trade all along,
and the whole genealogy has been the story of a family of smiths — which puts a small tremor under
Genesis 4:1 and the wordplay Eve makes there. *Tubal* is also the name of a nation in the Table of
Nations (10:2) and in Ezekiel 27:13, associated with trade in *"vessels of bronze"*, so the family
name may be doing double duty as an ethnic one.

**Naamah.** She is named in the same verse and given nothing else — no husband, no child, no act.
Genealogies of this shape name women only when something depends on them, which is why the bare
mention has always attracted speculation; Genesis Rabbah makes her Noah's wife, another rabbinic
strand makes her a singer alongside Jubal, and neither is in the text. She is not the Naamah of
1 Kings 14:21. See the notes on [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]].
