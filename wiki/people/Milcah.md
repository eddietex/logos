# Milcah

## Overview

Nahor's wife, Haran's daughter, and — through a genealogy tacked onto the end of Genesis 22 — the
grandmother of Rebekah and the great-grandmother of Leah and Rachel.

She enters in the verse that pairs the two marriages: *"Abram and Nahor married wives. The name of
Abram's wife was Sarai, and the name of Nahor's wife was Milcah, the daughter of Haran who was also
the father of Iscah"* (Genesis 11:29). **Her father is named and Sarai's is not**, in the same
sentence, and that asymmetry is what has kept readers arguing about who Sarai was for two thousand
years. Milcah's own position is unambiguous and worth stating: she marries her father's brother, so
her husband [[Nahor (brother of Abram)]] is her uncle — a union the Torah will later prohibit in the
reverse direction (Leviticus 18:12–14) and not in this one, though Genesis passes no comment either
way.

**The name is royal.** *Milkâ* stands beside *melek*, **king**, and *malkâ*, **queen** — so her name
and Sarai's — see [[Sarah]] — *princess*, are both titles, in the same verse, in a family whose men are
named after towns on the Balikh. It has been noticed that *Malkatu* is a title of Ishtar and
*Šarratu* one of the moon-god Sin's consort, and that both [[Ur of the Chaldees]] and
[[Haran (city)]] were Sin's principal cult centres; that is a real observation about the onomastics
and a very long way from being an argument about what these women were. Genesis makes nothing of it.

**She stays behind.** When Terah takes the family out of Ur (11:31), Nahor and Milcah are not on the
list. Genesis returns to her once, at 22:20–24, in a notice that reads like an afterthought and is
placed with precision: immediately after the binding of Isaac, at the moment Isaac needs a wife.
*"Milcah, she also has borne children to your brother Nahor: Uz his firstborn, Buz his brother,
Kemuel the father of Aram … and Bethuel."* And then: *"Bethuel became the father of Rebekah."* Eight
sons, and the eighth is the one the story needs.

Her line is therefore the maternal line of Israel. Rebekah is her granddaughter; Laban is her
great-grandson; Leah and Rachel are her great-great-granddaughters. The branch that did not go to
Canaan supplies the women of the branch that did, for three generations running.

She is mentioned once more in the Bible in a different sense entirely — Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah,
**Milcah**, and Tirzah, the five daughters of Zelophehad (Numbers 26:33; 27:1; 36:11), who are not
related to her and whose case establishes daughters' inheritance rights in Israelite law.
