# Pharaoh (of the Exodus)

## Overview

**The king of Egypt to whom Moses is sent — and whose refusal God announces before the first request is
made.** He is not [[Pharaoh (of the oppression)]], who *"died"* in the course of Moses' years in
Midian (Exodus 2:23); Exodus gives neither man a personal name, and the wiki keys the page to the
narrative rather than to a dynasty. *Pharaoh* is a title — Egyptian *pr-ʿȝ*, *the great house*. The
identification with any known king (Ramesses II is the most often proposed, Thutmose III or
Amenhotep II on an earlier chronology) is not recoverable from Scripture, and the wiki adopts none.
This page grows as Exodus 5–14 is ingested.

**He first appears only in God's speech.** *"I will send you to Pharaoh"* (3:10); *"Who am I, that I
should go to Pharaoh?"* (3:11). And then the forecast: *"I know that the king of Egypt won't give you
permission to go, no, not by a mighty hand. I will reach out my hand and strike Egypt with all my
wonders which I will do among them, and after that he will let you go"* (3:19–20). **The request for a
three days' journey is to be made to a king whose refusal is already known.** The Hebrew of 3:19 can
also be read *unless by a mighty hand*, as the Septuagint and many versions take it; both readings are
recorded on [[The Burning Bush]].

**The contest is named before it begins.** God sends Moses so that Pharaoh will send Israel — *šālaḥ*
both times — and Moses, *"heavy of mouth"* (*kəbad-peh*, 4:10), will face a king whose heart is
*heavy* (*kābēd*, 7:14).

**His hardening and his loss are both announced before he appears.** On Moses' road back, God says
*"I will harden his heart and he will not let the people go"* (4:21) — *ʾăḥazzēq*, *I will make
strong* — the first hardening statement in Exodus; and gives Moses the message that will end the
contest: *"Israel is my son, my firstborn … Let my son go, that he may serve me; and you have refused
to let him go. Behold, I will kill your son, your firstborn"* (4:22–23). **The refusal is put in the
past tense before it has happened, and the tenth plague is announced before the first.** See
[[Moses Returns to Egypt]] and [[Hardening of the Heart]].

**His first words are a question, and the rest of his story answers it.** *"Who is Yahweh, that I
should listen to his voice to let Israel go? I don't know Yahweh, and moreover I will not let Israel
go"* (5:2) — *lōʾ yādaʿtî ʾet-YHWH*, the construction of his predecessor's *"who didn't know Joseph"*
(1:8). He hears the request for a feast as a labour stoppage — *"you make them rest"*, ***hišbattem***,
the Sabbath verb (5:5) — and that same day withdraws the straw and keeps the quota (5:6–9). **He
calls the request idleness twice** (*"You are idle! You are idle!"*, 5:17) **and the word of Yahweh
*"lying words"*** (5:9). His orders go out as *kōh ʾāmar parʿōh*, *"This is what Pharaoh says"* (5:10)
— the messenger formula Moses had just used for Yahweh. **Yahweh's reply is about him, not to him**:
*"Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh, for by a strong hand he shall let them go"* (6:1). See
[[Bricks Without Straw]] and
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

**In 6:2–13 he is the destination of a command he does not hear.** God's answer to his *"I don't know
Yahweh"* (5:2) is spoken to Israel: *"you shall know that I am Yahweh your God"* (6:7). It promises
deliverance *"from under the burdens of the Egyptians"*, which answers his *"Get back to your
burdens!"* (5:4), and judgment through *"great judgments"* (6:6). Moses doubts that Pharaoh will listen
when Israel has not (6:12). See [[I Am Yahweh]].

**In 6:28–7:13 he asks for a wonder and is given one.** God foretells it: *"When Pharaoh speaks to
you, saying, 'Perform a miracle!'"* (7:9) — **the only sign in the contest that he requests.**
Aaron's rod becomes a *tannîn* before him and his servants; he calls his *"wise men and the
sorcerers"*, and his magicians do the same; and *"Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods"* (7:10–12).
**He draws no conclusion.** *"Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he didn't listen to them; as Yahweh
had spoken"* (7:13) — the qal of *ḥāzaq*, with no agent named. **Everything God said about him at
7:3–4 — the hardening, the not listening — comes true in one verse**, and the stated purpose of
what follows is that *"the Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* (7:5). In later Scripture the
*tannîn* becomes his emblem: Ezekiel 29:3 calls a Pharaoh *"the great monster that lies in the
middle of his rivers"* (WEB). See [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]].

**At the first plague he is met at his own habit.** He *"goes out to the water"* in the morning, and
Moses is waiting on the bank (7:15). God has called his heart *kābēd*, *heavy* (7:14) — the root of the
*"heavier work"* he laid on Israel (5:9). He is told to his face, *"In this you shall know that I am
Yahweh"* and *"until now you haven't listened"* (7:16–17). The river is struck *"in the sight of
Pharaoh"*; his magicians do the same; his heart is *ḥāzaq*, *"as Yahweh had spoken"*; and ***"Pharaoh
turned and went into his house, and he didn't even take this to heart"*** (7:23) — *lōʾ-šāt libbô
gam-lāzōʾt*, the *zōʾt*, *this*, of God's *"In this you shall know"*. **His people dig for water; he
goes home.** He asks for nothing, and the plague runs seven days. See [[Water Turned to Blood]].

**At the frogs he asks for the first time, and hardens his own heart for the first time.** The king
who said *"I don't know Yahweh"* (5:2) says ***"Entreat Yahweh"*** and promises, *"I will let the people
go"* (8:8). Offered the choice of hour, he says *"Tomorrow"* (8:10). When he sees *"a respite"*, ***"he
hardened his heart"*** (8:15) — *wəhakbēd ʾet-libbô*, the hiphil of *kābēd*: **the first time Exodus
makes him the subject of his own hardening**, though the narrator still adds *"as Yahweh had spoken"*.
His magicians tell him *"This is God's finger"* (8:19), and he does not listen. **At the flies he
bargains twice** — *"sacrifice to your God in the land"* (8:25), then the wilderness, *"only you shall not
go very far away. Pray for me"* (8:28) — the first of the offers that each keep something back
(10:8–11, 24–26). Moses warns him not to *"deal deceitfully"* (*tālal*, the verb of Laban's changed
wages, Genesis 31:7), and he does: ***"Pharaoh hardened his heart this time also"*** (8:32), with no
*"as Yahweh had spoken"* at all. See [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]].

**At the livestock, the boils, and the hail he is told why he is still alive, and he confesses for
the first time.** After the pestilence he sends to check, and finds *"not so much as one"* of Israel's
herds dead, and still *"the heart of Pharaoh was stubborn"* (9:7). **After the boils, for the first
time, the narrator says Yahweh did it**: ***"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"*** (9:12). Then
God explains himself to Pharaoh: he could have been *"cut off from the earth"* with the pestilence,
***"but indeed for this cause I have made you stand: to show you my power, and that my name may be
declared throughout all the earth"*** (9:15–16), the verse Paul quotes in Romans 9:17. He *"still
exalts himself"* against God's people (9:17). His own court splits at the warning of hail: *"those
who feared Yahweh's word among the servants of Pharaoh"* take cover (9:20). **When the hail falls he
confesses**: ***"I have sinned this time. Yahweh is righteous, and I and my people are wicked"***
(9:27), the terms of Abraham's plea at Sodom (Genesis 18:23–25). He asks for prayer and promises
release a third time (9:28). Moses answers, *"I know that you don't yet fear Yahweh God"* (9:30). When
the storm stops, ***"he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his servants"*** (9:34). See
[[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]] and [[For This Very Purpose - Exodus 9.16 in Romans 9.17]].

**At the locusts and the darkness he is overruled by his own court, confesses again, and threatens
Moses with death.** God says *"I have hardened his heart, and the heart of his servants"* (10:1), and
asks him through Moses, ***"How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me?"*** (10:3) — *ʿānâ*,
the root of the affliction Egypt laid on Israel (1:11–12). Moses walks out on him (10:6). **Then his
servants speak to him for the first time**: *"How long will this man be a snare to us? … Don't you yet
know that Egypt is destroyed?"* (10:7). He calls Moses back and asks *"who are those who will go?"*
(10:8); told *everyone*, he answers with a taunt in the form of a blessing — *"Yahweh be with you if I
will let you go with your little ones!"* — and offers the men only, and has Moses and Aaron *"driven
out"* (10:10–11). **After the locusts he confesses a second time, more fully than the first**: ***"I have
sinned against Yahweh your God, and against you. Now therefore please forgive my sin again"***
(10:16–17) — *śāʾ nāʾ ḥaṭṭāʾtî*, the words of Joseph's brothers to Joseph (Genesis 50:17, WLC) — and
calls the locusts *"this death"*. **Every hardening in the chapter is Yahweh's** (10:1, 20, 27). After
three days of darkness he makes his last offer — everyone, but *"let your flocks and your herds stay
behind"* (10:24) — and when Moses refuses *"a hoof"*, ***"Get away from me! Be careful to see my face
no more; for in the day you see my face you shall die!"*** (10:28). See [[Locusts and Darkness]] and
[[Our Little Ones Stayed in Goshen - Genesis 50.8 and the Bargaining of Exodus 10]].

**At the last announcement he says nothing.** Moses tells him that *"About midnight"* Yahweh will go
out into Egypt and *"all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who
sits on his throne"* (11:4–5) — **his own heir, the price named at 4:23** — and that *"All these
servants of yours will come down to me, and bow down themselves to me"* (11:8). He is not given a reply.
God says once more *"Pharaoh won't listen to you"* (11:9, the words of 7:4), and the narrator closes the
cycle: *"Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he didn't let the children of Israel go out of his
land"* (11:10). See [[The Last Plague Announced]].

**At midnight his own firstborn dies, and he gives up everything.** *"From the firstborn of Pharaoh who
sat on his throne"* (12:29) — the price named at 4:23 paid. ***"Pharaoh rose up in the night, he, and all
his servants"***, and there is a great cry in Egypt (12:30). **He summons Moses and Aaron by night** — the
text does not say he saw their faces, after *"in the day you see my face you shall die"* (10:28) — and
withdraws every condition he has set since chapter 8: *"Rise up, get out from among my people, both you and
the children of Israel; and go, serve Yahweh, as you have said! Take both your flocks and your herds, as
you have said"* (12:31–32). **The bargaining of 8:25–28 and 10:8–11, 24 ends in Moses' own terms**,
quoted back to him twice. ***"And bless me also!"*** — **the king who asked *"Who is Yahweh?"* (5:2) asks
to be blessed by Yahweh's servants**, as an earlier Pharaoh was blessed unasked by Jacob (Genesis 47:7,
10). No reply is recorded. See [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]] and
[[Jacob Blessed Pharaoh - The Lesser Blessed by the Greater]].

**The day after, he becomes a line in Israel's catechism.** The son who asks *"What is this?"* is to be told
*"When Pharaoh stubbornly refused to let us go, Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt"*
(13:15). ***Hiqšâ*, *made hard*, is the verb of God's forecast *"I will harden (ʾaqšeh) Pharaoh's
heart"*** (7:3) — its only other use in Exodus (WLC lemma search). **In the forecast God is the subject;
in the catechism Pharaoh is.** The two descriptions stand fifteen verses apart in the father's mouth and
God's, and the text does not reconcile them. See [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] and
[[Hardening of the Heart]].

### What is ahead

The pursuit, and the change of heart — *"What is this we have done, that we have let Israel go from
serving us?"* (14:5) — and the sea. These are written as
their pericopes arrive.

## Timeline / key events

- Named as the king to whom Moses is sent; his refusal foretold, and his compulsion by God's hand —
  Exodus 3:10–11, 18–20, [[The Burning Bush]]
- His heart's hardening foretold; the death of his firstborn announced as the price of refusing God's
  firstborn — Exodus 4:21–23, [[Moses Returns to Egypt]]
- Refuses Moses and Aaron: *"Who is Yahweh? … I don't know Yahweh"*; withdraws the straw and keeps the
  quota; calls the people idle — Exodus 5:1–18, [[Bricks Without Straw]]
- His compulsion foretold: *"by a strong hand he shall let them go"* — Exodus 6:1
- The object of a renewed command: *"Go in, speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt, that he let the children of
  Israel go out of his land"* — Exodus 6:10–13, [[I Am Yahweh]]
- Asks for a wonder; sees Aaron's rod become a *tannîn* and swallow his magicians' rods; his heart
  hardened, *"as Yahweh had spoken"* — Exodus 7:8–13, [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]]
- Met at the river in the morning; told *"In this you shall know that I am Yahweh"*; watches the Nile
  turned to blood and his magicians match it; goes into his house and *"didn't even take this to
  heart"* — Exodus 7:14–25, [[Water Turned to Blood]]
- Asks Moses to entreat Yahweh and promises release; chooses *"Tomorrow"*; hardens his heart at the
  respite; offers sacrifice *"in the land"*, then *"not very far away"*; hardens his heart *"this time
  also"* — Exodus 8:1–32, [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]]
- Loses his livestock and checks Israel's; his heart hardened by Yahweh after the boils; told *"for
  this cause I have made you stand"*; confesses *"I have sinned this time"* under the hail; promises
  release a third time; *"sinned yet more"* — Exodus 9:1–35, [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]
- Told by his servants *"Egypt is destroyed"*; offers the men only, then everyone but the herds;
  confesses *"I have sinned against Yahweh your God, and against you"* and asks forgiveness; his heart
  hardened by Yahweh twice; threatens Moses with death — Exodus 10:1–29, [[Locusts and Darkness]]
- Told that his firstborn, the heir *"who sits on his throne"*, will die at midnight with all the
  firstborn of Egypt, and that his servants will bow to Moses; his heart hardened by Yahweh — Exodus
  11:1–10, [[The Last Plague Announced]]
- His firstborn struck at midnight; rises in the night; summons Moses and Aaron and releases the people,
  their children, and their herds *"as you have said"*; asks *"bless me also!"* — Exodus 12:29–32,
  [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]]
- Remembered in the son's catechism as the one who *"stubbornly refused to let us go"* — Exodus 13:15,
  [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]]

## Appears in

- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:10–11, 18–20; **present only in God's forecast.** The king who
  *"won't give you permission to go"* until Egypt is struck
- [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] — Exodus 4:21–23; **still only in God's speech.** *"I will harden his
  heart"*; *"Israel is my son, my firstborn"*; *"I will kill your son, your firstborn"*
- [[Bricks Without Straw]] — Exodus 5:1–6:1; **on stage at last.** *"Who is Yahweh?"*; the bricks
  without straw; *"You are idle!"*; and God's *"Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh"*
- [[I Am Yahweh]] — Exodus 6:6–13; **offstage, and answered.** *"Great judgments"*; *"from under the
  burdens"*; and Moses' *"How then shall Pharaoh listen to me?"*
- [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]] — Exodus 6:27; **the audience that defines them.** Moses and Aaron are *"those
  who spoke to Pharaoh king of Egypt"*, *hamədabbərîm*, a participle for what has barely begun
- [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] — Exodus 7:1–13; **the audience, and the first sign.** Moses
  made *"as God"* to him; *"Perform a miracle!"*; his magicians matched and swallowed; *"he didn't
  listen to them"*

  this time"*; *"he sinned yet more"*

  his throne"*; *"All these servants of yours will come down to me"*; *"Pharaoh won't listen to you"*
- [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]] — Exodus 12:29–32; **bereaved, and conceding everything.** *"The firstborn of Pharaoh who
