# Pharaoh (of the Exodus)

## Overview

**The king of Egypt to whom Moses is sent — and whose refusal God announces before the first request is
made.** He is not [[Pharaoh (of the oppression)]], who *"died"* in the course of Moses' years in
Midian (Exodus 2:23); Exodus gives neither man a personal name, and the wiki keys the page to the
narrative rather than to a dynasty. *Pharaoh* is a title — Egyptian *pr-ʿȝ*, *the great house*. The
identification with any known king (Ramesses II is the most often proposed, Thutmose III or
Amenhotep II on an earlier chronology) is not recoverable from Scripture, and the wiki adopts none.
This page grows as Exodus 5–14 is ingested.

**He first appears only in God's speech.** *"I will send you to Pharaoh"* (3:10); *"Who am I, that I
should go to Pharaoh?"* (3:11). And then the forecast: *"I know that the king of Egypt won't give you
permission to go, no, not by a mighty hand. I will reach out my hand and strike Egypt with all my
wonders which I will do among them, and after that he will let you go"* (3:19–20). **The request for a
three days' journey is to be made to a king whose refusal is already known.** The Hebrew of 3:19 can
also be read *unless by a mighty hand*, as the Septuagint and many versions take it; both readings are
recorded on [[The Burning Bush]].

**The contest is named before it begins.** God sends Moses so that Pharaoh will send Israel — *šālaḥ*
both times — and Moses, *"heavy of mouth"* (*kəbad-peh*, 4:10), will face a king whose heart is
*heavy* (*kābēd*, 7:14).

**His hardening and his loss are both announced before he appears.** On Moses' road back, God says
*"I will harden his heart and he will not let the people go"* (4:21) — *ʾăḥazzēq*, *I will make
strong* — the first hardening statement in Exodus; and gives Moses the message that will end the
contest: *"Israel is my son, my firstborn … Let my son go, that he may serve me; and you have refused
to let him go. Behold, I will kill your son, your firstborn"* (4:22–23). **The refusal is put in the
past tense before it has happened, and the tenth plague is announced before the first.** See
[[Moses Returns to Egypt]] and [[Hardening of the Heart]].
