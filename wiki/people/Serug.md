# Serug

## Overview

Abraham's great-grandfather, and the clearest case in the register of a personal name that is also a
town.

*"Serug lived thirty years, and became the father of Nahor. Serug lived two hundred years after he
became the father of Nahor"* (Genesis 11:22–23) — two hundred thirty years in all. That is his whole
appearance in Genesis; he recurs at 1 Chronicles 1:26 and, as *Saruch*, at Luke 3:35.

**Sarugi.** Assyrian records name a town *Sarugi* in the Balikh valley, a short journey west of
[[Haran (city)]]; the site is modern Suruç, in southeastern Turkey. It is one of four names in this
register — with Nahor, Terah, and less certainly Reu — that match places in the small district the
family will eventually settle in. Whether that means the genealogy preserves the memory of an
Amorite clan whose ancestors gave their names to, or took them from, the towns of its home region,
or whether personal names and place names there simply drew on the same stock of words, is not
decidable from the evidence. It is, either way, one of the few points where Genesis 11 touches
something attested outside the Bible.

**The Hebrew name** *Śərûg* is usually connected to *śārag*, *to intertwine* or *entwine* — a rare
root, used of tangled roots and interlaced branches (Job 40:17 uses a related form). Genesis offers
no etymology and makes no play on it.

Later tradition gave him a career Genesis does not: the *Book of Jubilees* and Syriac chroniclers
make him the generation in which idolatry was invented or consolidated, which is an inference from
Joshua 24:2's report that Abraham's fathers *"served other gods"* rather than from anything in this
chapter. See [[Out of Ur - Terah's Journey and Abram's Call]].
