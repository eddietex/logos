# Gideon

## Overview

Gideon son of Joash, of the clan of Abiezer in Manasseh, is the fifth deliverer of Judges and the first
whose call is narrated. He enters the book hiding. Midian has taken the harvest for seven years, and he
is *beating out wheat in the wine press, to hide it from the Midianites* (6:11) when Yahweh's angel
sits under his father's oak and greets him: *Yahweh is with you, you mighty man of valor!* (6:12).

His first words are a complaint, not an answer. *If Yahweh is with us, why then has all this happened
to us? Where are all his wondrous works which our fathers told us of?* (6:13). He knows the exodus as
something his fathers *told*, and he concludes that Yahweh *has cast us off*. Sent to *save Israel*,
he objects with his smallness, *my family is the poorest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my
father's house* (6:15). He receives the promise Moses received at the bush, *Surely I will be with
you* (6:16; Exodus 3:12). See [[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]].

He asks for a sign, and the sign is fire from the rock that consumes his offering (6:17–21). When he
realizes who has spoken with him, he expects to die: *I have seen Yahweh's angel face to face!* He is
told *Peace be to you … You shall not die*, and builds an altar he names *Yahweh is Peace* (6:22–24).

The pattern of his call recurs through his story. He obeys, but at night and afraid (6:27). He asks for
the fleece twice (6:36–40). Before the battle he is given the dream of the barley loaf (7:9–15). The
judge who most needs reassurance is the one Scripture gives the most signs. Hebrews 11:32 names him
first among the judges in its list of those of faith; see [[Faith]].

His first task is at home. *That same night* Yahweh tells him to throw down *the altar of Baal that
your father has*, cut down the Asherah beside it, and offer his father's second bull on a new altar to
Yahweh, burned with the Asherah's wood (6:25–26). He does it with ten of his servants, by night,
*because he feared his father's household and the men of the city* (6:27). It is the first time in
Judges that anyone obeys the law's *you shall break down their altars* (Exodus 34:13; Judges 2:2), and
the altar he breaks belongs to Israelites. In the morning the town demands his death: *Bring out your
son, that he may die* (6:30). His father refuses. Joash says that a god should defend himself, and names
his son *Jerub-Baal*, *Let Baal contend against him, because he has broken down his altar* (6:32). From
then on the narrator uses both names: *Jerubbaal, who is Gideon* (7:1). See
[[Gideon Throws Down Baal's Altar]] and
[[Break Down Their Altars - Exodus 34.13, Deuteronomy 7.5, Judges 2.2, and Judges 6.25-32]].

When Midian, Amalek, and the children of the east gather in the valley of Jezreel, *Yahweh's Spirit came
on Gideon* (6:34). In Hebrew the Spirit *clothed* him. He blows the ram's horn, and Abiezer, the clan
whose town had demanded his death, follows him first; then all Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali
(6:34–35). With the army gathered he asks for a sign. He asks God to *save Israel by my hand, as you have
spoken*: dew on a fleece on the threshing floor and the ground dry (6:36–38). Then he asks for the
reverse. He begins the second request with Abraham's words at Sodom, *Don't let your anger be kindled
against me, and I will speak but this once*, and calls it *a trial*, the verb Judges had used for God
testing Israel (6:39). God does both and says nothing (6:40). The text does not judge the request.
Readers have taken it both as a fault and as a weak man's honest request, and the wiki records both
readings. See [[The Fleece]] and [[Don't Let Your Anger Be Kindled - Genesis 18.30-32 and Judges 6.39]].

The next morning *Jerubbaal, who is Gideon*, camps at the spring of Harod with Midian north of him by
the hill of Moreh (7:1). Then Yahweh speaks again, for the first time since the altar. The army is *too
many*, *lest Israel brag against me, saying, "My own hand has saved me"* (7:2). Gideon had just asked
twice that God *save Israel by my hand*. He proclaims the law of war's dismissal, *Whoever is fearful
and trembling, let him return* (7:3; Deuteronomy 20:8), and twenty-two thousand go. He brings the ten
thousand to the water, and Yahweh keeps the three hundred who lapped (7:4–7). Gideon says nothing in
the whole scene. He does what he is told. The man who sends the fearful home is himself afraid
(6:27), and three verses later Yahweh says to him *if you are afraid to go down* (7:10), with the same
Hebrew word as the proclamation. He is not sent home. The promise is *By the three hundred men … will
I save you, and deliver the Midianites into your hand* (7:7). See [[Gideon's Three Hundred]] and
[[My Hand Has Saved Me - Deuteronomy 8.17 and Judges 7.2]].

*That same night* Yahweh sends him down to the enemy camp with a promise in the perfect tense, *I have
delivered it into your hand*, and an offer: *But if you are afraid to go down, go with Purah your servant*
(7:9–10). *If you are afraid* uses the same word as the proclamation that sent twenty-two thousand home.
He goes with Purah (7:11). The text never says he was afraid, but he takes the option given only to a
man who was. At the edge of the camp he overhears a Midianite tell a dream of a barley loaf that knocks a
tent flat, and another interpret it: *This is nothing other than the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a
man of Israel* (7:13–14). The enemy knows his name. It is the only sign in his story that he did not ask
for, and the only one he hears rather than sees. He worships. It is the only time in Judges the verb is
used of worshiping Yahweh. Then he goes back and says Yahweh's own sentence to his men: *Arise; for
Yahweh has delivered the army of Midian into your hand!* (7:15). See [[The Dream of the Barley Loaf]]
and [[Spies Who Hear the Enemy's Fear - Joshua 2 and Judges 7]].

He divides the three hundred into three companies and arms them with horns, empty jars, and torches.
No sword is handed out (7:16). *Watch me, and do likewise*, he tells them (7:17). The man who asked for
signs is now the sign his men follow. He writes the shout himself, *For Yahweh and for Gideon!* (7:18),
and the army makes it *The sword of Yahweh and of Gideon!* (7:20), taking *sword* from the Midianite's
interpretation (7:14). It puts his name beside Yahweh's in the battle Yahweh had cut down *lest Israel
brag against me* (7:2). The text does not comment. At the middle watch the horns sound, the jars
break, and Israel *stood in his place* while *Yahweh set every man's sword against his fellow* (7:21–22).
Gideon's own part after that is to summon: the tribes pursue, and Ephraim, called late, takes the
waters and the princes Oreb and Zeeb, whose heads are brought to him (7:23–25). See
[[The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon]] and
[[Horns, a Shout, and a Fallen Enemy - Jericho and the Camp of Midian]].

After the battle the story follows Gideon, and Yahweh does not speak to him again in Judges 8. Ephraim
*contends* with him, *wayrîbûn*, the verb of his name *Jerub-Baal*, because it was not called to the
fight (8:1). He answers softly: *Isn't the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of
Abiezer?*, and gives the victory to God, and *their anger was abated* (8:2–3). Then he crosses the Jordan
with the three hundred, *faint, yet pursuing* (8:4), after *Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian*.
Succoth and Penuel refuse his men bread, and he threatens both (8:5–9). He takes the kings at Karkor
(8:10–12), has a young man of Succoth write down its seventy-seven princes and elders, *taught* the
elders *with thorns of the wilderness and briers*, breaks down Penuel's tower, and kills its men, which he
had not threatened (8:14–17). Then the reader learns that the kings had killed his brothers at Tabor.
*They were my brothers, the sons of my mother. As Yahweh lives, if you had saved them alive, I would not
kill you* (8:19). His firstborn, Jether, is too afraid to strike, and Gideon kills the kings himself and
takes the crescents from their camels (8:20–21). The man who was afraid (6:27; 7:10) now has an afraid
son. The text does not judge him. It shows a soft answer to a strong tribe and hard ones to small towns,
and a war that became, at its end, his own blood feud. See [[Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel]],
[[Penuel and Succoth - Jacob's Road and Gideon's Pursuit]],
[[Three Hundred Against the Kings - Abram's Pursuit and Gideon's]], and
[[A Gentle Answer - Gideon's Reply to Ephraim and Proverbs 15.1]].

Two details sit beside his call without comment. He has *ten men of his servants* (6:27), though he
called his clan *the poorest in Manasseh* (6:15). And the man who breaks down Ophrah's Baal altar later
makes an ephod and puts it *in Ophrah, his city*, where *all Israel played the prostitute with it*
(8:27).

Source: stored WEB Judges 6:11–40; 7:1–25; 8:1–21, 27; Deuteronomy 20:8; Genesis 18:32; Exodus 3:12; 34:13; Judges 2:2.

**The end of his story (8:22–35).** Israel offers him a dynasty, *Rule over us, both you, and your son,
and your son's son also; for you have saved us*, and he refuses: *I will not rule over you, neither shall
my son rule over you. Yahweh shall rule over you* (8:22–23). Then he asks for the gold earrings of the
plunder, seventeen hundred of gold, and makes an ephod that he puts in Ophrah; *all Israel played the
prostitute with it there; and it became a snare to Gideon, and to his house* (8:24–27). The land has
rest forty years. He has *seventy sons … for he had many wives*, and a concubine in Shechem bears him a
son he names *Abimelech* (8:28–31). He dies *in a good old age*, the phrase of Abraham's death, and is
buried in his father's tomb (8:32). After him Israel goes back to the Baals, forgets Yahweh, and shows
no *kindness to the house of Jerubbaal* (8:33–35). The narrator records both the refusal and the
ephod, and judges neither aloud. See [[Gideon's Ephod and Death]],
[[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]], and
[[Golden Earrings - Shechem's Oak, Aaron's Calf, and Gideon's Ephod]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 8:22–35; Genesis 25:8; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

**His house after him (9:1–6).** Judges 9 calls him only *Jerubbaal* (WLC). His son Abimelech asks
Shechem whether *all the sons of Jerubbaal, who are seventy persons* should *rule over you, or that one
rule over you* (9:2), with the *māšal* of Gideon's refusal. Then he kills the seventy *on one stone*
*at his father's house at Ophrah*, all but Jotham (9:5), and is made king (9:6). The *snare … to his
house* (8:27) and the *kindness* not shown *to the house of Jerubbaal* (8:35) are carried out within
six verses. See [[Abimelech Made King at Shechem]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 8:27, 35; 9:1–6.

**Remembered by his son (9:16–19).** Jotham's speech from Gerizim is the only place Judges 9 tells
Gideon's story back: *my father fought for you, and risked his life, and delivered you out of the hand
of Midian* (9:17). *Risked his life* is *wayyašlēk ʾet-napšô minneged*, *threw his life away in front*
(WLC). The man who asked for signs and went with three hundred is remembered for the risk. Jotham's
question is whether Shechem has *done to him according to the deserving of his hands* (9:16). The
good trees of the fable, who will not leave their fruit to be king, speak close to Gideon's *I will not
rule over you* (8:23), though Jotham does not say that any tree is Gideon. See [[Jotham's Fable]].
Source: stored WEB Judges 9:7–21; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

**Repaid for his house (9:22–57).** Gideon's name runs through his son's fall. Gaal asks *Isn't he the
son of Jerubbaal?* (9:28). Abimelech's methods echo his father's: three companies (7:16; 9:43), *do as I
have done* (7:17; 9:48), and a tower broken down with *nātaṣ* (8:17; 9:45) (WLC). The narrator counts the
killing of the seventy as a wrong done to Gideon: *God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech, which he did
to his father* (9:56), and the chapter's last words name *Jotham the son of Jerubbaal* (9:57). See
[[The Fall of Abimelech]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 7:16–17; 8:17; 9:28, 43–57.

Samuel's farewell names him by his other name, first among the deliverers: *Yahweh sent Jerubbaal, Bedan,
Jephthah, and Samuel* (1 Samuel 12:11, stored WEB). See [[Samuel's Farewell Address]] and [[Bedan]].

## Timeline / key events

- Judges 6:11 — threshing wheat in a winepress at Ophrah, hiding from Midian.
- Judges 6:12–16 — greeted, commissioned, objects, promised *I will be with you*.
- Judges 6:17–21 — asks a sign; his offering consumed by fire from the rock.
- Judges 6:22–24 — *face to face*; *You shall not die*; builds the altar *Yahweh is Peace*.
- Judges 6:25–28 — throws down his father's Baal altar and cuts down the Asherah by night; offers the
  second bull on a new altar.
- Judges 6:29–32 — the town demands his death; Joash defends him and names him *Jerub-Baal*.
- Judges 6:33–35 — clothed by the Spirit; blows the horn; Abiezer, Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali
  muster.
- Judges 6:36–40 — asks for the fleece, wet and then dry; God grants both.
- Judges 7:1–8 — camps at the spring of Harod; sends home the fearful, twenty-two thousand, then all but
  the three hundred who lapped.
- Judges 7:9–15 — sent down to the camp by night, *if you are afraid*, with Purah; hears the dream of the
  barley loaf and its interpretation; worships, and tells the three hundred *Arise*.
- Judges 7:16–25 — horns, jars, and torches; *For Yahweh and for Gideon!*; the camp routed by its own
  swords; summons the tribes and Ephraim; receives the heads of Oreb and Zeeb.
- Judges 8:1–3 — answers Ephraim's complaint; *their anger was abated*.
- Judges 8:4–9 — crosses the Jordan, *faint, yet pursuing*; refused bread by Succoth and Penuel; threatens
  both.
- Judges 8:10–12 — strikes the camp at Karkor; takes Zebah and Zalmunna.
- Judges 8:13–17 — punishes the elders of Succoth; breaks down Penuel's tower and kills its men.
- Judges 8:18–21 — learns the kings killed his brothers at Tabor; *As Yahweh lives*; Jether afraid; kills
  the kings and takes the crescents.
- Judges 8:22–23 — offered hereditary rule; *Yahweh shall rule over you*.
- Judges 8:24–27 — asks for the earrings; makes an ephod and puts it in Ophrah; it becomes *a snare*.
- Judges 8:28–31 — forty years' rest; seventy sons and many wives; Abimelech born in Shechem.
- Judges 8:32 — dies *in a good old age*; buried in the tomb of Joash in Ophrah.
- Judges 8:33–35 — Israel returns to the Baals and shows his house no kindness.
- Judges 9:1–6 — as *Jerubbaal*: his seventy sons killed at his house in Ophrah by Abimelech; Jotham survives.
- Judges 9:16–19 — remembered by Jotham: *my father fought for you, and risked his life*.
- Named as *Jerubbaal* among the deliverers in Samuel's farewell — 1 Samuel 12:11.
