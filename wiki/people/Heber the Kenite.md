# Heber the Kenite

## Overview

### The house at peace with Jabin — Judges 4:17, 21

The reason for 4:11 arrives: *there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the
Kenite* (4:17), so Sisera runs to that house. The tent he reaches is called *the tent of Jael the wife
of Heber*, and the narrator repeats *Jael Heber's wife* at the moment she kills him (4:21). Heber never
appears in person and is never said to know what happened. Whether the peace was his own policy, and
whether his wife was bound by it, the text does not say. A household related to Moses' family, at
peace with Israel's oppressor, is where the oppressor's general dies. See [[Jael]] and
[[Jael and Sisera]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 4:17–24.

*Now Heber the Kenite had separated himself from the Kenites, even from the children of Hobab, Moses'
brother-in-law, and had pitched his tent as far as the oak in Zaanannim, which is by Kedesh* (Judges
4:11). The verse interrupts the muster and has no consequence within 4:1–16. It places a tent near
Kedesh before the battle, and it ties the household to Hobab and so to Moses' family by marriage.

The verb *nipərād*, *separated himself* (WLC), says he had left the Kenites who went up with Judah to
the south of Arad (1:16). The next pericope adds that *there was peace between Jabin the king of
Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite* (4:17), and that Sisera flees to the tent of Heber's wife,
Jael. A family related to Moses and at peace with Israel's oppressor: Judges states both facts and
does not reconcile them.

Not the Heber of Asher's line (Genesis 46:17), who has the same name in Hebrew (WLC lemma search);
the texts do not connect them.

Source: stored WEB Judges 1:16; 4:11, 17; Genesis 46:17; Hebrew WLC.
