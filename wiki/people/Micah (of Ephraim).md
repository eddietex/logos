# Micah (of Ephraim)

## Overview

*A man of the hill country of Ephraim, whose name was Micah* (Judges 17:1, stored WEB). He opens the
book's epilogue (Judges 17–21), the first chapters with no judge in them. He takes his mother's *eleven
hundred pieces of silver*, hears her curse the thief, confesses *I took it*, and is blessed by her in
Yahweh's name (17:2). From the silver she has *a carved image and a molten image* made, and it stands
*in the house of Micah* (17:3–4). He has *a house of gods*, makes *an ephod, and teraphim*, and fills the
hand of one of his sons as priest (17:5). When a young Levite of Bethlehem Judah passes, he hires him for
*ten pieces of silver per year, a suit of clothing, and your food*, and says, *Now know I that Yahweh will
do good to me, since I have a Levite as my priest* (17:10, 13). See [[Micah's Idol and Levite]].

**The name.** The narrator calls him *Mîkāyəhû* in 17:1 and 4, and *Mîkâ* from 17:5 on, through chapter 18
(WLC). The long form carries Yahweh's name and is usually read *who is like Yahweh?*; that meaning is
reported, not argued here. The shortening comes as the image enters his house; whether the narrator
means it, the text does not say.

**Everything in Yahweh's name.** Micah never names another god. His shrine, image, ephod, and priest are
all set up with Yahweh's blessing invoked over them, and the chapter's verdict is not a charge of apostasy
but the refrain *Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes* (17:6). Deuteronomy 27:15 curses *the
man who makes an engraved or molten image* (stored WEB); Micah's mother had one made for him. See
[[A Carved Image and a Molten Image - Deuteronomy 27.15 and Micah's House]].

**Ahead.** In Judges 18 the Danites' spies lodge at his house, and their army takes his image, ephod,
teraphim, and Levite; he pursues them and is told to turn back (stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**The name and the book.** He is not the prophet Micah of the book of [[Micah]]; the qualifier keeps the
two apart, as the naming conventions require when a person shares a book's name.
