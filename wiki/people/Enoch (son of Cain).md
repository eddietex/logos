# Enoch (son of Cain)

Cain's firstborn, and the man the Bible's first city is named after. Not to be confused with
Enoch the son of Jared, the seventh from Adam in the line of Seth, who *"walked with God, and he
was not, for God took him"* (5:24) — a different man in a different line, and the more famous of
the two. On the shared names between the two genealogies see
[[Two Lines from Adam - Cain's Genealogy and Seth's]].

## Overview

He is given one verse and no actions: *"Cain knew his wife. She conceived, and gave birth to Enoch.
He built a city, and called the name of the city, after the name of his son, Enoch"* (4:17). Then
he becomes a link — *"To Enoch was born Irad"* — and the line moves past him toward Lamech.

What is notable about him is entirely what is done *to* him. Founders name cities after themselves;
Cain names his after his son. It is the one tender detail in the account of a man the previous
passage sentenced, and Genesis makes nothing of it. A minority reading of 4:17 takes Enoch rather
than Cain as the builder — the Hebrew pronoun is not decisive, and it is faintly odd for a father
to name a city after a child — but WEB and nearly all translations read Cain as the subject, which
is also where the irony lands: the man told he would wander builds walls. See [[Enoch (city)]].

His name, *Ḥănôk*, is connected to the root *ḥānak*, "to dedicate" or "to train up" (the noun
*ḥănukkâ*, "dedication", is from it). A dedication is what one does at the founding of a building,
which may be the point of the naming, or may be another of Genesis's sound-plays.
