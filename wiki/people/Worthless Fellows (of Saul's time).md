# Worthless Fellows (of Saul's time)

## Overview

The unnamed men who reject Saul on the day he is made king. They are qualified by period because *sons of
bəliyyaʿal* recur throughout the books stored so far (see
[[Sons of Belial - Worthless Men from Deuteronomy to Samuel]]), and this group is spoken of again at 11:12.

### *How could this man save us?* — 1 Samuel 10:27

After the people shout *Long live the king!* and Saul goes home to Gibeah with *the army … whose hearts God had
touched*, *certain worthless fellows said, "How could this man save us?" They despised him, and brought him no
present* (10:26–27). Their question uses *yāšaʿ*, *save* (*mah-yōšiʿēnû zeh*, WLC). That is the verb of Yahweh's
promise that the prince *will save my people* (9:16) and of Samuel's *your God, who himself saves you* (10:19).
Their contempt, *wayyiḇzuhû*, uses the verb of *those who despise me* in the oracle against Eli's house (2:30,
WLC). Withholding a *minḥâ*, a gift to a superior, refuses the king's rank. Saul answers with silence. See
[[Saul Chosen by Lot at Mizpah]].

### Spoken of after Jabesh — 1 Samuel 11:12–13

After the victory at Jabesh Gilead, the people ask Samuel, *Who is he who said, 'Shall Saul reign over us?' Bring
those men, that we may put them to death!* Saul refuses: *No man shall be put to death today; for today Yahweh has
rescued Israel* (11:12–13). The quoted question is not the one recorded at 10:27, *How could this man save us?*,
though it amounts to the same refusal. The rescue itself has answered 10:27 in its own verb: *təšûʿâ*, *rescue*
(11:9, 13, WLC), from *yāšaʿ*, *save*. See [[Saul Rescues Jabesh Gilead]]. The text does not say that the men of 11:12 are the same men as 10:27. The wiki treats
them as the same group, as most readers do, and flags that as a reading.
