# Peninnah

## Overview

### The rival — 1 Samuel 1:2–7

The second wife of [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]]. *Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children*
(1:2). At the yearly sacrifice Elkanah gives portions *to Peninnah his wife, and to all her sons and her
daughters* (1:4). The narrator calls her [[Hannah]]'s *rival*, *ṣārāṯāh* (1:6, WLC), the noun of the
root Leviticus 18:18 uses when it forbids marrying a wife's sister *to be a rival*. She *provoked her
severely, to irritate her, because Yahweh had shut up her womb*, and did so *year by year* (1:6–7). The
provocation is *kaʿas*, *vexation*, three times in two verses (WLC).

She never speaks. The text gives her children and her taunting, and no motive beyond the reason it names
for the taunt — Hannah's barrenness. Elkanah's love for Hannah (1:5) stands immediately before it, and the
reader may connect them, as with Leah and Rachel (Genesis 29:30–31); the text does not. She does not
appear after 1:7. See [[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]], [[The Barren Matriarchs]], and [[Envy]].

**Name.** *Pəninnâ* (WLC) is often connected with *pənînîm*, *rubies* or *pearls* (Proverbs 3:15; 31:10);
this is a conventional gloss, not verified here.
