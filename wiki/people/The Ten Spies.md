# The Ten Spies

## Overview

### Caleb’s inheritance — Joshua 14:6–15

Caleb remembers the fellow scouts who made the people’s heart melt (8), contrasting their discouragement with his own faithful report. They are recalled collectively and are not present at Gilgal; Joshua is Caleb’s addressee, not one of the opposing scouts.

See [[Caleb Claims Hebron]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:6–15; comparisons as cited.


The ten men sent with Caleb and Joshua who said *We aren't able*. The number is arrived at by
subtraction: Numbers 13 lists twelve (4–15), and the chapter never says *ten*. The page is named so
that it does not collide with the passage [[The Twelve Spies]].

**They are named once.**

By the WLC tags Gaddiel, Gaddi, Sethur, Nahbi, and Geuel occur nowhere else. The other names recur
for other people. None of the ten is one of the [[The Twelve Tribal Princes|princes]] of Numbers 1,
though verse 2 calls each sent man a *nāśîʾ*.

**What they said was not all false.** They carried the fruit and said *it flows with milk and honey*
(27). The people were strong and the cities fortified (28), and Caleb does not deny it. The narrator
calls only verses 32–33 a *dibbâ*, an evil report. It has a land that eats its people, *all* its
people huge, and Nephilim, and it adds a claim about how the Canaanites saw them, which they could
not know.

**What happened to them.** Numbers 14:36–37 says the men who *made all the congregation to murmur*
by bringing up the evil report *died by the plague before Yahweh*. The verse gives no names. They
die at once, while the rest of the generation has forty years. Joshua 14:8 has Caleb call them *my
brothers who went up with me*, and says they *made the heart of the people melt* (WEB; *not yet
ingested*). See [[The People Refuse the Land]].

**Deuteronomy does not separate them.** In Moses' retelling the twelve bring back fruit and one
verdict, *It is a good land which Yahweh our God gives to us* (Deuteronomy 1:25). The frightening
report is heard only inside the people's murmuring, as what *our brothers* said: the people taller,
the cities *fortified up to the sky*, the sons of the Anakim (1:28). There are no names and no
dissenting two in that unit; Caleb and Joshua are named only in the sentence that follows (1:36, 38).
See [[The Spies and the Refusal to Go Up]].
