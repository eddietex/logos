# Moses

## Overview

**The deliverer, lawgiver, and prophet of Israel — and, at his first appearance, a baby in a basket
whom no one in the scene but an Egyptian princess names.** He is the central human figure of
Exodus through Deuteronomy, and this page grows pericope by pericope as Exodus is ingested. What is
written below is what the text has shown so far; what is ahead is marked as ahead.

**He is born under a death sentence.** Pharaoh's third measure against Israel was to cast every
newborn son into the river (Exodus 1:22). Moses is born to a Levite couple ([[Amram]] and
[[Jochebed]], named at 6:20), hidden three months, and then put into the river exactly as the decree
required — but inside a papyrus *tēbâ*, the word for Noah's ark, sealed with tar and pitch (2:3).
His sister watches; [[Pharaoh's Daughter (of Moses' time)]] finds him, pities him, knows him for a
Hebrew, hires his own mother to nurse him, and when he is weaned adopts him: *"he became her son"*
(2:10). See [[The Birth of Moses]].

**His name is a pun that runs forward.** *"She named him Moses, and said, 'Because I drew him out of
the water'"* (2:10). The princess explains it by what was done to him (*māšâ*, *to draw out*), but
*Mōšeh* is the active participle — ***the one who draws out*** — and the man will draw Israel out of
the water of the sea. The name is usually derived from Egyptian *msy*, *born*, *son* (as in
Ahmose, Thutmose, Ramesses); on that derivation an Egyptian princess gave an Egyptian name, and the
narrator heard a Hebrew prophecy in it. See
[[Drawn Out of the Water - The Name of Moses]].

**He grows up between two households.** Nursed by his Hebrew mother, raised in Pharaoh's house,
*"instructed in all the wisdom of the Egyptians"* in Stephen's telling (Acts 7:22) — the inverse of
[[Joseph]], who came into Egypt a Hebrew slave and was made Egyptian by Pharaoh's grant. **Hebrews
makes the choice between the two houses his act of faith**: *"By faith, Moses, when he had grown up,
refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter"* (Hebrews 11:24).

**His rescue is told without God.** The birth narrative has no divine speech, no divine act, and no
mention of God at all; the deliverer is preserved entirely by women and by what looks like chance.
See [[Providence]].

**His first acts as a man are a failure.** Grown, he *"went out to his brothers, and looked at
their burdens"* (2:11) — *his brothers* twice in a verse, the narrator settling which house he
belongs to. He sees an Egyptian striking a Hebrew, **looks this way and that**, strikes the Egyptian
dead with the same verb (*nākâ*), and hides him in the sand. The next day he rebukes a Hebrew
striking his fellow and is answered: *"Who made you a prince and a judge over us? Do you plan to kill
me, as you killed the Egyptian?"* (2:14). **He has acted as a judge with no commission, and the
people he acted for refuse him.** *"Moses was afraid"*, Pharaoh seeks his life, and he flees. Exodus
does not say whether the killing was right; Stephen calls it avenging the oppressed and blames the
Hebrews who *"didn't understand"* that God was giving deliverance *"by his hand"* (Acts 7:24–25).
See [[Moses Flees to Midian]] and
[[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].

**The fear and Hebrews 11:27 stand in tension.** Exodus: *"Moses was afraid"* (2:14), and he *"fled
from the face of Pharaoh"* (2:15). Hebrews: *"By faith, he left Egypt, not fearing the wrath of the
king"*. Either Hebrews means the exodus itself (when Moses does face Pharaoh unafraid, 10:28–29), or
it means the flight and speaks of the motive beneath the fear. **The wiki records both readings and
adopts neither.**

**In Midian he repeats Jacob's exile.** He flees a threat to his life, sits down by *the* well,
drives off shepherds bullying seven sisters, and waters their flock — the third betrothal at a well
in the Torah. **The verb is *yāšaʿ*, *to save*** (2:17), the verb of the sea (14:30), and the
daughters report *"An Egyptian delivered us"* — *nāṣal*, the verb of 3:8 — so that **the first
people Moses saves in Exodus are Midianite women, and they take him for an Egyptian.** He dwells with
their father, the priest of Midian ([[Jethro]], called Reuel here), marries [[Zipporah]], and names
his son [[Gershom]]: *"I have lived as a foreigner in a foreign land"* (2:22) — the *gēr* of Genesis
15:13, said by Abraham's offspring of himself for the first time. See
[[The Betrothal at the Well]] and
[[A Foreigner in a Foreign Land - Gershom and the Law of the Stranger]].

**He is, at this point, a man who intervenes for the wronged three times** — an Egyptian against a
Hebrew, a Hebrew against a Hebrew, shepherds against women — **and only the third, done for
strangers, is rewarded.** The deliverance itself begins without him, at 2:23–25, when God hears,
remembers, sees, and knows.

**At Horeb he is called, and refuses.** Keeping Jethro's flock, he leads it *"to the back of the
wilderness"* and comes to *"God's mountain"*; he turns aside to see a bush that burns and is not
consumed, and only then does God call him — *"Moses! Moses!"* — and he answers *hinnēnî*, *"Here I am"*,
the word of Abraham at the knife and Jacob on the way to Egypt (3:1–4). Told to take off his sandals on
holy ground, **he hides his face, *"for he was afraid to look at God"*** (3:6) — the fear of 2:14 turned
the other way. Then the commission, *"I will send you to Pharaoh"* (3:10), and **five objections**:
*"Who am I?"* (3:11); *"What is his name?"* (3:13); *"They will not believe me"* (4:1); *"I am not
eloquent … I am slow of speech"* (4:10); and *"Oh, Lord, please send someone else"* (4:13). **The man
who once acted without being sent will not go now that he is.** God answers each with what he will be or
do — *ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*, *"I will be with you"*; *ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh*; three signs; *"I will be with your
mouth"* — and the last with anger and with [[Aaron]], who will be Moses' mouth as Moses will be *"to him
as God"* (4:16). **The narrator never records Moses' consent.** See [[The Burning Bush]] and
[[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]].

**His speech is the first question about him that Scripture leaves open.** *"Not a man of words"*,
*heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue* (*kəbad-peh ûkəbad lāšôn*, 4:10) — and *kābēd* is the word that
will describe Pharaoh's heart (7:14). Stephen says the reverse: *"mighty in his words and works"* (Acts
7:22). **The wiki records the tension and adopts neither standard reconciliation** (Stephen describes
the man God's presence made; or *words* means the Torah he gave, not his delivery).

**He goes back, and is nearly killed on the way.** He asks Jethro's leave to *"see whether"* his
brothers *"are still alive"*, saying nothing of the bush, and is sent *"in peace"* (4:18); God tells him
*"all the men who sought your life are dead"* (4:19) — the *bāqaš* of 2:15 — and he sets out with his
wife and sons on a donkey and *"God's rod"* in his hand (4:20). On the road he is given the end of the
contest before it starts: Pharaoh's heart will be hardened, and Pharaoh is to be told *"Israel is my
son, my firstborn … I will kill your son, your firstborn"* (4:21–23). **Then, at a lodging place,
*"Yahweh met Moses and wanted to kill him"*** — *bāqaš* again, five verses after the seekers were
declared dead — **and it is [[Zipporah]], not Moses, who acts**: she circumcises their son with a flint,
and *"he let him alone"* (4:24–26). The Hebrew names no one in 4:24, only *him*, and the wiki records
both Moses and the son as the object. **Moses says nothing in the scene.** Then Aaron meets him at the
mountain and kisses him, and before the elders it is Aaron who speaks and does the signs; *"the people
believed"* (4:27–31) — **the third objection, *"they will not believe me"* (4:1), answered at the first
telling.** His route home repeats Jacob's: sent back by God, leave of a father-in-law, attacked on the
road, a brother's kiss. See [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] and
[[Wrestlings of God - Rachel at Naphtali's Birth and Jacob at the Jabbok]].

**He goes in to Pharaoh, and it makes everything worse.** With Aaron — and without the elders the
plan had named (3:18) — he delivers *"Let my people go"* (5:1) and hears *"Who is Yahweh? … I don't
know Yahweh"* (5:2). The answer is more bricks and no straw; the Israelite officers are beaten, appeal
to Pharaoh as *"your servants"*, and coming out find Moses and Aaron waiting and curse them: *"May
Yahweh look at you, and judge"* (5:21). **It is the second time his own people have refused him, and
the first time in God's name** — the man of 2:14 asked *"who made you … a judge?"*; these hand him to
the Judge. **Moses' reply is his first complaint to God**: *"Lord, why have you brought trouble on
this people? Why is it that you have sent me? … you have not rescued your people at all"* (5:22–23) —
God's *nāṣal* of 3:8 quoted back as unkept, and the *hărēʿōtâ* he will use again at Numbers 11:11.
**God, angry at his refusal at 4:14, does not rebuke the complaint**; he answers *"Now you shall
see"* (6:1). See [[Bricks Without Straw]] and
[[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].

**God answers him by saying the promise again, and Israel does not listen to it.** *"I am Yahweh"*,
four times in seven verses (6:2–8). God recalls the fathers, the covenant, the groaning heard, and
the covenant remembered, then makes seven promises: *bring out*, *rescue*, *redeem*, *take*, *be*,
*bring in*, *give*. **The second, *wəhiṣṣaltî*, *"I will rescue"*, answers Moses' *"you have not
rescued"* (5:23) in the same verb.** Moses carries it to the people, and *"they didn't listen to
Moses for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage"* (6:9). His fear at 4:1 has come true. Sent back
to Pharaoh, he argues from the lesser case to the greater: *"the children of Israel haven't listened
to me. How then shall Pharaoh listen to me, who am of uncircumcised lips?"* (6:12). The bush's *"slow
of speech"* now uses the covenant's word for exclusion, *ʿārēl*. **God does not answer the
objection.** He repeats the command, now to *"Moses and … Aaron"* together (6:13). See
[[I Am Yahweh]], [[Two Commissions - Exodus 3 and Exodus 6]], and
[[Uncircumcised Lips, Ears, and Heart]].

**Then the story stops to say who he is.** The genealogy of 6:14–27 begins with Reuben and Simeon
and settles on Levi: Levi, Kohath, Amram, and Amram's sons by his aunt Jochebed, *"Aaron and Moses"*
(6:20). **The unnamed son of unnamed Levite parents in 2:1 is given his line**, and the questions
asked of him in chapter 2 — *"Who made you a prince and a judge over us?"* (2:14), and the Midianite
women's *"An Egyptian"* (2:19) — are answered with a family tree. **His own wife and sons are not in
it**; Aaron's are. The list closes on the two brothers, *"that Aaron and Moses"* (6:26) and *"that
Moses and Aaron … who spoke to Pharaoh"* (6:27), and the narrative resumes with his objection
unchanged (6:28–30). See [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]].

**When the narrative resumes he repeats the objection, and this time God answers it.** *"Behold, I
am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh listen to me?"* (6:30). The answer is the bush's
arrangement turned toward the king: ***"I have made you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother
shall be your prophet"*** (7:1). At 4:16 he was to be *lēʾlōhîm*, *as God*, to Aaron; at 7:1 he is
*ʾĕlōhîm* to Pharaoh, with no preposition. **The text does not make him divine; it gives him God's
place in a chain of speech** — God commands him, he speaks to Aaron, Aaron speaks to Pharaoh (7:2).
God also concedes his point: *"Pharaoh will not listen to you"* (7:4). **Then he does what he is
told without a word**: *"As Yahweh commanded them, so they did"* (7:6) — the first time Exodus
reports his obedience in the formula Genesis used of Noah. **He is eighty** (7:7). At the first
sign the rod is Aaron's and so is the act; Moses is present and silent. See
[[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] and [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

**At the first plague he meets Pharaoh at the river's bank** — *śəpat hayəʾōr*, the bank where his
mother laid him in the basket (2:3) — with the rod *"which was turned to a serpent"* (7:15). He carries
God's sentence to the king's face, *"In this you shall know that I am Yahweh"*, and a charge: *"until
now you haven't listened"* (7:16–17). God's *"I will strike with the rod that is in my hand"* is spoken
through him, and at 7:20 the text does not say whether his hand or Aaron's lifts it. **The verb is
*nākâ*, the verb of his own secret blow at 2:12**; this one is public, commanded, and named afterwards as
Yahweh's (7:25). **The man *"heavy of mouth"* (4:10) is sent to a king *"heavy of heart"* (7:14).** See
[[Water Turned to Blood]].

**In the next three plagues he becomes Pharaoh's intercessor.** Pharaoh asks him to *"Entreat
Yahweh"* (8:8) — *ʿātar*, the verb of Isaac's prayer for Rebekah (Genesis 25:21) — and Moses hands
the king the choice of hour (*"I give you the honor"*, *hitpāʾēr ʿālay*), so that the timing proves
*"there is no one like Yahweh our God"* (8:9–10). He *"cried to Yahweh"* (8:12), with the *ṣāʿaq* of
the foremen's cry to Pharaoh (5:15), and twice ***"Yahweh did according to the word of Moses"***
(8:13, 31) — the obedience formula turned round. **He also bargains, and does not give way**: he
refuses to sacrifice *"in the land"* because Israel's offering is *"the abomination of the Egyptians"*,
holds to *"three days' journey"* and *"as he shall command us"* (8:26–27), and tells Pharaoh to his
face not to *"deal deceitfully any more"* (8:29). At the flies he is sent to *"stand"* before Pharaoh at
the water (8:20) with *hityaṣṣēb*, the verb of his sister standing watch over his basket (2:4). See
[[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]].

**At the livestock, the boils, and the hail, he warns, throws, stretches, and prays.** He carries the
demand twice more, *"Let my people go, that they may serve me"* (9:1, 13). The pestilence needs no
action from him at all: it is *"Yahweh's hand"* (9:3). At the boils he throws *"ashes of the furnace"*
toward the sky *"in the sight of Pharaoh"*, and the magicians *"couldn't stand before Moses"* (9:8–11).
**Before the hail he gives the longest speech of the plagues so far**: *"for this cause I have made
you stand"* (9:16), and the first warning that offers a way of escape (9:19). He stretches the rod to
the sky (9:23). Pharaoh confesses and asks for prayer a third time. Moses goes out of the city to
***"spread abroad my hands to Yahweh"*** — the first time Scripture describes that gesture of prayer —
*"that you may know that the earth is Yahweh's"* (9:29). He also tells Pharaoh plainly, ***"I know
that you don't yet fear Yahweh God"*** (9:30), and he prays anyway. **The chapter closes with the
first *bəyad-mōšeh* in Exodus**: *"as Yahweh had spoken through Moses"*, literally *by the hand of
Moses* (9:35). See [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]].

**At the locusts and the darkness he walks out, refuses every bargain, and is told never to come
back.** God tells him the purpose before he goes in: *"that you may tell in the hearing of your son,
and of your son's son"* (10:2). He and Aaron ask Pharaoh, in God's name, ***"How long will you refuse
to humble yourself before me?"*** (10:3), and after the threat of locusts ***"He turned, and went out
from Pharaoh"*** (10:6) without waiting for an answer. Pharaoh's servants call him *"a snare"* (10:7).
Asked *"who are those who will go?"*, he names everyone — *"our young and … our old … our sons and …
our daughters … our flocks and … our herds"* (10:9) — and he and Aaron are *"driven out"* (10:11). He
stretches out the rod for the locusts and prays a fourth time for Pharaoh (10:13, 18). At the darkness
he stretches out only his hand (10:22). **To the last offer he answers *"Not a hoof shall be left
behind … we don't know with what we must serve Yahweh, until we come there"*** (10:26). Pharaoh says
*"in the day you see my face you shall die!"*, and Moses answers, ***"You have spoken well. I will see
your face again no more"*** (10:28–29) — though he is still speaking to Pharaoh at 11:4–8. See
[[Locusts and Darkness]].

**At the last announcement he is great in Egypt's eyes, and angry.** God tells him *"Yet one plague
more"* and has him tell the people to ask for silver and gold (11:1–2). **The narrator says *"the man
Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants, and in the sight of the
people"*** (11:3) — *hāʾîš mōšeh … məʾōd*, the construction of Numbers 12:3, *"the man Moses was very
humble"* (WLC). Still before Pharaoh — the seam with 10:29 is left visible on the passage page — he
gives God's words: midnight, the firstborn from throne to mill, the great cry, the distinction, and
Pharaoh's servants bowing to *him*, *"Get out"* (11:4–8). ***"He went out from Pharaoh in hot anger"***
(11:8), *bāḥŏrî-ʾāp* — **the first time Exodus calls Moses angry**; until now *ʾap* has been God's, and
against him (4:14; WLC). See [[The Last Plague Announced]].

**At the Passover he relays the law, and adds to it.** God gives the calendar, the lamb, and the feast
to Moses and Aaron together (12:1–20). **Moses passes on only part of it to *"all the elders of
Israel"*** — nothing of the month or the seven days — and adds three things God's speech did not
contain: the hyssop, the order *"none of you shall go out of the door of his house until the
morning"*, and *"the destroyer"*, whom Yahweh will not let in (12:21–23). He gives the elders the
children's question and its answer, *"It is the sacrifice of Yahweh's Passover"* (12:26–27). **The people
bow and worship, as they did at his first report (4:31), and do *"as Yahweh had commanded Moses and
Aaron"*** (12:27–28). See [[The Passover]].

**On the night itself he is sent for, and his terms are met to the letter.** Pharaoh *"called for Moses
and Aaron by night"* (12:31) — **an awkward fit with *"I will see your face again no more"* (10:29)**, which
the text does not resolve: *called for* may mean *sent for*, and the passage page records both readings.
*"Rise up, get out … go, serve Yahweh, as you have said! Take both your flocks and your herds, as you have
said"* (12:31–32) — **every condition Moses refused is withdrawn, in the words *"as you have said"***,
twice. Pharaoh adds *"bless me also!"*, and no answer is recorded. **Moses had forecast that Pharaoh's
servants would bow to him and say *"Get out"* (11:8); what is narrated is Pharaoh saying it.** The people
do *"according to the word of Moses"* in asking for the silver and gold (12:35), and with Aaron he receives
the second Passover law, whose ban on *"the uncircumcised"* (*ʿārēl*) is given through the man who called
his own lips uncircumcised (6:12, 30; WLC). See [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]].

**Then God gives him two verses and he speaks fourteen.** *"Sanctify to me all the firstborn … It is
mine"* (13:2) is the whole of God's word in the pericope, to Moses alone; **Moses turns it into a
festival, a catechism, and a law of redemption** (13:3–16), none of which God is reported saying here —
the pattern of 12:21–27, where he added the hyssop, the door, and the destroyer. He begins not with the
firstborn but with the day: *"Remember this day, in which you came out of Egypt, out of the house of
bondage"* (13:3) — *zākôr*, the form of *"Remember the Sabbath day"* (20:8; WLC) — names the month
*Abib* for the first time, and gives every future father the words to say: *"It is because of that which
Yahweh did for me when I came out of Egypt"* (13:8). **The speech starts in the plural and from 13:5 is
all singular** (WLC): a law for a crowd handed to each household. The text does not say whether he is
expanding God's word or relaying parts the narrator did not record. See
[[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] and
[[When Your Son Asks - Telling the Exodus to the Next Generation]].

**At the sea he speaks for God before God has told him what to say, and is answered with a question.** He
carries Joseph's bones, keeping an oath sworn four hundred years before (13:19; Genesis 50:25). When the
chariots appear and the people turn on him — *"Because there were no graves in Egypt, have you taken us away
to die in the wilderness?"* (14:11), with the *"What is this you have done to us?"* of Genesis's deceived
kings (Genesis 12:18; 26:10; WLC) — **he answers with his first *"Don't be afraid"***: *"Stand still, and see
the salvation of Yahweh … Yahweh will fight for you, and you shall be still"* (14:13–14). ***Hityaṣṣəbû***,
*take your stand*, is the verb of his sister, who *"stood far off, to see what would be done to him"* at the
Nile (2:4; WLC). **Then God asks him *"Why do you cry to me?"*** (14:15) — a cry the narrator gives to the
people, not to him — and tells him to make them march. **He stretches out his hand twice**, and each time
the act is Yahweh's (14:21, 27); the rod is commanded (14:16) and not mentioned again. **The pericope ends
with the first time he is called Yahweh's *servant***: *"they believed in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses"*
(14:31; *ʿabdô*, WLC), and the verb of the close, *"Yahweh saved"* (*yāšaʿ*, 14:30), is the verb of his own
first rescue, *"Moses stood up and helped them"* (2:17; WLC). See [[The Crossing of the Sea]] and
[[Baptized into Moses - The Sea in 1 Corinthians 10 and Hebrews 11]].

**Then he leads the first song in Scripture.** *"Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to
Yahweh"* (15:1) — the verb *šîr* has no occurrence in Genesis (WLC lemma search). **He sings with the people,
not to them**, and the Song's *"I"* is a crowd's (15:1–2). **He is not named inside the Song at all**: the
hand stretched over the sea, which the prose gave to him (14:21, 27), the Song gives to God — *"You
stretched out your right hand"* (15:12). **The servant believed in at 14:31 disappears into his own song.**
His sister answers it with the refrain (15:20–21). See [[The Song of the Sea]] and [[Praise and Song]].

**Then the first murmuring, and his first cry in the wilderness.** He *"led Israel onward from the Red
Sea"* — *wayyassaʿ*, the causative of the march verb, with him as subject (15:22; WLC) — into three days
without water. At Marah **the people *"murmured against Moses"*, the first *lûn* in Scripture** (15:24;
WLC lemma search), and he does not answer them: ***"he cried to Yahweh"*** (15:25), the verb of his prayer
over the frogs (8:12 WEB; WLC). **He is shown a tree and throws it in, and the water is made sweet** —
*wayyašlēk*, the verb of his rod thrown down at the bush (4:3) and of Pharaoh's decree to *"cast every son …
into the river"* (1:22; WLC). **He is not named again in the pericope**: the statute, the test, and *"I am
Yahweh who heals you"* are God's (15:25–26). **The pairing of his cry with *heal* returns when he prays for
Miriam** — *"Moses cried to Yahweh, saying, 'Heal her, God, I beg you!'"* (Numbers 12:13; WEB; WLC; *not
yet ingested*). See [[Bitter Water at Marah]] and [[Murmuring]].

**At the manna he refuses the credit and the blame together.** The congregation says to him and Aaron
*"you have brought us out (hôṣēʾtem) into this wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger"*
(16:3); **he answers in the same verb with God as subject** — *"you shall know that Yahweh has brought you
out (hôṣîʾ)"* (16:6; WLC) — and twice asks ***"Who are we?"*** (*wənaḥnû māh*, 16:7, 8), ending
***"Your murmurings are not against us, but against Yahweh."*** **He names the food by its giver**: *"It is
the bread which Yahweh has given you to eat"* (16:15). **He is angry with Israel for the first time** —
*wayyiqṣōp*, the verb of Pharaoh's anger at his officers (Genesis 40:2; WLC) — at those who kept the bread
overnight against his word, and it *"bred worms, and became foul"* (16:20). **He announces the first
*Sabbath* in Scripture** — *"Tomorrow is a solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to Yahweh"* (16:23) — only when the
rulers report the double gathering; God had told him of the sixth day at 16:5, and the text does not say he
passed it on. **God's rebuke for the Sabbath-breakers is addressed to him in the plural** — *"How long do you
refuse …?"* (16:28). See [[Manna and Quail]].

**At Rephidim the complaint becomes a quarrel, and he names it as a test of God.** *"The people quarreled
(wayyāreb) with Moses"* — *rîb*, the verb of the herdsmen at Isaac's wells (Genesis 26:20; WLC) — and he
answers in two lines that say one thing: ***"Why do you quarrel with me? Why do you test Yahweh?"*** (17:2),
**the first time in Scripture a human being is said to test God** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy).
**He is in danger for the first time from his own people**: *"They are almost ready to stone me"* (17:4) —
*sāqal*, the verb he used when he feared the Egyptians would stone Israel (8:26 WEB; 8:22 Hebrew; WLC). **He
cries to Yahweh for the third time** (8:12 WEB; 15:25; 17:4; WLC), and asks, of *"these people"* (*lāʿām
hazzeh*), what he is to do with them — the words he used on their behalf after the bricks (5:22). **God sends
him to walk in front of them**, with the elders, carrying *"the rod … with which you struck the Nile"* (17:5)
— **the striking credited to him**, though at 7:19 the rod was Aaron's to stretch out. He strikes the rock God
stands on, *"in the sight of the elders of Israel"*, and the place is named *Massah* and *Meribah* (17:7).
**Numbers 20 repeats the scene at Kadesh, and there striking the rock costs him the land** (Numbers 20:8–12;
WEB; *not yet ingested*). See [[Water from the Rock]] and
[[The Rock That Followed Them - Exodus 17, Numbers 20, and 1 Corinthians 10]].

**In the same camp he fights his first battle, and fights it with his hands.** *"Then Amalek came"* (17:8), and
Moses, with no recorded word from God, gives orders to a man the text has never named: *"Joshua, … Choose men for us, and
go out, fight with Amalek. Tomorrow I will stand on the top of the hill with God's rod in my hand"* (17:9).
***"When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed. When he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed"*** (17:11). **His
hands become *kəbēdîm*, *heavy*** — the word of his *"heavy"* mouth at the bush (4:10) and of Pharaoh's heart
(7:14) (WLC) — **and, as Aaron once became his mouth, Aaron and Hur become his hands**: they sit him on a stone
and hold them up *"until sunset"*, and his hands are ***ʾĕmûnâ***, *"steady"*, the word's first occurrence in
Scripture (17:12; WLC). **Then he is the first person in Scripture told to write** (17:14; WLC), and he builds
the first altar in Exodus and names it ***YHWH nissî***, *"Yahweh our Banner"* (17:15; Hebrew *my banner*).
**The next chapter repeats the word**: Jethro tells him *"the thing is too heavy for you"* (18:18). See [[The Battle with Amalek]].

**At the Mountain of God his father-in-law comes, and Moses listens to a foreigner.** Jethro brings Zipporah and
the two sons — the second named for the first time, ***Eliezer***, *"My father's God was my help and delivered me
from Pharaoh's sword"* (18:4) — and **Moses goes out, bows, kisses him, and tells the story of the exodus for the
first time to anyone**, crediting Yahweh at both ends (18:7–8). **The next day he sits *"to judge the people"*
from morning to evening** — *šāpaṭ*, the office the Hebrew denied him at 2:14 (WLC) — because *"the people come to
me to inquire of God"* (18:15). **Jethro tells him *"The thing that you do is not good … the thing is too heavy
(kābēd) for you. You are not able to perform it yourself alone"*** (18:17–18) — **the *heavy* of his mouth (4:10)
and his hands (17:12)** (WLC) — and he keeps the part only he can do, standing *"before God"* and teaching the
statutes, and appoints *"able men"* over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. ***"So Moses listened to the voice
of his father-in-law, and did all that he had said"*** (18:24). **Numbers and Deuteronomy tell the same burden in his
own mouth** — *"I am not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too heavy for me"* (Numbers 11:14; WEB)
— and Deuteronomy credits the plan to him, not to Jethro (Deuteronomy 1:9–18). See [[Jethro's Counsel]] and
[[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]].

**At Sinai Moses carries words between God and the whole assembly.** He reports the covenant
offer to the elders and the people's united assent to Yahweh, sanctifies the people, and brings
them to the mountain's foot (19:3–17). God's public speech is expressly intended to establish
trust in him (19:9). When summoned to the summit, he is sent down again to warn the people;
his reminder that a boundary is already in place does not cancel the new command (19:20–25).
The chapter ends with him among the people, not with him receiving the tablets. This return
with Israel realizes the promised setting of the sign at the bush (3:12). See
[[Israel at Mount Sinai]] and
[[On This Mountain - The Sign at the Bush and the Assembly at Sinai]].

**The people now request his mediation.** After God's commandments, the frightened assembly asks
Moses to speak instead, promising to listen (20:19). He reassures them and explains the test's
purpose: fear of God that prevents sin (20:20). They remain far off as he approaches the thick
darkness where God is (20:21). He does not receive the tablets within this unit. Deuteronomy 5:28
approves the people's request; it is not automatically a refusal of God. See
[[The Ten Commandments]] and [[The Ten Commandments Retold - Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5]].

**The mediator now receives what he is to pass on.** At the thick darkness God gives him words to
*"tell the children of Israel"* (20:22): the altar instructions, and then *"the ordinances which
you shall set before them"* (21:1), beginning with the Hebrew servant. Moses does not speak in this
unit; the request of 20:19 is being answered by his silence and his hearing. Exodus 24:3–7
narrates his telling, writing, and reading. See [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

### What is ahead

The renewed covenant and the tabernacle built (34–40); and the rest of the Torah. These
sections are written as their pericopes arrive.


### The cases of violence and injury — Exodus 21:12–36

Moses continues receiving the ordinances he is to set before Israel (20:22; 21:1), now on killing,
injury, and negligent ownership. His role is inferred from the ongoing speech; he is not named in
21:12–36 and does not adjudicate a narrated case here. His judicial work in Exodus 18 now has
concrete cases specifying different remedies.
See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]].
### Property and restitution — Exodus 22:1–15

Moses continues receiving the ordinances (21:1), now on theft, damage, deposits, loans, and hire.
He is not named in 22:1–15. The cases give him and the judges of Exodus 18 a procedure for disputes
that have no witness: an oath, or a hearing *"before God"* (22:8–11). See
[[Laws of Property and Restitution]].

### Holiness and compassion — Exodus 22:16–31

Moses continues receiving the ordinances (21:1). He is not named in 22:16–31, where God speaks in the
first person about the alien, the widow, the fatherless, and the poor. The Moses who named his son
*Gershom* because he had *"lived as a foreigner in a foreign land"* (2:22) now receives the law
*"You shall not wrong an alien … for you were aliens in the land of Egypt"* (22:21); the text does
not connect the two. See [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].

### Receives the laws of justice and the calendar — Exodus 23:1–19

Moses remains the recipient of the address begun in 20:22 and the ordinances introduced in 21:1. He
is not named or given a new action in this unit. What he is to transmit now covers truthful courts,
assistance to enemies, protection of aliens, rest, and three annual feasts. See
[[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The angel promised — Exodus 23:20–33

Moses is not named in the closing promise of the Book of the Covenant; he is its hearer by context
(20:22; 21:1). The promise of an angel *"before you"* will come back to him after the calf, at 32:34
and 33:2, and he refuses departure without God’s own presence (33:15; [[Show Me Your Glory]] ); the
further plea at 34:9 is now recorded in [[The Covenant Renewed]].
See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]] and
[[An Angel Before You - Genesis 24, Exodus 23, and Exodus 33]].

### The covenant ratified, and forty days in the cloud — Exodus 24:1–18

**The mediator acts at last, and does almost everything himself.** Moses tells the people
*"all Yahweh's words, and all the ordinances"*, receives their *"will we do"*, and then writes the
words down — the first time Scripture says he wrote what God had spoken, after the single memorial of
17:14. He builds the altar and the twelve pillars, divides the blood, reads *"the book of the
covenant"*, and throws the blood on the people with the formula *"this is the blood of the covenant"*
(24:3–8). Young men offer the sacrifices; Moses handles the blood. **He is the only one allowed to
*come near*** (24:2): he goes up with the seventy-three who see God and eat, then on with Joshua, then
alone into the cloud, where he waits six days before God calls, and stays *"forty days and forty
nights"* (24:12–18). He leaves the disputes to Aaron and Hur (24:14) — the work Jethro had told him
not to carry alone (18:18). See [[The Blood of the Covenant]],
[[The Blood of the Covenant - Exodus 24, the Last Supper, and Hebrews 9]], and
[[Forty Days and Forty Nights - The Flood, Sinai, Horeb, and the Wilderness]].

### Shown the pattern — Exodus 25:1–9

**The first thing Moses hears in the cloud is a request he must carry down: an offering, from the
willing only** (25:2). He is to collect the materials and to see that everything is made *"according to
all that I show you, the pattern of the tabernacle"* (25:9). *Show* is singular and addressed to him
(*marʾeh ʾôtəkā*, MAM): **Moses is the one who sees the design**, as he was the only one who came near
(24:2). Exodus does not say what he saw. Hebrews 8:5 and Acts 7:44 recall the pattern *"shown to you on
the mountain"* (quoting 25:40, the next pericope). The offering he collects is narrated at 35:4–36:7,
where he finally has to stop the people from bringing more (36:6). See
[[The Offering for the Sanctuary]].

### Promised a meeting place — Exodus 25:10–40

**The ark is designed around a conversation with Moses.** *"There I will meet with you (ləkā, singular),
and I will tell you from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubim … all that I command you for
the children of Israel"* (25:22; MAM). **Moses is to be the one who hears**, at a fixed place, on behalf of
everyone; Numbers 7:89 shows him doing it *(not yet ingested)*. The chapter closes with the warning
Hebrews will quote: *"See that you make them after their pattern, which has been shown to you on the
mountain"* (25:40). The verb *morʾeh* is a passive participle, *being shown* (MAM): **Moses is still
looking while God speaks.** Hebrews 8:5 and Acts 7:44 both remember him as the man who saw the
pattern. See [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] and
[[After Their Pattern - Exodus 25.40 in Acts 7 and Hebrews 8]].

**Moses receives directions for erection as well as fabrication.** Exodus 26:30 recalls the design
shown to him on the mountain; 26:33–35 tells him where to put the veil and the three furnishings.
The instructions anticipate the assembly he will perform in 40:18–28; they are not a report that
he has already left the cloud or built the tent. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].

### The lamp service prescribed — Exodus 27:1–21

In Exodus 27:8 Moses is again referred to what was shown on the mountain; in 27:20 he must
command the children of Israel to bring oil. His role combines receiving the design and
transmitting a recurring obligation. He is not here described as either building the altar
or tending the lamp himself. See [[The Altar and the Court]].

### The priestly garments prescribed — Exodus 28:1–43

Moses is told to bring his brother and nephews near *"from among the children of Israel"*,
to instruct the wise-hearted makers, and later to clothe, anoint, consecrate, and sanctify the
priests himself (Exodus 28:1, 3, 41). He is the one who installs the priesthood, though he does
not wear its garments. See [[The Priestly Garments]].

### The ordination prescribed — Exodus 29:1–46

Moses is the one who performs every act of the ordination: he washes, clothes, and anoints Aaron,
clothes the sons, kills the animals, applies the blood, waves the portions, and burns the offerings
(Exodus 29:4–26). The breast of the ram of consecration is *"your portion"* (29:26). He acts as
priest before a priest exists, and the chapter does not name his office. God promises to meet him at
the tent's door, *"to speak there to you"* (29:42). See [[The Ordination of the Priests]] and
[[Ordination Commanded and Performed - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 8]].

Exodus 30 tells Moses to make the incense altar, collect the half-shekel *"atonement money"*, set
the basin between tent and altar, and compound the anointing oil and the incense himself —
*"take to yourself"* (30:23, 34). He is to anoint the tent, every piece of furniture, and Aaron and
his sons (30:26–30). Exodus 40:26–27 and 40:31 (local WEB) later have Moses burn the first incense
and wash at the basin himself. See [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]].

### The makers appointed and the tablets delivered — Exodus 31

Moses receives the names of Bezalel and Oholiab and the promise of wisdom for the company (31:1–11).
The making previously commanded to him is assigned to skilled workers. He must speak the Sabbath law
to Israel (31:12–17), and receives the two tablets when God finishes speaking (31:18), fulfilling
the promise of 24:12. This chapter does not yet narrate his descent or his relay of the
instructions.

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Moses pleads for the people rather than taking up the prospect of a nation made from himself. His
first appeal invokes deliverance, Egypt’s judgment of God, and the patriarchal oath; Yahweh
relents (32:10–14). He then breaks the tablets, destroys the calf, confronts Aaron, and commands
the camp judgment. His next-day attempt at atonement is explicitly uncertain (32:30). His
conditional request to be erased if forgiveness is refused is not accepted as a substitution; God
assigns erasure to the sinner (32:32–33). See [[The Golden Calf]].

### Presence after the calf — Exodus 33

Moses meets Yahweh at the tent outside the camp and returns to the people (33:7–11). He turns his
personal favor into a plea for the nation, refusing to travel without divine presence (33:12–17).
His further request to see glory is answered with a promise of goodness and mercy, but a limit on
sight (33:18–23). His access does not make him exempt from the danger described in 33:20. See
[[Show Me Your Glory]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

Moses cuts the new stones and ascends Sinai alone (34:1–4). On hearing the proclamation he bows,
then asks for presence, pardon, and the people to be God's inheritance (34:8–9). He is commanded
to write and remains forty days and nights without bread or water (34:27–28). The last writing
notice must be read beside God's promise to write in 34:1; the possible subjects and relation to
Deuteronomy are recorded in [[The Replacement Tablets - Exodus 34 and Deuteronomy 10]]. See [[The Covenant Renewed]].

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

Moses comes down with the tablets unaware that his face shines (34:29). He calls the
frightened Aaron and rulers back, then speaks the commands to all Israel (34:30–32). He veils
himself only after speaking and removes the covering before Yahweh (34:33–35). His visible
distinction is joined to continuing instruction, not to a refusal of access. Exodus does not
report the radiance fading; Paul's later interpretation is examined in
[[The Shining Face and the Veil - Exodus 34 and 2 Corinthians 3]].

See [[The Shining Face of Moses]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

Moses assembles all Israel, begins with Sabbath, and relays the invitation for materials and
skilled work (35:1–19). The congregation leaves his presence and the willing bring their gifts
(35:20–29). He communicates the specification rather than personally making every object.
The narrative begins to enact the instructions he received before the calf.

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The makers and the surplus — Exodus 35:30–36:7

After announcing Yahweh's choice of the makers (35:30–35), Moses calls the skilled and willing
workers and hands over the contributions (36:2–3). When they report that supply exceeds the
work's need, he has the camp told to stop bringing (36:4–6). His mediation includes listening
to the people doing the work; he neither makes every object himself nor keeps collecting
without reference to the commissioned task.

See [[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]].

### The enclosure made — Exodus 36:8–38

The workers now make the enclosure corresponding to the instructions Moses received in Exodus
26. He is not named or given a new action in 36:8–38; the unit follows his summons and handling
of the contributions (36:2–6). His raising of the tent in 40:18–28 remains a distinct, later
stage, not an act to attribute to him during the making of these components.

See [[The Tabernacle Built]].

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The design received by Moses in chapters 25 and 30 becomes manufactured furnishings in
chapter 37. He is not named or given a new action in this unit. His later placement of the
ark, table, lampstand, and incense altar, and the beginning of their use, belong to 40:20–27.
The account distinguishes the transmitter of the design from the makers and from installation.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38:1–31

The inventory is counted according to his command (38:21), and the making is measured against what
Yahweh commanded him (38:22). Administrative responsibility and transmitted divine instruction meet
here, without a new speech or the final erection of the tabernacle.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

Moses is named in each of the seven obedience notices (39:1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29, 31).
The work is measured against the command he received; he is not said to manufacture or inspect
the garments in this unit. Inspection and blessing belong to 39:43, beyond this pericope.
His role as transmitter of the design is therefore distinct from both craft and installation.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The people bring the tabernacle and its equipment to Moses (39:33–41). He sees that the
work conforms to Yahweh’s command and blesses them (39:43). The mediator who received the design
now assesses its execution; the text does not give his inspection procedure or blessing’s words.
His approval closes the making report, before his raising of the tabernacle in chapter 40.

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

Moses receives the command, raises the tabernacle, places the testimony and furnishings,
sets out bread, lights lamps, burns incense, and offers burnt and meal offerings (40:1–29).
He washes hands and feet with Aaron and his sons and completes the court (40:30–33).
The repeated obedience notices lead to completion, but not unrestricted access: when the
cloud and glory fill the tent, he cannot enter (40:35). This stands beside his entry into
the mountain cloud in 24:18 and the outside tent in 33:9. The settings and moments differ;
40:35 names the filling as the obstacle, not a new sin or permanent rejection of Moses.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

### The meal offering prescribed — Leviticus 2

The speech begun to Moses in 1:1–2 continues with grain-offering instructions. He is not named anew in chapter 2; this is mediated instruction, not a narrated act of offering. See [[The Meal Offering]].

### The trespass-offering instruction — Leviticus 5:14–19

Receives the new speech at 5:14; the valuation language belongs to the instruction delivered to him, without a narrated act of appraisal.

See [[The Trespass Offering for Holy Things and Unknown Guilt]].

### The burnt offering — Leviticus 1:1–17

Moses receives speech from the Tent of Meeting and is told to address Israel (1:1–2). Exodus 40:35
reports his inability to enter the glory-filled tent; Leviticus 1:1 reports a call from it, without
saying that he has now entered. The two reports should not be flattened into either unrestricted
entry or permanent exclusion.

See [[The Burnt Offering]] . Source: stored WEB Leviticus 1; comparisons identified on the passage
page.

### The peace offering — Leviticus 3

The peace-offering law continues the address to Moses begun in Leviticus 1:1–2. Chapter 3 does not name him anew or describe him sacrificing; it transmits instructions for offerers and Aaron's sons.

See [[The Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 3; contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Priest and congregation — Leviticus 4:1–21

Moses receives a new divine address and must speak to Israel (1–2). The cases concern the
anointed priest and congregation; Moses is transmitting their duties, not performing these
offerings in a narrated event.

See [[The Sin Offering for Priest and Congregation]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:1–21;
contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Ruler and individual — Leviticus 4:22–35

The speech entrusted to Moses in 4:1–2 continues with ruler and individual cases
(22–35). He is the contextual mediator, not a named offerer or officiant in these verses.

See [[The Sin Offering for Ruler and Individual]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:22–35; contextual
comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Confession and offerings according to means

The address begun at Leviticus 4:1–2 continues through confession and offerings scaled to means (5:1–13). Moses mediates the instruction; no new action by him is narrated.

See [[Confession and Offerings According to Means]]. Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 4–5; 7:38; Exodus 28:1.

### Fraud, restitution, and forgiveness — Leviticus 6:1–7

Receives the new divine speech (1). The law specifies repayment to the owner and a valued ram
brought to the priest; Moses does not adjudicate a narrated dispute here.

See [[Restitution for Fraud and False Oaths]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:1–7; contextual comparisons documented there.

### Altar fire and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:8–23

Moses receives both speeches (8, 19) and is told to command Aaron and his sons (9). These are instructions to transmit, not a report of his performing the rites.

See [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:8–23; comparisons documented there.

### Holy flesh and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:24–7:10

Moses receives the speech at 6:24 and must address Aaron and his sons (25). The passage prescribes
their portions; it does not narrate Moses performing these rites.

See [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:24–7:10.

### Thanksgiving and peace-offering meals — Leviticus 7:11–21

The speech introduced at 6:24 continues through the peace-offering eating rules
(7:11–21). Moses mediates instructions about bread, deadlines, and ritual eligibility;
he is not newly named or described as performing a sacrifice in this unit.

See [[Thanksgiving, Vows, and the Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6–7.
