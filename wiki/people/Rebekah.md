# Rebekah

## Overview

The second matriarch, and Genesis introduces her the way it introduces almost nobody — **decades
early, in a subordinate clause, at the end of somebody else's genealogy.**

> **23** Bethuel became the father of Rebekah. These eight Milcah bore to Nahor, Abraham’s brother.

That is all of her for now. She is the only granddaughter named in a register of sons; she is
mentioned before the problem she solves has been raised; and she is not mentioned again for a
chapter and a half. **The notice is placed immediately after the binding of Isaac and immediately
before Sarah's death** — that is, at the exact seam where the covenant needs a next generation and
the current one is ending. See [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]].

**Her family position is the point of the placement.** Her father is [[Bethuel]], son of
[[Nahor (brother of Abram)]] and [[Milcah]] — so she comes from the branch of [[Terah]]'s household
that never left Mesopotamia, and she is [[Abraham]]'s great-niece. When Abraham makes his servant
swear *"you shall not take a wife for my son of the daughters of the Canaanites … But you shall go to
my country, and to my relatives"* (24:3–4), the reader already knows who is waiting at the other
end.

### In this pericope — Genesis 24:1–67

**The longest chapter in Genesis is hers, and she is characterised almost entirely by verbs.**

Two adjectives — *"very beautiful to look at, a virgin"* (24:16) — and then: *came out, went down,
filled, came up, said, hurried, let down, gave, finished, said, hurried, emptied, ran, drew.*
**She is the most physically active person in the Abraham cycle**, and every verb in the sequence is
work.

**She arrives before the prayer is over.** *"Before he had finished speaking, behold, Rebekah came
out"* (24:15) — and she is introduced by a full pedigree in that same verse, *born to Bethuel the son
of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother*, which is the reader being told the answer while the
servant is still watching.

**She passes the sign and then exceeds it.** [[Abraham's Servant]] asked God for a woman who would
say a sentence. She says the sentence — *"Drink, and I will also give your camels a drink"* — and
then does it, unprompted, for ten animals, *"until they have finished drinking"* (24:19). Ten camels
at the end of a desert journey will drink until they stop. **The test she cannot know is being
applied is a test of exactly one thing, and it is not beauty or lineage; it is whether she will do a
great deal of hard work for a stranger who asked for a cupful.** See [[A Wife for Isaac]].

***"I will go."*** *ʾĒlēk* — one word in Hebrew, and **the only question anybody in Genesis puts to
a bride**. It is asked to break a deadlock about a departure date, not to settle whether the marriage
happens; the family agreed at 24:51. **That does not make the answer smaller.** She is the only
matriarch who consents on the page, the only one who travels to her marriage, and the only person in
the patriarchal narratives besides Abraham who leaves country, kindred, and father's house for a land
she has not seen. **Abraham was commanded to and was seventy-five. She is asked, and she is promised
nothing.** See [[Leave Your Country - The Two Lek-Lekha Commands]].

**And her family blesses her with the oath sworn at Moriah.** *"Let your offspring possess the gate
of those who hate them"* (24:60) is Genesis 22:17 with a feminine pronoun and a synonym for
*enemies* — the same verb, the same construct chain, and a clause that occurs nowhere else in the
Hebrew Bible. **Nobody in that room was on the mountain, and the servant never told them the
promise.** It is the only time in Genesis the offspring promise is spoken over a woman as her own.
See [[Possess the Gate of Those Who Hate Them]].

**Then she gets down off the camel and covers her face.** She sees a man walking in a field, asks who
he is, is told *"It is my master"*, and veils herself (24:65) — the chapter that has gone to enormous
lengths to make her visible ending by hiding her, and in the same family five chapters later a veil
will let one sister be married in another's place (29:23–25).

**She is brought into Sarah's tent** (24:67), still standing and still called by the dead woman's
name three years on. Genesis reports the succession in eleven words, and adds the sentence that is
**the first time in the Bible a husband is said to love his wife**.

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it, not a substitute for it.

She is barren for twenty years (25:21), is the only woman in Genesis to receive an oracle about her own pregnancy — *"Two nations are in your womb …
The elder will serve the younger"* (25:23) — and is the only matriarch whose favouritism the
narrator states outright: *"Rebekah loved Jacob"* (25:28). She engineers the stolen blessing in
chapter 27, sends Jacob away to save his life, and never sees him again. Genesis records no death
scene for her; she is named only as an occupant of the grave, in Jacob's instructions at 49:31.

**She and Isaac are the only patriarchal couple in Genesis with no second woman in the household**,
and Genesis 26:8 gives them the one glimpse of affection in the cycle — *"Isaac was caressing
Rebekah, his wife"*, seen through a window by a Philistine king.

## Timeline / key events

- Born to [[Bethuel]]; named in the notice that reaches Abraham after Moriah — Genesis 22:23,
  [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]]
- Meets Abraham's servant at the well outside the city of Nahor; waters ten camels unasked —
  Genesis 24:15–20, [[A Wife for Isaac]]
- Given the gold nose-ring and bracelets, and runs to tell her mother's house — Genesis 24:22–28
- Consents to go: *"I will go"* — Genesis 24:58
- Blessed by her family in the words of the Moriah oath — Genesis 24:60,
  [[Possess the Gate of Those Who Hate Them]]
- Veils herself at the sight of Isaac in the field — Genesis 24:64–65
- Marries [[Isaac]] in his mother Sarah's tent; he loves her, and is comforted after his mother's
  death — Genesis 24:67
- Barren twenty years; Isaac prays and she conceives — Genesis 25:21 *(not yet ingested)*
- Receives the oracle of the two nations — Genesis 25:23 *(not yet ingested)*
- Called Isaac's sister before Abimelech, the third wife-sister episode — Genesis 26:7
  *(not yet ingested)*
- Devises the stolen blessing and sends Jacob to Haran — Genesis 27:5–17, 42–46 *(not yet ingested)*
- Buried in the cave of Machpelah; named only in Jacob's burial instructions — Genesis 49:31
  *(not yet ingested)*

## Appears in

- [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]] — Genesis 22:23; one clause, an entire generation early,
  and the reason the genealogy is in the book
- [[A Wife for Isaac]] — Genesis 24:15–67; arrives before the prayer is finished, passes a sign she

  never heard it, and is brought into the tent of the woman she replaces

## Related people

- [[Bethuel]] — her father
- [[Nahor (brother of Abram)]] — her grandfather
- [[Milcah]] — her grandmother
- [[Isaac]] — her husband, and her father's cousin; at 24:67 the first husband in Scripture said to
  love his wife
- [[Laban]] — her brother, who runs the negotiation, takes the gifts, and tries to keep her ten more
  days; she will send her own son to him a lifetime later
- [[Abraham's Servant]] — the unnamed man who prays for her arrival and hands her over in one clause
- [[Abraham]] — her great-uncle, and her father-in-law
- [[Sarah]] — her mother-in-law, who dies before she is fetched; Genesis 24:67 says Isaac was
  comforted after his mother's death, which is the only thing it ever puts between the two women
