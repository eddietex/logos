# God

## Overview

The one who acts in the Bible's first sentence and never leaves the story. Genesis 1:1–2:3
introduces him with no origin, no genealogy, and no rivals — he simply *is*, prior to the
heavens and the earth, and everything else in the account exists because he speaks it into being.

The name used throughout this first pericope is **Elohim**, a grammatically plural form taking
singular verbs, the generic Hebrew word for God used here for God as creator of everything and
everyone. From Genesis 2:4 the text switches to **YHWH Elohim** ("the LORD God"), the personal
covenant name, as the focus narrows from the cosmos to a garden and a relationship. Tracking
which name a passage uses is one of the standard tools of Pentateuchal source criticism, and is
worth noting on each passage page as ingestion proceeds.

What Genesis 1 establishes about him, before any other page can add to it:

- **He creates by speech.** *"God said… and it was so."* No struggle, no material handed to him,
  no assistance.
- **He evaluates.** Seven times he sees what he has made and calls it good, the last time *very*
  good. Creation carries his verdict of worth intrinsically.
- **He separates and names.** Light from darkness, waters from waters, day from night — ordering
  is as much his creative act as making is.
- **He blesses** (1:22, 1:28, 2:3) — the first thing he does toward living creatures, and toward
  the seventh day.
- **He rests, and makes rest holy** (2:2–3) — not from exhaustion, but as a completion; see
  [[Sabbath]].
- **He speaks of himself in the plural** at the one moment he makes humanity (1:26) — see the
  note on the passage page.

### Yahweh God, from Genesis 2:4

[[The Garden of Eden]] introduces a second portrait, and the shift in name marks it. The God who
spoke the cosmos into order is now shown with his hands in the material — and Genesis makes no
attempt to reconcile the two pictures, because the point of setting them adjacent is that they
are one being.

- **He works like a craftsman.** He *forms* the man from dust as a potter forms clay (*yāṣar*),
  *plants* a garden, *builds* (*bānâ*) a woman. Where chapter 1 has him commanding from outside,
  here he is inside the scene.
- **He gives life from himself.** He breathes into the man's nostrils — personally, at close
  range — and dust becomes a living being. Life is not a property granted but a breath lent; see
  [[Life and Death]].
- **He provides before he commands.** Garden, food, beauty, and work all precede the single
  prohibition of 2:17.
- **He entrusts.** The garden is given to be cultivated and kept, and the naming of the animals is
  left entirely to the man: *"whatever the man called every living creature became its name"*. He
  makes room for a will other than his own — which is what makes Genesis 3 possible.
- **He judges something "not good."** *"It is not good for the man to be alone"* (2:18), the first
  negative verdict in the Bible, spoken over his own creation and immediately acted on.
- **He is anthropomorphic without embarrassment.** He forms, breathes, plants, takes, closes up
  flesh, brings the woman to the man, and in 3:8 walks in the garden in the cool of the day.
