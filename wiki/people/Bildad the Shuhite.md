# Bildad the Shuhite

## Overview

The second of Job's three friends, and the blunt one. Where [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] opens his
first speech with an apology for speaking, Bildad opens his with *"How long will you speak these
things? Shall the words of your mouth be a mighty wind?"* (8:2), and within two verses he has said
the thing no one else says out loud: *"If your children have sinned against him, he has delivered
them into the hand of their disobedience"* (8:4). Job's ten children died in a single afternoon,
and Bildad's account of it is that they got what they deserved. It is the cruellest sentence in the
book, and it is offered as consolation.

His authority is tradition, stated more baldly than Eliphaz states it: *"Please inquire of past
generations. Find out about the learning of their fathers … shall they not teach you, tell you,
and utter words out of their heart?"* (8:8, 10). His three speeches shrink as the book goes on —
twenty-two verses in chapter 8, twenty-one in chapter 18, and six in chapter 25 — and the last of
them is the clearest sign that the friends have run out of material. Whether the brevity of chapter
25 is design or textual damage is disputed; see the Notes.

He is a **Shuhite**, most often connected with [[Shuah]], a son of Abraham by [[Keturah]] sent
*"eastward, to the east country"* (Genesis 25:2, 6) — which would place him, like the rest of the
cast, among the peoples east and south-east of Canaan rather than in Israel. The other standing
candidate is Assyrian Sūḫu on the middle Euphrates; neither can be confirmed, and the book never
uses the word as a place name at all.

## In the ingested passages

His first speech is ingested as [[Bildad's First Speech]], and it is the shape of everything he will
say. **One question, asked to close a case:** *"Does God pervert justice? Or does the Almighty
pervert righteousness?"* (8:3) — Abraham's question at Sodom (Genesis 18:25), used not as a lever
but as a lid. **One application:** the children died, so they sinned (8:4). **One offer:** Job is
still alive, so he still has time — seek God early, be pure and upright, and *"your latter end would
greatly increase"* (8:5–7). **One authority:** the fathers, because *"we are but of yesterday, and
know nothing"* (8:8–10). **One image:** the papyrus that withers without water (8:11–19). **And one
verdict:** *"God will not cast away a blameless man"* (8:20).

Three things about it reach further than the speech. **He listens to Job closely and turns his words
round.** *Seek diligently* (8:5) is the verb of Job's last line to God, *"You will seek me
diligently, but I shall not be"* (7:21); *cast away* (8:20) is Job's *"I loathe my life"* (7:16);
*a mighty wind* (8:2) is Job's own description of a desperate man's words (6:26). Chapter 18's
reversal of *tear* (18:4 against 16:9) is the same habit, already formed. **His prediction comes
true.** *"Your latter end would greatly increase"* is the most exactly fulfilled sentence any friend
speaks — *"Yahweh blessed the latter end of Job more than his beginning"* (42:12), the same two
nouns — and he is rebuked by God before it happens. **And his last line describes his own fate.**
*"Those who hate you shall be clothed with shame"* (8:22): at 42:7–9 the men who must be covered by
Job's prayer are the three friends.

His second speech is ingested as [[Bildad's Second Speech]], and it is the fullest sample
of him the wiki has. Four verses of irritation, seventeen verses on the fate of the wicked, and no
reply of any kind to what Job had just said — not to the witness in heaven, not to the demand for a
pledge, not to *"there is no violence in my hands"*, and not to the plea of a dying man. Chapter 8
had at least ended with an offer (*"he will yet fill your mouth with laughter"*); chapter 18 ends
with sulfur scattered on a habitation and a name erased from the street.

Three things about the speech are worth carrying to any later Bildad material. **He argues by
reversal.** Job said at 16:9 that God *tore* him in his wrath; 18:4 hands the verb back —
*"You who tear yourself in your anger"* — and the theological problem dissolves into a
psychological one. Job said at 14:7 that a felled tree has hope of sprouting; 18:16 dries the roots
and cuts the branch. **He needs the trap to be self-entered.** The six snare-words of 18:8–10 are
built around *"he is cast into a net by his own feet"*, because a snare laid for an innocent man
would be Job's case exactly. **And he uses the dead children twice.** At 8:4 he named them; at
18:19 — *"neither son nor grandson among his people"* — he lists them among a wicked man's standard
fittings, which is worse.

His conclusion, *"This is the place of him who doesn't know God"* (18:21), is the precise inverse of
the narrator's opening verdict on Job, *"one who feared God, and turned away from evil"* (1:1). He
is not saying anything false about the wicked. He has no way to discover that he is not looking at
one.

In Job 15–17 he does not speak. He is present, and he is included twice.

In [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]] he is inside Eliphaz's *"we"*: *"What do you know, that we don't
know? … With us are both the gray-headed and the very aged men"* (15:9–10). The friends argue as a
bloc, and Eliphaz claims their combined authority.

In [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] Job answers the bloc in the plural — *"You
are **all** miserable comforters!"* (16:2) and *"as for **you all**, come on now again; I shall not
find a wise man among you"* (17:10). Bildad's own second speech follows immediately in chapter 18,
and it takes the invitation literally.

And he is answered in kind. [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] opens by returning his own *"How
long"* (19:2 against 18:2), reassigns his net to God (*"God … has surrounded me with his net"*,
19:6), grants his uprooted tree and names the gardener (*"My hope he has plucked up like a tree"*,
19:10), and answers his erased name with the wish that Job's words be cut into rock *"forever"*
(19:23–24). The reader is holding the result of that last exchange: Job's words were written in a
book, and Bildad's name survives inside them.

## Timeline / key events

- **Job 2:11–13** — [[Seven Days of Silence]]. Comes from [[Shuah]] by arrangement with the other
  two, fails to recognise Job, weeps, tears his robe, throws dust over his head, and sits on the
  ground seven days and seven nights without speaking.
- **Job 8:1–22** — [[Bildad's First Speech]]: God does not pervert justice; your children got what
  they earned; seek God and your latter end will increase; inquire of past generations; the papyrus
  without water; God will not cast away a blameless man.
- **Job 15–17** — silent, but included in Eliphaz's *"we"* and in Job's *"you all"*.
- **Job 18:1–21** — [[Bildad's Second Speech]]. Twenty-one verses on the fate of the wicked: the
  lamp put out, six snares in four verses, the firstborn of death, the march to the king of terrors,
  sulfur on the habitation, and a name erased from the street.
- **Job 25:1–6** — the third speech, six verses: *"How then can man be just with God? Or how can he
  who is born of a woman be clean? … how much less man, who is a worm, the son of man, who is a
  worm!"* The last thing any of the friends says.
- **Job 42:7–9** — included in *"your two friends"*, rebuked with Eliphaz, and required to bring
  sacrifices and be prayed for by Job.

## Appears in

- [[Seven Days of Silence]] — Job 2:11–13. **The man who will explain ten dead children as a sentence
  they earned (8:4) tore his own robe for them first**, and kept the full seven-day mourning term on
  the ground beside their father. The book records both and reconciles neither, and the gap between
  them is the clearest measure it gives of what the doctrine costs the man who holds it.
- [[Eliphaz's First Speech]] — Job 4:1–5:27, silent, and inside the *"we"* of 5:27: *"we have
  searched it, so it is."* Eliphaz's 5:4, the fool's children *"crushed in the gate"*, stops short of
  naming Job's; **Bildad's first speech will not stop short** (8:4).
- [[The Arrows of the Almighty]] — Job 6:1–7:21, inside the plural of 6:21–29: *"You see a terror,
  and are afraid"*, *"Teach me"*, *"Please return."* Job's *"my brothers have dealt deceitfully as a
  brook"* (6:15) is the first time he indicts the three together, and Bildad's reply opens on it:
  *"How long will you speak these things? Shall the words of your mouth be a mighty wind?"* (8:2).
- [[Bildad's First Speech]] — Job 8:1–22, his own and his first: the children named as proof (8:4),
  the conditional offer of restoration (8:5–7) that chapter 18 withdraws, the fathers (8:8–10), and
  the papyrus (8:11–19). See [[The Papyrus Without Water - Bildad's Plants, Psalm 1, and Jeremiah 17]].
- [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]] — Job 9:1–10:22; **answered in one line and never addressed again.** *"Truly I know that

  both — *"I am blameless… I despise my life… he destroys the blameless and the wicked"* (9:21–22) —

  man who *"flees like a shadow"* (14:2); and his papyrus that dies without water (8:11–13) by the
  felled tree that revives *"through the scent of water"* (14:9). He answers 12:7's animals at 18:3:
  *"Why are we counted as animals?"*
- [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]] — as part of Eliphaz's *"we"* and *"with us"*.
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — as one of the three answered in the plural.
- [[Bildad's Second Speech]] — his own, and the fullest statement of retribution in the book.
- [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] — answered point by point: his *"How long"*, his net, his tree,

- [[Zophar's Second Speech]] — whose closing formula (*"the portion of a wicked man from God"*,

## Notes

- **The name.** *Bildad* is uncertain; proposals include a form of *Bel has loved* (with the
  Babylonian divine name) and a Semitic construction meaning *son of contention* or *the darling of
  Hadad*. None is secure, and unlike *Eliphaz* it does not obviously mean anything in Hebrew.
- **Chapter 25's six verses.** In the third cycle the pattern of the dialogue visibly breaks: Bildad
  gets six verses, Zophar gets none at all, and parts of Job's replies in chapters 26–27 sound like
  the friends' doctrine rather than his. Two readings. **Textual disorder** — the third cycle
  suffered damage or dislocation in transmission, and material now in Job's mouth (26:5–14,
  27:13–23) originally belonged to Bildad and to a missing third speech of Zophar; this is a common
  reconstruction and various editions rearrange accordingly. **Deliberate collapse** — the
  breakdown *is* the point, dramatizing the exhaustion of the friends' position, with Bildad
  managing a short reprise and Zophar unable to speak at all. The wiki takes no position and will
  record the arguments on the passage pages when chapters 25–27 are ingested; the observation worth
  keeping now is that the second cycle, where Job 15–17 sits, is the last one in which the *pattern*
  is intact — the three speakers all speak, in order, at length. The *argument*, on the wiki's
  reading, has already failed by then: chapters 18 and 20 are two recitations of the same doctrine
  that engage nothing Job said in chapters 16, 17, or 19. See [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].
