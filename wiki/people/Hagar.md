# Hagar

## Overview

An Egyptian slave in Abram's household, given to him as a second wife to produce the heir his wife
could not, and driven out when she did. She is the first person in the Bible met by the angel of
Yahweh, the first woman since Eve God speaks to directly, the recipient of Scripture's first birth
annunciation — and **the only human being in the Bible who gives God a name.**

**The name.** *Hāgār* has no secure Hebrew etymology. The old rabbinic wordplay reads it as *hā-gēr*,
*the sojourner* — the feminine of the word Genesis uses for Abram's own status in the land
(*[[Land and Sojourning]]*), which if nothing else is a good description of her. Arabic *hājar*, to
emigrate, is the root behind *hijra* and is the form her name takes in Islamic tradition. Neither is
demonstrably the origin; the name may simply be foreign, which would fit a woman the text identifies
by her country three times.

**Her status.** She is a *šipḥâ* — a female household slave, the property of the wife rather than the
husband. That is what makes Sarai's proposal legally coherent and what makes 16:6 possible: when
Abram says *"your maid is in your hand"*, he is stating an ownership fact, not making a decision. At
16:3 she is given *"to be his wife"* (*lə-ʾiššâ*), which raises her status without transferring the
ownership; at 21:10 Sarah calls her *"this servant"* again.

### In this pericope — Genesis 16:1–16

**Six verses in which she does not speak, and eight in which she is the only one who does.**

In 16:1–6 she is the object of every verb: *taken*, *given*, *went in to*, *conceived*, *dealt
harshly with*. She is not addressed by anyone. **Neither Sarai nor Abram ever says her name** — Sarai
says *my servant* (16:2, 16:5), Abram says *your maid* (16:6) — and the narrator says it seven times,
as if to make up the difference. Her one act in the household is a look: *"When she saw that she had
conceived, her mistress was despised in her eyes"* (16:4). She is punished for it with *ʿānâ*, the
verb Genesis 15:13 used one chapter earlier of Egypt afflicting Israel, and she runs.

Then, in the wilderness, everything reverses.

- ***"Yahweh's angel found her"*** (16:7) — nobody from the household is looking.
- ***"Hagar, Sarai's servant, where did you come from? Where are you going?"*** (16:8) — she is
  addressed by name, and asked two questions. She answers one. She says she is fleeing from Sarai and
  does not say she is going to Egypt, which is what the road to Shur means.
- ***"Return to your mistress, and submit yourself under her hands"*** (16:9) — the hardest sentence
  in her story, and the wiki does not soften it. See the passage page.
- ***"I will greatly multiply your offspring, that they will not be counted for multitude"*** (16:10)
  — the patriarchal offspring promise, in its own Hebrew formula (*harbâ ʾarbeh*), spoken directly to
  a woman. **No other woman in Scripture receives it in her own right.**
- ***"You shall call his name Ishmael, because Yahweh has heard your affliction"*** (16:11) — the
  Bible's first annunciation, and the name is a permanent statement that her affliction was heard.
- ***"She called the name of Yahweh who spoke to her, 'You are a God who sees'"*** (16:13) — *ʾĒl
  Rŏʾî*. Others in Genesis call *on* God's name or name a place after what God did there; the
  construction here, with God as the object of the naming, occurs nowhere else in the Bible.

She goes back. She bears a son. Abram gives him the name the angel gave her, which means she told
him. Sarai is not mentioned in the last two verses.

**What is remarkable about the chapter is the address list.** Between the covenant of 15:21 and the
appearance of 17:1, the only human being God speaks to in Genesis is this woman.

### Where the rest of her is

- **Genesis 17:20; 21:9–21** — the second expulsion, which is not a flight and from which she does
  not return. Sarah sees Ishmael *"mocking"* and demands that both be cast out; God tells Abraham to
  listen to his wife's voice again (21:12, the same idiom as 16:2); Hagar and the boy nearly die of
  thirst; *"God heard the voice of the boy"* (21:17); a well is opened; and she ends the episode free
  in the wilderness of Paran, choosing an Egyptian wife for her son. She is the only woman in Genesis
  who acquires a wife for her child — a father's prerogative everywhere else in the book. See
  [[The Two Flights of Hagar]].
- **Genesis 25:12** — named one last time, in the heading of Ishmael's *toledot*: *"whom Hagar the
  Egyptian, Sarah's servant, bore to Abraham"*. Still the Egyptian, still the servant.
- **Galatians 4:21–31** — Paul's allegory, the only mention of her in the New Testament, in which she
  stands for Sinai, slavery, and the present Jerusalem. It is an argument about covenants that uses
  her name, not a reading of her story. See [[Hagar and Sarah in Galatians]].
- **In later tradition** — *Genesis Rabbah* 61:4 and Rashi identify her with Keturah (Genesis 25:1),
  taken back after Sarah's death; other midrash makes her a daughter of Pharaoh given to Abram in
  Genesis 12. In Islam, Hājar is a central figure: the well of Genesis 16:14 is identified with Zamzam
  at Mecca, and her search for water between Ṣafā and Marwa is re-enacted by every pilgrim on the
  hajj. None of this is in Genesis; all of it is reception history and the wiki records it as such.

## Timeline / key events

- An Egyptian slave in Sarai's possession; plausibly among the *"female servants"* Abram was paid for
  his wife in Egypt (12:16) — Genesis 16:1, [[Hagar and Ishmael]]
- Given by Sarai to Abram as a wife, in Abram's tenth year in Canaan — Genesis 16:3
- Conceives, and lets her mistress see what she thinks of her — Genesis 16:4
- Afflicted by Sarai with Abram's permission, and flees toward Egypt — Genesis 16:6
- Found by the angel of Yahweh at a spring on the way to Shur; addressed by name and questioned —
  Genesis 16:7–8
- Told to return and submit; promised offspring beyond counting; told her son's name and its meaning
  — Genesis 16:9–12
- Names God *ʾĒl Rŏʾî*, *a God who sees*; the well is called Beer Lahai Roi — Genesis 16:13–14
- Bears Ishmael when Abram is eighty-six — Genesis 16:15–16
- Cast out with Ishmael after Isaac's weaning; nearly dies in the wilderness of Beersheba; God hears
  the boy and opens her eyes to a well — Genesis 21:9–19 *(not yet ingested)*
- Settles with Ishmael in the wilderness of Paran and takes him a wife out of Egypt — Genesis
  21:20–21 *(not yet ingested)*
- Named in the heading of Ishmael's genealogy — Genesis 25:12 *(not yet ingested)*
