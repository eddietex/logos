# Man of Benjamin (of Eli's time)

## Overview

### The runner from the battle — 1 Samuel 4:12–17

*A man of Benjamin ran out of the army, and came to Shiloh the same day, with his clothes torn, and with
dirt on his head* (4:12). He is not named. *ʾÎš-binyāmin* (WLC) is the form for a member of the tribe;
the period qualifier follows [[Man of God (of Eli's time)]], because the same words, *a man of Benjamin*, introduce
Saul's father Kish at 9:1, and Saul calls himself *a Benjamite* (9:21). The torn clothes and the earth on the
head are mourning signs before he speaks (compare Joshua 7:6). He tells the city first, and *all the city
cried out* (13); then he hurries to Eli, who cannot see him, and identifies himself: *I am he who came out
of the army, and I fled today out of the army* (16). The narrator calls him *hamməḇaśśēr*, *he who brought
the news* (17, WLC); in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel, the Piel of *bāśar* occurs only here and
at 31:9. His report rises from the army to Eli's sons to the ark: *Israel has fled before the Philistines,
and there has been also a great slaughter among the people. Your two sons also, Hophni and Phinehas, are
dead, and God's ark has been captured* (17). At the last clause Eli falls and dies (18).

The Amalekite who brings David word of Saul's death is introduced in nearly the same words, *with his
clothes torn, and earth on his head* (2 Samuel 1:2, WEB checked at bible-api.com). A late Jewish tradition
identifies this runner as Saul (recalled from secondary literature, not checked); the text does not say so.
