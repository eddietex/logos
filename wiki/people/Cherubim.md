# Cherubim

## Overview

**Heavenly figures who stand at the boundary of God's presence.** Hebrew ***kərûb***, plural
***kərubîm*** (MAM). Scripture never introduces them. They first appear already in place at Genesis
3:24, and the reader is expected to know what they are.

**In Genesis and Exodus they do two things: they guard, and they frame.**

- **They guard.** At the end of Eden, *"he placed cherubim at the east of the garden of Eden, and a
  flaming sword which turned every way, to guard the way to the tree of life"* (Genesis 3:24). The verb is
  ***wayyaškēn***, the hiphil of ***šākan*** (MAM), *he caused to dwell* or *stationed*. It is the
  verb of Exodus 25:8, *"that I may dwell among them"*.
- **They frame.** On the ark, two cherubim of hammered gold are made *"of one piece with the mercy
  seat"*. Their wings are spread upward over it and their faces turned *"toward one another"* and down
  toward it (Exodus 25:18–20). The space between them is where God promises to speak: *"from between the
  two cherubim"* (25:22). Cherubim are also worked into the inner curtains of the tabernacle (26:1).

**Exodus does not describe them.** It gives them wings and faces and nothing else: no number of faces,
no bodies, no size. Genesis gives them nothing at all. Later texts say more. Ezekiel's visions describe
cherubim in detail, and later Israel names God *"Yahweh of Armies, who sits above the cherubim"*
(1 Samuel 4:4; *yōšēb hakkərubîm*, MAM; compare Psalm 80:1). Those are *not yet ingested*, and the wiki
does not read their details back into Genesis or Exodus. **They are not the winged infants of later
European art.** Composite guardian figures beside thrones and temple doors are well attested in the
ancient Near East, and many scholars compare the biblical cherubim with them. That comparison is
reported here, not argued.

**They are made images, commanded by the God who forbade images** (Exodus 20:4). The wiki sets out the
tension in [[The Ten Commandments]] and [[Idolatry]]. The text's own answer, as far as it gives one, is
that the cherubim are not worshipped and that the space they frame is empty.

**The figures now extend across textiles.** Exodus 26:1, 31 prescribes cherubim on the inner
curtains and on the veil separating the holy place from the most holy. The screen at the tent's
entrance has no cherubim specified (26:36). Their number and appearance on the textiles are not
given, and their role as guardians here is an Eden-based interpretation, not an explicit statement
of Exodus. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].

  [[The Fall]]

  other; God will speak *"from between the two cherubim"* — Exodus 25:18–22,
  [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]]

- *"Cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat"* — Hebrews 9:5, *not yet ingested*
