# Mahalath

## Overview

**Esau's third wife, married for a reason** — and the only one of his wives whose marriage Genesis
explains.

She is named once, in a single verse, and the verse gives her a father, a brother, a grandfather, and
a husband before it gives her anything of her own: *"Esau went to Ishmael, and took, besides the
wives that he had, Mahalath the daughter of Ishmael, Abraham's son, the sister of Nebaioth, to be his
wife"* (28:9). **She does not speak, is not described, bears no recorded child under this name, and
never appears again.** See [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]].

**What she is in the narrative is a correction that misses.** [[Esau]] has just watched his brother
be blessed and sent to Paddan Aram with an explicit ban on Canaanite wives, and he draws the right
conclusion — *"the daughters of Canaan didn't please Isaac, his father"* (28:8). **His remedy is to
marry inside the family, and the branch of the family he reaches is [[Ishmael]]'s**, which the
promise had already stepped past at 21:12. **She is his first cousin**, since Ishmael and [[Isaac]]
are half-brothers, and the marriage joins the two elder sons the covenant passed over. **He also
keeps the wives he already had** — *"besides the wives that he had"* — so [[Judith]] and
[[Basemath (daughter of Elon)]] remain, and the grievance of 26:35 is untouched.

**Her identification runs through her brother, not only her father.** *"The sister of Nebaioth"* is
how Genesis marks the male relative who negotiates a marriage: [[Rebekah]] is introduced as *"the
sister of Laban"* and it is [[Laban]] who answers for her at 24:29, 50, and 55. **Nebaioth is
Ishmael's firstborn** (25:13) and is never seen; the prophets keep him as a nation whose rams serve
at the altar (Isaiah 60:7).

**Genesis calls her something else ten chapters later.** The register at 36:3 lists Esau's wives as
Adah daughter of Elon, Oholibamah daughter of Anah, and ***Basemath, Ishmael's daughter, sister of
Nebaioth*** — the same patronymic and the same brother, under a different name, while *Basemath* in
the earlier notice belonged to Elon's daughter instead. **The wiki flags the disagreement and adopts
no harmonization**, per its standing practice; the case is set out in full at
[[The Names of Esau's Wives - Genesis 26 and Genesis 36]].

**The name itself is not securely explained.** *Maḥălat* is usually connected either to *māḥôl*,
**dance**, or to *ḥālâ*, **to be weak or sick**, and neither is better than a proposal. It recurs as
a musical or liturgical term in the headings of Psalms 53 and 88, where it is untranslated in most
English versions, and as the name of Rehoboam's wife at 2 Chronicles 11:18 — **a granddaughter of
David, and unrelated.** The etymologies are recorded here as attributions, not findings.

## Timeline / key events

- Born to [[Ishmael]], sister of Nebaioth his firstborn — implied by Genesis 25:13; 28:9
- Married by [[Esau]] after he sees that Canaanite wives displease his father, in addition to the
  wives he already had — Genesis 28:9, [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]]
- Listed as **Basemath**, *"Ishmael's daughter, sister of Nebaioth"*, in the register of Esau's wives
  — Genesis 36:3 *(not yet ingested)*

## Appears in

- [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]] — Genesis 28:9; the last verse of the pericope and the whole of
  her narrative existence. She is the object of the only decision Genesis ever shows Esau reasoning
  his way to, and the reasoning is sound up to the final step

## Related people

- [[Esau]] — her husband, who marries her as a corrective and does not put away the wives the
  correction was for
- [[Ishmael]] — her father, dead by roughly thirteen years at this point on the book's own arithmetic
  (16:16; 21:5; 25:17), which is why *"went to Ishmael"* is ordinarily read as *went to Ishmael's
  household*
- [[Abraham]] — her grandfather, named in her own verse as the thing that makes the match a family
  match at all
- [[Judith]] and [[Basemath (daughter of Elon)]] — the Hittite wives she is added to rather than
  substituted for
- [[Isaac]] — her father-in-law, whose displeasure is the stated cause of the marriage and who is
  never said to be reconciled by it
