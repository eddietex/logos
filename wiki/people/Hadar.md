# Hadar

## Overview

**The eighth and last king in Edom's list, and the only one Genesis leaves alive.**

> **39** Baal Hanan the son of Achbor died, and Hadar reigned in his place. The name of his city was
> Pau. His wife’s name was Mehetabel, the daughter of Matred, the daughter of Mezahab.

**Seven times the formula has run *X died, and Y reigned in his place*. The eighth time it stops.**
Hadar takes the throne, his city is named, his wife is named and given a pedigree — and there is no
death notice. **The register simply ends with a king on the throne**, which is the ordinary way a
king list behaves when it is compiled during a reign rather than after one. See
[[The Descendants of Esau]] and [[Kingship and Empire]].

**The Chronicler noticed and fixed it.** 1 Chronicles 1:50–51 reads the name as **Hadad** and adds
*"Hadad died"* before continuing to the chiefs — **a later hand closing an open list**, and one of the
clearest small signs of how the two versions of this register relate. A number of Hebrew manuscripts
and the Samaritan Pentateuch read *Hadad* at Genesis 36:39 as well; *dalet* and *resh* are all but
identical in the square script, and the confusion is one of the commonest in the Hebrew Bible.

**Whether he is Hadad or Hadar changes what the name means.** *Hadad* is the Canaanite and Aramean
storm god, borne also by the fourth king in this list —
see [[Hadad (son of Bedad)]]. *Hădar* is a Hebrew word for **splendour** or **majesty**. **The reading
that is probably original is the theophoric one**, and the one the Masoretic text of Genesis preserves
is the flattering one.

## His wife

**[[Mehetabel]] is the only woman in the king list**, and she is given a genealogy two generations
deep entirely through women: *"the daughter of Matred, the daughter of Mezahab."* **No other woman in
the Hebrew Bible is traced that way.** Genesis supplies it for the wife of a foreign king in the last
verse of a register and never explains why.
