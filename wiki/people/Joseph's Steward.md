# Joseph's Steward

## Overview

**The unnamed head of [[Joseph]]'s household in Egypt, and the only servant in the Joseph cycle whose
speech Genesis records.** He appears in two chapters and does three things: he tells ten terrified
foreigners that their God has given them treasure (43:23), he serves the feast that [[Benjamin]]'s
arrival occasions, and he plants a silver cup in a sack and then rides out to accuse the men he
planted it on (44:1–12).

**Hebrew calls him ***ʾăšer ʿal-bêtô***, *the one who is over his house*** — and ***over his house***
is the phrase Genesis has been using of [[Joseph]] himself. [[Potiphar]] *"made him overseer **over
his house**"* (39:4); Pharaoh tells him *"You shall be **over my house**"* (41:40). **The third man to
hold the position is the first one Genesis does not name.**

**His page exists because he speaks.** The standing line in this wiki is that unnamed functionaries
with no recorded words get no page — the interpreter of 42:23, the keeper of the prison (39:21–23),
the magicians of 41:8. **He crosses it twice over**: a sentence about God at 43:23 that is
entirely his own, an accusation delivered on the road (44:6, speaking the words Joseph gave him at
44:4–5), a ruling of his own devising at 44:10, and a role that drives the plot of an entire chapter. The comparison is
[[Abraham's Servant]], who is also unnamed, also a chief of household, and also entrusted with the
errand that decides a family's future.

## Timeline / key events

- **Genesis 43:16–17** — Joseph, seeing Benjamin, gives him three orders: *"Bring the men into the
  house, and butcher an animal, and prepare; for the men will dine with me at noon."* **He does it
  without comment**, and the narrator repeats the doing in full (*"The man did as Joseph commanded,
  and the man brought the men to Joseph's house"*), **which is how Genesis marks a servant who is
  exactly reliable**
- **Genesis 43:19–23** — the brothers intercept him at the door with a rehearsed speech about the
  money in their sacks. **His reply is the theological centre of the chapter**: *"Peace be to you.
  Don't be afraid. Your God, and the God of your father, has given you treasure in your sacks. I
  received your money."* **Then he brings [[Simeon]] out to them**
- **Genesis 43:24** — water for their feet, fodder for their donkeys: **the hospitality formula of
  18:4 and 24:32, performed by an Egyptian for Hebrews**
- **Genesis 44:1–2** — he fills the sacks, returns the money a second time, and puts Joseph's silver
  cup in Benjamin's. *"He did according to the word that Joseph had spoken"* — **the same formula of
  exact obedience as 43:17, now in service of a frame-up**
- **Genesis 44:4–13** — he overtakes them on the road, delivers the accusation, hears their oath
  (*"With whomever of your servants it is found, let him die"*), **amends the sentence himself**
  (*"he with whom it is found will be my slave; and you will be blameless"*), and searches from the
  eldest to the youngest

## The sentence at 43:23

> **23** He said, "Peace be to you. Don't be afraid. Your God, and the God of your father, has given
> you treasure in your sacks. I received your money."

**Both halves are true and he has no way of knowing either.**

*"Your God, and the God of your father, has given you treasure"* — ***maṭmôn***, *hidden treasure*,
the word for buried silver. **He calls the thing that has terrified them for a year a gift**, and he
attributes it to a God who is not his. **Joseph gave the order at 42:25; the steward gives the
credit to [[God]]** — and he is, as the reader knows and he does not, **describing [[Providence]]
correctly.**

*"I received your money"* — ***kaspəkem bāʾ ʾēlāy***, *your money came to me.* **Either he is
covering for his master with a kindly lie, or the household books were squared out of Joseph's own
funds and the sentence is literally true.** Genesis does not say.

**What makes the verse remarkable is where it sits.** The narrator has said nothing about God since
39:23 and will say nothing until 46:2; every mention in between is inside a character's quotation
marks (41:16, 25, 32; 42:18, 28; 43:14, 29). **The most accurate of them all is spoken by an Egyptian
butler to men who do not believe him.**

## Related people

- [[Joseph]] — his master, and **the man whose own career began in this job** (39:4). Every order the
  steward receives is executed exactly, **including the one that frames an innocent man**
- [[Simeon]] — **released by his hand** at 43:23, in four words, with no reunion recorded
- [[Benjamin]] — **served by him at the feast and framed by him on the road.** The cup goes into his
  sack at 44:2 and is found there at 44:12
- [[Abraham's Servant]] — **the wiki's other unnamed chief of household**, sent on the errand that
  secures the next generation (24:2–67). **Both are trusted with a family's future and neither is
  given a name**; the difference is that Abraham's servant prays (24:12) and this one reports what
  someone else's God has done
- [[Potiphar]] — **the household Joseph learned this office in.** *ʾăšer ʿal-bêtô* runs from 39:4 to
  43:16 without comment

## Appears in

- [[The Second Journey]] — Genesis 43:16–17, 19–25
- **Genesis 44:1–13** *(not yet ingested)* — the silver cup, the pursuit, and the amended sentence

## Themes associated

- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — **he stands between Joseph and the brothers in both directions**: the
  one who tells them not to be afraid, and the one who runs them down
- [[Hospitality]] — water, feet, fodder, a slaughtered beast (43:16, 24), **and then three tables**
- [[Providence]] — **he states it plainly** (43:23) **in the middle of the book's longest stretch of
  narratorial silence about God**
- [[Deception]] — **an honest servant executing a deception**, twice, without a recorded opinion
  about either
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — **he is the household's chief servant**, he softens a death oath into
  slavery at 44:10, and the brothers' fear at 43:18 is of being made what he is
