# Asenath

## Overview

**The mother of two tribes of Israel, and an Egyptian priest's daughter who never speaks.**

> **45** Pharaoh called Joseph’s name Zaphenath-Paneah; and he gave him Asenath, the daughter of
> Potiphera priest of On as a wife. Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.

**She is named three times in Genesis and every one is the same formula** — *Asenath, the daughter of
Potiphera priest of On* (41:45, 50; 46:20). **The book never varies it, never shortens it, and never
gives her a line, an age, an opinion, or a death.** She is identified by her father and her father's
office on all three occasions, **including the verse that records her giving birth.**

### The name

***ʾĀsənat*** is Egyptian, and the usual explanation is ***ns-nt***, ***she belongs to Neith*** —
**Neith being the ancient goddess of Sais, a war and weaving deity and one of the oldest in the Egyptian
pantheon.** Other proposals read the first element as *iw.s-n-* , *she belongs to*, with a different
divine name. **What the proposals have in common is that her name is theophoric and the god in it is not
Israel's.**

**Genesis does not remark on this**, any more than it remarks on her father's priesthood or on
[[Potiphar]]'s name, which contains the sun god. See [[Names and Naming]] and [[Idolatry]].

### The marriage Genesis does not comment on

**This is the most conspicuous silence attaching to her.**

Genesis is a book that **devotes a whole chapter to finding a wife for [[Isaac]] from the right family**
(24:3–4), **records [[Esau]]'s Hittite marriages as a grief to his parents** (26:35), **has [[Rebekah]]
use that grief as her argument for sending [[Jacob]] away** (27:46), and **has Isaac repeat his father's
charge to Jacob almost word for word** (28:1–2).

**Then Pharaoh gives Joseph an Egyptian wife and the narrator moves on in the same sentence.**

- **No negotiation, no bride-price, no oath, no messenger, no journey.** Every other covenant marriage in
  Genesis is a transaction with terms; **this one is a gift from an employer.**
- **No consent recorded from either party.** *"He gave him Asenath"* — Joseph is the indirect object.
- **No objection, and no defence.** Genesis neither justifies the marriage as unavoidable nor flags it as
  a compromise.

**The wiki records the silence as a silence and declines to fill it.** See
[[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] and [[Marriage]].

### What she is the mother of

**Two sons, born in the seven years of plenty and before the famine** (41:50): [[Manasseh]] and
[[Ephraim]]. **Jacob adopts both as his own at 48:5** — *"Ephraim and Manasseh, even as Reuben and Simeon,
will be mine"* — **which makes her, in the genealogies that follow, the mother of two of the twelve
tribes of Israel.** At the census of Numbers 1, **Ephraim and Manasseh together outnumber every other
tribe but Judah.**

**She is therefore an Egyptian woman from a sun-cult household who stands in the ancestry of the whole
northern kingdom**, and **the Hebrew Bible never mentions the fact again after 46:20.**

### The later tradition, and why the wiki keeps it separate

**Because Genesis says so little, later readers said a great deal.**

- **Rabbinic tradition in several places makes her the daughter of [[Dinah]]**, conceived at Shechem and
  raised in Egypt — **which converts the problem marriage into an Israelite one.**
- **A related strand identifies [[Potiphera]] with [[Potiphar]]**, making Asenath the daughter of
  [[Potiphar's Wife]] — **which ties the reward to the humiliation.**
- ***Joseph and Aseneth***, a Greek romance usually dated between the first century BC and the second
  century AD, gives her a conversion narrative, a repentance, and a long speech. **It is the only
  extended treatment of her in antiquity and it is a work of fiction about a woman the Bible leaves
  blank.**

**None of this is in Genesis, and the wiki holds all of it at arm's length.** The text supplies a name,
a father, a father's office, a city, and two sons.

## Appears in

- [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]] — Genesis 41:45, 50; **the only woman in the chapter, and she
  is given rather than married.** Pharaoh hands her over in the same verse that renames Joseph, **so the
  wife and the Egyptian name are two halves of one act of assimilation** — the third, in the verse
  before, was the shaving. **She bears two sons in the years of plenty and the text names her by her
  father's office even then.** Her sons' naming speeches are Joseph's and mention God twice; **they do
  not mention her**
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 46:20; ***"Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On."***
  **The register gives an Egyptian woman a full pedigree inside Israel's founding roll**, and gives one
  to nobody else in it. **It flags [[Shaul (son of Simeon)]]'s Canaanite mother, honours her, and passes
  over [[Shua's Daughter]] in silence** — **three foreign mothers, three treatments, and Genesis offers
  no rule**

## Related people

- [[Joseph]] — her husband, given to her by a king, on a day she is not recorded as having any part in
- [[Potiphera]] — her father, *priest of On*, and the phrase the text attaches to her every time
- [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]] — her sons, and through [[Jacob]]'s adoption at 48:5, two tribes of Israel
- [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] — who arranges the marriage as part of Joseph's investiture
- [[Potiphar]] — no relation the text asserts, though **the two men's names are the same Egyptian name**
  and a later tradition joined the households; see [[Potiphera]]
- [[Rebekah]] and [[Rachel]] — the wives the book went to great lengths to source from the right family,
  **against whom the brevity of 41:45 is measured**
