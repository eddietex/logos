# The Daughters of Jerusalem

## Overview

A collective group in [[Song of Solomon]], addressed by the woman again and again. They are
the Song's standing audience. Like [[Lot's Daughters]], the group is given a page because
it recurs and does things in the poems. It is not a historical body of women
whose membership can be identified. The book never names any of them.

They first appear in [[Dark and Lovely]], where the woman addresses them: *"I am dark, but
lovely, you daughters of Jerusalem"* (1:5). They are the onlookers she asks not to stare
(1:6). Whether they also give the answer in 1:8, *"most beautiful among women"*, is open.
The WEB supplies no speaker cue there, and the role pages leave it undecided.

In [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]] they are put under oath for the first time: *"I adjure
you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, or by the hinds of the field, that you not stir up,
nor awaken love, until it so desires"* (2:7). They do not answer. Their role here is to be
bound by the woman's oath. Whether the charge means *do not hurry love* or *do not disturb
these lovers* is left open on the passage page. The *"daughters"* among whom she is a lily
(2:2) are young women in general and are not counted as this group.

In [[Seeking Him by Night]] the woman adjures them a second time, in exactly the words of 2:7,
the Hebrew identical letter for letter (3:5; Sefaria, *Miqra according to the Masorah*). Again
they do not answer, and again the oath follows a scene of the lovers together. They take no part
in the night search itself; the city of 3:2–3 is unnamed and is not identified with Jerusalem.

In [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]] they are named twice, under two names. The inside of Solomon's
carriage is *"paved with love, from the daughters of Jerusalem"* (3:10). The phrase may mean they
inlaid it as a gift of love, or that it was decorated with love by them; the passage page sets out
the readings. Then *"daughters of Zion"* are summoned: *"Go out … and see king Solomon"* (3:11). The
Hebrew command is a double feminine plural imperative, *ṣǝʾênâ ûrǝʾênâ* (Sefaria, *Miqra according
to the Masorah*). *Daughters of Zion* appears only here in the Song. Because it stands in parallel
with *daughters of Jerusalem* in the verse before, and [[Zion]] stands for Jerusalem in Hebrew poetry,
this page treats the two as one group. That is a reading, not a statement of the text. For the first
time they are not adjured but told to look; they are the audience of a public spectacle.

In [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]] they are adjured a third time, but not with the refrain. After the
watchmen have beaten her, the woman puts them under oath with a request: *"If you find my beloved,
that you tell him that I am faint with love"* (5:8). For the first time she asks them to act on her
behalf, as go-betweens. The Hebrew is literally *what will you tell him?* (Sefaria, *Miqra according
to the Masorah*), and their reply in the next verse, *"that you do so adjure us?"* (5:9; now
ingested in [[Altogether Lovely]]), shows them answering the oath for the first time.

Later stored verses, not yet ingested, repeat the refrain (8:4).

The name connects them with [[Jerusalem]] without saying they live in the city in a
particular scene. The Song makes them an audience and, at times, a chorus. It does not say
whether they are friends, attendants, or rivals, and the page does not decide.

Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon, read for the queue; only 1:1–6:3 is ingested.

In [[Altogether Lovely]] the questioners ask how her beloved differs from another and why she
adjures them (5:9). After her description ends in an address to the daughters themselves
(5:16), they ask where he has gone and offer to seek him with her (6:1). The identification
of the questioners with the daughters follows this dialogue; the stored WEB gives no
speaker labels in these verses. They call her fairest among women and offer cooperation;
the opening question is not enough to establish mockery or rivalry. Source: stored WEB
Song of Solomon 5:9–6:3.

## Timeline / key events

- Song of Solomon 1:5–6 — addressed by the woman, who answers their look by calling herself
  lovely. [[Dark and Lovely]]
- Song of Solomon 2:7 — put under oath in the first refrain not to awaken love.
  [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]]
- Song of Solomon 3:5 — put under oath a second time, in the same words. [[Seeking Him by Night]]
- Song of Solomon 3:10–11 — the source of the carriage's inlay of love; as *"daughters of Zion"*,
  summoned to see king Solomon crowned. [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]]
- Song of Solomon 5:8 — put under oath a third time, to tell the beloved she is faint with love.
  [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]]

- Song of Solomon 5:9–6:3 — question what makes her beloved special, receive her description, and offer
  to help seek him (5:9, 16; 6:1).

## Appears in

- [[Dark and Lovely]] — Song of Solomon 1:5–8; addressed in 1:5, and possibly the voices of 1:8.
- [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]] — Song of Solomon 1:9–2:7; adjured in 2:7.
- [[Seeking Him by Night]] — Song of Solomon 3:1–5; adjured again in 3:5.
- [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]] — Song of Solomon 3:6–11; named in 3:10, and, as the daughters
  of Zion, summoned in 3:11.
- [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]] — Song of Solomon 5:2–8; adjured in 5:8 to carry her message.
- [[Altogether Lovely]] — Song of Solomon 5:9–6:3.

## Related people

- [[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]] — the speaker who addresses them.
- [[The Man (Song of Solomon)]] — the beloved she asks them to find and tell (5:8); they ask about him in
  [[Altogether Lovely]] (5:9–6:1).
- [[Solomon]] — named in the comparison of 1:5; the king they are summoned to see (3:11).
- [[The Watchmen (Song of Solomon)]] — the Song's other collective; the woman turns to the daughters
  after the watchmen beat her (5:7–8).

## Themes associated

- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — the onlookers whose stare the woman answers (1:6); told to go out
  and see the king (3:11).
- [[Love]] — addressees of the Song's adjurations about love (2:7; 3:5), and the message *"I am faint with love"* (5:8); the
  refrain of 8:4 not yet ingested.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — put under oath *"by the roes, or by the hinds of the field"* (2:7; 3:5); and
  without the animals, to carry a message (5:8).
