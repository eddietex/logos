# The Nearer Kinsman (Ruth)

## Overview

A kinsman of [[Elimelech]]'s family in [[Bethlehem]], closer in the order of redemption than [[Boaz]],
and never named. Boaz tells [[Ruth (person)]] of him at the threshing floor: *there is a kinsman nearer
than I* (Ruth 3:12). The next morning he passes the gate, and Boaz calls him over with *pəlōnî ʾalmōnî*
(4:1, WLC), which the WEB renders *friend* and which means *so-and-so*. The page is named by his role
and the book, because the text gives him no other name.

**At the gate.** Before ten elders Boaz offers him the first right to redeem the parcel of land that was
Elimelech's, and he accepts: *I will redeem it* (4:3–4). When Boaz adds that the day he buys the field he
acquires it also from *Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead on his
inheritance* (4:5), he withdraws: *I can't redeem it for myself, lest I endanger my own inheritance.
Take my right of redemption for yourself* (4:6). He says *Buy it for yourself* and draws off his shoe
(4:8), the custom the narrator explains for confirming a redemption (4:7).

**How the book treats him.** The narrator passes no verdict. He acts within his rights: Deuteronomy's
levirate duty binds a brother (Deuteronomy 25:5), and he is not one, and the land law of Leviticus 25:25
says the kinsman *shall come, and redeem*, but names no widow. His reason, *lest I endanger my own
inheritance*, uses *šāḥaṯ*, *ruin*, the verb of Onan's refusal at Genesis 38:9 (WLC); Onan is condemned,
this man is not. His anonymity is the clearest mark the book puts on him: in a chapter about raising up
*the name of the dead*, the one man who declined to do it is left without a name. That contrast is a
reader's observation; the text does not draw it. Deuteronomy's refuser is given a byword in place of a
name, *The house of him who had his shoe removed* (Deuteronomy 25:10); this man draws off his own shoe and
is given nothing.

**What he would have lost.** The text says only *my own inheritance*. The usual explanation is that a son
by Ruth would inherit Elimelech's land in Mahlon's name, so that the price would be paid out of the
kinsman's estate for land that left it (commonly given; the text does not spell it out). The written form
of 4:5, *I acquire*, read as *you acquire* (WLC ketiv and qere), bears on what he was told; see
[[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]].
