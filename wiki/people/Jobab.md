# Jobab

## Overview

**The second king of Edom, named in half a verse — and the man the Septuagint decided was Job.**

> **33** Bela died, and Jobab, the son of Zerah of Bozrah, reigned in his place.

That is everything Genesis says. A father, a home town, a predecessor, and a successor: *"Jobab died,
and Husham of the land of the Temanites reigned in his place"* (36:34). **He is one of eight kings in
a list where no son succeeds a father** and every entry is the same formula — see
[[The Descendants of Esau]]. His city, [[Bozrah]], is the one royal seat in the list that can still
be located, and it is the city Isaiah 63:1 and Amos 1:12 aim their oracles against.

## The identification with Job

**The Greek translators of the book of Job appended a note to its last verse**, and that note is the
earliest surviving attempt to say where the book of Job happens and who its hero was:

> This man is described in the Syriac book as living in the land of Ausitis, on the borders of Idumea
> and Arabia … He was the fifth from Abraham. And these were the kings who reigned in Edom … Balak
> the son of Beor … and after him Jobab, who is called Job.

**The appendix supplies a genealogy Job does not have** — making him a son of Zerah, a grandson of
Reuel, a great-grandson of Esau — and identifies him outright with the Jobab of Genesis 36:33. It is a
harmonisation, not evidence: **the book of Job never calls its hero a king, never names his father,
never mentions Edom, and gives him no throne to lose** among the extremely detailed losses of chapter
1. And the arithmetic does not work — Zerah of Bozrah is a Reuelite name, but the appendix has to
assume it.

**What makes the guess intelligible is that everything else points the same direction.** Job lives in
[[Uz]], and Lamentations 4:21 puts Uz in Edomite hands. His first friend is
[[Eliphaz the Temanite]], and [[Teman]] is Edomite and proverbial for wisdom. Genesis 36 contains a
Uz (36:28), an Eliphaz (36:4), a Teman (36:11), and a Jobab — **four of the book of Job's names in one
chapter.** See [[Jobab of Bozrah and the Septuagint's Job]].

**The wiki records the identification and does not adopt it.** It is the reason the Edomite reading of
Uz dominates later Christian tradition, and the reason the book of Job is often printed with a
patriarchal setting; it is also a second-century-BC inference from the same coincidence of names
anybody can see.

## The name

***Yôbāb*** and ***ʾIyyôb*** are not the same word. *Job* is probably from the root *ʾyb*, **to be
hostile** — *the one treated as an enemy*, which the book exploits — or from an Amorite name meaning
*where is the father?*. *Jobab* looks like a reduplicated form of a different root. **The Greek
rendered both as *Ιωβ-* and the distinction stopped being audible**, which is very likely how the
identification started.

Three other Jobabs appear in Scripture, none related: a son of Joktan (Genesis 10:29), a king of
Madon (Joshua 11:1), and two Benjaminites (1 Chronicles 8).
