# Nahor (brother of Abram)

## Overview

The brother who stays, and whose household ends up supplying wives to the next two generations of
the family that left.

He is named three times in the pericope and is the subject of a verb exactly once. Terah fathers
*"Abram, Nahor, and Haran"* (11:26, 11:27); *"Abram and Nahor married wives … the name of Nahor's
wife was Milcah, the daughter of Haran"* (11:29) — so he marries his own niece, his dead brother's
daughter. And then, when the family leaves: *"Terah took Abram his son, Lot the son of Haran, his
son's son, and Sarai his daughter-in-law"* (11:31). **Nahor and Milcah are not on the list.** Genesis
does not say he refused, or was left, or followed later; it simply does not include him, and the
omission is the only characterization he gets.

**He carries his grandfather's name.** [[Nahor (son of Serug)]] is Terah's father, so the register
ends with a Nahor and the household begins with another — which is why both pages in this wiki are
qualified, and why *"the city of Nahor"* (24:10) and *"the God of Nahor"* (31:53) are genuinely
ambiguous as to which man is meant.

### Why he matters later

Nahor's branch is the one Genesis keeps in view. Genesis 22:20–24 reports that Milcah bore him eight
sons, the last of them **Bethuel**, *"and Bethuel became the father of Rebekah"* — a notice inserted
into the Abraham narrative at exactly the point where a wife for Isaac becomes the outstanding
problem. Abraham then sends his servant back *"to my country and to my relatives"*, to *"the city of
Nahor"* (24:4, 10), and Rebekah comes from there. A generation later Jacob flees to the same
household and marries Leah and Rachel, daughters of Laban, Rebekah's brother (28:2; 29:4–28).

So the family divides in Genesis 11 and the two halves stay married to each other for three
generations. The line that received the promise takes its wives from the line that stayed behind in
Mesopotamia, which is one of the quieter structural facts of the patriarchal narratives and is set up
by a single omission from a travel list.

**Genesis 31:53 puts him in a sentence with his brother's God.** Laban's oath at the boundary heap:
*"The God of Abraham, and the God of Nahor, the God of their father, judge between us."* Whether
those are one God under three descriptions or two family deities named side by side is disputed —
Jacob's response, swearing *"by the fear of his father Isaac"*, is often read as a deliberate refusal
of the equation. Read with Joshua 24:2's *"they served other gods"*, the verse is the closest thing
in Scripture to a glimpse of what the branch that stayed behind worshipped. See
[[Out of Ur - Terah's Journey and Abram's Call]].
