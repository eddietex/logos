# Nahor (brother of Abram)

## Overview

The brother who stays, and whose household ends up supplying wives to the next two generations of
the family that left.

He is named three times in the pericope and is the subject of a verb exactly once. Terah fathers
*"Abram, Nahor, and Haran"* (11:26, 11:27); *"Abram and Nahor married wives … the name of Nahor's
wife was Milcah, the daughter of Haran"* (11:29) — so he marries his own niece, his dead brother's
daughter. And then, when the family leaves: *"Terah took Abram his son, Lot the son of Haran, his
son's son, and Sarai his daughter-in-law"* (11:31). **Nahor and Milcah are not on the list.** Genesis
does not say he refused, or was left, or followed later; it simply does not include him, and the
omission is the only characterization he gets.

**He carries his grandfather's name.** [[Nahor (son of Serug)]] is Terah's father, so the register
ends with a Nahor and the household begins with another — which is why both pages in this wiki are
qualified, and why *"the city of Nahor"* (24:10) and *"the God of Nahor"* (31:53) are genuinely
ambiguous as to which man is meant.

### Why he matters later

Nahor's branch is the one Genesis keeps in view. Genesis 22:20–24 reports that Milcah bore him eight
sons, the last of them **[[Bethuel]]**, and then *"Bethuel became the father of Rebekah"* — a notice
inserted into the Abraham narrative at exactly the point where a wife for Isaac becomes the
outstanding problem.

**And it reports twelve, not eight.** Four more sons come from a concubine — *"His concubine, whose
name was Reumah"* (22:24), see [[Reumah]], the Bible's first *pîlegeš* — which brings the house to
the same number as Ishmael's
twelve princes and Jacob's twelve sons, and by the same arithmetic of a wife plus a second woman. See
[[Twelve Sons - Nahor, Ishmael, and Jacob]].

**The eleven other names are a map rather than a family.** Uz and Buz are lands (Job 1:1;
Lamentations 4:21; Jeremiah 25:23); Kemuel is *"the father of Aram"*, so the Arameans are his
grandsons; **Chesed** stands behind *Kaśdîm*, the **Chaldeans** of [[Ur of the Chaldees]], the city
his brother left; Maacah is a small Aramean kingdom below Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:14; 2 Samuel 10:6);
Tebah is plausibly the town David plunders at 2 Samuel 8:8. Hazo, Pildash, Jidlaph, Gaham, and Tahash
are attested nowhere else. **The genealogy is Genesis describing the Aramean peoples as a household**,
and none of the eleven has a page in this wiki; they are handled on the passage page, on the
precedent of [[The Table of Nations]].

Abraham then sends his servant back *"to my country, and to my relatives"*, to *"the city of Nahor"*
(24:4, 10), and Rebekah comes from there. A generation later Jacob flees to the same household and
marries Leah and Rachel, daughters of Laban, Rebekah's brother (28:2; 29:4–28).

So the family divides in Genesis 11 and the two halves stay married to each other for three
generations. The line that received the promise takes its wives from the line that stayed behind in
Mesopotamia, which is one of the quieter structural facts of the patriarchal narratives and is set up
by a single omission from a travel list.

**Genesis 31:53 puts him in a sentence with his brother's God.** Laban's oath at the boundary heap:
*"The God of Abraham, and the God of Nahor, the God of their father, judge between us."* Whether
those are one God under three descriptions or two family deities named side by side is disputed —
Jacob's response, swearing *"by the fear of his father Isaac"*, is often read as a deliberate refusal
of the equation. Read with Joshua 24:2's *"they served other gods"*, the verse is the closest thing
in Scripture to a glimpse of what the branch that stayed behind worshipped. See
[[Out of Ur - Terah's Journey and Abram's Call]].

## Timeline / key events

- Born to [[Terah]], second-named of three sons — Genesis 11:26–27
- Marries [[Milcah]], his brother Haran's daughter — Genesis 11:29
- Not named among those who leave Ur with Terah — Genesis 11:31, [[From Shem to Abram]]
- Fathers eight sons by [[Milcah]] and four by his concubine [[Reumah]], including [[Bethuel]],
  Rebekah's father — Genesis 22:20–24, [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]]
- His city is where Abraham's servant finds Rebekah, and 24:15 identifies her by him rather than by
  her father — Genesis 24:10, 15, [[A Wife for Isaac]]
- Named in Laban's oath alongside Abraham — Genesis 31:53 *(not yet ingested)*

## Appears in

- [[From Shem to Abram]] — Genesis 11:26–29
- [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]] — Genesis 22:20–24; twelve sons by two women, reported to
  Abraham in the only piece of news from Mesopotamia in the cycle, and assembled around one clause at
  the end of it
- [[A Wife for Isaac]] — Genesis 24:10, 15; *the city of Nahor* is the servant's destination, and the
  narrator introduces Rebekah as *the wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother*'s granddaughter rather than
  by her own father
- [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]] — Genesis 29:5; **called [[Laban]]'s father**, where 22:22–23, 24:24,
  and 28:2 make him Laban's great-grandfather through [[Bethuel]]. The two standing explanations are
  that ***bēn*** covers *descendant* — as *Jehu son of Nimshi* does at 2 Kings 9:2, 20, and as 28:13's
  *"the God of Abraham your father"* does two chapters earlier for a grandson — or that a household is
  being identified by its head of line, which is how a stranger asking directions would be answered.
  **Neither requires an error and the wiki adopts neither**; it records that Genesis uses two
  filiations for the same man five chapters apart and offers no gloss
