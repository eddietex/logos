# Enosh

## Overview

Seth's son, and the last name in Genesis 4. He does nothing in the text — the whole of his
appearance is *"A son was also born to Seth, and he named him Enosh. At that time men began to call
on Yahweh's name"* (4:26). But the second half of that verse is dated by the first, so what the
chapter finally records is not an act of his but the state of the world in his generation, and the
state of the world is that people have started addressing God.

His name is *ʾĕnôš*, which is simply another Hebrew word for *human being*. It is used mostly in
poetry, and it tends to carry a note of smallness or frailty — *"what is man, that you think of
him?"* (Psalm 8:4) is *ʾĕnôš*. So the opening of the Sethite line reads Adam (*humanity*), Seth
(*appointed*), Enosh (*mortal*): two of the first three names in the line of promise mean nothing
more than *a person*.

What "began" in his time is genuinely disputed. *Qārāʾ bešēm YHWH*, "to call on the name of
Yahweh", covers both petition and public proclamation; it is the phrase used of Abram building
altars (12:8, 13:4) and the phrase Joel and Paul use for salvation itself. Against that, an old
rabbinic reading takes the verb *huḥal* from the homonymous root meaning *profane* and reads 4:26
as the beginning of idolatry rather than of worship. The verse also sits awkwardly beside Exodus
6:3. All of it is set out on [[Calling on the Name of Yahweh]].

Genesis 5:6–11 gives him the formal entry — born when Seth was 105, father of Kenan, dead at 905 —
and Luke 3:38 names him (as *Enos*) four steps from the top of Jesus's genealogy.
