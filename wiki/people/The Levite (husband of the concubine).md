# The Levite (husband of the concubine)

## Overview

### His account at Mizpah — Judges 20:3–7

Asked *how did this wickedness happen?*, the Levite, *the husband of the woman who was murdered*, speaks
once more: *The men of Gibeah rose against me … They intended to kill me, and they raped my concubine, and
she is dead. I took my concubine, and cut her in pieces … for they have committed lewdness and folly in
Israel. Behold, you children of Israel, all of you, give here your advice and counsel* (20:4–7). His account
differs from 19:22–25 in two places. The men demanded to *know* him, and he says *they intended to kill me*.
And it leaves out *the man laid hold of his concubine, and brought her out to them* (19:25). The Hebrew calls
the men *baʿălê haggiḇʿâ*, *the lords of Gibeah* (20:5, WLC). After verse 7 he is not mentioned again; the
war is the assembly's. See [[The War Against Benjamin]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 19:22–25; 20:3–7; Hebrew
WLC, 2026-11-17.

### The journey and the night — Judges 19:1–30

He is never named. *A certain Levite living on the farther side of the hill country of Ephraim, who took
for himself a concubine out of Bethlehem Judah* (19:1). He is *gār*, a sojourner, there (19:1, WLC), as
Micah's Levite was in Bethlehem (17:7); the two are not the same man, and the wiki does not connect them
except by the road between the same two places. See
[[Two Levites from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17 and 19]].

When she leaves him and goes home, he follows after four months *to speak kindly to her, to bring her
again*, literally *to speak to her heart* (19:3; WLC *ləḏabbēr ʿal-libbāh*). Her father keeps him five
days; he refuses the fifth night and leaves at evening (19:4–10). Near Jebus he will not lodge *in the city
of a foreigner that is not of the children of Israel* and goes on to Gibeah of Benjamin (19:11–14). He
tells the old man of Gibeah he is going *to Yahweh's house* (19:18; the Greek is reported to read *my
house* — not checked).

When the men of Gibeah demand him, *the man laid hold of his concubine, and brought her out to them*
(19:25). The Hebrew of 19:25 does not say which man; *his concubine* and his own words at 20:5 point to
him. In the morning he opens the door *to go his way*, finds her with her hands on the threshold, and
says *Get up, and let us be going!* — *but no one answered* (19:27–28). At home he *took a knife*, the
*maʾăḵelet* of Genesis 22, and cut her *limb by limb, into twelve pieces*, and sent them through Israel
(19:29). See [[Cut Limb by Limb - The Burnt Offering, the Concubine, and Saul's Oxen]].

The narrator calls him *her husband*, *ʾîšāh*, when he goes after her (19:3), and *her lord*, *ʾăḏōnehā*,
at the door (19:26–27). The chapter passes no verdict on him. His own account to the assembly at Mizpah —
*They intended to kill me, and they raped my concubine, and she is dead* (20:5, stored WEB; not yet
ingested) — omits who put her outside. Readers divide over whether the text condemns him; the wiki records
the details and not a verdict.

## Timeline / key events
- Takes a concubine from Bethlehem Judah; she leaves him (19:1–2).
- Goes after her; five days at her father's house (19:3–9).
- Passes Jebus, lodges with the old man at Gibeah (19:10–21).
- Puts her out to the men of Gibeah (19:25).
- Finds her at the threshold; cuts her in twelve pieces and sends them through Israel (19:27–30).
- Tells the assembly at Mizpah, and asks for counsel (20:4–7).
