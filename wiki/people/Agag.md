# Agag

## Overview

### King of the Amalekites — 1 Samuel 15

The king of [[Amalek]] in Saul's war. Saul *took Agag the king of the Amalekites alive*, and *Saul and the people
spared Agag, and the best of the sheep* (15:8–9). Saul presents him as proof of obedience, *I … have brought Agag the
king of Amalek* (20). Samuel has him brought: *Agag came to him cheerfully. Agag said, "Surely the bitterness of death
is past."* (32). The word rendered *cheerfully*, *maʿădannōṯ* (WLC), is of uncertain sense (*cheerfully*,
*delicately*, and *in fetters* have been proposed; recalled, not checked), so whether Agag comes relieved or resigned
is not settled. Samuel answers *As your sword has made women childless, so your mother will be childless among
women!* and *cut Agag in pieces before Yahweh in Gilgal* (33). The verb, *wayšassēp̄*, occurs only here in Genesis–2
Samuel (morphhb lemma 8158). Sparing him is part of what costs Saul the kingdom. See [[Saul Rejected over Amalek]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 15:8–9, 20, 32–33. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.

### In Balaam's oracle — Numbers 24:7

Balaam says of Israel *His king shall be higher than Agag* (Numbers 24:7), and in the same speech *Amalek was the
first of the nations* (24:20). The verse gives no ancestry or chronology for Agag. Whether the Agag of the oracle is
the king Saul spared, an earlier king of the same name, or a name borne by Amalek's kings, the text does not say.
This page keeps both under one name and leaves the identity open; it does not adopt the dynastic-title reading.

Source: stored WEB Numbers 23:27–24:25.

### *The Agagite*

Haman in Esther is *the son of Hammedatha the Agagite* (Esther 3:1, WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-20; not
stored). Reading the gentilic as a link to this Agag, and the book as the old war with Amalek, is common (recalled);
Esther 3:1 itself does not explain it.
