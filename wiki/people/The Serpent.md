# The Serpent

## Overview

The speaker who opens [[The Fall]], and one of the most consequential characters in Scripture to
be given so little description. Genesis introduces it in a single clause: *"Now the serpent was
more subtle than any animal of the field which Yahweh God had made"* (3:1). Three things are
established there and nothing else is.

- **It is a creature.** *Which Yahweh God had made* — the serpent stands on the creature side of
  the one line Genesis cares about. There is no rival power in the chapter, no dualism, no
  backstory of rebellion.
- **It is an animal of the field**, ranked among the same creatures the man named in 2:19–20 and
  cursed *"above all livestock, and above every animal of the field"* (3:14).
- **It is *ʿārûm*** — shrewd, subtle, crafty. The word is not pejorative in itself; Proverbs uses
  it for the prudent man who sees trouble coming (12:16, 14:8, 22:3). It is chosen here for its
  sound: the humans in the previous verse are *ʿărummîm*, naked.

Its method is entirely verbal, and it occupies four sentences. It asks a question that misstates
the command (*"any tree"*), lets the woman correct it and watches her misstate it back, denies
the stated consequence (*"You won't really die"*), and supplies a motive for the prohibition
— God is protecting his own prerogative. It never tells her to eat and it never touches the
fruit. After 3:5 it does not speak again, and when God conducts the interrogation it is the only
party not asked a question; it goes straight to sentence.

That sentence — belly, dust, and hostility between its offspring and the woman's, head against
heel (3:14–15) — is the last Genesis has to say about it. It does not appear again in the book.

## The identification with Satan

Genesis makes none. Nothing in the Hebrew Bible identifies this serpent with the *śāṭān* ("the
accuser") who appears in Job 1–2, Zechariah 3, and 1 Chronicles 21 — a title with an article
before it is a name, and a member of the heavenly court rather than an enemy of God. The two
figures are joined later:

- **Wisdom of Solomon 2:24** (2nd–1st c. BC, in the Apocrypha) — *"by the envy of the devil death
  entered into the world"*, the earliest surviving text to make the link.
- **John 8:44** — the devil as *"a murderer from the beginning"* and *"the father of lies"*, which
  reads this scene without naming it.
- **Romans 16:20** — *"the God of peace will quickly crush Satan under your feet"*, applying 3:15.
- **2 Corinthians 11:3** — *"the serpent deceived Eve in his craftiness"*, three verses before
  Paul calls Satan an angel of light.
- **Revelation 12:9 and 20:2** — the decisive verse for later readers, which stacks every name in
  one apposition: *"the great dragon… the old serpent, he who is called the devil and Satan, the
  deceiver of the whole world."*

Whether this draws out something Genesis implies or reads a later theology back into a story
about an animal is a genuine and open question, and worth keeping open rather than collapsing.
What Genesis insists on either way is that temptation reached the humans from *inside* the
created order, through something under God's authority — which is why the chapter can hold
anyone responsible at all.
