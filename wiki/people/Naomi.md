# Naomi

## Overview

### *A son is born to Naomi* — Ruth 4:13–17

The book ends with her. The women who asked *Is this Naomi?* (1:19) bless Yahweh to her, *who has not left
you today without a near kinsman*, and say the child will be to her *a restorer of life* and *sustain you in
your old age* (4:14–15). *Mēšîḇ*, *restorer*, is the hiphil of *šûḇ*; the only other hiphil of the root in
Ruth is her own *Yahweh has brought me home again empty* (*hĕšîḇanî*, 1:21, WLC). They tell her Ruth *loves
you* and *is better to you than seven sons* — she had lost two and said she had no more in her womb (1:5,
11–12). She takes *the child* (*yeleḏ*, the word of her dead sons at 1:5) into her bosom and becomes his
nurse, *ʾōmeneṯ* (4:16), and the neighbors name him with the words *A son is born to Naomi* (17). She says
nothing in the scene; her lament of 1:20–21 is not withdrawn, but answered. Whether 4:16 describes a formal
adoption is debated and not settled here. See [[The Birth of Obed]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:5, 11–12,
19–21; 4:13–17; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### The seller of Elimelech's land — Ruth 4:1–12

At the gate Naomi is named as the one who *is selling the parcel of land, which was our brother
Elimelech's* (4:3), and Boaz buys it *from the hand of Naomi* (5, 9). It is the first the book says of
any land of hers; she said she came home *empty* (1:21), and she had a field to sell. How a widow held it
the book does not explain. She is not present in the scene. See [[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 4:1–12; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### *Shall I not seek rest for you?* — Ruth 3

Naomi, who prayed on the road that Yahweh would grant each widow *rest … in the house of her husband*
(1:9), now acts on it herself: *My daughter, shall I not seek rest for you, that it may be well with you?*
(3:1). She calls Boaz *our kinsman* (2), knows he will be winnowing that night, and gives Ruth the plan in
detail, ending *he will tell you what to do* (3–4). At dawn she greets Ruth with *mî ʾat bittî*, literally
*Who are you, my daughter?* (16, WLC; WEB *How did it go*), receives the barley sent so that Ruth would not
come to her *empty* — her own word of 1:21 — and tells her to wait: *the man will not rest until he has
settled this today* (17–18). The woman who said Yahweh had brought her back empty is now the one who
plans, waits, and is provided for. See [[Ruth at the Threshing Floor]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 3:1–18; 1:9, 21; Hebrew WLC,
2026-11-17.

### *One of our near kinsmen* — Ruth 2

Naomi sends Ruth to glean, *Go, my daughter* (2:2), and at evening receives an ephah of barley and the food
Ruth saved from the meal (2:18). She blesses the man before she knows his name (2:19), and when she hears it
she blesses him by Yahweh, *who has not abandoned his kindness to the living and to the dead*, and says,
*The man is a close relative to us, one of our near kinsmen* (2:20). It is her first blessing since the road
(1:8–9) and the first *gōʾēl* in the book. The woman who said Yahweh had *testified against* her (1:21)
now speaks of kindness that includes her dead. She tells Ruth to stay with Boaz's young women (2:22). See
[[Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:8–9, 21; 2:2, 18–22.

### *Call me Mara* — Ruth 1:19–22

At Bethlehem the whole town is stirred, and the women ask, *Is this Naomi?* (1:19). She refuses the name:
*Don't call me Naomi. Call me Mara; for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me* (1:20). *Mārāʾ*,
*bitter*, is spelled in the WLC with a final *ʾālep* and occurs only here in the files read; the place Marah
of Exodus 15:23 shares the root. *I went out full, and Yahweh has brought me home again empty. Why do you
call me Naomi, since Yahweh has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?* (1:21). She makes
God the subject of four verbs, using *Shaddai* twice and *Yahweh* twice. *Shaddai … hēmar*, *the Almighty
has made bitter*, recurs in the WLC files read only in Job's oath (Job 27:2). She says she came back
*empty*; the narrator's next words are *and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter-in-law, with her* (1:22). The
narrator keeps calling her Naomi. See [[Naomi Returns to Bethlehem]] and [[Call Me Mara - Marah, Naomi, and Job 27.2]]. Source:
stored WEB Ruth 1:19–22; Exodus 15:23; Job 27:2; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### *Go, return* — Ruth 1:6–18

Hearing that *Yahweh had visited his people in giving them bread* (1:6), she sets out for Judah with her
two daughters-in-law, and on the road tries three times to send them back (8, 11–13, 15). She blesses them
first: *May Yahweh deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead, and with me* (8), with the word
*ḥeseḏ*, and wishes them *rest … in the house of her husband* (9). Her argument is the levirate custom
carried to its end: she has no sons left, is too old to marry, and could not ask them to wait for sons born
now (11–13). She reads her losses as *Yahweh's hand has gone out against me* (13), a reading the narrator
never confirms or denies. *Mar-lî*, *it is bitter for me* (13, WLC), anticipates the name Mara (1:20). When
Ruth swears to stay, Naomi *stopped urging her* (18), literally stopped speaking to her. See [[Ruth Clings to Naomi]]. Source:
stored WEB Ruth 1:6–18, 20; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

Wife of [[Elimelech]] of Bethlehem Judah and mother of [[Mahlon]] and [[Chilion]] (Ruth 1:2). She goes to
Moab with her husband and sons in a famine, and there loses all three: *Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died;
and she was left with her two sons* (1:3); *Mahlon and Chilion both died, and the woman was bereaved of her
two children and of her husband* (1:5). The verb in both verses is *šāʾar*, *to be left* (WLC). By verse 5
the narrator calls her only *the woman*.

She is the figure the book is about as much as Ruth is. Its last scene is hers: *Naomi took the child, and
laid him in her bosom* (4:16), and the women say *A son is born to Naomi* (4:17). In the WLC of Ruth the
word for *child*, *yeleḏ*, occurs only at 1:5, of her dead sons, and 4:16, of Obed.

**The name.** *Noʿŏmî* is usually connected with *nʿm*, *pleasant*. She will refuse it on her return: *Don't
call me Naomi. Call me Mara; for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me* (1:20; see above).

See [[Famine and Death in Moab]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:1–5, 20; 4:16–17; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-17.

## Timeline / key events

- Goes to Moab in the famine with Elimelech and her sons — Ruth 1:1–2
- Widowed — Ruth 1:3
- Her sons marry Orpah and Ruth; about ten years in Moab — Ruth 1:4
- Both sons die; she is *left* — Ruth 1:5
- Hears that Yahweh has given bread; sets out for Judah — Ruth 1:6–7
- Urges Orpah and Ruth to go back; Orpah goes, Ruth swears to stay — Ruth 1:8–18
- Arrives in Bethlehem at the start of barley harvest; asks to be called Mara — Ruth 1:19–22
- Sends Ruth to glean; blesses Boaz and names him a near kinsman — Ruth 2:2, 18–22
- Sends Ruth to Boaz at the threshing floor; *seek rest for you*; *Wait, my daughter* — Ruth 3:1–5, 16–18
- Named as the seller of Elimelech's parcel of land, which Boaz buys *from the hand of Naomi* — Ruth 4:3, 9
- Blessed by the women at Obed's birth; takes the child into her bosom; *A son is born to Naomi* — Ruth 4:14–17
