# Keturah

## Overview

**Abraham's second wife, described in nine words, and the mother of sixteen of his descendants.**

> **1** Abraham took another wife, and her name was Keturah.

That is Genesis 25:1, and it is the whole of her introduction; there is no more. Genesis gives her no
father, no country, no household, no scene, and no word of speech. She is not said to be beautiful, barren, old, young,
Canaanite, or Aramean. **She bears six sons in one verse and disappears**, and the four verses that
follow are her descendants rather than her.

Set against the book's treatment of the other two women in Abraham's household — [[Sarah]], who has
three chapters and a renaming, and [[Hagar]], who has two flights and the only place-name in
Scripture given by a woman — **the silence around her is total and looks deliberate.** The cycle is
over; the last thing it needs from Abraham is a demonstration that the promise of offspring was kept
past all measure, and she supplies it.

### What she is called, and the wiki does not choose

The text uses two different words in six verses.

| verse | word | reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 25:1** | *ʾiššâ*, **wife** — the same noun used of Sarah | a second marriage of full standing |
| **Genesis 25:6** | *"the sons of Abraham's **concubines**"* — *pîlagšîm*, plural, which can only be her and Hagar | a secondary union, and the sons are provided for rather than heirs |
| **1 Chronicles 1:32** | *"the sons of Keturah, Abraham's **concubine**"* | the Chronicler resolves it the second way |

**Genesis states both and reconciles neither**, which is exactly what it did with Hagar — *"his
wife"* at 16:3 and *"the servant"* at 21:10. In both cases the practical question, which of the sons
inherits, is settled by a different sentence: *"Abraham gave all that he had to Isaac"* (25:5). See
[[Marriage]] and [[Slavery and Servitude]].

### The chronology problem

Sarah dies when Abraham is a hundred thirty-seven (23:1 with 17:17). He dies at a hundred
seventy-five (25:7). **The narrative order puts Keturah, six sons, seven grandsons, and three
great-grandsons into the thirty-eight years in between** — and this is the man who fell on his face
laughing at the proposal that a hundred-year-old could father a child (17:17), and whose body Romans
4:19 calls *"as good as dead"*.

Three proposals, none of them adopted here:

1. **The notice is not chronological.** Genesis regularly files a genealogy where it is structurally
   useful rather than where it belongs in time — [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]] puts
   Nahor's twelve at 22:20 so that a bride is on the page before she is needed. On this reading
   Keturah may have been taken years earlier, and *"another wife"* states rank rather than sequence.
2. **The vigour of 17:16 and 21:1–2 persists.** The traditional harmonisation, and the one most of
   the medieval commentators take.
3. **She is Hagar under another name** — *qəṭûrâ* read from *qəṭōret*, **incense** — returning after
   Sarah's death. This is *Genesis Rabbah* 61:4 and Rashi, it is old and widely repeated, **and it
   contradicts 25:6, where the concubines are plural.** Recorded as reception history and not
   adopted.

### Her line

Six sons, seven grandsons, three great-grandsons — **sixteen names in four verses**, and the map is
Arabia and the Transjordan.

| son | descendants |
|---|---|
| **Zimran** | — |
| **Jokshan** | Sheba, Dedan; and Dedan's Asshurim, Letushim, Leummim |
| **Medan** | — |
| **Midian** | Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, Eldaah |
| **Ishbak** | — |
| **Shuah** | — |

**Midian is the one the rest of the Bible cares about**: the country Moses flees to and marries
into (Exodus 2:15–21; 3:1), the caravan that sells Joseph into Egypt (Genesis 37:28, 36), Gideon's
enemy (Judges 6–8), and the people behind Baal Peor (Numbers 25; 31). **Israel fights the Midianites
for four books and no writer ever mentions that they are Abraham's grandchildren.** *(Midian has no
page of his own yet; see the note on `passages/Genesis/The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael`
for why, and for the name the page must take when it is made.)*

**Two of the names are already in the book under other fathers.** *Sheba* and *Dedan* are sons of
Raamah in Ham's line at 10:7, and *Sheba* appears again under Joktan at 10:28. Tribal names in this
region attach to territory rather than descent, and a genealogy of peoples files one wherever it is
currently tracing. See [[The Table of Nations]] and [[The Nations]].

**And her sons come back into Scripture as worshippers.** Isaiah 60:6 has *"the camels of Midian and
Ephah … all from Sheba"* arriving at Zion with gold and incense, in the same oracle whose next verse
gathers Kedar and Nebaioth from Ishmael's line. **All five names are from this one pericope.** See
[[Abraham's Other Sons in Isaiah 60]].

## Timeline / key events

- Taken by Abraham as *another wife*, in a verse that gives her no ancestry, country, or age —
  Genesis 25:1, [[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]]
- Bears Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah — Genesis 25:2
- Her grandsons and great-grandsons listed: sixteen descendants in four verses — Genesis 25:3–4
- Her sons given gifts and sent *"eastward, to the east country"*, away from Isaac, while Abraham is
  still alive — Genesis 25:6
- Counted among *"Abraham's concubines"* in that same verse, and called his concubine outright at
  1 Chronicles 1:32
