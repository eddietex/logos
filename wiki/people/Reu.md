# Reu

## Overview

The sixth generation of the post-flood register, and the first man in the Bible whose whole life is
shorter than the *remainder* of most of his ancestors' lives.

*"Reu lived thirty-two years, and became the father of Serug. Reu lived two hundred seven years
after he became the father of Serug"* (Genesis 11:20–21) — two hundred thirty-nine years in all,
exactly matching his father [[Peleg]]'s. He is named nowhere else in Genesis, and appears in
1 Chronicles 1:25 and, as *Ragau*, in Luke 3:35.

**The name is the ordinary Hebrew word for a companion.** *Rəʿû* stands beside *rēaʿ*, **friend,
neighbour** — the noun of *"love your neighbour as yourself"* (Leviticus 19:18) — and beside
*rāʿâ*, to shepherd. Genesis makes nothing of it. Unlike his father, whose name gets a full sentence
of explanation at 10:25, Reu gets no etymology and no comment.

**He may also be a place.** Serug, Nahor, and Terah all correspond to towns attested in the Balikh
valley around [[Haran (city)]], and *Reu* has been connected — less securely than the other three —
to a place-name *Ra'gau* in the same region. The pattern is real and the individual identification
is not firm; see the note on the Balikh names in [[From Shem to Abram]].

He sits at the middle of the register's second collapse. His grandfather [[Eber]] lived 464 years;
he and his father live 239 apiece, his son [[Serug]] 230, and his grandson
[[Nahor (son of Serug)]] 148. On the register's own numbers he is born in 1787 from creation and
dies in 2026 — outliving his own father by thirty years, and dying inside Abram's lifetime on
either reckoning of when Abram was born.
