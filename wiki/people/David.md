# David

## Overview

Son of [[Jesse]], second king of Israel, and the father of [[Solomon]]. The narrative of his life belongs
to 1 and 2 Samuel and the opening of 1 Kings, none of which has yet been ingested, so this page is a
placeholder that grows as the wiki reaches him. He enters the wiki by name in the heading of Proverbs,
where he is mentioned only to identify Solomon: *"The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, king of
Israel"* (Proverbs 1:1). See [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]].

That patronym carries weight even in passing. It places the book in the line of the dynasty to which
God promised, through Nathan, *"I will set up your offspring after you … He will build a house for my
name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever"* (2 Samuel 7:12–13; WEB, checked against
bible-api.com). Ecclesiastes uses the same identification for its Preacher, *"the son of David, king in
Jerusalem"* (Ecclesiastes 1:1). In the New Testament *"son of David"* becomes a messianic title.

Earlier wiki pages mention him in connection pages — his eating of the bread of the Presence and his
confession in Psalm 51 — without a page to link to: see
[[The Bread of the Presence - David's Hunger and Matthew 12]] and
[[Sin Against God - Joseph at 39.9 and David at Psalm 51]].

Proverbs 4:3–4 recalls a teacher receiving instruction from his father. The heading at
1:1 makes a David–Solomon association possible, but 4:1–9 names neither man.
[[Get Wisdom]] records the scene as unnamed family instruction, not an established event in
David's biography. The mother's only child in the WEB (4:3) is not independent evidence
for the composition of the royal household. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 1:1; 4:1–9.

### Named in the Preacher's heading

[[Vanity of Vanities]] (Ecclesiastes 1:1–11) now supplies the second ingested wisdom heading that
names David. The heading identifies the Preacher as his son; no deed of David is narrated. Unlike
Proverbs 1:1, this heading does not name Solomon. See [[The Preacher (Ecclesiastes)]].
Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:1; Proverbs 1:1.

### The last word of Ruth 4:17

[[The Birth of Obed]] ends *He is the father of Jesse, the father of David* (Ruth 4:17): the first time the
wiki's narrative reading reaches his name in his own family line. His father is [[Jesse]] and his
great-grandparents [[Boaz]] and [[Ruth (person)]]; the genealogy of 4:18–22 traces the line back to Perez.
Nothing of his life is narrated here. Source: stored WEB Ruth 4:17, 22.

### The last word of Ruth — Ruth 4:22

*Jesse became the father of David* (Ruth 4:22). The genealogy of [[The Generations of Perez]] ends the book
on his name, tenth from [[Perez]], without the word *king*; Judges had ended *there was no king in Israel*
(Judges 21:25). Matthew 1:6 supplies the title, *King David* (WEB at bible-api.com, 2026-11-17). See
[[From Perez to David - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, Matthew 1, and Luke 3]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 4:18–22;
Judges 21:25.

### *A man after his own heart* — 1 Samuel 13:14

David's first appearance in the narrative of Samuel is unnamed. At Gilgal Samuel tells Saul *your kingdom will not
continue. Yahweh has sought for himself a man after his own heart, and Yahweh has appointed him to be prince over
his people* (13:14). *ʾÎš kilḇāḇô* (WLC) is read either of a man whose heart matches Yahweh's or of a man of
Yahweh's choosing (both readings recorded). Paul applies the phrase to David at Pisidian Antioch, *I have found
David the son of Jesse, a man after my heart, who will do all my will* (Acts 13:22, WEB checked at bible-api.com,
2026-11-18; not stored). The name arrives at the anointing (16:13, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]] and
[[A Man After His Own Heart - 1 Samuel 13.14, 16.7, and Acts 13.22]].

### *A neighbor of yours who is better than you* — 1 Samuel 15:28

Still unnamed, David is the subject of the second sentence on Saul. When the skirt of Samuel's robe tears, Samuel
says *Yahweh has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has given it to a neighbor of yours who is better
than you* (15:28). *Better*, *ṭôḇ*, was the word for Saul at his introduction, with no one in Israel better than he
(9:2, WLC). At Endor Samuel will repeat the sentence and add the name: *given it to your neighbor, even to David*
(28:17), and at En Gedi David will cut the skirt of Saul's robe (24:4) (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[Saul Rejected over Amalek]] and
[[The Torn Robe - 1 Samuel 15.27-28, 24.4-5, 28.17, and 1 Kings 11.29-31]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 9:2; 15:27–28; 24:4; 28:17. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.

### Anointed in the middle of his brothers — 1 Samuel 16:1–13

The narrative names him at last. Yahweh sends Samuel to Jesse of Bethlehem, *for I have provided a king for myself
among his sons* (16:1), literally *I have seen*, *rāʾîṯî* (WLC). Seven sons pass before Samuel, beginning with Eliab,
whose height Yahweh dismisses: *man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh looks at the heart* (16:7). David is
not at the sacrifice. He is *the youngest*, *haqqāṭān*, and *keeping the sheep*, *rōʿeh baṣṣōʾn* (16:11, WLC). When he
is brought he is *ruddy, with a handsome face and good appearance* (16:12). *ʾAḏmônî*, *ruddy*, occurs in the Hebrew
files checked only for him (16:12; 17:42) and for Esau at birth (Genesis 25:25). Samuel anoints him with *the horn of
oil … in the middle of his brothers*, and *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on David from that day forward* (16:13). His
name is the last thing the scene supplies. He is anointed, not made king: Saul reigns to the end of the book. Jesse
has eight sons by 16:10 and 17:12, seven by 1 Chronicles 2:15, which calls David *the seventh* (WEB checked at
bible-api.com); the two counts are recorded on [[Jesse]]. See [[Samuel Anoints David]],
[[Ruddy - Esau and David]], and
[[Taken from Following the Sheep - 1 Samuel 16.11, 2 Samuel 7.8, and Psalm 78.70-72]].

Source: stored WEB Genesis 25:25; 1 Samuel 16:1–13; 17:12, 42. 1 Chronicles 2:13–15 checked at bible-api.com
(`translation=web`), 2026-11-20. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.

### The harpist in Saul's house — 1 Samuel 16:14–23

The first thing David does after his anointing is play for the king he will replace. Saul's servants look for *a
skillful player on the harp*, and a young man answers *I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who is skillful in
playing, a mighty man of valor, a man of war, prudent in speech, and a handsome person; and Yahweh is with him*
(16:18). Saul sends for *David your son, who is with the sheep* (16:19). David comes with Jesse's gift, *stood before
him*, and *He loved him greatly; and he became his armor bearer* (16:21); Saul asks to keep him, *for he has found
favor in my sight* (16:22). When the evil spirit comes, *David took the harp, and played with his hand; so Saul was
refreshed* (16:23). He says nothing in the pericope; his first quoted words are at 17:26. He is the first person in
1 Samuel said to be loved, and the verb *nāḡan*, *play*, is used in the Hebrew files checked only of his playing for
Saul (16:16–23; 18:10; 19:9, WLC). See [[David Plays the Harp for Saul]] and
[[The Harp of David - Jubal, the Prophets, and the Sweet Psalmist]].

**Contradiction flagged.** Here Saul knows David, his father, and his name; in 17:55–58 he asks *whose son is this
youth?* (stored WEB). Both accounts are recorded on [[David Plays the Harp for Saul]] and
[[Whose Son Is This Youth - 1 Samuel 16.18-22 and 17.55-58]], and not reconciled.

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:14–23; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### The shepherd at the battle line — 1 Samuel 17:12–40

Chapter 17 introduces him again, as if 16:14–23 had not happened: *the son of that Ephrathite of Bethlehem Judah*,
the youngest of eight, going *back and forth from Saul to feed his father's sheep* (17:12–15). Jesse sends him with
food to *see how your brothers are doing* (17:17–18), as Jacob sent Joseph (Genesis 37:14). He leaves the sheep with
a keeper and the baggage with a keeper, runs to the line, and hears Goliath (17:20–23).

His first words in the book are a question: *What shall be done to the man who kills this Philistine, and takes away
the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?*
(17:26). He is the first in chapter 17 to name God (WLC). Eliab accuses him of *pride* and *naughtiness of … heart*
(17:28); Saul calls him *a youth* against *a man of war* (17:33). He answers with the flock: the lion and the bear
taken by the beard and killed, and *Yahweh who delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear,
he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine* (17:34–37). He takes off Saul's helmet, mail, and sword, *for
I have not tested them*, and goes with a staff, *five smooth stones out of the brook*, a shepherd's bag, and a sling
(17:38–40). In the files checked, *bear* is used only of these beasts and of Hushai's simile for him, *like a bear
robbed of her cubs … Your father is a man of war* (2 Samuel 17:8, WEB checked at bible-api.com; WLC). See
[[David Comes to the Camp]], [[Sent to See His Brothers - Joseph at Shechem and David at Elah]], and
[[To Defy the Living God - 1 Samuel 17 and 2 Kings 19]].

**The seam, again.** 17:12–15 introduces a David whom 16:18–22 had already brought to court, and 17:15 reads as a
bridge between the two. The contradiction note above stands.

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:18–22; 17:12–40; Genesis 37:14. 2 Samuel 17:8 checked at bible-api.com
(`translation=web`), 2026-11-21. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### The stone and the sword — 1 Samuel 17:41–54

Goliath sees him and *disdained him; for he was but a youth, and ruddy, and had a good looking face* (17:42), the
words of 16:12 turned to contempt. David answers: *You come to me with a sword, with a spear, and with a javelin; but I
come to you in the name of Yahweh of Armies, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied* (17:45). He says
what will happen *today*, and why: *that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel, and that all this
assembly may know that Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear; for the battle is Yahweh's* (17:46–47). He runs to
meet the Philistine, slings one stone into his forehead, and the narrator sums up: *there was no sword in the hand of
David* (17:48–50). Then he draws Goliath's own sword, kills him, and cuts off his head (17:51). He brings the head to
Jerusalem, still Jebusite in the books stored so far (Judges 1:21), and puts the armor *in his tent* (17:54); both
details are recorded as unexplained. Goliath's sword is later at Nob (21:9, not yet ingested). See
[[David Kills Goliath]], [[Ruddy - Esau and David]],
[[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]], and
[[The Head and the Armor - Goliath at Elah and Saul at Gilboa]].

**Contradiction flagged, still.** 2 Samuel 21:19 in the Hebrew has Elhanan kill *Goliath the Gittite*; recorded on
[[Goliath and Elhanan - 1 Samuel 17 and 2 Samuel 21.19]], not reconciled.

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:12; 17:41–54; 21:9; Judges 1:21. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### Jesse's son in Saul's house — 1 Samuel 17:55–18:5

Brought before Saul by Abner with the head still in his hand, he is asked *Whose son are you, you young man?* and
answers *I am the son of your servant Jesse the Bethlehemite* (17:57–58). It is his only line in the pericope, and it
names his father, not himself. Saul's ignorance of him after 16:18–22 is recorded on
[[Whose Son Is This Youth - 1 Samuel 16.18-22 and 17.55-58]], not reconciled.

Everything else is done to him. *The soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David* (18:1); Saul *took him that
day* and kept him from his father's house (18:2), ending the coming and going of 17:15; Jonathan makes a covenant
with him and gives him his robe, his clothing, his sword, his bow, and his sash (18:3–4). Saul's clothing he had taken
off, *I can't go with these* (17:39); Jonathan's, the same word *maddîm* (WLC), he keeps. Sent out by Saul, he
*behaved himself wisely*, *yaśkîl* (WLC; the verb of *good success* in Joshua 1:7–8), and is set over the men of war,
*good in the sight of all the people* (18:5). The text does not say whether he loved Jonathan in return; the narrator
gives the love only from Jonathan's side here. See [[Jonathan Loves David]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:18–22; 17:15, 38–39, 55–58; 18:1–5; Joshua 1:7–8. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-21.

### Ten thousands — 1 Samuel 18:6–16

The song of the women puts him above the king: *Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands* (18:7). He
says nothing in the pericope, and does nothing the narrator reports except play, escape, go out and come in, and behave
wisely. He plays his harp *as he did day by day* (18:10); Saul throws the spear, and he escapes *twice* (18:11). The
narrator gives the reason twice, as a fact and not a promise: *Yahweh was with him* (18:12, 14), the words Saul's
servant had used of him at 16:18. Sent from Saul's presence to command a thousand, he *went out and came in before the
people*, the phrase of Moses' prayer for a shepherd (Numbers 27:17), and *all Israel and Judah loved David* (18:13,
16). Saul's fear and the people's love grow in the same verses. See [[Saul's Jealousy of David]] and
[[Saul Has Slain His Thousands - 1 Samuel 18.7, 21.11, and 29.5]].

Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:17; 1 Samuel 16:18; 18:6–16. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### The king's son-in-law — 1 Samuel 18:17–30

Offered Merab, he answers *Who am I, and what is my life, or my father's family in Israel, that I should be son-in-law
to the king?* (18:18), the *mî ʾānōḵî* of Moses at the bush (Exodus 3:11) and of his own prayer at 2 Samuel 7:18 (WLC).
Offered Michal through Saul's servants, he answers *I am a poor man, and little known* (18:23); *rāš*, *poor*, is in the
Hebrew files checked otherwise only in Nathan's parable of the poor man (2 Samuel 12:1–4, WLC). Then *it pleased David
well to be the king's son-in-law* (18:26). Before the days are up he goes with *his men*, the first men of his own in
the book, kills two hundred Philistines, brings their foreskins *in full number*, and is given Michal (18:27). The text
says Michal loved him (18:20, 28), and never that he loved her; it does not say whether he knew the offers were traps.
The chapter closes on him: *David behaved himself more wisely than all the servants of Saul, so that his name was
highly esteemed* (18:30). See [[David Marries Michal]] and [[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]].

Source: stored WEB Exodus 3:11; 1 Samuel 18:17–30. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28; 2 Samuel 7:18;
12:1–4 in the WLC only.

### Hidden, defended, and restored — 1 Samuel 19:1–7

The order to kill him is now spoken (19:1). He says nothing in the pericope. Jonathan tells him to hide *in a secret
place* (19:2), the verb used of Saul *hidden among the baggage* (10:22, WLC), and defends him to Saul as one who *has
not sinned against you*, whose *works have been very good*, and who *put his life in his hand, and struck the
Philistine* (19:4–5); the idiom of risk is said of him, not by him. Saul swears he will not die, and David is brought
back *in his presence, as before* (19:6–7). See [[Jonathan Speaks for David]] and
[[My Life in My Hand - Jephthah, David, and the Medium of Endor]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 10:22; 19:1–7. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.

### Down through the window — 1 Samuel 19:8–17

He wins again, *with a great slaughter* (19:8), and plays again for Saul while the evil spirit is on him (19:9). The
third spear misses: *he slipped away out of Saul's presence*, *wayyip̄ṭar*, a verb that occurs in the Hebrew files
checked only here (WLC), and *fled, and escaped that night* (19:10). It is the first time *bāraḥ*, *flee*, is used of
him; the fugitive years begin here. His wife saves him. Michal warns him, lets him down through the window, and puts
the teraphim in his bed (19:11–13). He says nothing in the pericope; the only words given him are the ones Michal
invents, *Let me go! Why should I kill you?* (19:17). Saul now calls him *my enemy* (19:17). See
[[Michal Helps David Escape]] and [[Let Down Through the Window - Rahab and Michal]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 19:8–17. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.
