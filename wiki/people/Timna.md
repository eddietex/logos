# Timna

## Overview

**Listed three times in one chapter, in three different capacities, and never given a word.**

> **12** Timna was concubine to Eliphaz, Esau’s son; and she bore to Eliphaz Amalek. …
> **22** The children of Lotan were Hori and Heman. Lotan’s sister was Timna. …
> **40** … chief Timna, chief Alvah, chief Jetheth,

**Concubine, sister, chief.** Genesis 36 puts the same name in the register of Esau's line, in the
register of the Horites Esau's line displaced, and in the final list of chiefs *"according to their
habitations"* — and never remarks on the fact. See [[The Descendants of Esau]].

**She is the only woman in the chapter given a status rather than a patronymic.** The other three
women in Esau's household are *the daughter of Elon*, *the daughter of Anah*, *Ishmael's daughter*;
Timna is ***pîlegeš***, **concubine**, which in Genesis is a household position with a claim on
inheritance — the word used of Hagar's standing at 25:6, of Bilhah at 35:22, and of Reumah at 22:24.
**Her son is counted in the wife's line**: *"she bore to Eliphaz Amalek. These are the sons of Adah,
Esau's wife"* (36:12). That is the standard reckoning, and it means [[Amalek]] enters the register as
[[Adah (wife of Esau)]]'s grandson without Adah being his grandmother.

## Why the three entries are probably one woman

**36:22 makes her Horite nobility.** Lotan is the firstborn of [[Seir the Horite]] and the first chief
in the Horite list; his sister marries — or is taken by — the firstborn son of Esau. **The chapter
that Deuteronomy 2:12 describes as a conquest records it as two marriages**: Timna into Eliphaz's
household, and [[Oholibamah]], granddaughter of Zibeon the Horite, into Esau's own. See
[[Edom]].

**36:40's *chief Timna* is a different kind of entry.** That list is organised *"after their places"*,
and several of its eleven names are territorial rather than personal — *Mibzar* means **fortress**,
*Magdiel* has the shape of a place name, and *Oholibamah* two verses later is Esau's wife. **Timna is
also a valley**: the copper-mining district in the Arabah north of the Gulf of Aqaba, worked from the
Chalcolithic period onward and heavily worked in the Egyptian New Kingdom, still called Timna today.
**A clan, its territory, and its ancestress carry one name**, which is how tribal genealogy normally
works and which this chapter makes unusually easy to see.

## What later readers did with her

**The rabbinic tradition gave her the biography Genesis withheld.** *b. Sanhedrin* 99b reads the three
entries together — a princess of the Horites who wanted to attach herself to Abraham's house, was
refused, and said she would rather be a concubine in that family than a noblewoman elsewhere — and
then draws the moral from the result: *"from her descended Amalek, who afflicted Israel. Why? Because
they should not have rejected her."* **It is midrash, not exegesis**, and the wiki records it as a
reading rather than as a fact. Its interest is that it is one of the few places the tradition treats
Amalek's existence as somebody's fault other than Amalek's.

## Appears in

- [[The Descendants of Esau]] — Genesis 36:12, 22, 40; **three entries, three capacities, no
  speech and no act.** She is the point at which the chapter's two families — Esau's incomers and
  the Horites they displaced — are recorded as having merged, and she is the mother of the nation
  Israel is told to blot out
