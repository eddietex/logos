# Naphtali

## Overview

**Bilhah's second son, Jacob's sixth, and the only child in the list named after a fight.**

> **8** Rachel said, “I have wrestled with my sister with mighty wrestlings, and have prevailed.” She
> named him Naphtali. (30:8)

***Naptûlê ʾĕlōhîm niptaltî ʿim-ʾăḥōtî gam-yākōltî.*** **The Hebrew literally says *wrestlings of God I
have wrestled with my sister, and I have prevailed*** — and the WEB's ***mighty*** is translating
***ʾĕlōhîm***. The phrase can be a superlative (*godlike struggles*, which is the WEB's reading) or a
statement about who the contest was really with. **Genesis does not decide, and the wiki adopts
neither.** See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Names and Naming]].

**What is not ambiguous is the verb *yākōl*, and where it turns up next.** Two chapters later a man
wrestles Jacob at the ford of the Jabbok and cannot **prevail** against him (32:25), and the new name is
explained: *"you have fought with God and with men, and have **prevailed**"* (32:28). **The mother who
did not bear him and the father who is renamed both claim, in the same book and the same household, to
have struggled with *ʾĕlōhîm* and won.** See
[[Wrestlings of God - Rachel at Naphtali's Birth and Jacob at the Jabbok]].

**And note who [[Rachel]] names as the opponent: *with my sister.*** It is the passage's one explicit
statement of what the whole birth sequence is — not a woman contending with God for a child, but two
women contending with each other and counting children as points. [[Bilhah]], who bore him, says
nothing.

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

Jacob's blessing is four words in Hebrew and among the most obscure in the chapter: *"Naphtali is a
doe set free, who bears beautiful fawns"* (49:21) — an image with no narrative behind it anywhere in
Genesis. Moses' blessing gives the tribe *"the west and the south"* (Deuteronomy 33:23). Its territory
is the far north-west of the land, around Galilee; **Barak, who fights Sisera, is from Kedesh in
Naphtali** (Judges 4:6), and Deborah's song singles the tribe out for risking its life on the heights
(5:18). Tiglath-Pileser deports it early (2 Kings 15:29), which is what Isaiah 9:1 is about — **and
Matthew 4:13–16 quotes that verse over Capernaum, making Naphtali's territory the region Jesus'
ministry begins in.**
