# Reumah

## Overview

Nahor's concubine, named once, and **the first *pîlegeš* in the Bible.**

> **24** His concubine, whose name was Reumah, also bore Tebah, Gaham, Tahash, and Maacah.

One verse, four sons, and a name the text goes out of its way to supply — *"His concubine, **whose
name was** Reumah"*, where the register could simply have said *his concubine bore*. Genesis names
her and then never mentions her again. See [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]].

***Pîlegeš*** is not *mistress*. It denotes a secondary wife of lower legal standing whose children
are nonetheless reckoned to the father's house — which is exactly what happens here: **Reumah's four
sons are counted in the total that makes [[Nahor (brother of Abram)]]'s house twelve.** Eight by
[[Milcah]], four by Reumah. Without her verse the number is eight, and the parallel with Ishmael's
twelve princes and Jacob's twelve sons does not exist. See
[[Twelve Sons - Nahor, Ishmael, and Jacob]].

**The comparison the text sets up and declines to make.** [[Abraham]]'s household ran the same
structure — a wife who could not conceive, a second woman brought in, and a son by each — and
Genesis gives it two full chapters of consequence: [[Hagar]] fleeing into the wilderness (16), the
weaning feast, and a mother and a boy sent into a desert with a skin of water (21). **Nahor's
household ran it too, and Genesis reports it in one verse, with no barrenness, no rivalry, no
grievance, and no comment.** The branch that stayed behind gets the arrangement without the cost.
The book does not say why, and it puts the two four verses apart.

**The name.** *Rəʾûmâ* has no secure derivation. The usual proposals read it from *rāʾam*, to be
exalted, or from *rəʾēm*, the wild ox — and a third takes it as related to *Aramaic* forms meaning
*pearl* or *coral*. None is more than a guess, and unlike most Genesis names the text supplies no
etymology.

Her four sons — **Tebah, Gaham, Tahash, and Maacah** — are, like Milcah's eight, tribal and
geographical names rather than characters. *Maacah* is a small Aramean kingdom below Hermon
(Deuteronomy 3:14; 2 Samuel 10:6); *Tebah* is plausibly the city of Hadadezer's that David plunders
at 2 Samuel 8:8; *Gaham* and *Tahash* are otherwise unattested. **Her line is the Aramean periphery,
where Milcah's is the Aramean centre** — and the family tree is doing the work of a map.
