# Asher

## Overview

### Levitical cities — Joshua 21:1–42

The tribe descended from him contributes cities and suburbs to the Levites (21:6, 30–31). The ancestor is not an actor in this distribution.

See [[The Cities of the Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:1–42.

### The last three lots — Joshua 19:24–48

The descendants receive the fifth lot: a boundary from Helkath past Carmel westward, east to Beth Dagon and Zebulun, then north *even to great Sidon* and *the fortified city of Tyre*, ending at the sea by Achzib, with a stated twenty-two cities and their villages (24–31). Genesis 49:20 and Deuteronomy 33:24–25 speak of rich food and oil; this register is silent on produce, and names Sidon and Tyre as limits, not conquests.

See [[The Lots of Asher, Naphtali, and Dan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 19:24–48; comparisons as cited.

### Manasseh’s inheritance — Joshua 17:1–13

The tribal territory meets Manasseh on the north and includes Manassite holdings (10–11). Asher in
verse 7 is a boundary starting point whose precise referent is not established here; it is not
assumed to be a separate identified town.

See [[The Inheritance of Manasseh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:1–13.

### Moses blesses the tribes — Deuteronomy 33:1–29

Asher receives blessing with children, acceptance among brothers, oil, strong bars, and enduring
strength (24–25). The prosperity corresponds broadly to the rich food of Genesis 49:20.

See [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 33:1–29; comparisons as cited.


### The responsive curses — Deuteronomy 27:11–26

The tribe bearing this ancestor’s name is assigned to Mount Ebal for the curse (13). This concerns the descendants, not the patriarch. The assignment does not pronounce this tribe morally inferior or uniquely cursed.

See [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:11–26.


### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

The tribe is represented by [[Ahihud]], son of Shelomi, in the division of Canaan (27). This concerns the patriarch’s descendants.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

The tribe bearing his name has 53,400 men in the second military census (Numbers 26). The register
names ancestral families, not a new action by the patriarch.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


**Zilpah's second son, Jacob's eighth, and the only one of the twelve named for what other people will
say.**

> **13** Leah said, “Happy am I, for the daughters will call me happy.” She named him Asher. (30:13)

***Bəʾošrî… ʾiššərûnî bānôt*** — from ***ʾāšar***, *to call happy, to pronounce blessed* — **and
*ʾĀšēr*.** The root is the one behind the *"Blessed is the man"* that opens the Psalter (Psalm 1:1) and
the Beatitudes. **[[Leah]]'s speech has no divine name and no divine act in it**, and neither does
[[Gad]]'s, four verses earlier: **these two namings are the only ones in the passage without God.**
*Fortune has come*, and *the daughters will call me happy* — **luck, and reputation.** See
[[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Names and Naming]].

***The daughters*** — ***bānôt*** — **is the idiom for women in general**, and Leah is imagining being
spoken well of by a village. **Ruth 4:11 is eventually the village doing it**, blessing a bride *"like
Rachel and like Leah, which two built the house of Israel"*. See
[[Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel]].

[[Zilpah]], who bore him, says nothing at either of her sons' births.

### In this pericope — Genesis 49:20

> **20** “Asher’s food will be rich. He will produce royal dainties. (49:20)

**One line about food, and it is the only saying in the poem with no animal, no weapon, no territory
and no verdict.** ***Maʿădannê-melek***, *royal dainties* — **produce good enough for a king's table.**

**The name means *happy*, and [[Leah]] coined it at 30:13** — *"Happy am I! For the daughters will call
me happy"* — **but the saying does not pun on it**, which makes him one of the eight sons whose name
the poem leaves alone. **Moses' blessing says the same thing in oil**: *"let him dip his foot in oil"*
(Deuteronomy 33:24), **and the allotment on the slopes north of Carmel is olive country**, which is
what both poems are about. See
[[Jacob's Blessing and the Blessing of Moses - Genesis 49 and Deuteronomy 33]].

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

The tribe's allotment is the fertile coastal strip north of Carmel (Joshua 19:24–31), olive country,
which is what both blessings are about. **Judges 1:31–32 records that it failed to drive out the
inhabitants and lived among them**, and Deborah's song reproaches it for staying at the harbours
(5:17). Its one memorable descendant is **Anna the prophetess**, *"of the tribe of Asher"*, who is in
the temple when the infant Jesus is brought in (Luke 2:36) — **the only one of the ten northern tribes
named in the New Testament as a living identity.**

### The census — Numbers 1:1–54

His tribe numbers 41,500, ninth in size, with Pagiel son of Ochran (13, 41).

See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 1.

### The camp — Numbers 2:1–34

Encamps next to Dan on the north (27), with Pagiel son of Ochran as prince; 41,500, unchanged from chapter 1.

See [[The Camp Around the Tent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 2.

### The altar gifts — Numbers 7:72–77

The tribe of Asher gives on the eleventh day of twelve, by the hand of Pagiel. Its gift is identical to every other tribe's.

See [[The Offerings of the Twelve Princes]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 7.

### With Dan — Numbers 10:26

In Dan's camp, under Pagiel son of Ochran.

See [[The March from Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 10.

- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:2; **not named individually, and one of the four sons of

  that calls their mothers Jacob's *wives*, against *concubine* and *servant* two verses apart in the

- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:1–38; **one of the ten who go down, and he
  acts and speaks only as part of a plural.** The chapter gives *"they said"* and *"they said to one
  another"* and never separates them: **the protest of innocence, the words *"we are twelve brothers…
  and one is no more"*, and the confession of 42:21 — *"we are certainly guilty concerning our
  brother"* — are all spoken by the group and attributed to no one.** Only [[Reuben]] and [[Simeon]]
  are singled out. He bows to [[Joseph]] without recognising him (42:6–8), spends three days in
  custody, and carries home grain and money he did not expect

- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:7–13; **one of the plural, and the plural does the
  chapter's most important thing.** He protests the theft, joins the oath that would have killed
  whoever held the cup (*"With whomever of your servants it is found, let him die"*, 44:9), tears his
  clothes when it is found in [[Benjamin]]'s sack, **and turns his donkey around** — with the road to
  Canaan open, a court's word that he is *blameless*, and nothing but [[Benjamin]] to go back for
  (44:13). **Genesis records the decision in nine words and attributes it to no one.** After 44:13 the
  ten have no further speech; [[Judah]] speaks for all of them

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — Genesis 45:3–4, 15, 21–26; **one of the plural, and the plural is
  silent for twelve verses.** He cannot answer when the governor says *"I am Joseph"* — ***nibhălû***,
  *they were terrified at his presence* (45:3) — comes near when told to (45:4), is kissed and wept on
  (45:15), takes **one change of clothing** where [[Benjamin]] takes five and three hundred pieces of
  silver (45:22), and is told *"see that you don't quarrel on the way"* (45:24). **Genesis records not
  one word of his spoken in Egypt.** *"After that his brothers talked with him"* (45:15) **is the whole
  of the reunion conversation the book reports.** The single sentence the ten deliver, in Canaan, is
  *"Joseph is still alive, and he is ruler over all the land of Egypt"* (45:26) — **and it leaves out
  the sale**, which is the omission 50:15 is still living off twenty years later
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 46:17; **four sons, two grandsons, and a daughter.** *"Serah their
  sister"* — **[[Serah]] is the only granddaughter named anywhere in the register of seventy**, and
  **Scripture never says why**; it names her twice more (Numbers 26:46; 1 Chronicles 7:30) in lists
  that had no need of her
- [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]] — Genesis 49:20; **one line, about food.** **The only saying in the poem
