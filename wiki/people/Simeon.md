# Simeon

## Overview

**Leah's second son, named for a hearing Genesis never prints.**

*"Because Yahweh has heard that I am hated, he has therefore given me this son also"* (29:33) —
***šāmaʿ***, *heard*, and ***Šimʿôn***. **The naming speech is the only place in the Bible where Leah
says out loud what the narrator said at 29:31**, and she attributes the knowledge of it to God rather
than to anyone in the household. **This is the second of three times in the pericope that God is said to
hear one of the two sisters, and Genesis records not one word of prayer from either of them.** See
[[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Prayer]].

### Genesis 34 — the sword at Shechem

**He is one of the two brothers in the Dinah episode, and the narrator names the relationship that
explains the arithmetic.** *"On the third day, when they were sore, two of Jacob's sons, Simeon and
Levi, **Dinah's brothers**, each took his sword, came upon the unsuspecting city, and killed all the
males"* (34:25) — **full brothers, both [[Leah]]'s, in a household of four mothers.** Of twelve
brothers the two who act are the two who share her mother, and Genesis states the fact without
drawing the inference.

**The circumcision was their proposal**, answered *"with deceit"* in the narrator's own word (34:13,
***bĕmirmâ***, the word Isaac used of Jacob at 27:35), and **the third day is the medical peak of the
pain.** *"Came upon the unsuspecting city"* is ***beṭaḥ***, *in security* — the city is killed in the
state the treaty produced in it. **Jacob's objection at 34:30 is about consequences** — *"you have
troubled me, to make me odious to the inhabitants of the land"* — **and the brothers answer with a
question the chapter ends on: *"Should he deal with our sister as with a prostitute?"*** Genesis gives
them the last word and withholds its verdict for fifteen chapters. See
[[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] and
[[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]].

### What is still ahead

This page grows as the remaining pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of them.

He is the brother [[Joseph]] binds before the others' eyes and keeps in Egypt as security (42:24) —
**Genesis does not say why him**, and the readings range from second-eldest after the disqualified
[[Reuben]] to the Dinah affair to nothing at all.

**Jacob's deathbed puts him and Levi in a single curse**, the only two of the twelve to share one:

> **5** Simeon and Levi are brothers. Their swords are weapons of violence. **6** My soul, don't come
> into their council… for in their anger they killed men. **7** Cursed be their anger, for it was
> fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel. I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel.
> (49:5–7)

**The scattering happens to both and means opposite things.** Levi is scattered and becomes the
priesthood; **Simeon is scattered and disappears** — its allotment is a set of towns inside Judah's
territory (Joshua 19:1, 9), it is the smallest tribe by the second census (Numbers 26:14), and **it is
missing from Moses' blessing in Deuteronomy 33 altogether.**

## Appears in

- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 29:33; the second son, named for God hearing what nobody
  in the household says out loud. **His mother's speech is the one place she states her own position
  directly**, and it is a naming, not a complaint
- [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] — Genesis 34:13–31; **one of the two who plan it, wait three
  days, and carry it out**, and one of the two voices in the chapter's unanswered last line. The
  chapter gives him no speech of his own — the brothers speak in the plural throughout
- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:23; **a name in a list.** Second of
  Leah's six in the Bible's first complete roster of the twelve, one chapter after the massacre at
  Shechem and with no reference to it
