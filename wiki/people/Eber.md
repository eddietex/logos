# Eber

## Overview

Shem's great-grandson, and the name the word **Hebrew** stands next to.

He is introduced before he is born. Genesis 10:21 opens Shem's branch of [[The Table of Nations]] by
identifying him not by his father, as every other entry in the chapter is identified, but by a
descendant four generations down: *"Children were also born to Shem, the father of all the children
of Eber, the elder brother of Japheth."* Only then, at 10:24, does the line arrive — Arpachshad
fathers Shelah, Shelah fathers Eber — and at 10:25 Eber fathers [[Peleg]] and Joktan, and the branch
forks into the line that runs to Abram and the line that runs into Arabia.

**The name and the word.** *ʿĒber* and *ʿibrî*, **Hebrew**, differ by one consonant, and both sit
beside the verb *ʿābar*, **to cross over, to pass through**. Genesis 14:13 will call Abram *hāʿibrî*,
*the Hebrew* — the first use of the term and, notably, in the mouth of an outsider's report rather
than in Israel's own self-description. Two derivations are usually offered and they are not exclusive:

- **From Eber**, so that *Hebrew* means *descendant of Eber* — which is what 10:21 seems to set up,
  since it makes *"the children of Eber"* a category worth naming in advance.
- **From *ʿābar***, so that it means *one from across* — from beyond the river, which is how Joshua
  24:2–3 describes Abraham's origin. The Septuagint renders 14:13 *ho perátēs*, *the one who crossed
  over*, which shows this reading was already current.

There is a third possibility that touches neither: the *ʿapiru* or *ḫabiru* of second-millennium
Akkadian and Egyptian texts, a social class of displaced, stateless people rather than an ethnic
group. The resemblance is real, the phonology is imperfect, and the relationship is unproven. The wiki
records all three and adjudicates none.

**What he does: nothing.** Like almost everyone in Genesis 10 he is a name in a genealogy. He acquires
numbers in the linear register of Genesis 11:16–17 — thirty-four years old at Peleg's birth and four
hundred thirty after it, four hundred sixty-four in all, the longest span in that register after
Shem's own six hundred. On the received Hebrew figures he outlives every other man in it, Shem
included, and dies sixty-four years after Abram does. He appears in the Chronicler's copy of the Table
(1 Chronicles 1:18–19, 25) and in Luke's genealogy of Jesus (Luke 3:35). He is not the Eber of
Numbers 24:24, where *ʿēber* is probably a place, nor the Heber of Judges 4–5.

**Why the chapter bothers.** Genesis 10 gives every branch a terminus and no destination. Shem's is
the exception: the chapter announces, before listing anyone, that this is the line *"of all the
children of Eber"* — and the reader who has no idea yet what an Eberite is finds out at Genesis
14:13. The Table has already flagged which of its seventy the book intends to follow.

  Genesis 10:21
- Fathers [[Peleg]] and Joktan; the line forks — Genesis 10:25

  (Luke 3:35)
