# Caleb (son of Jephunneh)

## Overview

### Levitical cities — Joshua 21:1–42

Retains Hebron’s fields and villages while its city and suburbs go to Aaron’s descendants (11–13). The text distinguishes these holdings without mapping their boundaries.

See [[The Cities of the Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:1–42.

### Achsah’s springs — Joshua 15:13–19

Caleb receives Hebron under Yahweh’s command, drives out Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, and offers Achsah in marriage to the conqueror of Debir. He grants her both upper and lower springs when she asks (13–19). The land grant of chapter 14 now leads to action and household provision.

See [[Achsah Asks for Springs]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:13–19.

### Caleb’s inheritance — Joshua 14:6–15

At eighty-five Caleb asks for the hill country promised forty-five years earlier. He credits Yahweh with preserving his life, recalls his faithful report, and receives Hebron from Joshua (6–14). His claimed strength serves a still-future campaign; this scene grants land without narrating its capture.

See [[Caleb Claims Hebron]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:6–15; comparisons as cited.


### The sentence and the ascent — Deuteronomy 1:34–46

Caleb alone is named as the exception in verse 36, with land promised to him and his children
because he wholly followed Yahweh. Joshua is also promised entry, separately in verse 38 as the one
who will lead Israel into its inheritance; Caleb’s exception must not be read as excluding Joshua.

See [[The Sentence and the Presumptuous Ascent]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 14 and 20 for comparisons.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

Caleb represents Judah in the allotment commission (19). The faithful spy becomes an administrator of the promised inheritance; this unit does not yet narrate his personal allotment.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

Named in Moses' retelling of the oath as *Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite* (12; *hakkənizzî*,
Miqra according to the Masorah), one of the two who *followed Yahweh completely*. This is the verse the
page already cites for the epithet; see *Whose family he is from* below.

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

Named with Joshua as the surviving exception to the death of the former military census generation
(65), fulfilling Numbers 14:30.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


The spy from Judah, and the first voice in the wiki's sequence to say *we are well able*. He enters
at Numbers 13:6 as one name in a list, *Of the tribe of Judah, Caleb the son of Jephunneh*, and he
is not among the princes of Numbers 1, 7, or 10; Nahshon holds that place. Nothing else about him is
given before he speaks.

**He speaks once in Numbers 13.** *Caleb stilled the people before Moses, and said, "Let us go up at
once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it!"* (13:30). The Hebrew doubles both verbs,
*ʿālōh naʿăleh* and *yākôl nûkal* (WLC), and the others answer with the same verb negated, *lōʾ
nûkal* (31). He does not mention Yahweh here. He does not dispute any fact in the report. He
disputes the conclusion. See [[The Twelve Spies]].

**His survival.** He tears his clothes with Joshua and pleads (14:6–9). Numbers 14:24 calls him *my
servant Caleb*, *because he had another spirit with him, and has followed me fully*, and promises
him the land he went into. He and Joshua alone of the men who were sent stay alive (14:38), and he
and Joshua alone of the counted generation enter (14:30). Numbers 26:65 and 32:12 confirm it (stored WEB, now ingested). See [[The People Refuse the Land]] and
[[Wholly Followed - Mille Ahare in Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, and 1 Kings]].

**At eighty-five he asks for the hill country.** Joshua 14:7 has him say, *I was forty years old
when Moses the servant of Yahweh sent me from Kadesh Barnea to spy out the land*, and 14:10 has him
eighty-five. He asks for the place of the Anakim and receives Hebron (14:12–14), and 15:14 and
Judges 1:20 have him drive out the three sons of Anak (Joshua now ingested; Judges not yet ingested). See
[[Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1]].

**Whose family he is from.** Numbers 13:6 places him in Judah. Numbers 32:12 and Joshua 14:6 call
him *the Kenizzite*, and Kenaz is an Edomite name at Genesis 36:11, 15, while Genesis 15:19 lists
Kenizzites among the peoples of the land. So a man who leads Judah's claim may come from outside
Israel by descent. The text does not explain it, and it may also be a separate Kenaz; 1 Chronicles
4:15 has a later Kenaz in the family. The wiki records both data.

**Two Calebs, or one.** 1 Chronicles 2:18 has *Caleb the son of Hezron*, in Judah's line, and 1
Chronicles 4:15 has *Caleb the son of Jephunneh*. They may be two men, or one man with two
genealogies. The wiki keeps this page to the son of Jephunneh and flags the question.
[[Hur (companion of Moses)]] and [[Hur (grandfather of Bezalel)]] touch it through 1 Chronicles
2:19–20.

**The name.** *Kālēb* (3612 in the tags) has the same consonants as *keleb*, *dog* (3611). Some
readers take that as the name's meaning and some look for another derivation; both are recalled and
neither is in the text. *Jephunneh* (*yəpunneh*) occurs only for his father and a later Asherite by
the tags (1 Chronicles 7:38). The father has no page of his own, since he is only a patronymic.
