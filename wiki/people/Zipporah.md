# Zipporah

## Overview

**Moses' wife — a Midianite, a priest's daughter, one of seven sisters at a well — who is given to
him in a single clause and later saves his life with a flint knife.** She enters at Exodus 2:21:
*"He gave Moses Zipporah, his daughter."* No courtship is narrated, no bride-price, and no words of
hers; she is the eldest or the youngest of the seven only by tradition. ***Ṣippōrâ*** means
***bird*** (*ṣippôr*); the narrator does not comment on the name.

**Her marriage closes the third betrothal-at-a-well scene in the Torah**, after Rebekah (Genesis 24)
and Rachel (Genesis 29). **She is the first of the three brides not drawn from Abraham's kin** — the
endogamy that drives Genesis 24 and 28 is simply absent — though Midian descends from Abraham
through Keturah (Genesis 25:2), so the distance is one of degree. See [[The Betrothal at the Well]]
and [[Moses Flees to Midian]].

**She bears Gershom** (2:22) and, by 4:20 and 18:3–4, a second son, Eliezer. **The naming of
Gershom is Moses' speech, not hers** — unlike the matriarchs of Genesis, who name most of Jacob's
sons, she names neither child in the text.

### What is ahead

**Exodus 4:24–26**, the most obscure three verses in the book: on the way back to Egypt *"Yahweh met
Moses and wanted to kill him"*, and *"Zipporah took a flint, and cut off the foreskin of her son, and
cast it at his feet; and she said, 'Surely you are a bridegroom of blood to me'"*. **The Midianite
wife performs the covenant sign the Hebrew husband has neglected**, and the attack stops. **Exodus
18:2** has her sent back to her father at some point and returned at the mountain of God. **Numbers
12:1**, Miriam and Aaron speaking *"against Moses because of the Cushite woman whom he had
married"*, is sometimes read as Zipporah (Midian and *Cushan* stand in parallel at Habakkuk 3:7) and
sometimes as a second wife; the wiki will record both when Numbers is reached. These sections are
written as their pericopes arrive.
