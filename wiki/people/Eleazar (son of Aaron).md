# Eleazar (son of Aaron)

## Overview

### Renewed tablets and onward journey — Deuteronomy 10:1–11

He ministers in Aaron’s place after Aaron dies and is buried at Moserah (6). Succession agrees with
Numbers 20:25–28; the death location differs from Mount Hor there. Deuteronomy does not retell the
garment transfer or identify who buried Aaron.

See [[New Tablets and the Journey Onward]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 10:1–11.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

Eleazar the priest is named with Joshua to divide the inheritance (17). His responsibility extends to land distribution alongside tribal representatives.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

Addressed with Moses and the princes when Reuben and Gad make their request (2), and the first of the
three whom Moses commands *concerning them*, to give Gilead if the two tribes cross armed and land in
Canaan if they do not (28–30). The agreement will outlive Moses, and Eleazar is one of its executors.

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### The war against Midian — Numbers 31:1–54

Eleazar meets the returning army with Moses (13), gives the soldiers *the statute of the law* for
purifying metal by fire and *the water for impurity* (21–24), receives the one-in-five-hundred levy as
*Yahweh's wave offering* (29, 41), and with Moses brings the officers' gold into the Tent of Meeting (54).
His son, not he, goes with the army.

See [[The War Against Midian]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 31.

### Joshua commissioned — Numbers 27:12–23

Joshua is set before Eleazar and the congregation (19, 22), and is to *stand before Eleazar the
priest, who shall inquire for him by the judgment of the Urim before Yahweh* (21). Eleazar thus
becomes the channel of guidance for Moses' successor. No inquiry is narrated in this unit.

See [[Joshua Commissioned]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:12–23.


### The daughters’ inheritance — Numbers 27:1–11

Eleazar stands with Moses, the princes, and the congregation when the daughters present their
petition at the Tent entrance (2). No separate response by the priest is recorded; Moses brings
the case to Yahweh.

See [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:1–11.


### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

He now shares the census with Moses (1–3, 63), occupying the place Aaron held at the first count
(64). His priestly succession is visible in administration as well as garments.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


### Baal Peor — Numbers 25:1–18

Eleazar is named as Phinehas’s father (7, 11). The action and ensuing covenant belong to his son;
Eleazar’s own action is not narrated.

See [[Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 25:1–18.


### The death of Aaron — Numbers 20:22–29

Eleazar receives Aaron’s garments from Moses and descends with him after Aaron’s death
(25–28). The earlier anticipation of his succession is now narrated. Neither burial by
Eleazar nor corpse contact is reported; anointing and a seven-day rite are not described here.

See [[The Death of Aaron]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 20:22–29.

### The red heifer — Numbers 19:1–22

Eleazar supervises the killing and burning outside the camp and sprinkles blood seven times
toward the Tent (3–6). The priest must then wash and remains unclean until evening (7).
Authorized service can entail temporary impurity without being disobedience.

See [[The Red Heifer]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 19.

### The rebellion of Korah — Numbers 16:1–40

Eleazar collects the bronze censers after the burning. They are beaten into an altar covering to
warn against unauthorized incense offering (36–40). The objects remain holy although their offerers
have died.

See [[The Rebellion of Korah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:1–40; comparisons as cited.

### Lamps and weekly bread — Leviticus 24:1–9

The bread is assigned to Aaron and his sons (9). Eleazar is included as a surviving
son identified in Leviticus 10:12, not individually named in 24:1–9. This adds a
specific most holy food to the priestly family’s provision.

See [[The Lamps and the Bread Before Yahweh]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 24:1–9.

**[[Aaron]]'s third son, and his successor.** His name means *God has helped*.

> **25** Eleazar Aaron's son took one of the daughters of Putiel as his wife; and she bore him
> Phinehas. These are the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites according to their families.
> (Exodus 6:25)

**He is the only one of Aaron's four sons whose marriage and son the genealogy records**, and its
last verse before the summary is about him. **The list follows the priestly line one generation past
Aaron, through him, to [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]].** In Numbers he takes Aaron's garments on Mount
Hor and succeeds him as high priest (Numbers 20:25–28, by reference), and he divides the land with
Joshua (Joshua 14:1, by reference). The Mount Hor succession is now recorded in [[The Death of Aaron]]; the Joshua section awaits ingestion. See
[[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]].

The wiki qualifies the page because a later Eleazar, the son of Abinadab, keeps the ark (1 Samuel
7:1).

### The lamp service prescribed — Exodus 27:1–21

Exodus 27:21 assigns the lamp outside the veil to Aaron and his sons collectively. Eleazar
is included by the family list of 6:23 and the explicit appointment list of 28:1; he is not
individually named in 27:21. This is a prescribed duty, not a report that his service has
begun. See [[The Altar and the Court]].

### The priestly garments prescribed — Exodus 28:1–43

Exodus 28:1 names Eleazar individually among Aaron's sons brought near *"that he may minister to
me in the priest's office"* — the first time Exodus appoints the four by name. Coats, sashes,
headbands, and linen breeches are prescribed for the sons (28:40–43), and Moses is to anoint
and consecrate them (28:41). The ordination is commanded in chapter 29; no service is yet
narrated. See [[The Priestly Garments]].

### The ordination prescribed — Exodus 29:1–46

Exodus 29 includes Eleazar among *"his sons"* — not by name — who are washed, clothed in
coats, belts, and headbands, and sprinkled with the altar's blood and the anointing oil with their
garments (29:4, 8–9, 21). The sons share in laying hands on the offerings, in the blood on the right
ear, thumb, and big toe, and in the meal of the ram of consecration (29:10, 20, 32). The breast and
thigh of later peace offerings are assigned to Aaron and his sons *"forever"* (29:28). Only Aaron's
head receives the poured oil (29:7). See [[The Ordination of the Priests]].

Exodus 30 includes Eleazar among Aaron's *"sons"* — not by name — who wash hands and feet at the
basin before entering the tent or approaching the altar, *"that they not die"* (30:19–21), and who
are anointed with the holy oil (30:30). Numbers 16:37–39 (verified WEB) later has Eleazar beat the
censers of Korah's company into a covering for the altar. See
[[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] and
[[Strange Incense - Exodus 30.9, Leviticus 10, Numbers 16, and Uzziah]].

### Garments included in the makers' commission — Exodus 31

Exodus 31:10 includes Aaron's sons collectively among those for whom garments must be made. The
individual identification comes from 28:1; this verse does not name each son or narrate his service.
Their clothing is part of the workers' commission in 31:6–11.

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

Exodus 35:19 includes Aaron's sons collectively in the public commission to make priestly
garments. The individual identification comes from 28:1, not from a personal name in this
verse. The people's offerings support the holy garments (35:21), but no action by this son,
completed vesting, or ordination is reported here.

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

The coats and associated linen clothing are made for Aaron and his sons (39:27–29).
Identification with the four sons named in 28:1 is contextual: this unit supplies no individual
names, acts, or distribution of particular items among them. It advances from prescribed clothing
to manufactured clothing without yet narrating their dressing or ordination.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The garments of Aaron’s sons are included among the completed articles brought to Moses
(39:41). The four sons are identified by the earlier appointment list in 28:1, not named again
here. This is a report of their prepared clothing, not of their dressing, ordination, or service.
Moses’ inspection and blessing apply to the completed work and its makers (39:43).

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

Aaron’s sons are to be washed, clothed, and anointed for priestly service (40:12–15),
and they wash hands and feet with Moses and Aaron at the basin (40:31–32). Eleazar (son of Aaron)
is included through the explicit family list in Exodus 28:1, not named individually in
chapter 40. The general obedience statement in 40:16 should be retained, while the
detailed erection account should not be expanded into an individually narrated ordination
for each son. No separate act or distinction among the four brothers is supplied here.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

### The meal offering prescribed — Leviticus 2

Aaron’s sons present the offering and receive its most holy remainder (2:2–3, 10). Inclusion here is collective, from the family appointment in Exodus 28:1; the chapter neither names him individually nor narrates his performance of the rite. See [[The Meal Offering]].

### The burnt offering — Leviticus 1:1–17

Included collectively among Aaron’s sons, identified by Exodus 28:1, in the prescribed blood and
altar duties (Leviticus 1:5, 7–8, 11). No son is individually named or assigned a separate deed
here; this is ritual instruction, not a report that he has performed it.

See [[The Burnt Offering]] . Source: stored WEB Leviticus 1; comparisons identified on the passage
page.

### The peace offering — Leviticus 3

Included collectively as one of Aaron's sons identified in Exodus 28:1, in the prescribed blood handling and altar service of Leviticus 3:2–5, 8–11, 13–16. No individual son is named or reported performing this rite; the instructions define the family's office.

See [[The Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 3; contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Altar fire and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:8–23

Included collectively among Aaron’s sons, identified in Exodus 28:1, in the commands for altar service and holy eating. This unit neither names him individually nor reports his performance; the successor in verse 22 is not identified by personal name.

See [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:8–23; comparisons documented there.

### Holy flesh and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:24–7:10

Included collectively among Aaron’s sons, identified in Exodus 28:1, as recipients of the
holy-food and portion rules (6:25; 7:10). He is not individually named or described performing
these rites.

See [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:24–7:10.

### Aaron and his sons consecrated — Leviticus 8:1–36

Included among Aaron’s sons, identified by Exodus 28:1 rather than named individually here.
Moses washes and clothes the sons, marks their right ears, thumbs, and toes with blood, and
sprinkles them and their garments with oil and blood (6, 13, 24, 30). They share the commanded
meal and seven-day charge; their obedience is affirmed (31–36). Installation is now narrated,
not merely prescribed.

See [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 8; Exodus 28:1; 29; Leviticus 7:38.

### The eighth-day service — Leviticus 9

Included collectively among Aaron's sons, who deliver blood and portions in the inaugural service
(9:9, 12–13, 18). He is not individually named or assigned a separate action here. See
[[The Eighth Day and the Appearing Glory]] .

### Strange fire and the surviving priests — Leviticus 10:1–11

Is named with Aaron and Ithamar after his brothers’ deaths. The surviving priests must refrain from loosening their hair or tearing their clothes and remain at the tent because Yahweh’s anointing oil is on them (6–7).

See [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:1–11.

### Rebuked over the goat — Leviticus 10:12–20

Called one of Aaron's sons "who were left" (12, 16), Eleazar receives the priestly portions and, with Ithamar, bears Moses' anger when the people's sin offering is found burned (16). Aaron answers for them; Eleazar's own part in the decision is not described.

See [[The Uneaten Sin Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:12–20.

### The surviving line — Numbers 3:1–13

With Nadab and Abihu dead and childless, he and his brother *ministered in the priest's office in the presence of Aaron their father* (4). The two Levite speeches that follow give the tribe to Aaron *and to his sons* (9), so he is included in both the priesthood and the gift. 1 Chronicles 24:1–2 makes the same point (WEB verified at bible-api.com).

See [[The Generations of Aaron and the Levites Given]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 3.

### Prince of the princes — Numbers 3:14–51

*Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest shall be prince of the princes of the Levites* (32), with the oversight of those who keep the sanctuary. The surviving son now stands over the three Levite princes. His scope here is general; 4:28 and 4:33 will place Gershon and Merari under Ithamar.

See [[The Levites and the Firstborn Counted]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 3.

### The oils and the oversight — Numbers 4:1–49

Verse 16 gives him the oil for the light, the sweet incense, the continual meal offering, the anointing oil, and *the requirements of all the tabernacle*. He is also among the *sons* who go in with Aaron to cover the holy things (5, 15, 19). Gershon and Merari are put under his brother.

See [[The Service of Kohath, Gershon, and Merari]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 4.
