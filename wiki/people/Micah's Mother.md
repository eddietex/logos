# Micah's Mother

## Overview

### The curse, the blessing, and the image — Judges 17:2–4

Unnamed, like most mothers in Judges. Her *eleven hundred pieces of silver* are taken, and she utters *a
curse* on the thief in her son's hearing (17:2, stored WEB). The verb, *ʾālît*, is the only occurrence of
*ʾālâ* as a verb in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges; the noun is Leviticus 5:1's *voice of
adjuration*. When her son confesses, she answers *May Yahweh bless my son!* (17:2) and dedicates the
silver *to Yahweh from my hand for my son, to make a carved image and a molten image* (17:3). She gives
*two hundred pieces of silver* to a silversmith, and the image is made (17:4). What she did with the other
nine hundred the text does not say. See [[Micah's Idol and Levite]].

She is the chapter's chief actor before the Levite arrives. Her curse, her blessing, and her dedication
are all in Yahweh's name, and the thing dedicated is what Deuteronomy 27:15 curses. See
[[A Carved Image and a Molten Image - Deuteronomy 27.15 and Micah's House]].

### The same sum as Delilah's

The sum is the one each Philistine lord paid Delilah (16:5). Some readers have suggested she is Delilah;
the text gives no ground for it, and the wiki does not adopt it. See
[[Eleven Hundred Pieces of Silver - Delilah and Micah's Mother]].
