# Onan

## Overview

**[[Judah]]'s second son, and the only man in the Bible whose name became an English word for
something the text does not condemn him for.**

> **8** Judah said to Onan, “Go in to your brother’s wife, and perform the duty of a husband’s brother
> to her, and raise up offspring for your brother.”
> **9** Onan knew that the offspring wouldn’t be his; and when he went in to his brother’s wife, he
> spilled it on the ground, lest he should give offspring to his brother.
> **10** The thing which he did was evil in Yahweh’s sight, and he killed him also. (38:8–10)

### What the verses say

**The instruction is the Bible's first levirate summons.** ***Yabbēm*** — a verb built on ***yābām***,
*the brother-in-law* — and Judah issues it as though it needed no explanation, four books before
Deuteronomy 25:5–10 codifies the institution. **The child would be counted to [[Er]]**, would inherit
Er's portion, and **would displace Onan from the double share he stands to gain as the surviving
eldest.** Genesis states that this is what he understood: *"Onan knew that the offspring wouldn't be
his."*

**The act is coitus interruptus, described in three Hebrew words** — ***wəšiḥēt ʾarṣâ***, *he ruined
it groundward*; the verb is the flood's *šāḥat*, *to corrupt, to destroy* (6:11–13). **The Hebrew is
frequentative**: *whenever he went in to his brother's wife*. **He took the marital right every time
and withheld the obligation it was granted for every time**, which is what makes it fraud rather than
a lapse.

**And the motive is stated inside the same sentence as the act**: *"lest he should give offspring to
his brother."* **Genesis does not leave the reason to inference**, the way it left [[Er]]'s
wickedness unspecified one verse above. **What 38:10 calls evil is *"the thing which he did"*, and the
thing he did is described by its purpose.**

### What the verses do not say

**The wiki states this plainly because the verse has carried a great deal of weight that it does not
contain.** The text names **a defrauded brother, a defrauded widow, and a duty taken up in form and
refused in substance.** It does not mention the seed as such, does not generalise, and does not
legislate. **Deuteronomy 25:7–10 — the law that eventually covers exactly this refusal — penalises it
with public humiliation, not death**, which is a further reason to read 38:10 as a judgment on this
man in this case rather than a rule.

**The moral tradition built on the verse is real, old, and outside what Genesis reports**, and the
wiki records its existence without adjudicating it. What the wiki does adopt is the chapter's own
framing: **Onan is the second of three men who owe [[Tamar]] a son, and the second who does not
produce one.** The third is [[Shelah (son of Judah)]], withheld by their father — and **Judah's own
verdict at 38:26 puts himself in the same category as his sons**, not above them. See
[[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]] and [[Sin]].

**The name.** ***ʾÔnān*** is from ***ʾôn*** — *vigour*, *generative strength*, and by extension
*wealth*. It is the word in *"Reuben, you are my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my
strength"* (49:3), and in Hosea 12:3's play on the same root. **The son named for generative strength
is the one who refuses to generate**, and Genesis makes nothing of it. See [[Names and Naming]].

**He is the second of the only two individuals the narrator says Yahweh killed in Genesis**, and the
first is his brother. Both are in this paragraph, three verses apart. See [[Retribution]].

## Timeline / key events

- Ordered to perform the levirate duty for [[Er]] — Genesis 38:8
- Withholds it, *"lest he should give offspring to his brother"*; *"he killed him also"* —
  Genesis 38:9–10

## Appears in

- [[Judah and Tamar]] — Genesis 38:4, 8–10; **the Bible's first levirate summons and its first
  refusal.** He takes the right and withholds the duty, repeatedly, and **the verse that condemns him
  states his reason in the same breath as his act**
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 46:12; **listed and subtracted in the same verse**, with [[Er]]: *"but
  Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan."* **The two of them are the only deaths the register
  reports**

## Related people

- [[Judah]] — his father, who orders the duty and then declines the equivalent one himself
- [[Er]] — his elder brother, for whom he refuses to raise offspring
- [[Tamar]] — his brother's widow, defrauded twice before she acts
- [[Shelah (son of Judah)]] — the third son, withheld for the same reason Onan withheld himself
- [[Shua's Daughter]] — his mother
