# Oholibamah

## Overview

**Esau's third wife in the register of Genesis 36, unmentioned anywhere before it — and the only wife
of a patriarch's son who is also listed as a chief.**

> **2** … and Oholibamah the daughter of Anah, the daughter of Zibeon, the Hivite;
> **5** Oholibamah bore Jeush, Jalam, and Korah. …
> **41** chief Oholibamah, chief Elah, chief Pinon,

***ʾĀholîbāmâ*** means **tent of the high place** — *ʾōhel*, tent, plus *bāmâ*, the raised shrine the
Deuteronomic historian spends four hundred years complaining about. **It is the most overtly cultic
name in the chapter**, and Ezekiel 23 later builds two allegorical sisters, *Oholah* and *Oholibah*,
out of the same element. Genesis makes nothing of it.

## The descent, and the problem in it

**She is Horite on both sides, and the chapter cannot quite say how.** At 36:2 she is *"the daughter
of Anah, the daughter of Zibeon, the Hivite"*; at 36:24 *"the children of Zibeon: Aiah and Anah"* —
so [[Anah]] is Zibeon's **son**, and 36:2's second *daughter* has to mean **granddaughter**, which
Hebrew *bat* can carry. **The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint, and the Syriac all read *son* at
36:2**, which suggests the line was hard to read early. 36:25 settles her parentage the other way
round and without difficulty: *"These are the children of Anah: Dishon and Oholibamah, the daughter of
Anah."*

**The ethnic label moves too.** Her father is *the Hivite* at 36:2 and a Horite at 36:20–25. Hittite,
Hivite, Horite — the chapter uses all three of the same milieu, and the consonants of the last two
(*ḥwy* / *ḥry*) are close enough that one is likely a scribe's version of the other.

**What survives all of it is the fact that matters**: Esau's third marriage is into
[[Seir the Horite]]'s family, the people *"the inhabitants of the land"* whom Deuteronomy 2:12 says
his descendants destroyed. **The conquest Deuteronomy reports, Genesis records as two marriages** —
hers, and [[Timna]]'s into Eliphaz's household. See [[The Descendants of Esau]] and [[Edom]].

## Her sons, and one of them twice

**Jeush, Jalam, and Korah** are born in Canaan (36:5), repeated at 36:14, and made chiefs at 36:18 —
the smallest of the three branches, three chiefs against Eliphaz's seven and Reuel's four.

**Korah is also listed at 36:16 among the chiefs descended from [[Eliphaz (son of Esau)]]**, that is,
in [[Adah (wife of Esau)]]'s line rather than hers. The Samaritan Pentateuch and part of the Greek
tradition omit him there. The passage page records the doubling without choosing. (He is not the
Levite Korah of Numbers 16.)

## A chief in her own name

**36:40–43 lists *chief Oholibamah* among eleven chiefs given *"after their places"* and *"according
to their habitations"*** — a list organised territorially rather than genealogically, in which
*Mibzar* means **fortress**, *Magdiel* has the shape of a place name, and *Timna* is both a woman four
verses earlier and a copper-mining valley in the Arabah. **A woman's name standing as a district is
the clearest sign in the chapter that this final register is a map rather than a family tree.**

## The list she is absent from

**Genesis 26:34 and 28:9 name Esau's wives as [[Judith]], Basemath daughter of Elon, and
[[Mahalath]]** — and Oholibamah is in none of them, just as Judith is absent from this chapter. **She
is one of the three discrepancies between the two registers**, and the one that cannot be explained by
a woman having two names, because there is no one in the earlier list for her to be. See
[[The Names of Esau's Wives - Genesis 26 and Genesis 36]].

## Timeline / key events

- Married by Esau; daughter of [[Anah]], of Zibeon's house, of the Horites of Seir — Genesis 36:2, 25
- Bears Jeush, Jalam, and Korah in the land of Canaan — Genesis 36:5, 14
- Moves with the household to the hill country of Seir — Genesis 36:6–8
- Her three sons made chiefs in the land of Edom — Genesis 36:18
- *Chief Oholibamah* among the chiefs listed by habitation — Genesis 36:41

## Appears in

- [[The Descendants of Esau]] — Genesis 36:2, 5, 14, 18, 25, 41; **the wife Genesis introduces only
  once it has stopped telling the story.** Her marriage is where the incoming family and the
  displaced one are recorded as having joined, her parentage is the chapter's most tangled line, and
  her name appears at the end as a place
