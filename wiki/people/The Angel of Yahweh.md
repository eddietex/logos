# The Angel of Yahweh

## Overview

*Malʾak YHWH* — literally **Yahweh's messenger**. The figure enters the Bible at Genesis 16:7, in a
desert, addressing an Egyptian slave, and it enters carrying the problem it never puts down: he is
sent by God and he speaks as God, and the text will not say which.

*Malʾāk* is an ordinary Hebrew noun for an envoy, used of human messengers throughout Scripture
(Genesis 32:3; Judges 6:35). It is not a word for a species of being. The Greek *angelos* behind
English *angel* translates it exactly, and the English word's later baggage — wings, immortality, a
separate order of creature — is not in the Hebrew noun at all. What the Hebrew says is *the one sent
by Yahweh*.

### The identity problem, stated from the text

In every scene the pattern is the same and the seam is never explained.

| Passage | Introduced as | Speaks as | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis 16:7–13 | *Yahweh's angel* found her | *"**I** will greatly multiply your offspring"* (16:10) | she names **Yahweh** who spoke to her (16:13) |
| Genesis 22:11–18 | *Yahweh's angel* called from the sky | *"you have not withheld your son, your only son, from **me**"* (22:12) | Abraham names the place *Yahweh will provide* |
| Exodus 3:2–6 | *Yahweh's angel appeared in a flame* | *"I am the God of your father"* | Moses hides his face *"for he was afraid to look at God"* |
| Judges 6:11–24 | *Yahweh's angel* sat under the oak | *"Have not I sent you?"* (6:14, where the text says *Yahweh* turned to him) | Gideon: *"I have seen Yahweh's angel face to face"*, and is told he will not die |
| Judges 13:3–22 | *Yahweh's angel appeared to the woman* | refuses to give his name — *"why do you ask about my name, since it is incomprehensible?"* | Manoah: *"We shall surely die, because we have seen God"* |

Three standing readings, none of which the Old Testament rules out:

1. **A messenger who speaks in the sender's voice.** In ancient diplomatic practice an envoy delivered
   a message in the first person as the one who sent him; the alternation is a convention of
   messenger-speech, not a claim about the messenger's nature. This is the most economical reading and
   explains the grammar without explaining the reactions.
2. **A mode of Yahweh's own presence** — the way God appears when appearing directly would kill the
   viewer. This is what the reactions in Genesis 16:13, Judges 6:22–23 and 13:22 are actually about:
   every one of them is a fear of having seen God and lived, not of having met a messenger.
3. **A distinct divine figure**, developed in Second Temple Judaism (the *Angel of the Presence*, and
   the exalted mediator figures of the apocalypses) and, in classical Christian reading from Justin
   Martyr onward, identified as pre-incarnate appearances of the Son. The New Testament does not make
   this identification: Acts 7:30–38 and Galatians 3:19 treat the angel of Sinai as an angel, and
   Hebrews 1 argues at length that the Son is *not* an angel.

**The wiki's practice.** It reports the alternation where the text alternates, does not harmonize it,
and does not read later Christology back into Genesis. Reading 1 accounts for the words; reading 2
accounts for the reactions; the text supplies both and resolves neither.

### In this pericope — Genesis 16:1–16

The first appearance, and the details are worth having exactly.

- **He *finds* her** (16:7). Not *appeared*, not *came to* — *māṣāʾ*. She is not sought by anyone in
  the household and she is not looking for him.
- **He is at a spring on the road to Shur**, which is the road to Egypt — the frontier, the last water
  before the desert crossing.
- **He asks two questions and she answers one** (16:8). He also addresses her by name and by owner:
  *"Hagar, Sarai's servant"* — a formula used of no one else in Genesis.
- **He speaks three times in three consecutive verses** (16:9, 10, 11), each introduced by the full
  formula *"Yahweh's angel said to her"*. The repetition is unusual enough that many read the three as
  originally separate sayings brought together; as the text stands it gives the sequence
  *command → promise → annunciation*.
- **At 16:10 he takes the divine first person**: *"I will greatly multiply your offspring"* — the
  covenant's own formula, which only God can speak.
- **At 16:13 the narrator resolves nothing and Hagar resolves everything**: *"She called the name of
  Yahweh who spoke to her, 'You are a God who sees.'"* The one who spoke was introduced as the angel;
  the one she names is Yahweh.

He appears to her once more, at 21:17, where he is *"the angel of God"* rather than of Yahweh, calls
from the sky rather than standing at a spring, and hears the boy rather than the mother. Hagar is the
only person in Genesis he appears to twice.

### The same problem without the title — Genesis 18:1–19:1

Genesis 18 never uses the phrase *malʾak YHWH*, and it is the chapter where the alternation is
widest. *"Yahweh appeared to him"* (18:1); what Abraham sees is *"three men"* (18:2); he addresses
one in the singular and feeds three; the speaker at 18:10 takes the divine first person and is named
*Yahweh* at 18:13; *"the men"* leave for Sodom at 18:22 while *"Abraham stood yet before Yahweh"*;
and at 19:1 the party that arrives in the city is *"the two angels"*.

**Read as the book stands, the arithmetic resolves: three come, one is Yahweh, two go on as
messengers.** What the chapter will not do is say so — and the *malʾāk* label is applied only after
the one who is Yahweh has stayed behind, which is the tidiest the seam ever gets and still not tidy.
Reading 1 above (an envoy speaking in his sender's voice) accounts for 19:1; reading 2 (a mode of
God's own presence) accounts for a meal eaten under a tree by someone the narrator calls Yahweh. See
[[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]] and [[Entertaining Angels Unawares]].

## Timeline / key events

- Finds Hagar at a spring in the wilderness on the way to Shur; commands, promises, and announces a
  birth — Genesis 16:7–12, [[Hagar and Ishmael]]
- Not named as such, but present in the widest form of the same alternation: three men at Mamre, one
  of whom is Yahweh and two of whom reach Sodom as *"the two angels"* — Genesis 18:1–19:1,
  [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]]
- Calls to Hagar out of the sky at Beersheba and opens her eyes to a well — Genesis 21:17–19
  *(not yet ingested)*
- Calls to Abraham from the sky at the binding of Isaac, twice, and swears by himself — Genesis
  22:11–18 *(not yet ingested)*
- Appears in a flame in a bush and is then called *Yahweh* and *God* — Exodus 3:2–6
- Goes before Israel in the wilderness, with the warning *"my name is in him"* — Exodus 23:20–23
- Blocks Balaam's road with a drawn sword — Numbers 22:22–35
- Sits under the oak at Ophrah and commissions Gideon — Judges 6:11–24
- Announces Samson's birth to a woman who is barren, and refuses to give his name — Judges 13:3–22
- Stands between earth and sky with a drawn sword over Jerusalem — 2 Samuel 24:16; 1 Chronicles
  21:15–16
- Feeds Elijah under the broom tree — 1 Kings 19:5–7
- Stands in the heavenly court beside Joshua the high priest and rebukes the accuser — Zechariah
  3:1–5
