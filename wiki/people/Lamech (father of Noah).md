# Lamech (father of Noah)

## Overview

The ninth generation from Adam, and the only person who speaks in Genesis 5.

The chapter is a register — ten identical entries, no scenes, no dialogue — and Lamech's is the one
that stops to say something. He fathers a son and, instead of the usual formula, the text records
his voice: *"He named him Noah, saying, 'This one will comfort us in our work and in the toil of our
hands, caused by the ground which Yahweh has cursed'"* (5:29). It is the only sentence spoken by a
human being in the chapter, the only mention of the curse, and the only occurrence of the divine
name **Yahweh** in a passage that otherwise says *God* — and it is inside his quotation, not in the
narrator's voice.

What he says is a backward reference of unusual precision. *"The ground which Yahweh has cursed"* is
Genesis 3:17, and *"toil"* is that verse's word. Nine generations and — by the chapter's own
arithmetic — more than a thousand years after the sentence in the garden, the ground still feels
like a sentence to the people working it, and one man is on record hoping for relief. It is the only hope any human being expresses between the
closing of Eden and the flood. See [[Comfort from the Cursed Ground - Naming Noah]].

The name he gives is a sound-play, as names in Genesis usually are: **[[Noah]]** is *nōaḥ*, from
*nûaḥ*, "to rest", while the verb Lamech uses is *yenaḥămēnû*, from *nāḥam*, "to comfort" — related
in sound, not in root. Rest and comfort, in one breath, over a newborn.

He lives 777 years and dies five years before the flood he named his son in hope of (5:31). Seven
generations back in the parallel line, another **Lamech** — [[Lamech (son of Methushael)]] — sang
the Bible's ugliest poem, claiming to be avenged *"seventy-seven times"* (4:24). Two men with the
same name, one number, and opposite uses of it: a boast about killing, and a lifespan. Genesis never
connects them; given how much else in these two chapters is deliberately paired, most readers take
it as design, and it cannot be demonstrated either way. See
[[Two Lines from Adam - Cain's Genealogy and Seth's]].

### A note on this page's title

The wiki disambiguates the two Lamechs by parentage everywhere else — the Cainite is
[[Lamech (son of Methushael)]]. Applying the same rule here would give *Lamech (son of Methuselah)*,
which differs from the other page's title by two letters in a word most readers do not read closely.
This page is titled by his son instead, deliberately, because the pair has to be legible at a glance
in a link.
