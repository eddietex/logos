# Ahijah (son of Ahitub)

## Overview

A priest with Saul's army at Gibeah, *Ahijah, the son of Ahitub, Ichabod's brother, the son of Phinehas, the son
of Eli, the priest of Yahweh in Shiloh, wearing an ephod* (1 Samuel 14:3, stored WEB). *ʾĂḥiyyâ* (WLC) is commonly
glossed *brother of Yahweh* (recalled, not checked). Qualified by his father from the start, because a prophet
Ahijah of Shiloh is expected in Kings (recalled, not checked).

He is the great-grandson of [[Eli]], so a member of the house of which the man of God said *I will raise me up a
faithful priest* in its place (2:35, stored WEB). The relation between Ahijah and [[Ahimelech (son of Ahitub)]],
the priest at [[Nob]] (21:1–9; 22:9–20, stored WEB; 21:1–9 in [[David at Nob]], ch. 22 not yet ingested), is commonly
debated: brothers, or one man under two names (recalled, not checked). The wiki keeps them as separate pages.

### The ephod, the ark, and the broken-off inquiry — 1 Samuel 14:1–23

Ahijah is with Saul's six hundred at Gibeah, *wearing an ephod* (*nōśēʾ ʾēp̄ôḏ*, WLC), while Jonathan goes over to
the garrison (14:2–3). When the Philistine camp falls into tumult, Saul says to him, *Bring God's ark here* (14:18);
the Masoretic text reads *ark* twice, and the Septuagint is commonly reported to read *ephod* (recalled, not
checked). While Saul is still talking to him, the tumult grows, and Saul says *Withdraw your hand!* (14:19). The
inquiry is cut off before any answer. The verb for his carrying the ephod, *nōśēʾ*, is the same participle used of
the armour-bearer, *nōśēʾ ḵēlāyw* (14:1, WLC). See [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 2:35; 7:1–2; 14:1–19; 22:9–20. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### Let us draw near here to God — 1 Samuel 14:36–37

When Saul proposes a night raid and the people agree, *the priest said, "Let us draw near here to God"* (14:36). The
verse does not name him; Ahijah is the priest placed with Saul at 14:3 and 14:18–19, and the wiki takes the priest to
be him, presumably. It is the priest, not the king, who stops to ask. Saul asks, and *he didn't answer him that day*
(14:37). In 14:19 Saul had cut the priest's inquiry off; here the inquiry is made and no answer comes. See
[[Saul's Rash Oath]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 14:3, 18–19, 36–37.
