# Beeri the Hittite

## Overview

**Judith's father, named once in the Bible and only as her father.**

> **34** When Esau was forty years old, he took as wife Judith, the daughter of Beeri the Hittite,
> and Basemath, the daughter of Elon the Hittite.

**He is one of the two Hittite fathers-in-law Esau acquires at forty**, and the only thing Genesis
records about him is the marriage. **When the wives are listed again at 36:2–3 he is not in the
list**, and neither is his daughter — one of the several ways the two registers fail to line up. See
[[The Names of Esau's Wives - Genesis 26 and Genesis 36]].

***Bəʾērî*** stands on ***bəʾēr***, **well**, and reads as **my well** or **of the well** — a
perfectly ordinary Semitic name, and one that happens to arrive at the end of the chapter in which
Isaac digs, loses, re-digs, and re-names six of them. **Genesis does not point at the coincidence**,
and the wiki records it as a coincidence.

**The name is borne once more in the Hebrew Bible**, by the father of the prophet Hosea (Hosea 1:1) —
a different man, several centuries later, and not a Hittite.

**On being called a Hittite.** *Bənê-Ḥēt*, the children of Heth, are a people Genesis places in the
hill country around [[Hebron]] and treats with unusual courtesy: they are the assembly Abraham
negotiates with for the cave at Machpelah, and [[Ephron the Hittite]] sells it to him in the most
polite transaction in the book (23:3–20). **Whether they are connected to the Anatolian Hittite
empire is disputed and probably unanswerable**; Genesis uses the name of a resident population of
Canaan, and the later prohibition lists include them among the peoples Israel is not to intermarry
with (Deuteronomy 7:1–3). See [[The Children of Heth]].
