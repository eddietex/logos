# Aaron

## Overview

**Moses' elder brother, Israel's first high priest — and, at his first appearance, a name God offers
in anger to a man who has refused to go.** He enters Scripture at Exodus 4:14, inside God's answer to
Moses' fifth objection, *"Oh, Lord, please send someone else"* (4:13). This page grows as Exodus and
the rest of the Torah are ingested; what is ahead is marked as ahead.

**He is introduced by three facts and a movement.** *"What about Aaron, your brother, the Levite? I
know that he can speak well. Also, behold, he comes out to meet you. When he sees you, he will be glad
in his heart"* (4:14). **He is already travelling before Moses has agreed to anything** — the help is
on the road before the call is accepted — and they meet *"on God's mountain"* (4:27). See
[[The Burning Bush]].

**Why *the Levite*, when Moses is a Levite too, is not explained.** It may anticipate the priesthood
that will be his line's; it may reflect a use of *Levite* for a function as well as a descent; or it
may be plain full identification. The wiki records all three and adopts none.

**His office is defined before he has one.** *"He will be your spokesman to the people; and it will
happen, that he will be to you a mouth, and you will be to him as God"* (4:16). **Moses is to put
words in his mouth as God puts them in Moses'**, a chain the text makes explicit at 7:1: *"I have made
you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet."* **Joseph had spoken to his
brothers through an interpreter (Genesis 42:23); Aaron is the first person in Scripture whom God
appoints to be another man's mouth.** A rabbinic tradition (Babylonian Talmud,
Zevachim 102a) holds that the priesthood passed to Aaron's line because of Moses' refusal here; the
text does not say so.

**Brother without rivalry, at first.** After Cain and Abel, Isaac and Ishmael, Jacob and Esau, and
Joseph and his brothers, **Aaron is the first brother in Scripture whose coming is announced as
gladness** (4:14). The rivalry, when it comes (Numbers 12), is over prophetic standing and not an
inheritance. See [[Brotherhood]].

**He meets Moses as foretold, and speaks for him at once.** *"Yahweh said to Aaron, 'Go into the
wilderness to meet Moses.' He went, and met him on God's mountain, and kissed him"* (4:27). **His first
act in Scripture is to go out and kiss his brother**, with no quarrel to get past — unlike Esau's (Genesis 33:4) or Joseph's
(45:15). Moses tells him *"all Yahweh's words"* and the signs; and before the elders **it is Aaron who
*"spoke all the words which Yahweh had spoken to Moses, and did the signs in the sight of the
people"*** (4:30) — signs given into Moses' hand. *"The people believed"* (4:31). See
[[Moses Returns to Egypt]].

**At the first audience he is beside Moses at every step and has not one word assigned to him.**
*"Moses and Aaron came, and said to Pharaoh"* (5:1); *"They said"* (5:3); Pharaoh rebukes *"you, Moses
and Aaron"* (5:4); and the beaten officers find *"Moses and Aaron, who stood in the way"*, and curse
them both (5:20–21). **Then it is Moses alone who returns to Yahweh** (5:22). The mouth appointed at
4:16 speaks with Moses here rather than for him, and the text does not say which of them said what.
See [[Bricks Without Straw]].

**At 6:13 Yahweh speaks to him and to Moses together for the first time.** *"Yahweh spoke to Moses and
to Aaron, and gave them a command"* (*ʾel-mōšeh wəʾel-ʾahărōn*). God had spoken to Aaron once before,
alone, to send him to meet Moses (4:27). Here the command is given to the two of them as a pair,
directly after Moses' objection that he has *"uncircumcised lips"* (6:12). The command is not
explained, and it does not answer the objection; it adds a second person to it. See [[I Am Yahweh]].

**The genealogy of 6:14–27 names his family and puts him first.** He is Amram and Jochebed's elder
son (6:20), and the list gives him what it gives no one else: a wife with a full pedigree,
[[Elisheba]], *"the daughter of Amminadab, the sister of Nahshon"* — a woman of Judah's leading house
(6:23); four sons, [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]], [[Abihu]], [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]], and [[Ithamar]];
and a grandson, [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]] (6:25). **Moses' wife and sons are not in the list.**
Then the two brothers are named in both orders: *"These are that Aaron and Moses"* (6:26), the birth
order, and *"These are that Moses and Aaron"* (6:27), the order of the commission. **It is a priestly
pedigree before Exodus has said anything about priests.** See [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]] and
[[Elisheba Daughter of Amminadab - Aaron's Marriage into Judah]].

**At 7:1 he is named Moses' prophet — the second *nābîʾ* in Scripture.** *"I have made you as God
to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet"*. The first prophet, Abraham, was defined
by intercession (Genesis 20:7); **Aaron is defined by speech on another's behalf**, which makes 7:1
the Bible's working definition of a prophet: the mouth of someone else. **He is eighty-three, three
years older than Moses** (7:7). **Then he does the first sign before Pharaoh, and it is his rod**:
*"Aaron cast down his rod before Pharaoh and before his servants, and it became a serpent"* — a
*tannîn*, not the *nāḥāš* of 4:3. The court's magicians do the same; ***"Aaron's rod swallowed up
their rods"*** (7:10–12). **At the bush the rod was Moses'; in Pharaoh's court it is Aaron's**, and
the text does not say whether it is the same staff. See [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] and
[[Swallowed Up - Pharaoh's Dream, Aaron's Staff, and the Sea]].

**At the first plague the rod is in his hand again.** God tells Moses to tell Aaron to *"stretch
out your hand over the waters of Egypt"* — rivers, streams, pools, and *"all their ponds of water"* —
and the water becomes blood even in the stored jars (7:19). *Nəṭēh yādəkā*, *stretch out your hand*, is
the verb of God's own *"outstretched arm"* (6:6) and *"when I stretch out my hand on Egypt"* (7:5): **the
prophet's hand does what God said his would do.** The text leaves unclear whose hand lifts the rod at
7:20. See [[Water Turned to Blood]].

**At the frogs and the lice the rod is still his, and then it disappears.** He stretches his hand
over the waters and the frogs come up (8:5–6); he strikes *"the dust of the earth"* and it becomes lice
*"on man, and on animal"* (8:16–17) — the first plague the magicians cannot copy. **At the flies there
is no rod and no Aaron**: *"Yahweh did so"* (8:24). He is summoned with Moses (8:8, 25), but only Moses
speaks. See [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]].

**At the boils he takes the ashes with Moses, and then the rod is Moses'.** God tells *"Moses and
Aaron"* to take *"handfuls of ashes of the furnace"*, but specifies *"let Moses sprinkle it"*
(9:8), and the narrative follows that: *"Moses sprinkled it up toward the sky"* (9:10). **At the hail
it is *"Moses stretched out his rod"* (9:23)**, which had been Aaron's role at the blood, frogs, and
lice. Aaron is summoned with Moses when Pharaoh confesses (9:27) but says nothing. **The rod has
passed from his hand to Moses' hand** at the point where the narrative begins to say the word comes
*"by the hand of Moses"* (9:35). See [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]].

**At the locusts and the darkness he goes in, is driven out, and drops out of the scene.** He goes in
with Moses to ask *"How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me?"* (10:3), is brought back
with him (10:8), driven out with him (10:11), and summoned with him to hear Pharaoh's second confession
(10:16). **Every speaking verb that is singular is Moses'** (10:9, 25), and from 10:18 the narrative
follows Moses alone: *"He went out from Pharaoh, and prayed"*; *"Pharaoh called to Moses"* (10:24).
Aaron is not named at the darkness. See [[Locusts and Darkness]].

**In the last announcement he is named once, in the narrator's summary.** God speaks to Moses alone,
and Moses alone speaks to Pharaoh (11:1–8); then *"Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before
Pharaoh"* (11:10). **The cycle that began with Aaron's rod (7:10) ends with his name in a closing
formula.** See [[The Last Plague Announced]].

**At the Passover he is addressed with Moses, and silent.** *"Yahweh spoke to Moses and Aaron in the
land of Egypt"* (12:1): the first law Israel receives is given to both brothers. Moses alone relays it
(12:21), and the people do *"as Yahweh had commanded Moses and Aaron"* (12:28). **The rite has no
priest** — every household kills its own lamb — and the man who will be Israel's first high priest has
no part in it but to hear it. See [[The Passover]].

**On the night of the departure he is summoned with Moses and addressed with him.** Pharaoh *"called
for Moses and Aaron by night"* (12:31), and the second Passover law — who may eat — is spoken *"to Moses
and Aaron"* (12:43) and obeyed *"as Yahweh commanded Moses and Aaron"* (12:50). **He says nothing in the
pericope.** See [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]].

**At the sea he is not mentioned, and in the Song he is only a sister's brother.** Exodus 13–14 does not
name him. **The Song introduces *"Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron"*** (15:20) — the only time
Aaron appears in the pericope, and not as an actor. **Why she is named by Aaron and not by Moses** the text
does not say; Aaron is the elder (7:7), and Numbers 12 will set Miriam and Aaron together against Moses
(reported, not decided). See [[The Song of the Sea]].

**In the wilderness of Sin he becomes an object of the complaint, a voice at the glory, and the keeper of
the first thing laid up before God.** *"The whole congregation … murmured against Moses and against
Aaron"* (Exodus 16:2) — **the first time he is named with Moses as the one complained against**; at Marah
it had been Moses alone (15:24). He shares Moses' answer, *"Who are we, that you murmur against us?"* (16:7).
**Then Moses gives him the words to speak** — *"Tell all the congregation … 'Come near before Yahweh'"*
(16:9), the mouth arrangement of 4:16 — **and *"As Aaron spoke … behold, Yahweh's glory appeared in the
cloud"*** (16:10), the first *kəbôd YHWH* in Scripture (WLC). **And he lays up the jar of manna *"before
the Testimony, to be kept"*** (16:33–34) — ***hinnîaḥ***, *set down*, *deposit* (WLC) — **his first act
before the presence, done before there is a tabernacle, an ark, a Testimony, or a priesthood.** The verse
is told from after Sinai, and the wiki records the anachronism as the text's own. See
[[Manna and Quail]] and [[The Glory of God]].

**On the hill at Rephidim he holds up Moses' hand.** Moses goes up *"the top of the hill"* with *"Aaron, and
Hur"* (17:10), and when Moses' hands grow heavy, ***"Aaron and Hur held up his hands, the one on the one side, and
the other on the other side"*** (17:12). **He says nothing and the text does not say which side is his.** **The
appointment of 4:14–16 has a second form**: at the bush he was made Moses' mouth; here, with Hur, he is Moses'
hands. *Tāmak*, *held up*, occurs once before in Scripture, when Joseph *"held up his father's hand"* (Genesis
48:17; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). See [[The Battle with Amalek]].

**At the Mountain of God he eats with a foreign priest.** When Jethro offers *"a burnt offering and sacrifices for
God"*, ***"Aaron came with all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God"*** (18:12).
**The future high priest's first meal *"before God"* is at a Midianite's sacrifice**, before any altar law or
ordination. He is not mentioned in the counsel that follows. See [[Jethro's Counsel]].

**At Sinai Aaron is singled out for access without an ordination being narrated.** God tells
Moses to bring Aaron up with him, while the priests and the people must not break through
(19:24). Aaron is thus distinguished from those groups for this ascent; the text does not yet
identify him here as high priest or name the unnamed priests. The chapter ends with Moses
going down, so Aaron's actual ascent should not be reported as an event already narrated in
this unit. See [[Israel at Mount Sinai]].

**He goes up, sees God, and is left in charge.** The ascent promised at 19:24 happens at 24:9: Aaron,
his sons Nadab and Abihu, and seventy elders go up with Moses and *"saw the God of Israel"*, and *"ate
and drank"* (24:10–11). **He has no part in the blood ceremony** — the sacrifices are offered by
*"young men"* and Moses handles the blood (24:5–8) — so the future high priest watches the covenant
made without officiating. When Moses goes on up, he leaves the elders with *"Aaron and Hur"* to settle
disputes (24:14). It is to this Aaron, left in charge, that the people will come when Moses delays
(32:1). See [[The Blood of the Covenant]].

### What is ahead

The priestly
garments and ordination (28–29); **the golden calf, which he makes** (32); and his death on Mount Hor
(Numbers 20). These sections are written as their pericopes arrive.

### The lamp service prescribed — Exodus 27:1–21

Aaron and his sons receive a specific sanctuary duty before the appointment and garments
of chapter 28: they must keep the lamp in order outside the veil from evening to morning
(Exodus 27:21). Israel supplies the oil. The command anticipates their service; it does not
narrate their ordination or place them within the most holy space. See [[The Altar and the Court]]
and [[From Evening to Morning - The Lamp in Exodus 27 and Leviticus 24]].

### The priestly garments prescribed — Exodus 28:1–43

**Aaron is named for *"the priest's office"*** and becomes the subject of the whole chapter
(Exodus 28:1). He is to wear the ephod, breastplate, blue robe, coat, turban, sash, gold plate,
and linen breeches. He bears the names of the children of Israel on his shoulders and on his
heart *"for a memorial"*, the Urim and Thummim and *"the judgment of the children of Israel"*,
and *"the iniquity of the holy things"* on his forehead (28:12, 29–30, 38). The bells are heard
*"that he not die"* (28:35). The garments are prescribed, not yet made or worn; the ordination
follows in chapter 29. See [[The Priestly Garments]], [[Urim and Thummim - The Breastplate of Judgment and Later Inquiry]], and [[Twelve Stones and Twelve Names - The Breastplate and the New Jerusalem]].
