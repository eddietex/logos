# Reuben

## Overview

### *Why did you sit among the sheepfolds?* — Judges 5:15–16

*By the watercourses of Reuben, there were great resolves of heart. Why did you sit among the
sheepfolds? To hear the whistling for the flocks? At the watercourses of Reuben, there were great
searchings of heart* (5:15–16). Reuben resolved, deliberated, and did not come. *Resolves* and
*searchings* differ by one consonant (*ḥiqqəqê*, *ḥiqrê*, WLC). The tribe that pledged in Numbers
32:17 to be *ready armed to go before the children of Israel* is the first the Song shames for
staying behind. See [[The Tribes Called by Name - Genesis 49, Deuteronomy 33, and Judges 5]] and
[[Those Who Did Not Come - Meroz, Succoth and Penuel, and Jabesh Gilead]]; also
[[The Song of Deborah]].

Source: stored WEB Numbers 32:17; Judges 5:15–16; Hebrew WLC.

### The altar of witness — Joshua 22:10–34

The Reubenite descendants help build the altar by the Jordan, answer the charge of rebellion, and
with Gad name it *A Witness Between Us that Yahweh is God* (34). The patriarch does not act.

See [[The Altar of Witness by the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:10–34; comparisons as cited.

### The eastern tribes sent home — Joshua 22:1–9

The Reubenite descendants are commended for remaining with their brothers and released to return
with wealth to the eastern possession (1–9). The patriarch does not act here.

See [[The Eastern Tribes Sent Home]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:1–9; comparisons as cited.

### Levitical cities — Joshua 21:1–42

The tribe descended from him contributes cities and suburbs to the Levites (21:7, 36–37). The ancestor is not an actor in this distribution.

See [[The Cities of the Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:1–42.

### Refuge cities appointed — Joshua 20:1–9

Bezer in the wilderness plain is assigned from Reuben’s tribe as an eastern refuge (8), repeating the city named in Deuteronomy 4:43. The descendants, not the patriarch, are meant.

See [[The Cities of Refuge Appointed]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 20:1–9.

### Benjamin’s inheritance — Joshua 18:11–28

Named as Bohan’s father in the boundary stone (17). This geographical notice does not narrate an action by the patriarch or fix Bohan’s generation.

See [[The Inheritance of Benjamin]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 18:11–28; comparisons as cited.

### The survey at Shiloh — Joshua 18:1–10

The descendant tribe’s eastern inheritance through Moses is confirmed as already received (7),
outside the seven new portions.

See [[The Land Surveyed at Shiloh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 18:1–10; comparisons as cited.

### Judah’s borders — Joshua 15:1–12

Bohan is called son of Reuben in the name of the boundary stone (6). This does not narrate an action of the patriarch or establish the precise generation intended.

See [[The Borders of Judah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:1–12.

### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

The descendants receive the plain and cities from the Arnon-valley Aroer, with the Jordan as border (15–23). Dibon, built by Gad in Numbers 32:34, is listed here in Reuben’s share (17); the difference is retained, not explained by an invented transfer.

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### The twelve stones — Joshua 4:1–24

Reuben’s descendants cross armed with Gad and half of Manasseh as Moses instructed (12–13). About
forty thousand describes the combined force, not Reuben alone. Crossing begins service without yet
discharging it.
See [[The Twelve Stones from the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 4:1–24.

### The eastern tribes pledge to cross — Joshua 1:10–18

Joshua reminds *the Reubenites* (12), with Gad, Reuben and the half-tribe of Manasseh, of Moses' word: families and livestock stay east of the Jordan, and all the *mighty men of valor* cross armed ahead of their brothers and help them until Yahweh gives the others rest (13–15). They answer *All that you have commanded us we will do* (16) and set death for anyone who rebels against Joshua's word (18). The tribe, not the patriarch, is in view.

See [[The Eastern Tribes Pledge to Cross]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 1:10–18.

### Moses blesses the tribes — Deuteronomy 33:1–29

Moses prays for the tribe to live and not become few (6). This differs from the removal of
preeminence in Genesis 49:3–4 without explicitly reversing that verdict.

See [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 33:1–29; comparisons as cited.


### The covenant in Moab — Deuteronomy 29:1–29

The Reubenites receive a share of the defeated kings’ land (8). The reference is to the tribe, not the patriarch personally.

See [[The Covenant in Moab and the Hidden Things]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 29:1–29.

### The responsive curses — Deuteronomy 27:11–26

The tribe bearing this ancestor’s name is assigned to Mount Ebal for the curse (13). This concerns the descendants, not the patriarch. The assignment does not pronounce this tribe morally inferior or uniquely cursed.

See [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:11–26.


### The father's wife — Deuteronomy 22:30

*"A man shall not take his father's wife, and shall not uncover his father's skirt."* Reuben is not named,
but he is the one man in Genesis who did this, with Bilhah (Genesis 35:22), and lost the firstborn's place
for it (49:4). Deuteronomy gives the prohibition without a penalty. See
[[Lying with the Father's Concubine - A Claim on the Succession]].

See [[Marriage Accusations and Sexual Offenses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 22:30; Genesis 35:22; 49:4.

### Remembered deeds and the rain-fed land — Deuteronomy 11:1–17

The tribal ancestor appears in Dathan and Abiram's lineage (6). Their judgment is one of the deeds the assembly is told it has seen.

See [[Remember His Deeds and Guard Your Hearts]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 11:1–17.

### Eastern refuge cities — Deuteronomy 4:41–43

Bezer, in the wilderness and plain country, is designated for the Reubenites (43).
The tribal descendants, not the patriarch, receive this refuge provision.

See [[Three Cities Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:41–43.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

The Reubenites receive land with Gad from the Arnon through Gilead to the Jabbok and along the
Jordan (12, 16–17). Their families and livestock may remain, but their warriors must help the other
tribes (18–20). These are the patriarch’s descendants.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

The tribe has already received its inheritance east of the Jordan (14–15); no Reubenite official is named for this western distribution. This concerns the descendants, not an action by the patriarch.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

With Gad, the tribe has *a very great multitude of livestock* and asks for the conquered land east of
the Jordan: *Don't bring us over the Jordan* (1–5). Named first in verse 1 and second, after Gad, in most of
the chapter. Moses calls the two tribes *an increase of sinful men* in their fathers' place (14), and they
answer by promising to go armed before Israel until every tribe has its inheritance (16–19). Reuben builds
Heshbon, Elealeh, Kiriathaim, Nebo, Baal Meon, and Sibmah, and changes some of their names (37–38). The
firstborn's tribe takes the first settled land, on the east bank; Deborah will later ask it *Why did you
sit among the sheepfolds?* (Judges 5:16; WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested).

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

The tribe bearing his name has 43,730 men in the second military census (Numbers 26). The register
names ancestral families, not a new action by the patriarch. He retains the firstborn title (5).

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


### The rebellion of Korah — Numbers 16:1–40

The Reubenite affiliation of Dathan, Abiram, and On is stated at the opening (1). The patriarch is
an ancestral designation, not an actor, and the text does not charge the whole tribe with their
rebellion.

See [[The Rebellion of Korah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:1–40; comparisons as cited.

**Jacob's firstborn, and the first of the twelve to lose what being firstborn was for.**

He is born in the verse after God opens [[Leah]]'s womb, and his mother's naming speech is an argument
addressed to a husband who is not there: *"Because Yahweh has looked at my affliction; for now my
husband will love me"* (29:32). **The name is transparent Hebrew — *rəʾû bēn*, *see, a son* — and Leah
does not use that sense.** She glosses ***rāʾâ***, *looked*, from the clause about God, and then adds a
second clause that puns on nothing. **Genesis hands the reader an obvious etymology and has the mother
decline it**, which it does again at [[Joseph]]. See [[Names and Naming]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 29:31–30:24

**He is the only one of the twelve children who does anything.** *"Reuben went in the days of wheat
harvest, and found mandrakes in the field, and brought them to his mother, Leah"* (30:14) — **old
enough to walk in a field at harvest, young enough to bring his mother a present**, and the errand
starts the only conversation the two sisters have in the Bible. **Genesis gives him no words.** The
mandrakes are traded for a night with his father, and the son born of that night is named for the
transaction rather than for the plant. See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 35:1–29

**One sentence, with no preparation, no scene, and no consequence — and it costs him everything.**

> **22** While Israel lived in that land, Reuben went and lay with Bilhah, his father’s concubine,
> and Israel heard of it. Now the sons of Jacob were twelve.

***Wayyišmaʿ*** — *he heard* — **is the verb from the previous chapter**, where *"Jacob heard that he
had defiled Dinah … and held his peace"* (34:5). **Twice in two chapters Genesis tells us what this
father heard and refuses to tell us what he thought.** And here the Hebrew text itself will not finish
the sentence: **a *piska bĕʾemṣaʿ pasuq*, a section break in the middle of a verse**, falls after
*"Israel heard of it"*, one of very few in the Bible. The Septuagint supplies *"and it appeared evil
before him"*; **the wiki records that as an ancient reading of the break, not as a recovered Hebrew
clause.**

**The act is not only a sexual offence; it is how the Bible's later politics announces a claim on a
man's position** — Abner with Saul's concubine (2 Samuel 3:7–8), Absalom with David's ten on the
palace roof *"in the sight of all Israel"* (2 Samuel 16:21–22), Adonijah executed for merely asking
for Abishag (1 Kings 2:22). **Genesis supplies none of that apparatus for Reuben**: no counsellor, no
audience, no stated motive. See
[[Lying with the Father's Concubine - A Claim on the Succession]], which sets out the three readings
the wiki holds open — a bid for the succession, an act on [[Leah]]'s behalf now that [[Rachel]] is
three verses dead, and the narrator simply recording an offence without a motive.

**And the verse that ruins him is the verse that completes the house.** *"Now the sons of Jacob were
twelve"*, and then the roster — in which he is named first and titled: ***"Reuben (Jacob's
firstborn)"*** (35:23). **Genesis states the rank and the disqualification back to back and comments
on neither.** See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 37:1–36

**He wins the argument and loses the boy, and the chapter is careful to show that those are two different
things.**

> **21** Reuben heard it, and delivered him out of their hand, and said, “Let’s not take his life.”
> **22** Reuben said to them, “Shed no blood. Throw him into this pit that is in the wilderness, but lay no
> hand on him”—that he might deliver him out of their hand, to restore him to his father.

**He does not say *don't do it*.** He says *not blood* — and proposes the cistern **the others had already
named as the disposal method** (37:20). **The speech contains no rescue**, and the narrator has to supply
one: *"that he might deliver him out of their hand, to restore him to his father."* **It is the only motive
Genesis states for anybody in the chapter, and it belongs to the plan that fails.**

***"Lay no hand on him."*** ***ʾAl-tišləḥû-bô yād*** — **the angel's clause at Moriah**, where the voice
from heaven stops Abraham with *"don't lay your hand on the boy"* (22:12), **and the boy there and the boy
here are both a *naʿar*.** At Moriah a divine voice arrests the knife; **here the eldest brother speaks the
same words in a chapter where no divine voice speaks at all**, and what they buy is not a rescue but a
slave sale. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

**He is not present for the sale.** *"Reuben returned to the pit; and saw that Joseph wasn't in the pit;
and he tore his clothes"* (37:29) — **and his surprise is the strongest narrative evidence for the reading
that two accounts of the sale have been interleaved here**, one in which the brothers sell Joseph and one
in which Midianites take him out of a pit the brothers had only meant to leave him in. See
[[Ishmaelites or Midianites - The Caravan That Bought Joseph]].

***"The child is no more; and I, where will I go?"*** ***Hayyeled ʾên wa-ʾănî ʾānâ ʾănî-bāʾ*** — **and the
grammar of the grief is about himself.** The boy is gone, and *where do I go?* **It is the eldest son's
exposure he names, not the boy's fate**, and Jacob will use the first clause back at him at 42:36.

**Then his silence, and it is total.** He tears his clothes at 37:29; his father tears his clothes at
37:34; **the same gesture, five verses apart, for the same boy — and the firstborn who knows the boy is
alive stands among *"all his sons"* comforting a father he could undeceive in one sentence** (37:35).
**Genesis gives him no further word in the chapter, and no word about it for the rest of the book.**

### In this pericope — Genesis 49:3–4

**Jacob says nothing about 35:22 for fourteen chapters**, and then, from his deathbed:

> **3** “Reuben, you are my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my strength; excelling in
> dignity, and excelling in power. **4** Boiling over like water, you shall not excel; because you went
> up to your father’s bed, then defiled it. He went up to my couch. (49:3–4)

**Verse 3 is the fullest statement of firstborn right anywhere in Genesis and verse 4 removes it.**
***Rēʾšît ʾônî***, *the beginning of my strength*, **is the technical phrase Deuteronomy 21:17 uses in
the law forbidding exactly this transfer**, and the wiki records the collision without resolving it.
***Paḥaz kammayim***, WEB *"Boiling over like water"*, **is a hapax** — *unstable*, *reckless*,
*wanton* and *frothing* are all readings of a word that occurs once.

**The harshest thing in it is the grammar.** **Jacob addresses him as *you* — *you went up to your
father's bed* — and then stops**: ***"He went up to my couch."*** **The father turns from second
person to third mid-verse and finishes the sentence to the room.** Genesis offers no comment.

**The double portion goes to Joseph, the priesthood to [[Levi]], the kingship to [[Judah]]** — and 1
Chronicles 5:1–2 states the redistribution outright: *"he was the firstborn; but… his birthright was
given to the sons of Joseph."* **The son of the hated wife loses the birthright over the slave of the
loved one**, which is the configuration Deuteronomy 21:15–17 was written about. See
[[Birthright and the Firstborn]] and
[[Loved and Hated - Genesis 29 and Deuteronomy 21.15-17]].

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

The tribe settles east of the Jordan (Numbers 32), is reproached by Deborah for staying home
(Judges 5:15–16), and produces no judge, no king, and no prophet. **Moses' blessing gives it four
words: *"Let Reuben live, and not die"*** (Deuteronomy 33:6) — **the barest saying in that poem**, and
the counterpart to the barest in this one. See
[[Jacob's Blessing and the Blessing of Moses - Genesis 49 and Deuteronomy 33]].

### The census — Numbers 1:1–54

His tribe heads the census, 46,500, seventh in size. The register calls him *Israel's firstborn* (20), the title kept even after Genesis 49:4. Elizur son of Shedeur stands for the tribe (5).

See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 1.

### The camp — Numbers 2:1–34

His tribe heads the south camp and sets out second (10–16). Reuben keeps the leadership of a camp, and yields the first place to Judah. Numbers does not give a reason.

See [[The Camp Around the Tent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 2.

### The altar gifts — Numbers 7:30–35

The tribe of Reuben gives on the fourth day of twelve, by the hand of Elizur. Its gift is identical to every other tribe's. The firstborn's tribe gives fourth, because the order is the camp's.

See [[The Offerings of the Twelve Princes]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 7.

### The second standard — Numbers 10:18

Reuben's standard follows the tabernacle, with Elizur son of Shedeur over its army.

See [[The March from Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 10.

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** *The Reubenites* receive the east bank from Moses (12:6) — the tribe, not the patriarch. See [[The Kings Struck Down]].
