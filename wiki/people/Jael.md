# Jael

## Overview

### *Blessed above women* — Judges 5:6, 24–27

The Song names her twice. First as a date, *in the days of Shamgar the son of Anath, in the days of
Jael, the highways were unoccupied* (5:6), before her deed is told. Then with the verdict the prose
withheld: *Jael shall be blessed above women, the wife of Heber the Kenite; blessed shall she be
above women in the tent* (5:24), set directly after the curse on Meroz (5:23). The killing is
retold: *He asked for water. She gave him milk. She brought him butter in a lordly dish. She put her
hand to the tent peg, and her right hand to the workmen's hammer* (5:25–26), four verbs for the blow
(*hālmâ, māḥăqâ, māḥăṣâ, ḥālpâ*, WLC), and his fall *at her feet* (5:27).

**The tension with 4:21, weighed and still open.** The prose says *he was in a deep sleep*; the Song
does not mention sleep and has him *bow* and *fall* *at her feet*. One reading: the Song tells the
same scene as poetry, its repeated verbs slowing a collapse, and *kāraʿ*, *bowed*, describes
crouching or collapse elsewhere (Genesis 49:9; Judges 7:5–6), not necessarily a man standing. The
other reading: the Song knows a different killing, face to face, perhaps while he drank. The text
supports the first without ruling out the second; the wiki records both and does not choose. See
[[The Song of Deborah]], Summary.

**The moral question.** The Song's blessing is a verdict, not an argument. It does not address the
peace between Jabin and Heber's house (4:17) or the duties of a host; the difficulty recorded above
stands beside the blessing, not beneath it. The same words of blessing are given to Mary in Luke
1:28, 42 (WEB); see [[Blessed Among Women - Jael in Judges 5.24 and Mary in Luke 1]].

*Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite* (Judges 4:17), the woman into whose hand Yahweh *sold* Sisera, as
Deborah had said (4:9). She has not been mentioned before 4:17, and the tent where she kills Sisera is
called hers, not her husband's.

**What she does.** Sisera, running on foot from the rout at the Kishon, comes to her tent *for there was
peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite* (4:17). She goes out to meet
him and invites him in: *Turn in, my lord, turn in to me; don't be afraid* (4:18). She covers him with a
rug, gives him milk when he asks for water, and covers him again (4:19). When he is in a deep sleep she
takes a tent peg and a hammer, goes *softly* to him, and drives the peg through his temple *into the
ground* (4:21). When Barak comes in pursuit she goes out to meet him too and shows him *the man whom
you seek* (4:22).

**What the text says about her.** Judges 4 reports her actions and gives no verdict on them, and it
never says what she was thinking or whether she planned the killing when she invited him in. Judges 5
calls her *blessed above women … above women in the tent* (5:24, next pericope). The narrator of 4:23
gives the victory to God: *God subdued Jabin*.

**The open question.** She is a host who kills her guest, from a household at peace with the king
whose general he is. Against that stand Sisera's twenty years of oppression (4:3), Yahweh's word about
a woman's hand (4:9), and the Song's blessing. The text sets out both and does not decide between them.
The wiki records both and does not choose. See [[Hospitality]] and [[Deception]].

**Account to compare.** Judges 4:21 has Sisera asleep when struck; Judges 5:27 has him *bowed* and
*fell* *at her feet*, which some read as a man standing. Weighed in the section above; still open.

**Her name.** *Yāʿēl* is also the word for *mountain goat*; reported, not verified.
