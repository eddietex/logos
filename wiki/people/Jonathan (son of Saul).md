# Jonathan (son of Saul)

## Overview

The son of [[Saul]], introduced without a patronymic as the commander of one thousand men at
[[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] (13:2); 13:16 calls him *Jonathan his son* (stored WEB). Not to be confused with
[[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]], the Levite priest of the Danites' image (Judges 18:30). *Yônāṯān* (WLC) is
usually glossed *Yahweh has given* (the gloss is not checked against a lexicon). The page grows as 1 Samuel is
ingested.

### First blow at Geba — 1 Samuel 13:1–15

Jonathan's first act is the one that starts the war: he *struck the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba*
(13:3), *nəṣîḇ pəlištîm* (WLC). The next verse reports what *all Israel heard*: *Saul had struck the garrison*
(13:4). The text records the change of name without comment. The Philistines answer with a great army at
[[Michmash]], Israel scatters, and at [[Gilgal]] Saul, waiting for Samuel, offers the burnt offering and is told
his kingdom will not continue (13:5–14). Jonathan is not at Gilgal. The dynasty forfeited there would have been
his, and the next chapter makes him the hero of Michmash (14:1–23, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 13:1–16; Judges 18:30. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-18.

### One of two swords — 1 Samuel 13:16–23

Called *Jonathan his son* (13:16), he holds Geba with Saul while the Philistine raiders go out from Michmash. With no
smith in Israel, *neither sword nor spear was found in the hand of any of the people … but Saul and Jonathan his son
had them* (13:22). The verse sets up his attack on the garrison at *the pass of Michmash* (13:23; 14:1, stored WEB;
not yet ingested). His sword later passes to David with his robe (18:4, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[No Smith in Israel]] and
[[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 13:16–23; 14:1; 18:4.

### It may be that Yahweh will work for us — 1 Samuel 14:1–23

Jonathan goes over the pass with only his armour-bearer, and *didn't tell his father* (14:1). His reason is given as
a statement about Yahweh rather than a promise from him: *It may be that Yahweh will work for us; for there is no
restraint on Yahweh to save by many or by few* (14:6). *ʾÛlay*, *it may be* (WLC), marks the venture as uncertain;
the thing that is certain is Yahweh's freedom to save with two. He sets a sign in advance, and takes the harder
answer, *Come up to us*, as Yahweh's: *Yahweh has delivered them into the hand of Israel* (14:8–12). He climbs *on
his hands and on his feet*, and about twenty fall before him (14:13–14). The rest is the trembling, the earthquake,
and the Philistines' own swords (14:15, 20), and the narrator ends *So Yahweh saved Israel that day* (14:23).

The WLC spells his name *yônāṯān* through the pericope except in verses 6 and 8, where it has the long form
*yəhônāṯān*, with the divine name written out (morphhb lemmas 3129 and 3083); those are the verses of his confession
and his sign. The wiki records the spelling and draws no conclusion from it. His armour-bearer answers him *I am with
you according to your heart* (14:7), *kilḇāḇeḵā* (WLC), the phrase Samuel had used of the man Yahweh sought *after
his own heart* (13:14). Gideon's story had made the same point about numbers (Judges 7:2, 7). See
[[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]],
[[Yahweh Saved Israel That Day - The Sea and the Pass at Michmash]], and
[[My Hand Has Saved Me - Deuteronomy 8.17 and Judges 7.2]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 13:14; 14:1–23; Judges 7:2, 7. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### My father has troubled the land — 1 Samuel 14:24–46

Jonathan was over the pass when his father swore the people from food, and *didn't hear* (14:27). In the forest he
dips the end of his rod in the honeycomb, *and his eyes brightened*. Told of the oath, he answers with a verdict on
his father: *My father has troubled the land* (14:29), *ʿāḵar* (WLC), the verb of Joshua's sentence on Achan (Joshua
7:25) and of Jephthah to his daughter (Judges 11:35). The lot that found Achan finds him (14:41–42). He hides nothing:
*I certainly did taste a little honey … and behold, I must die* (14:43). The people refuse: *Shall Jonathan die, who
has worked this great salvation in Israel? … he has worked with God today!*, and they *rescued* him, *wayyip̄dû*
(14:45, WLC), the verb of redemption. Where Jephthah's daughter died under her father's vow, Jonathan is redeemed from
his father's oath. See [[Saul's Rash Oath]],
[[A Father's Oath and His Child - Jephthah's Daughter and Jonathan]], and
[[Taken by Lot - Achan, Saul, and Jonathan]].

Source: stored WEB Joshua 7:25; Judges 11:35; 1 Samuel 14:24–46. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### First of Saul's sons — 1 Samuel 14:49

*Now the sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchishua* (14:49). In the summary of the reign Jonathan is only a
name in a list, the first of three, beside two sisters, a mother, and a cousin who commands the army. He and
Malchishua are the two sons in every list of Saul's sons (14:49; 31:2; 1 Chronicles 8:33; 9:39, the last two WEB
at bible-api.com). Here he is spelled *yônāṯān* (WLC). See [[Saul's Wars and Household]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 14:49; 31:2. 1 Chronicles 8:33; 9:39 checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-19. Hebrew WLC.

## Timeline / key events

- Commands a thousand of Saul's three thousand at Gibeah of Benjamin — 1 Samuel 13:2.
- Strikes the Philistine garrison at Geba; the blow is reported as Saul's — 13:3–4.
- Holds Geba with Saul; one of the two armed men in Israel's army — 13:16, 22.
- Goes over the pass with his armour-bearer, sets a sign, climbs to the garrison, and strikes about twenty men; Yahweh routs the camp — 14:1–23.
- Eats honey unaware of Saul's oath; *My father has troubled the land*; taken by lot; *behold, I must die*; rescued by the people — 14:27–45.
- Named first of Saul's sons in the summary of the reign — 14:49.
