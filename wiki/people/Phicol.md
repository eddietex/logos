# Phicol

## Overview

**The commander of Abimelech's army, named three times in Genesis, silent in all three.**

He appears at the treaty with Abraham (21:22, 21:32) and again a generation later at Isaac's treaty
at the same place (26:26). He never speaks, never fights, and never does anything the narrator
records except arrive and leave. **That is not an oversight; it is his function.** A treaty needs a
witness of rank, and the man who commands the army is the person whose presence tells the other party
what the alternative to agreeing would have been.

**On the name.** *Pîkōl* has no accepted Hebrew etymology. The folk reading — *pî-kōl*, **mouth of
all**, hence *spokesman of everyone* — is old, attractive, and unprovable; it would fit a herald
better than a general. Others have proposed a non-Semitic origin, Anatolian or Aegean, on the
grounds that Genesis places him in [[The Land of the Philistines]] and that the historical
Philistines came from that world. Nothing decides it. Like *Abimelech*, the name may be a
title rather than a personal name, which is one of the ways the two treaty scenes might be
reconciled.

### In this pericope — Genesis 21:22–34

> **22** At that time, Abimelech and Phicol the captain of his army spoke to Abraham, saying, “God is
> with you in all that you do.

The WEB has both men speaking; the Hebrew verb is singular (*wayyōʾmer*) with a compound subject, and
everything that follows is Abimelech's — *"swear to me"*, *"my son"*, *"the kindness that I have done
to you"*. **Phicol is there to be seen, not heard.**

He is named again at the end, in the departure notice: *"Abimelech rose up with Phicol, the captain
of his army, and they returned into the land of the Philistines"* (21:32). The repetition of his full
title at both ends of the scene is a formal bracket — the same way a modern document names its
witnesses twice.

**And the title is the pericope's only military fact.** Nothing in these thirteen verses is violent.
A well has been *"violently taken away"* by servants and is settled by a complaint and a gift; the
general stands by while sheep change hands. See [[The Treaty at Beersheba]].

### At Isaac's treaty — Genesis 26:26

*"Then Abimelech went to him from Gerar, and Ahuzzath his friend, and Phicol the captain of his
army."* The same name and the same title, one patriarch later, at the same well, for the same kind of
agreement — with a third man added, *Ahuzzath his friend*, which in ANE usage is a court office
rather than a personal remark.

**Whether this is the same man is unanswerable and probably the wrong question.** The options are the
ones the wiki keeps for [[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]]: one long-serving officer across two
patriarchal generations; a hereditary or titular office held by two men; or one tradition attached to
both Abraham and Isaac. Genesis 26:1's *"besides the first famine that was in the days of Abraham"*
shows the book knows it is telling a second version of something. See
[[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]].
