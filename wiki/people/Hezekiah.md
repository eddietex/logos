# Hezekiah

## Overview

King of Judah, whose reign is narrated in 2 Kings 18–20, Isaiah 36–39, and 2 Chronicles 29–32 — none
of which has yet been ingested, so this page is a placeholder that grows as the wiki reaches him. He
enters the wiki by name in an ingested passage in the heading of the second Solomonic collection of
Proverbs: *"These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out"*
(Proverbs 25:1). See [[The Glory of Kings]].

**What the heading says, and what it does not.** It does not make Hezekiah an author. It credits the
sayings of chapters 25–29 to [[Solomon]] and their transmission to *"the men of Hezekiah"* — people
of his court, unnamed — who *"copied out"* (Hebrew *heʿtîqû*) the collection. It therefore places the
book's compilation, at least of this part, no earlier than his reign, some two and a half centuries
after Solomon. Who those men were, and whether they also shaped the rest of the book, the text does
not say. See [[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]].

Earlier wiki pages already mention him in passing, from books not yet ingested: his prayer on his
sickbed, *"Remember now, Yahweh"* (Isaiah 38:3), on
[[Remember Me - Genesis 40.14 and the Prayers of the Condemned]]; his plea *"Be my security"* (Isaiah
38:14) on
[[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]]; and the boil healed
with a cake of figs (2 Kings 20:7; Isaiah 38:21) on
[[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]]. His reign is also the setting of a Simeonite
campaign against the remnant of Amalek (1 Chronicles 4:43); see [[Amalek]].
