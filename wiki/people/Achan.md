# Achan

## Overview

### Remembered at the Jordan — Joshua 22:20

The delegation recalls him as *Achan the son of Zerah*: *wrath fell on all the congregation of
Israel. That man didn't perish alone in his iniquity* (20). The patronymic is shortened from the
four generations of 7:1, 18. Zerah is his ancestor, not his father. His case becomes a precedent:
one member's trespass endangers the whole people, which is why an eastern altar is the western
tribes' business.

See [[The Altar of Witness by the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:10–34; comparisons as cited.

### The capture of Ai — Joshua 8:1–29

Achan is not named in chapter 8, but the battle is fought twice because of him. Two details look
back. Ai's goods and livestock are given to Israel by command (2, 27) — what Achan took by theft at
Jericho, Israel now receives by Yahweh's word; the irony is a common reading, left implicit by the
text. And the heap of stones over the king of Ai is described in the same words as
the heap over Achan: *a great heap of stones … to this day* (*gal-ʾăḇānîm gāḏôl ʿaḏ hayyôm hazzeh*,
WLC 7:26; 8:29).

See [[The Capture of Ai]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 8:1–29; WLC (morphhb) where Hebrew is named.

### The sin of Achan — Joshua 7:1–26

A man of Judah, *the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah* (7:1, 18) — four generations
of ancestry, given twice, because the selection in 7:16–18 narrows to him through exactly those
steps: tribe, clan, household, man. He took from the devoted things of [[Jericho]] what Joshua 6:18–19
had forbidden, and Israel was routed at [[Ai]] before anyone knew why.

His confession is the fullest statement of motive in the conquest narrative: *When I saw among the
plunder a beautiful Babylonian robe, two hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold weighing
fifty shekels, then I coveted them and took them* (7:21). The sequence *saw … coveted … took*
repeats the roots of Genesis 3:6 (WLC); see [[Saw, Coveted, Took - Eve and Achan]]. He hid the goods
in the ground under his tent, with the silver beneath.

He confesses when Joshua, addressing him as *My son*, asks him to give glory to Yahweh (7:19–20). He
is stoned and burned in the [[Valley of Achor]], with his goods, livestock, and tent; the text lists
his sons and daughters among those brought there, and the wiki's passage page records the tension
this raises with Deuteronomy 24:16 rather than resolving it. A great heap of stones is raised over
him.

**His name.** Joshua spells it *ʿāḵān* (WLC 7:1). Joshua's sentence plays on the valley's name and
the verb *ʿāḵar*, *trouble* (7:25). 1 Chronicles 2:7 gives the name as *Achar, the troubler of
Israel, who committed a trespass in the devoted thing* (WEB, verified at bible-api.com, 2026-11-10),
making the pun into the name itself. Whether the Chronicler's spelling is a deliberate wordplay or a
variant form is not stated in either text.

**Later memory.** Joshua 22:20 cites him when the western tribes confront the eastern ones: *Didn't
Achan the son of Zerah commit a trespass in the devoted thing, and wrath fell on all the
congregation of Israel? That man didn't perish alone in his iniquity.* He becomes Israel's standing
example of one man's sin bringing wrath on the whole community.

**Carmi** his father shares a name with a son of Reuben (Genesis 46:9); the two are different men in
different tribes. Neither Carmi nor Zabdi has a page.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 6–7, 22:20; WLC (morphhb), retrieved 2026-11-10.
