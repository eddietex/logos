# Ruth (person)

## Overview

### *Spread the corner of your garment* — Ruth 3

At the end of the harvest Naomi plans a night visit to Boaz on the threshing floor, and Ruth answers, *All
that you say, I will do* (3:5). She washes, anoints herself, dresses, waits until he has eaten and drunk,
uncovers his feet, and lies down (3:3–7). When he wakes and asks *Who are you?*, she does not wait to be
told what to do, as Naomi said she would be (4), but asks: *I am Ruth your servant. Therefore spread the
corner of your garment over your servant; for you are a near kinsman* (9). *Corner* is *kənāp̄*, the
*wings* of Boaz's blessing at 2:12, and the request joins marriage to the redeemer's duty. Boaz calls her
kindness greater than the first, *because you didn't follow young men, whether poor or rich*, and says the
whole town knows she is *a worthy woman*, *ʾēšeṯ ḥayil* (10–11). She leaves before dawn with six measures
of barley so as not to go home *empty* (14–17). The chapter does not call her *the Moabitess*. See [[Ruth at the Threshing Floor]],
[[Under His Wings - Ruth 2.12, 3.9, and the Refuge of the Psalms]], and
[[At Night with a Man Who Had Drunk - Genesis 19.30-38 and Ruth 3]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 3:1–18; 2:12;
Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### *Since I am a foreigner* — Ruth 2

Ruth asks to glean *after him in whose sight I find favor* (2:2) and *happened to come* to Boaz's field (2:3).
The overseer reports that she asked permission and has worked since morning (2:7). When Boaz tells her to stay
in his field, she bows to the ground and asks, *Why have I found favor in your sight, that you should take
knowledge of me, since I am a foreigner?* (2:10), putting *lǝhakkîrēnî*, *recognize me*, beside *noḵriyyâ*,
*foreigner* (WLC). Boaz has heard what she did for Naomi and blesses her as one who has come to take refuge
under the wings of the God of Israel (2:11–12). She eats until satisfied, saves what is left for Naomi, gleans
an ephah of barley, and stays through the barley and wheat harvests, living with her mother-in-law (2:14–23).
See [[Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz]] and [[Under His Wings - Ruth 2.12, 3.9, and the Refuge of the Psalms]]. Source: stored WEB
Ruth 2:1–23; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### *Where you go, I will go* — Ruth 1:6–18

On the road to Judah, after Naomi has urged both widows three times to go back, Orpah goes, and *Ruth
joined with her* (14), *dāḇəqâ*, the verb of Genesis 2:24 and of Deuteronomy's *cling to him*. Her first
words of her own follow: *Where you go, I will go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my
people, and your God my God. Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried* (16–17). She seals it
with a self-curse oath in Yahweh's name, *May Yahweh do so to me, and more also, if anything but death parts
you and me* (17), the only such oath in the books the wiki has read spoken by a woman. Naomi sees she is
*determined*, *mitʾammeṣeṯ* (18), from the verb of *Be strong and courageous*. The text describes a vow of
belonging to a people and its God; it uses no word for conversion. See [[Ruth Clings to Naomi]],
[[Go Back or Cling - Joshua 23.12 and Ruth 1.14-16]], and
[[God Do So to Me - The Self-Curse Oath in Ruth and 1 Samuel]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:6–18; Genesis
2:24; Deuteronomy 10:20; 31:6–7; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

A Moabite woman who marries into a family of Bethlehem Judah sojourning in Moab (Ruth 1:4), and the
figure the book of [[Ruth]] is named for. The page carries `(person)` because the book holds the bare
name.

She enters the book as a name in a list: *They took for themselves wives of the women of Moab. The name of
the one was Orpah, and the name of the other was Ruth* (1:4). Her husband is not identified until 4:10,
*Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon*. After about ten years her husband dies, and no child is reported
(1:4–5). She does nothing and says nothing in 1:1–5; her first words come at 1:10 and 1:16–17.

The narrator calls her *the Moabitess* repeatedly (1:22; 2:2, 21; 4:5, 10), and she calls herself *a
foreigner* (2:10). Deuteronomy 23:3 bars *a Moabite* from Yahweh's assembly; Ruth 4:17–22 ends her story in
the line of David. See [[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].

**The name.** The text does not explain it; proposed etymologies are uncertain and are not given here.

**Arrival.** She says nothing in 1:19–22. Naomi tells the town she came home *empty* (1:21) with Ruth
beside her; the narrator's summary names her at once, *Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter-in-law, with her,
who returned out of the country of Moab* (1:22). *Haššāḇâ*, *who returned* (WLC), is the chapter's word for
going home, used of a woman arriving for the first time. See [[Naomi Returns to Bethlehem]].

See [[Famine and Death in Moab]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:4–5, 22; 2:2, 10, 21; 4:5, 10, 17–22;
Deuteronomy 23:3.

## Timeline / key events

- Married to Mahlon (4:10) in Moab — Ruth 1:4
- Widowed after about ten years, without a child reported — Ruth 1:4–5
- Sets out with Naomi for Judah; refuses to go back and vows to stay with her — Ruth 1:6–18
- Arrives in Bethlehem with Naomi, *Ruth the Moabitess … who returned*, at the start of barley harvest — Ruth 1:22
- Gleans in the field of Boaz through the barley and wheat harvests — Ruth 2:1–23
- Goes to Boaz at the threshing floor and asks him to spread his garment over her as *gōʾēl* — Ruth 3:1–18
