# The Watchmen (Song of Solomon)

## Overview

A collective group in [[Song of Solomon]]: *"The watchmen who go about the city"*. The woman meets
them during both of her night searches, and they find her both times. Like
[[The Daughters of Jerusalem]], the group has a page because it recurs and acts. It is not a
historical body that can be identified, and the Song never names any of its members or the city it
watches. The page was put off at 3:3, where the watchmen only found her, and created at 5:7, where
they act again.

In [[Seeking Him by Night]] she goes about an unnamed city at night looking for the one she loves.
*"The watchmen who go about the city found me"* (3:3). She asks them, *"Have you seen him whom my
soul loves?"* No answer is recorded. She passes them, and finds him almost at once (3:4). Here they
are only part of the night city, and no threat.

In [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]] the same line returns word for word in the Hebrew, *mǝṣāʾunî
haššōmǝrîm hassōḇǝḇîm bāʿîr* (3:3; 5:7; Sefaria, *Miqra according to the Masorah*). Then they act:
*"They beat me. They bruised me. The keepers of the walls took my cloak away from me"* (5:7). The
Hebrew verbs are *hikkûnî pǝṣāʿûnî*, *they struck me, they wounded me*; *p-ṣ-ʿ* is a word for real
injury, the *wound* of Exodus 21:25 (Sefaria). She does not speak to them this time.

**One group or two.** *The keepers of the walls*, *šōmǝrê haḥōmôt* (5:7), is a second phrase with the
same verb, *š-m-r*, *to keep, to guard*. It may name the same men in poetic parallel, or a second
group posted on the walls. The Song does not say. This page keeps both phrases together, and records
the question rather than settling it.

**Why they beat her.** The text gives no reason. Readers have explained it as the city's treatment of
a woman found alone in the streets at night, as part of a dream, or as an image of the cost of seeking
love. Some allegorical readings make the watchmen religious leaders or teachers. The wiki records
these readings and adopts none.

Their name is the ordinary Hebrew *šōmǝrîm*, *keepers*: the same verb is used of keeping a garden, a
flock, or a vineyard. The woman uses a different verb, *n-ṭ-r*, for the vineyards she was made to keep
at 1:6, *nōṭērâ* (Sefaria). The wiki notes the difference and does not build on it.

Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 3:1–5; 5:2–8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the
Masorah*.

## Timeline / key events

- Song of Solomon 3:3 — find the woman searching the city at night; she asks them whether they have
  seen him, and no reply is recorded. [[Seeking Him by Night]]
- Song of Solomon 5:7 — find her again, beat and wound her; *"the keepers of the walls"* take her
  cloak. [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]]

## Appears in

- [[Seeking Him by Night]] — Song of Solomon 3:3; they find her, and she questions them.
- [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]] — Song of Solomon 5:7; they find her, beat her, and take her cloak.
