# Abihu

## Overview

**[[Aaron]]'s second son, never named without [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]].**

> **23** … and she bore him Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. (Exodus 6:23)

His name is usually read *he is my father*. **Every time he appears it is in a pair**: at his birth
(6:23), on the mountain with the elders (24:1, 9), and in his death for *"strange fire"* (Leviticus
10:1–2; Numbers 26:61, checked in the WLC). Numbers' summary of Aaron's sons is a single clause about
the two of them. See [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]].

### The lamp service prescribed — Exodus 27:1–21

Exodus 27:21 assigns the lamp outside the veil to Aaron and his sons collectively. Abihu
is included by the family list of 6:23 and the explicit appointment list of 28:1; he is not
individually named in 27:21. This is a prescribed duty, not a report that his service has
begun. See [[The Altar and the Court]].

### The priestly garments prescribed — Exodus 28:1–43

Exodus 28:1 names Abihu individually among Aaron's sons brought near *"that he may minister to
me in the priest's office"* — the first time Exodus appoints the four by name. Coats, sashes,
headbands, and linen breeches are prescribed for the sons (28:40–43), and Moses is to anoint
and consecrate them (28:41). The ordination is commanded in chapter 29; no service is yet
narrated. See [[The Priestly Garments]].

### The ordination prescribed — Exodus 29:1–46

Exodus 29 includes Abihu among *"his sons"* — not by name — who are washed, clothed in
coats, belts, and headbands, and sprinkled with the altar's blood and the anointing oil with their
garments (29:4, 8–9, 21). The sons share in laying hands on the offerings, in the blood on the right
ear, thumb, and big toe, and in the meal of the ram of consecration (29:10, 20, 32). The breast and
thigh of later peace offerings are assigned to Aaron and his sons *"forever"* (29:28). Only Aaron's
head receives the poured oil (29:7). See [[The Ordination of the Priests]].

Exodus 30 includes Abihu among Aaron's *"sons"* — not by name — who wash hands and feet at the
basin before entering the tent or approaching the altar, *"that they not die"* (30:19–21), and who
are anointed with the holy oil (30:30). See
[[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]]. The same chapter forbids *"strange incense"*
(30:9); Leviticus 10:1 (verified WEB) now narrates Nadab and Abihu offering *"strange fire"*. See
[[Strange Incense - Exodus 30.9, Leviticus 10, Numbers 16, and Uzziah]].

### Garments included in the makers' commission — Exodus 31

Exodus 31:10 includes Aaron's sons collectively among those for whom garments must be made. The
individual identification comes from 28:1; this verse does not name each son or narrate his service.
Their clothing is part of the workers' commission in 31:6–11.

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

Exodus 35:19 includes Aaron's sons collectively in the public commission to make priestly
garments. The individual identification comes from 28:1, not from a personal name in this
verse. The people's offerings support the holy garments (35:21), but no action by this son,
completed vesting, or ordination is reported here.

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

The coats and associated linen clothing are made for Aaron and his sons (39:27–29).
Identification with the four sons named in 28:1 is contextual: this unit supplies no individual
names, acts, or distribution of particular items among them. It advances from prescribed clothing
to manufactured clothing without yet narrating their dressing or ordination.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The garments of Aaron’s sons are included among the completed articles brought to Moses
(39:41). The four sons are identified by the earlier appointment list in 28:1, not named again
here. This is a report of their prepared clothing, not of their dressing, ordination, or service.
Moses’ inspection and blessing apply to the completed work and its makers (39:43).

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

Aaron’s sons are to be washed, clothed, and anointed for priestly service (40:12–15),
and they wash hands and feet with Moses and Aaron at the basin (40:31–32). Abihu
is included through the explicit family list in Exodus 28:1, not named individually in
chapter 40. The general obedience statement in 40:16 should be retained, while the
detailed erection account should not be expanded into an individually narrated ordination
for each son. No separate act or distinction among the four brothers is supplied here.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

### The meal offering prescribed — Leviticus 2

Aaron’s sons present the offering and receive its most holy remainder (2:2–3, 10). Inclusion here is collective, from the family appointment in Exodus 28:1; the chapter neither names him individually nor narrates his performance of the rite. See [[The Meal Offering]].

### The burnt offering — Leviticus 1:1–17

Included collectively among Aaron’s sons, identified by Exodus 28:1, in the prescribed blood and
altar duties (Leviticus 1:5, 7–8, 11). No son is individually named or assigned a separate deed
here; this is ritual instruction, not a report that he has performed it.

See [[The Burnt Offering]] . Source: stored WEB Leviticus 1; comparisons identified on the passage
page.

### The peace offering — Leviticus 3

Included collectively as one of Aaron's sons identified in Exodus 28:1, in the prescribed blood handling and altar service of Leviticus 3:2–5, 8–11, 13–16. No individual son is named or reported performing this rite; the instructions define the family's office.

See [[The Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 3; contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Altar fire and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:8–23

Included collectively among Aaron’s sons, identified in Exodus 28:1, in the commands for altar service and holy eating. This unit neither names him individually nor reports his performance; the successor in verse 22 is not identified by personal name.

See [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:8–23; comparisons documented there.

### Holy flesh and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:24–7:10

Included collectively among Aaron’s sons, identified in Exodus 28:1, as recipients of the
holy-food and portion rules (6:25; 7:10). He is not individually named or described performing
these rites.

See [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:24–7:10.

### Aaron and his sons consecrated — Leviticus 8:1–36

Included among Aaron’s sons, identified by Exodus 28:1 rather than named individually here.
Moses washes and clothes the sons, marks their right ears, thumbs, and toes with blood, and
sprinkles them and their garments with oil and blood (6, 13, 24, 30). They share the commanded
meal and seven-day charge; their obedience is affirmed (31–36). Installation is now narrated,
not merely prescribed.

See [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 8; Exodus 28:1; 29; Leviticus 7:38.

### The eighth-day service — Leviticus 9

Included collectively among Aaron's sons, who deliver blood and portions in the inaugural service
(9:9, 12–13, 18). He is not individually named or assigned a separate action here. See
[[The Eighth Day and the Appearing Glory]] .

### Strange fire and the surviving priests — Leviticus 10:1–11

Offers fire with incense in his own censer alongside Nadab, without Yahweh’s command, and dies by fire from before Yahweh (1–2). The bodies are carried out in their coats (4–5); the text does not identify drunkenness as the cause.

See [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:1–11.

### The aftermath — Leviticus 10:12–20

Not named, but his death lies behind the phrase "his sons who were left" (12, 16) and Aaron's "such things as these have happened to me" (19).

See [[The Uneaten Sin Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:12–20.

### The deaths that date the Day of Atonement — Leviticus 16:1–34

Leviticus 16:1 places the law *"after the death of the two sons of Aaron, when they came near
before Yahweh, and died"*. He is not named there. The chapter answers the deaths with a rule of
entry rather than a further account of the offense; see
[[Nadab, Abihu, and the Guarded Entry - Leviticus 10 and 16]].

See [[The Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 16; comparisons as cited.

### Dead without children — Numbers 3:1–13

Named second (2), and again paired with Nadab in death (4). The verse adds that they *had no children*, which is why neither brother's name carries on in the priesthood. See also [[Nadab, Abihu, and the Guarded Entry - Leviticus 10 and 16]].

See [[The Generations of Aaron and the Levites Given]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 3.
