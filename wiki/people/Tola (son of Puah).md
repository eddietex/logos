# Tola (son of Puah)

## Overview

### The judge from Issachar — Judges 10:1–2

*After Abimelech, Tola the son of Puah, the son of Dodo, a man of Issachar, arose to save Israel. He
lived in Shamir in the hill country of Ephraim* (10:1). He *judged Israel twenty-three years, and
died, and was buried in Shamir* (10:2). That is all the text says of him.

He *arose*. No one is said to raise him up, as Yahweh raised up Othniel and Ehud (3:9, 15). He
arose *to save Israel*, but no enemy is named, and the verse follows straight on from Abimelech's
civil war. He is one of the few in the book of whom the narrator says plainly *he judged Israel*.
Othniel, Deborah, and Jephthah are the others before Ibzan (3:10; 4:4; 12:7).

His name and his father's repeat the names of Issachar's sons, *Tola* and *Puvah* (Genesis 46:13),
whose clans the census counts (Numbers 26:23). The WLC uses the same lemmas (8439, 6312) in all three
places, though it spells the father's name *pûʾâ* here and *puwwâ* in Genesis and Numbers. Why the
judge carries his tribe's founding names, the text does not say.

He is qualified by his father's name because *Tola* is also the name of Issachar's firstborn son,
who has no page yet. Tola is not the Puah of Exodus 1:15, the midwife, whose name is spelled with
a different letter (*pûʿâ*, lemma 6326).
