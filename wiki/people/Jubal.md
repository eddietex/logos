# Jubal

## Overview

The younger son of Lamech and Adah, and the Bible's origin of music: *"His brother's name was
Jubal, who was the father of all who handle the harp and pipe"* (4:21).

Two instruments are named, and the pair is chosen to cover a category rather than to list a
repertoire. *Kinnôr* is the lyre or harp — the instrument David plays to soothe Saul (1 Samuel
16:23), the one the exiles hang on the willows of Babylon (Psalm 137:2), the standing instrument of
temple worship. *ʿÛgāb* is a pipe or flute, and occurs only four times in the Hebrew Bible. Strings
and winds: between them, music as such.

As with his brothers, *father of* means originator of a practice, not ancestor of a bloodline. And
as with his brothers, Genesis offers no evaluation whatsoever. Music will run the temple, carry
half the Psalms, and be the medium of most of the Bible's theology of praise, and its first
appearance in Scripture is as a craft founded in the line of Cain, two verses before that line's
head sings a song boasting of murder. That the Bible's first recorded song and its account of the
origin of song sit in the same paragraph is unlikely to be an accident, and the passage still
declines to draw the moral. See [[City and Civilization]].

His name and Jabal's are near-homophones, and both play on *yābal* / *yôbēl* — the latter being the
ram's-horn trumpet that gives the jubilee its name, which makes the sound-play especially apt for
the musician. Genesis prefers this kind of resonance to actual etymology throughout chapters 1–11.
