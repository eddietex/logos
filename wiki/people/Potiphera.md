# Potiphera

## Overview

**Joseph's father-in-law, a priest of the Egyptian sun cult, and a man Genesis mentions three times
without a hint of discomfort.**

> **45** Pharaoh called Joseph’s name Zaphenath-Paneah; and he gave him Asenath, the daughter of
> Potiphera priest of On as a wife. Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.

**He never speaks, never acts, and is never described except by his office.** All three occurrences —
41:45, 41:50, and 46:20 — **use the identical formula**, *Potiphera priest of On*, **and all three are
there to identify his daughter.** He exists in the text as [[Asenath]]'s provenance.

### The name, and the collision with Potiphar

***Pôṭî Pheraʿ*** is Egyptian: ***pȝ-dỉ-pȝ-rʿ***, ***he whom Ra has given*** — **a perfectly ordinary
theophoric name of a type attested from the Twenty-first Dynasty onward.** ***Pôṭîpar***, the captain of
the guard who buys Joseph at 37:36, **is the same name in a contracted form.**

**Genesis spells the two differently and never remarks on the resemblance**, and **there is no indication
in the text that they are the same man**:

| | [[Potiphar]] | Potiphera |
|---|---|---|
| **office** | *sārîs* and *śar haṭṭabbāḥîm*, captain of the guard | *kōhēn ʾŌn*, priest of On |
| **where** | the court, with a house and a prison in it | [[On]], a cult centre |
| **spelling** | *Pôṭîpar* (37:36; 39:1) | *Pôṭî Pheraʿ* (41:45, 50; 46:20) |
| **relation to Joseph** | owner, then jailer | father-in-law |

**Later Jewish tradition identified them**, which makes [[Asenath]] the daughter of
[[Potiphar's Wife]] and converts Joseph's reward into a marriage into the house that framed him. **It is
a satisfying shape and the text gives it no support at all.** **The wiki records the name coincidence and
does not adopt the identification.**

**What the coincidence does show** is that **both Egyptian households Joseph enters are named for Ra**,
and that **Genesis knows what Egyptian names look like well enough to give two of them in two forms.**

### Priest of On

***Kōhēn ʾŌn*** — and *kōhēn* is **the ordinary Hebrew word for priest, the one used of Aaron and of
[[Melchizedek]] at 14:18.** **Genesis does not reach for a pejorative.** It is the same noun it uses of
the priesthood it approves of, applied without qualification to a functionary of the sun cult at
**Heliopolis** — see [[On]].

**By the standards of the rest of the Torah this is remarkable.** Deuteronomy 7:3–4 forbids exactly this
marriage and gives the reason; Exodus 34:15–16 forbids it; **and Genesis records it as part of a
promotion.**

**The one later echo is at 47:22, 26**, where **the priests of Egypt are the single class exempted from
Joseph's land reform** — *"only the land of the priests he didn't buy"* — **because they had an
allowance from Pharaoh.** **Joseph's administration leaves his father-in-law's caste in possession of its
land**, and Genesis reports that without comment either. See [[Priesthood]] and
[[Wealth and Possessions]].

## Appears in

- [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]] — Genesis 41:45, 50; **a name, an office, and a city, given
  twice in identical words.** He is the **first named foreign priest in Scripture after [[Melchizedek]]**
  and the only one Genesis makes a patriarch's relative. **The text neither defends the connection nor
  apologises for it**, and the son-in-law who marries into his house is the one figure in Genesis the
  book never once criticises
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 46:20; **named a third time, and a third time by his office** —
  *"priest of On."* **He is the only priest named in the pericope, and he is Egyptian**

## Related people

- [[Potiphar]] — **the same Egyptian name in a shorter form**, a different office and a different city;
