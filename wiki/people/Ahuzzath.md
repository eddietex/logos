# Ahuzzath

## Overview

**Named once in the Bible, in a list of three men walking from Gerar to Beersheba, and silent.**

> **26** Then Abimelech went to him from Gerar, and Ahuzzath his friend, and Phicol the captain of
> his army.

***"His friend"* is an office, not a remark.** *Mērēʿēhû*, from *rēaʿ*, is the ancient Near Eastern
court title ***the king's friend*** — a standing position at court, roughly a chief adviser or
confidant, and one the Hebrew Bible names elsewhere: Hushai the Archite is *"the king's friend"* to
David (2 Samuel 15:37; 16:16), and Zabud son of Nathan holds the same title under Solomon
(1 Kings 4:5). **The ancient versions render the word variously**, and one old line of reading takes
it as *groomsman* or *companion*; the court-office sense is the one the Hebrew parallels support.

**What his presence does is complete the embassy.** A king, a counsellor, and a general go from
[[Gerar]] to [[Beersheba]] to ask a man they expelled for an oath. **Genesis 21's delegation had two
members; this one has three** — the single difference between the two treaty scenes in the elements
they share — and the addition raises the rank of the visit rather than changing its business. See
[[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]].

**On the name.** *ʾĂḥuzzat* stands on the root *ʾāḥaz*, **to grasp** or **take hold**, whose noun
*ʾăḥuzzâ* means **a holding** or **a possession** — the word Abraham uses of the burial plot he wants
to buy at Hebron, *ʾăḥuzzat-qeber*, *a possession for a burying place* (23:4, 9, 20). **A man whose
name means *possession* walks into a dispute about who owns the water**, which Genesis does not
mention and probably does not intend. Nothing else is recoverable about him.
