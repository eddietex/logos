| [[Moses]] | Moses exhorts Israel to remember its forty years and addresses the imagined claim of self-made wealth (2, 17). His witness against apostasy closes the unit (19–20); no new journey or action by Moses is narrated. Deuteronomy 9:1–6: Moses answers the fear of superior enemies with Yahweh’s presence, then forbids the victorious people to credit their righteousness (1–6). This continues his address, not a narrated crossing. Deuteronomy 9:7–29: Moses recalls receiving and breaking the tablets, destroying the calf, and fasting in intercession (9–21). He prays for Aaron as well as the people, appealing to redemption and the patriarchs rather than Israel’s merit (20, 26–29).  Deuteronomy 10:1–11: Moses makes the wooden ark, cuts replacement tablets, ascends and descends, and deposits the writing (1–5). His forty-day stay and heard intercession lead to a renewed command to lead the people (10–11). Exodus 37:1 credits the ark’s making to Bezalel; Deuteronomy credits Moses. Deuteronomy 11:1–17: addresses the eyewitnesses, contrasts Egypt's foot-watered fields with the land of rain, and warns against a deceived heart.
