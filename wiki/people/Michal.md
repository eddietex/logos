# Michal

## Overview

### The younger daughter — 1 Samuel 14:49

*The name of the firstborn Merab, and the name of the younger Michal* (14:49). *Haqqəṭannâ*, *the younger* (WLC),
is the word Laban used of Rachel (Genesis 29:26, stored WEB). She does nothing in the summary; she is named here so
that she is known when she acts. See [[Saul's Wars and Household]].

### She loved David — 1 Samuel 18:20–28

*Michal, Saul's daughter, loved David* (18:20), and the narrator says it again as the last thing Saul *saw and knew*
(18:28). In the Hebrew files checked, *ʾāhaḇ* with a woman as subject occurs only here, at Genesis 25:28 (Rebekah and
Jacob), and at Ruth 4:15 (Ruth and Naomi) (WLC): she is the one woman in those files whose love for a husband is
stated. The text never says David loved her. Saul is told, *the thing pleased him*, and he makes her love the bait,
*that she may be a snare to him* (18:21; *môqēš*, the word for the nations' gods in Exodus 23:33 and Judges 2:3). The
bride-price is a hundred Philistine foreskins, and Saul means David to die collecting them (18:25). David brings two
hundred, and *Saul gave him Michal his daughter as wife* (18:27), in nearly the words of Laban giving Rachel (Genesis
29:28, WLC). She is the younger daughter given, as Rachel was, while the elder, [[Merab]], goes to [[Adriel]]. See
[[David Marries Michal]], [[A Bride-Price of Foreskins - Shechem, Saul, and David]], and
[[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]].

**The two readings of 2 Samuel 21:8.** The WLC there names *the five sons of Michal the daughter of Saul, whom she bore
to Adriel*, and 2 Samuel 6:23 says she had no child to the day of her death (both WLC; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB
stored). Many translations read *Merab* at 21:8 (recalled, not checked). Both are recorded; neither is chosen.

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 14:49; 18:17–30; Genesis 25:28; 29:26, 28; Ruth 4:15. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-28; 2 Samuel 6:23; 21:8 in the WLC only.

### The window and the bed — 1 Samuel 19:11–17

She is *Michal, David's wife*, *mîḵal ʾištô* (19:11, WLC), and for one night she is the only person between David and
her father. Saul's messengers are at the house *to watch him, and to kill him in the morning*; she tells him, *If you
don't save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be killed* (19:11). *Tomorrow you will be killed*, *māḥār ʾattâ mûmāṯ*,
uses the *hophal* of Saul's oath five verses earlier, *he shall not be put to death* (19:6, WLC). She *let David down
through the window* (19:12), the sentence of Rahab at Jericho (Joshua 2:15, WLC). Then she lays *the teraphim* in the
bed, with *a pillow of goats' hair at its head*, covers it with clothes (19:13), and tells the messengers *He is sick*
(19:14). When her father asks *Why have you deceived me thus, and let my enemy go …?*, she lies a second time and puts
the lie in David's mouth: *He said to me, 'Let me go! Why should I kill you?'* (19:17).

David says nothing to her in the pericope, and the text gives no word of thanks or farewell. The narrator does not
judge her act or her lies. The nearest precedent is [[Rachel]], who used her father's teraphim against him for a husband
in flight and also stopped the search with a lie about a body (Genesis 31:19, 34–35). Whose teraphim Michal used, the
text does not say. The other is [[Rahab]],
who let the spies down through her window and misdirected the king's men (Joshua 2:4–5, 15). Rahab is rescued by the
scarlet line in her window. Michal's next scene in 1 Samuel is her father giving her to another man (25:44). See
[[Michal Helps David Escape]], [[Let Down Through the Window - Rahab and Michal]],
[[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]], and
[[Why Have You Deceived Me - Laban, the Gibeonites, Michal, and the Medium]].

**The two windows.** At 2 Samuel 6:16 she *looked out through the window*, *bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn*, saw David dancing before
the ark, *and she despised him in her heart* (WLC; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB stored). The phrase is the one of
19:12. The love stated at 18:20 and 18:28 is not stated again after 19:17.

**Ahead (not yet ingested).** While David is a fugitive, Saul gives her to *Palti the son of Laish, who was of
Gallim* (25:44, stored WEB). David demands her back (2 Samuel 3:13–16), and her last scene is at the window (2 Samuel
6:16–23) (WLC; paraphrased).
