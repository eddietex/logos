# The Philistines

## Overview

### Trembling and confusion at the pass — 1 Samuel 14:1–23

Their garrison at the pass sees Jonathan and his armour-bearer and mocks, *Behold, the Hebrews are coming out of the
holes where they had hidden themselves!*, then calls *Come up to us, and we will show you something!* (14:11–12). The
call is the sign Jonathan has asked for. About twenty fall in the first slaughter (14:13–14). Then *a trembling in the
camp, in the field, and among all the people*, including *the garrison, and the raiders*; *the earth quaked*, and the
trembling is *ḥerdaṯ ʾĕlōhîm*, literally *a trembling of God* (14:15, WLC). The multitude *melted away* (14:16), and
when Saul arrives they are *striking each other with their swords in very great confusion* (14:20). The Hebrews who
had been in their camp desert to Israel (14:21). Jonathan calls them *these uncircumcised* (14:6), Samson's word for
them (Judges 14:3; 15:18). The Song of the Sea had named *the inhabitants of Philistia* first among the peoples who
would tremble (Exodus 15:14). See [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]] and
[[Yahweh Saved Israel That Day - The Sea and the Pass at Michmash]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 14:1–23; Exodus 15:14; Judges 14:3; 15:18. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### No smith in Israel — 1 Samuel 13:16–23

From the camp at [[Michmash]] their *raiders*, *hammašḥîṯ* (WLC), the word of *the destroyer* in Exodus 12:23, go
out in three companies toward Ophrah and the land of Shual, Beth Horon, and the valley of Zeboim (13:17–18). The
narrator then reports their policy in their own words: *Lest the Hebrews make themselves swords or spears*. There is
no smith in Israel; every Israelite goes down to them to sharpen his plowshare, mattock, ax, and sickle, at one
*payim* a tool (13:19–21), and on the day of battle only Saul and Jonathan are armed (13:22). The text calls it a
decision of theirs and names no metal; the common reading that they controlled iron is not in the verse. Their
garrison goes out to the pass of Michmash (13:23). See [[No Smith in Israel]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 13:16–23; Exodus 12:23. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-19.

### The garrison at Geba, and the army at Michmash — 1 Samuel 13:3–7

Jonathan strikes *the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba*, *nəṣîḇ pəlištîm* (13:3, WLC), the word of the
garrison at the hill of God (10:5). The Philistines hear of it, and Israel *was considered an abomination*,
literally *stank*, among them (13:4). They assemble *thirty thousand chariots, and six thousand horsemen, and
people as the sand which is on the seashore* and camp at [[Michmash]], *eastward of Beth Aven* (13:5). Israel
hides in caves and pits or crosses the Jordan (13:6–7). In Saul's mouth the threat is the reason for the
sacrifice: *Now the Philistines will come down on me to Gilgal* (13:12). The Hebrew reads *šəlōšîm ʾelep̄ reḵeḇ*,
*thirty thousand chariots* (WLC); some Greek and Syriac witnesses are commonly reported to read three thousand
(recalled, not checked). See [[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 10:5; 13:3–12. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-18.

### A garrison at the hill of God — 1 Samuel 10:5

The third sign Samuel gives Saul is at *the hill of God, where the garrison of the Philistines is*, *nəṣiḇê
pəlištîm* (10:5, WLC). *Nəṣîḇ* may mean a garrison, a post, or an officer; translations differ. Its other uses as
a Philistine post are at 13:3–4 (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The Spirit comes on Saul under their garrison,
before any battle. This is another sign that the summary at 7:13, *they stopped coming within the border of
Israel*, does not describe the time of Saul's anointing. The tension is recorded, not resolved. See
[[Samuel Anoints Saul]] and [[The Hill of God]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 7:13; 10:5; 13:3–4. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The oppressor Saul is sent against — 1 Samuel 9:16

Yahweh tells Samuel the man from Benjamin *will save my people out of the hand of the Philistines; for I have looked
upon my people, because their cry has come to me* (9:16). The verse assumes Philistine pressure on Israel, after 7:13
said they *were subdued* and *Yahweh's hand was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel*. The tension is
recorded here as it was at 7:13, not resolved; the Philistine garrisons and wars of chapters 10, 13, and 14 (stored
WEB; not yet ingested) bear on it. See [[Saul Comes to the Seer]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 7:13; 9:16. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### Subdued under Samuel — 1 Samuel 7:7–14

Hearing that Israel has gathered at Mizpah, *the lords of the Philistines went up against Israel* (7:7). While
Samuel is offering, *Yahweh thundered with a great thunder on that day on the Philistines, and confused them;
and they were struck down before Israel* (7:10), the verb and phrase of Israel's defeat at 4:2 turned around
(WLC). Israel pursues them to below Beth Kar (7:11). Then *the Philistines were subdued*, *wayyikkānəʿû*, the
verb Judges never used of them, *and they stopped coming within the border of Israel. Yahweh's hand was against
the Philistines all the days of Samuel* (7:13). Towns *from Ekron even to Gath* are restored (7:14). The summary
stands in tension with the Philistine garrisons and wars of 9:16; 10:5; 13:3–5, which the text does not
reconcile; the question is recorded on the passage page. See [[Samuel Judges Israel at Mizpah]] and
[[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 4:2; 7:7–14; 9:16; 10:5; 13:3–5. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The ark sent home — 1 Samuel 6:1–18

After *seven months* the Philistines ask *the priests and the diviners* what to do with the ark (6:1–2). The
experts answer like men who have learned from chapter 5: do not send it *empty*, but with *a trespass
offering*, five golden tumors and five golden mice, *for the number of the lords* (3–4); *give glory to the God
of Israel* in the hope that he will *lighten* his hand (5, WLC *yāqēl*); and do not *harden your hearts, as the
Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts* (6). At 4:8 they had placed the plagues *in the wilderness*; now
they cite the Exodus correctly. They then test it: unyoked milk cows, calves shut up at home. If the cows go
to Beth Shemesh, it was Yahweh; if not, *It was a chance that happened to us* (7–9). The cows go straight, and
*the lords of the Philistines went after them to the border of Beth Shemesh*, watched, and *returned to Ekron
the same day* (12, 16). The offering is listed by city: Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, Ekron (17). See
[[The Ark Returns to Israel]], [[It Was a Chance - Miqreh in 1 Samuel 6, Ruth 2, and Ecclesiastes]], and
[[Never Yoked - The Red Heifer and the Philistine Cows]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 4:8; 6:1–18. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The ark in Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron — 1 Samuel 5:1–12

The victors carry the ark *from Ebenezer to Ashdod* and set it beside [[Dagon]], who falls before it twice
(5:1–4). Then *Yahweh's hand was heavy on the people of Ashdod*, with tumors (6), and the men of Ashdod say
*his hand is severe on us, and on Dagon our god* (7). *All the lords of the Philistines*, *sarnê p̄əlištîm*
(WLC), gather and send the ark on to Gath, which suffers *a very great confusion* (8–9), and then to Ekron,
whose people cry out before it arrives (10). The lords gather a second time and decide to send it home;
*the cry of the city went up to heaven* (11–12). The enemy who had said *that you not be servants to the
Hebrews* (4:9) now pleads to be rid of the Hebrews' God. See [[The Ark Among the Philistines]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 4:9; 5:1–12. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The ark taken at Aphek — 1 Samuel 4:1b–11

The Philistines camp at [[Aphek]] against Israel at [[Ebenezer]] and win the first battle (4:1–2). When Israel
brings the ark into its camp, they hear the shout and are afraid: *God has come into the camp … Who shall
deliver us out of the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods that struck the Egyptians with all kinds
of plagues in the wilderness* (7–8). They speak of Israel's God in the plural (*hāʾĕlōhîm hāʾaddîrîm
hāʾēlleh*, WLC) and misplace the plagues *in the wilderness*. Then they rally one another, *Be strong, and
behave like men … that you not be servants to the Hebrews, as they have been to you* (9), confirming the
oppression of Judges 13:1, and win: thirty thousand Israelites fall, and they take the ark (10–11). See
[[The Ark Is Captured]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 4:1–11; Judges 13:1. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The lords and Delilah — Judges 16:4–22

*The lords of the Philistines*, *sarnê pəlištîm* (WLC), the five rulers of 3:3, come up to Delilah and
offer her *eleven hundred pieces of silver* each to *Entice* Samson and learn how he may be bound *to
afflict him* (16:5). They supply the cords, keep an ambush in her inner room, and come with *the money in
their hand* when she sends for them (8–9, 12, 18). Then *The Philistines laid hold on him, and put out his
eyes; and they brought him down to Gaza, and bound him with fetters of brass* (21). They never take him by
strength; they buy him. See [[Samson and Delilah]] and [[Delilah]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 3:3;
16:4–22; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-16.

### The Gazites at the gate — Judges 16:1–3

*The Gazites* hear that Samson is in their city with a prostitute, surround the place, and lie in wait all
night *in the gate of the city*, quiet, saying *Wait until morning light, then we will kill him* (16:2). He
rises at midnight and carries off the doors, posts, and bar of their gate (3). The Hebrew does not say who
told them (*to the Gazites, saying*, WLC). Gaza, which Judah took in 1:18, is a Philistine city here, and
it is where they will later bring Samson blind (16:21). See [[Samson at Gaza]]. Source: stored WEB Judges
1:18; 16:1–3, 21; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-16.

### The fields, the wife, and Lehi — Judges 15:1–20

Samson burns their standing grain, shocks, and olive groves (15:4–5). They burn his wife and her father in
return (6), and lose many to *a great slaughter* (8). Then they invade Judah, *to do to him as he has done
to us* (10), and the men of Judah, who say *the Philistines are rulers over us* (11), hand Samson to them
bound. At [[Lehi]] they shout as they meet him, and he kills a thousand of them with a jawbone (14–16).
Samson calls them *the uncircumcised* (18). The chapter ends *in the days of the Philistines* (20): his
twenty years are dated by their rule. See [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 15:1–20.

### They ruled over Israel — Judges 14:1–20

*Now at that time the Philistines ruled over Israel* (14:4). The book shows the rule only from inside a
Philistine town: [[Timnah]] has *daughters of the Philistines* (14:1), a feast, thirty young men, and
*the men of the city* (14:11, 18). Samson's parents call them *the uncircumcised* (14:3), the word of
Genesis 34:14. The thirty companions threaten to burn his wife *and your father's house with fire*
(14:15), and ask *Have you called us to impoverish us?*, a verb, *yāraš* (WLC), that Judges 1 uses for
Israel driving out the land's people. Samson strikes thirty men of [[Ashkelon]] for their clothes
(14:19). The narrator says Yahweh *sought an occasion against the Philistines* (14:4). See [[Samson's Riddle]].

Source: stored WEB Genesis 34:14; Judges 1:19–33; 14:1–20; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-16.

### Forty years, and only a beginning — Judges 13:1, 5

*Yahweh delivered them into the hand of the Philistines forty years* (13:1), the longest oppression
in the book. No outcry follows. The deliverer announced against them is promised only to *begin to
save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines* (13:5). In the WLC of Judges, *kānaʿ*, *subdued*, is used
of Moab, Jabin, Midian, and Ammon (3:30; 4:23; 8:28; 11:33) and never of the Philistines; 1 Samuel 7:13
is the first verse to say it of them. See [[The Announcement of Samson's Birth]] and
[[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 13:1–25; 3:30; 4:23; 8:28; 11:33; 1 Samuel 7:13 (WEB at bible-api.com); Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-16.

### Sold into their hand — Judges 10:6–7, 11

Israel serves *the gods of the Philistines* (6), and Yahweh sells Israel *into the hand of the Philistines, and into the hand of the children of Ammon* (7). The story that follows is only about Ammon. The Philistine oppression is told from 13:1: *Yahweh delivered them into the hand of the Philistines forty years*. Yahweh also lists the Philistines among the peoples he saved Israel from (11), which points back to Shamgar (3:31).

See [[Israel's Repentance Under Ammon]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 10:6–18; 3:31; 13:1.

A people of the southern coastal plain, ruled in Joshua and Judges by *five lords*. The Table of
Nations traces them to the Casluhim, *which the Philistines descended from* (Genesis 10:14). In
Genesis they are the people of Gerar: Abraham *lived as a foreigner in the land of the Philistines
many days* (21:34), and Isaac goes *to Abimelech king of the Philistines, to Gerar* (26:1), where
*The Philistines envied him* (26:14). At the Exodus, God does not lead Israel *by the way of the land
of the Philistines, although that was near*, lest they *see war* and turn back (Exodus 13:17); the
Song of the Sea has *the inhabitants of Philistia* tremble (15:14).

Joshua 13:2–3 puts *all the regions of the Philistines* and *the five lords of the Philistines* first
in *the land that still remains*, naming them by city: Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron. Judges
1:18 says Judah *took* Gaza, Ashkelon, and Ekron. Yet Judges 3:3 opens its list of the nations Yahweh
left *to test Israel* with *the five lords of the Philistines*. Both statements stand in the text; the
wiki does not decide how far Judah's taking reached. The tension is recorded on
[[The Nations Left to Test Israel]].

The first Israelite in Judges to fight them is [[Shamgar]]: *After him was Shamgar the son of Anath,
who struck six hundred men of the Philistines with an ox goad. He also saved Israel* (3:31). No
oppression by them is described there, and no Philistine leader is named. See
[[Shamgar and the Ox Goad]].

The Philistines are the enemy of Samson's story (Judges 13–16). It ends at their feast: *The lords of the
Philistines gathered them together to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god, and to rejoice; for they
said, "Our god has delivered Samson our enemy into our hand"* (16:23). The lords and the people, with *about
three thousand men and women* on the roof, die when Samson pulls down the house (27–30). The book's
deliverance formula, *delivered … into our hand*, is in their mouths and given to [[Dagon]]; the narrator
never says Dagon did anything. Their war with Israel does not end there. See [[The Death of Samson]] and
[[Dagon Fallen - Samson's Pillars and the Ark in Ashdod]].

This page covers the people. The region is [[The Land of the Philistines]]; the rulers of Genesis are
[[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]] and [[Abimelech (of Isaac's time)]].

Sources: stored WEB Genesis 10:14; 21:34; 26:1, 14; Exodus 13:17; 15:14; Joshua 13:2–3; Judges 1:18;
3:3, 31; 13:5; 16:5, 23–30.
