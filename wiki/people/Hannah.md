# Hannah

## Overview

### She asked, and she gave — 1 Samuel 1:1–28

Hannah is the loved, barren wife of [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]]. *Peninnah had children, but Hannah
had no children* (1:2), *because Yahweh had shut up her womb* (1:5–6). At Shiloh, year after year, her
rival provokes her *severely, to irritate her*, and she weeps and will not eat (1:6–7). Her husband's
*Am I not better to you than ten sons?* (1:8) gets no reply.

After the meal she goes to the temple and prays *in bitterness of soul … weeping bitterly* (1:10). She
vows: if *Yahweh of Armies* will *look at the affliction of your servant, and remember me, and not forget
your servant*, and give her *a boy*, she will give him to Yahweh *all the days of his life, and no razor
shall come on his head* (1:11). She is the first person in the books stored so far to address God as
*Yahweh of Armies* (WLC *YHWH ṣəḇāʾôṯ*; the narrator uses it first, at 1:3). She prays silently, *only her
lips moved* (1:13), and [[Eli]] takes her for drunk. Her answer is the chapter's best-known line: *I have
not been drinking wine or strong drink, but I poured out my soul before Yahweh* (1:15). She asks not to be
counted *a wicked woman*, *baṯ-bəlîyāʿal* (1:16, WLC) — the term the narrator will use of Eli's own sons
(2:12). Eli blesses her; *the woman went her way, and ate* (1:18).

*Yahweh remembered her* (1:19). She names the boy [[Samuel]], *Because I have asked him of Yahweh* (1:20),
keeps him until he is weaned (1:22–23), and brings him to Shiloh with three bulls, an ephah of flour, and
wine (1:24). She reminds Eli who she is, *I am the woman who stood by you here, praying* (1:26), and gives
the boy: *As long as he lives he is given to Yahweh* (1:28). The verb *šāʾal*, *ask*, carries her story,
and its participle *šāʾûl* has the consonants of Saul's name (1:28; 9:2, WLC). See
[[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]] and [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].

**Within the pattern.** She belongs with Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, and Samson's mother, but differs from
each: no angel announces her son, as with Samson's mother (Judges 13:3); no husband prays, as Isaac does
(Genesis 25:21); and she asks for the child directly, with a vow, and gives him back. See
[[The Barren Matriarchs]].

**Name.** *Ḥannâ* (WLC) is usually connected with *ḥēn*, *favor, grace*; she asks Eli, *Let your servant
find favor*, *ḥēn*, *in your sight* (1:18). The etymology is a conventional gloss, not verified here; the
wordplay on *ḥēn* is a reader's observation.

### She sang — 1 Samuel 2:1–11

Having given the boy, Hannah *prayed, and said* (2:1), and what follows is a song with no request in it.
*My heart exults in Yahweh! My horn is exalted in Yahweh* (2:1). She never names Peninnah, Samuel, or her
own barrenness. Her story appears once, in a general form, *the barren has borne seven. She who has many
children languishes* (2:5), inside a list of reversals: the mighty broken and the stumbling armed, the full
hired out for bread and the hungry fed, the dead and Sheol, the poor raised from the dust to *the throne
of glory* (2:4–8). The song borrows the Rock and *Yahweh kills, and makes alive* from the Song of Moses
(Deuteronomy 32:4, 39), shares a line with Psalm 113:7–8, and ends on *his king* and *his anointed*
(2:10), the first royal use of *māšîaḥ* in the books stored so far, sung before Israel has a king. She
will bear *three sons and two daughters* after Samuel (2:21, stored WEB; not yet ingested), six children,
not the song's seven. See [[The Song of Hannah]],

## Timeline / key events

- Barren, provoked by her rival at Shiloh year by year — 1 Samuel 1:2–8.
- Vows and prays silently at the temple; Eli takes her for drunk, then blesses her — 1:9–18.
- Conceives and bears Samuel — 1:19–20.
- Weans him and gives him to Yahweh at Shiloh — 1:21–28.
- Sings: *My heart exults in Yahweh* — 2:1–10.
