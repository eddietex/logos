# Nahor (son of Serug)

## Overview

Abraham's grandfather, and the shortest-lived person named in the Bible up to this point.

*"Nahor lived twenty-nine years, and became the father of Terah. Nahor lived one hundred nineteen
years after he became the father of Terah"* (Genesis 11:24–25). A hundred and forty-eight years in
all — less than a sixth of Noah's nine hundred fifty, and the low point of the collapse that runs
through the whole register. His son [[Terah]] will live 205, and Abraham 175, so the fall is not
monotonic; it bottoms out here.

**He is not the Nahor who married Milcah.** His grandson, Abram's brother, carries his name — see
[[Nahor (brother of Abram)]] — which is why both pages in this wiki are qualified. Genesis
distinguishes them only by context, and the ambiguity is real: *"the city of Nahor"* (24:10) and
*"the God of Nahor"* (31:53) are usually taken to mean the grandson, but the elder Nahor's name is
also the name of *Naḫur*, a city well attested in the eighteenth-century Mari archives on the Balikh
river, near [[Haran (city)]]. It is one of four names in this register that correspond to places in
that district.

He appears at 1 Chronicles 1:26 and, as *Nachor*, at Luke 3:34, and nowhere else. He does nothing
and says nothing. On the register's arithmetic he is born in 1849 from creation and dies in 1997 —
the second of the ten to die, one year after [[Peleg]], and one of three men in the register — with
[[Arpachshad]] and Peleg — whose own father is still alive when they die.
