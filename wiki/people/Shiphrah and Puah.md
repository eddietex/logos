# Shiphrah and Puah

## Overview

**The two midwives who disobey the king of Egypt because they fear God — and the first people
Exodus names after the sons of Israel.** *"The king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives, of whom
the name of the one was Shiphrah, and the name of the other Puah"* (Exodus 1:15). **The king who
gives the order has no name in the book; the women who refuse it do.** A single page holds both,
because the text never separates them: they are addressed together, act together, answer together,
and are rewarded together. The precedent is [[Job's Children]] and [[The Sons of God]].

**Their act is the chapter's turning point and its only stated reason for anything**: *"But the
midwives feared God, and didn't do what the king of Egypt commanded them, but saved the baby boys
alive"* (1:17). **It is the first refusal in Scripture of a king's command on God's account**,
and its ground is not conscience or compassion — though both are there — but *the fear of God*, the fear that outranks
the fear of the king. Compare Acts 5:29, *"We must obey God rather than men."* See
[[The Fear of God]].

***"Saved the baby boys alive"*** uses the root of Joseph's last great sentence — *"God meant it for
good … to save many people alive"* (Genesis 50:20). **Joseph saved Egypt from a throne; the midwives
save Israel from one.** See [[You Meant Evil Against Me - The Two Intentions at Genesis 50.20]].

**Their answer to the king is a lie, or at best a half-truth**: *"the Hebrew women aren't like the
Egyptian women; for they are vigorous, and give birth before the midwife comes to them"* (1:19).
*Ḥāyôt*, *vigorous*, is from *ḥāyâ*, *to live* — the verb Pharaoh used for the daughters he would let
live — and so a pun that answers a death sentence with a word for life. **The text does not judge
the answer.** It says *"God dealt well with the midwives"* (1:20), and when it gives the reason, the
reason is the fear: *"Because the midwives feared God, he gave them families"* (1:21). Three readings
— the deception was right, the fear was rewarded and the lie forgiven, or it was not a lie at all —
are set out on [[The Oppression in Egypt]] and
[[The Midwives and Rahab - Deceiving the King to Save Life]], without a choice between them.

**Their reward is *bāttîm*, houses** — households and descendants — **the very thing Pharaoh was
trying to prevent**, and the word Exodus 1:1 opened with (*"every man and his household"*).

### Hebrew, or midwives to the Hebrews?

***Hamyalldōt hāʿibriyyōt*** can mean *the Hebrew midwives* (the WEB) or *the midwives of the
Hebrews* — Egyptian women who serve Hebrew mothers. The second is old: the Septuagint's phrasing
allows it, and Josephus is usually cited as taking them for Egyptians. It would make them, like
Job, Gentiles who fear God. **The names lean toward the WEB's reading**: *Shiphrah* is Semitic
(*šāpar*, *to be beautiful* — so *beauty* or *fair one*), and *Puah* is usually explained from a
Semitic word too, though the etymology is uncertain. The wiki follows the WEB and records the
alternative.

**Two midwives cannot have served a people that *"filled the land"***; they are best read as the
heads of their trade — the women the king could summon by name. That is an inference, and the text
does not state it.
