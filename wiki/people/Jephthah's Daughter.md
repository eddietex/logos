# Jephthah's Daughter

## Overview

### The vow kept — Judges 11:34–40

She is never named. When her father comes home from Ammon, *behold, his daughter came out to meet him
with tambourines and with dances. She was his only child. Besides her he had neither son nor daughter*
(11:34). She is the one who comes out of the doors of his house, and so she is what his vow named:
*whatever comes out of the doors of my house to meet me … I will offer it up for a burnt offering*
(11:31).

*With tambourines and with dances*, *bətuppîm ûbimḥōlôt* (WLC), is how the women went out after Miriam
at the sea (Exodus 15:20). She greets a victory as Israel's women do. *Only child*, *yəḥîdâ* (WLC), is
the word God used of Isaac, *your son, your only son* (Genesis 22:2). In the WLC of Genesis–Judges it is
used only of the two of them.

Her father blames her, *you are one of those who trouble me*, and then explains: *I have opened my mouth
to Yahweh, and I can't go back* (11:35). She answers him with the law of vows, and with the reason the
vow is owed: *do to me according to that which has proceeded out of your mouth, because Yahweh has taken
vengeance for you on your enemies* (11:36). Her words are close to Numbers 30:2, *He shall do according
to all that proceeds out of his mouth*. She does not cite it.

She asks one thing for herself: *Leave me alone two months, that I may depart and go down on the
mountains, and bewail my virginity, I and my companions* (11:37). She goes, and comes back. *He did with
her according to his vow which he had vowed. She was a virgin* (11:39). The narrator does not say what
was done. Whether she was offered as a burnt offering, as the vow said, or dedicated to Yahweh
unmarried, is an old dispute. The wiki sets out both readings on [[Jephthah's Vow]] and does not decide
between them.

What the text does say is that she was remembered. *The daughters of Israel went yearly to celebrate the
daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite four days in a year* (11:40). The verb is *tānâ*, *to recount*, the
verb of the Song of Deborah's *there they will rehearse Yahweh's righteous acts* (5:11). In the WLC of
Genesis–Judges it is used only those two times. She has no name, and Israel's women went every year to
tell her story.

She speaks more in her passage than anyone but her father. Every word she says holds him to the vow,
and her only request is for time.
