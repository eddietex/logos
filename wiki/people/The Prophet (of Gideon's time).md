# The Prophet (of Gideon's time)

## Overview

### Sent in answer to the cry — Judges 6:7–10

*When the children of Israel cried to Yahweh because of Midian, Yahweh sent a prophet to the children
of Israel* (6:7–8). He is unnamed, he has no tribe, place, or father, and he appears only here. The
Hebrew calls him *ʾîš nābîʾ*, *a man, a prophet* (6:8, WLC), the masculine form of Deborah's title,
*ʾiššâ nəbîʾâ*, *a woman, a prophetess* (4:4, WLC).

His whole act is one speech, in the messenger form *Yahweh says, the God of Israel*. It recalls the
exodus in the words of the Ten Words' preface (*out of the house of bondage*, Exodus 20:2), recalls
the conquest (*drove them out from before you, and gave you their land*), quotes a command (*You shall
not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you dwell*, the clause of Joshua 24:15), and ends on
the charge *But you have not listened to my voice* (6:10). He announces no sentence and gives no
promise. Yahweh's angel comes to Ophrah in the next verse (6:11).

He is the third voice in Judges to bring the same charge, after the angel at Bochim (2:1–3) and Yahweh
himself (2:20–21); see
[[I Brought You Up from Egypt - The Indictments at Bochim and in Gideon's Day]].

Whether his speech belonged to the earliest text of Judges is a question some scholars raise, because a
Dead Sea Scroll fragment (4QJudg^a) is reported to lack 6:7–10. That is reported, not checked.

**Why he has a page.** He is a speaking figure, and the book gives him a scene of his own. Under the
title rule in `AGENTS.md`, an unnamed figure known only by a title takes the period as its qualifier;
he is keyed to Gideon, whose story his speech introduces.

Joshua 24:15. Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.
