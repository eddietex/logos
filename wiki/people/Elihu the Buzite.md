# Elihu the Buzite

## Overview

The fourth speaker in Job, and the only one the book neither introduces in advance nor mentions
afterwards. He appears at 32:2 — after the three friends have *"ceased to answer Job"* and Job has
signed his oath — speaks for six chapters (32:6–37:24), and is answered by no one. God begins at 38:1 as
though he had not spoken, and the epilogue rebukes the three friends by name (42:7–9) and leaves him out.

He is introduced more fully than anyone else in the book: *"Elihu the son of Barachel, the Buzite, of
the family of Ram"* (32:2). Job has no genealogy, and the friends have a town each; Elihu has a father, a
clan, and a family, and the names are confessional — *ʾĔlîhûʾ*, *He is my God*; *Barakʾēl*, *God
blesses*. *Buzite* ties him to [[Buz]], the brother of Uz among Nahor's sons (Genesis 22:21) — so the one
speaker with a pedigree is, by it, kin to Abraham and to Job's own country (see [[Uz]]). The Ram of his
family is unidentified.

**He is angry, and the narrator says so four times** (32:2–5): at Job, *"because he justified himself
rather than God"*, and at the three friends, *"because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned
Job"*. **He is young**, and says so first — *"I am young, and you are very old"* (32:6) — and his claim to
speak is the book's one argument against the authority of age: *"there is a spirit in man, and the
breath of the Almighty gives them understanding. It is not the great who are wise, nor the aged who
understand justice"* (32:8–9), aimed at Eliphaz's *"gray-headed and the very aged"* (15:10). And **he is
full of words** — *"my breast is as wine which has no vent; like new wineskins it is ready to burst"*
(32:19).

What sets him apart from the friends is that he has listened. He addresses Job by name, which none of
them does (33:1, 31); he quotes Job back to himself, once word for word (33:11 from 13:27); and he
answers Job's real complaint, that God does not speak, rather than an invented crime. His answer is that
God does speak — *"once, yes twice"* (33:14), by dream and by pain — and that suffering is a warning
meant to save, not only a payment for sin. He offers himself as the man-sized party Job asked for, of
clay and without terror (33:6–7), and describes the heavenly mediator Job wanted — *"an angel, an
interpreter, one among a thousand"* — who brings a man back from the pit and for whom God says *"I have
found a ransom"* (33:23–24).

But his account, like the friends', ends in *"I have sinned"* (33:27). He is angry at Job for justifying
himself (32:2), and ends his first speech by offering to justify Job himself, if Job will hear him
(33:32). **The book's verdict on him is silence**, and readers have heard it both ways: as tacit approval
(he is not rebuked), or as dismissal (he is not answered). Many modern scholars take his chapters as a
later addition; the wiki records that view and reads the book as received.

## Timeline / key events

- **Job 32:1–5** — introduced after the friends fall silent; his wrath kindled against Job and against
  the friends.
- **Job 32:6–22** — the preface to the friends: youth, the spirit in man, the wineskins, and no
  flattering titles.
- **Job 33:1–33** — the first speech to Job: man of clay, Job quoted, God speaking by dream and by pain,
  the angel-interpreter and the ransom, and *"I desire to justify you"*.
- **Job 34–37** *(not yet ingested)* — three further speeches, headed at 34:1, 35:1 and 36:1.

## Related people

- [[Job (person)]] — whom he addresses by name and quotes, and who never answers him.
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — whose appeal to age (15:10) he overturns (32:9) and whose night vision (4:13)
  he repeats word for word (33:15).
- [[Bildad the Shuhite]], [[Zophar the Naamathite]] — the other two of *"these three men"*, condemned by
  him for condemning Job without an answer (32:3, 12).
- [[God]] — whose breath gives him life and understanding (32:8; 33:4), and who answers Job without
  mentioning him.
- [[Nahor (brother of Abram)]] — the father of Buz, if *Buzite* is read genealogically (Genesis 22:21).
