# Elihu the Buzite

## Overview

The fourth speaker in Job, and the only one the book neither introduces in advance nor mentions
afterwards. He appears at 32:2 — after the three friends have *"ceased to answer Job"* and Job has
signed his oath — speaks for six chapters (32:6–37:24), and is answered by no one. God begins at 38:1 as
though he had not spoken, and the epilogue rebukes the three friends by name (42:7–9) and leaves him out.

He is introduced more fully than anyone else in the book: *"Elihu the son of Barachel, the Buzite, of
the family of Ram"* (32:2). Job has no genealogy, and the friends have a town each; Elihu has a father, a
clan, and a family, and the names are confessional — *ʾĔlîhûʾ*, *He is my God*; *Barakʾēl*, *God
blesses*. *Buzite* ties him to [[Buz]], the brother of Uz among Nahor's sons (Genesis 22:21) — so the one
speaker with a pedigree is, by it, kin to Abraham and to Job's own country (see [[Uz]]). The Ram of his
family is unidentified.

**He is angry, and the narrator says so four times** (32:2–5): at Job, *"because he justified himself
rather than God"*, and at the three friends, *"because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned
Job"*. **He is young**, and says so first — *"I am young, and you are very old"* (32:6) — and his claim to
speak is the book's one argument against the authority of age: *"there is a spirit in man, and the
breath of the Almighty gives them understanding. It is not the great who are wise, nor the aged who
understand justice"* (32:8–9), aimed at Eliphaz's *"gray-headed and the very aged"* (15:10). And **he is
full of words** — *"my breast is as wine which has no vent; like new wineskins it is ready to burst"*
(32:19).

What sets him apart from the friends is that he has listened. He addresses Job by name, which none of
them does (33:1, 31); he quotes Job back to himself, once word for word (33:11 from 13:27); and he
answers Job's real complaint, that God does not speak, rather than an invented crime. His answer is that
God does speak — *"once, yes twice"* (33:14), by dream and by pain — and that suffering is a warning
meant to save, not only a payment for sin. He offers himself as the man-sized party Job asked for, of
clay and without terror (33:6–7), and describes the heavenly mediator Job wanted — *"an angel, an
interpreter, one among a thousand"* — who brings a man back from the pit and for whom God says *"I have
found a ransom"* (33:23–24).

But his account, like the friends', ends in *"I have sinned"* (33:27). He is angry at Job for justifying
himself (32:2), and ends his first speech by offering to justify Job himself, if Job will hear him
(33:32). In his second and third speeches (34–35) **he stops being new**: he restates Bildad (34:12) and
Eliphaz (34:7; 35:6–8), puts in Job's mouth a sentence Job had given to the wicked and disowned (34:9 and
35:3, from 21:15–16), and concludes that Job *"adds rebellion to his sin"* (34:37). His one new argument
there is a strong one — that a God who could gather back *"his spirit and his breath"* and end all flesh
(34:14–15) cannot be the world's enemy. And his last word on Job, *"he multiplies words without
knowledge"* (35:16), is **the first word God says about Job at 38:2** — which is the best evidence for
reading him as the whirlwind's herald, to be weighed every time against 42:7. See
[[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]].

His fourth speech (36–37) is his best, and it is where the herald reading is strongest. Its first half
puts his one new idea in a single line — *"He delivers the afflicted by their affliction, and opens their
ear in oppression"* (36:15) — and turns it on Job as a charge: *"you have chosen this rather than
affliction"* (36:21). Its second half, from *"Behold, God is great, and we don't know him"* (36:26), is a
hymn to God in the weather that names, one by one, the rain, cloud, lightning, thunder, snow, ice and sky
God will ask Job about in chapter 38, and examines Job in God's own grammar: *"Do you know …? Can you,
with him, spread out the sky?"* (37:15–18). He even says his heart trembles at the storm (37:1). **But his
conclusion is the reverse of God's.** The storm proves, for Elihu, that no one should ask to speak with God
(37:19–20) and that *"We can't reach the Almighty"* (37:23); God speaks out of it in the next verse and asks
Job to answer him. He claims to be *"perfect in knowledge"* (36:4) in the speech where he gives God the same
title (37:16), and his last words — *"He doesn't regard any who are wise of heart"* (37:24) — are the last
any human speaks before God does. See
[[From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1]].

**The book's verdict on him is silence**, and readers have heard it both ways: as tacit approval
(he is not rebuked), or as dismissal (he is not answered). Many modern scholars take his chapters as a
later addition; the wiki records that view and reads the book as received.

## Timeline / key events

- **Job 32:1–5** — introduced after the friends fall silent; his wrath kindled against Job and against
  the friends.
- **Job 32:6–22** — the preface to the friends: youth, the spirit in man, the wineskins, and no
  flattering titles.
- **Job 33:1–33** — the first speech to Job: man of clay, Job quoted, God speaking by dream and by pain,
  the angel-interpreter and the ransom, and *"I desire to justify you"*.
- **Job 34:1–37** — the second speech, to *"wise men"* about Job: Job's oath quoted (34:5), the wicked's
  profit question put in his mouth (34:9), God's justice grounded in his holding every breath
  (34:14–15), no trial needed (34:23), and *"I wish that Job were tried to the end"* (34:36).
- **Job 35:1–16** — the third speech, to Job: righteousness gives God nothing (35:6–8), the oppressed
  unanswered because none asks *"Where is God my Maker?"* (35:10), and *"he multiplies words without
  knowledge"* (35:16).
- **Job 36:1–25** — the fourth speech, to Job: *"One who is perfect in knowledge is with you"* (36:4);
  the bound man whose ear God opens (36:8–12), *"He delivers the afflicted by their affliction"* (36:15),
  and *"you have chosen this rather than affliction"* (36:21).
- **Job 36:26–37:24** — the hymn: *"God is great, and we don't know him"* (36:26); rain, thunder, lightning,
  snow, and ice; *"Listen to this, Job"* (37:14); *"We can't reach the Almighty"* (37:23); and his last
  words, *"He doesn't regard any who are wise of heart"* (37:24). God answers Job, not Elihu, in the next
  verse.
- **Job 38:1** — God answers Job out of the whirlwind in the next verse, and does not mention him; his
  *"without knowledge"* (35:16) opens God's speech (38:2), and his *"once, yes twice"* (33:14) is in Job's
  reply (40:5).

- [[Elihu on God's Justice]] — Job 34:1–35:16
- [[Elihu on God's Greatness]] — Job 36:1–37:24
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:1–40:5, **not named and not answered.** God speaks out of the storm
  Elihu was describing, takes up his subjects and his *"Do you know …?"* (37:15–18; 38:22–38), and reaches the
  conclusion he denied: God can be reached, and speaks to Job. Elihu's *"without knowledge"* is God's first
  charge (38:2); his formula *"once, yes twice"* (33:14) is Job's last word (40:5). See
  [[From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1]] and
  [[Once, Yes Twice - Job 33.14, 40.5, and Psalm 62.11]].
- [[Behemoth and Leviathan]] — Job 40:6–41:34, **not named.** His question at 34:17, *"Will you condemn him who
  is righteous and mighty?"*, is the question God asks Job at 40:8; his *"voice of his majesty"* (37:4) is the
  thunder of 40:9; and his *"what do you give him?"* (35:7) is said by God, without Elihu's conclusion, at
  41:11.
- [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]] — Job 42:1–6, **not named.** The first half of 42:4, *"Listen, now, and I
  will speak"*, is in neither of God's speeches; its nearest wording in the book is Elihu's *"Hold your peace,
  and I will speak"* (33:31) — or Job's own 13:22. And Job's *"I have uttered that which I did not understand"*
  (42:3) concedes Elihu's *"without knowledge"* (34:35; 35:16) in God's words, not his.
- [[The Restoration of Job]] — Job 42:7–9, **not named.** God's verdict names Eliphaz and *"your two friends"*;
  the fourth speaker is neither rebuked nor commended, and offers no sacrifice. His anger of 32:2–5 (*wayyiḥar
  ʾap*) is matched by God's *ḥārâ ʾappî* (42:7) — but God's falls on the friends only. Whether the silence means
  the speeches were added later or that Elihu needed no verdict, the text does not say.

## Related people

- [[Job (person)]] — whom he addresses by name and quotes, and who never answers him.
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — whose appeal to age (15:10) he overturns (32:9) and whose night vision (4:13)
  he repeats word for word (33:15); whose *"drinks iniquity like water"* (15:16) and profit question
  (22:2–3) he restates at 34:7 and 35:6–8.
- [[Bildad the Shuhite]], [[Zophar the Naamathite]] — the other two of *"these three men"*, condemned by
  him for condemning Job without an answer (32:3, 12).
- [[God]] — whose breath gives him life and understanding (32:8; 33:4), and who answers Job without
  mentioning him.
- [[Nahor (brother of Abram)]] — the father of Buz, if *Buzite* is read genealogically (Genesis 22:21).
