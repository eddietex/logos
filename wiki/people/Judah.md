# Judah

## Overview

**Leah's fourth son, and the first of her naming speeches that asks her husband for nothing.**

> **35** She conceived again, and bore a son. She said, “This time will I praise Yahweh.” Therefore she
> named him Judah. Then she stopped bearing. (29:35)

***ʾÔdeh ʾet-YHWH*** — from ***yādâ***, *to praise, to acknowledge* — **and *Yəhûdâ*.** Her first
three sons were arguments addressed to Jacob: *now my husband will love me*, *Yahweh has heard that I
am hated*, *now this time will my husband be joined to me.* **The fourth mentions no husband at all**,
and the wiki notes what the popular reading of that progression leaves out: **at 30:20 she goes back**
— *"now my husband will live with me"* — so the arc is real but not a graduation. See
[[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Leah]].

**This is the name the rest of the Bible runs through.** The tribe of David; the kingdom that outlives
the northern one by a century and a half; the province *Yehud*, from which the word **Jew**; and the
genealogies of Matthew 1:2–3 and Luke 3:33. **All of it comes out of a naming speech by a wife nobody
chose.**

### In this pericope — Genesis 37:1–36

**He proposes the sale, and he argues it on money.**

> **26** Judah said to his brothers, “What profit is it if we kill our brother and conceal his blood?
> **27** Come, and let’s sell him to the Ishmaelites, and not let our hand be on him; for he is our
> brother, our flesh.” His brothers listened to him.

***Mah-beṣaʿ*** — ***beṣaʿ*** is **gain, and characteristically dishonest gain**: the word in the
qualification for judges, *"men of truth, hating unjust gain"* (Exodus 18:21), and in the prophets'
complaints about men *"greedy for gain"*. **His objection to murdering his brother is that murder is
unremunerative.** He then adds the chapter's **only appeal to kinship** — *"he is our brother, our
flesh"*, *ʾāḥînû bəśārēnû*, the idiom Laban used of Jacob at 29:14 — **and deploys it in favour of a
sale.**

***"Conceal his blood."*** ***Wəkissînû ʾet-dāmô*** — **and covering blood is the one thing Genesis has
already said the ground will not do.** Abel's blood *"cries to me from the ground"* (4:10). **Judah
proposes exactly the operation Scripture says is impossible.** See [[Do Not Cover My Blood]] and
[[Blood]].

***"His brothers listened to him."*** **The chapter never says that of [[Reuben]]**, whose intervention
had to be narrated as an intention because the speech did not contain one. **The succession is moving in
this verse and Genesis does not announce it** — the speaker the brothers obey is the fourth son, not the
first. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**And the question he hands his father is the question Tamar will hand him.** *"Examine it, now"* —
***hakker-nāʾ*** (37:32) — **is what the brothers say when they deliver the coat, and *hakker-nāʾ*,
*"discern, please, whose are these"*, is what Tamar says when she returns his pledge one chapter later**
(38:25). **The man who composed the first is answered with the second**, and the placement of Genesis 38
is probably the reason. **Genesis draws no line between them.** See
[[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]] and
[[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 38:1–30

**Genesis stops the Joseph story to give him the only chapter in the book that is his.** Joseph is not
named in it; neither is Jacob. **He goes down from his brothers** — *wayyēred*, the same verb the book
is using in the same week for Joseph's descent to Egypt — settles at [[Adullam]] with a Canaanite
friend, and **marries a Canaanite**, in three verbs and no negotiation: *"he saw … he took … he went in
to her."* See [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] and [[Shua's Daughter]].

**Then he buries two sons and withholds the third.** [[Er]] is *"wicked in Yahweh's sight"* and is
killed; [[Onan]] refuses the levirate duty Judah himself orders and is killed; and Judah sends
[[Tamar]] home *"until Shelah, my son, is grown up"* — **with a reason the narrator supplies and he does
not say aloud**: *"lest he also die, like his brothers."* **He has concluded the woman is killing his
sons**, in a chapter that has just told the reader twice who did.

**He is caught by the arrangement he never closed.** He takes his veiled daughter-in-law for a
prostitute at the gate of [[Enaim]], offers a young goat — **the third goat this family uses to settle
something that is not about goats** — and hands over his ***signet, cord, and staff*** as security.
**She asks for his identity rather than for money**, and he gives it away for a night and writes it off
rather than be laughed at: *"Let her keep it, lest we be shamed."*

**Three months later he sentences her to death on hearsay** — *"Bring her out, and let her be burned"*
— **with no hearing, no witnesses, and no jurisdiction**, she being in her father's house and he a
resident alien in Adullam. **And then she sends him his own seal with two words he composed himself.**

> **26** Judah acknowledged them, and said, “She is more righteous than I, because I didn’t give her to
> Shelah, my son.”

***Ṣādəqâ mimmennî*** — **the first unqualified admission of fault by anyone in Genesis.** Adam blames
the woman; Cain asks a question; Abraham explains the sister arrangement twice; Jacob, caught, makes a
speech about his own hard usage. **Judah is handed evidence, recognises it, and states the verdict
against himself in three Hebrew words** — **and the ground he gives is not the sex but the broken
obligation**: *because I didn't give her to Shelah.* He does not plead the veil, though the veil is
what fooled him. See [[Righteousness]] and [[Judah and Tamar]].

**The twins born of it, [[Perez]] and [[Zerah]], put Perez at the head of the genealogy that closes
Ruth and opens Matthew.** See
[[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 43:1–34

**The chapter is his, and it is where the succession that began at 37:27 completes.**

[[Jacob]] has refused to let [[Benjamin]] go (42:38) and [[Reuben]] has already offered two of his own
sons as forfeit and been answered with nothing (42:37). **Judah waits until the grain runs out, states
the condition twice without embellishment, and then makes the offer that moves his father.**

> **8** Judah said to Israel, his father, "Send the boy with me, and we'll get up and go, so that we
> may live, and not die, both we, and you, and also our little ones. **9** I'll be collateral for him.
> From my hand will you require him. If I don't bring him to you, and set him before you, then let me
> bear the blame forever, **10** for if we hadn't delayed, surely we would have returned a second time
> by now."

***ʾĀnōkî ʾeʿerbennû*** — ***ʿārab***, **to stand surety for another man's debt**, with the pronoun
supplied when the verb did not need it. **He is not promising to try; he is putting himself on the
note.** And *"let me bear the blame forever"* — ***wəḥāṭāʾtî ləkā kol-hayyāmîm***, *I shall have
sinned against you all the days* — **is not a penalty he will pay but a status he will occupy.**
Reuben offered a transaction in lives he did not own; **Judah offers to become, permanently, the man
who did this.** Jacob's answer is *"If it must be so, then do this"* (43:11). See
[[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]].

**The verse has a precedent in his own history, and it is the opposite of this one.** ***ʿĒrābôn*** —
*pledge* — is what he hands [[Tamar]] at [[Enaim]]: his signet, his cord and his staff, **security he
leaves behind for a goat he never delivers** (38:17–20). **The first time Judah gives a pledge it is
his identity, and he writes it off rather than be laughed at. The second time it is himself, and he
keeps it** (44:32–33).

**Verse 8 is also an argument, and it is his father's own.** *"So that we may live, and not die"* is
Jacob's phrase from 42:2, **handed back to him widened from one household to three generations** —
*"both we, and you, and also our little ones."* **Verse 10 is the only sharp thing he says**, and it
is true: the delay was Jacob's, and [[Simeon]] has been in an Egyptian prison for the length of the
argument.

**Nothing else in the chapter is his.** He is one of the eleven who bow at 43:26, one of the men
seated in birth order at 43:33 — fourth — and one of those who drink at 43:34. **His next words are
the longest speech in Genesis.**

### In this pericope — Genesis 44:1–34

**He keeps it, and Genesis gives him seventeen verses to do it in.**

The chapter opens with a silver cup planted in [[Benjamin]]'s sack and closes with Judah offering to
take the sentence for it. **In between he does three things and none of them is a defence.**

**He leads them back.** *"Judah and his brothers came to Joseph's house"* (44:14) — ***yəhûdâ
wəʾeḥāyw***, **the group named after its fourth son.** [[Reuben]] is not mentioned in the chapter at
all. The transfer that began with *"his brothers listened to him"* at 37:27 **is here simply written
into the subject of a sentence.**

**He confesses the wrong crime.** Offered three ways out — the cup is not his, the steward has
declared the rest of them *blameless*, and nobody has charged him with anything —

> **16** Judah said, "What will we tell my lord? What will we speak? Or how will we clear ourselves?
> God has found out the iniquity of your servants. Behold, we are my lord's slaves, both we, and he
> also in whose hand the cup is found."

***Niṣṭaddāq*** — *how shall we clear ourselves* — **is from *ṣādēq*, and the root appears in this
reflexive stem nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible.** **It is also his own word**: *ṣādəqâ mimmennî*,
*"she is more righteous than I"* (38:26). ***ʿĀwōn*** is guilt-as-liability, **the standing an act
leaves behind**, and the act is twenty-two years old. **He does not say the cup was stolen and does
not say it was not.** [[God]]'s single mention in the chapter is in this sentence.

**Then he speaks.** 44:18–34 is **the longest speech in Genesis**, and it is **almost entirely other
men's words** — Joseph's own terms quoted back (44:19–23), [[Jacob]]'s *"my wife bore me two sons"*,
*"surely he is torn in pieces"* and the *Sheol* vow (44:27–29), **and his own 43:9 pledge repeated
almost verbatim** (44:32). He says *the boy* eight times and *my father* fifteen. **He asks for
nothing, denies nothing, and argues from one premise only**: *"his life is bound up in the boy's
life"* (44:30) — **that the sentence has a second defendant, in Canaan, who will not survive it.** See
[[His Life Is Bound Up in the Boy's Life - Nephesh Tied to Nephesh]].

> **33** Now therefore, please let your servant stay instead of the boy, my lord's slave; and let the
> boy go up with his brothers.

***Taḥat hannaʿar*** — ***instead of the boy*** — **the preposition Genesis used at 22:13 for the ram
offered instead of [[Isaac]].** **The man who asked *mah-beṣaʿ*, what profit, and sold one son of
[[Rachel]] into Egypt offers to stay there in place of the other.** He had staked the pledge in a tent
to win an argument with his father; **nobody in Egypt can enforce it, and he performs it anyway.** See
[[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]] and
[[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]].

**And the speech's last clause is not about him.** *"Lest I see the evil that will come on my father"*
— ***yimṣāʾ***, *that will find him* — **the chapter's eighth and final use of the verb that had been
hunting a cup.** The next verse is *"Then Joseph couldn't control himself."*

**Every clause of 44:20 is a knife he cannot see.** *"A child of his old age"* is ***yeled zəqūnîm***,
**the narrator's stated cause of the hatred at 37:3–4**; *"his brother is dead"* is said to the
brother; *"he alone is left of his mother"* is true only if that is so; **and *"his father loves him"*
offers paternal favouritism as grounds for mercy to the man it nearly killed.** See
[[A Child of His Old Age - Ben-Zequnim from Joseph to Benjamin]].

*"The sceptre will not depart from Judah… until he comes to whom it belongs"* (49:10). See

## Appears in

- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 29:35; the fourth son, and **the first naming speech in
  the passage with no husband in it.** His mother stops bearing in the same verse, and the name she
  gives him is the one the kingship, the surviving kingdom, and the genealogies of the Gospels are built
  on
- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:23; **a name in a list**, and the last
  of Leah's six before the roster turns to [[Rachel]]'s two. The tribe that takes the kingship is
  fourth in the only ordering Genesis gives
- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:26–27, 32; **his first words in the Bible, and they are a
  costing.** *"What profit is it if we kill our brother and conceal his blood?"* — and then the chapter's
  one appeal to kinship, used to argue for a sale rather than against one. **"His brothers listened to
  him"**, which the chapter never says of the firstborn. **The *hakker-nāʾ* he helps hand his father at
  37:32 is the phrase Tamar hands him at 38:25**
- [[Judah and Tamar]] — Genesis 38:1–30; **the only chapter in Genesis that is his, and Joseph is not
  in it.** He marries outside the line, orders a levirate duty and then declines the equivalent one,
  hands his seal to a veiled woman at a gate, sentences her to burn without a hearing, and — shown his
  own signet — says the first unqualified admission of fault in the book. **The line of David starts in
  the last four verses**
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42; **not named once, and the silence is loud.**
  He had said *"he is our brother, our flesh"* (37:27) as an argument for selling rather than killing,
  **and *"his brothers listened to him"*** — ***šāmaʿ***, the exact verb whose failure they confess at
  42:21 (*"he begged us, and we wouldn't listen"*). **They could hear a sales pitch and not a boy, and
  the pitch was his.** He takes no part in the confession, the report, or [[Reuben]]'s failed surety;
  **he speaks first thing in the next chapter, and what he offers is himself** (43:9). Genesis puts his
  silence exactly where Reuben's self-justification is
- [[The Second Journey]] — Genesis 43:3–10; **the chapter where he takes the cycle over.** He states
  Joseph's condition twice and does not argue it (*"You shall not see my face, unless your brother is
  with you"*), answers his father's grievance, and then offers ***ʿārab*** — **himself, not his sons,
  and *forever*, not until the boy is back.** Jacob, who did not reply to [[Reuben]] at all, replies
  *"If it must be so, then do this."* **It is the same root as the *ʿērābôn* he left with [[Tamar]] at
  38:17–20**, and the first time he offers collateral he cannot walk away from
- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:14–34; **the chapter where the pledge is performed
  and the arc that began at 37:26 closes.** He leads the brothers back (*"Judah and his brothers"*,
  44:14), confesses a crime nobody charged — ***"God has found out the iniquity of your servants"***
  (44:16), using a reflexive of *ṣādēq* found nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible and last heard from him
  as *"she is more righteous than I"* (38:26) — and then delivers **the longest speech in Genesis**,
  built almost entirely out of [[Joseph]]'s words and [[Jacob]]'s. **He asks for no mercy and offers no
  defence**; his single argument is that *"his life is bound up in the boy's life"* (44:30), and his
  single request is to serve the sentence himself — ***taḥat hannaʿar***, *instead of the boy* (44:33).
  **The last thing he says is about his father's face**, and the next verse is 45:1
- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — Genesis 45; **not named once, and the chapter is the answer to him.**
  *"Then Joseph couldn't control himself"* (45:1) follows directly on *"lest I see the evil that will
  come on my father"* (44:34), **and the verb of his approach — *wayyiggaš*, 44:18 — comes back as the
  invitation**: ***gəšû-nāʾ ʾēlay***, *"Come near to me, please"* (45:4). **Everything he asked for at
  44:33 happens**: the boy goes up with his brothers (45:24), the father is fetched, and the sentence
  is never carried out. **Genesis gives him no line, no reaction and no acknowledgement**, and the
  surety of 43:9 is discharged without the word being used. See
  [[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]]

## Related people

- [[Leah]] — his mother, whose fourth naming speech he is
- [[Levi]] — his elder brother; between them, the priesthood and the kingship, both out of the unloved
  wife
- [[Reuben]] — the firstborn whose role he assumes among the brothers
- [[Joseph]] — whose sale he proposes and whose brother he later offers to replace as a slave
- [[Simeon]] — the second son, cursed with Levi where Judah is blessed
- [[Jacob]] — his father, who gives him the sceptre from the deathbed
- [[Tamar]] — his daughter-in-law twice over, who is owed a son by his house and takes one from him;
  **the only person in Scripture he concedes anything to**
- [[Shua's Daughter]] — his Canaanite wife, and the rule of 24:3 broken in one sentence
- [[Er]], [[Onan]], [[Shelah (son of Judah)]] — his three sons by her; two killed and one withheld
- [[Perez]] and [[Zerah]] — his sons by Tamar; **Perez heads the line that runs to David**
- [[Hirah the Adullamite]] — his *rēaʿ*, **the only friend Genesis gives a patriarch**
