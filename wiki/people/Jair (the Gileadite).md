# Jair (the Gileadite)

## Overview

### The judge of Gilead — Judges 10:3–5

*After him Jair, the Gileadite arose, and he judged Israel twenty-two years. He had thirty sons who
rode on thirty donkey colts, and they had thirty cities, which are called Havvoth Jair to this day,
which are in the land of Gilead. Jair died, and was buried in Kamon* (10:3–5).

The first judge from east of the Jordan. *The Gileadite*, *haggilʿādî*, is also Jephthah's title
(11:1; 12:7, WLC). The text gives him no tribe and no father. His thirty sons riding donkeys mark
standing, as the Song of Deborah's *you who ride on white donkeys* (5:10) does. The Hebrew of 10:4
spells the *cities* the same way as the *donkey colts*, *ʿăyārîm* (both tagged lemma 5895 in the
morphhb data). The ordinary word for *cities* is *ʿārîm*. Whether that is a pun or a variant spelling
is reported, not checked.

**Two Jairs and one name.** Numbers 32:41 and Deuteronomy 3:14 credit the name *Havvoth Jair* to
*Jair the son of Manasseh*, in Moses' time. Judges 10:4 attaches it to this Jair's sons, in *the land
of Gilead*, *to this day*. The wiki keeps the two men on separate pages. Whether the second is a
descendant who renewed the name, or whether one tradition has been told two ways, the text does not
say. See [[Jair (son of Manasseh)]] and
[[The Villages of Jair - Numbers 32.41, Deuteronomy 3.14, Joshua 13.30, and Judges 10.4]].
