# Cushan Rishathaim

## Overview

### The first oppressor — Judges 3:7–11

*King of Mesopotamia* (8, 10, stored WEB), into whose hand Yahweh *sold* Israel for eight years, and
whom Yahweh *delivered … into* Othniel's hand. He is the first enemy of the judges period with a
name. He does nothing in the text: no campaign, no speech, no city. Israel *served* him (8), and then
Othniel's *hand prevailed against* him (10).

**The name.** *Kûšan rišʿātayim* (WLC), given in full four times in three verses. *Rišʿātayim* ends in
*-ayim*, like *nahărayim*, *two rivers*, in his title *melek ʾăram nahărāyim* (8). The noun *rišʿâ*
means *wickedness*, and the name is commonly read as *Cushan of double wickedness*: a name Israel gave
him, not his own. That reading is reported, not checked. *Cushan* recalls *Cush*, and Habakkuk 3:7
pairs *the tents of Cushan* with Midian (WEB, recorded on [[Cush]]; not yet ingested). Whether this
king has anything to do with either is not known; the wiki records the likeness of the names only.

**His kingdom.** Verse 8 says *king of Aram Naharaim*; verse 10 says only *king of Aram* (WLC). The WEB
gives *Mesopotamia* both times. See [[Aram Naharaim]]. How a king from the upper Euphrates came to
rule Israel, and where, is not said. No historical identification is adopted here.

See [[Othniel the First Judge]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 3:7–11. Hebrew from the Westminster
Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-13.
