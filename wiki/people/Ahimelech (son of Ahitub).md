# Ahimelech (son of Ahitub)

## Overview

The priest of Yahweh at [[Nob]], and the central priestly figure of 1 Samuel 21–22. In 21:1–9 he is called *Ahimelech
the priest*; chapter 22 supplies his patronymic, *Ahimelech the priest, the son of Ahitub* (22:9, 11, 20, stored WEB;
not yet ingested). Qualified from the start by his father's name — matching [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]] and
[[Ahitub (son of Phinehas)]] — because `Ahimelech the Hittite` appears later in the same book (1 Samuel 26:6, stored WEB;
not yet ingested) and an `Ahimelech son of Abiathar` is named in 2 Samuel 8:17 and 1 Chronicles 24:3 (recalled, not
checked).

By his patronymic *son of Ahitub*, Ahimelech belongs to the priestly house of [[Eli]] through [[Phinehas (son of Eli)]]
and [[Ahitub (son of Phinehas)]] (14:3; 22:9, 20), and thus stands under the sentence spoken against Eli's house at
Shiloh (2:27–36; 3:11–14; see [[The Word Against the House of Eli - 1 Samuel 2.27-36 and 1 Kings 2.27]]). His exact
relation to [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]] — the priest wearing an ephod with Saul at Gibeah in 14:3 — is not stated in 1
Samuel; readers debate whether they are brothers or one man under two names, and the wiki keeps them as separate pages.

### Holy bread and Goliath's sword — 1 Samuel 21:1–9

When [[David]] arrives at [[Nob]] alone after fleeing Saul's court, *Ahimelech came to meet David trembling, and said to
him, "Why are you alone, and no man with you?"* (21:1) — the same trembling with which the elders of Bethlehem met Samuel
(16:4). David answers with a fabricated secret royal mission from [[Saul]], claiming *the young men* have been sent to a
certain place, and asks for *five loaves of bread … or whatever is available* (21:2–3).

Ahimelech replies that he has *no common bread, but there is holy bread; if only the young men have kept themselves from
women* (21:4). Once David assures him of three days' abstinence and the holiness of the young men's *vessels* (21:5),
Ahimelech gives him *the show bread that was taken from before Yahweh, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken
away* (21:6). Under Leviticus 24:5–9 and 22:10–16, the removed show bread belongs to Aaron and his sons to eat in a holy
place; here Ahimelech exercises priestly judgment to feed a hungry fugitive (who claims to be on the king's urgent
service), requiring ritual cleanness rather than priestly lineage. The narrator records no rebuke of Ahimelech, and Jesus
later cites the episode in Matthew 12:3–4 and Mark 2:25–26.

Asked next for *spear or sword*, Ahimelech tells David that *the sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you killed in the
valley of Elah, is here wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod. If you would like to take that, take it; for there is no
other except that here* (21:8–9). What Ahimelech does not know — or does not suspect — is that David is fleeing Saul,
and that [[Doeg]] the Edomite, one of Saul's servants, is *detained before Yahweh* watching (21:7). In chapter 22 Doeg
reports the bread, the sword, and an inquiry of Yahweh not narrated in chapter 21 (22:9–10); Ahimelech defends David as
Saul's most faithful servant and protests his own ignorance (22:14–15), and Saul orders Doeg to slaughter Ahimelech and
eighty-five priests wearing a linen ephod, leaving only his son Abiathar to escape to David (22:16–20, stored WEB; not
yet ingested).

**Discrepancies recorded, not resolved.**
- **Mark 2:26 (Abiathar).** Mark 2:26 places David's eating of the show bread *at the time of Abiathar the high priest*
  (recorded on [[The Bread of the Presence - David's Hunger and Matthew 12]]), whereas 1 Samuel 21:1–9 names *Ahimelech*
  and 22:20 identifies Abiathar as Ahimelech's son.
- **Inquiring of God (21:1–9 vs. 22:10, 15).** Doeg accuses Ahimelech of having *inquired of Yahweh for him* (22:10),
  and Ahimelech asks *Have I today begun to inquire of God for him?* (22:15), yet 21:1–9 narrates only the gift of bread
  and Goliath's sword.
- **Relation to [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]].** Whether the two *sons of Ahitub* (14:3; 22:9) are brothers or the same
  priest is left open.
