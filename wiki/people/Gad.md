# Gad

## Overview

### The twelve stones — Joshua 4:1–24

Gad’s descendants cross armed with Reuben and half of Manasseh (12–13), putting Joshua 1’s pledge
into action. The roughly forty thousand belong to the combined contingent.
See [[The Twelve Stones from the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 4:1–24.

### The eastern tribes pledge to cross — Joshua 1:10–18

Joshua reminds *the Gadites* (12), with Gad, Reuben and the half-tribe of Manasseh, of Moses' word: families and livestock stay east of the Jordan, and all the *mighty men of valor* cross armed ahead of their brothers and help them until Yahweh gives the others rest (13–15). They answer *All that you have commanded us we will do* (16) and set death for anyone who rebels against Joshua's word (18). The tribe, not the patriarch, is in view.

See [[The Eastern Tribes Pledge to Cross]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 1:10–18.

### Moses blesses the tribes — Deuteronomy 33:1–29

Gad receives enlargement, lioness imagery, a first portion, and praise for acting with Israel’s
leaders (20–21). This can be compared with the eastern allotment and obligation to assist the other
tribes in Numbers 32; the poem does not retell that negotiation.

See [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 33:1–29; comparisons as cited.


### The covenant in Moab — Deuteronomy 29:1–29

The Gadites receive a share of the eastern inheritance (8). This recalls the descendants’ allotment rather than the patriarch’s own action.

See [[The Covenant in Moab and the Hidden Things]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 29:1–29.

### The responsive curses — Deuteronomy 27:11–26

The tribe bearing this ancestor’s name is assigned to Mount Ebal for the curse (13). This concerns the descendants, not the patriarch. The assignment does not pronounce this tribe morally inferior or uniquely cursed.

See [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:11–26.


### Eastern refuge cities — Deuteronomy 4:41–43

Ramoth in Gilead is designated for the Gadites (43). The tribal descendants, not
the patriarch, receive this refuge provision.

See [[Three Cities Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:41–43.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

The Gadites share the grant described with Reuben in verses 12 and 16–17. Settlement does not
release their warriors from crossing with their brothers; return waits for the others’ possession
(18–20). The patriarch is not a participant.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

The tribe’s eastern inheritance is already received (14–15), explaining its absence from the western allotment roster. The patriarch is not a participant.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

With Reuben, the tribe asks for Jazer and Gilead, *a place for livestock*, and is told by Moses that
staying behind would repeat the spies' discouragement (1–15). Named first from verse 2 onward. The two
tribes offer to go armed before Israel and not return home until every tribe has inherited (16–19);
Moses binds the offer *before Yahweh*, with *be sure your sin will find you out* if they break it (20–24).
Gad builds Dibon, Ataroth, Aroer, Atrothshophan, Jazer, Jogbehah, Beth Nimrah, and Beth Haran (34–36) —
the allotment Jacob's saying about a troop pressing on Gad (Genesis 49:19) fits. Joshua 13 assigns some of
the same towns to Reuben; see the passage page.

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

The tribe bearing his name has 40,500 men in the second military census (Numbers 26). The register
names ancestral families, not a new action by the patriarch.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


**Zilpah's first son, Jacob's seventh, and the one tribal name in the list glossed by a word the
prophets later have to argue with.**

> **11** Leah said, “How fortunate!” She named him Gad. (30:11)

The Hebrew is ***bəgād*** as written and ***bāʾ gād***, *fortune has come*, as read in the Masoretic
margin. ***Gād*** is the ordinary noun for *luck* or *fortune* — **and it is also a divine name.**
Isaiah 65:11 arraigns those *"who forsake Yahweh… who prepare a table for **Fortune**"*, where the
Hebrew word is *Gad*, and the deity is attested across the ancient Near East and preserved in place
names like Baal Gad (Joshua 11:17). **Genesis records [[Leah]]'s exclamation and comments on none of
this.** See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Names and Naming]].

**Gad and [[Asher]] are the only two of the twelve whose naming speeches contain no divine name and no
divine act.** Leah's first four sons had *Yahweh* in three of them and her last two have *ʾĕlōhîm*;
these two have neither, and no verb whose subject is God. **Luck, and then reputation.** And they are
the two she names when she is not barren and is matching her sister move for move — **[[Zilpah]] is
produced at 30:9 because Leah has *stopped* bearing, not because she cannot.**

Zilpah, who bore him, is not recorded saying anything at either of her sons' births.

### In this pericope — Genesis 49:19

> **19** “A troop will press on Gad, but he will press on their heel. (49:19)

**Five Hebrew words, four of them built out of his name, and it is the densest wordplay in the
Bible:** ***gād gədûd yəgûdennû wəhûʾ yāgud ʿāqēb.*** **The pun is on a second root** — ***gədûd***, a
raiding band — **where his mother's naming at 30:11 heard *gād*, *fortune*.** **The line turns on
itself**: *raiders will raid him, and he will raid their heel.*

***ʿĀqēb***, *heel*, **is the word of [[Jacob]]'s own birth and his own name** (25:26; 27:36) — **the
heel he was born holding and the pun [[Esau]] mocked him with.** **He uses it once in the poem, of
somebody else's enemies**, and Genesis does not remark on it.

**The saying suits the allotment.** **Gad settles east of the Jordan** (Numbers 32:34–36), **exposed
on the frontier and raided from it for most of its history**; **1 Chronicles 12:8 calls its men
*"mighty men of valour… whose faces were like the faces of lions"*.** **Moses' blessing keeps the
predatory image and upgrades it** — *"he dwells as a lioness, and tears the arm"* (Deuteronomy
33:20–21). See [[Jacob's Blessing and the Blessing of Moses - Genesis 49 and Deuteronomy 33]].

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

**The tribe takes land east of the Jordan** — with Reuben
and half of Manasseh, on their own request, in the negotiation of Numbers 32 — **and so is one of the
tribes settled outside the land the promise names.** The Mesha Stele, a Moabite inscription from the
ninth century, mentions the men of Gad living in Ataroth *"from of old"*: **one of the few tribal names
of Genesis attested outside the Bible.**

### The census — Numbers 1:1–54

His tribe numbers 45,650, the only figure not ending in hundreds, with Eliasaph son of Deuel (14, 25). Eleventh in the name list, he moves to third in the census, behind Simeon, which is his place in the camp at 2:14.

See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 1.

### The camp — Numbers 2:1–34

Takes the third place in Reuben's camp on the south (14), which matches the census order of chapter 1: the son of Leah's servant stands where Levi might have been expected. His prince is Eliasaph, and his father is *Reuel* here, Deuel at 1:14, 7:42, and 10:20 (WLC).

See [[The Camp Around the Tent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 2.

### The altar gifts — Numbers 7:42–47

The tribe of Gad gives on the sixth day of twelve, by the hand of Eliasaph. Its gift is identical to every other tribe's.

See [[The Offerings of the Twelve Princes]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 7.

### With Reuben — Numbers 10:20

In Reuben's camp, under Eliasaph son of Deuel, as at 1:14 and not the Reuel of 2:14.

See [[The March from Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 10.

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** *The Gadites* receive the east bank from Moses (12:6) — the tribe, not the patriarch. See [[The Kings Struck Down]].
