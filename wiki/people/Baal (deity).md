# Baal (deity)

## Overview

The god Israel serves in place of Yahweh in the cycle of Judges. Judges 2:11 has *the Baals*,
*habbəʿālîm*, plural with the article; 2:13 has the singular, *Baal* (*labbaʿal*), with *the
Ashtaroth* (WLC; stored WEB). *Baʿal* is an ordinary Hebrew word for *owner, master, husband*, and as
a divine title it was attached to many local gods. The plural is usually read as the Baal of many
places rather than many unrelated gods; that reading is reported here, not settled.

Before Judges, the name has appeared in the wiki only in compounds: the god of Peor (see
[[Baal Peor]]) and places named for a Baal, such as [[Baal Zephon]], [[Baal Gad]], and
[[Baal Meon]]. Judges 2 is the first place the god is named by himself, and the summary makes him the
standing alternative to Yahweh for the whole book. Judges 3:7 will pair *the Baals* with *the
Asheroth* (stored WEB).

The page is qualified `(deity)` from the start because the name is expected to collide later:
1 Chronicles is recalled as naming both a man and a town called Baal (not yet stored, so not checked).
Qualifying now means no bare `Baal.md` is ever created.

Source: stored WEB Judges 2:11–13; 3:7. Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-13.
