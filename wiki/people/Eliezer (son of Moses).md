# Eliezer (son of Moses)

## Overview

**Moses' second son by Zipporah, named for the first time when his grandfather brings him back to his father at the
Mountain of God.** *"The name of the other was Eliezer, for he said, 'My father's God was my help and delivered me
from Pharaoh's sword'"* (Exodus 18:4). ***ʾĔlîʿezer*** means *my God is help*. Exodus never reports his birth;
4:20 sets *"his sons"*, plural, on the donkey for Egypt when only [[Gershom]] has been born on the page (2:22), so
the second son exists in the story before he has a name. See [[Jethro's Counsel]].

**His name is the answer to his brother's.** Gershom's name — *"I have lived as a foreigner in a foreign land"* —
has no God in it; Eliezer's has God twice, *ʾēl* in the name and *"My father's God"* in the explanation, and it
remembers the flight: *"Pharaoh's sword"* is the danger of 2:15, *"he sought to kill Moses"*. **One son is named
for the exile, the other for the escape.** *"My father's God"* is the phrase of the bush, where God introduced
himself to Moses as *"the God of your father"* (3:6).

**The *help* in his name is *ʿēzer*, the noun of Genesis 2:18**, *"I will make him a helper (ʿēzer)"*; in the Torah
it occurs otherwise only there, in 2:20, and in Moses' blessing (Deuteronomy 33:7, 26, 29) (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). And **he shares his name with Abram's steward, *"Eliezer of Damascus"*** (Genesis 15:2) —
the name's only other bearer in the Torah (WLC). The two men are unrelated; see [[Eliezer of Damascus]].

### His line

**Chronicles names his descendants among the Levites**: *"The sons of Moses: Gershom and Eliezer"*; *"The sons of
Eliezer were: Rehabiah the chief; and Eliezer had no other sons; but the sons of Rehabiah were very many"*
(1 Chronicles 23:15, 17; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **Five generations on, *"Shelomoth and his brothers"*, of
Eliezer's line, are *"over all the treasures of the dedicated things"*** in David's day (1 Chronicles 26:25–26;
WEB; *not yet ingested*). **One son, and that son's sons *"very many"*** — and, like Gershom's line, no priesthood.

**The name is borne by several later, unrelated men**; each will get its own page, qualified, when its pericope is
reached.
