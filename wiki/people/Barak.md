# Barak

## Overview

### Singing with Deborah — Judges 5:1, 12, 15

Barak sings the Song with Deborah (5:1), though the verb is feminine singular and agrees with her
(*wattāšar*, WLC). The Song addresses him once: *Arise, Barak, and lead away your captives, you son
of Abinoam* (5:12), and places him with the tribe that came: *As was Issachar, so was Barak. They
rushed into the valley at his feet* (5:15). It gives him no further part. The killing of Sisera, the
honor of 4:9, is Jael's in the Song as in the prose (5:24–27). See [[The Song of Deborah]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 5:1, 12, 15, 24–27; Hebrew WLC.

### Second to the tent — Judges 4:22

Barak is still pursuing when Jael comes out to meet him: *Come, and I will show you the man whom you
seek* (4:22). He goes in and finds Sisera dead with the peg in his temple. Deborah had told him the
journey *won't be for your honor; for Yahweh will sell Sisera into a woman's hand* (4:9), and it
happens: the general he was sent against is killed before he arrives, by a woman who is not of Israel.
The text does not say how Barak responded. He sings with Deborah in Judges 5. See [[Jael and Sisera]]
and [[Into a Woman's Hand - Judges 4.9, 4.21, and 9.53-54]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 4:9, 22; 5:1.

*Barak the son of Abinoam out of Kedesh Naphtali* (Judges 4:6), the commander of the battle against
Sisera. Deborah summons him and puts Yahweh's command to him as a question, *Hasn't Yahweh … commanded*,
which suggests he had been told before. He answers with a condition: *If you will go with me, then I
will go; but if you will not go with me, I will not go* (4:8). Deborah agrees and tells him the
journey *won't be for your honor; for Yahweh will sell Sisera into a woman's hand* (4:9).

He musters ten thousand of Zebulun and Naphtali at Kedesh, goes up Mount Tabor, and at Deborah's word
comes down against the chariots. *Yahweh confused Sisera … before Barak* (4:15). Barak pursues to
Harosheth, and Sisera's army is destroyed to the last man (4:16). Sisera himself escapes on foot.

The text never calls him a judge or a savior, and no Spirit comes on him. Judges does not comment on
his condition in 4:8. Hebrews 11:32 names him, and not Deborah, among those of faith: *Gideon, Barak,
Samson, Jephthah* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com). Whether 4:8 is hesitation, or a request for the
prophet's presence in battle, the text leaves open.

**His name.** *Bārāq* is also the word for *lightning*; that reading is reported, not verified.

Source: stored WEB Judges 4:1–16; Hebrews 11:32 (WEB) via bible-api.com, 2026-11-13; Hebrew WLC.
