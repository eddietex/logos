# Hadad (son of Bedad)

## Overview

**The fourth king of Edom, and the only one Genesis credits with doing anything.**

> **35** Husham died, and Hadad, the son of Bedad, who struck Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in
> his place. The name of his city was Avith.

**One relative clause, and it is the only military event in forty-three verses** — the chapter's
second and last piece of narrative, after [[Anah]]'s hot springs at 36:24. **Edom beats [[Midian]] on
[[Moab (place)]]'s ground**, which means a campaign fought across a third party's territory by two
peoples the Bible otherwise keeps in the background. See [[The Descendants of Esau]].

**It is a note from a regional history Scripture never tells.** Nothing else in the Bible records this
war, dates it, or refers back to it. Genesis preserves the clause because it was in the king list it
was copying, and adds nothing.

## The name

***Hadad*** is the name of the **Canaanite and Aramean storm god** — Hadad, Adad, the weather deity of
the whole Levant and Mesopotamia, the one whose title *Baal* eventually swallows the name. **It is the
most conspicuously pagan name in the chapter**, borne by a king of the nation Israel calls *brother*,
and Genesis does not remark on it.

**It is also common.** A **Hadad** is among Ishmael's twelve sons at 25:15; another **Hadad** is the
eighth and last king in this list at 1 Chronicles 1:50, where the Chronicler reads *Hadad* for
Genesis 36:39's [[Hadar]]; and **Hadad the Edomite** is the adversary God raises up against Solomon at
1 Kings 11:14–22, a survivor of David's campaign who escapes to Egypt as a child and comes back. Later
Aramean kings of Damascus are *Ben-Hadad*, **son of Hadad**. **The name runs through the whole region
for a thousand years**, which is the same point the chapter's Reuels, Eliphazes, and Uzzes make.
