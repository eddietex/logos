# The Chief Baker

*Titled by his office, as Genesis leaves him; see the naming note on [[The Chief Cupbearer]], which
applies to both.*

## Overview

**The man the chapter needs in order to prove anything.**

> **2** Pharaoh was angry with his two officers, the chief cup bearer and the chief baker. (40:2)

***Śar hāʾôpîm***, *chief of the bakers* — a real Egyptian palace office, and the counterpart to the
cupbearer's: **between them the two men are responsible for everything that goes into the king's
mouth**, which is why an offence by either is a matter for the king personally and why both end up in
the same cell. **Genesis never says what either of them did.**

### He asks because the first answer was good

> **16** When the chief baker saw that the interpretation was good, he said to Joseph, “I also was in my
> dream, and behold, three baskets of white bread were on my head.
> **17** In the uppermost basket there were all kinds of baked food for Pharaoh, and the birds ate them
> out of the basket on my head.” (40:16–17)

***"When the chief baker saw that the interpretation was good."*** **Genesis states his motive before it
states his sentence**, which is the cruellest sequence in the chapter. *"I also"* — ***ʾap-ʾănî*** — is
a man joining in.

***"Three baskets of white bread."*** ***Sallê ḥōrî***, uncertain — *white bread*, or *wicker*, or
*openwork*. **Egyptian reliefs show servants carrying stacked baskets on the head exactly as described**,
and the dream, like the cupbearer's, is simply the job being done.

**Except that it is interrupted.** *"The birds ate them out of the basket on my head"* — **the one
element in either dream that is not the office performed**, and it is already the interpretation before
[[Joseph]] gives it. **Birds eating what should have reached a table is Scripture's image of a death
without burial**: Deuteronomy 28:26, Goliath's boast at 1 Samuel 17:44, Jeremiah 7:33. See
[[Burial and the Grave]].

### The same sentence, two words longer

> **19** Within three more days, Pharaoh will lift up your head from off you, and will hang you on a
> tree; and the birds will eat your flesh from off you.” (40:19)

**Joseph gives him verse 13 with *mēʿāleykā*, *from off you*, added** — the same opening formula, the
same three-day count, the same royal subject, **and the pun turned over.** He does not soften it, delay
it, or decline. See [[Lift Up Your Head - One Idiom and Two Verdicts]].

***"Hang you on a tree"*** — ***tālâ ʿal-ʿēṣ***, most likely impalement or the public exposure of a body
already executed, which is how the practice appears throughout the Old Testament, and **the seed of a
thread that runs to Deuteronomy 21:22–23 and from there to Galatians 3:13.** See
[[Hanged on a Tree - Genesis 40, Deuteronomy 21, and Galatians 3]].

**He says nothing after this.** Genesis gives him no reply, no protest, and no death scene: *"but he
hanged the chief baker, as Joseph had interpreted to them"* (40:22), **half a verse, in a sentence whose
main clause is about the accuracy of an interpretation.** He is not mentioned again in Scripture.

### Why the chapter cannot do without him

**An interpreter who is right only when the news is good has proved nothing.** The chapter's claim is
that *"interpretations belong to God"* (40:8), and **the test of that claim is a prediction the
predictor has no interest in making and no power to arrange.** The baker's death is what makes the
cupbearer's restoration evidence rather than coincidence — **which is exactly the use the cupbearer
himself puts it to two years later**: *"as he interpreted to us, so it was. He restored me to my office,
and he hanged him"* (41:13).

## Timeline / key events

- Offends the king of Egypt, with the chief cupbearer, and is imprisoned in the captain of the guard's
  house — Genesis 40:1–3, [[The Cupbearer and the Baker]]
- Assigned, with the cupbearer, to Joseph — Genesis 40:4
- Dreams three baskets and the birds — Genesis 40:16–17, told only after he hears the first
  interpretation is favourable
- Told the three baskets are three days, and that Pharaoh will lift up his head *from off him* —
  Genesis 40:18–19
- Hanged on the third day, at Pharaoh's birthday feast — Genesis 40:20, 22
- Cited by the restored cupbearer as proof that the Hebrew's readings held — Genesis 41:13 *(not yet
  ingested)*

## Appears in

- [[The Cupbearer and the Baker]] — Genesis 40:1–23; **the other half of the diptych, and the half that
  makes it evidence.** He volunteers his dream because his colleague's answer was favourable, hears the
  same sentence with two Hebrew words added, and is disposed of in half a verse. **His is the only
  judicial execution actually carried out in Genesis** — [[Judah]] sentences [[Tamar]] to burning at
  38:24 and revokes it — and the narration gives him no last word
