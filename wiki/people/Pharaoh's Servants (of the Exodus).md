# Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)

## Overview

**Pharaoh's court — the *ʿăbādîm*, *servants*, who watch every sign, share every plague, and are the
first in Egypt to tell their king he has lost.** Exodus never names them or counts them, and never
separates one from another, so one page holds the group, as it does for
[[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]], [[Shiphrah and Puah]], and [[Job's Children]]. The qualifier
follows the rule for figures known only by a title: they appear with [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]].
They are not the magicians, though the magicians are presumably among them.

**Their name is the book's key word.** *ʿEbed*, *servant*, is the noun of *ʿābad*, *to serve*, the root
the whole of Exodus is built on (see [[Exodus]]). **Pharaoh has servants; Israel is in Egypt's service;
and the demand Moses carries is that Israel be let go to *serve* Yahweh.** The court is the one group in
the story that is already called what Israel is being made into.

**For six chapters they are an audience.** The foremen are *"a stench … in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in
the eyes of his servants"* (5:21). Aaron's rod is cast down *"before Pharaoh and before his servants"*
(7:10), and the Nile struck *"in the sight of his servants"* (7:20). The frogs come into their houses
(8:3–4), the flies swarm on them (8:21, 24), and the flies are taken from them (8:29, 31). See
[[Water Turned to Blood]] and [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]].

**At the hail they divide.** *"Those who feared Yahweh's word among the servants of Pharaoh made their
servants and their livestock flee into the houses"*; those who *"didn't respect Yahweh's word"* left
theirs in the field (9:20–21). **They are the first Egyptians in Exodus said to fear Yahweh.** Then
Moses says to Pharaoh, *"as for you and your servants, I know that you don't yet fear Yahweh God"*
(9:30), and when the storm stops Pharaoh *"hardened his heart, he and his servants"* (9:34). See
[[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]] and [[The Fear of God]].

**At the locusts God claims their hearts and they speak.** *"I have hardened his heart, and the heart of
his servants"* (10:1). Moses threatens to fill *"the houses of all your servants"* (10:6) and walks out;
and for the first time they talk to their king: ***"How long will this man be a snare to us? Let the men
go, that they may serve Yahweh, their God. Don't you yet know that Egypt is destroyed?"*** (10:7).
**Their *"How long"* is God's question of three verses before** (10:3); their *"Don't you yet know"*
takes up Moses' *"you don't yet fear"* (9:30). **They are hardened, and they see clearly at the same
time** — they have stopped trying to keep Israel, and they still propose only the men. Pharaoh brings
Moses back on their word, and then makes their offer his own (10:8–11). See [[Locusts and Darkness]].

**The two things the text says of them together are not reconciled.** God hardened their hearts
(10:1), and they plead for Israel's release (10:7). The page records both.

### What is ahead

- 11:3 — Moses is *"very great … in the sight of Pharaoh's servants"*
- 11:8 — *"All these servants of yours will come down to me, and bow down themselves to me"*
- 12:30 — *"Pharaoh rose up in the night, he, and all his servants"*
- 14:5 — *"the heart of Pharaoh and of his servants was changed towards the people"*: *"What is this we
  have done, that we have let Israel go from serving us?"*

These are written as their pericopes arrive.
