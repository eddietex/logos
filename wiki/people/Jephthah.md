# Jephthah

## Overview

### The vow — Judges 11:29–40

*Then Yahweh's Spirit came on Jephthah* (11:29). He marches through Gilead and Manasseh, and before the
battle he vows: if Yahweh gives Ammon into his hand, *whatever comes out of the doors of my house to meet
me, when I return in peace … shall be Yahweh's, and I will offer it up for a burnt offering* (11:30–31).
Yahweh gives them into his hand, and he strikes twenty cities (11:32–33).

His only child comes out to meet him, *with tambourines and with dances* (11:34). He tears his clothes:
*Alas, my daughter! You have brought me very low, and you are one of those who trouble me; for I have
opened my mouth to Yahweh, and I can't go back* (11:35). He blames her and then names the cause, his own
mouth. She holds him to it (11:36). After two months, *he did with her according to his vow which he had
vowed* (11:39). Whether that was a burnt offering or a life of virginity is disputed; see the passage.

He came to office by a contract with the elders, and he brings the same terms to Yahweh. The Spirit is on
him before the vow. The text does not say the vow was needed, or that Yahweh wanted it. He comes home *in
peace*, as he asked. He had been driven from his father's house without an inheritance (11:2), and now he
loses his only heir.

See [[Jephthah's Vow]], [[Jephthah's Daughter]], and [[Your Only Son - Genesis 22 and Judges 11]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 11:29–40; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

### The message to Ammon's king — Judges 11:12–28

His first act as head is an embassy. *What have you to do with me, that you have come to me to fight
against my land?* (11:12). When Ammon's king claims the land from the Arnon to the Jabbok (11:13), Jephthah
sends a history of the road from Egypt: Israel took neither Moab's land nor Ammon's, was refused by Edom
and Moab, went around them, and took Sihon's Amorite land when Sihon fought and *Yahweh … delivered*
him (11:15–22). Then four arguments: Yahweh dispossessed the Amorites, and Ammon may keep what *Chemosh
your god* gives it (11:23–24); Balak never fought for the land (11:25); Israel has held it *three hundred
years* (11:26). He ends, *I therefore have not sinned against you … May Yahweh the Judge be judge today*
(11:27). The king *didn't listen* (11:28).

The man chosen for his strength opens with words, and with the Torah's history used as evidence. His
history follows Numbers' wording, credits Yahweh as Deuteronomy does, and departs from both on Moab's
response and on Balak. See [[Jephthah's History of the Journey - Numbers 20-22, Deuteronomy 2, and Judges 11]].

See [[Jephthah's Message to the King of Ammon]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 11:12–28; comparisons as cited there.

### Driven out and called back — Judges 11:1–11

*Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty man of valor, and he was the son of a prostitute. Gilead became
the father of Jephthah* (11:1). He is introduced as the answer to the princes' question, *Who is the man
who will begin to fight against the children of Ammon?* (10:18). His half-brothers, the sons of Gilead's
wife, drive him out: *You will not inherit in our father's house, for you are the son of another woman*
(11:2). He flees to [[Tob]], and *outlaws*, *ʾănāšîm rêqîm* (WLC), the *empty men* Abimelech hired
(9:4), gather to him (11:3).

When Ammon attacks, the elders of Gilead come for him. He is offered *chief*, *qāṣîn* (11:6), and
answers with a charge: *Didn't you hate me, and drive me out of my father's house? Why have you come to
me now when you are in distress?* (11:7). The offer is raised to *head over all the inhabitants of
Gilead* (11:8), the reward of 10:18. He makes Yahweh's victory the condition of the agreement, *if … Yahweh
delivers them before me, will I be your head?* (11:9). The elders answer with an oath, *Yahweh will be
witness between us* (11:10). The people make him *head and chief*, and he speaks *all his words before
Yahweh in Mizpah* (11:11).

He is the first deliverer in Judges whom Yahweh does not raise up or call. The people choose him by a
contract, and Yahweh's name enters the account through Jephthah's own condition. His father's name is
also the region's and the clan's (Numbers 26:29); the text treats this Gilead as a man with a wife and
sons, and does not explain the name. The name *yiptāḥ*, *he opens* from the verb form, is also the
town [[Iphtah]] in Judah (Joshua 15:43; same lemma in the WLC).

The Spirit and his vow follow (11:29–40, above). Ephraim and *Shibboleth* (12:1–7) are in stored WEB
and not yet ingested.

See [[Jephthah Called from Tob]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 9:4; 10:18; 11:1–11; Numbers 26:29;
Joshua 15:43; Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

## Timeline / key events
- Born to Gilead by a prostitute; driven out by his brothers, without inheritance — Judges 11:1–2
- Lives in the land of Tob with *outlaws* — Judges 11:3
- Brought back by the elders of Gilead and made *head and chief* — Judges 11:5–11
- Sends two embassies to Ammon's king and argues Israel's title to the land — Judges 11:12–28
- The Spirit comes on him; he vows a burnt offering of whatever comes out of his door — Judges 11:29–31
- Defeats Ammon, twenty cities from Aroer to Minnith — Judges 11:32–33
- His only daughter comes out to meet him; he keeps the vow — Judges 11:34–39
