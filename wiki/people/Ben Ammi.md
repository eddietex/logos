# Ben Ammi

## Overview

**The younger of the two sons born in the cave, and the only one of the pair whose name Genesis
lets the reader translate.**

> **38** The younger also bore a son, and called his name Ben Ammi. He is the father of the children
> of Ammon to this day.

*Ben ʿAmmî* is **son of my kinsman** — or *son of my people*; *ʿam* carries both, and in the
old idiom of kinship terms it means the near relative on the father's side. **The name says what
happened without saying it.** Genesis does not gloss it because it does not need to: any Hebrew
reader hears it, which is exactly the difference from [[Moab (person)]] one verse earlier, where
the pun (*mē-ʾāb*, *from father*) is available but unstated and the Septuagint has to supply it.

That asymmetry is the seed of a long reading. **Rabbinic tradition takes the two names as a measure
of the two sisters** — the elder brazen, naming her son after the act; the younger discreet, saying
the same thing in a form that could pass — and finds the difference reflected in how the two nations
are treated afterwards, since Deuteronomy 2:9 tells Israel not even to *contend* with Moab while
2:19 only says not to harass Ammon (*Bava Kamma* 38b). The wiki records the reading as reception. It
is a real observation about the two verses and a great deal of weight to hang on a preposition.

### And then, like his cousin, nothing

He has no life, no words, no death, and no listed descendants. **The nation is what he is for.**
After Genesis 19:38 the Hebrew Bible never uses the name again; what it uses is *bənê ʿAmmôn*, *the
children of Ammon*, roughly a hundred times — a people who besiege Jabesh Gilead, humiliate David's
envoys, receive one of Amos's fiercest oracles, and hold their land, Deuteronomy insists, because
God gave it to *"the children of Lot"*.
