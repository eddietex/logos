# Chedorlaomer

## Overview

King of [[Elam]], overlord of the cities of the plain for twelve years, and the man the Bible's
first war is named after.

He is listed third of four in Genesis 14:1 — *"Amraphel, king of Shinar, Arioch, king of Ellasar,
Chedorlaomer, king of Elam, and Tidal, king of Goiim"* — and is plainly the senior partner anyway.
The five cities *"served Chedorlaomer for twelve years, and in the thirteenth year, they rebelled"*
(14:4); it is he who *"came, and the kings who were with him"* in the fourteenth (14:5); the battle
line is drawn *"against Chedorlaomer king of Elam"* first (14:9); and the whole campaign is
summarized afterwards as *"the slaughter of Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him"* (14:17).
The other three are his coalition. See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

**What he does is a standard imperial reprisal.** Tribute for twelve years, refusal in the
thirteenth, a punitive expedition in the fourteenth that sweeps down the Transjordan striking the
Rephaim, Zuzim, Emim, and Horites, turns at El Paran, comes back through Kadesh, and then destroys
the rebel coalition in the valley of Siddim. He takes the goods, the food, and the people of Sodom
and Gomorrah, and among them [[Lot]] — which is the only reason Genesis is telling the story.

**And what ends him, narratively, is three hundred eighteen men.** *"He divided himself against
them by night, he and his servants, and struck them, and pursued them to Hobah, which is on the
left hand of Damascus"* (14:15). Genesis spends nine verses assembling an empire and one clause
dismantling it. Chedorlaomer is not said to be killed, does not speak, and never appears again.

## The other three kings

**Amraphel of Shinar, Arioch of Ellasar, and Tidal of Goiim** are named twice each — in the roll of
14:1 and the battle order of 14:9 — and given nothing else: no acts, no words, no fate. The wiki
covers them here rather than making three pages out of a name and a kingdom apiece; each earns its
own page only if something later in Scripture picks it up, and nothing does.

- **Amraphel of [[Shinar]]** — Shinar is the Babel plain of Genesis 10:10 and 11:2, so the coalition
  includes Babylonia. Nineteenth-century scholarship identified Amraphel with Hammurabi; the
  equation requires phonetic moves nobody now defends, and it has been abandoned.
- **Arioch of Ellasar** — the name matches Hurrian *Arriwuk*, attested at Mari, and recurs as the
  captain of the guard in Daniel 2:14. Ellasar is unidentified; Larsa in southern Mesopotamia is the
  usual guess and is not secure.
- **Tidal, king of Goiim** — *Goiim* is not a country. It is *gôyim*, the ordinary Hebrew word for
  **nations**, the word behind [[The Nations]], so the title reads *king of nations*. *Tidal*
  corresponds well to Hittite *Tudḫaliya*, a name borne by several kings, which would make him a
  ruler of a mixed northern confederation.

**None of the four has been identified with a known ruler.** The names are linguistically plausible
for second-millennium Mesopotamia and Anatolia — the Elamite element *Kudur-* stands behind
*Chedorlaomer*, and *Laomer* is often connected with the Elamite deity Lagamar — but no inscription
names any of them, and no such coalition is attested. The wiki records the plausibility of the names
and the absence of the men, and takes no position on the chapter's historicity.
