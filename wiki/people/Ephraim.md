# Ephraim

*Unqualified for now. **The name will later belong to a tribe, to a hill country, to the northern kingdom
as a whole in the prophets, and to a town in John 11:54**, so a collision is certain once the wiki reaches
those books. `AGENTS.md`'s rule is to qualify in the pass that creates the second page rather than in
anticipation; **the later pass should rename this page and fix its inbound links.***

## Overview

**Joseph's second son, named for fruitfulness in a country his father calls an affliction.**

> **52** The name of the second, he called Ephraim: “For God has made me fruitful in the land of my
> affliction.”

***ʾEprayim*** is built on ***pārâ***, *to be fruitful*, **in what looks like a dual or a locative
form** — *double fruitfulness*, or *fruit land*. **Genesis gives the gloss in the verse, as it does for
[[Manasseh]].** See [[Names and Naming]].

### The verb in the name is the creation verb

***Pārâ*** is **not an ordinary word for prospering.** It is:

- **the blessing on the creatures of the fifth day** — *"Be fruitful, and multiply"* (1:22) — and **on
  humanity on the sixth** (1:28);
- **the blessing reissued after the flood** (9:1, 7);
- **God's word to Abram at the renaming** — *"I will make you exceedingly fruitful"* (17:6) — and
  **to Jacob at Bethel** (35:11).

**Joseph applies it to two sons born to an Egyptian priest's daughter, outside the land, with no covenant
word spoken over him anywhere in the book.** **It is the most confident theological claim he makes about
his own life**, and it is made in a birth announcement. See [[Blessing]] and [[Creation]].

### "The land of my affliction"

***ʾEreṣ ʿonyî*** — **and this is the sentence that keeps Genesis 41 from being a success story.**

**He says it with a new name, a wife, a signet ring, a chariot, and a country.** **At the peak of
everything the chapter has given him, Joseph's own word for Egypt is *affliction*.**

**The root is *ʿānâ*, and it is the root the Exodus is built on.**

- **Genesis 15:13** — Abram is told his offspring will be sojourners in a land not theirs, *"and will
  serve them. They will **afflict** them four hundred years."*
- **Exodus 3:7** — *"I have surely seen the **affliction** of my people who are in Egypt."*

**Joseph names a son with the word of the prophecy his family is living inside**, and **Genesis does not
point it out.** See [[Suffering]],
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]], and
[[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].

### The younger who takes the right hand

**At 48:13–20 Joseph presents both boys to the dying [[Jacob]]** — **Manasseh at the right hand,
carefully, because he is the elder** — **and Jacob crosses his arms.** Joseph tries to move the hand
(*"Not so, my father"*), and Jacob will not: *"his younger brother will be greater than he, and his
offspring will become a multitude of nations"* (48:19).

**It is the fourth reversal of birth order in Genesis** — [[Isaac]] over [[Ishmael]], [[Jacob]] over
[[Esau]], [[Perez]] over [[Zerah]] — **and the only one accomplished without deceit, purchase, or
violence.** **An old blind man does deliberately what an old blind man was once tricked into doing**
(27:1–29); see [[The Stolen Blessing]] and [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

### What becomes of the name

**The leading tribe of the north.** Joshua is an Ephraimite (Numbers 13:8, 16); Shiloh and the ark sit in
Ephraim's territory; **Jeroboam is an Ephraimite and the kingdom he founds is called *Ephraim* by Hosea,
Isaiah and Jeremiah more or less interchangeably with *Israel*.** **Hosea 11:8 — *"How can I give you up,
Ephraim?"* — is addressed to the name Joseph coined in a delivery room in Egypt.**

## Appears in

- [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]] — Genesis 41:50, 52; **the second of two sons born in the years
  of plenty, and the one whose name carries the chapter's sting.** The naming speech uses **the creation
  and covenant verb *pārâ*** of two half-Egyptian children, and **calls the country that made his father
  its governor *the land of my affliction*** — a word whose root runs straight to Exodus 3:7
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 46:20, 27; **one of the two *"sons of Joseph, who were born to him
  in Egypt"*** — counted into the seventy and out of the sixty-six. **Genesis names [[Manasseh]] first
  here, as it did at 41:51–52**, and the order will be reversed by a pair of crossed hands at 48:14
