# Zophar the Naamathite

## Overview

The third and least patient of Job's friends, and the one who gets the fewest words. He speaks
twice — chapters 11 and 20 — and in the third cycle he does not speak at all, which is either the
clearest sign that the friends have been argued into silence or the clearest sign that the text of
Job 25–27 has been disturbed (see [[Bildad the Shuhite]] for both readings).

His first speech is the one that says the quiet part. *"Should your boastings make men hold their
peace? … Oh that God would speak, and open his lips against you, that he would show you the secrets
of wisdom! … Know therefore that God exacts of you **less than your iniquity deserves**"* (11:3, 5–6).
Zophar's position is not merely that Job is guilty but that he is getting off lightly, and he is
the only friend to say so. He then demands repentance in the plainest terms of any of the three
(11:13–20), and his second speech (chapter 20) is twenty-nine verses on the brevity of the wicked
man's triumph — the sweetness of evil turning to the venom of asps in his belly, riches swallowed
and vomited up.

He is a **Naamathite**, from a [[Naamah]] otherwise unlocated; the Naamah of Joshua 15:41 is in the
Judean lowlands and does not suit the book's eastern setting, so the place is usually taken to be a
site in northern Arabia or Edom that has left no other trace. **The Septuagint makes him king of the
Minaeans**, a real south-Arabian people — worth recording as evidence that the Greek tradition read
the friends' origins the same direction the Hebrew gentilics point, and worth discounting as one of
three royal titles that translator handed out.

## In the ingested passages

**Both of his speeches are now ingested.** The first is [[Zophar's First Speech]], Job 11:1–20, and
it answers the prayer of chapters 9–10 as though it had been a boast. Four things in it define him.

**He is the friend who says the quiet part.** *"Know therefore that God exacts of you less than your
iniquity deserves"* (11:6) — or, on the other reading of *yaššeh*, *God has forgotten some of your
iniquity*. Either way, the ten dead children and the sores are a discount. Eliphaz suggested
discipline and Bildad blamed the children; Zophar enlarges Job's own guilt until it covers every
fact, which is the doctrine's logic once a man refuses to confess.

**He builds his rebuke out of Job's words.** *Yiṣdaq* (11:2) is Job's legal question of 9:2; *mock*
(11:3) is what Job said God does (9:23); *"he passes by… who can hinder him?"* (11:10) is 9:11–12
verbatim; and the misery that passes *"like waters that have passed away"* (11:16) is Job's own
image of the friends as a failing wadi (6:15). And once he misquotes: *"For you say, 'My doctrine is
pure. I am clean in your eyes'"* (11:4) puts Bildad's word *zak* (8:6) into a prayer Job addressed to
God (10:7).

**He wishes God would speak, in Job's own formula, and is granted it.** *Mî-yittēn*, *oh that*
(11:5), is the shape of every wish Job makes (6:8; 14:13; 19:23; 23:3; 31:35). God does open his lips
(38:1), and his wrath is against the friends (42:7).

**And his offer is the most generous of the first cycle.** The face lifted without spot (11:15),
fear removed, misery forgotten, darkness made morning (11:17, reversing 10:22), and the covenant's
own *"you shall lie down, and no one shall make you afraid"* (11:19; Leviticus 26:6). Nearly every
clause comes true in the epilogue, at his own expense: *"Yahweh accepted Job"* (42:9) is literally
*Yahweh lifted Job's face*, in the verse that names Zophar among the three who need it, and *"many
shall court your favor"* (11:19) begins with the friends bringing their bulls and rams to Job. See
[[None Shall Make You Afraid - The Covenant Blessing of Security]] and
[[A Shock of Grain in Its Season - Eliphaz's Promise and the Epilogue]].

His second and final speech is [[Zophar's Second Speech]], and it is the last thing he ever says. Twenty-nine verses, and the only concession any of the three makes to being provoked:
*"I have heard the reproof which puts me to shame. The spirit of my understanding answers me"*
(20:3), replying to Job's warning about the sword at 19:29.

Three features of it define him. **The metaphor is digestive, and it is his own.** Wickedness sweet
in the mouth, hidden under the tongue, saved and savoured, and then cobra venom in the belly;
riches swallowed and vomited up; wrath raining on a man in the act of eating. Nowhere else do the
friends come as close to saying that sin is intrinsically destructive rather than merely penalised.
**The thesis concedes too much.** *"The triumphing of the wicked is short"* (20:5) grants that they
triumph, and moves the claim from *the wicked suffer* to *the wicked suffer eventually*, which
cannot support reading a verdict off a man's present ruin. Job dismantles exactly that in chapter
21. **And 20:19 drafts the indictment**: *"For he has oppressed and forsaken the poor. He has
violently taken away a house"* is the first actual offence anyone in the book names, still in the
third person. Two chapters later [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] makes it personal and invents the details
(22:6–9).

What is missing from chapter 20 is everything he offered in chapter 11. No call to repent, no *"put
away unrighteousness"*, no *"you shall be secure"*. He came with a door and left without one.

Verse 27 is worth marking on its own: *"The heavens shall reveal his iniquity. The earth shall rise
up against him."* Four chapters earlier Job had summoned the same two witnesses for the defence —
*"Earth, don't cover my blood … my witness is in heaven"* (16:18–19). Neither man can hear the
other's use of the figure.

In Job 15–17 he does not speak, and is included in the same two ways as Bildad.

He is inside Eliphaz's *"we"* and *"with us"* at 15:9–10 in [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]], and inside
Job's plurals at 16:2 and 17:10 in [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]]. Job's *"You
are all miserable comforters"* is addressed to a group whose position, at this point in the book,
Zophar has stated more severely than either of the others.

There is a particular edge in that. When Job says at 16:17 *"there is no violence in my hands, and
my prayer is pure"*, the standing rebuttal in the room is Zophar's 11:6, that God is exacting less
than his iniquity deserves. The reader knows which of the two is right.

## Timeline / key events

- **Job 2:11–13** — [[Seven Days of Silence]]. Comes from [[Naamah]] by arrangement with the other
  two, fails to recognise Job, weeps, tears his robe, throws dust over his head, and sits on the
  ground seven days and seven nights without speaking.
- **Job 11:1–20** — [[Zophar's First Speech]]: the multitude of words rebuked, the wish that God
  would speak, *"less than your iniquity deserves"*, the God higher than heaven and deeper than
  Sheol, the wild donkey's colt, and the last offer of a future any friend makes.
- **Job 12:1–14:22** — [[Though He Slay Me]]. Answered, in the plural with the other two: his
  *"secrets of wisdom"* met with *"who doesn't know such things as these?"* (12:3), his four regions of
  the cosmos with four classes of creature (12:7–8), and his *mî-yittēn* taken back by its owner
  (13:5; 14:4, 13).
- **Job 15–17** — silent, and included in the friends' *"we"* and Job's *"you all"*.
- **Job 20:1–29** — [[Zophar's Second Speech]]. *"The triumphing of the wicked is short"*, and
  twenty-nine verses on how short: the ascent to the clouds and the fall to the dungheap, the
  swallowed riches vomited up, wrath raining on a man mid-meal, and heaven and earth called as
  witnesses for the prosecution.
- **Job 21:1–34** — [[Why Do the Wicked Live]]. Answered directly by Job's *"Why do the wicked live,
  become old, yes, and grow mighty in power?"*, the book's frontal rebuttal of everything Zophar has
  just said. Job's *"mock on"* (21:3) is singular, and Zophar's own charge of mocking (11:3) is the
  likeliest reason; his *"for a moment"* (*ʿădê-rāgaʿ*, 20:5) becomes the wicked man's *"in an
  instant"* (*bəregaʿ*, 21:13) descent to Sheol; and his children begging from the poor (20:10)
  become children dancing to harp and pipe (21:11–12). He never replies.
- **Job 22:1–30** — silent through [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]], which takes his third-person charge,
  *"he has oppressed and forsaken the poor"* (20:19), and makes it second-person and specific
  (22:6–9), and repeats his *"lift up your face"* (11:15) at 22:26. He does not speak again.
- **Job 23–24** — silent through [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]. On one textual hypothesis
  24:18–24, which sounds like the friends rather than Job, is a fragment of his missing third
  speech; the wiki records the hypothesis and reads the verses as Job's.
- **Job 25:1–6** — silent through [[Bildad's Third Speech]], where his turn should follow and does
  not. **He has no third speech**; whether that is design or damage (27:13–23 is the usual candidate
  for a lost Zophar speech) is recorded on [[Bildad's Third Speech]] and [[Bildad the Shuhite]].
- **Job 42:7–9** — included in *"your two friends"*, rebuked, and required to bring sacrifices and
  be prayed for by Job.

## Appears in

- [[Seven Days of Silence]] — Job 2:11–13. **The friend who will tell Job that God exacts less than
  his iniquity deserves (11:6) kept the same silent week as the other two**, having travelled
  further than either if the Minaean identification is anything at all. Nothing separates him from
  Eliphaz and Bildad until they start talking, and then he goes furthest.
- [[Eliphaz's First Speech]] — Job 4:1–5:27, silent, and inside the *"we"* of 5:27. The conditional
  promise Eliphaz makes at 5:19–26 is the one Zophar will repeat at 11:13–19, the last time any
  friend offers Job a future.
- [[The Arrows of the Almighty]] — Job 6:1–7:21, inside the plural of 6:21–29, and **the speech he
  will plunder.** At 20:8–9 Zophar describes the wicked man's end in three images taken from Job 7:
  the dream and the vision of the night (7:14), the eye that saw him and sees him no more (7:8), and
  the place that knows him no more (7:10). Job's lament for himself becomes Zophar's portrait of the
  wicked.
- [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]] — as part of Eliphaz's *"we"* and *"with us"*.
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — as one of the three answered in the plural.
- [[Zophar's First Speech]] — Job 11:1–20, his own and his first; harsher at the start and more
  generous at the end than either of the others' first speeches.
- [[Though He Slay Me]] — Job 12:1–14:22, the reply to his first speech, addressed to all three.
  **The first sentence is aimed at him**: *"No doubt, but you are the people, and wisdom shall die
  with you"* (12:2), after his *"secrets of wisdom"* (11:6). His four dimensions (11:8–9) are answered
  by Job's four creatures (12:7–8); his tents (11:14) by the robbers' tents that prosper (12:6); his
  wish that God would speak against Job (11:5) by Job's *"Will you show partiality to him?… He will
  surely reprove you"* (13:8–10), which 42:7 fulfils against Zophar too.
- [[Zophar's Second Speech]] — his own, and his last.
- [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] — the speech whose closing threat (19:28–29) provokes him, and
  whose plea for pity (19:21) he ignores.
- [[Bildad's Second Speech]] — the speech his own repeats in different imagery, with a matching
  closing formula.
- [[Bildad's First Speech]] — Job 8:1–22, silent; the speech his own first one (chapter 11) follows
  after Job's reply, with the same shape — a rebuke for Job's words, the claim that God's wisdom is
  beyond him, and a closing offer of restoration on condition of repentance.
- [[Why Do the Wicked Live]] — Job 21, the answer to his last speech, which he does not answer:
  his *short* triumph of the wicked (20:5) met by a long life, an easy death, and a well-attended
  funeral.
- [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] — Job 22, silent; his charge of 20:19 named and fabricated, and his
  promise of 11:15–19 repeated in its fullest form.
- [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]] — Job 23–24, silent; proposed by some reconstructions as
  the original speaker of 24:18–24, a reading the wiki records but does not adopt.
- [[Bildad's Third Speech]] — Job 25, silent; Bildad's six verses are the last the friends say, and
  the place in the cycle where Zophar's third speech would follow is empty.

## Related people

- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] and [[Bildad the Shuhite]] — the other two, whose doctrine he pushes

## Themes associated

- [[Retribution]] — carried to its limit: not only does suffering measure guilt, it *under*measures
  it.
- [[Wisdom]] — 11:7–9, *"Can you fathom the mystery of God? … deeper than Sheol"*, a genuinely fine
  passage on divine inscrutability, deployed to close down a question rather than to open one.
- [[Judgment]] — the whole of chapter 20, and specifically judgment as a stored inheritance:
  *"All darkness is laid up for his treasures"* (20:26), *"the heritage appointed to him by God"*
  (20:29).
- [[Suffering]] — as a receipt, and a discounted one.
- [[Sin]] — 20:12–15, sin as something eaten for the taste of it and then acting as poison in the
  body that swallowed it.
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — 20:6–7, the head in the clouds and the perishing *"like his own
  dung"*.
- [[Work]] — 20:18, labour whose fruit is confiscated before it can be enjoyed.

## Notes

- **The name.** *Ṣōpar*, of uncertain meaning; often linked to *ṣippôr*, *bird*, or to a root
  meaning *to chirp* or *to leap*. Nothing in the book plays on it.
- **His best passage, and its use.** Job 11:7–9 — *"Can you fathom the mystery of God? Or can you
  probe the limits of the Almighty? They are high as heaven. What can you do? They are deeper than
  Sheol. What can you know?"* — is close in substance to what God himself will say out of the
  whirlwind, and is worth marking as the wiki's cleanest example of the friends' problem. Zophar's
  premise is correct and God will confirm it; his inference from it is that Job should stop
  talking, and God's inference from it is that Job should keep listening. The same doctrine of
  divine inscrutability functions in Zophar's mouth as a gag and in God's mouth as an invitation.
  With chapter 11 ingested the overlap is visible in the vocabulary: *ḥēqer*, the searched-out depth
  (11:7; 38:16), and the deep, Sheol, and the breadth of the earth (11:8–9; 38:16–18). See
  [[Higher Than Heaven, Deeper Than Sheol - The Measure of the Unsearchable]].
