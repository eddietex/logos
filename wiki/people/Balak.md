# Balak

## Overview

### The hire remembered without his name — Deuteronomy 23:4

Deuteronomy 23:4 excludes Ammon and Moab together *"because they hired against you Balaam"*. Balak, the
king of Moab who sent for Balaam (Numbers 22:2–6), is not named, and Ammon, which has no part in Numbers
22, is joined to the hire. The text does not explain the addition.

See [[The Assembly and the Holy Camp]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 23:1–14; Numbers 22.

### Balaam’s last oracles — Numbers 23:27–24:25

After a third sacrificial station at Peor, he angrily dismisses Balaam and attributes the loss of
promised honor to Yahweh (24:10–11). His attempted curse has produced three blessings; the final
oracle now predicts harm to his own people.

See [[Balaam's Last Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 23:27–24:25.


### The first two oracles — Numbers 22:36–23:26

He receives Balaam at the border, supplies sacrifices, and moves him to another viewpoint after the
first blessing. The second oracle produces a demand for neither curse nor blessing (23:25); royal
patronage cannot control Yahweh’s word.

See [[Balaam's First Two Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:36–23:26.


### Balaam’s donkey — Numbers 22:22–35

Balak does not appear in person in this scene. His princes accompany Balaam as the journey resumes under the angel’s restriction (35); the king’s summons has not given Balaam control over what he may say.

See [[Balaam's Donkey]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:22–35.

Son of [[Zippor]] and king of Moab at this time (2, 4). Fear after Israel’s victories leads him to
seek a curse before attempting military expulsion. He sends a second, more distinguished embassy
when the first returns unsuccessful; his offer of honor cannot determine God’s instruction.
