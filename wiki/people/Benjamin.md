# Benjamin

## Overview

**The twelfth son, the last, the only one born in the land, and the only one his father names.**

He arrives in a single verse that also kills his mother, and he is named twice in it:

> **18** As her soul was departing (for she died), she named him Benoni, but his father named him
> Benjamin. (35:18)

**This is the one naming in Genesis that is overruled.** Eleven of his brothers were named by women —
[[Leah]], [[Rachel]], and, for the slaves' four sons, by the mistresses who did not bear them — and
none of those names is contested by anybody. **The twelfth is named by a dying woman and renamed
over her body by [[Jacob]], in the same verse, with no reason given.** See [[Names and Naming]].

***Ben-ʾônî*** **means two things and Genesis glosses neither.** ***ʾÔn*** is **vigour, virility,
strength** — it is the noun in *"Reuben, you are my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my
**strength**"* (49:3). From a homonymous root, *ʾāwen* is **trouble, sorrow, iniquity**, which is the
sense almost every translation takes here. **So his mother's name for him is either *son of my
strength* or *son of my sorrow*** — a boast or a lament, spoken in the same breath — **and the man who
replaces it is the man who will use the other sense about [[Reuben]] fourteen chapters later.**

***Bin-yāmîn*** **means *son of the right hand*, or *son of the south*** — *yāmîn* is the ordinary
Hebrew for both, because a Hebrew speaker orients facing east and the south is on the right. The
right hand is the side of favour and of strength (Psalm 110:1; Genesis 48:13–18, where Jacob crosses
his hands precisely because the right one matters). **Whether Jacob meant honour or a compass point,
the tribe's territory is in fact the southernmost strip of the northern kingdom**, and the name fits
the map as well as the sentiment.

**And the birthplace is contradicted seven verses later.** 35:16–19 has him born on the road to
Ephrath, in Canaan; 35:26 closes the roster with *"These are the sons of Jacob, who were born to him
in Paddan Aram."* **He is the one exception to a summary that does not admit one.** The wiki flags it
and adopts none of the three available readings; see
[[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 35:1–29

He is born, named, renamed, and listed. **That is his entire presence in Genesis so far**, and in all
of it he is the object of every verb — *she named*, *his father named*, and then a name in a list at
35:24, second of [[Rachel]]'s two sons and last of the twelve.

**He never meets his mother.** [[Rachel]] dies as he is being born, which makes him the only one of
the twelve with no mother in the narrative, and — Genesis will make a great deal of this later — **the
only full brother [[Joseph]] has.**

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

He becomes the fulcrum of the Joseph story without doing anything. [[Jacob]] will not let him go down
to Egypt — *"if harm should happen to him… you would bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to Sheol"*
(42:38) — and the entire second half of the Joseph narrative is a test built around him: the demand
that he be produced (42:20), [[Reuben]]'s absurd offer of his own two sons as surety (42:37),
[[Judah]]'s pledge of himself instead (43:9), the portion five times everyone else's at Joseph's
table (43:34), the silver cup planted in his sack (44:2), and Judah's speech offering to be enslaved
in his place (44:18–34) — **the speech that breaks Joseph and ends the concealment.**

At the reunion, *"he fell on his brother Benjamin's neck and wept, and Benjamin wept on his neck"*
(45:14) — **in the verbs of 33:4**; see
[[Fell on His Neck and Wept - The Running Embrace in Genesis and Luke]].

**Genesis records no word he ever speaks.** He is talked about, bargained for, wept over, and given
five portions, and the book never once quotes him.

Jacob's deathbed blessing makes him a predator: *"Benjamin is a ravenous wolf. In the morning he will
devour the prey. At evening he will divide the plunder"* (49:27) — **the shortest and most violent of
the twelve**, and the tribe lives up to it. Ehud the left-handed judge is a Benjaminite (Judges 3:15),
as are the seven hundred left-handed slingers who *"could sling a stone at a hair and not miss"*
(Judges 20:16) — **left-handed men from the tribe of the right hand.** The tribe is nearly
exterminated at Gibeah (Judges 19–21), a story told in the vocabulary of Sodom; see
[[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]]. It produces Israel's first king, Saul (1 Samuel 9:1–2),
and, a thousand years later, *"of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews"* (Philippians 3:5) —
Paul, who carries Saul's name.

## Related people

- [[Rachel]] — his mother, who dies bearing him and names him with her last breath; he is the *another
  son* she asked for when she named [[Joseph]] (30:24)
- [[Jacob]] — his father, the only one of the twelve to name a son, and the man who will refuse to let
  him out of his sight
- [[Joseph]] — his only full brother, and the one whose test the whole second half of Genesis builds
  around him
- [[Reuben]], [[Simeon]], [[Levi]], [[Judah]], [[Dan (person)]], [[Naphtali]], [[Gad]], [[Asher]],
  [[Issachar]], [[Zebulun]] — his half-brothers, ten of the eleven born in Paddan Aram
- [[Leah]], [[Bilhah]], [[Zilpah]] — his father's other three wives, and the mothers of ten of his
  brothers
- [[Dinah]] — his half-sister, not counted in the roster he closes
