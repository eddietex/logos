# Benjamin

## Overview

### The first sign at Rachel's tomb — 1 Samuel 10:2

Samuel sends the newly anointed Saul to *Rachel's tomb, on the border of Benjamin at Zelzah* (10:2), *biḡḇûl
binyāmin* (WLC). The grave of the mother who died bearing Benjamin (Genesis 35:16–19) marks the tribe's border, and
the first of the Benjamite king's three signs is given there. Genesis puts the grave *on the way to Ephrath (also
called Bethlehem)* (35:19), in Judah. The disagreement is recorded in
[[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]] and not resolved. See [[Samuel Anoints Saul]] and
[[Rachel's Tomb]].

Source: stored WEB Genesis 35:16–19; 1 Samuel 10:2. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The smallest of the tribes supplies the king — 1 Samuel 9:1–21

*There was a man of Benjamin, whose name was Kish* (9:1): the opening words of the Benjamite runner of 4:12 return
for Saul's father. In the WLC the ketiv divides the tribal name, *mbn ymyn*, and the qere reads *mibbinyāmîn*; Kish's
ancestor is *ʾîš yəmînî*, *a Benjamite*, and Saul calls himself *ben-yəmînî* (9:1, 21, WLC). Yahweh tells Samuel he
will send *a man out of the land of Benjamin* to be anointed prince (9:16), and Saul objects that he is *of the
smallest of the tribes of Israel*, his family *the least of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin* (9:21). The
smallness has a history: the tribe was reduced to six hundred men in Judges 20 and rebuilt by the wives of Judges
21. Benjamin, the youngest son, was seated *according to his youth* at Joseph's table and given *five times as much*
(Genesis 43:33–34); Saul, of the least family, is seated at the head of the guests and given the reserved portion
(9:22–24). See [[Saul Comes to the Seer]], [[Saul]], and
[[The Least in My Father's House - Judges 6.15 and 1 Samuel 9.21]].

Source: stored WEB Genesis 43:33–34; Judges 20–21; 1 Samuel 4:12; 9:1–24. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### *A man of Benjamin* runs to Shiloh — 1 Samuel 4:12

The runner who brings the news of the ark's capture to Shiloh is *a man of Benjamin*, *ʾîš-binyāmin* (4:12,
WLC), with torn clothes and dirt on his head; see [[Man of Benjamin (of Eli's time)]]. The same words
introduce Saul's father Kish at 9:1 (stored WEB; not yet ingested). In the same chapter, Phinehas' wife dies
in childbirth naming her son for her grief, as Rachel did when she named Benjamin *Benoni* (Genesis
35:17–18); see [[Do Not Be Afraid, You Have a Son - Rachel and Phinehas' Wife]]. See
[[The Ark Is Captured]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 4:12, 19–22; 9:1; Genesis 35:17–18. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### Wives for the six hundred — Judges 21:1–25

The war leaves Benjamin six hundred men at the rock of Rimmon, and Israel has sworn at Mizpah, *None of us
will give his daughter to Benjamin as a wife* (21:1). The tribes weep at Bethel, *why has this happened in
Israel, that there should be one tribe lacking in Israel today?* (21:3), and *grieved for Benjamin their
brother* (21:6). They proclaim peace to the six hundred (21:13), give them the four hundred virgins spared
at Jabesh Gilead (21:12–14), and, when *there still weren't enough* (21:14), tell them to lie in wait at
Shiloh's yearly feast and *catch* the dancing daughters (21:19–21). The stated aim is the tribe's
survival: *There must be an inheritance for those who are escaped of Benjamin, that a tribe not be blotted
out from Israel* (21:17), in the words of the levirate law (Deuteronomy 25:6, WLC). Benjamin returns to its
inheritance and rebuilds the burned cities (21:23). In 1 Samuel the tribe gives Israel its first
king, Saul of Gibeah, whose first act as king is to save Jabesh Gilead (1 Samuel 11, WEB checked at
bible-api.com; not yet ingested). See [[Wives for Benjamin]] and
[[Jabesh Gilead and Benjamin - Judges 21, 1 Samuel 11, and 1 Samuel 31]]. Source: stored WEB Judges
21:1–23; Deuteronomy 25:6; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### The war against Benjamin — Judges 20:1–48

Asked to *deliver up the men, the wicked fellows, who are in Gibeah, … Benjamin would not listen to the
voice of their brothers the children of Israel* (20:13). The tribe gathers at Gibeah: 26,000 swordsmen and
700 of Gibeah, with 700 left-handed slingers who *could sling a stone at a hair and not miss* (15–16), the
phrase used of Ehud (3:15). Twice it defeats all Israel, killing 22,000 and 18,000 (21, 25). Israel calls it
*Benjamin my brother* (23, 28). On the third day *Yahweh struck Benjamin before Israel* (35): Gibeah is
burned, 25,000 fall, every town is struck *and the livestock*, and six hundred men hold out four months at
[[The Rock of Rimmon]] (46–48). The ancestor's blessing called Benjamin *a ravenous wolf* that *at evening
… will divide the plunder* (Genesis 49:27); here Benjamin is the plunder. The wiki notes the reversal; the
text does not draw it. See [[The War Against Benjamin]] and
[[Left-Handed Men of Benjamin - Judges 3.15 and 20.16]]. Source: stored WEB Genesis 49:27; Judges 3:15;
20:13–48.

### *The men of the place were Benjamites* — Judges 19:14–25

The Levite reaches *Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin* at sunset (19:14). No one takes him in; his host is
an Ephraimite sojourner, *but the men of the place were Benjamites*, *bənê yəmînî* (19:16, WLC). The men of
the city, *certain wicked fellows*, surround the house and abuse the Levite's concubine through the night
(19:22–25). The tribe whose name means *son of the right hand* is the setting of the book's Sodom. The war
that follows is Benjamin's near-extinction (see [[The War Against Benjamin]]; chapter 21 not yet ingested). See [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]],
[[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], and [[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]]. Source: stored WEB Judges
19:14–25; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### Ammon crosses the Jordan — Judges 10:9

*The children of Ammon passed over the Jordan to fight also against Judah, and against Benjamin, and against the house of Ephraim, so that Israel was very distressed* (9). The oppression begins in Gilead and reaches Benjamin west of the river.

See [[Israel's Repentance Under Ammon]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 10:6–18.

### *After you, Benjamin* — Judges 5:14

*Came out of Ephraim, after you, Benjamin, among your peoples* (5:14). Benjamin is among the tribes
that came. The Song praises the tribe that Judges 20–21 will nearly destroy. See
[[The Song of Deborah]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 5:14.

### Ehud the Benjamite — Judges 3:12–30

The second savior is *Ehud the son of Gera, the Benjamite, a left-handed man* (15). The Hebrew,
*ben-hayəmînî ʾîš ʾiṭṭēr yad-yəmînô* (WLC), sets the tribe's name, *son of the right hand*, beside a
man *restricted in his right hand*. *Gera* is one of Benjamin's sons in Genesis 46:21. The same phrase
returns for Benjamin's seven hundred left-handed slingers in the war against the other tribes
(20:16). See [[Left-Handed Men of Benjamin - Judges 3.15 and 20.16]].

See [[Ehud and Eglon]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 3:12–30; 20:16; Genesis 46:21; Hebrew WLC
(OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-13.

### The Jebusites not driven out — Judges 1:21

The tribe's first appearance in Judges is a failure: *The children of Benjamin did not drive out the
Jebusites who inhabited Jerusalem, but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem
to this day* (21). It is Joshua 15:63 with Benjamin's name for Judah's, matching Benjamin's allotment
of Jerusalem in Joshua 18:28. Joshua 15:63 says *couldn't* (*lōʾ yāḵəlû*); Judges 1:21 says *did not*
(*lōʾ hôrîšû*), without the verb of ability (WLC). It is also the first *did not drive out* in the
chapter's list of tribal failures, and Benjamin, first to fail here, is the tribe Israel nearly
destroys at the book's end.

See [[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 1:8–21; comparisons as cited.

### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

Genesis 46:21 lists Ard and Naaman among Benjamin’s sons; Numbers 26:40 places them
under Bela. Both genealogical presentations are retained without supplying an unstated
harmonization.

The tribe bearing his name has 45,600 men in the second military census (Numbers 26). The register
names ancestral families, not a new action by the patriarch.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


**The twelfth son, the last, the only one born in the land, and the only one his father names.**

He arrives in a single verse that also kills his mother, and he is named twice in it:

> **18** As her soul was departing (for she died), she named him Benoni, but his father named him
> Benjamin. (35:18)

**This is the one naming in Genesis that is overruled.** Eleven of his brothers were named by women —
[[Leah]], [[Rachel]], and, for the slaves' four sons, by the mistresses who did not bear them — and
none of those names is contested by anybody. **The twelfth is named by a dying woman and renamed
over her body by [[Jacob]], in the same verse, with no reason given.** See [[Names and Naming]].

***Ben-ʾônî*** **means two things and Genesis glosses neither.** ***ʾÔn*** is **vigour, virility,
strength** — it is the noun in *"Reuben, you are my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my
**strength**"* (49:3). From a homonymous root, *ʾāwen* is **trouble, sorrow, iniquity**, which is the
sense almost every translation takes here. **So his mother's name for him is either *son of my
strength* or *son of my sorrow*** — a boast or a lament, spoken in the same breath — **and the man who
replaces it is the man who will use the other sense about [[Reuben]] fourteen chapters later.**

***Bin-yāmîn*** **means *son of the right hand*, or *son of the south*** — *yāmîn* is the ordinary
Hebrew for both, because a Hebrew speaker orients facing east and the south is on the right. The
right hand is the side of favour and of strength (Psalm 110:1; Genesis 48:13–18, where Jacob crosses
his hands precisely because the right one matters). **Whether Jacob meant honour or a compass point,
the tribe's territory is in fact the southernmost strip of the northern kingdom**, and the name fits
the map as well as the sentiment.

**And the birthplace is contradicted seven verses later.** 35:16–19 has him born on the road to
Ephrath, in Canaan; 35:26 closes the roster with *"These are the sons of Jacob, who were born to him
in Paddan Aram."* **He is the one exception to a summary that does not admit one.** The wiki flags it
and adopts none of the three available readings; see
[[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 35:1–29

He is born, named, renamed, and listed. **That is his entire presence in Genesis so far**, and in all
of it he is the object of every verb — *she named*, *his father named*, and then a name in a list at
35:24, second of [[Rachel]]'s two sons and last of the twelve.

**He never meets his mother.** [[Rachel]] dies as he is being born, which makes him the only one of
the twelve with no mother in the narrative, and — Genesis will make a great deal of this later — **the
only full brother [[Joseph]] has.**

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

He becomes the fulcrum of the Joseph story without doing anything. [[Jacob]] will not let him go down
to Egypt — *"if harm should happen to him… you would bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to Sheol"*
(42:38) — and the entire second half of the Joseph narrative is a test built around him: the demand
that he be produced (42:20), [[Reuben]]'s absurd offer of his own two sons as surety (42:37),
[[Judah]]'s pledge of himself instead (43:9, see [[The Second Journey]]), the portion five times
everyone else's at Joseph's
table (43:34), the silver cup planted in his sack ([[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]), and Judah's
speech offering to be enslaved in his place — **the speech that breaks Joseph and ends the
concealment.**

**Genesis records no word he ever speaks.** He is talked about, bargained for, wept over, embraced,
and given five portions and then five garments, and the book never once quotes him — see
[[Joseph Reveals Himself]].

### In this pericope — Genesis 49:27

> **27** “Benjamin is a ravenous wolf. In the morning he will devour the prey. At evening he will
> divide the plunder.” (49:27)

Jacob's deathbed blessing makes him a predator — **the shortest of the twelve sayings, the most violent
of them, and the last word of the poem.**

**The contrast with everything the book has said about him is total.** **He is the child [[Jacob]]
would not send, the one [[Judah]] stood surety for, the one whose sack the cup was planted in** — **a
possession to be protected for eleven chapters, and never once quoted.** **The last thing his father
says about him is *plunder*.**

**Nothing in Genesis prepares it and the rest of Scripture obliges it.** Ehud, Gibeah, Saul, and the
man who was *"ravaging the assembly"* before Damascus (Acts 8:3) are all Benjamites. **Moses' blessing
says the opposite** — *"the beloved of Yahweh will dwell in safety by him"* (Deuteronomy 33:12) —
**which is the sharpest single disagreement between the two tribal poems after [[Levi]]'s.** See
[[Jacob's Blessing and the Blessing of Moses - Genesis 49 and Deuteronomy 33]].

### What is still ahead (continued)

Ehud the left-handed judge is a Benjaminite (Judges 3:15),
as are the seven hundred left-handed slingers who *"could sling a stone at a hair and not miss"*
(Judges 20:16) — **left-handed men from the tribe of the right hand.** The tribe is nearly
exterminated at Gibeah (Judges 19–21), a story told in the vocabulary of Sodom; see
[[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]]. It produces Israel's first king, Saul (1 Samuel 9:1–2),
and, a thousand years later, *"of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews"* (Philippians 3:5) —
Paul, who carries Saul's name.
