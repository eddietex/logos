# Othniel

## Overview

### The first judge — Judges 3:7–11

*Yahweh raised up a savior to the children of Israel, who saved them, even Othniel the son of Kenaz,
Caleb's younger brother* (9). He is the first judge the book names and the only one whose story is
nothing but the cycle of 2:11–19: Israel sins, is sold to [[Cushan Rishathaim]] for eight years, and
cries out; Yahweh raises him up; *Yahweh's Spirit came on him, and he judged Israel; and he went out to
war* (10); *his hand prevailed*; *the land had rest forty years, then Othniel the son of Kenaz died*
(11). He speaks no word in the pericope, and no fault is recorded of him.

He is titled both ways the book uses: *a savior*, *môšîaʿ* (9), and one who *judged Israel*,
*wayyišpōṭ* (10, WLC). He is the only judge the book has already shown in action before his own story
begins: the reader knows him from Kiriath Sepher (1:13). The phrase *Caleb's younger brother* is the same Hebrew as 1:13,
and the uncle/nephew question below stands unchanged. The Spirit clause, *wattəhî ʿālāyw rûaḥ YHWH*,
is the one used of Balaam in Numbers 24:2 (WLC); see
[[The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah]].

See [[Othniel the First Judge]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 3:7–11; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-13.

### Caleb's younger brother — Judges 1:11–13

Judges repeats the capture of Kiriath Sepher and the marriage to Achsah, adding one phrase: Othniel
the son of Kenaz, *Caleb's younger brother* (13). Joshua 15:17 has only *ʾăḥî kālēb*; Judges 1:13 adds
*haqqāṭōn mimmennû*, *the younger than he* (WLC). The WEB attaches *younger* to Othniel, as it does at
3:9. The Hebrew could also be read as describing Kenaz, which would make Othniel Caleb's nephew; the
wiki does not decide. Two chapters later he is the first judge, *Yahweh raised up a savior … even
Othniel the son of Kenaz* (3:9, stored WEB).

See [[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 1:8–21; comparisons as cited.

Son of Kenaz who captures Kiriath Sepher (Debir) and receives Caleb’s daughter Achsah as wife (17). Verse 18 presents Achsah as having him ask her father for a field. The WEB places the brother-of-Caleb description beside his patronymic; this page does not settle the family tree beyond the translated wording.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:13–19.
