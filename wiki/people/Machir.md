# Machir

## Overview

### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

Half his descendants receive half Gilead, Ashtaroth, and Edrei (31). The verse identifies him as Manasseh’s son; the recipients are families, not an account of the ancestor himself campaigning.

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

Moses recalls giving Gilead to Machir (15), within the distribution between Reuben, Gad, and
Manasseh (12–16). Numbers 32:39–40 identifies the conquering children of Machir; the abbreviated
grant does not require the ancestral individual to be present.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### Marriage and tribal inheritance — Numbers 36:1–13

Named as Gilead's father and Manasseh's son in the petitioning clan's ancestry (1). The family
heads act; the ancestor is not said to be present.

See [[The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 36; comparisons as cited.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

*The children of Machir the son of Manasseh went to Gilead, took it, and dispossessed the Amorites who
were therein. Moses gave Gilead to Machir the son of Manasseh; and he lived therein* (39–40). The clan of
the last person named in Genesis takes and receives the region by conquest — the verse already quoted on
this page under *What becomes of him*, now ingested.

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### The daughters’ inheritance — Numbers 27:1–11

Numbers 27:1 places Machir between Manasseh and Gilead in the petitioners' ancestry. His role here
is genealogical, not as a living participant in the hearing.

See [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:1–11.


### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

Named as Manasseh’s son and Gilead’s father (29), linking the family remembered in Genesis 50:23 to
the new census.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


**The last person named in Genesis.**

> **23** Joseph saw Ephraim's children to the third generation. The children also of Machir, the son
> of Manasseh, were born on Joseph's knees. (50:23)

**[[Manasseh]]'s son and [[Joseph]]'s grandson**, named once in the book and never given a word, an
act, or a description. **What Genesis records about him is that his children were held by his
grandfather**, and then the book ends three verses later.

**The name means roughly *sold* or *bought*** — the root *mākar*, **which is the verb Genesis used
of his grandfather at 37:28**, *"they sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver"*,
and which the brothers repeat back at 45:4–5. **The wiki notes the coincidence and does not build
on it**: Genesis supplies no etymology for this name, as it does for [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]]
one chapter earlier (41:51–52), and a name-meaning the text does not gloss is not the text's point.
See [[Names and Naming]].

***"Born on Joseph's knees."*** ***ʿAl-birkê yōsēp*** — **the adoption idiom, and Genesis uses it
exactly twice**: here, and at 30:3, where [[Rachel]] gives [[Bilhah]] to [[Jacob]] *"that she may
bear on my knees, and I also may obtain children by her"* (in [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]]).
**Whether the gesture at 50:23 is legal adoption or a great-grandfather's lap, Genesis does not
say**, and the two uses are far enough apart in tone that the wiki reports the idiom rather than
deciding what it does here. See [[Adoption]].

## What becomes of him

**Genesis leaves him an infant's father in Egypt. The rest of the Torah makes him a clan and then a
territory**, and none of it is in this book:

- **Numbers 26:29 names Machir in Manasseh’s line**, and the register traces the listed families
  through Gilead. It does not explicitly call Machir Manasseh’s only son.
- **Numbers 32:39–40** — *"The children of Machir the son of Manasseh went to Gilead, took it, and
  dispossessed the Amorites who were therein"* — **and Moses gives them Gilead**
- **Joshua 17:1** calls him *"the firstborn of Manasseh, the father of Gilead, because he was a man
  of war"*
- **Judges 5:14**, in the Song of Deborah, has *"out of Machir governors came down"* — **the clan
  name standing in for the tribe two centuries later**

**The last name in a book about a promised land belongs to the family that settles east of the
Jordan, outside it.** **Genesis does not know that and the wiki records the irony as an irony**, not
as anything the chapter is doing.
