# Eve

## Overview

The first woman. She is not called Eve in [[The Garden of Eden]] — throughout Genesis 2 she is
*ʾishshâ*, "woman", and she receives the name Eve (Hebrew *Ḥawwâ*, glossed in the text as
"living", *"because she was the mother of all living"*) only at Genesis 3:20, after the fall.

Her creation answers the Bible's first *"not good"*: *"It is not good for the man to be alone."*
God's provision is described as an *ʿēzer kenegdô* — a helper corresponding to him. Neither word
implies subordination: *ʿēzer* is used far more often of God helping Israel than of any human
helping another, and *kenegdô* means "like opposite him", a counterpart who matches. The animals
are paraded past the man first and none of them qualifies, which is the narrative's way of
establishing that she is of a different order entirely from every other creature.

She is built (*bānâ*) from the man's *ṣēlāʿ* — traditionally "rib", but the word means "side"
everywhere else in the Hebrew Bible, most often the side of the tabernacle. The man's reaction is
recognition, not naming: *"This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh."* From that the
narrator draws the union of 2:24, one flesh — a return to the single body she was taken from.

Genesis 2 gives her no words. Her first recorded speech is her conversation with the serpent in
Genesis 3, and the New Testament's two direct references to her (2 Corinthians 11:3, 1 Timothy
2:13–14) both look at that scene rather than this one.
