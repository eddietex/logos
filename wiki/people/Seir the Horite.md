# Seir the Horite

## Overview

**The eponym of the country Esau settles in, and the father of the people Esau's descendants
displaced — and Genesis gives him eleven verses of genealogy, which it gives to no other dispossessed
population anywhere.**

> **20** These are the sons of Seir the Horite, the inhabitants of the land: Lotan, Shobal, Zibeon,
> Anah,
> **21** Dishon, Ezer, and Dishan. These are the chiefs who came of the Horites, the children of Seir
> in the land of Edom.

***Yōšəbê hā-ʾāreṣ***, **the inhabitants of the land**, is the standing biblical phrase for a
population about to be replaced — it is what the Canaanites are called in the conquest texts. **The
label is applied here and then the register runs anyway**: seven sons, some twenty grandchildren, and
seven chiefs, in a chapter whose subject is the family that took their country. See
[[The Descendants of Esau]].

## The name before the man

**Genesis derived *Seir* from a baby's appearance before it ever mentioned this man.** At 25:25 Esau
is born *"red all over, like a hairy garment"* — ***śēʿār***, **hair** — and the wiki's page on
[[Esau]] notes that the verse seeds two nations and explains neither of the man's own names. **So the
book has two accounts of where *Seir* comes from**: a pun on Esau's body at 25:25, and a Horite
ancestor at 36:20, who would have carried the name generations earlier. **Genesis states both and
notices neither**, which is what it does with Beersheba as well (21:31; 26:33).

**Which way the naming actually ran is not recoverable.** *Śēʿîr* is a plausible descriptive name for
the country on its own — *shaggy*, of scrub-covered hills — and an ancestor named for his territory is
as common in tribal genealogy as a territory named for an ancestor.

## The Horites

**Who they were is unsettled**, and the two standard answers point in different directions.

- ***Ḥōr*, **hole** or **cave** — hence the old rendering **cave-dwellers**, and a long tradition of
  imagining them in the rock-cut dwellings the Nabateans later made famous at Petra.
- **The Hurrians**, a non-Semitic people well attested across the second-millennium Near East. **The
  personal names in this list are not obviously Semitic**, which is the strongest argument for it —
  but the Hurrian heartland is in the far north, and nothing outside the Bible puts them in the
  Arabah.

## What happened to them

**Deuteronomy supplies what Genesis withholds, twice.**

> The Horites also lived in Seir in the past, but the children of Esau succeeded them. They destroyed
> them from before them, and lived in their place, as Israel did to the land of his possession.
> *(Deuteronomy 2:12)*

**The comparison is Deuteronomy's own** — *as Israel did* — and it is made in the middle of the same
speech that forbids Israel to take a single foot of Seir, *"because I have given Mount Seir to Esau
for a possession"* (2:5). **A conquest is used as the precedent for a land grant that forbids another
conquest.** Deuteronomy 2:22 repeats it. See [[Edom]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].

**Genesis reports none of that.** What Genesis reports is two marriages: [[Oholibamah]], granddaughter
of Seir's son Zibeon, becomes Esau's third wife, and [[Timna]], the sister of Seir's firstborn Lotan,
becomes the concubine of Esau's firstborn and the mother of [[Amalek]]. **The two accounts are not
incompatible — displacement and intermarriage routinely happen together — but the book of Genesis
records only the second, and it records it without comment.**

## Timeline / key events

- Father of Lotan, Shobal, Zibeon, [[Anah]], Dishon, Ezer, and Dishan; *the inhabitants of the land*
  — Genesis 36:20–21
- His sons and grandsons listed, including the Uz of 36:28 — Genesis 36:22–28
- Seven chiefs of the Horites, *"according to their chiefs in the land of Seir"* — Genesis 36:29–30
- His descendants dispossessed by the children of Esau — Deuteronomy 2:12, 22
- Copied by the Chronicler — 1 Chronicles 1:38–42
