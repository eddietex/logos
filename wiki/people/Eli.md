# Eli

## Overview

### The priest on his seat at Shiloh — 1 Samuel 1:3, 9–17, 25–28

Eli is introduced through his sons: *The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, priests to Yahweh, were
there* (1:3). He is first seen *sitting on his seat by the doorpost of Yahweh's temple* (1:9). The seat is
a *kissēʾ*, the ordinary word for *throne* (WLC); in the WLC of 1 Samuel 1–4 it occurs at 1:9, at 2:8
(*the throne of glory*), and at 4:13, 18, where Eli, sitting on it, hears the ark is taken, falls
backward, and dies (not yet ingested). His genealogy is not given here, and the text does not say how he
is related to Aaron.

He watches [[Hannah]] praying silently, *Eli saw her mouth*, and takes her for drunk: *How long will you
be drunk? Get rid of your wine!* (1:12–14). When she explains, he answers at once with a blessing, *Go in
peace; and may the God of Israel grant your petition that you have asked of him* (1:17), without asking
what the petition is. When she returns with the weaned child, she gives the boy to him (1:25–28). The
chapter shows a priest who misreads prayer and then blesses it; the next shows him unable to restrain his
sons (2:12–36, not yet ingested). The pairing is the narrative's, made by placement. See
[[Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son]].

**Name.** *ʿĒlî* (WLC) is usually glossed *ascended* or *my height*; conventional gloss, not verified
here.

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 1:3, 9–17, 25–28; 2:8, 12; 4:13, 18. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-17.

**The child before him.** After Hannah's song, *the child served Yahweh before Eli the priest* (2:11). The
song itself, sung within his hearing at Shiloh, praises the God who makes the poor *inherit the throne of
glory* (2:8); *kissēʾ* is the word for Eli's own *seat* (1:9; 4:18). The pairing is a reader's
observation. See [[The Song of Hannah]].

### The father who heard — 1 Samuel 2:20–25

Eli blesses [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]] and [[Hannah]] at the yearly sacrifice: *May Yahweh give you
offspring from this woman for the petition which was asked of Yahweh* (2:20), and Yahweh gives five more
children (2:21). His second blessing of Hannah is answered as his first was. Meanwhile his sons, *wicked
men* who *didn't know Yahweh* (2:12), seize the sacrificial meat and lie with the women who served at the
door of the Tent of Meeting (2:13–17, 22). Eli is *very old* (2:22; ninety-eight at 4:15, stored WEB; not
yet ingested). He *heard*, and rebukes them: *If one man sins against another, God will judge him; but if
a man sins against Yahweh, who will intercede for him?* (2:25). They do not listen, *because Yahweh
intended to kill them* (2:25). His rebuke is the only act the pericope gives him; the next charges him
with honouring his sons above Yahweh (2:29), and 3:13 that *he didn't restrain them* (stored WEB; not yet
ingested). The law of the rebellious son (Deuteronomy 21:18–21) prescribes a further step he does not
take; the comparison is a reader's. See [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] and
[[Did Not Listen to the Voice of Their Father - Deuteronomy 21.18 and 1 Samuel 2.25]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 2:12–25, 29; 3:13; 4:15; Deuteronomy 21:18–21. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The man of God's sentence — 1 Samuel 2:27–36

An unnamed [[Man of God (of Eli's time)]] comes to Eli with Yahweh's word. It reminds him that Yahweh chose
*the house of your father* in Egypt to be his priests and gave it the offerings (2:27–28), then charges
him: *Why do you kick at my sacrifice … and honor your sons above me, to make yourselves fat with the best
of all the offerings of Israel my people?* (2:29). The charge turns from *you* plural to *you* singular and
back (WLC: *tiḇʿăṭû* 2mp, *wattəḵabbēḏ* 2ms, *ləhaḇrîʾăḵem* 2mp), so Eli is counted among those fattened.
Yahweh revokes the promise that his house would *walk before me forever*: *those who honor me I will honor,
and those who despise me will be cursed* (2:30). No one in his house will grow old; one man left at the
altar will grieve him; his two sons will die *in one day*; and Yahweh will raise *a faithful priest* with
*a sure house* (2:31–35). *Honor* is *kāḇēḏ*, *be heavy*; Eli will die *an old man, and heavy*, *kāḇēḏ*
(4:18, WLC; stored WEB; not yet ingested). His response is not recorded here. 1 Samuel gives him no
genealogy; the descent from [[Ithamar]] is a reconstruction from 1 Chronicles 24:3 with 1 Samuel 14:3 and
22:20, reported, not stated. The word against his house is said to be fulfilled when Solomon removes
Abiathar (1 Kings 2:27, WEB checked at bible-api.com). See [[A Man of God Against the House of Eli]] and
[[The Word Against the House of Eli - 1 Samuel 2.27-36 and 1 Kings 2.27]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 2:27–36; 4:18; 14:3; 22:20. 1 Kings 2:27 and 1 Chronicles 24:3 WEB checked at
bible-api.com, 2026-11-17. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The boy hears what Eli had heard — 1 Samuel 3:1–18

Eli lies *in his place*, his eyes *beginning to grow dim, so that he could not see* (3:2); *kēhôṯ*, *dim*, is
the root of Isaac's failing eyes (Genesis 27:1) and of Moses' that were *not dim* (Deuteronomy 34:7, WLC).
Three times the boy runs to him, and twice he sends him back; *the third time … Eli perceived that Yahweh
had called the child* and teaches him the answer, *Speak, Yahweh; for your servant hears* (3:5–9). The word
that follows is against Eli himself: Yahweh will judge his house *for the iniquity which he knew, because
his sons brought a curse on themselves, and he didn't restrain them*, and has sworn that its iniquity
*shall not be removed with sacrifice or offering forever* (3:13–14). *Kihâ*, *restrain* (3:13), shares its
consonants with *kēhôṯ*, *dim* (3:2); morphhb lemmatizes them as homonyms (3543 b, 3544), and whether the
echo is intended is not recoverable. Eli rebuked his sons (2:23–25); this verse judges the rebuke
insufficient. In the morning Eli calls *Samuel, my son*, lays an oath on him (*God do so to you, and more
also*), hears every word, and says *It is Yahweh. Let him do what seems good to him* (3:16–18). The text
records the sentence without comment; readers take it as submission or as resignation. See
[[Yahweh Calls Samuel]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 2:23–25; 3:1–18; Genesis 27:1; Deuteronomy 34:7. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### On his seat by the road — 1 Samuel 4:12–18

When the ark is taken to the battle at [[Ebenezer]], Eli waits *sitting on his seat by the road watching;
for his heart trembled for God's ark* (4:13). He is ninety-eight, and *his eyes were set, so that he could
not see* (15): the dimming of 3:2 is complete, and he can only hear. He hears the city cry out and asks *What
does the noise of this tumult mean?* (14), the Philistines' question about Israel's shout in the same form
(4:6, *meh qôl*, WLC). The [[Man of Benjamin (of Eli's time)]] tells him Israel has fled, his two sons are dead,
*and God's ark has been captured* (17). He survives the news of his sons: *When he made mention of God's
ark, Eli fell from off his seat backward by the side of the gate; and his neck broke, and he died; for he
was an old man, and heavy* (18). The *seat*, *kissēʾ*, is the one he sat on when the book opened (1:9), and
the word of Hannah's *throne of glory* (2:8, WLC). *Old*, *zāqēn*, answers *there will not be an old man in
your house* (2:31); *heavy*, *kāḇēḏ*, shares its root with *you honor your sons above me* (2:29) and with
*glory*, *kāḇôḏ*, in his grandson's name three verses on (WLC; a widely noted wordplay the text does not
comment on). The narrator then gives him the judges' formula for the first time: *He had judged Israel forty
years* (18). The Septuagint is commonly reported to read *twenty* (recalled, not checked). His fear for the
ark before his sons is the order the narrative keeps, and it is the one thing told in his favor at his
death. See [[The Ark Is Captured]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 1:9; 2:8, 29–31; 3:2; 4:6, 12–22. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-17.

### The priest of Yahweh in Shiloh, after his death — 1 Samuel 14:3

His title outlives him. The priest with Saul's army is introduced by a four-generation genealogy that ends with
Eli's name and office: *Ahijah, the son of Ahitub, Ichabod's brother, the son of Phinehas, the son of Eli, the priest
of Yahweh in Shiloh, wearing an ephod* (14:3, stored WEB). The man of God had said there would *not be an old man in
your house forever*, and that Yahweh would *raise me up a faithful priest* (2:32, 35, stored WEB); the house still
carries the ephod a generation later, at the side of a king whose own house has just been told it will not continue
(13:13–14). Saul calls on Ahijah to inquire, and cuts him off (14:18–19). See [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]] and
[[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]].

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 2:32, 35; 13:13–14; 14:3, 18–19.

## Timeline / key events

- Priest at Shiloh, with his sons Hophni and Phinehas — 1 Samuel 1:3, 9.
- Takes Hannah for drunk, then blesses her petition — 1:12–17.
- Receives the weaned Samuel — 1:25–28.
- The child serves Yahweh before him — 2:11.
- Blesses Elkanah and Hannah; hears of his sons' sins and rebukes them; they do not listen — 2:20–25.
- A man of God pronounces judgment on his house: no old man in it, his sons to die in one day, a faithful priest to replace it — 2:27–36.
- Teaches the boy Samuel to answer Yahweh; hears from him the sentence on his house, and accepts it: *It is Yahweh* — 3:1–18.
- Waits on his seat for news of the ark; at the word that it is taken, falls backward and dies, at ninety-eight; *He had judged Israel forty years* — 4:12–18.
- Named at the head of his great-grandson Ahijah's genealogy, *the priest of Yahweh in Shiloh* — 14:3.
