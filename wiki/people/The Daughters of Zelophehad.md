# The Daughters of Zelophehad

## Overview

### Manasseh’s inheritance — Joshua 17:1–13

Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah appeal together to Eleazar, Joshua, and the princes (3–4).
The promised inheritance is now granted among their father’s brothers; verses 5–6 expressly include
daughters in the accounting of Manasseh’s shares.

See [[The Inheritance of Manasseh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:1–13.

### Marriage and tribal inheritance — Numbers 36:1–13

Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Noah marry their father's brothers' sons (10–12). Their
inheritance is retained, but their freedom of choice is bounded by paternal tribal family (6–8).
Numbers 27:7–8 grants inheritance; Numbers 36 qualifies marriage for heiresses rather than
revoking their title.

See [[The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 36; comparisons as cited.

### The daughters’ inheritance — Numbers 27:1–11

Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah jointly petition before Moses, Eleazar, the princes, and
the congregation (1–4). They ask for their father's name and possession to be preserved despite
his lack of sons. Yahweh affirms their claim and commands the inheritance to pass to them (7).
Their collective page remains appropriate: no individual sister is assigned a separate speech or
act.

See [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:1–11.


Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah are named together in Numbers 26:33. Their father
has no sons. They are preserved as a collective page because this unit assigns them no
individual acts. Noah is not the flood patriarch, Milcah is not Nahor’s wife, and Tirzah
is not the city. Their petition follows the census in Numbers 27:1–11.
