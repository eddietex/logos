# Adoni-Bezek

## Overview

### Judah goes up first — Judges 1:1–7

The first enemy king in Judges. His name means *lord of Bezek*, and the narrative finds him at
Bezek when Judah and Simeon strike the Canaanites and Perizzites there (4–5). He flees, is caught,
and has his thumbs and big toes cut off (6). He then says that seventy kings with their thumbs and
big toes cut off *scavenged under my table*, and passes judgment on himself: *As I have done, so God
has done to me* (7). He is brought to Jerusalem and dies there.

His words are the only evaluation the passage gives of the maiming. The Hebrew of his verdict is
*kaʾăšer ʿāśîtî kēn šillam-lî ʾĕlōhîm*, *as I have done, so God has repaid me* (WLC, OpenScriptures
morphhb). He speaks of *ʾĕlōhîm*, not of Yahweh, the name the narrator uses (4). The seventy kings
are his own claim, and may be a boast.

**Adoni-Zedek, and an open question.** Joshua 10:1 names *Adoni-Zedek king of Jerusalem* (stored
WEB), and this king is brought to Jerusalem to die. The two names differ in one element. Some
readers take them as one man under two forms of the name, and it is often said that the Septuagint
of Joshua 10 reads *Adonibezek*. That has not been checked here. The accounts differ in place,
captor, and death: Adoni-Zedek is killed and hanged by Joshua and thrown into the cave at Makkedah
(Joshua 10:26–27), while Adoni-Bezek is maimed by Judah and dies at Jerusalem. The wiki keeps two
pages and does not identify them. See [[Adoni-Zedek]].
