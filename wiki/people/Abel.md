# Abel

## Overview

The second son of Adam and Eve, a shepherd, and the first human being to die. He is given four
things in Genesis and no more: a name, a trade, an offering, and a death. He never speaks. His
birth is reported without any of the thanksgiving that greeted his brother — *"Again she gave
birth, to Cain's brother Abel"* — and even that sentence identifies him by his relation to Cain.

His name is *hebel*, the Hebrew for breath or vapour: the word Ecclesiastes uses thirty-eight
times for whatever cannot be held on to (*"vanity of vanities"*). The narrator offers no gloss for
it, which leaves it working as an epitaph attached in advance.

In [[Cain and Abel]] he brings *"some of the firstborn of his flock and of its fat"* — the best
animals and the best part of them — and *"Yahweh respected Abel and his offering"*. The text gives
no reason for the acceptance, and names the man before the gift both times it mentions them
together. That order is the seed of everything later Scripture says about him.

He is killed in a field by his brother, and the narrative marks the relation twice in the verse
that does it. After that he does not appear in Genesis again except as an absence: Seth is born
*"instead of Abel, for Cain killed him"* (4:25).

What the Bible has to say about Abel it says almost entirely posthumously, and at length.

- **His blood speaks.** *"The voice of your brother's blood cries to me from the ground"* (4:10) —
  the man who is given no words alive becomes, dead, a voice God answers.
- **He is the first righteous man.** Jesus dates the shed blood of the prophets *"from the blood
  of righteous Abel"* (Matthew 23:35, Luke 11:51), making him the first entry in the record of
  the martyred.
- **He is the first believer.** *"By faith Abel offered to God a more excellent sacrifice than
  Cain, through which he had testimony given to him that he was righteous… and through it he,
  being dead, still speaks"* (Hebrews 11:4) — the opening name in the roll of faith.
- **His blood is the measure of Christ's.** Hebrews 12:24 sets *"the blood of sprinkling that
  speaks better than that of Abel"* as the last term in its description of what believers have
  come to.

See [[Cain and Abel in the New Testament]] for how those readings fit together and what they take
from a passage that says so little.

## Appears in

- [[Cain and Abel]]
- [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]] — named once, at 4:25, as the person Seth is given
  *"instead of"*. It is the last time Genesis mentions him.
- [[Whoever Sheds Man's Blood]] — the law written against his death: Genesis 9:5–6 requires the
  reckoning his blood cried for at 4:10, and grounds it in the image every victim bears.
- [[Do Not Cover My Blood]] — not an appearance, but the clearest measure of his reach. Job, on the
  far side of the canon and outside Israel, addresses the earth and forbids it to do to his blood
  what Genesis 4:11 says it did to Abel's: *"Earth, don't cover my blood. Let my cry have no place
  to rest"* (Job 16:18). The conception that innocent blood has a voice, and that burial silences
  it, starts here and runs to Revelation 6:10.
