# Manoah

## Overview

### The father who asks — Judges 13:2–23

*A certain man of Zorah, of the family of the Danites, whose name was Manoah* (13:2). He is the one
named; his wife, who is barren, is not. Yet every visit of [[The Angel of Yahweh]] goes to her, and
Manoah learns everything second-hand.

He is the one who prays. *Manoah entreated Yahweh*, *wayyeʿtar* (13:8, WLC), asking that the man of God
*come again to us, and teach us what we should do to the child*. *Wayyeʿtar* is the verb of Isaac's
prayer for barren Rebekah (Genesis 25:21). *God listened to the voice of Manoah* (13:9), and the
angel comes again, to the woman, *as she sat in the field*, with Manoah absent. She fetches him.

He asks three questions and none is answered as he means it. *Are you the man who spoke to my wife?*
gets *I am* (13:11). *What shall the child's way of life and mission be?* (13:12; *mišpaṭ*, the noun of
the root *judge*, WLC) gets the rules already given to his wife (13:13–14). *What is your name, that
when your words happen, we may honor you?* gets *Why do you ask about my name, since it is
incomprehensible?* (13:17–18). His offer of a young goat as a meal is refused and redirected: *If you
will prepare a burnt offering, you must offer it to Yahweh* (13:16). The narrator adds, *For Manoah
didn't know that he was Yahweh's angel*.

He offers the goat and the meal offering *on the rock to Yahweh*, sees the angel go up in the flame,
falls on his face, and only then *knew that he was Yahweh's angel* (13:19–21). His conclusion is fear:
*We shall surely die, because we have seen God* (13:22; *môt nāmût*, WLC). His wife answers it (13:23).

The text does not mock him. He prays, is heard, and offers correctly. But it places him one step
behind his wife at every turn, the reverse of the man-first order the opening verse sets up. See
[[The Announcement of Samson's Birth]] and [[Manoah's Wife]].

*Mānôaḥ* (WLC lemma 4495) is usually connected with *nûaḥ*, *rest*; reported, not checked.

Source: stored WEB Genesis 25:21; Judges 13:2–23; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-16.

### The objecting father — Judges 14:2–10

In chapter 14 he is never named, only *his father*. With his wife he objects to Samson's choice in the
words of the patriarchs: *Isn't there a woman among your brothers' daughters, or among all my people,
that you go to take a wife of the uncircumcised Philistines?* (14:3); compare Genesis 24:3 and 28:1 (stored
WEB). Samson answers him alone, *Get her for me* (14:3). The narrator says *his father and his mother
didn't know that it was of Yahweh* (14:4). He goes down to Timnah, eats the honey from the lion's carcass
without being told where it came from (14:9), and *went down to the woman* to arrange the marriage
(14:10). The man who asked what the child's *way of life* would be (13:12) is twice kept from knowing what
his son is doing. See [[Samson's Riddle]].

Source: stored WEB Genesis 24:3; 28:1; Judges 13:12; 14:1–10.

### His grave — Judges 16:31

Samson's *brothers and all the house of his father* bring him up and bury him *between Zorah and Eshtaol in
the burial site of Manoah his father* (16:31). Manoah's name, absent since chapter 13 (in chapter 14 he
is only *his father*), returns as the owner of a grave; the text does not say when he died. The man who asked
what the child's *way of life and mission* would be (13:12) is named last at its end. Whether the *brothers* are sons of
Manoah born after Samson or kinsmen, the text does not say. See [[The Death of Samson]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 13:12; 16:31.

## Timeline / key events
- A Danite of Zorah, husband of a barren wife (Judges 13:2).
- Prays for the man of God to return (13:8).
- Meets the angel, asks about the child and the visitor's name, offers on the rock (13:11–20).
- Fears death for having seen God; answered by his wife (13:22–23).
- Objects to Samson's Philistine wife, then arranges the marriage (14:2–10).
- Samson is buried in his tomb (16:31).
