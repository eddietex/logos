# The Levite's Father-in-Law

## Overview

### Five days in Bethlehem — Judges 19:3–9

He is never named. *The father of the young lady* in Bethlehem Judah, the Levite's *ḥōtēn*, father-in-law
(19:4, 9, WLC). When the Levite arrives to fetch his daughter, *he rejoiced to meet him* (19:3), and he keeps
him three days, eating and drinking (19:4).

On the fourth morning he says, *Strengthen your heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward you shall go
your way* (19:5) — *səʿāḏ libbəḵā pat-leḥem* (WLC), the words of Abraham to his visitors (Genesis 18:5); see
[[A Morsel of Bread - Genesis 18.5 and Judges 19.5]]. Then *let your heart be merry*, and the Levite stays
(19:6–7). On the fifth day he urges again — *stay until the day declines*, and at evening, *the day is
ending. Stay here … and tomorrow go on your way early* (19:8–9). This time the Levite leaves.

The urging is told four times and given no motive. Readers have proposed generosity, a father's reluctance
to let his daughter go, or a host's custom; the text says only that he *rejoiced*. The effect is
structural: his excess of hospitality puts the travelers on the road at evening, where Gibeah's absence of
it waits. His daughter is never said to speak to him, and he is not seen again.
