# The Twelve Tribal Princes

## Overview

The twelve men who stand with Moses and Aaron at the census, one from each tribe. This is a group
page, because Numbers gives them a shared role and almost no individual action: they recur as a set
in the camp (chapter 2), the dedication gifts (chapter 7), and the order of march (chapter 10).
Only [[Nahshon]] has his own page.

| Asher | Pagiel | Ochran |
| Gad | Eliasaph | Deuel (Reuel at 2:14) |
| Naphtali | Ahira | Enan |

The text calls them *those who were called of the congregation, the princes of the tribes of their
fathers* and *the heads of the thousands of Israel* (16). *Nāśîʾ* is WEB's *prince*; the word
marks a tribal leader and does not imply a royal family here.

Several names look like confessions, built on *ʾēl*, *Shaddai*, or *ṣûr*. That is an observation
about their form; the meanings were not checked against a lexicon, and the text never comments on
them. A later Eliab (the son of Pallu) and David's brother are different men, and if either gets a
page this one's Eliab should be named *Eliab (son of Helon)*.

See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 1; Hebrew WLC.

### The camp — Numbers 2:1–34

The twelve reappear in the chapter in camp order: Nahshon, Nethanel, and Eliab on the east; Elizur, Shelumiel, and Eliasaph on the south; Elishama, Gamaliel, and Abidan on the west; Ahiezer, Pagiel, and Ahira on the north. Each is *the prince of the children of* his tribe. Eliasaph's father is Reuel at verse 14.

See [[The Camp Around the Tent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 2.
