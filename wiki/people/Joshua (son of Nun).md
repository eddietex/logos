# Joshua (son of Nun)

## Overview

### Courage and succession — Deuteronomy 31:1–8

Moses publicly charges Joshua to go with Israel and cause the people to inherit the sworn land (3, 7–8). Yahweh goes before both people and successor; Joshua’s leadership does not replace divine presence. This encouragement supplies a public address without repeating Numbers 27’s laying on of hands.

See [[Be Strong as Joshua Takes the Lead]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:1–8; Deuteronomy 31:16–18 and 34:7 where compared.

### Moses’ plea — Deuteronomy 3:23–29

Yahweh commands Moses to commission, encourage, and strengthen Joshua, who will cross before the
people and cause them to inherit the land (28). This is a command, not a narrated commissioning
ceremony; Numbers 27:22–23 supplies such a ceremony.

See [[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:23–29.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

Moses appeals to what Joshua has seen happen to the two kings as grounds for confidence about the
kingdoms ahead (21–22). This is a remembered exhortation, not a repetition of Numbers 27’s public
laying on of hands.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### The sentence and the ascent — Deuteronomy 1:34–46

Joshua is promised entry and charged with bringing Israel into its inheritance (38). Moses must
encourage the successor who will enter where he cannot. This anticipates the leadership transition
narrated in Numbers 27:12–23; Deuteronomy does not repeat that commissioning ceremony here.

See [[The Sentence and the Presumptuous Ascent]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 14 and 20 for comparisons.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

Joshua is named with Eleazar to divide the land (17). The successor commissioned in Numbers 27 now receives a specific settlement responsibility.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

Named twice in the chapter. In Moses' retelling of the oath, he and Caleb are the two who *followed
Yahweh completely* (12). Then Moses commands *concerning* Reuben and Gad to Eleazar, *Joshua the son of
Nun*, and the tribal heads (28): Joshua will be the one to hold the eastern tribes to their word. Joshua
1:12–18 and 22:1–9 show him doing it (WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested).

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### Commissioned — Numbers 27:12–23

When Moses asks for a man to go out and come in before the congregation, Yahweh names *Joshua the
son of Nun, a man in whom is the Spirit* (18). It is the first verse to say the Spirit is in him; it
does not say when he received it, and his earlier record is not cited as a reason. Moses sets him
before Eleazar and the whole congregation, lays his hands on him, and commissions him (22–23). He
receives some of Moses' *authority* (20; Hebrew *mēhôdəkā*, *from your majesty*, MAM via Sefaria —
the partitive reading is interpretive), *that all the congregation … may obey*. Unlike Moses, he is to
*stand before Eleazar the priest*, who will inquire for him by the Urim (21). Joshua says nothing in
the unit. See [[Joshua Commissioned]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:12–23.


### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

Named with Caleb as the surviving exception to the former military census generation (65). His
commissioning remains the later unit at 27:12–23.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


**Moses' commander and servant, and his successor — who walks into Exodus without a word of introduction and
is given a sword before he is given a father.** *"Moses said to Joshua, 'Choose men for us, and go out, fight
with Amalek'"* (Exodus 17:9). **It is the first time the name appears in Scripture** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy), and the narrator explains nothing: no tribe, no patronymic, no reason Moses should have a
man to send. **His first recorded act is obedience** — *"So Joshua did as Moses had told him"* (17:10), in the
same form of words the narrator had just used of Moses at the rock (*"Moses did so"*, 17:6; WLC) — **and his
second is a victory**: *"Joshua defeated Amalek and his people with the edge of the sword"* (17:13). Then God
singles him out as the hearer of the memorial: *"Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the
ears of Joshua"* (17:14). **Why him the text does not say.** See [[The Battle with Amalek]].

**His name is not the one he was born with.** Numbers lists him among the spies as *"Hoshea the son of Nun"*, of
the tribe of Ephraim, and adds: ***"Moses called Hoshea the son of Nun Joshua"*** (Numbers 13:8, 16; WEB) — Hebrew *hôšēaʿ* to *yəhôšuaʿ*, with the divine name prefixed (WLC). **Exodus uses the later name
from his first line**, so the narrator is writing from after a renaming it never reports. **The name is
commonly derived from *Yahweh* and *yāšaʿ*, *to save*** — the verb of *"Thus Yahweh saved Israel that day"*
(Exodus 14:30; WLC); that etymology is standard but is not spelled out by the text. **In Greek it is
*Iēsous***, the name the New Testament uses both for him (Acts 7:45; Hebrews 4:8; SBLGNT) and for Jesus. See
[[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]].

**The rest of Exodus keeps him at Moses' side.** He is *"his servant"* (*məšārətô*, MAM) who goes up the mountain
with Moses (24:13; [[The Blood of the Covenant]]) — not among the seventy-four who saw God, but further up than
they; how far, the text does not say, and the next time he appears he is coming down with Moses. He hears *"the noise of war in the camp"* as they come down to the calf (32:17); and he is *"his
servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man"*, who *"didn't depart from the Tent"* (33:11) — **the first time
Exodus names his father**. Chapter 32 is ingested in [[The Golden Calf]]; chapter 33 is ingested in
[[Show Me Your Glory]]. **Numbers and Deuteronomy make him the successor** —
*"a man in whom is the Spirit"*, on whom Moses lays his hands (Numbers 27:18, 23; [[Joshua Commissioned]]),
*"full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands on him"* (Deuteronomy 34:9; WEB, verified;
not yet ingested) — and the book
[[Joshua]] is named for him.

### What is ahead

The succession in Deuteronomy 31 and 34, and the book of Joshua. The sentence and his survival
(Numbers 14) and the commissioning (Numbers 27) are now ingested below. These sections are written as their pericopes arrive.

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Joshua reappears with Moses on the descent and hears the shouting as war. Moses distinguishes the
sound from victory or defeat and identifies singing (32:17–18). The text gives Joshua no role in
making the calf or carrying out the camp judgment. His earlier military role does not turn the
noise into an actual battle. See [[The Golden Calf]].

### Presence after the calf — Exodus 33

Joshua is named as Moses’ servant, the son of Nun, and a young man. When Moses returns to the camp,
Joshua does not depart from the tent (33:11). This is attendance at the outside tent, not a narrated
appointment as an Aaronic priest; no reason for his remaining is stated. See [[Show Me Your Glory]]
.

### Forbid them — Numbers 11:28–29

Joshua hears that Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp and says, *My lord Moses, forbid them!* He is called *the servant of Moses,
one of his chosen men*. Moses asks, *Are you jealous for my sake?*, and refuses. It is the first time in the wiki's sequence that Joshua is
corrected. *Mibbəḥurāw* can mean *from his youth* as well as *of his chosen men* (WLC tags). See [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] and
[[Do Not Forbid Them - Joshua in Numbers 11 and John in Mark 9]].

### Hoshea called Joshua — Numbers 13

He is sent for Ephraim as *Hoshea the son of Nun* (8), and at the end of the list *Moses called
Hoshea the son of Nun Joshua* (16). Exodus and Numbers have called him Joshua since Exodus 17:9, so
the verse either reports an earlier naming or the narrator has been using the later name. It gives
no reason. In this chapter he says nothing: Caleb speaks at 13:30, and Joshua's first words on the
matter come at 14:6–9. Deuteronomy 32:44 still has *Hoshea* in WLC, where WEB prints *Joshua* and
Rahlfs has *Ἰησοῦς*. See [[The Twelve Spies]] and [[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]].

### He speaks — Numbers 14

Silent in Numbers 13, he stands with Caleb here: they tear their clothes and say *an exceeding good
land*, and *they are bread for us* (6–9). Yahweh names him with Caleb as the two who will enter
(30), and they alone of the men who went live (38). He is not named in the *my servant* of verse 24,
and Deuteronomy 1:38 gives him a different reason. See [[The People Refuse the Land]].
