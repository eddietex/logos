# Joshua (son of Nun)

## Overview

### Joshua's farewell to the leaders — Joshua 23:1–16

Old and *well advanced in years* (1–2), the words of 13:1 now in his own mouth, he gathers Israel's leaders for the first of two farewells. He credits every victory to Yahweh (3), says he has allotted the nations that remain (4), and passes on the charge of 1:7 to be *very courageous* in keeping the book of the law (6). He calls his death *the way of all the earth* (14); it is narrated at 24:29.

See [[Joshua's Farewell to the Leaders]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 23:1–16; comparisons as cited.

### The eastern tribes sent home — Joshua 22:1–9

He commends the eastern tribes for obedience, authorizes their return, renews the call to love and
serve Yahweh, blesses them, and orders plunder shared (1–8). Leadership ends an assignment without
ending covenant responsibility.

See [[The Eastern Tribes Sent Home]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:1–9; comparisons as cited.

### Levitical cities — Joshua 21:1–42

Receives the Levite heads’ request alongside Eleazar and the tribal heads (1–2).

See [[The Cities of the Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:1–42.

### Refuge cities appointed — Joshua 20:1–9

He receives the command to tell Israel to appoint the refuge cities (1–2). The following communal designation implements the law; no individual asylum hearing by Joshua is narrated.

See [[The Cities of Refuge Appointed]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 20:1–9.

### Joshua’s own inheritance — Joshua 19:49–51

Israel gives Joshua the city he requests under Yahweh’s command (49–50). He builds it and lives there; the distributor is also a recipient among the people. Verse 51 names him with Eleazar and the tribal household heads.

See [[Joshua's Own Inheritance]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 19:49–51.

### The survey at Shiloh — Joshua 18:1–10

He rebukes delayed possession, commissions the survey, receives its written results, and casts the
lots at Shiloh (3–10). Leadership joins exhortation, practical administration, and allocation before
Yahweh.

See [[The Land Surveyed at Shiloh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 18:1–10; comparisons as cited.

### Joseph asks for more — Joshua 17:14–18

He accepts Joseph’s claim of numbers, turns it into responsibility to clear the forest, and affirms that strong Canaanites with iron chariots can be driven out (15–18). No battle outcome is narrated here.

See [[The Children of Joseph Ask for More]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:14–18.

### Manasseh’s inheritance — Joshua 17:1–13

The daughters address him with Eleazar and the princes; the narrative reports the grant according to
Yahweh’s command (4). Leadership implements the ruling previously given through Moses.

See [[The Inheritance of Manasseh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:1–13.

### Achsah’s springs — Joshua 15:13–19

The grant to Caleb is made according to Yahweh’s command to Joshua (13). The local victories credited to Caleb and Othniel remain alongside the broader victories credited to Joshua in 10:36–39 and 11:21; the chronology is not explained.

See [[Achsah Asks for Springs]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:13–19.

### Caleb’s inheritance — Joshua 14:6–15

Caleb appeals to Joshua as a witness to the promise made through Moses. Joshua blesses him and grants Hebron (6, 13). The earlier removal of the Anakim credited to Joshua in 11:21–22 remains in tension with Caleb’s proposed expulsion in 14:12 and action in 15:14.

See [[Caleb Claims Hebron]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:6–15; comparisons as cited.


### The western division — Joshua 14:1–5

Joshua shares the distribution with Eleazar and tribal household heads (1). Leadership here means administering the inheritance by lot under the command transmitted through Moses, not acting as sole owner or distributor.

See [[The Division of the Land Begins]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:1–5; comparisons as cited.


### The land that remains — Joshua 13:1–7

Joshua is old and advanced in years, yet receives a concrete remaining commission: divide the land for nine tribes and half of Manasseh (1, 7). No numerical age is given here. His work now includes assigning land whose possession is still future.

See [[The Land That Remains]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:1–7.

### The whole land taken — Joshua 11:16–23

The summary credits Joshua with taking the land from Mount Halak to Baal Gad, killing its kings, and waging war *a long time* (16–18). He cuts off the Anakim from Hebron, Debir, and Anab (21) and gives the land to Israel by tribes (23). Joshua 13:1 immediately qualifies *the whole land* with land still remaining.

See [[The Whole Land Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:16–23.

### The northern coalition — Joshua 11:1–15

Joshua attacks suddenly, pursues the coalition, disables its horses, burns its chariots, and captures its cities (7–14). Verse 15 measures his obedience by the commands transmitted through Moses.

See [[The Northern Kings at the Waters of Merom]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:1–15.

### The southern campaign — Joshua 10:28–43

Joshua leads Israel through six city attacks, defeats Horam’s relief force, and returns to Gilgal (28–43). The repeated attribution to Yahweh does not remove Joshua and Israel’s agency in the killing.

See [[The Southern Cities Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:28–43; comparisons as cited.

### The five kings at Makkedah — Joshua 10:16–27

Joshua secures the hidden kings while continuing the pursuit (18–19), summons the commanders to put
their feet on the kings’ necks, and passes on the charge to be strong and courageous (24–25). He
kills the kings, displays their bodies, and orders their removal at sunset (26–27).

See [[The Five Kings in the Cave at Makkedah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:16–27.


### At Gibeon — Joshua 10:1–15

He answers Gibeon’s plea by marching all night with the whole army (6–9). His public address to sun
and moon is framed as speech to Yahweh (12); the narrator attributes the result to Yahweh listening
(14), not to Joshua’s independent control of the heavens.

See [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:1–15.


### The Gibeonite deception — Joshua 9:1–27

Joshua makes peace and a life-preserving covenant without the inquiry whose omission the narrator
identifies (14–15). After discovery he confronts the deception, imposes enduring service, and
protects the Gibeonites from Israel (22–27). Military success has not made his decisions infallible.

See [[The Gibeonite Deception]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 9:1–27; comparisons as cited.


### The law read at Ebal — Joshua 8:30–35

Joshua builds the altar, writes a copy of Moses’ law on stones, and reads it to the entire assembly (30–35). His leadership after Ai includes public instruction, not only military command; compare his charge in Joshua 1:7–8.

See [[The Law Read at Mount Ebal]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 8:30–35.

### The capture of Ai — Joshua 8:1–29

Yahweh's first word after Achor is *Don't be afraid, and don't be dismayed* (1) — the exact pairing
Moses used to the first generation at Deuteronomy 1:21 (WLC). Joshua takes *all the warriors*, as
ordered, and fills in the plan Yahweh outlines (*Set an ambush*, 2): the earlier rout becomes the
bait, *as at the first* (5–6). At Yahweh's command he stretches out the javelin toward the city, the
signal for the ambush (18–19), and *didn't draw back his hand* until Ai was destroyed (26) — the
outstretched hand of Moses' great acts, held now by the man who fought below it at Rephidim. He
burns Ai to *a heap forever* (28) and has the king's body taken down at sundown, as Deuteronomy
21:22–23 requires (29). See [[The Hand Held Out - Moses at Rephidim and Joshua at Ai]].

See [[The Capture of Ai]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 8:1–29; WLC (morphhb) where Hebrew is named.

### The sin of Achan — Joshua 7:1–26

Joshua's first defeat. He sends a reduced force to Ai on the spies' advice, then tears his clothes
and lies before the ark with the elders, asking why Yahweh brought Israel over the Jordan and what
he will do for his great name (6–9). Told to get up, he sanctifies the people, conducts the
selection, addresses Achan as My son while urging confession, and pronounces the wordplay sentence:
Why have you troubled us? Yahweh will trouble you today (25).

See [[The Sin of Achan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 7:1–26; WLC (morphhb) where Hebrew is named.

### The fall of Jericho — Joshua 6:1–27

Joshua transmits the marching orders, commands the shout, reserves the metals for Yahweh, and directs the spies to honor their oath to Rahab (6–26). His fame spreads because Yahweh is with him (27).

See [[The Fall of Jericho]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 6:1–27; comparisons identified above.


### The commander by Jericho — Joshua 5:13–15

Joshua moves from asking which side the armed man supports to falling down, worshiping,
and asking for instruction as a servant (13–14). He obeys the command to remove his shoes
(15). Leadership here begins with receiving authority, not issuing an order.

See [[The Commander of Yahweh's Army]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 5:13–15; comparisons as cited.

### Circumcision at Gilgal — Joshua 5:1–9

Joshua makes flint knives and circumcises the wilderness-born males at Yahweh’s command (2–7). The nation waits in camp to heal (8); this is leadership in covenant obedience before battle.

See [[The Second Circumcision at Gilgal]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 5:1–9; comparisons as cited.

### The twelve stones — Joshua 4:1–24

Joshua directs the representatives, sets stones in the river and at Gilgal, and teaches their
meaning (4–9, 20–24). Yahweh magnifies him before Israel (14), fulfilling 3:7.
See [[The Twelve Stones from the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 4:1–24.

### Crossing the Jordan — Joshua 3:1–17

Joshua moves the camp from Shittim, commands sanctification and the priests’ advance, and announces the stopping of the river (1, 5–13). Yahweh promises public confirmation of his leadership by the same presence given Moses (7). Joshua commands; the priests carry the ark into the water.

See [[Crossing the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 3:1–17.


### Rahab and the spies — Joshua 2:1–24

Joshua secretly sends two unnamed spies from Shittim to inspect the land and Jericho (1). They return directly to him with Rahab’s testimony of the inhabitants’ fear and a confident report of Yahweh’s gift (23–24). The text does not explain why he chooses two.

See [[Rahab and the Spies]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 2:1–24.

### The eastern tribes pledge — Joshua 1:10–18

Joshua's first act is to pass the command on: the officers are to tell the camp to prepare provisions for a crossing *within three days* (10–11). He reminds the Reubenites, Gadites and half-tribe of Manasseh of Moses' word in the language of Deuteronomy 3:18–20, adding *and shall help them* (14). They pledge to listen to him *just as we listened to Moses*, wish that Yahweh be with him *as he was with Moses*, put to death anyone who *rebels against your mouth* (*yamreh ʾet-pîkā*, MAM via Sefaria), and return Yahweh's own charge: *Only be strong and courageous* (16–18). His authority is accepted, and measured by Moses.

See [[The Eastern Tribes Pledge to Cross]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 1:10–18.

### After the death of Moses — Joshua 1:1–9

Introduced as *Moses' servant* (*məšārēt mōšeh*, the attendant's word of Exodus 24:13 and 33:11; MAM via Sefaria), Joshua receives Yahweh's first word after Moses' death: rise, cross the Jordan, and cause the people to inherit the sworn land (2, 6). He is promised *As I was with Moses, so I will be with you* (5) and told three times to be strong and courageous, the middle time to keep *all the law* and to meditate on *this book of the law* day and night (7–8). He raises no objection and the text describes no feelings. His first act follows in the next pericope.

See [[After the Death of Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 1:1–9.

### The death of Moses — Deuteronomy 34:1–12

Joshua is *full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands on him*, and Israel obeys him as Yahweh commanded Moses (9). Numbers 27:18 already calls him *a man in whom is the Spirit* before the laying on of hands; the order differs between the two accounts and is flagged rather than harmonized.

See [[The Death of Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 34:1–12.

### The Song of Moses — Deuteronomy 31:30–32:47

Joshua joins Moses in speaking the song to the people (44). Succession includes transmitting the warning as well as leading into the land; no independent speech by Joshua is reproduced.

See [[The Song of Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:30–32:47.


### The song as witness — Deuteronomy 31:14–23

Joshua accompanies Moses to the Tent for commissioning (14). The closing charge promises that he
will bring Israel into the sworn land and that God will be with him (23). The WEB's pronoun He
is clarified by the divine first-person land oath and presence promise, though the narrator does
not repeat Yahweh's name in that verse.

See [[A Song to Witness Against Israel]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:14–23.

### Courage and succession — Deuteronomy 31:1–8

Moses publicly charges Joshua to go with Israel and cause the people to inherit the sworn land (3, 7–8). Yahweh goes before both people and successor; Joshua’s leadership does not replace divine presence. This encouragement supplies a public address without repeating Numbers 27’s laying on of hands.

See [[Be Strong as Joshua Takes the Lead]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:1–8; Deuteronomy 31:16–18 and 34:7 where compared.

### Moses’ plea — Deuteronomy 3:23–29

Yahweh commands Moses to commission, encourage, and strengthen Joshua, who will cross before the
people and cause them to inherit the land (28). This is a command, not a narrated commissioning
ceremony; Numbers 27:22–23 supplies such a ceremony.

See [[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:23–29.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

Moses appeals to what Joshua has seen happen to the two kings as grounds for confidence about the
kingdoms ahead (21–22). This is a remembered exhortation, not a repetition of Numbers 27’s public
laying on of hands.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### The sentence and the ascent — Deuteronomy 1:34–46

Joshua is promised entry and charged with bringing Israel into its inheritance (38). Moses must
encourage the successor who will enter where he cannot. This anticipates the leadership transition
narrated in Numbers 27:12–23; Deuteronomy does not repeat that commissioning ceremony here.

See [[The Sentence and the Presumptuous Ascent]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 14 and 20 for comparisons.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

Joshua is named with Eleazar to divide the land (17). The successor commissioned in Numbers 27 now receives a specific settlement responsibility.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

Named twice in the chapter. In Moses' retelling of the oath, he and Caleb are the two who *followed
Yahweh completely* (12). Then Moses commands *concerning* Reuben and Gad to Eleazar, *Joshua the son of
Nun*, and the tribal heads (28): Joshua will be the one to hold the eastern tribes to their word. Joshua
1:12–18 and 22:1–9 show him doing it (WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested).

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### Commissioned — Numbers 27:12–23

When Moses asks for a man to go out and come in before the congregation, Yahweh names *Joshua the
son of Nun, a man in whom is the Spirit* (18). It is the first verse to say the Spirit is in him; it
does not say when he received it, and his earlier record is not cited as a reason. Moses sets him
before Eleazar and the whole congregation, lays his hands on him, and commissions him (22–23). He
receives some of Moses' *authority* (20; Hebrew *mēhôdəkā*, *from your majesty*, MAM via Sefaria —
the partitive reading is interpretive), *that all the congregation … may obey*. Unlike Moses, he is to
*stand before Eleazar the priest*, who will inquire for him by the Urim (21). Joshua says nothing in
the unit. See [[Joshua Commissioned]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:12–23.


### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

Named with Caleb as the surviving exception to the former military census generation (65). His
commissioning remains the later unit at 27:12–23.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


**Moses' commander and servant, and his successor — who walks into Exodus without a word of introduction and
is given a sword before he is given a father.** *"Moses said to Joshua, 'Choose men for us, and go out, fight
with Amalek'"* (Exodus 17:9). **It is the first time the name appears in Scripture** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy), and the narrator explains nothing: no tribe, no patronymic, no reason Moses should have a
man to send. **His first recorded act is obedience** — *"So Joshua did as Moses had told him"* (17:10), in the
same form of words the narrator had just used of Moses at the rock (*"Moses did so"*, 17:6; WLC) — **and his
second is a victory**: *"Joshua defeated Amalek and his people with the edge of the sword"* (17:13). Then God
singles him out as the hearer of the memorial: *"Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the
ears of Joshua"* (17:14). **Why him the text does not say.** See [[The Battle with Amalek]].

**His name is not the one he was born with.** Numbers lists him among the spies as *"Hoshea the son of Nun"*, of
the tribe of Ephraim, and adds: ***"Moses called Hoshea the son of Nun Joshua"*** (Numbers 13:8, 16; WEB) — Hebrew *hôšēaʿ* to *yəhôšuaʿ*, with the divine name prefixed (WLC). **Exodus uses the later name
from his first line**, so the narrator is writing from after a renaming it never reports. **The name is
commonly derived from *Yahweh* and *yāšaʿ*, *to save*** — the verb of *"Thus Yahweh saved Israel that day"*
(Exodus 14:30; WLC); that etymology is standard but is not spelled out by the text. **In Greek it is
*Iēsous***, the name the New Testament uses both for him (Acts 7:45; Hebrews 4:8; SBLGNT) and for Jesus. See
[[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]].

**The rest of Exodus keeps him at Moses' side.** He is *"his servant"* (*məšārətô*, MAM) who goes up the mountain
with Moses (24:13; [[The Blood of the Covenant]]) — not among the seventy-four who saw God, but further up than
they; how far, the text does not say, and the next time he appears he is coming down with Moses. He hears *"the noise of war in the camp"* as they come down to the calf (32:17); and he is *"his
servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man"*, who *"didn't depart from the Tent"* (33:11) — **the first time
Exodus names his father**. Chapter 32 is ingested in [[The Golden Calf]]; chapter 33 is ingested in
[[Show Me Your Glory]]. **Numbers and Deuteronomy make him the successor** —
*"a man in whom is the Spirit"*, on whom Moses lays his hands (Numbers 27:18, 23; [[Joshua Commissioned]]),
*"full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands on him"* (Deuteronomy 34:9; WEB, verified;
not yet ingested) — and the book
[[Joshua]] is named for him.

### What is ahead

The succession in Deuteronomy 31 and 34 is now ingested above, and the book of Joshua has begun
with [[After the Death of Moses]]. The sentence and his survival
(Numbers 14) and the commissioning (Numbers 27) are now ingested below. These sections are written as their pericopes arrive.

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Joshua reappears with Moses on the descent and hears the shouting as war. Moses distinguishes the
sound from victory or defeat and identifies singing (32:17–18). The text gives Joshua no role in
making the calf or carrying out the camp judgment. His earlier military role does not turn the
noise into an actual battle. See [[The Golden Calf]].

### Presence after the calf — Exodus 33

Joshua is named as Moses’ servant, the son of Nun, and a young man. When Moses returns to the camp,
Joshua does not depart from the tent (33:11). This is attendance at the outside tent, not a narrated
appointment as an Aaronic priest; no reason for his remaining is stated. See [[Show Me Your Glory]]
.

### Forbid them — Numbers 11:28–29

Joshua hears that Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp and says, *My lord Moses, forbid them!* He is called *the servant of Moses,
one of his chosen men*. Moses asks, *Are you jealous for my sake?*, and refuses. It is the first time in the wiki's sequence that Joshua is
corrected. *Mibbəḥurāw* can mean *from his youth* as well as *of his chosen men* (WLC tags). See [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]] and
[[Do Not Forbid Them - Joshua in Numbers 11 and John in Mark 9]].

### Hoshea called Joshua — Numbers 13

He is sent for Ephraim as *Hoshea the son of Nun* (8), and at the end of the list *Moses called
Hoshea the son of Nun Joshua* (16). Exodus and Numbers have called him Joshua since Exodus 17:9, so
the verse either reports an earlier naming or the narrator has been using the later name. It gives
no reason. In this chapter he says nothing: Caleb speaks at 13:30, and Joshua's first words on the
matter come at 14:6–9. Deuteronomy 32:44 still has *Hoshea* in WLC, where WEB prints *Joshua* and
Rahlfs has *Ἰησοῦς*. See [[The Twelve Spies]] and [[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]].

### He speaks — Numbers 14

Silent in Numbers 13, he stands with Caleb here: they tear their clothes and say *an exceeding good
land*, and *they are bread for us* (6–9). Yahweh names him with Caleb as the two who will enter
(30), and they alone of the men who went live (38). He is not named in the *my servant* of verse 24,
and Deuteronomy 1:38 gives him a different reason. See [[The People Refuse the Land]].

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** The western kings are those *Joshua and the children of Israel struck*, and Joshua gives the land *for a possession according to their divisions* — the act verse 6 assigns to Moses (7). Thirty-one kings are counted (24). See [[The Kings Struck Down]].
