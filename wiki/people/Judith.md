# Judith

## Overview

**Esau's first wife, a Hittite woman named once and never mentioned again — and the first Judith in
the Bible.**

> **34** When Esau was forty years old, he took as wife Judith, the daughter of Beeri the Hittite,
> and Basemath, the daughter of Elon the Hittite.
> **35** They grieved Isaac’s and Rebekah’s spirits.

**She is named, given a father, given an ethnicity, and given no words, no acts, and no further
verse.** What Genesis says about her is who she married, whose daughter she was, and what her
existence did to her parents-in-law.

***Yəhûdît* is the feminine of *Yəhûdî*** — *Judahite*, and so, in the later language of the Bible,
**Jewess**. **The first woman in Scripture to bear the name is a Hittite**, four chapters before
Judah is born (29:35) and many centuries before *Yəhûdî* means what it comes to mean. The name is
transparent Hebrew in a verse whose whole point is that these women are not of the family, which is
one of the reasons the register at 36:2–3 — where Esau's wives have different names — has been read
as evidence of separate traditions. See
[[The Names of Esau's Wives - Genesis 26 and Genesis 36]].

**Her offence is her ancestry, and the text does not say more than that.** Genesis records no
conduct, no religion, and no quarrel. **The grief is stated in the same breath as the marriage**, and
the reader is left to supply the reason from what the book has already established: Abraham made his
servant swear *"that you shall not take a wife for my son of the daughters of the Canaanites, among
whom I live"* (24:3), and had him travel four hundred miles instead. **Esau, at the same age his
father was married, took two women of the neighbourhood without consulting anyone.** See
[[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] and [[Marriage]].

**And she is the pretext for the next chapter's ending.** Rebekah's stated reason for sending Jacob
to Haran, spoken the moment the blessing has been stolen, is these marriages: *"I am weary of my life
because of the daughters of Heth. If Jacob takes a wife of the daughters of Heth, such as these, of
the daughters of the land, what good will my life do me?"* (27:46). **The grief of 26:35 is what she
uses**, and Isaac acts on it at once.

**On the Hittites of Genesis.** *Bənê-Ḥēt*, the children of Heth, are the people Abraham buys
Machpelah from in the most courteous transaction in the book (23:3–20), and Ephron the Hittite is
treated by Genesis with more respect than most of the family. **The family owns one field, bought
from Hittites, and its first Hittite marriages are a bitterness.** Genesis makes nothing of the
tension. See [[The Children of Heth]] and [[Ephron the Hittite]].

## Timeline / key events

- Married by [[Esau]] in his fortieth year — Genesis 26:34, [[Isaac in Gerar]]
- Named, with Basemath, as a bitterness of spirit to Isaac and Rebekah — Genesis 26:35
- Absent from the list of Esau's wives at Genesis 36:2–3 *(not yet ingested)*

## Appears in

- [[Isaac in Gerar]] — Genesis 26:34–35; one clause, and the grief that becomes Rebekah's pretext for
  sending Jacob away
- [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]] — Genesis 27:46; 28:9; unnamed in both verses and present in
  both. She is one of *"the daughters of Heth"* Rebekah says she loathes her life over, and she is
  one of the wives Esau keeps when he marries [[Mahalath]] *"besides the wives that he had"*

## Related people

- [[Esau]] — her husband, who takes two wives of the land at forty without reference to anyone
- [[Beeri the Hittite]] — her father, named once and only as her father
- [[Basemath (daughter of Elon)]] — the woman Esau marries in the same verse
- [[Isaac]] and [[Rebekah]] — her parents-in-law, and the only two people in the cycle whose feelings
  Genesis ever reports as shared
- [[Ephron the Hittite]] — the other Hittite the family deals with, and the one who sells them their
  only ground
