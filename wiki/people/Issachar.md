# Issachar

## Overview

**Leah's fifth son, Jacob's ninth, and a name pulled between two transactions.**

> **17** God listened to Leah, and she conceived, and bore Jacob a fifth son. **18** Leah said, “God has
> given me my hire, because I gave my servant to my husband.” She named him Issachar. (30:17–18)

***Yiśśāśkār*** is *there is hire*, from ***śākār***, wages. **The chapter has just used that root of a
husband**: *"I have surely hired you with my son's mandrakes"* (30:16, ***śākōr śəkartîkā***, infinitive
absolute). **So the pun points at the mandrakes and the mother points somewhere else** — she credits
having given [[Zilpah]] away, four verses and two children earlier. **Genesis lets the two readings
stand side by side and chooses neither.** See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Names and Naming]].

**And the verse before the naming is one of the passage's three divine acts**: *"God listened to
Leah."* **Genesis records God hearing her and records no prayer from her**, here or anywhere — the third
of three hearings in the pericope with nothing printed on the other side of them. See [[Prayer]].

**Note also what is not credited.** Leah conceives on the night she bought, and the mandrakes that
bought it are never mentioned again in the Bible. **[[Rachel]], who acquired them, stays barren for
another five verses**, and when she conceives the reason given is *"God remembered Rachel"* (30:22).

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

Jacob's blessing gives him a fourth pun on the same root and the least flattering line in the chapter:
*"Issachar is a strong donkey, lying down between the saddlebags. He saw a resting place, that it was
good… so he bows his shoulder to the burden, and becomes a servant doing forced labor"* (49:14–15) —
**a tribe named *hire* that ends up as *mas*, the corvée.** The territory is the Jezreel valley, the
best farmland in the north and the most fought-over.

The tribe's one moment of standing is 1 Chronicles 12:32: *"of the children of Issachar, men who had
understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do"* — **the verse that made the name a
byword for discernment**, and it has no narrative attached to it anywhere.
