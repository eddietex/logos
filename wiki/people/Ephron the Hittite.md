# Ephron the Hittite

## Overview

The man who sells Abraham a grave, and one of the most carefully drawn minor characters in Genesis —
drawn entirely out of what he says.

He owns a field at [[Hebron]] with a cave at the end of it, and he is **already in the room**:
*"Now Ephron was sitting in the middle of the children of Heth"* (23:10). Abraham has just asked the
assembly to *"entreat for me to Ephron the son of Zohar"* (23:8) as though the man were elsewhere and
would need to be approached through intermediaries. He was there the whole time. Genesis mentions it
in the same breath as his answer, and never says whether Abraham knew. See
[[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]].

### The three things he says

***"No, my lord, hear me. I give you the field, and I give you the cave that is in it. In the presence
of the children of my people I give it to you. Bury your dead."*** (23:11) — three times *I give*,
in public, unprompted. Read as a gift this is extraordinary generosity. Read as commerce it is the
seller **defining the lot**: Abraham had asked for the cave *"which is in the end of his field"*
(23:9), and Ephron's reply attaches the field to it. Whatever else is happening, the parcel just got
bigger.

***"My lord, listen to me. What is a piece of land worth four hundred shekels of silver between me
and you? Therefore bury your dead."*** (23:15) — **the number, delivered as a rhetorical question
about how little it matters.** He never demands it, never negotiates, and phrases the whole thing so
that no price has technically been asked. And Abraham *"listened to Ephron"* and weighed it out
without a word.

Those are his only speeches. He says nothing after the silver is weighed, and he is not mentioned
again in Genesis except as a name attached to the field — *"the field of Ephron"* (23:17; 49:30;
50:13), *"the field which Abraham purchased of the children of Heth"* (25:10). **He is remembered as a
location.**

### Was he generous or was he sharp?

The chapter never says, and the two readings are both fully compatible with every word of it.

- **The courtesy reading.** This is simply how Near Eastern property changed hands. Offering the
  thing free, refusing to name a price, and floating the figure as a question are the polite forms;
  the buyer's job is to insist on paying. Nothing improper occurs, and the elaborate manners are the
  point.
- **The sharp reading.** Four hundred shekels is a great deal of money. Jeremiah buys a field for
  seventeen (Jeremiah 32:9); David pays fifty for a threshing floor and oxen (2 Samuel 24:24); Omri
  buys an entire city site for six thousand (1 Kings 16:24). Ephron names a large number in the most
  gracious sentence available, to a man in mourning who has already declared he will pay full price
  and cannot afford to walk away. On this reading *"between me and you"* is the smoothest line in
  Genesis.

**The wiki records both and adopts neither.** What the text asserts is only that the transfer took
effect, and it asserts that twice (23:17–18, 23:20).

**On his name and his people.** *ʿEprôn*, son of *Ṣōḥar*; *ʿōprôn* would be *fawn*, and the root
*ʿāpār*, **dust**, is the same one Abraham used of himself at 18:27. He is *ha-Ḥittî*, of the
[[The Children of Heth]] — Heth being a son of Canaan in the Table of Nations (10:15), which makes
these people Canaanites on Genesis's own reckoning whatever their relation to the Anatolian
Hittites.

## Timeline / key events

- Sitting among the children of Heth when Abraham asks the assembly to approach him — Genesis 23:10,
  [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]]
- Offers the field and the cave publicly and without a price — Genesis 23:11
- Names four hundred shekels of silver, as a question — Genesis 23:15
- Receives the silver, weighed to the merchants' standard; the field, cave, and trees are deeded to
  Abraham — Genesis 23:16–18
- Named again as the field's former owner when Abraham is buried in it — Genesis 25:9–10,
  [[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]]; and twice more at 49:29–30 and 50:13
  *(not yet ingested)*
