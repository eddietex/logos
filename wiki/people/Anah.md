# Anah

## Overview

**The only person in Genesis 36 who does anything.**

> **24** These are the children of Zibeon: Aiah and Anah. This is Anah who found the hot springs in
> the wilderness, as he fed the donkeys of Zibeon his father.

**Forty-three verses of register, ninety-odd names, and one finite verb belonging to a human being.**
A man is out doing a menial job — feeding his father's donkeys, which is what a younger son or a
servant does — and he finds something. Genesis preserves the sentence and never says why. **It is the
kind of clause that survives in a genealogy because a family told it about itself**, and it is the
only window this chapter has. See [[The Descendants of Esau]].

## What he found

**Nobody knows.** The word is ***hayyēmim***, a **hapax legomenon** — it occurs nowhere else in the
Hebrew Bible — and the ancient translators did not agree:

| version | reading |
|---|---|
| **Vulgate** | *aquas calidas*, **hot springs** — followed by the WEB and most modern versions |
| **Targums, KJV** | **mules**, taking the word as *yēmîm* and making Anah the first breeder of a donkey and a horse |
| **Syriac** | **water** |
| **Septuagint** | transliterates and declines to decide |

**The hot-springs reading has geography behind it**: the Arabah rift is seismically active and dotted
with thermal springs, the best known at Callirrhoe east of the Dead Sea. **The mules reading has a
context behind it** — he is among donkeys when it happens — and a long rabbinic afterlife, in which
the crossbreeding is treated as an impious meddling with created kinds and Anah is disapproved of
accordingly. **The one sentence of narrative in Genesis 36 is a sentence nobody can translate with
confidence.**

## Which Anah

**The chapter names him in two positions and they do not sit easily together.**

- **36:24 — Anah son of Zibeon**, brother of Aiah, in the Horite register.
- **36:2 — *"Oholibamah the daughter of Anah, the daughter of Zibeon, the Hivite"***, which reads as
  though Anah were Zibeon's **daughter**.
- **36:25 — *"These are the children of Anah: Dishon and Oholibamah, the daughter of Anah"***, which
  is straightforward.
- **36:20, 29 — Anah listed among the sons of Seir and among the seven Horite chiefs.**

**The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint, and the Syriac all read *son* at 36:2**, and Hebrew *bat*
can in any case mean *granddaughter*, so the simplest account is one man: Zibeon's son, Seir's
grandson, a Horite chief, and [[Oholibamah]]'s father. **The wiki takes that reading and records that
the Masoretic text at 36:2 is awkward**, on the same principle it applies to the doubled Korah and to
Esau's wives — note the difficulty, do not smooth it.

**The ethnic label wobbles too**: *the Hivite* at 36:2, a Horite everywhere else. See
[[Seir the Horite]].

## Timeline / key events

- Son of Zibeon, grandson of [[Seir the Horite]]; brother of Aiah — Genesis 36:24
- Finds *hayyēmim* in the wilderness while feeding his father's donkeys — Genesis 36:24
- Father of Dishon and of [[Oholibamah]], who marries [[Esau]] — Genesis 36:25, 2

## Appears in
