# Deborah (Rebekah's nurse)

## Overview

**The only nurse in the Hebrew Bible with a name, and the Bible gives it to her at her funeral.**

She appears twice. The first time she is not named:

> **59** They sent away Rebekah, their sister, with her nurse, Abraham’s servant, and his men.
> (24:59)

The second time she is named, and it is a death notice:

> **8** Deborah, Rebekah’s nurse, died, and she was buried below Bethel under the oak; and its
> name was called Allon Bacuth. (35:8)

**One verse, dropped between two theophanies**, in a chapter that has God speaking at 35:1 and again
at 35:9. Genesis stops the return to Bethel for a woman it has mentioned once, roughly a century
earlier, as an item in a bride's departing party — **and gives her a name, a burial, and a place-name
made out of the weeping over her.**

***ʾAllôn Bākût***, **oak of weeping** — **the only tree in Scripture named for a funeral.** And the
verse does not say who wept. There is no mourner named, no duration, nothing like the seventy days
Egypt gives Jacob (50:3) or the thirty Israel gives Aaron (Numbers 20:29). **The grief is in the
place-name and nowhere in the narration.** See [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]] and
[[Burial and the Grave]].

***ʾŌmenet*** at 24:59 is the feminine participle of *ʾāman*, **to support, to carry, to be firm** —
the root behind *ʾāmēn*. It is used of a wet-nurse and of a foster-parent: Naomi becomes an *ʾōmenet*
to Obed (Ruth 4:16), and Isaiah has kings as *ʾōmənîm*, foster-fathers, to Zion (Isaiah 49:23).
**Her name means *bee*** — ***dəbōrâ***, the ordinary Hebrew noun — a name shared with the prophetess
and judge of Judges 4–5, who is a different woman and gets a different page.

### Two questions the verse raises, and the wiki answers neither

**How is she in Jacob's household at all?** She travelled from Paddan Aram to Canaan with
[[Rebekah]] (24:59); [[Jacob]] is coming the other way after twenty years away. **Either she made the
return journey** — Rebekah told Jacob *"I will send, and get you from there"* (27:45), and Genesis
never reports her sending anyone — **or she attached herself to Jacob's camp after Rebekah died**,
which Genesis also never reports. Both readings require an event the book withholds.

**Why does she get a death notice and Rebekah does not?** **Genesis never narrates Rebekah's death.**
The matriarch who received the oracle, engineered the blessing, and sent her son away for his life is
disposed of in a subordinate clause of Jacob's deathbed inventory: *"there they buried Isaac and
Rebekah his wife"* (49:31). **The nurse gets a verse, a named grave, and a tree; the mistress gets a
list entry.** The wiki flags the asymmetry and does not explain it. An old rabbinic reading takes the
mourning at *Allon Bacuth* as doubled — for the nurse and for Rebekah, whose death it supposes
reached Jacob at the same moment — **and that is a harmonization the text does not offer.**

## Timeline / key events

- Sent from Paddan Aram with Rebekah, unnamed, as part of the bridal party — Genesis 24:59,
  [[A Wife for Isaac]]
- Dies and is buried under the oak below Bethel; the tree is named *Allon Bacuth* — Genesis 35:8,
  [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]]

## Appears in

- [[A Wife for Isaac]] — Genesis 24:59; **named in the passage only as *her nurse*.** She leaves her
  country in the same clause as the bride and the servant, and Genesis records nothing about her — not
  a word, not an action, not a name
- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:8; **given a name at her burial
  and never elsewhere.** One verse between two appearances of God, in a chapter that buries four
  things; the oak she is put under is renamed *oak of weeping*, and the weepers are not identified

## Related people

- [[Rebekah]] — her mistress, whom she followed out of Paddan Aram and whose own death Genesis never
  reports
- [[Jacob]] — Rebekah's son, in whose household she dies, by a route the book does not describe
- [[Abraham's Servant]] — the other unnamed member of the party at 24:59, who has a page for the same
  reason she now does: Genesis gives him everything except a name
