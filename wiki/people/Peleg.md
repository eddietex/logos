# Peleg

## Overview

A name with a reason attached, and the reason is the only causal statement in
[[The Table of Nations]].

> **25** To Eber were born two sons. The name of the one was Peleg, for in his days the earth was
> divided. His brother’s name was Joktan.

*Peleg*, from *pālag*, **to divide, to split**; *"for in his days the earth was divided"* is *kî
bəyāmāyw niplǝgâ hāʾāreṣ*, the passive of the same root, so the verse is a pun of the kind Genesis
makes constantly — *nōaḥ* and *yenaḥămēnû* at 5:29, *bābel* and *bālal* at 11:9. Genesis 10 gives no
other etymology and offers no explanation for anything else in its thirty-two verses. Everywhere else
the nations simply *divide* (10:5, 32), *spread abroad* (10:18), and settle; here, and only here, the
chapter says that something happened.

**What was divided.** *ʾEreṣ* is the earth or the land, and by metonymy its population, which is how
Genesis uses it at 11:1 (*"the whole earth was of one language"*). Three readings have been given:

- **Babel.** The dispersal of [[The Tower of Babel]] is the only division of humanity Genesis
  narrates, and
  Peleg is fifth from Noah, which puts his generation squarely inside the Table's span. This is the
  standard reading and the one the chapter's placement invites. Against it, the verb is *pālag*, not
  the *pûṣ* of 11:4 and 11:8 or the *nāpaṣ* of 9:19, so it is an allusion rather than a
  back-reference.
- **The fork in his own family.** Eber's two sons are the division: Peleg's line runs to Abram
  (Genesis 11:18–26), Joktan's runs into Arabia and out of the narrative. The name would then be about
  the sentence it sits in. This reading is economical and explains why the note appears at exactly
  this name.
- **A physical division of the land** — continental drift, or the separation of landmasses by water.
  This appears in modern popular literature and has no support in the Hebrew, which uses *pālag* of
  dividing a channel for water (Job 38:25) and of splitting counsel or speech (Psalm 55:9), never of
  geology, and which in any case is describing something that happens *in his days*, within a single
  lifetime.

The wiki takes no position between the first two and notes the third as a modern reading without
ancient support.

**He does nothing else.** He is a name in the segmented Table and then a name with numbers in the
linear register that follows: thirty years old at Reu's birth, two hundred nine after it, two hundred
thirty-nine in all (Genesis 11:18–19) — less than half his father [[Eber]]'s span, and the first sharp
drop in the collapse of lifespans that runs from Noah's nine hundred fifty to Abraham's hundred and
seventy-five. He is in the Chronicler's Table (1 Chronicles 1:19, 25) and in Luke's genealogy of Jesus
(Luke 3:35).

**Why Genesis gives him nothing and his brother thirteen sons.** Joktan gets five verses, thirteen
descendants, and a territory *"from Mesha, as you go toward Sephar"* (10:26–30). Peleg gets a
sentence. That asymmetry is the Table's method: the branch that receives detail is the branch that is
being closed off, and the branch that receives nothing is the one the book will follow. Genesis does
the same with Cain against Seth, Ishmael against Isaac, and Esau against Jacob.

## Appears in

- [[The Table of Nations]] — Genesis 10:25, the chapter's one etymology and its one hint of a cause
- [[The Tower of Babel]] — not by name, but as the event his name is usually taken to date: the
  division of the earth Genesis 11:1–9 narrates falls inside his generation on the Table's own
  reckoning
