# Shem

## Overview

The eldest of Noah's three sons on the usual reading, and the one the rest of the Bible descends
from. Everything the canon eventually builds on him is still ahead of him at this point in Genesis;
in [[Noah and the Flood]] he is a name in a list, twice.

He is introduced at Genesis 5:32 with his brothers — *"Noah became the father of Shem, Ham, and
Japheth"* — and named again at 6:10 and 7:13 as one of the eight carried through the water. He is
given no act, no speech, no age, and no characteristic. That is worth stating plainly, because the
weight Shem carries later makes it easy to read him back into a narrative that does not yet contain
him.

**The name is *šēm*, which is the ordinary Hebrew word for "name".** Genesis has just used it, four
verses before his first appearance, in a phrase it clearly wants noticed: the offspring of the sons
of God were *"the mighty men who were of old, men of renown"* (6:4) — *ʾanšê haššēm*, literally *men
of the name*. And it will use it again at 11:4, when the builders of Babel say *"let's make ourselves
a name"* — *šēm* — and are scattered for it. Between those two grabs at a name sits a man who simply
*is* one, and whose line the promise runs through. Whether the narrator intends the play is not
provable, but Genesis 9:26 makes the name theological on its own terms: Noah's blessing is not on
Shem but on Shem's God — *"Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Shem"* — the only place in the primeval
history where God is identified by a human being's name, and the form the patriarchal formula will
take from Abraham onward.

**Birth order is genuinely unclear**, and the wiki notes it rather than settling it. The three are
listed as Shem, Ham, Japheth eleven times, which normally implies age order. But Genesis 9:24 calls
Ham *"his youngest son"*, which would put Ham third rather than second; and Genesis 10:21 describes
Shem in a phrase the Hebrew leaves ambiguous — either *"the brother of Japheth the elder"* or
*"the elder brother of Japheth"*. The listing order may therefore be theological rather than
chronological, putting first the son the narrative will follow, exactly as Genesis does with Isaac
over Ishmael and Jacob over Esau.

What follows him: he is the ancestor of the peoples of Genesis 10:21–31 — Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad,
Lud, Aram — and through Arpachshad the line of 11:10–26 runs to Terah and Abram. The modern term
*Semitic*, coined for a language family in the eighteenth century, is taken from his name and is
only loosely related to the genealogy that produced it.

## Timeline / key events

- Born to Noah in Noah's five hundredth year, first-named of three sons — [[From Adam to Noah]]
- Named among those who enter the ark, and enters it *"in the same day"* the flood begins —
  [[Noah and the Flood]]
- Comes out of the ark with his brothers a year and ten days after going in, named only as
  one of *"his sons"* — [[The Waters Recede]]
- Addressed by God for the first time, blessed with his father and made a party to the covenant with
  all flesh — [[The Covenant with Noah]]
- Covers his father with Japheth, and receives the blessing *"Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Shem"* —
  Genesis 9:18–29 *(not yet ingested)*
- Ancestor of Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, and Aram — Genesis 10:21–31 *(not yet ingested)*
- Fathers Arpachshad two years after the flood; the line runs from him to Abram — Genesis 11:10–26
  *(not yet ingested)*

## Appears in

- [[From Adam to Noah]] — named at 5:32 with his brothers, in the verse that leaves the register open
- [[Noah and the Flood]] — named at 6:10 and 7:13; given no act and no words
- [[The Waters Recede]] — not named at all; present as one of *"your sons"* in the command to
  disembark (8:16) and one of *"his sons"* in the doing of it (8:18)
- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — *"Noah and his sons"* (9:1), *"Noah and to his sons with him"* (9:8):
  addressed directly for the first time, and a covenant party in his own right, though still unnamed
  and still silent
