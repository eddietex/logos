# Shem

## Overview

The eldest of Noah's three sons on the usual reading, and the one the rest of the Bible descends
from. Everything the canon eventually builds on him is still ahead of him at this point in Genesis;
in [[Noah and the Flood]] he is a name in a list, twice.

He is introduced at Genesis 5:32 with his brothers — *"Noah became the father of Shem, Ham, and
Japheth"* — and named again at 6:10 and 7:13 as one of the eight carried through the water. He is
given no act, no speech, no age, and no characteristic. That is worth stating plainly, because the
weight Shem carries later makes it easy to read him back into a narrative that does not yet contain
him.

**The name is *šēm*, which is the ordinary Hebrew word for "name".** Genesis has just used it, four
verses before his first appearance, in a phrase it clearly wants noticed: the offspring of the sons
of God were *"the mighty men who were of old, men of renown"* (6:4) — *ʾanšê haššēm*, literally *men
of the name*. And it will use it again at 11:4, when the builders of Babel say *"let's make ourselves
a name"* — *šēm* — and are scattered for it. Between those two grabs at a name sits a man who simply
*is* one, and whose line the promise runs through. Whether the narrator intends the play is not
provable, but Genesis 9:26 makes the name theological on its own terms: Noah's blessing is not on
Shem but on Shem's God — *"Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Shem"* — the only place in the primeval
history where God is identified by a human being's name, and the form the patriarchal formula will
take from Abraham onward.

**Birth order is genuinely unclear**, and the wiki notes it rather than settling it. The three are
listed as Shem, Ham, Japheth eleven times, which normally implies age order. But Genesis 9:24 calls
Ham *"his youngest son"*, which would put Ham third rather than second; and Genesis 10:21 describes
Shem in a phrase the Hebrew leaves ambiguous — either *"the brother of Japheth the elder"* or
*"the elder brother of Japheth"*. The listing order may therefore be theological rather than
chronological, putting first the son the narrative will follow, exactly as Genesis does with Isaac
over Ishmael and Jacob over Esau.

### The garment, and a blessing that is not addressed to him — Genesis 9:22–27

In [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]] he acts for the first time, and he does it jointly with
[[Japheth]]: the two of them take a garment, lay it on both their shoulders, walk backwards into
their father's tent, and cover him with their faces turned away, *"and they didn't see their father's
nakedness"* (9:23). Six verbs, one grammatical subject, and no speech. It is the only recorded action
of his life, and it is an act of deliberate not-seeing set against [[Ham]]'s seeing and telling.

**What he receives is not a blessing on himself.** *"Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Shem"* (9:26) — the
first occurrence in Scripture of the *bārûk YHWH* formula that runs on through Genesis 14:20, 24:27,
Exodus 18:10, and 1 Samuel 25:32. Noah blesses God, and Shem's standing arrives obliquely, in the
genitive. He is given no land, no fruitfulness, and no promise of his own; what he is given is the
God, and **this is the first time in Scripture that God is identified by a human being's name**. That
is the form the patriarchal formula takes from Abraham onward — *the God of Abraham, the God of
Isaac, the God of Jacob* — and it starts here, in a drunk man's oracle, in the only place in the
primeval history where God is named after a person.

Note also which name he gets. Noah says **Yahweh** over Shem and **ʾĕlōhîm** over Japheth in the very
next verse, and nothing in the text comments on the difference. Genesis will spend forty chapters on
the line named with the covenant name.

### Introduced by a descendant — Genesis 10:21

[[The Table of Nations]] gives him twenty-six descendants and takes his branch **last**, which in
Genesis means it is the one being followed: Japheth's collateral line is cleared away first, then
Ham's, exactly as Cain's is run out before Seth's and Esau's before Jacob's. His sons are Elam,
Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, and Aram (10:22), and through Arpachshad the line of 11:10–26 runs to Terah
and Abram.

**He is introduced by a great-grandson, which happens to nobody else in the chapter.** Every other
entry is identified by its father; Shem is *"the father of all the children of Eber"* (10:21) — four
generations before [[Eber]] is born at 10:24. The Table flags which of its seventy the book intends
to follow before it lists a single name of his, and it does so with the word that stands one consonant
from *ʿibrî*, **Hebrew**.

**Elam and Asshur are the reason the Table is not a language chart.** Elamite is unrelated to any
known language family and is certainly not Semitic; Canaanite, listed under Ham, is Semitic and nearly
identical to Hebrew. Whatever organizes Genesis 10 — geography, political alignment, trade, historical
memory — it is not linguistic descent. The modern term *Semitic*, coined for a language family in the
eighteenth century, is taken from his name and is only loosely related to the genealogy that produced
it; the nineteenth century's *Hamitic* and *Japhetic*, and the racial taxonomies built on all three,
are further from the chapter still.

The phrase *"the elder brother of Japheth"* in the same verse is a third unsettled piece of evidence
about birth order, alongside the eleven listings and 9:24's *"youngest son"*: the Hebrew *ʾăḥî yepet
haggādôl* can equally mean *"the brother of Japheth the elder"*.

### The register, and the one date in it — Genesis 11:10–11

[[From Shem to Abram]] gives him the second of the Bible's two ten-generation registers, and hands
him the only absolute date in it: *"Shem was one hundred years old when he became the father of
Arpachshad **two years after the flood**."* Every other entry in Genesis 11 is relative to the one
before it; this clause is what ties the whole post-flood chronology back to Genesis 5's.

It also creates a friction Genesis does not resolve. Noah fathers his three sons at five hundred
(5:32) and the flood comes in his six hundredth year (7:11), so a Shem who is a hundred *two years
after* the flood was born when Noah was five hundred and two. Either 5:32 is a round figure for the
beginning of Noah's fathering — the same reading that has to be applied to Terah's seventy at 11:26
— or Shem is not the eldest of the three, which Genesis 9:24 and 10:21 already leave open.

His five hundred remaining years are the longest in the register, and the register does not say he
died. Genesis 11 drops the death formula of Genesis 5 entirely; adding his figures gives 600, and
placing them on the same reckoning has him dying in the year 2158 from creation — **thirty-five
years after Abraham**. On the text's own numbers the son of Noah is alive for the whole of
Abraham's life, and Genesis never once puts them in the same place. See
[[Ten Before and Ten After - The Registers of Genesis 5 and Genesis 11]].

## Timeline / key events

- Born to Noah in Noah's five hundredth year, first-named of three sons — [[From Adam to Noah]]
- Named among those who enter the ark, and enters it *"in the same day"* the flood begins —
  [[Noah and the Flood]]
- Comes out of the ark with his brothers a year and ten days after going in, named only as
  one of *"his sons"* — [[The Waters Recede]]
- Addressed by God for the first time, blessed with his father and made a party to the covenant with
  all flesh — [[The Covenant with Noah]]
- Covers his father with Japheth, walking in backwards, and hears *"Blessed be Yahweh, the God of
  Shem"* — [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]]
- Ancestor of Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, and Aram, and introduced as *"the father of all the
  children of Eber"* — [[The Table of Nations]]
- Fathers [[Arpachshad]] two years after the flood, at a hundred, and heads the second
  ten-generation register — [[From Shem to Abram]]

## Appears in

- [[From Adam to Noah]] — named at 5:32 with his brothers, in the verse that leaves the register open
- [[Noah and the Flood]] — named at 6:10 and 7:13; given no act and no words
- [[The Waters Recede]] — not named at all; present as one of *"your sons"* in the command to
  disembark (8:16) and one of *"his sons"* in the doing of it (8:18)
- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — *"Noah and his sons"* (9:1), *"Noah and to his sons with him"* (9:8):
  addressed directly for the first time, and a covenant party in his own right, though still unnamed
  and still silent
- [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]] — the garment and the backwards walk, and the oracle that
  blesses his God rather than him
- [[The Table of Nations]] — twenty-six descendants, his branch taken last, and an introduction that
  runs through Eber
- [[From Shem to Abram]] — Genesis 11:10–11, the head of the post-flood register and the holder of
  its only fixed date; on its own numbers he outlives Abraham by thirty-five years

## Related people

- [[Noah]] — his father, whom he covers without looking
- [[Ham]] and [[Japheth]] — his brothers, named with him every time he is named; Japheth acts with
  him at 9:23 as a single subject
- [[Canaan (son of Ham)]] — his nephew, consigned to him as *"a servant of servants"*
- [[Arpachshad]] — his son, born two years after the flood; the first person in the Bible born into
  the new world
- [[Eber]] — his great-grandson, and the descendant Genesis 10:21 introduces him by; on the
  register's numbers the one man who outlives him
- [[Peleg]] — Eber's son, through whom the line runs to Abram
- [[Abraham]] — nine generations down the register from him, and by its arithmetic his contemporary
  for a hundred and seventy-five years
- **His wife** — one of the eight in the ark, unnamed here and everywhere in Scripture
- [[Adam]] — ten generations back through [[Seth]]
- [[God]] — who is called, uniquely in the primeval history, *the God of Shem* (9:26)
