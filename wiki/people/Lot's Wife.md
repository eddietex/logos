# Lot's Wife

## Overview

**The whole of her in Genesis is one verse, and it is a clause and a consequence.**

> **26** But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt.

She has no name, no line of dialogue, no genealogy, and no scene before this one. She is a hand
being taken at 19:16 — *"the men grabbed his hand, his wife's hand, and his two daughters' hands"* —
and a verb at 19:26. Everything else about her is inference or later tradition.

**The verb is the point.** *Wattabbēṭ*, from *nābaṭ*, is the exact verb of the prohibition at 19:17:
*"Escape for your life! Don't look behind you"* — *ʾal-tabbîṭ ʾaḥăreykā*. Genesis writes the offence
in the words of the command, so that the sentence is legible without a word of explanation, and then
supplies no explanation. It does not say why she looked, what she was looking at, whether she
disbelieved, grieved, hesitated, or simply turned her head. **Every reading of her motive is
supplied from outside the text.**

*Mē-ʾaḥărāyw*, *from behind him*, is also doing work the English can carry: she is behind Lot, which
is where a fleeing household walks, and looking back is what the person at the rear can do without
stopping.

### The pillar

*Nəṣîb melaḥ*, **a pillar of salt**. The country she is standing in is the one place in the region
where that image is not a metaphor: the Lisan marl and the salt dome at Mount Sodom, at the southwest
foot of [[The Salt Sea]], throw up standing columns of rock salt as the softer material erodes away
from around them, and they weather and reform continually.

Josephus reports the pillar as still there and claims to have seen it (*Antiquities* 1.11.4). First
Clement 11 makes her *"a sign … set as a pillar of salt to this day"*. Wisdom 10:7 lists the region's
scars — *"a smoking wasteland … plants bearing fruit that does not ripen, and a pillar of salt
standing as a monument to an unbelieving soul"* — and it is Wisdom, not Genesis, that first assigns
her the unbelief. The wiki records the sequence: **Genesis gives an act, and the tradition supplies
the disposition behind it.**

### *"Remember Lot's wife"*

Luke 17:32. **Three words, and it is one of the shortest citations in the Gospels** — the only place
in the New Testament where Jesus points to a figure who has no name and one verse.

The setting matters. The saying sits inside the discourse on the day of the Son of Man, between the
sketch of Sodom's ordinary life (*"they ate, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they
built"*, Luke 17:28) and the sentence it introduces: *"Whoever seeks to save his life loses it, but
whoever loses his life preserves it"* (17:33). Immediately before it comes an instruction about
going back — *"let him who is on the housetop, and his goods in the house, not go down to take them
away"* (17:31). **In context she is an illustration of turning back toward property**, which is a
motive Genesis nowhere gives her; the wiki notes that Luke's use of her is itself an interpretation,
and the earliest one there is. See [[Remember Lot's Wife]].

### What Genesis does not say

- That she was from Sodom. Widely assumed and never stated; Genesis never gives her origin.
- That she doubted, disobeyed out of attachment, or loved the city. Wisdom 10:7 supplies unbelief,
  Luke supplies possessions, and later homiletics supplies a longer list.
- That the daughters saw it. The text does not say who noticed, or when.
- That Lot reacted. He does not speak again until 19:30–38, and never mentions her.
