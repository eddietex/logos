# Jethro

## Overview

**The priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law — the foreigner who takes in Israel's fugitive deliverer,
gives him a wife, employs him for forty years, and later teaches him how to govern.** He enters at
Exodus 2:16 as *"the priest of Midian"* with seven daughters, and is named **Reuel** at 2:18. From
3:1 onward Exodus calls him **Jethro** (*Yitrô*, perhaps *abundance* or *excellence*), and it is
under that name that he matters most. The wiki files him under *Jethro* for that reason; see *The
names* below.

**His first words are a rebuke for inhospitality.** His daughters come home early from the well and
report that *"an Egyptian delivered us out of the hand of the shepherds"* (2:19); he answers with
three questions and an order — *"Where is he? Why is it that you have left the man? Call him, that he
may eat bread"* (2:20). **The fugitive from Pharaoh's house is fed by a stranger's**, in the formula
of Abraham's welcome at Mamre. Moses *"was content to dwell with the man"*, and Reuel *"gave Moses
Zipporah, his daughter"* (2:21). See [[Moses Flees to Midian]] and [[Hospitality]].

**He is a priest outside the covenant line, and Exodus never names his god.** He stands with
[[Melchizedek]], *"priest of God Most High"* (Genesis 14:18), and [[Potiphera]], *"priest of On"*
(Genesis 41:45) — **foreign priests who bless or marry into the chosen line.** *Rəʿûʾēl* means
*friend of God*; whether the *ʾēl* is the God of Abraham, the narrative does not say. **What he says
in Exodus 18 is a confession made from outside**: *"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"*
(18:11), followed by a burnt offering, and Aaron and the elders eating bread with him *"before God"*
(18:12) — **the first burnt offering actually offered in Exodus is a Midianite's** (Moses asked Pharaoh for burnt offerings at 10:25, and the Passover is a sacrifice of another kind). Midian is Abraham's offspring
through [[Keturah]] (Genesis 25:2), so the man is kin at a distance.

**His blessing repeats his daughters' report.** *"Blessed be Yahweh, who has delivered you out of the
hand of the Egyptians"* (18:10) is the *nāṣal … miyyad* of 2:19, *"delivered us out of the hand of
the shepherds"* — the family that first called Moses a deliverer is the family that blesses God for
the deliverance.

### The names

| text | name | relation to Moses |
|---|---|---|
| Exodus 2:18 | **Reuel** | father of Zipporah |
| Exodus 3:1; 4:18; 18:1–12 | **Jethro** (*Yeter* at 4:18a in Hebrew) | *"his father-in-law, the priest of Midian"* |
| Numbers 10:29 | *"Hobab, the son of Reuel the Midianite, Moses' father-in-law"* | ambiguous — the appositive can attach to either |
| Judges 4:11 | **Hobab**, *ḥōtēn Mōšeh* | WEB *brother-in-law*; others *father-in-law* |

**Three standard explanations, none adopted**: one man with two names, or a personal name and a
title; *Reuel* as the clan father (grandfather of Zipporah) and *Jethro* as her actual father, which
reads *"their father"* at 2:18 loosely; or distinct traditions joined. **Hobab is the son of Reuel**
on the plain reading of Numbers 10:29, and so Moses' brother-in-law, which is how the WEB reads
Judges 4:11; the Hebrew *ḥōtēn* means a relative by marriage and is usually *father-in-law*. The
bare name *Reuel* also belongs to Esau's son, and the wiki keeps the two apart — see
[[Reuel (son of Esau)]].

### At the Mountain of God (Exodus 18)

**He hears, and comes.** *"Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard of all that God had done for
Moses, and for Israel his people"* (18:1), and brings Zipporah — *"after he had sent her away"*, at a time Exodus
does not narrate — and her two sons to the camp *"at the Mountain of God"* (18:2–5). **Moses goes out, bows, and
kisses him**; they ask *"each other of their welfare"* (*ləšālôm*), and Moses tells him the whole story (18:7–8).
**All three *šālôm* in Exodus are his** — *"Go in peace"* (4:18), this greeting, and the people going home *"in
peace"* (18:23) (WLC lemma search, Exodus).

**Then he confesses.** *"Jethro rejoiced"*; ***"Blessed be Yahweh, who has delivered you out of the hand of the
Egyptians"*** — the words God used of himself at the bush (*hiṣṣîl … miyyad miṣrayim*, 3:8; WLC); ***"Now I know
that Yahweh is greater than all gods"*** (18:9–11) — *"now I know"* is the angel's *ʿattâ yādaʿtî* at Moriah
(Genesis 22:12; WLC). **Whether this is a conversion the text does not say**: the confession is comparative, he
goes home to his own land, and Exodus never names another god as his. He *"took a burnt offering and sacrifices for
God"*, the first *ʿōlâ* actually made in Exodus, and Aaron and all the elders eat bread with him *"before God"*
(18:12). See [[Blessed Be God Who Delivered - Melchizedek and Jethro]].

**Then he counsels.** Watching Moses judge alone from morning to evening, he says ***"The thing that you do is not
good … the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to perform it yourself alone"*** (18:17–18), and lays out a
judiciary: Moses before God and teaching the statutes; *"able men which fear God: men of truth, hating unjust
gain"* over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens; the great cases up, the small ones settled below — ***"If you
will do this thing, and God commands you so"*** (18:19–23). **Moses *"listened to the voice of his father-in-law,
and did all that he had said"*** (18:24), and *"let his father-in-law depart"* (18:27). **From 18:13 on he is never
named, only *"Moses' father-in-law"*** (WLC). **Deuteronomy's retelling of the judges does not mention him**
(Deuteronomy 1:9–18), and Numbers has Moses ask *Hobab* to stay at the departure from Sinai (Numbers 10:29–32);
both are set out at [[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]] and in *The names*
above. See [[Jethro's Counsel]].

## Timeline / key events

- *The priest of Midian*, with seven daughters who water his flock — Exodus 2:16
- Asks why they are home early; told *"An Egyptian delivered us"* — Exodus 2:18–19
- Invites Moses to eat; Moses agrees to dwell with him; gives him Zipporah — Exodus 2:20–21
- Moses keeps his flock, and leads it to Horeb — Exodus 3:1, [[The Burning Bush]]
- Sends Moses back to Egypt: *"Go in peace"* — Exodus 4:18, [[Moses Returns to Egypt]]
- Hears of the exodus; brings Zipporah and her two sons to Moses at the Mountain of God — Exodus 18:1–7,
  [[Jethro's Counsel]]
- Blesses Yahweh, confesses him *"greater than all gods"*, offers a burnt offering, eats bread with Aaron and the
  elders before God — Exodus 18:9–12
- Advises Moses to appoint judges; Moses does all he said; returns to his own land — Exodus 18:13–27

## Appears in

- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — Exodus 2:16–21; **as Reuel, the priest of Midian.** Rebukes his
  daughters for leaving their rescuer at the well, feeds him, keeps him, and gives him Zipporah —
  the father of the house in the third betrothal-at-the-well scene, the first not kin to the groom
- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:1; **named, as *"Jethro, his father-in-law, the priest of Midian"*,
  in the first verse.** The first use of the name *Jethro*; the flock Moses is grazing when God calls
  him is his
- [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] — Exodus 4:18; **asked for leave, and gives it: *"Go in peace"*.** Moses
  tells him only that he wants to see whether his brothers are alive, nothing of the bush. Named
  *Yeter* and *Yitrô* in the same verse; and *ḥōtənô*, *his father-in-law*, shares its root with
  Zipporah's *ḥătan-dāmîm* seven verses later. **The leave Laban never gave Jacob** (Genesis 31:20)
- [[Jethro's Counsel]] — Exodus 18:1–27; **the priest who blesses, sacrifices, and advises.** Brings Moses' family;
  *"Blessed be Yahweh, who has delivered you"*; *"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"*; the first burnt
  offering made in Exodus; *"The thing that you do is not good"*; the judges of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and
  tens; *"and God commands you so"*; sent home in peace
