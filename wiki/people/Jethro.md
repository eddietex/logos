# Jethro

## Overview

**The priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law — the foreigner who takes in Israel's fugitive deliverer,
gives him a wife, employs him for forty years, and later teaches him how to govern.** He enters at
Exodus 2:16 as *"the priest of Midian"* with seven daughters, and is named **Reuel** at 2:18. From
3:1 onward Exodus calls him **Jethro** (*Yitrô*, perhaps *abundance* or *excellence*), and it is
under that name that he matters most. The wiki files him under *Jethro* for that reason; see *The
names* below.

**His first words are a rebuke for inhospitality.** His daughters come home early from the well and
report that *"an Egyptian delivered us out of the hand of the shepherds"* (2:19); he answers with
three questions and an order — *"Where is he? Why is it that you have left the man? Call him, that he
may eat bread"* (2:20). **The fugitive from Pharaoh's house is fed by a stranger's**, in the formula
of Abraham's welcome at Mamre. Moses *"was content to dwell with the man"*, and Reuel *"gave Moses
Zipporah, his daughter"* (2:21). See [[Moses Flees to Midian]] and [[Hospitality]].

**He is a priest outside the covenant line, and Exodus never names his god.** He stands with
[[Melchizedek]], *"priest of God Most High"* (Genesis 14:18), and [[Potiphera]], *"priest of On"*
(Genesis 41:45) — **foreign priests who bless or marry into the chosen line.** *Rəʿûʾēl* means
*friend of God*; whether the *ʾēl* is the God of Abraham, the narrative does not say. **What he says
in Exodus 18 is a confession made from outside**: *"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"*
(18:11), followed by a burnt offering, and Aaron and the elders eating bread with him *"before God"*
(18:12) — **the first burnt offering actually offered in Exodus is a Midianite's** (Moses asked Pharaoh for burnt offerings at 10:25, and the Passover is a sacrifice of another kind). Midian is Abraham's offspring
through [[Keturah]] (Genesis 25:2), so the man is kin at a distance.

**His blessing repeats his daughters' report.** *"Blessed be Yahweh, who has delivered you out of the
hand of the Egyptians"* (18:10) is the *nāṣal … miyyad* of 2:19, *"delivered us out of the hand of
the shepherds"* — the family that first called Moses a deliverer is the family that blesses God for
the deliverance.

### The names

| text | name | relation to Moses |
|---|---|---|
| Exodus 2:18 | **Reuel** | father of Zipporah |
| Exodus 3:1; 4:18; 18:1–12 | **Jethro** (*Yeter* at 4:18a in Hebrew) | *"his father-in-law, the priest of Midian"* |
| Numbers 10:29 | *"Hobab, the son of Reuel the Midianite, Moses' father-in-law"* | ambiguous — the appositive can attach to either |
| Judges 4:11 | **Hobab**, *ḥōtēn Mōšeh* | WEB *brother-in-law*; others *father-in-law* |

**Three standard explanations, none adopted**: one man with two names, or a personal name and a
title; *Reuel* as the clan father (grandfather of Zipporah) and *Jethro* as her actual father, which
reads *"their father"* at 2:18 loosely; or distinct traditions joined. **Hobab is the son of Reuel**
on the plain reading of Numbers 10:29, and so Moses' brother-in-law, which is how the WEB reads
Judges 4:11; the Hebrew *ḥōtēn* means a relative by marriage and is usually *father-in-law*. The
bare name *Reuel* also belongs to Esau's son, and the wiki keeps the two apart — see
[[Reuel (son of Esau)]].

### What is ahead

Jethro's flock on Horeb (3:1); his blessing on Moses' return, *"Go in peace"* (4:18); bringing
Zipporah and the two sons back to Moses at the mountain of God, his confession and sacrifice, and his
counsel on delegated judges (18:1–27). **The man who asked *"Why is it that you have left the man?"*
will be the man who tells Moses he cannot judge the people alone** — the judge the Hebrews refused at
2:14 sitting *"to judge the people"* from morning to evening (18:13), and his Midianite father-in-law
telling him how to do it.

- Invites Moses to eat; Moses agrees to dwell with him; gives him Zipporah — Exodus 2:20–21

## Appears in

- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — Exodus 2:16–21; **as Reuel, the priest of Midian.** Rebukes his
  daughters for leaving their rescuer at the well, feeds him, keeps him, and gives him Zipporah —
  the father of the house in the third betrothal-at-the-well scene, the first not kin to the groom
- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:1; **named, as *"Jethro, his father-in-law, the priest of Midian"*,
  in the first verse.** The first use of the name *Jethro*; the flock Moses is grazing when God calls
