# Dinah

## Overview

**Jacob's only named daughter, and the one birth in Genesis 29:31–30:24 with no gloss, no speech, and
no reason given.**

> **21** Afterwards, she bore a daughter, and named her Dinah. (30:21)

**One clause.** Every one of the eleven sons in the passage gets a sentence explaining his name; the
daughter gets none. **Her name is the feminine of *Dan*** — the same root ***dîn***, *judgment* — **and
Genesis, which glosses *Dan* fifteen verses earlier, does not gloss hers.** The wiki does not fill the
gap. See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Names and Naming]].

**She is in the list because Genesis 34 will need her.** The book refers to Jacob's *daughters*, plural,
at 37:35 and 46:7, and names exactly one. **Genesis 46:15 counts her among Leah's children in the
descent into Egypt** — the only one of Jacob's daughters ever counted — and then never mentions her
again.

**And what she is given instead of an etymology is the second half of the pericope's rarest noun.**
[[Rachel]] says *"God has taken away my **reproach**"* three verses later (30:23, ***ḥerpâ***), and
**the only other occurrence of that word in Genesis is Dinah's brothers at 34:14** — *"we can't do this
thing, to give our sister to one who is uncircumcised; for that is a **reproach** to us."* **The two
verses that use the word in this book are the birth of the woman who never speaks and the vengeance
taken in her name.**

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

Genesis 34 is her chapter and she has no line in it. She *"went out to see the daughters of the land"*
(34:1), is seized by Shechem son of Hamor, and the chapter thereafter is conducted entirely between men:
a marriage negotiation, a circumcision demanded in bad faith, and a massacre carried out by [[Simeon]]
and [[Levi]] on the third day. **Jacob's objection is about consequences — *"you have troubled me, to
make me odious to the inhabitants of the land"* (34:30) — and the brothers get the last word:
*"Should he deal with our sister as with a prostitute?"*** **Genesis records no reaction from Dinah, no
speech, and no outcome for her**, and Jacob's deathbed, which has something to say about ten of his
sons, does not mention her.

**The wiki will treat that silence as the chapter's central fact rather than an omission to be filled
in**, on the same practice used for [[Lot's Wife]] and [[Job's Children]].
