# Dinah

## Overview

**Jacob's only named daughter, and the one birth in Genesis 29:31–30:24 with no gloss, no speech, and
no reason given.**

> **21** Afterwards, she bore a daughter, and named her Dinah. (30:21)

**One clause.** Every one of the eleven sons in the passage gets a sentence explaining his name; the
daughter gets none. **Her name is the feminine of *Dan*** — the same root ***dîn***, *judgment* — **and
Genesis, which glosses *Dan* fifteen verses earlier, does not gloss hers.** The wiki does not fill the
gap. See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Names and Naming]].

**She is in the list because Genesis 34 needs her.** The book refers to Jacob's *daughters*, plural,
at 37:35 and 46:7, and names exactly one.

**And what she is given instead of an etymology is the second half of the pericope's rarest noun.**
[[Rachel]] says *"God has taken away my **reproach**"* three verses later (30:23, ***ḥerpâ***), and
**the only other occurrence of that word in Genesis is Dinah's brothers at 34:14** — *"we can't do this
thing, to give our sister to one who is uncircumcised; for that is a **reproach** to us."* **The two
verses that use the word in this book are the birth of the woman who never speaks and the vengeance
taken in her name.**

### Genesis 34 — her chapter, in which she has no line

**She goes out, and after that every verb in the chapter is done to her.** *"Dinah, the daughter of
Leah, whom she bore to Jacob, went out to see the daughters of the land"* (34:1) — ***lirʾôt***, *to
see* — **and the next verb in the chapter belongs to [[Shechem (son of Hamor)]]**: *"the prince of
the land, saw her. He took her, lay with her, and humbled her"* (34:2). **The seeing changes hands in
one verse.**

**She is named six times in thirty-one verses and quoted none.** Taken, humbled, loved, spoken to,
asked for, defiled, negotiated over, and at last *"took Dinah out of Shechem's house"* (34:26) —
**the only indication in the chapter of where she has been for the preceding twenty-two verses.**
Genesis records no speech from her, no consent, no refusal, no reaction, and no outcome. **The wiki
treats that silence as the chapter's central fact rather than a gap to fill**, on the practice
already used for [[Lot's Wife]] and [[Job's Children]].

**Two things are decided about her without her, by people on opposite sides.** Shechem asks his
father for her and offers her family any bride-price they name (34:4, 11–12) — which is, in the
Torah's own later terms, the remedy the law will prescribe (Exodus 22:16–17; Deuteronomy 22:28–29).
Her brothers answer *"with deceit"* (34:13) and make the whole town circumcise itself, then
[[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] — **her full brothers, both [[Leah]]'s, in a household of four mothers** —
kill every male in it on the third day. **Neither the offer nor the massacre is put to her.**

**Her father's recorded objection does not mention her.** *"You have troubled me, to make me odious
to the inhabitants of the land… I am few in number"* (34:30) is entirely about the household's
safety. **The brothers get the chapter's last word, and it is the only sentence in it that names what
was done to her as the thing being avenged**: *"Should he deal with our sister as with a
prostitute?"* (34:31). See [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]].

**Later readers supply a blame the text does not.** *Jubilees* 30 and a strand of rabbinic midrash
read her going out as the fault, some of it by attaching her mother's *"you must come in to me"*
(30:16) to the daughter. **Genesis says she went to see the daughters of the land and stops.** The
wiki records the readings as reception and adopts none.

**The one parallel the Bible gives her is allowed to speak.** Tamar, David's daughter, is raped by
her half-brother Amnon in the same verb (***ʿānâ***) and the same formula (*"no such thing ought to
be done in Israel"*), argues her case at length, and is overruled anyway — with a silent father and
an avenging full brother on the far side of it, exactly as here. See
[[Dinah and Tamar - Two Rapes and Two Avenging Brothers]].

### What is still ahead

**Genesis mentions her once more and then not again.** Genesis 46:15 counts her among Leah's children
in the descent into Egypt — **the only one of Jacob's daughters ever counted** — and Jacob's deathbed,
which has something to say about ten of his sons, does not mention her at all.

## Appears in

- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 30:21; one clause, in a passage of twelve naming speeches,
  and hers is the one that is not given. **The feminine of a name the same passage glosses**, and the
  only daughter Genesis names out of the daughters it says Jacob had
- [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] — Genesis 34:1–31; **her chapter, and she has no line in it.**
  Named six times, the object of nearly every verb, held in Shechem's house through the whole
  negotiation, and taken out of it at the end without a word recorded either way
- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:35; **not named, and counted in a plural.** *"All his sons and
  all his daughters rose up to comfort him"* — **the roster of 35:23–26 had counted twelve sons and no
  daughters at all**, and she is the only daughter of Jacob the book ever names. The chapter also sends
  Joseph alone to [[Shechem (city)]], **the town her brothers emptied three chapters earlier**, and
  mentions neither her nor what happened there
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 46:15; **counted.** *"With his daughter Dinah"* — **she is one of only
  two women inside the seventy**, the other being her niece [[Serah]], **and the register explicitly
  counts out the wives who are actually on the road** (46:26). **It is the last mention of her in
  Scripture, and it is a number**

## Related people

- [[Leah]] — her mother, whose seventh and last child she is
- [[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] — her full brothers, who destroy a city over her and are cursed for it at
  49:5–7
- [[Shechem (son of Hamor)]] — the prince who takes her, then loves her, then asks his father for
  her; killed with his father on the third day
- [[Hamor]] — his father, who negotiates for her without once mentioning what his son did
- [[Jacob]] — her father, whose recorded objection at 34:30 is about the family's safety
- [[Zebulun]] — her full brother, born immediately before her
- [[Dan (person)]] — the brother whose name shares her root, and whose name the book does gloss
