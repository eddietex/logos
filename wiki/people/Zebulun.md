# Zebulun

## Overview

**Leah's sixth son, Jacob's tenth, and the one name in the list Genesis gives two etymologies in a
single verse.**

> **20** Leah said, “God has endowed me with a good dowry. Now my husband will live with me, because I
> have borne him six sons.” She named him Zebulun. (30:20)

**Two roots, one name.** ***Zēbed ṭôb zəbādanî*** is ***zābad***, *to bestow, to endow*; ***yizbəlēnî***
is ***zābal***, *to dwell with, to honour* — **and *Zəbulûn* can be heard as either.** Genesis does this
nowhere else in the twelve except at [[Joseph]], four verses later. See
[[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Names and Naming]].

**And the second clause is the sentence that undoes the tidy reading of [[Leah]]'s arc.** Her fourth
son, [[Judah]], was named *"this time will I praise Yahweh"* — the first of her speeches with no husband
in it, and often read as the point where she stops asking Jacob for what he will not give. **Six
children in, she is back to *"now my husband will live with me."*** **The wiki states this plainly
because it is the honest shape of the character**: Leah's naming speeches move toward God and then move
back, and Genesis narrates both directions without comment.

### What is still ahead

This page grows as those pericopes arrive; what follows is the shape of it.

Jacob's blessing puns the *dwell* root a third time and sends the tribe to sea: *"Zebulun will **dwell**
at the haven of the sea. He will be for a haven of ships"* (49:13). **The allotment in Joshua
19:10–16 does not actually reach the coast**, which is one of the standing puzzles of the tribal
geography, and neither Genesis nor Joshua remarks on it. Moses' blessing pairs him with [[Issachar]] —
*"Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out"* (Deuteronomy 33:18) — as the birth order does.

**The tribe's best moment is Judges 5:18**, where Deborah's song names Zebulun and Naphtali as *"a
people who jeopardized their lives to the deaths"* on the heights of the field. And **Isaiah 9:1 pairs
Zebulun and Naphtali as the first land to be brought into contempt and the first to see a great
light** — the verse Matthew 4:13–16 quotes over Capernaum, making these two tribes' territory the
ground Jesus' ministry begins on.

## Appears in

- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 30:19–20; the tenth son, and **the only name in the
  passage given two Hebrew roots in one verse.** His mother's speech is also the one that shows her
  returning to the argument she had seemed to leave behind at Judah's birth
- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:23; **a name in a list.** Last of
  Leah's six in the Bible's first complete roster of the twelve, which orders the sons by mother
  rather than by age
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:1–38; **one of the ten who go down, and he
  acts and speaks only as part of a plural.** The chapter gives *"they said"* and *"they said to one
  another"* and never separates them: **the protest of innocence, the words *"we are twelve brothers…
  and one is no more"*, and the confession of 42:21 — *"we are certainly guilty concerning our
  brother"* — are all spoken by the group and attributed to no one.** Only [[Reuben]] and [[Simeon]]
  are singled out. He bows to [[Joseph]] without recognising him (42:6–8), spends three days in
  custody, and carries home grain and money he did not expect

- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:7–13; **one of the plural, and the plural does the
  chapter's most important thing.** He protests the theft, joins the oath that would have killed
  whoever held the cup (*"With whomever of your servants it is found, let him die"*, 44:9), tears his
  clothes when it is found in [[Benjamin]]'s sack, **and turns his donkey around** — with the road to
  Canaan open, a court's word that he is *blameless*, and nothing but [[Benjamin]] to go back for
  (44:13). **Genesis records the decision in nine words and attributes it to no one.** After 44:13 the
  ten have no further speech; [[Judah]] speaks for all of them
