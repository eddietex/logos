# Sisera's Mother

## Overview

### At the window — Judges 5:28–30

The last figure in the Song of Deborah, and the only one on the enemy side who speaks. She is unnamed,
known only as *Sisera's mother* (*ʾēm sîsərāʾ*, 5:28, WLC). *Through the window she looked out, and
cried: Sisera's mother looked through the lattice. 'Why is his chariot so long in coming? Why do the
wheels of his chariots wait?'* (5:28). Her *wise ladies* answer, and *she returned answer to herself*
(5:29): *Have they not found, have they not divided the plunder? A lady, two ladies to every man; to
Sisera a plunder of dyed garments, a plunder of dyed garments embroidered, of dyed garments embroidered
on both sides, on the necks of the plunder?* (5:30).

**What the reader knows and she does not.** By the time she speaks, the Song has already told her son's
death at Jael's feet (5:26–27). The prose had told it too (4:21–22). She is waiting for a chariot he had
abandoned (4:15). The Song stops before she learns, and its next words are *So let all your enemies
perish, Yahweh* (5:31).

**What she imagines.** The plunder she pictures is women, *raḥam raḥămātayim*, literally *a womb, two
wombs* (5:30, WLC), which WEB renders *a lady, two ladies*, and dyed and embroidered cloth. So the
Song ends its account of the enemy with a mother picturing Israel's women taken, set against Jael, the
woman who killed her son. The Song does not comment on her; whether the scene is meant as taunt, irony,
or something closer to pity is the reader's to weigh, and the wiki does not decide.

**Two mothers.** Deborah calls herself *a mother in Israel* (*ʾēm bəyiśrāʾēl*, 5:7, WLC); this is a
mother in Canaan, *ʾēm sîsərāʾ*. The Song uses the same noun for both. The contrast is a reading the
Song does not state.

**The window.** The scene shares its verb and nouns with Abimelech at the window in Genesis 26:8 and
the father at his lattice in Proverbs 7:6; see
[[Through the Window - Proverbs 7.6, Genesis 26.8, and Judges 5.28]].
