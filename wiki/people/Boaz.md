# Boaz

## Overview

A landowner of [[Bethlehem]] in the days of the judges, a kinsman of [[Elimelech]], and in the book of
[[Ruth]] the man who redeems Elimelech's land and marries [[Ruth (person)]]. The genealogy that ends the
book makes him the son of Salmon and the father of Obed, David's grandfather (4:21–22; not yet ingested).

**Introduced by the narrator.** *Naomi had a relative of her husband's, a mighty man of wealth, of the
family of Elimelech, and his name was Boaz* (2:1). The reader learns this before Ruth or Naomi says a
word about him. *Gibbôr ḥayil* (WLC) is the phrase used of Gideon (Judges 6:12), Jephthah (11:1), and
Saul's father Kish (1 Samuel 9:1); *ḥayil* is strength, wealth, or worth, and the WEB reads *wealth*
here. At 3:11 he calls Ruth *a worthy woman*, *ʾēšeṯ ḥayil*. See
[[A Worthy Woman - Proverbs 12.4, 31.10, and Ruth 3.11]].

### In the field — Ruth 2

He arrives from town and greets his reapers, *May Yahweh be with you*; they answer, *May Yahweh bless
you* (2:4). His first question about Ruth is whom she belongs to (2:5). He tells her to stay in his field
close to his young women, says he has ordered the young men *not to touch* her, and gives her the water
they draw (2:8–9). Asked why he notices a foreigner, he says he has heard *all about what you have done
for your mother-in-law*, and how she *left your father and your mother, and the land of your birth*, and
blesses her: *May Yahweh repay your work, and a full reward be given to you from Yahweh, the God of
Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge* (2:11–12). He feeds her at the meal until she is
satisfied, and orders his men to let her glean among the sheaves and to pull out grain for her and leave
it (2:14–16), going beyond what the gleaning laws require. Naomi blesses him and names him *one of our near
kinsmen*, a *gōʾēl* (2:20). See [[Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz]],
[[The Forgotten Sheaf - Gleaning in Leviticus 19 and 23, Deuteronomy 24, and Ruth 2]], and
[[Under His Wings - Ruth 2.12, 3.9, and the Refuge of the Psalms]].

He is drawn in the chapter as the man the harvest laws assume: he speaks of Yahweh to his workers,
protects the foreigner and the widow, and feeds her from his own table. The book gives no physical
description, no age except his later remark that Ruth did not go after *young men* (3:10), and no wife or
children before Ruth.

### At the threshing floor — Ruth 3

He winnows barley at night, eats and drinks, *his heart was merry*, and lies down at the end of the heap
of grain (3:2, 7). At midnight he wakes with a start and finds a woman at his feet (8). Asked to *spread
the corner of your garment over your servant; for you are a near kinsman* (9), he answers first with a
blessing, *You are blessed by Yahweh, my daughter*, and praises her kindness because she *didn't follow
young men, whether poor or rich* (10). He promises *all that you say*, because *all the city of my people
knows that you are a worthy woman* (11). Then he names the obstacle: *there is a kinsman nearer than I*
(12). He will not take the right out of turn, and he swears by Yahweh's life that he will redeem her if
the other will not (13). He keeps her until morning, sends her away before anyone can be recognized, and
says *Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor* (14). He fills her mantle with six
measures of barley, *Don't go empty to your mother-in-law* (15, 17), and goes into the city. Naomi's last
word about him is *the man will not rest until he has settled this today* (18). In the chapter he is a man
who has drunk at night and does nothing a reader of Genesis 19 or Judges 19 might fear; see [[Ruth at the Threshing Floor]] and
[[At Night with a Man Who Had Drunk - Genesis 19.30-38 and Ruth 3]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 3:1–18;
Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

### At the gate — Ruth 4:1–12

He goes up to the gate the next morning and sits down, and the nearer kinsman passes by. Boaz calls him
over without naming him, *pəlōnî ʾalmōnî*, *so-and-so* (WEB *friend*), and seats ten elders (4:1–2). He
keeps the order he swore to at 3:13: he offers the other man the land first, *If you will redeem it,
redeem it … I am after you* (4). Only when the man has said *I will redeem it* does Boaz name the widow
who comes with the field, *to raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance* (5). The man declines and
draws off his shoe, and Boaz declares before the elders and all the people that he has bought all that
was Elimelech's, Chilion's, and Mahlon's, and acquired *Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon … to be my
wife* (9–10). The town blesses him: *treat you worthily in Ephrathah, and be famous in Bethlehem* (11) —
*ḥayil* again, the word the narrator used of him at 2:1. Whether he managed the order of disclosure to
bring about the refusal is a question readers ask; the text reports the words and not his intent. See
[[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]] and [[The Nearer Kinsman (Ruth)]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 4:1–12; Hebrew WLC, 2026-11-17.

**Ahead.** *So Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife* (4:13). Not yet ingested.

**Traditions recorded elsewhere.** A later Jewish tradition identifies the judge [[Ibzan]] of Bethlehem
with Boaz (Bava Batra 91a; recalled, not verified); Judges makes no such link. 1 Chronicles 2:11–12 and
Matthew 1:5 also list him in the line of David (by reference; not yet ingested, and not checked here).
