# Tamar

## Overview

**The daughter-in-law of [[Judah]], and the first person in Scripture declared more righteous than
someone else.**

> **26** Judah acknowledged them, and said, “She is more righteous than I, because I didn’t give her to
> Shelah, my son.” (38:26)

***Tāmār*** means ***date palm*** — the tree that is the standing biblical image of the flourishing
righteous (*"the righteous will flourish like the palm tree"*, Psalm 92:12), and a name given to two
more women in the Hebrew Bible, both in David's house.

**Genesis gives her no father, no town, and no people.** It names Judah's wife as *"Shua's daughter"*,
a Canaanite, in the same sentence as her father — **and about Tamar's origin it says nothing at all.**
She is the only person in the chapter whose background is withheld, and **the silence has been filled
both ways for two thousand years**: Jubilees 41:1 makes her an Aramean, several rabbinic readings make
her an Israelite, and a long tradition makes her a Canaanite on the assumption that Judah married
locally twice. **The wiki adopts none of them.** See [[Judah and Tamar]].

### What happens to her, in order

**She is given to [[Er]], who dies** — *"wicked in Yahweh's sight"*, with no offence specified. **She
is given to [[Onan]] under the levirate duty, and he defrauds her of the only thing it was for** —
*"lest he should give offspring to his brother"* — **and dies too.** Then Judah sends her back to her
father's house *"until Shelah, my son, is grown up"*, and the narrator supplies the intention the
sentence omits: *"lest he also die, like his brothers."*

**That leaves her in the worst position the culture had.** She is **sent home but not released**: still
bound to Judah's house, so she cannot remarry; not taken into it, so she cannot bear. **There is no
remedy available to her.** The release ceremony of Deuteronomy 25:7–10 — in which the woman herself
brings the refusing brother before the elders, pulls off his sandal, and spits — **is four books and
several centuries away**, and it is the mechanism this chapter exists in the absence of. See
[[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

### What she does

**Genesis states her motive before she acts, which it does for nobody else in the chapter.**

> **14** …for she saw that Shelah was grown up, and she wasn’t given to him as a wife.

**Not desire, not revenge, not destitution — breach of contract.** She removes the garments of her
widowhood, covers herself with the ***ṣāʿîp*** (a veil the Hebrew Bible mentions in only one other
place — [[Rebekah]]'s, at 24:65), and sits at the gate of [[Enaim]] on the road to [[Timnah]] where
her father-in-law is going to shear.

**Then she negotiates.** He offers a young goat; **she asks what he will leave as security until it
comes**, and names the three items: ***his signet, his cord, and his staff***. **She does not ask for
money. She asks for his identity** — the seal that signs his contracts and the staff that shows his
standing — and he hands them over.

**Three months later she is sentenced to burn on hearsay, and she still does not name him.** She sends
the pledge back privately, with six words: *"By the man, whose these are, I am with child. Please
discern whose are these."* ***Hakker-nāʾ*** — **the same phrase Judah and his brothers used on their
father over a bloodied coat, one chapter earlier** (37:32). See
[[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]].

**It is the most restrained act in Genesis and it is also the riskiest.** She has the evidence to
destroy him in front of the town and she gives it to him in private instead. **If he denies the seal,
she burns.**

### What comes of it

**She bears twins** — [[Perez]] and [[Zerah]] — and **Genesis never mentions her again.** The rest of
Scripture does:

- **Ruth 4:12.** The elders of Bethlehem bless Boaz's marriage in her name: *"let your house be like
  the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah."* **Two childless widows owed a levirate duty, two men
  who had to be made to perform it, one town** — and the book of Ruth draws the comparison itself.
- **Ruth 4:18–22; 1 Chronicles 2:3–5.** Perez heads the genealogy that runs to **David**.
- **Matthew 1:3.** *"Judah became the father of Perez and Zerah by Tamar."* **One of four women named
  in the genealogy of Jesus**, and the only one whose story Genesis tells.

See [[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]].

### The verdict, and what it is about

***Ṣādəqâ mimmennî*** can be read *"she is more righteous than I"*, which is how the WEB and most
versions take it, or as an absolute — ***she* is in the right, not I** — which the grammar also allows.
**On either reading the important thing is the reason Judah attaches**: *"because I didn't give her to
Shelah, my son."* **He does not weigh her deception against his**; he identifies his own breach of
obligation as the wrong, and hers as the response to it. See [[Righteousness]].

**The wiki does not sand this down.** She disguises herself, solicits her father-in-law, and conceives
by him — and **Genesis records no penance, no rebuke, and no divine comment.** What it records is the
verdict of the man she deceived, the two sons she bore, and the genealogies that keep her. **It is one
of the clearest places in Scripture where narrative approval and moral tidiness come apart**, and the
schema's instruction is to state that rather than resolve it.

## Appears in

- [[Judah and Tamar]] — Genesis 38:6–30; **the whole of her.** Twice widowed and sent home, she takes a
  pledge instead of a price, declines to name the man who is about to have her burned, and is declared
  righteous by him in three Hebrew words. **She speaks five times and the narrator states her motive
  before she acts** — both of which Genesis does for no other woman in the book

## Note on the name

**Two other women in Scripture are called Tamar, both in David's house**: his daughter, raped by her
half-brother Amnon (2 Samuel 13), and Absalom's daughter (2 Samuel 14:27). **The first of them is
already discussed on** [[Dinah and Tamar - Two Rapes and Two Avenging Brothers]] **and will need a
page when Samuel is ingested** — at which point this page is renamed
`Tamar (daughter-in-law of Judah)` and the other becomes `Tamar (daughter of David)`, per `AGENTS.md`,
with the inbound links fixed in the same pass. **Until a second page exists there is no collision**,
so this one keeps the bare title for now. There is also a place called Tamar (Ezekiel 47:19; 48:28).
