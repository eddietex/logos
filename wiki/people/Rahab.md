# Rahab

## Overview

### Salmon's wife in Matthew — Matthew 1:5

Matthew 1:5 reads *Salmon became the father of Boaz by Rahab* (WEB at bible-api.com, 2026-11-17; not yet
ingested). Neither Joshua nor Ruth gives her a husband: Joshua 6:25 says only that *she lives in the middle
of Israel to this day*, and Ruth's genealogy (4:20–21) names Salmon and Boaz with no mothers. Matthew does
not say his Rahab is the woman of Jericho; the identification is the usual reading, and would make her
Boaz's mother. See [[The Generations of Perez]], [[Salmon]], and [[From Perez to David - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, Matthew 1, and Luke 3]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 6:25; Ruth 4:20–21.

### The fall of Jericho — Joshua 6:1–27

The promised rescue is now accomplished: the spies bring her family and possessions out, initially outside Israel’s camp (22–23). Verse 25 then locates her among Israel to the narrator’s day. No admission rite or interval is specified; her sheltering of the messengers is the stated reason for survival.

See [[The Fall of Jericho]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 6:1–27; comparisons identified above.


A prostitute living in Jericho whose house is on the wall (1, 15). She shelters Joshua’s spies, confesses Yahweh’s sovereignty, and negotiates protection for her household. Her occupation is stated; the story does not reduce her to it. She is not the poetic Rahab mentioned in Job.

Source: stored WEB Joshua 2:1–24.

### Michal at the window — 1 Samuel 19:12

*Michal let David down through the window*, *wattōreḏ … bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn* (1 Samuel 19:12, WLC), is the sentence of
Joshua 2:15, *she let them down by a cord through the window*. In the Hebrew files checked these are the only two
women who let anyone out through a window, and both turn the king's men away with a false word. See
[[Let Down Through the Window - Rahab and Michal]] and [[Michal Helps David Escape]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 2:15;
1 Samuel 19:11–17. Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-28.

## Timeline / key events

- Hides the spies under flax and misdirects the king’s messengers (4–7).
- Confesses what she has heard and asks an oath for her family (9–14).
- Lowers the spies through her window, directs their escape, and ties the scarlet line (15–21).
