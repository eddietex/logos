# Lemuel's Mother

## Overview

The unnamed mother credited with teaching the words preserved in Proverbs 31:1–9. She
addresses [[Lemuel]] as the son of her womb and of her vows, speaks directly about the dangers
of royal self-indulgence, and requires advocacy for the poor. Her teaching is explicitly
preserved; she is not merely an unnamed mother mentioned beside a father’s instruction.

Her personal name, country, and the circumstances of her vows are not supplied. The page uses
her relationship to Lemuel as its name rather than inventing an identification with Bathsheba,
a royal office, or a birth narrative. The heading’s WEB term revelation does not itself narrate
a prophetic call. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 31:1–9.

### The closing poem of Proverbs

[[The Worthy Woman]] follows her teaching in Proverbs 31:10–31, but does not name its
speaker or identify the praised woman as Lemuel’s mother. The two units both value wise
speech and care for the poor. Their adjacency supports comparison while leaving authorship
and the subject’s identity unsettled. No extra life event is inferred. Source: stored WEB.
