# Solomon

## Overview

Song of Solomon 1:1 is now ingested in [[Love Better Than Wine]]. Its heading explicitly associates the
song with Solomon, while verses 2–4 do not name the beloved or identify the woman.
The king's rooms are a royal reference, not proof that every lover's voice is Solomon's.
Source: newly stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:1–4.

In [[Dark and Lovely]] his name appears inside an image. The woman is lovely *"like Solomon's
curtains"* (Song of Solomon 1:5), set in parallel with Kedar's tents. The comparison assumes royal
furnishings worth admiring and makes no claim about his presence in the scene.
Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:5.

Proverbs 30:1 now introduces [[Agur]] under his own heading: see [[The Words of Agur]]. This is
an attribution boundary, not an appearance by Solomon; the words of 30:1–14 are not assigned to him.


Son of [[David]] and Bathsheba, third king of Israel, builder of the temple, and the Old Testament's
emblem of wisdom. The wiki meets him first not in the narrative of Samuel and Kings, which has not
yet been ingested, but in the heading of the book that carries his name: *"The proverbs of Solomon,
the son of David, king of Israel"* (Proverbs 1:1). See [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]].

The historical books give the background the heading assumes. At his birth *"Yahweh loved him"*, and
sent Nathan to name him *Jedidiah*, *"for Yahweh's sake"* (2 Samuel 12:24–25). As king he asked for
*"an understanding heart to judge your people, that I may discern between good and evil"* (1 Kings
3:9), and was told *"there has been no one like you before you, and after you none will arise like
you"* (3:12). His wisdom *"excelled the wisdom of all the children of the east and all the wisdom of
Egypt"*; *"He spoke three thousand proverbs; and his songs numbered one thousand five"*, and *"spoke of
trees … of animals, of birds, of creeping things, and of fish"* (4:29–33). The phrase *"the children
of the east"* is the one Job 1:3 uses of Job, which places Solomon's wisdom and Job's world in the
same international tradition. See [[Wisdom]].

**The wiki's pages attribute to him only what the texts attribute.** Proverbs names him at 1:1, 10:1,
and 25:1, and also contains collections it assigns to *"the wise"* (22:17; 24:23), to Agur (30:1), and
to Lemuel (31:1), with one Solomonic collection *"copied out"* by Hezekiah's men (25:1). Song of
Solomon is headed *"which is Solomon's"* (1:1). Ecclesiastes is the words of *"the Preacher, the son of
David, king in Jerusalem"* (1:1) and does not name him. See
[[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]].

**The same histories that celebrate his wisdom record his failure.** *"When Solomon was old, his wives
turned away his heart after other gods; and his heart was not perfect with Yahweh his God, as the heart
of David his father was"* (1 Kings 11:4). Nehemiah cites him as the warning case: *"among many nations
was there no king like him … Nevertheless foreign women caused even him to sin"* (Nehemiah 13:26). The
wisest king's book warns, at length, against exactly the seduction 1 Kings 11 describes — a tension
the wiki will take up when the lectures of Proverbs 5–7 are ingested. Jesus names him twice: *"even
Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these"* (Matthew 6:29), and *"someone greater than
Solomon is here"* (Matthew 12:42).

Quotations from Samuel, Kings, Nehemiah, Song of Solomon, Ecclesiastes, and Matthew on this page are
WEB, checked against bible-api.com (2026-10-25). Ecclesiastes 1:1–11 is ingested in
[[Vanity of Vanities]]; Song of Solomon 1:1–4 is now ingested in [[Love Better Than Wine]].
Both are checked against stored WEB; Samuel, Kings, Nehemiah, and Matthew remain unstarted.

Proverbs 4:3–4 recalls a teacher receiving instruction from his father. The heading at
1:1 makes a David–Solomon association possible, but 4:1–9 names neither man.
[[Get Wisdom]] records the scene as unnamed family instruction, not an established event in
Solomon's biography. The mother's only child in the WEB (4:3) is not independent evidence
for the composition of the royal household. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 1:1; 4:1–9.

Proverbs 8:15–18 does not name him, but it states as Wisdom's claim what 1 Kings 3 narrates of him:
*"By me kings reign"* (8:15), and *"With me are riches, honor"* (8:18, *ʿōšer wəḵāḇôḏ*), the pair God
gave Solomon *"that which you have not asked, both riches and honor"* (1 Kings 3:13, *gam-ʿōšer
gam-kāḇôḏ*). See [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; 1 Kings 3:13 WEB at
bible-api.com; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

Proverbs 10:1 repeats his name at the beginning of the sentence collection.
[[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]] treats this as the collection's attribution, not as evidence that
the wise son of its opening saying is Solomon himself, or that the father and mother are
identifiable royal parents. The sayings turn to general household, work, and speech ethics. Source:
stored WEB Proverbs 10:1–32.

[[A False Balance]] (Proverbs 11:1–31) continues the collection attributed to Solomon in 10:1. He is
not named in this chapter, and its counselors, traders, women, and households are not identified as
people in his biography. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 11:1–31.

[[Whoever Loves Correction]] (Proverbs 12:1–28) continues the collection attributed to Solomon at
10:1. Correction, counsel, household life, and labor are presented as general wisdom; neither the
wife nor any other figure is identified as someone in his biography. Source: stored WEB Proverbs
10:1; 12:1–28.

[[Hope Deferred]] (Proverbs 13:1–25) continues the collection attributed to Solomon at 10:1. Solomon
is not named in the unit, and the son and father it addresses are types, not figures in his
biography. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 13:1–25.

[[A Way That Seems Right]] (Proverbs 14:1–35) continues the collection attributed to Solomon at
10:1. Solomon is not named, and the king of 14:28 and 14:35, whose glory is a populous people and
whose favour goes to a wise servant, is a type rather than an identification of Solomon. Source:
stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 14:1–35.

[[A Gentle Answer]] (Proverbs 15:1–33) continues the collection attributed to Solomon at 10:1. Solomon is
not named, and no king appears in the chapter. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 15:1–33.

[[The Plans of the Heart]] (Proverbs 16:1–33) continues the collection attributed to Solomon at 10:1,
and holds its first cluster of royal sayings (16:10–15). Solomon is not named, and the king of those
sayings is a type rather than an identification. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 16:1–33.

[[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]] (Proverbs 17:1–28) continues the collection attributed to Solomon at
10:1. Solomon is not named; the *prince* of 17:7 and the *officials* of 17:26 are types. Source: stored
WEB Proverbs 10:1; 17:1–28.

[[A Strong Tower]] (Proverbs 18:1–24) continues the collection attributed to Solomon at 10:1. Solomon is
not named, and no king appears in the chapter. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 18:1–24.

### Proverbs 19 in comparison

Proverbs 19 continues the collection attributed to Solomon at 10:1. The chapter's ruler and king (6,
12), servant and princes (10), and household members (13–14, 18, 26–27) are not identified as people
in his biography. Its account of wisdom reaches from receiving instruction to controlling anger,
aiding the poor, and recognizing the limits of human plans.

See [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] . Sources: stored WEB references cited above;
Proverbs 10:1; 19:1–29.

### Proverbs 20 in comparison

Proverbs 20 remains within the collection attributed to Solomon at 10:1. Its kings are generic
figures: feared like a lion, discerning in judgment, winnowing the wicked, and upheld by love
and faithfulness (2, 8, 26, 28). None is identified as Solomon. The unit treats sobriety,
commerce, counsel, parents, vows, and inward character as matters of wisdom; it supplies no new
biographical event for him.

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Sources: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 20:1–30 and the Job passages cited above.

### Proverbs 21 in comparison

Chapter 21 belongs to the collection headed *"The proverbs of Solomon"* (10:1) and opens on the
king's heart in Yahweh's hand. No king is named; the saying is attributed to a king's collection,
not told about Solomon. See [[The King's Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 21:1.

### Proverbs 22 in comparison

Proverbs 22:16 ends the collection headed *"The proverbs of Solomon"* (10:1); verse 17 begins *"the
words of the wise"*. Solomon is not named in 22:1–16, and the attribution belongs to the collection.
See [[A Good Name]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 10:1; 22:16–17.

### Proverbs 22:17 — a collection not his

With 22:17 the book attributes its words to someone else: *"listen to the words of the wise"*. Solomon
is neither named nor credited in 22:17–24:22, and the wiki does not file that collection under him;
the next heading to name him is 25:1. See [[Thirty Excellent Things]] and
[[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs
22:17; 24:23; 25:1.

### Proverbs 25:1 — the men of Hezekiah

The third and last heading to name him opens the second Solomonic collection: *"These also are
proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out"* (25:1). The attribution of
chapters 25–29 is his; their transmission belongs to [[Hezekiah]]'s court, two and a half centuries
later. The chapter begins with kings searching out a matter and with an unsearchable royal heart
(25:2–3), but no king in 25:2–7 is named, and the wiki does not identify any of them with Solomon. See
[[The Glory of Kings]] and [[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]].
Source: stored WEB Proverbs 25:1–7.

### Lemuel’s heading — Proverbs 31:1

The next attribution boundary is now ingested in [[The Words of King Lemuel]]. The heading names
[[Lemuel]] and credits [[Lemuel's Mother]] with his instruction. It supplies no identification
with Solomon or Bathsheba. The anthology’s royal subject matter is not a warrant to collapse
its named speakers. This is an attribution comparison, not an appearance by Solomon.
Source: stored WEB Proverbs 31:1–9.

### The Preacher's heading — Ecclesiastes 1:1

[[Vanity of Vanities]] opens [[Ecclesiastes]], whose speaker is now tracked separately as
[[The Preacher (Ecclesiastes)]]. The heading associates him with David and Jerusalem without naming
Solomon. This is compatible with a Solomonic reading, but the name must not be supplied as an
explicit statement of the verse. The new passage belongs here as an attribution comparison,
not as a confirmed additional event in Solomon's life. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:1–11;
Proverbs 1:1.

### Wisdom above all before him — Ecclesiastes 1:12–18

In [[The Burden of Wisdom]] the Preacher claims *"great wisdom above all who were before me in
Jerusalem"* (1:16). The claim resembles the incomparable wisdom 1 Kings 3:12 says God gave
Solomon (quoted on [[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]]), but it
also implies several predecessors in the city, which sits awkwardly with Solomon's position
after David alone. Solomon is still not named. This remains an attribution comparison, not an
appearance. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:12–18.

### Great works and treasure — Ecclesiastes 2:1–11

[[The Trial of Pleasure]] catalogues houses, vineyards, gardens, pools, servants, flocks, silver
and gold, *"the treasure of kings"*, and singers (2:4–8). The picture resembles the building and
wealth 1 Kings 4–10 assigns to Solomon (paraphrased; those chapters are not stored), and 1 Kings
3:13's promise of *"riches and honor"* (quoted on
[[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]]). As in 1:16, *"all who were
before me in Jerusalem"* (2:9) implies several predecessors. Solomon is still not named. This is
an attribution comparison, not an appearance. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 2:1–11.

### The king's successor — Ecclesiastes 2:12

[[The Wise and the Fool Share One End]] asks *"what can the king's successor do? Just that which has
been done long ago"* (2:12), and 2:18–19 (not yet ingested) speaks of the man who will rule over the
Preacher's labor. Readers who identify the Preacher with Solomon think of Rehoboam (1 Kings 11:43, not
stored; paraphrased). Ecclesiastes names neither king nor heir. This is an attribution comparison,
not an appearance. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 2:12, 18–19.
