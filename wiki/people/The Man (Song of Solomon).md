# The Man (Song of Solomon)

## Overview

The male beloved addressed in [[Song of Solomon]], tracked as a literary role rather
than identified automatically with [[Solomon]]. In [[Love Better Than Wine]], his love
is valued above wine, his oils have fragrance, and his name is likened to oil poured
out (1:2–4). He is addressed and desired; no speech is explicitly assigned to him in
this opening unit.

Verse 4 mentions a king who has brought the woman into his rooms. Reading that king
as the beloved, and both as Solomon, is an interpretive possibility, not a named
identification in verses 2–4. This page does not posit a distinct rival shepherd or
supply a historical courtship plot. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:1–4.

In [[Dark and Lovely]] he is addressed as *"you whom my soul loves"* and pictured as a
shepherd. He grazes a flock, rests it at noon, and has companions with flocks of their own
(1:7). Having a royal figure in 1:4 and a shepherd in 1:7 does not by itself prove two
different men. Love poetry can cast one beloved in more than one role, and it can also
change speakers and scenes. This page keeps one role page and does not decide the question.
Whether he gives the answer of 1:8 is open, because the WEB supplies no speaker cue.
Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:5–8.

In [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]] a male voice plainly speaks for the first time, by the
forms of address: *"I have compared you, my love, to a steed in Pharaoh's chariots"* (1:9).
He praises her cheeks and neck (1:10), tells her *"you are beautiful … Your eyes are doves"*
(1:15), and calls her a lily among thorns (2:2). In the Hebrew of 1:15–16 the feminine *hinnāk
yāpâ* is answered by the masculine *hinnəkā yāpeh* (Sefaria, *Miqra according to the
Masorah*), so the exchange is between a man and a woman. She describes him in turn: a sachet
of myrrh, henna from En Gedi, an apple tree among the trees of the wood, whose shadow and
fruit she enjoys (1:13–14; 2:3). He brought her to the banquet hall, and his left hand is
under her head (2:4–6). A king sits at his table in 1:12. As in 1:4, whether that king is the
beloved is not stated. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:9–2:7.

In [[Rise Up, My Love]] he is first heard, then seen. He comes *"leaping on the mountains, skipping on
the hills"*, like a roe or a young deer (2:8–9), the animal of the oath in 2:7. He stands outside
*"our wall"* and looks in through the windows and the lattice. His speech is reported by the woman:
he calls her out twice, giving the spring as his reason (2:10–13). The voice that calls her *"my
dove"* and asks to see her face and hear her voice (2:14) is his by the feminine suffixes (Sefaria,
*Miqra according to the Masorah*). She says *"He browses among the lilies"* (2:16), which keeps the
shepherd image of 1:7, and sends him to the mountains of Bether (2:17). No king appears in this unit.
Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 2:8–17.

In [[Seeking Him by Night]] he does not speak and is not described. He is only *"him whom my soul
loves"*, four times (3:1–4), the phrase she first used of him at 1:7. He is absent from her bed,
sought through the city, asked about, and then found, held, and brought into her mother's house.
Every verb that touches him is hers. Where he was is not said. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon
3:1–5.

In [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]] the man is not named, not addressed, and does not speak.
[[Solomon]] is named three times: his carriage comes up from the wilderness with sixty armed men, and
the daughters of Zion are told to see him crowned *"in the day of his weddings"* (3:7, 9, 11). The man
has been called *king* (1:4, 12) and will call the woman *"my bride"* in the next units (4:8–5:1, not
yet ingested), so many readers take the procession as his wedding, with the man as Solomon or in the
role of Solomon. Others read the king's splendor as a contrast with the shepherd of 1:7. This page
does not identify the man with Solomon; the unit never uses either lover's voice. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 3:6–11; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

## Timeline / key events

- Song of Solomon 1:2–4 — the object of desire and praise in the opening poem;
  his fragrance and name are celebrated, without a personal name supplied.
- Song of Solomon 1:7–8 — sought as a shepherd resting his flock at noon; perhaps the one
  who answers her.
- Song of Solomon 1:9–2:7 — praises her and is praised; brings her to the banquet hall and
  embraces her.
- Song of Solomon 2:8–17 — comes over the hills, looks through the lattice, calls her out in
  spring, and is told to turn and be like a roe on the mountains of Bether.
- Song of Solomon 3:1–4 — absent at night, sought through the city, found, held, and brought
  into her mother's house; silent throughout.
- Song of Solomon 3:6–11 — not clearly present. A procession names King Solomon on his wedding
  day; whether that is this man is the open question of the unit.
