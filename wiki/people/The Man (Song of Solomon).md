# The Man (Song of Solomon)

## Overview

The male beloved addressed in [[Song of Solomon]], tracked as a literary role rather
than identified automatically with [[Solomon]]. In [[Love Better Than Wine]], his love
is valued above wine, his oils have fragrance, and his name is likened to oil poured
out (1:2–4). He is addressed and desired; no speech is explicitly assigned to him in
this opening unit.

Verse 4 mentions a king who has brought the woman into his rooms. Reading that king
as the beloved, and both as Solomon, is an interpretive possibility, not a named
identification in verses 2–4. This page does not posit a distinct rival shepherd or
supply a historical courtship plot. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:1–4.

In [[Dark and Lovely]] he is addressed as *"you whom my soul loves"* and pictured as a
shepherd. He grazes a flock, rests it at noon, and has companions with flocks of their own
(1:7). Having a royal figure in 1:4 and a shepherd in 1:7 does not by itself prove two
different men. Love poetry can cast one beloved in more than one role, and it can also
change speakers and scenes. This page keeps one role page and does not decide the question.
Whether he gives the answer of 1:8 is open, because the WEB supplies no speaker cue.
Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:5–8.

In [[Delight and the Unawakened Love]] a male voice plainly speaks for the first time, by the
forms of address: *"I have compared you, my love, to a steed in Pharaoh's chariots"* (1:9).
He praises her cheeks and neck (1:10), tells her *"you are beautiful … Your eyes are doves"*
(1:15), and calls her a lily among thorns (2:2). In the Hebrew of 1:15–16 the feminine *hinnāk
yāpâ* is answered by the masculine *hinnəkā yāpeh* (Sefaria, *Miqra according to the
Masorah*), so the exchange is between a man and a woman. She describes him in turn: a sachet
of myrrh, henna from En Gedi, an apple tree among the trees of the wood, whose shadow and
fruit she enjoys (1:13–14; 2:3). He brought her to the banquet hall, and his left hand is
under her head (2:4–6). A king sits at his table in 1:12. As in 1:4, whether that king is the
beloved is not stated. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 1:9–2:7.

In [[Rise Up, My Love]] he is first heard, then seen. He comes *"leaping on the mountains, skipping on
the hills"*, like a roe or a young deer (2:8–9), the animal of the oath in 2:7. He stands outside
*"our wall"* and looks in through the windows and the lattice. His speech is reported by the woman:
he calls her out twice, giving the spring as his reason (2:10–13). The voice that calls her *"my
dove"* and asks to see her face and hear her voice (2:14) is his by the feminine suffixes (Sefaria,
*Miqra according to the Masorah*). She says *"He browses among the lilies"* (2:16), which keeps the
shepherd image of 1:7, and sends him to the mountains of Bether (2:17). No king appears in this unit.
Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 2:8–17.

In [[Seeking Him by Night]] he does not speak and is not described. He is only *"him whom my soul
loves"*, four times (3:1–4), the phrase she first used of him at 1:7. He is absent from her bed,
sought through the city, asked about, and then found, held, and brought into her mother's house.
Every verb that touches him is hers. Where he was is not said. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon
3:1–5.

In [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]] the man is not named, not addressed, and does not speak.
[[Solomon]] is named three times: his carriage comes up from the wilderness with sixty armed men, and
the daughters of Zion are told to see him crowned *"in the day of his weddings"* (3:7, 9, 11).
The royal language of 1:4, 12 and his address *"my bride"* in
[[A Garden Locked and Opened]] (4:8–5:1) lead many readers to take the procession as his
wedding, with the man as Solomon or in the role of Solomon. Others read the king's splendor as a contrast with the shepherd of 1:7. This page
does not identify the man with Solomon; the unit never uses either lover's voice. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 3:6–11; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

In [[You Are All Beautiful]] he is the only speaker, for the first time over a whole unit. He describes
the woman from her eyes down to her breasts, using flocks, a pomegranate, David's tower, and twin
fawns (4:1–5), and sums it up: *"You are all beautiful, my love. There is no spot in you"* (4:7). He
repeats her line of 2:17, *"Until the day is cool, and the shadows flee away"*, and answers it with
his own intention: *"I will go to the mountain of myrrh, to the hill of frankincense"* (4:6). He is not
called king and does not yet call her *bride*. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 4:1–7; Hebrew read
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

In [[A Garden Locked and Opened]] he goes on speaking and calls her *"my bride"* and *"my sister, my bride"* (4:8–12).
He calls her down from Lebanon and the heights of Amana, Senir, and Hermon (4:8). She has
*ravished his heart* with one glance (4:9). He returns the woman's opening words, saying of her love
what she said of his, that it is better than wine (4:10; 1:2; same Hebrew, Sefaria). He pictures her
as a locked garden and a sealed fountain (4:12–15). When she invites him into *"his garden"* (4:16) he
answers in her words, *"I have come into my garden"*, and says he has gathered, eaten, and drunk
(5:1). He is not named and not called king. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 4:8–5:1; Hebrew read
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

In [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]] he is outside at night, knocking. His words are quoted by the woman:
*"Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled; for my head is filled with dew, and my hair
with the dampness of the night"* (5:2). He calls her *sister* without *bride* for the first time. When
she delays, he puts his hand in through the latch opening (5:4); when she opens, *"my beloved left;
and had gone away"* (5:6). He does not answer when she calls. Why he left is not said, and he does
not appear again in the unit. The verb *ʿāḇār*, *passed on*, is the root of the *flowing* myrrh on the
lock in 5:5 (Sefaria). Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 5:2–8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah*.

In [[Altogether Lovely]] he is the subject of the woman's sustained praise, not a speaker.
His head, hair, eyes, cheeks, lips, hands, body, legs, appearance, and mouth are described
through precious materials, animals, plants, and sweetness (5:10–16). She calls him her
friend as well as her beloved. At 6:2 she says he has gone to his garden to feed and gather
lilies; at 6:3 she affirms their mutual belonging. These are her statements, not a narrated
return or a speech of reconciliation from him. The poem does not name him Solomon or call
him king here. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 5:9–6:3.

In [[My Dove Is Unique]] he is understood to resume direct praise of the woman (6:4–9).
Her eyes overcome him, and the hair, teeth, and temples imagery of chapter 4 returns.
He calls her his unique dove amid queens, concubines, and innumerable virgins. Those women
are not explicitly called his own, and neither his kingship nor an identification with
Solomon is established. The speaker of the closing question (6:10) is uncertain: it may
continue his praise or express the other women's admiration. Renewed speech does not
supply a narrated reunion after 5:6. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 4:1–3; 5:6; 6:4–10.

In [[Return, Return, Shulammite]], the WEB marks the reply about gazing at the Shulammite as Lover (6:13). The extended
praise is understood as his: he admires her body and expresses his intention to climb
the palm tree and take its fruit (7:1–8). The captive king of 7:5 is an image, not proof
that this man is Solomon. The voice may change within 7:9; the English text leaves it unmarked.
Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 6:11–7:9.

In [[Come into the Fields, My Beloved]] (Song of Solomon 7:10–8:4),
His desire is affirmed by the woman (7:10), who invites him into the countryside
and promises him love and stored fruits (7:11–13). She imagines kissing him openly,
bringing him home, giving him wine, and being embraced (8:1–3). He has no narrated
reply here; these wishes do not establish a new event or identify him as her brother.
Source: stored WEB Song 7:10–8:4; comparisons at 2:7; 3:5; 5:8.

### Love strong as death — Song of Solomon 8:5–7

In [[Love Is Strong as Death]], he is the beloved on whom the woman leans (8:5), likely addressed in the memory
of the apple tree and the request for a seal on heart and arm. Neither his personal
name nor a reply is supplied; the mother mentioned in the address remains unnamed.

Source: stored WEB Song 8:5–7; comparisons use the stored Song and Proverbs 6:30–35.

In [[Our Sister and My Own Vineyard]], He is probably the unnamed man in whose eyes the woman found peace (8:10). The verse
does not identify him with Solomon, who is named in the vineyard account that follows.
There is no securely assigned speech of his in this unit.
Source: stored WEB Song 8:8–12.

## Timeline / key events

- Song of Solomon 1:2–4 — the object of desire and praise in the opening poem;
  his fragrance and name are celebrated, without a personal name supplied.
- Song of Solomon 1:7–8 — sought as a shepherd resting his flock at noon; perhaps the one
  who answers her.
- Song of Solomon 1:9–2:7 — praises her and is praised; brings her to the banquet hall and
  embraces her.
- Song of Solomon 2:8–17 — comes over the hills, looks through the lattice, calls her out in
  spring, and is told to turn and be like a roe on the mountains of Bether.
- Song of Solomon 3:1–4 — absent at night, sought through the city, found, held, and brought
  into her mother's house; silent throughout.
- Song of Solomon 3:6–11 — not clearly present. A procession names King Solomon on his wedding
  day; whether that is this man is the open question of the unit.
- Song of Solomon 4:1–7 — describes her body part by part and declares her all beautiful and without
  blemish; says he will go to the mountain of myrrh.
- Song of Solomon 4:8–5:1 — calls her his bride and a locked garden; enters the garden at her
  invitation.
- Song of Solomon 5:2–6 — knocks at night and asks her to open; is gone when she does, and does not
  answer her call.

- Song of Solomon 5:9–6:3 — described as altogether lovely and as her friend; said to be in his garden;
  silent throughout.
- Song of Solomon 6:4–10 — renews praise; her eyes overcome him; royal identity remains unproved.

- Song of Solomon 6:11–7:9 — questions the gaze and voices admiration and desire.

- Song of Solomon 7:10–8:4 — invited and desired; no answer narrated.

- Song of Solomon 8:8–12 — family and vineyard exchange. [[Our Sister and My Own Vineyard]]
