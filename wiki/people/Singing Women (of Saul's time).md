# Singing Women (of Saul's time)

## Overview

### Out of all the cities of Israel — 1 Samuel 18:6–7

The unnamed women who *came out of all the cities of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet king Saul, with tambourines,
with joy, and with instruments of music*, when the army came home from the killing of the Philistine (1 Samuel 18:6).
They *sang to one another as they played*: *Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands* (18:7). The
verb of their singing, *wattaʿănênâ* (WLC; morphhb lemma 6030 c), is the verb of Miriam's *answered them* at the sea
(Exodus 15:21), and the women going out with tambourines and dances repeat the scene of Exodus 15:20 and Judges 11:34
(stored WEB). They say nothing else and are not named again; their song is.

The text does not say whether they meant the second line as a slight. In Hebrew poetry a larger number often answers a
smaller one, as in *the ten thousands of Ephraim … the thousands of Manasseh* (Deuteronomy 33:17, stored WEB); Saul
hears it as a ranking: *What can he have more but the kingdom?* (18:8). The couplet goes on without them: the servants
of Achish quote it at Gath (21:11) and the Philistine princes at Aphek (29:5), each time as *they sang to one another
… in dances* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Saul's Jealousy of David]] and
[[Saul Has Slain His Thousands - 1 Samuel 18.7, 21.11, and 29.5]].

The period qualifier follows the convention for unnamed groups, like [[Ministering Women (of Eli's time)]]; it asserts
nothing about who they were beyond what 18:6 says.
