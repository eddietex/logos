# Shechem (son of Hamor)

The prince of the town of the same name. Not to be confused with [[Shechem (city)]], the place in
the pass between Gerizim and Ebal — **both pages carry qualifiers**, as `AGENTS.md` requires when a
person and a place share a name, because `scripts/link-check.sh` resolves links by basename and
Obsidian picks between two files called `Shechem.md` by an unstated rule. The collision was
scheduled on [[Hamor]]'s page a pericope in advance, at 33:19.

## Overview

**Genesis gives him four things in six verses, and they do not fit together: force, love, honour,
and a price.**

> **2** Shechem the son of Hamor the Hivite, the prince of the land, saw her. He took her, lay with
> her, and humbled her.
> **3** His soul joined to Dinah, the daughter of Jacob, and he loved the young lady, and spoke
> kindly to the young lady. (34:2–3)

**The three verbs of verse 2** — ***wayyiqqaḥ***, ***wayyiškab***, ***wayĕʿannehā***, *took, lay
with, humbled* — **end on the verb the Torah will use for rape** (Deuteronomy 22:24, 29) and that
2 Samuel 13 uses of Amnon. **The verb that opens verse 3** — ***dābaq***, *joined, clung* — **is the
one-flesh verb of Genesis 2:24**, and *"spoke kindly to"* is ***dibber ʿal-lēb***, *spoke to her
heart*, the idiom of courtship and comfort (Ruth 2:13; Hosea 2:14). **Genesis sets the two verses
side by side and reconciles nothing.** See [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]].

**He is the only character in the chapter the narrator compliments.** *"The young man didn't wait to
do this thing, because he had delight in Jacob's daughter, and he was honored above all the house of
his father"* (34:19) — said of the speed with which he submitted to circumcision, and said of a man
who has already taken by force the woman he is now buying. **He offers an open bride-price**, *"Ask
me a great amount for a dowry, and I will give whatever you ask of me, but give me the young lady as
a wife"* (34:12): ***mōhar*** and ***mattān***, **which is the remedy Exodus 22:16–17 and
Deuteronomy 22:28–29 will later prescribe for exactly this case.** The wiki records that and adopts
no verdict from it; the law is four books away and Genesis 34 cites nothing.

**He never speaks to Dinah, and Dinah never speaks at all.** His one request about her is made to
his father (34:4), in the same construction Samson uses at Judges 14:2, and his one speech in the
chapter is addressed to *"her father and to her brothers"* (34:11). He is killed with Hamor on the
third day after the circumcision he went first for, and **Dinah is taken out of his house** (34:26)
— the only indication in the chapter of where she has been throughout the negotiation.

**Whether the town is named for the man or the man for the town, Genesis does not say**, and the
place is already a place in Abram's day, four generations earlier (12:6). The wiki does not decide
it.

## Timeline / key events

- Sees Dinah, takes her, and humbles her; then loves her and asks his father for her as a wife —
  Genesis 34:2–4, [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]]
- Offers her father and brothers any bride-price they name — Genesis 34:11–12
- Puts the circumcision terms to the city assembly with his father, and is the first to accept them
  — Genesis 34:18–24
- Killed with Hamor by [[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] on the third day; Dinah is taken out of his house —
  Genesis 34:25–26

## Appears in

- [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] — Genesis 34:1–31; **the chapter's instigator and one of its
  two named dead.** He acts in verses 2–4, negotiates in 11–12, carries the assembly in 18–24, and is
  killed in 26 — and the narrator calls him *honored above all the house of his father* in the middle
  of it

## Related people

- [[Hamor]] — his father, who conducts the negotiation for him and dies with him
- [[Dinah]] — whom he takes, and then wants as a wife; she is never recorded speaking to him or about
  him
- [[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] — her full brothers, who propose the circumcision he accepts and kill him
  on the third day
- [[Jacob]] — whom he addresses as *her father*, and who takes no part in the negotiation
