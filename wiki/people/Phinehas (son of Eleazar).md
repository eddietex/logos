# Phinehas (son of Eleazar)

## Overview

### His father buried on his hill — Joshua 24:29–33

Eleazar is buried in *the hill of Phinehas his son, which was given him in the hill country of
Ephraim* (33). The verse names Phinehas only as the hill's owner; it does not say he became high
priest.

**Tension, flagged.** A hill *given* to a priest stands beside the statements that Levi received
no inheritance — *The offerings of Yahweh … are his inheritance* (13:14), *Yahweh … is their
inheritance* (13:33) — and that the priests' thirteen cities were in Judah, Simeon, and Benjamin
(21:4; all stored WEB). The text does not say who gave the hill or what kind of holding it was. Both
statements are recorded here without harmonizing them. See [[The Hill of Phinehas]].

See [[The Burials of Joshua, Joseph, and Eleazar]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 24:29–33; comparisons as cited.

### The altar by the Jordan — Joshua 22:10–34

Phinehas leads ten princes into Gilead to question the eastern tribes before the congregation
goes to war (13–14). The delegation recalls Peor, where he stopped the plague, and says Israel
has *not cleansed* itself of it *to this day* (17); see the contradiction recorded on the
passage page. He accepts the eastern tribes' answer: *Today we know that Yahweh is among us*, and
*you have delivered the children of Israel out of Yahweh's hand* (31). This is his first act in
the land, and it is a hearing, not a spear.

See [[The Altar of Witness by the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:10–34; comparisons as cited.

### The war against Midian — Numbers 31:1–54

Phinehas goes with the twelve thousand *with the vessels of the sanctuary and the trumpets for the alarm
in his hand* (6). The text does not say why he goes rather than his father, nor what the vessels were; he
has no further act in the chapter.

See [[The War Against Midian]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 31.

### Baal Peor — Numbers 25:1–18

Phinehas kills Zimri and Cozbi with a spear, stopping the plague (7–9). Yahweh calls this sharing
his jealousy and making atonement for Israel, and grants a covenant of peace and everlasting
priesthood to him and his offspring (10–13). This is explicit divine approval within this
narrative, not a general command for private violence.

See [[Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 25:1–18.


**[[Aaron]]'s grandson, and the last name in the genealogy of Moses and Aaron.**

> **25** Eleazar Aaron's son took one of the daughters of Putiel as his wife; and she bore him
> Phinehas. These are the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites according to their families.
> (Exodus 6:25)

**The list follows only one line past Aaron's sons, and it ends with him.** His mother is unnamed;
her father, Putiel, occurs nowhere else in the books the wiki has reached. ***Pînəḥās*** is commonly
derived from Egyptian *pꜣ-nḥsy*, *the Nubian*; the wiki records that as the usual etymology, not as
certain.

**The reason he is named becomes clear in Numbers.** At Peor he kills an Israelite man and a
Midianite woman, and is given *"a covenant of peace"* and an everlasting priesthood for his
descendants (Numbers 25:6–13, now ingested). **The genealogy that answers *who is
this Moses?* ends on the heir in whom Aaron's priesthood is confirmed.** See
[[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]] and
[[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]], which reads his zeal beside
Levi's sword at Shechem.

The wiki qualifies the page because a later Phinehas is a son of Eli (1 Samuel 1:3).
