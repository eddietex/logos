# Terah

## Overview

The man who set out for Canaan and stopped, and the only person in Genesis 11:10–32 who is given a
death.

He arrives as the last entry in the post-flood register — *"Terah lived seventy years, and became
the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran"* (11:26) — in a sentence built exactly like the one that
closed the first register: *"Noah was five hundred years old, then Noah became the father of Shem,
Ham, and Japheth"* (5:32). Same construction, a round age, three sons, and no total. Then Genesis
does something it has not done: it gives him a *toledot* of his own. *"Now this is the history of
the generations of Terah"* (11:27) opens the section that runs to 25:11 — the entire Abraham cycle.
**Genesis never gives Abraham a *toledot*.** The founding figure of Israel is filed under his
father's name, and his father is this man.

### What he does — Genesis 11:27–32

Four verses, and three of them are losses.

- He buries a son. *"Haran died before his father Terah in the land of his birth, in Ur of the
  Chaldees"* (11:28) — the first premature death in Scripture, and the only death recorded in the
  pericope apart from his own.
- He marries off the two who are left, to Sarai and Milcah — [[Sarah]] and [[Milcah]], in the names
  the wiki files them under — and one of them turns out to be barren (11:30), in a genealogy that
  has done nothing for seventeen verses but father sons.
- **He takes the family and goes.** *"Terah took Abram his son, Lot the son of Haran, his son's son,
  and Sarai his daughter-in-law … They went from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan"*
  (11:31). He is the subject of the verb. The first journey toward the land of promise in the Bible
  is undertaken by Abraham's father, before any promise has been spoken, and Genesis gives no reason
  for it — no call, no vision, no word from God, who does not appear in the chapter at all.
- **And he stops.** *"They came to Haran and lived there."* The destination is named and not
  reached. *"The days of Terah were two hundred five years. Terah died in Haran"* (11:32).

**The next verse is Genesis 12:1.** The primeval history ends on a man who started for Canaan and
died four hundred miles short of it, and the Abraham cycle opens by telling his son to leave.

### The two problems he creates

**Chronology.** Terah is 70 at 11:26 and dies at 205; Abram leaves Haran at 75 (12:4). If Abram was
born in Terah's seventieth year, he leaves sixty years before his father dies — and Acts 7:4 says
Abraham left Haran *"when his father was dead."* The resolution standard since antiquity is that
11:26 gives the age at which Terah *began* fathering, and Abram, listed first because he is the son
the book follows, was actually born when Terah was 130. That reads 11:26 exactly as it reads 5:32,
which is a point in its favour. It also makes [[Haran (son of Terah)]] the eldest, which the rest of
the text supports: Haran's daughter is old enough to marry, and his son travels as an adult.

**Religion.** Genesis says nothing about what Terah worshipped. Joshua 24:2 does: *"Your fathers
lived of old time beyond the River, even Terah, the father of Abraham and the father of Nahor, and
they served other gods."* That is the Bible's own verdict, delivered seven hundred years later, and
it turns the family's departure into something being rescued from rather than merely a migration.
Both [[Ur of the Chaldees]] and [[Haran (city)]] were principal centres of the moon-god *Sin*, which
is suggestive and is not what Joshua says; his charge is generic. Later tradition filled the silence
enthusiastically — the *Book of Jubilees* and the midrash *Genesis Rabbah* make Terah an idol-maker
and give Abram the story of smashing his father's stock, which is a homily on Joshua 24:2, not a
reading of Genesis. See [[Out of Ur - Terah's Journey and Abram's Call]].

**The name** *Teraḥ* has no Hebrew etymology and Genesis offers none. It has been connected to
*Til-ša-turaḫi*, a place on the Balikh near Haran, and — more loosely, and with the moon-cult in
view — to *yārēaḥ*, moon. Neither is secure. He is one of four names in the register that correspond
to towns in that small district.

**What the text withholds.** Terah is never evaluated. He is not called righteous or wicked, he
never speaks, God never addresses him, and the narrator passes no judgment on the stop at Haran.
Everything anyone thinks about him — that he failed, that he was on his way, that he was an idolater
rescued too late — is inference. What Genesis actually does is put a man's whole life between a
scattering and a call, and let the reader notice where he died.

## Timeline / key events

- Born to [[Nahor (son of Serug)]] in Nahor's twenty-ninth year — Genesis 11:24
- At seventy, begins fathering: Abram, Nahor, and Haran — Genesis 11:26, [[From Shem to Abram]]
- His son [[Haran (son of Terah)]] dies before him in Ur — Genesis 11:28
- Takes Abram, Sarai, and Lot out of [[Ur of the Chaldees]] toward [[Canaan]] — Genesis 11:31
- Settles in [[Haran (city)]] instead, and dies there at 205 — Genesis 11:31–32
- Named as an idolater by Joshua at Shechem — Joshua 24:2
- Named in 1 Chronicles 1:26 and, as Thara, in Luke 3:34
