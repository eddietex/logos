# Miriam

## Overview

**The sister of Moses and Aaron, who first appears unnamed — standing far off to watch what will be
done to her brother — and speaks one sentence that saves him.** In Exodus 2:1–10 she is only *"his
sister"* (2:4, 7) and *"the young woman"*, *hāʿalmâ* (2:8). The name *Miriam* first appears at the
sea, *"Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron"* (15:20), and Numbers 26:59 gives Amram and
Jochebed one daughter: *"She bore to Amram Aaron and Moses, and Miriam their sister."*

**The identification of the sister of 2:4 with Miriam is an inference**, near-universal in Jewish
and Christian reading and supported by the fact that Numbers knows of no other sister — but Exodus
does not make it. The page is filed under *Miriam* on that basis, and what 2:1–10 says of the unnamed
sister is kept separate from what is said of Miriam later.

**At the river she watches and she speaks.** *"His sister stood far off, to see what would be done
to him"* (2:4) — literally *to know*, *lədēʿâ*, the verb the king failed at in 1:8. When Pharaoh's
daughter opens the basket she steps forward with a single offer: *"Should I go and call a nurse for
you from the Hebrew women, that she may nurse the child for you?"* (2:7). **She asks nothing,
claims nothing, and gives the princess a way to keep the child alive without saying so.** The one
word she gets back — *"Go"* — sends her for her own mother. See [[The Birth of Moses]].

**Her watch from far off has a predecessor in Genesis.** Hagar *"sat down opposite him, a good way
off"* so as not to see Ishmael die (Genesis 21:16); the sister stands far off in order to know.
See [[Watching from Far Off - Hagar and Moses' Sister]].

### What is ahead

Miriam leads the women's song at the sea (Exodus 15:20–21); she and Aaron speak against Moses and
she is struck with leprosy and shut out of the camp seven days (Numbers 12); she dies at Kadesh
(Numbers 20:1); and Micah names her with her brothers among the leaders God sent: *"I sent before you
Moses, Aaron, and Miriam"* (Micah 6:4). These sections are written as their pericopes arrive.
