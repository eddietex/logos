# Bera

## Overview

King of [[Sodom]] — named once, in a list, and thereafter only by his title.

*"They made war with Bera, king of Sodom"* (Genesis 14:2). It is the only occurrence of the name in
the Bible. For the remaining twenty-two verses he is *the king of Sodom*, which is how Genesis
handles a man whose office matters and whose person does not. He is the first citizen of Sodom to
speak, and his four words of dialogue are the only speech any Sodomite is given until the mob at
19:9. See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

**He loses a battle in the tar pits and survives it.** *"The kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled, and
some fell there, and those who remained fled to the hills"* (14:10). Whether he is among the fallen
or the fled is left open by the grammar, and 14:17 settles it: he is alive, and he comes out to meet
Abram.

**And he asks for the one thing he was not going to be given.** *"Give me the people, and take the
goods for yourself"* (14:21). By the customary law of the region the whole recovery belongs to the
man who made it; the king of Sodom asks a favor and phrases it as a division, and the favor he asks
for — his subjects — is the half a rescuer would return anyway. Abram's answer refuses the other
half in the strongest terms available: *"I have lifted up my hand to Yahweh … that I will not take a
thread nor a sandal strap nor anything that is yours, lest you should say, 'I have made Abram
rich'"* (14:22–23). The refusal is about credit, and it is directed at this man by name.

**The chapter uses him as a foil.** Two kings come out to Abram in the same valley on the same day.
[[Melchizedek]] brings bread and wine and a blessing, asks nothing, and is given a tenth of
everything. Bera brings nothing, opens with an imperative, and is given nothing at all. Genesis
introduces him first (14:17) and then makes him wait four verses while the other king arrives,
blesses, is tithed, and leaves.

**Nothing else is told of him.** He does not appear in Genesis 18 or 19; the city he rules is
destroyed five chapters later, and Genesis does not say whether he lived to see it.
