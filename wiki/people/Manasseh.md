# Manasseh

*Unqualified for now. **The name will later belong to a tribe, to a territory on both sides of the
Jordan, and to a king of Judah** (2 Kings 21), so a collision is certain once the wiki reaches those
books. `AGENTS.md`'s rule is to qualify in the pass that creates the second page rather than in
anticipation; **the later pass should rename this page and fix its inbound links.***

## Overview

### Levitical cities — Joshua 21:1–42

The tribe descended from him contributes cities and suburbs to the Levites (21:5–6, 25, 27). The ancestor is not an actor in this distribution.

See [[The Cities of the Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:1–42.

### Refuge cities appointed — Joshua 20:1–9

Golan in Bashan is assigned from Manasseh’s tribe as an eastern refuge (8), repeating Deuteronomy 4:43. The name denotes the descendants.

See [[The Cities of Refuge Appointed]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 20:1–9.

### The survey at Shiloh — Joshua 18:1–10

The eastern half-tribe retains Moses’ grant (7); the house of Joseph retains its northern borders
(5). The corporate descendants, not the patriarch, are meant.

See [[The Land Surveyed at Shiloh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 18:1–10; comparisons as cited.

### Joseph asks for more — Joshua 17:14–18

The tribe joins Ephraim in the petition (17). The promise of expulsion (18) stands in tension with the immediately preceding inability and forced-labor notices (12–13); the promise is not a report that expulsion occurred.

See [[The Children of Joseph Ask for More]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:14–18.

### Manasseh’s inheritance — Joshua 17:1–13

The western allotment includes ten parts beside the eastern holdings, explicitly accounting for
daughters inheriting among sons (5–6). Cities are assigned within Asher and Issachar, but their
Canaanite inhabitants remain and later undergo forced labor (11–13).

See [[The Inheritance of Manasseh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:1–13.

### Ephraim’s inheritance — Joshua 16:1–10

Named with Ephraim among Joseph’s children taking their inheritance (4). Ephraim also receives cities and villages within Manasseh’s inheritance (9); this chapter does not yet give Manasseh’s own full boundary.

See [[The Inheritance of Ephraim]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 16:1–10.

### The western division — Joshua 14:1–5

The descendants are one of Joseph’s two tribes (4). With the eastern half already allotted in Joshua 13:29–31, the western division is for nine tribes and a half (2–3). The patriarch is not the recipient in person.

See [[The Division of the Land Begins]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:1–5; comparisons as cited.


### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

The eastern half-tribe receives Bashan, the sixty villages of Jair, and holdings for half the children of Machir (29–31). These are descendants’ allotments, not the patriarch’s personal occupation.

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### The twelve stones — Joshua 4:1–24

The eastern half-tribe crosses armed with Reuben and Gad (12–13). This fulfills the promise to
cross, not the whole obligation to serve until their brothers receive rest. The patriarch is not the
actor.
See [[The Twelve Stones from the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 4:1–24.

### The eastern half-tribe pledges — Joshua 1:10–18

*The half-tribe of Manasseh* (12) is bound with Reuben and Gad to the Moses agreement, though Numbers 32 never records the half-tribe accepting the condition (see [[The Two and a Half Tribes - Numbers 32, Deuteronomy 3, and Joshua 1, 4, 22]]). Here it is simply addressed alongside them and joins the pledge (16–18). The tribe, not the patriarch, is in view.

See [[The Eastern Tribes Pledge to Cross]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 1:10–18.

### The death of Moses — Deuteronomy 34:1–12

*The land of Ephraim and Manasseh* is part of the view from Nebo (2); the name denotes tribal territory.

See [[The Death of Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 34:1–12.

### Moses blesses the tribes — Deuteronomy 33:1–29

Manasseh’s thousands share Joseph’s blessing with Ephraim’s ten thousands (17). The tribe is
included within Joseph rather than given an independent saying.

See [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 33:1–29; comparisons as cited.


### The covenant in Moab — Deuteronomy 29:1–29

The half-tribe of the Manassites shares the eastern inheritance (8). The statement does not assign the whole tribe east of the Jordan.

See [[The Covenant in Moab and the Hidden Things]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 29:1–29.

### Eastern refuge cities — Deuteronomy 4:41–43

Golan in Bashan is designated for the Manassites (43). This concerns the eastern
tribal community, not an action by the patriarch.

See [[Three Cities Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:41–43.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

The half-tribe receives the rest of Gilead and all Bashan (13). Jair and Machir specify Manassite
holdings (14–15). The command following this combined allocation addresses the eastern recipients
collectively (18–20); Numbers 32 had recorded the negotiation with Reuben and Gad.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### Marriage and tribal inheritance — Numbers 36:1–13

The tribal inheritance is protected against transfer through an heiress's marriage (1–9). The
daughters marry within Manasseh's families, and their property stays there (12).

See [[The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 36; comparisons as cited.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

Half the tribe has already received an eastern inheritance (14–15), while Hanniel son of Ephod represents Manasseh in the western distribution (23). The two notices account for the divided tribe.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

*The half-tribe of Manasseh the son of Joseph* is included in the grant of Sihon's and Og's kingdoms
(33), though it had not asked and was not party to the condition. The chapter then explains it after the
fact: Manassite clans — Machir, Jair, and Nobah — had taken land in Gilead by their own conquest (39–42).
This is the first time *half-tribe* is used of Manasseh in Genesis through Numbers; the tribe Jacob adopted
now settles on both banks of the Jordan.

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### The daughters’ inheritance — Numbers 27:1–11

The daughters' descent through Machir, Gilead, Hepher, and Zelophehad locates their inheritance
claim within Manasseh's families (1). Descent establishes the family to which the requested
possession belongs.

See [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:1–11.


### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

The tribe bearing his name has 52,700 men in the second military census (Numbers 26). The register
names ancestral families, not a new action by the patriarch.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


**Joseph's firstborn, named for forgetting his father's house — by a man who had not forgotten it.**

> **51** Joseph called the name of the firstborn Manasseh, “For”, he said, “God has made me forget all my
> toil, and all my father’s house.”

***Mənaššeh*** is a participle from ***nāšâ***, *to forget* — ***one causing to forget*** — and
**Genesis supplies the etymology in the same breath, as it does for most of Jacob's sons.** See
[[Names and Naming]].

### The hardest naming speech in Genesis

**Every other naming speech in the book records a deliverance, a grievance, a hope, or a joke.** This one
**gives thanks for an erasure.**

**Two things are put in one clause and forgotten together**: ***ʿămālî***, *my toil* — the thirteen years
of slavery, the false charge, the prison — **and *kol-bêt ʾābî*, *all my father's house*.** **Joseph
does not distinguish between the suffering and the family**; the sentence treats them as one burden
lifted.

**And he credits God with lifting it.** *"God has made me forget"* is a piel with *ʾĕlōhîm* as subject.
**It is offered as a mercy received, not as a lapse confessed.**

**Three things sit against the name, and Genesis reconciles none of them.**

1. **He has not forgotten.** 42:9 — *"Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed about them"*; 43:27 —
   he asks after *"your father, the old man of whom you spoke"*; 45:3 — the first sentence after he
   discloses himself is ***"Does my father still live?"*** **The name is falsified by the man who gave
   it.**
2. **He never sent word.** Seven years of plenty, absolute authority over Egypt, and Hebron about nine
   days away. **Genesis records no message and offers no reason.**
3. **[[Ephraim]]'s name, given in the next verse, contradicts the mood of this one.** *"The land of my
   affliction"* is not the speech of a man who has stopped remembering what he lost.

**The wiki flags the tension and adopts no resolution.** The readings on offer — that the name is a
grief so complete it inverts, that it is a genuine and temporary relief, that it is Joseph making peace
with a life he does not expect to leave — **are all imported.** The text gives a name, a gloss, and no
comment.

### Firstborn, and then not

**41:51 calls him *the firstborn*, *habbəkôr*** — and **the book will spend chapter 48 taking the
position off him.** Jacob, blind and dying, **adopts both boys as his own** (48:5), **crosses his hands
over their heads**, and puts his right on the younger; when Joseph objects — *"Not so, my father, for
this is the firstborn"* (48:18) — **Jacob refuses to move it**: *"his younger brother will be greater
than he"* (48:19).

**It is the fourth time Genesis has done this** — [[Isaac]] over [[Ishmael]], [[Jacob]] over [[Esau]],
[[Perez]] over [[Zerah]] — **and the first time the displaced son is not wronged in the doing of it.**
See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] and
[[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]].

**What he keeps is worth stating, because it distinguishes him from every other passed-over elder son in
the book.** **He is not cursed, not sent away, and not disinherited**: *"He also will become a people,
and he also will be great"* (48:19). **[[Ishmael]] is put out with a skin of water, [[Esau]] gets a
blessing that is a description of subjection, [[Zerah]] gets nothing but a scarlet thread** — **Manasseh
loses precedence and keeps a future.** See
[[Two Blind Fathers - Genesis 27 and Genesis 48]].

**And he is named in the formula anyway.** *"God make you as Ephraim and as Manasseh"* (48:20) **puts
both brothers into the blessing Israel says over its sons**, in the order the chapter established. See
[[God Make You as Ephraim and as Manasseh - The Blessing Formula]].

### What becomes of the name

**A tribe, and the only one settled on both banks of the Jordan** — half in Gilead and Bashan
(Numbers 32:33; Joshua 13:29–31), half west of the river. **Gideon is a Manassite** (Judges 6:15).
**In most later tribal lists Joseph does not appear as a name at all**, because his two sons stand in his
place, **which is how the double portion of the firstborn reaches him** (1 Chronicles 5:1–2). See
[[The Twelve Sons and the Lists That Reorder Them]].

### The census — Numbers 1:1–54

His tribe numbers 32,200, the smallest, with Gamaliel son of Pedahzur (10, 35). At the second census it is 52,700 (26:34).

See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 1.

### The camp — Numbers 2:1–34

Next to Ephraim on the west (20), with Gamaliel son of Pedahzur as prince. The firstborn of Joseph stands second, and his 32,200 is the smallest figure.

See [[The Camp Around the Tent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 2.

### The altar gifts — Numbers 7:54–59

The tribe of Manasseh gives on the eighth day of twelve, by the hand of Gamaliel. Its gift is identical to every other tribe's.

See [[The Offerings of the Twelve Princes]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 7.

### With Ephraim — Numbers 10:23

In Ephraim's camp, under Gamaliel son of Pedahzur.

See [[The March from Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 10.

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** *The half-tribe of Manasseh* receives part of the east bank from Moses (12:6). See [[The Kings Struck Down]].
