# Manasseh

*Unqualified for now. **The name will later belong to a tribe, to a territory on both sides of the
Jordan, and to a king of Judah** (2 Kings 21), so a collision is certain once the wiki reaches those
books. `AGENTS.md`'s rule is to qualify in the pass that creates the second page rather than in
anticipation; **the later pass should rename this page and fix its inbound links.***

## Overview

**Joseph's firstborn, named for forgetting his father's house — by a man who had not forgotten it.**

> **51** Joseph called the name of the firstborn Manasseh, “For”, he said, “God has made me forget all my
> toil, and all my father’s house.”

***Mənaššeh*** is a participle from ***nāšâ***, *to forget* — ***one causing to forget*** — and
**Genesis supplies the etymology in the same breath, as it does for most of Jacob's sons.** See
[[Names and Naming]].

### The hardest naming speech in Genesis

**Every other naming speech in the book records a deliverance, a grievance, a hope, or a joke.** This one
**gives thanks for an erasure.**

**Two things are put in one clause and forgotten together**: ***ʿămālî***, *my toil* — the thirteen years
of slavery, the false charge, the prison — **and *kol-bêt ʾābî*, *all my father's house*.** **Joseph
does not distinguish between the suffering and the family**; the sentence treats them as one burden
lifted.

**And he credits God with lifting it.** *"God has made me forget"* is a piel with *ʾĕlōhîm* as subject.
**It is offered as a mercy received, not as a lapse confessed.**

**Three things sit against the name, and Genesis reconciles none of them.**

1. **He has not forgotten.** 42:9 — *"Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed about them"*; 43:27 —
   he asks after *"your father, the old man of whom you spoke"*; 45:3 — the first sentence after he
   discloses himself is ***"Does my father still live?"*** **The name is falsified by the man who gave
   it.**
2. **He never sent word.** Seven years of plenty, absolute authority over Egypt, and Hebron about nine
   days away. **Genesis records no message and offers no reason.**
3. **[[Ephraim]]'s name, given in the next verse, contradicts the mood of this one.** *"The land of my
   affliction"* is not the speech of a man who has stopped remembering what he lost.

**The wiki flags the tension and adopts no resolution.** The readings on offer — that the name is a
grief so complete it inverts, that it is a genuine and temporary relief, that it is Joseph making peace
with a life he does not expect to leave — **are all imported.** The text gives a name, a gloss, and no
comment.

### Firstborn, and then not

**41:51 calls him *the firstborn*, *habbəkôr*** — and **the book will spend chapter 48 taking the
position off him.** Jacob, blind and dying, **adopts both boys as his own** (48:5), **crosses his hands
over their heads**, and puts his right on the younger; when Joseph objects — *"Not so, my father, for
this is the firstborn"* (48:18) — **Jacob refuses to move it**: *"his younger brother will be greater
than he"* (48:19).

**It is the fourth time Genesis has done this** — [[Isaac]] over [[Ishmael]], [[Jacob]] over [[Esau]],
[[Perez]] over [[Zerah]] — **and the first time the displaced son is not wronged in the doing of it.**
See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] and
[[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]].

### What becomes of the name

**A tribe, and the only one settled on both banks of the Jordan** — half in Gilead and Bashan
(Numbers 32:33; Joshua 13:29–31), half west of the river. **Gideon is a Manassite** (Judges 6:15).
**In most later tribal lists Joseph does not appear as a name at all**, because his two sons stand in his
place, **which is how the double portion of the firstborn reaches him** (1 Chronicles 5:1–2). See
[[The Twelve Sons and the Lists That Reorder Them]].

## Appears in

- [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]] — Genesis 41:50–51; **born at the height of his father's power
  and named for what that power cost.** The speech is **one of only two Joseph makes in the chapter that
  are about himself**, and both credit God. **He is called *the firstborn* in the verse that names him**,
  which is the designation chapter 48 exists to reverse
