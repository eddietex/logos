# Judah and Tamar

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 38:1–30

## Summary

**Genesis stops the Joseph story dead and does not come back to it for a chapter.** Joseph is sold in
the last verse of 37 and bought in the first verse of 39, and between those two clauses the book turns
aside to [[Judah]] — **the brother who proposed the sale** — and spends thirty verses on a marriage,
three sons, two deaths, a roadside transaction, and a set of twins. **Joseph is not named once. Jacob
is not named once. God speaks not a word**, exactly as in the chapter before, **and yet acts twice —
the only two times in Genesis the narrator says outright that Yahweh killed a named individual.**

**The chapter is the Bible's most sustained account of a woman with no legal standing getting what she
is owed by a man who has all of it.** [[Tamar]] is twice widowed, sent back to her father's house, and
left there while the third son grows up and is not given to her. **She has no advocate, no property,
and no voice in the household that owes her a child.** What she has is a road, a veil, and the
knowledge that her father-in-law will be on that road. **She takes a pledge instead of a price**, and
three months later the pledge is the whole of her defence.

**The hinge is two words Judah has heard before.** *Hakker-nāʾ* — *"discern, please"* — is what
[[Tamar]] sends to him with his own signet, cord, and staff (38:25). **It is also what Judah and his
brothers said to their father one chapter earlier**, handing him a bloodied coat: *"Examine it, now,
whether it is your son's coat or not"* (37:32). **The man who composed the question is handed it
back**, and the wiki takes the pairing as the reason Genesis 38 sits exactly where it does. See
[[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]].

**And Judah answers it with the first unqualified admission of fault in the book.** *"She is more
righteous than I"* — ***ṣādəqâ mimmennî*** (38:26). **No patriarch before him concedes anything.**
Adam blames the woman, Cain asks a question, Abraham explains the sister arrangement twice, Jacob says
nothing at all when he is caught. **Judah is handed evidence, recognises it, and states the verdict
against himself in three Hebrew words**, with no bargaining and no request for mercy. See
[[Righteousness]].

**What the chapter is structurally is the beginning of the line of David.** Perez, the second-born who
comes out first, stands at the head of the genealogy that closes Ruth (4:18–22), runs through Boaz and
Jesse to David, and arrives in Matthew 1:3 with **Tamar named in it** — one of four women in that
genealogy, and the only one whose story is told in Genesis. See
[[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]].

### 38:1–5 — a descent, a Canaanite marriage, and three names

> **1** At that time, Judah went down from his brothers, and visited a certain Adullamite, whose name
> was Hirah.
> **2** Judah saw there a certain Canaanite man named Shua’s daughter. He took her, and went in to her.
> **3** She conceived, and bore a son; and he named him Er.
> **4** She conceived again, and bore a son; and she named him Onan.
> **5** She yet again bore a son, and named him Shelah: and he was at Chezib, when she bore him.

***"At that time, Judah went down from his brothers."*** ***Wayyēred*** — **the same verb the book has
just used for the other journey.** Joseph is carried *down* to Egypt at 37:25, 28, and 39:1; **Judah
goes *down* from his brothers in the same week and under his own power.** The two descents are set side
by side and Genesis says nothing about the pairing. *Mē-ʾet ʾeḥāyw*, *from his brothers* — **he leaves
the household whose worst act he had just organised**, and the chapter never says why.

***"A certain Adullamite, whose name was Hirah."*** [[Adullam]] is in the Shephelah, the low hill
country west of Hebron on the Canaanite side of the ridge — **later the cave David hides in** (1 Samuel
22:1) and a town in the Judahite list (Joshua 15:35). [[Hirah the Adullamite]] is called Judah's
***rēaʿ***, *friend* or *companion*, at 38:12 and 38:20, **and he is the only friend any patriarch is
given in Genesis.** He runs the errand with the goat and takes the embarrassment back.

***"A certain Canaanite man named Shua's daughter. He took her, and went in to her."*** **Three verbs
and no negotiation.** He *saw*, he *took*, he *went in to her* — and the sequence is the one Genesis
uses when it disapproves: *"the sons of God saw the daughters of men … and they took for themselves
wives"* (6:2). **Genesis reports it without a syllable of comment**, which is what it also does for
Esau's Hittite wives and for Joseph's Egyptian one. See [[Shua's Daughter]].

**And it breaks the rule the book has spent three chapters establishing.** *"You shall not take a wife
for my son of the daughters of the Canaanites"* (24:3), repeated by Isaac at 28:1 — **and the line of
David comes out of the marriage that ignores it, via a second Canaanite woman.** The thread is on
[[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]]. **The wiki records the collision and does not resolve
it**: Genesis states the principle, narrates its breach, and passes no verdict either time.

***"He named him Er … she named him Onan … she named him Shelah."*** **The father names the first and
the mother names the next two**, which is the ordinary distribution in Genesis and worth noticing only
because **all three names are about what the men will turn out not to have.**

- ***ʿĒr*** is a near-anagram of ***raʿ***, *evil* — **the word the very next verse uses of him**:
  *"Er was wicked (*raʿ*) in Yahweh's sight."* It also sits one consonant from ***ʿărîrî***,
  *childless*, which is what he dies.
- ***ʾÔnān*** is from ***ʾôn***, *vigour*, *generative strength*, *wealth* — the word in Jacob's
  *"Reuben, you are my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my strength"* (49:3). **The son named
  for generative strength is the one who refuses to generate.**
- ***Šēlâ*** is the survivor, and the name is left unglossed. See [[Er]], [[Onan]], and
  [[Shelah (son of Judah)]].

***"And he was at Chezib, when she bore him."*** **A throwaway geographic note, and the name is
*deception*.** ***Kəzîb*** is from ***kāzab***, *to lie*, *to prove false*, *to fail* — the root behind
Micah's pun on the same town, *"the houses of Achzib will be a deceitful thing (*ʾakzāb*) to the kings
of Israel"* (Micah 1:14). **Genesis records that the son Judah will withhold by a lie was born at a
place called Lie**, and makes no remark. The wiki records the wordplay as a feature of the Hebrew and
**does not claim the narrator intended it**. See [[Chezib]] and [[Deception]].

### 38:6–11 — two deaths, a duty refused, and a promise not kept

> **6** Judah took a wife for Er, his firstborn, and her name was Tamar.
> **7** Er, Judah’s firstborn, was wicked in Yahweh’s sight. Yahweh killed him.
> **8** Judah said to Onan, “Go in to your brother’s wife, and perform the duty of a husband’s brother
> to her, and raise up offspring for your brother.”
> **9** Onan knew that the offspring wouldn’t be his; and when he went in to his brother’s wife, he
> spilled it on the ground, lest he should give offspring to his brother.
> **10** The thing which he did was evil in Yahweh’s sight, and he killed him also.
> **11** Then Judah said to Tamar, his daughter-in-law, “Remain a widow in your father’s house, until
> Shelah, my son, is grown up”; for he said, “Lest he also die, like his brothers.” Tamar went and
> lived in her father’s house.

***"Her name was Tamar."*** ***Tāmār*** — ***date palm***, **and the chapter gives her no father, no
town, and no people.** Genesis names the nationality of Judah's wife in one clause and is silent about
Tamar's, and the silence has been filled both ways for two thousand years: **the text does not say she
is a Canaanite and does not say she is not.** She is the only person in the chapter whose origin is
withheld, and **she is the one the genealogies keep.** See [[Tamar]].

***"Er, Judah's firstborn, was wicked in Yahweh's sight. Yahweh killed him."*** **The first divine act
in fourteen chapters of narrative, and it is an execution with no charge specified.** Genesis says
*raʿ bəʿênê YHWH* and stops. **No offence is named, then or ever** — 1 Chronicles 2:3 repeats the
sentence verbatim and adds nothing. **The book that supplies a reason for the flood, for Sodom, and for
Babel supplies none here.**

**And it is one of only two occasions in Genesis where the narrator says Yahweh killed a particular
man.** Both are in this paragraph, three verses apart. **God destroys cities and a world elsewhere in
the book; he is never otherwise reported as killing an individual**, and the two he kills are the two
sons who stand between Tamar and an heir. See [[Retribution]] and [[Judgment]].

***"Perform the duty of a husband's brother to her."*** ***Yabbēm*** — **a verb built on the noun
*yābām*, the brother-in-law**, and this is its first appearance in Scripture. **The institution is
being assumed, not introduced**: Judah issues the instruction as though it needed no explanation, and
the law that will codify it is four books away (Deuteronomy 25:5–10). **Genesis 38 is therefore the
evidence that levirate marriage is older than the Torah's version of it**, and it differs from that
version at two points — **the duty here falls to a brother who is not the only one available, and the
father-in-law turns out to be liable for it too.** The full comparison, with Ruth 3–4, Leviticus 18:16,
and the Sadducees' question at Matthew 22:23–33, is on
[[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].

***"He spilled it on the ground."*** ***Wəšiḥēt ʾarṣâ*** — **literally *he ruined it groundward*, and
the verb is the flood's**: *"the earth was corrupt (*šāḥat*) … I will destroy (*mašḥîtām*) them with
the earth"* (6:11–13). **What Genesis condemns is stated in the same verse and is not the act but the
motive**: *"lest he should give offspring to his brother."* **He takes the marital right and withholds
the obligation it was granted for**, repeatedly — the Hebrew is frequentative, *whenever he went in* —
**and the chapter's word for it is *raʿ*, the same word it used of his brother.** The later use of this
verse as the Bible's text against masturbation is **reading past what the verse itself gives as the
reason**, and the wiki says so plainly: Genesis names the defrauded brother, not the spilled seed. See
[[Onan]] and [[Sin]].

***"Remain a widow in your father's house, until Shelah, my son, is grown up."*** **A sentence with a
stated condition and an unstated intention, and Genesis gives both.** The condition goes to Tamar; **the
reason goes to the reader** — *"for he said, 'Lest he also die, like his brothers'"* — and it is the
narrator doing for Judah what it did for [[Reuben]] at 37:22, supplying the motive the speech omits.
**Judah has concluded that the woman is killing his sons.** Genesis has just told us twice who killed
them.

**So the arrangement is a holding action dressed as a betrothal**, and it puts Tamar in the worst
position the culture had: **sent home, but not released.** A widow in her father's house is
maintained; a widow *bound to a house that will not take her back* can neither remarry nor bear, and
she has **no mechanism to compel performance** — the release ceremony of Deuteronomy 25:7–10, where
the woman herself brings the refusing brother before the elders, does not exist yet. **The chapter's
whole plot is the absence of that remedy.** See [[Marriage]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

### 38:12–19 — a veil at the gate, and a pledge instead of a price

> **12** After many days, Shua’s daughter, the wife of Judah, died. Judah was comforted, and went up to
> his sheep shearers to Timnah, he and his friend Hirah, the Adullamite.
> **13** Tamar was told, “Behold, your father-in-law is going up to Timnah to shear his sheep.”
> **14** She took off her the garments of her widowhood, and covered herself with her veil, and wrapped
> herself, and sat in the gate of Enaim, which is by the way to Timnah; for she saw that Shelah was
> grown up, and she wasn’t given to him as a wife.
> **15** When Judah saw her, he thought that she was a prostitute, for she had covered her face.
> **16** He turned to her by the way, and said, “Please come, let me come in to you,” for he didn’t know
> that she was his daughter-in-law. She said, “What will you give me, that you may come in to me?”
> **17** He said, “I will send you a young goat from the flock.” She said, “Will you give me a pledge,
> until you send it?”
> **18** He said, “What pledge will I give you?” She said, “Your signet and your cord, and your staff
> that is in your hand.” He gave them to her, and came in to her, and she conceived by him.
> **19** She arose, and went away, and put off her veil from her, and put on the garments of her
> widowhood.

***"Judah was comforted."*** ***Wayyinnāḥem*** — **and the previous chapter ended with a man who
*refused* to be comforted** (37:35, *wayməʾēn ləhitnaḥēm*), and who is still refusing twenty-two years
later. **Genesis puts the same verb eight verses apart, negated for the father and completed for the
son**, and moves straight from Judah's consolation to his walk up the road.

***"Went up to his sheep shearers."*** **Shearing is a festival as much as a job** — it is the occasion
Nabal is feasting at when David's men come (1 Samuel 25:4–8), and the one Absalom uses to get Amnon
drunk and kill him (2 Samuel 13:23–29). **The reader is being told there will be wine and a crowd**, and
the wiki notes the shearing festivals as the setting of three of the Hebrew Bible's blunter stories.

***"She took off her the garments of her widowhood, and covered herself with her veil."*** **The WEB's
*took off her the garments* is a printing artefact of the translation, not a variant; the Hebrew is
simply *she removed the garments of her widowhood from upon her*.** ***Ṣāʿîp***, **the veil, occurs in
the Hebrew Bible only here, at 38:19, and at Genesis 24:65** — where [[Rebekah]] covers herself on
first seeing the man she is to marry. **The same rare word does bridal modesty in one chapter and
roadside anonymity in another**, and both times it is a woman putting a cloth between herself and a man
she is about to be joined to. See [[Nakedness and Covering]].

**What the veil does here is the opposite of what tradition says it does.** Genesis does not say a veil
marked a prostitute — **it says Judah could not see her face** (38:15). *"He thought that she was a
prostitute, **for she had covered her face**."* **The inference is his, and the narrator supplies the
grounds so the reader can see how thin they are.** See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

***"Sat in the gate of Enaim."*** ***Petaḥ ʿênayim*** — ***the opening of Enaim***, and ***ʿênayim***
**is the Hebrew for *two eyes*, or *two springs*.** The phrase can be read as a place name, which is how
the WEB and most versions take it, **or as a description — *the opening of the eyes*** — and the Hebrew
supports both. **It is the chapter's most concentrated joke and its most serious one**: the man whose
eyes are about to fail him sits down with a veiled woman at Eye-Opening. **The wiki reports both
readings and adopts the place-name one**, since 38:21 has Hirah asking the men *of her place* about *the
one at Enaim by the road*, which wants a location. See [[Enaim]].

***"For she saw that Shelah was grown up, and she wasn't given to him as a wife."*** **The narrator
states her motive in plain words, and it is the only motive anyone in the chapter is given.** Not
desire, not revenge, not poverty — **breach of contract.** Genesis is careful here in a way it was not
careful in chapter 37, where it declined to characterise a single actor: **it tells us why Tamar does
this before she does it**, and the verdict at 38:26 is therefore not a surprise but a confirmation.

***"What will you give me, that you may come in to me?"*** **She opens the negotiation, prices it, and
then declines the price.** He offers a ***gədî ʿizzîm***, a young goat — **the third goat in the family
story and the third time one is used to settle something that is not about goats**: two kids' skins
took the blessing off a blind father (27:9, 15–16), a goat's blood took a son off a sighted one
(37:31), **and now a goat is offered as a fee and never arrives.** See
[[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]].

***"Will you give me a pledge?"*** ***ʿĒrābôn*** — **a word that occurs in the Hebrew Bible only in
this chapter** (38:17, 18, 20), **and which has had an extraordinary afterlife.** It passes into Greek
as ***ἀρραβών***, and the New Testament uses it three times for the Holy Spirit as God's down payment
on what he has promised: *"who also gave us the down payment of the Spirit in our hearts"*
(2 Corinthians 1:22; also 5:5; Ephesians 1:14). **The theological term for the guarantee of an unpaid
promise enters Scripture as the security a woman takes from a man who is not going to come back.**

***"Your signet and your cord, and your staff that is in your hand."*** ***Ḥōtām, pətîl, maṭṭeh*** —
**and she asks for the three things a man cannot replace.** The seal on its cord, worn at the neck, is
his **signature**: it is what stamps a contract, and Scripture's image for what is most inalienably
one's own — *"set me as a seal on your heart"* (Song of Songs 8:6), *"though Coniah … were the signet
on my right hand, yet would I pluck you from there"* (Jeremiah 22:24). **The staff is his authority in
the open.** **She does not ask for money.** She asks for his identity, and **he hands it over for a
night and walks away without it.** See [[Oaths and Vows]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]].

### 38:20–23 — a friend, a wrong word, and a man who decides to stop looking

> **20** Judah sent the young goat by the hand of his friend, the Adullamite, to receive the pledge from
> the woman’s hand, but he didn’t find her.
> **21** Then he asked the men of her place, saying, “Where is the prostitute, that was at Enaim by the
> road?” They said, “There has been no prostitute here.”
> **22** He returned to Judah, and said, “I haven’t found her; and also the men of the place said,
> ‘There has been no prostitute here.’”
> **23** Judah said, “Let her keep it, lest we be shamed. Behold, I sent this young goat, and you
> haven’t found her.”

***"Where is the prostitute?"*** **Hirah asks with a different word than the narrator used, and the
change is the paragraph's whole point.** At 38:15 Judah takes her for a ***zônâ*** — an ordinary
prostitute. **Hirah goes looking for a *qədēšâ*** (38:21, 22) — **a *consecrated woman*, from
*qādaš*, *to be holy*: a woman attached to a sanctuary.** **The errand has been dignified in the
telling.** Whether *qədēšâ* denotes sacred prostitution or simply a class of cult personnel is
genuinely disputed, and Deuteronomy 23:17–18 forbids the office to Israelites under both the masculine
and feminine forms. **The wiki records the shift in vocabulary as the text's own comment on how such an
errand gets described by the man sent to run it**, and does not settle what the institution was.

***"There has been no prostitute here."*** **Said twice, in the same words, by the townsmen and then by
Hirah reporting them** — and **both times it is literally true.** There was no *qədēšâ* at Enaim. There
was a widow in a veil.

***"Let her keep it, lest we be shamed."*** ***Pen nihyeh lābûz*** — *lest we become an object of
contempt*. **He drops the search to protect his name**, and what he abandons in order to protect it is
the seal with his name on it. **Genesis lets the sentence stand without comment for three months and
one verse.** Note also the pronoun: ***we***. **Hirah is implicated in the embarrassment and will not
be implicated in anything else**; he disappears from Scripture in this verse.

### 38:24–26 — a death sentence, a question, and three words

> **24** About three months later, Judah was told, “Tamar, your daughter-in-law, has played the
> prostitute. Moreover, behold, she is with child by prostitution.” Judah said, “Bring her out, and let
> her be burned.”
> **25** When she was brought out, she sent to her father-in-law, saying, “By the man, whose these are,
> I am with child.” She also said, “Please discern whose are these—the signet, and the cords, and the
> staff.”
> **26** Judah acknowledged them, and said, “She is more righteous than I, because I didn’t give her to
> Shelah, my son.” He knew her again no more.

***"Bring her out, and let her be burned."*** **Six words, no hearing, no witnesses, and no interval.**
He is told, and he sentences. **The jurisdiction is worth stating exactly, because it is the measure of
what Tamar is up against**: she is living *in her father's house*, not Judah's, and Judah is a resident
alien in Adullam with no office at all — **and he pronounces a capital sentence on a woman in another
man's household and expects it carried out.** It is, and she is brought out.

**Burning is the severest penalty the Torah knows**, reserved there for a priest's daughter who
prostitutes herself (Leviticus 21:9) and for a man who marries a woman and her mother (Leviticus
20:14). **The ordinary penalty for adultery is stoning** (Deuteronomy 22:23–24). **Judah reaches past
it.** See [[Judgment]].

**And the charge is a charge of adultery, which means he has not forgotten the arrangement.** Tamar can
only be guilty if she is bound to his house — *"Tamar, **your daughter-in-law**"*, the informant says,
and Judah does not correct the description. **He is invoking the tie in order to kill her that he had
declined to honour in order to marry her off.**

***"By the man, whose these are, I am with child."*** **She does not name him.** She sends the objects
and lets them speak, **in private, to him, before she is in front of the crowd with a name** — and
the wiki notes what the arrangement costs her: **if he denies the seal, she burns.** **It is the most
restrained act in Genesis.** She has the evidence to destroy him publicly and she gives him the chance
to convict himself instead.

***"Please discern whose are these."*** ***Hakker-nāʾ*** — **and the phrase occurs in Genesis exactly
twice.** Here, and at 37:32, where Judah and his brothers hand Jacob the bloodied coat and say
*"Examine it, now, whether it is your son's coat or not."* **The chapters are adjacent; the speaker of
the first is the addressee of the second; the object is a piece of a man's identity in both; and both
times the person holding it knows what the person receiving it does not.** Genesis draws no line
between them and puts them ninety verses apart. The full case is on
[[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]]. See also [[Deception]] and
[[Language and Speech]].

**The verb is *nākar* both times, and it governs the whole cycle.** Jacob *recognises* the coat and
concludes wrongly (37:33); Judah *recognises* the pledge and concludes rightly (38:26); and Joseph will
*recognise* his brothers while they *do not recognise* him (42:7–8). **Three recognitions in six
chapters, and only the middle one produces the truth.**

***"She is more righteous than I."*** ***Ṣādəqâ mimmennî*** — **two words and a preposition, and they
are the most important thing Judah says in the Bible.** The Hebrew comparative can be read *more
righteous than I*, which is how the WEB and most versions take it, or as an absolute — ***she* is in
the right, not I** — which is how several modern translations render it and which the grammar allows.
**Either way he loses**, and the reason he gives is not the sex but the contract: *"because I didn't
give her to Shelah, my son."*

**He does not plead ignorance, though he was ignorant.** He does not mention the veil, though the veil
is what fooled him. **He identifies the wrong as his own earlier breach of an obligation**, which is the
one thing in the affair he did with his eyes open. **No one else in Genesis does this.** See
[[Righteousness]] and [[Judah]].

***"He knew her again no more."*** ***Wəlōʾ-yāsap ʿôd lədaʿtāh*** — **the standard reading, and the
one the WEB gives.** A minority tradition vocalises the verb as *he did not cease to know her* — the
difference is whether the root is *yāsap*, *to add*, or *ʾāsap*, *to gather in, to cease* — and the
consonants permit both. **The wiki reports the variant and adopts the majority reading**, which is also
the one the rest of the chapter assumes: Tamar bears twins and is never mentioned again.

### 38:27–30 — a hand, a thread, and a breach

> **27** In the time of her travail, behold, twins were in her womb.
> **28** When she travailed, one put out a hand, and the midwife took and tied a scarlet thread on his
> hand, saying, “This came out first.”
> **29** As he drew back his hand, behold, his brother came out, and she said, “Why have you made a
> breach for yourself?” Therefore his name was called Perez.
> **30** Afterward his brother came out, that had the scarlet thread on his hand, and his name was
> called Zerah.

**The chapter that began with a firstborn who died childless ends with a birth in which the firstborn
is decided twice and the second decision holds.** *Təʾômîm*, **twins — and it is the second set in
Genesis**, forty chapters after Rebekah's. **The parallel runs deep enough to be structural**: two
brothers in a womb, a contest at the moment of birth, a midwife or a narrator marking who is ahead,
and **the one who should be first displaced by the one behind him.** See
[[Two Sets of Twins - Jacob and Esau, Perez and Zerah]] and [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

***"The midwife took and tied a scarlet thread on his hand, saying, 'This came out first.'"*** **She is
recording a legal fact, not decorating a baby.** Primogeniture turns on the order of birth, and **a hand
is not a birth** — which the next verse establishes by letting the hand go back in. **The thread is the
Bible's first attempt at documentary evidence and it is wrong within a verse.** The wiki notes that
*šānî*, scarlet, is the thread of the tabernacle hangings and of the cleansing rites (Exodus 25:4;
Leviticus 14:4), **and that Rahab's scarlet cord at Joshua 2:18 is a different word in a different
story**; no connection is drawn between them here.

***"Why have you made a breach for yourself?"*** ***Mah-pāraṣtā ʿāleykā pāreṣ*** — **and *pāraṣ* is a
violent word**: to *break through* a wall, to *burst out*. It is what Yahweh does to Uzzah (2 Samuel
6:8, *Perez-uzzah*) and what a breached city suffers. ***Pereṣ*** — **[[Perez]], *breach*** — is named
for the wall he came through, **and the wall was his brother.** The midwife's exclamation becomes the
name, which is how five of the six naming notices in this chapter work. See [[Names and Naming]].

***"His name was called Zerah."*** ***Zeraḥ***, from ***zāraḥ***, **to *rise* or *shine*, as the sun
does** — plausibly for the scarlet that showed first, the dawn-colour at the edge of the birth. **He is
the elder by the thread and the younger by the record**, and his line runs to Achan, who is *"son of
Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah"* (Joshua 7:1) — **the man whose theft
gets Israel beaten at Ai.** See [[Zerah]].

**And Perez's line runs to David.** *"Now this is the history of the generations of Perez"* — Hezron,
Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse, David (Ruth 4:18–22). **The book of Ruth ends on
this chapter's second-born**, and the elders of Bethlehem bless Boaz's marriage in this chapter's own
terms: *"let your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah"* (Ruth 4:12). **Two
childless widows, two men who owed them a levirate duty, and one town** — and Ruth's narrator makes the
comparison himself. See
[[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]].

## People

- [[Judah]] — **the chapter is his, and it is the only one in Genesis that is.** He goes down, marries
  a Canaanite, buries a wife and two sons, withholds the third, takes his daughter-in-law for a
  prostitute on a road, hands over his seal, sentences her to burn on hearsay, and then — handed his
  own signet — says **the first unqualified admission of fault by anyone in the book**: *"She is more
  righteous than I, because I didn't give her to Shelah, my son."* **The man who priced his brother at
  38:26 stops pricing**
- [[Tamar]] — **new page.** Twice widowed, sent home, and left there. **The only motive Genesis states
  in the chapter is hers** — *"she saw that Shelah was grown up, and she wasn't given to him as a wife"*
  — and it is a breach of contract, not desire or revenge. She takes a pledge instead of a price,
  names no one when she could have, and **is the first person in Scripture declared righteous in
  comparison with someone else.** Named in Ruth 4:12 and Matthew 1:3; **no origin, no father, no people
  given**
- [[Er]] — **new page.** Judah's firstborn, given a wife and a single verdict: *"wicked in Yahweh's
  sight."* **No offence is ever specified**, here or at 1 Chronicles 2:3. **One of the two men in
  Genesis the narrator says Yahweh killed**
- [[Onan]] — **new page.** Takes the marital right and withholds what it was granted for, *"lest he
  should give offspring to his brother"* — **and the verse states that motive as the thing that was
  evil.** The second man Yahweh kills. **His name is now an English word for something the text does
  not condemn him for**
- [[Shelah (son of Judah)]] — **new page.** The third son, promised to Tamar and never given; **he
  grows up offstage and never speaks.** Born at [[Chezib]], *deception*. **Renamed the older
  [[Shelah (son of Arpachshad)]] page in this pass**, per `AGENTS.md`, since two `Shelah.md` files
  would have collided silently
- [[Perez]] — **new page.** The second twin out, named for the breach he made — **and the line of
  Boaz, David, and Matthew 1:3.** Ruth 4:12 blesses a Bethlehem marriage in his name
- [[Zerah]] — **new page.** The hand and the scarlet thread; **first by the evidence and second by the
  birth.** Ancestor of Achan (Joshua 7:1)
- [[Shua's Daughter]] — **new page.** Judah's Canaanite wife, named only as her father's daughter —
  *Bath-shua* at 1 Chronicles 2:3, where the patronymic has hardened into a name. **Mother of all
  three sons, and dead in half a verse** (38:12)
- [[Hirah the Adullamite]] — **new page.** Judah's ***rēaʿ***, and **the only friend a patriarch is
  given in Genesis.** He carries the goat, asks the town for a *qədēšâ*, and takes the answer back;
  *"lest **we** be shamed"* is the last thing said about him
- **The midwife (38:28–29)** — unnamed, and **she does two consequential things**: ties the thread that
  records the wrong firstborn, and speaks the sentence that becomes Perez's name. **No page**, per the
  wiki's standing practice for anonymous single-line figures, set at the midwife of 35:17
- **Shua (38:2, 12)** — named, Canaanite, and nothing else; **the only thing the chapter tells us about
  him is that he had a daughter.** No page
- [[Joseph]], [[Jacob]] — **neither is named in the chapter**, which is the only stretch of the cycle
  where that is true. The chapter's placement between 37:36 and 39:1 is the reason they are both
  relevant and both absent
- [[Reuben]] — not present; **38:11's narrator-supplied motive is the same device used for him at
  37:22**, where the intention had to be stated because the speech did not contain it
- [[Rebekah]] — not present; **her veil at 24:65 is one of the Hebrew Bible's three *ṣāʿîp*** and the
  other two are in this chapter

## Places

- [[Adullam]] — **new page.** A Canaanite town in the Shephelah where Judah settles among his friend
  Hirah's people; **later the cave David escapes to** (1 Samuel 22:1) and a name in the Judahite town
  list (Joshua 15:35). **It is the first place any son of Jacob lives apart from the household**
- [[Chezib]] — **new page.** Named in a clause with nothing else in it — *"he was at Chezib, when she
  bore him"* — **and the name is from the root for *lie* or *fail*.** Almost certainly the Achzib of
  Joshua 15:44, on which Micah 1:14 makes the pun explicit
- [[Timnah]] — **new page.** The sheep-shearing town Judah goes *up* to; the road to it runs past
  Enaim. **Probably the same Timnah that Samson marries into** (Judges 14:1), in Philistine country
  on the Sorek. **Not to be confused with [[Timna]]**, the Horite concubine of Genesis 36:12
- [[Enaim]] — **new page.** *Petaḥ ʿênayim*, the gate or opening of Enaim, **and the name means *two
  eyes* or *two springs*.** Where Tamar sits veiled; **the one location in Genesis whose name is a
  comment on the scene**
- [[Canaan]] — the land Judah goes down into the middle of, and whose daughters the book's marriage
  rule excludes
- [[Hebron]] — not named, but the household Judah leaves at 38:1 is the one Jacob was settled in at
  37:14; **the chapter's geography is the low country west of it**

## Themes

- [[Righteousness]] — ***ṣādəqâ mimmennî***, *"she is more righteous than I"*: **the first time in
  Scripture one person declares another more in the right than himself**, and the ground given is a
  broken obligation, not the sexual act
- [[Marriage]] — a Canaanite wife taken in three verbs; **the Bible's first levirate marriage**,
  refused; and a betrothal held open indefinitely by a father-in-law who has no intention of closing it
- [[Deception]] — a veil that the text never calls a disguise-for-prostitution, a son withheld behind a
  stated excuse, and **a son born at a town called *Lie***
- [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] — **a firstborn who dies childless, a second son who refuses to
  raise seed for him, a third withheld — and twins whose order is marked, reversed, and recorded the
  second way**
- [[Judgment]] — *"let her be burned"*, pronounced without hearing or jurisdiction and reversed by the
  defendant's evidence in two verses; and **the only two individual executions Yahweh performs in
  Genesis**
- [[Retribution]] — *"Yahweh killed him"*, twice, with a reason given the second time and none the
  first
- [[Names and Naming]] — **six namings in thirty verses**: Er, Onan, Shelah, Perez, Zerah, and the
  place-name Chezib, **and at least four of them are wordplays the narrator never acknowledges**
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — *"he thought that she was a prostitute, **for she had covered her
  face**"*; a town called *eye-opening*; and *hakker-nāʾ*, *discern*, as the chapter's turn
- [[Nakedness and Covering]] — the garments of widowhood put off and put back on in six verses, and
  the ***ṣāʿîp*** that also veils [[Rebekah]] at 24:65
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — the ***ʿērābôn***, a word found nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible, which the New
  Testament borrows for the Spirit as God's guarantee
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — a goat offered, a seal surrendered, and a man who writes off both rather
  than be laughed at
- [[Life and Death]] — three deaths in eleven verses, two of them by divine act and one in half a
  clause, against two births in the last four
- [[Sin]] — Er's unspecified wickedness, Onan's specified one, Judah's admitted one, **and the chapter's
  refusal to rank them**
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Judah leaves the covenant household and lives in Canaanite towns among
  Canaanite friends; **the line of David is conceived there**
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — **there is none.** Tamar has no advocate, and the law that would later
  give her one (Deuteronomy 25:7–10) does not exist yet. **She has to be her own**
- [[Language and Speech]] — *hakker-nāʾ*; *zônâ* upgraded to *qədēšâ* by the man sent to pay her; and
  *"there has been no prostitute here"*, true twice over

## Notes

- **On the boundary, which was used as drawn.** The plan's box read *Judah and Tamar — Genesis 38:1–30*,
  and the chapter is one of the most self-contained units in the book: **it opens with *"at that time"*
  and a departure, and closes on a birth, with no character crossing either edge.** 37:36 sells Joseph
  to Potiphar and 39:1 buys him from the Ishmaelites; **nothing in Genesis 38 depends on either.** No
  alternative split was seriously considered.
- **On the title, kept from the plan.** *Judah and Tamar* is what the chapter is universally called and
  what a reader will search for. **Alternatives considered and rejected**: *The Pledge* (elegant, and
  it hides both people); *Tamar and the Pledge* (better on the protagonist, worse on recall);
  *She Is More Righteous Than I* (a phrase-title, which this wiki reserves for connection pages). **No
  other book has a passage page by this title**, so no `(Book)` qualifier is needed.
- **A silent collision found and fixed: `Shelah`.** Judah's third son and the post-flood patriarch of
  Genesis 10:24 and 11:14–15 have **the same English spelling and different Hebrew vocalisation**
  (*Šēlâ* / *Šelaḥ*). `scripts/link-check.sh` resolves targets by basename and **would have passed two
  files called `Shelah.md`** while Obsidian picked one of them for every inbound `[[Shelah]]`. Per
  `AGENTS.md`, neither name is fixed by the directory map, so **both take a qualifier**: the existing
  page was renamed to [[Shelah (son of Arpachshad)]] and its six inbound links fixed in this pass, and
  the new page is [[Shelah (son of Judah)]]. **The older page had itself predicted this collision** and
  it still happened, which is the argument for doing the rename in the pass that causes it.
- **A collision left un-pre-empted, deliberately: `Tamar`.** 2 Samuel 13's Tamar, David's daughter,
  **will need this page renamed when Samuel is ingested** — and she is already discussed by name on
  [[Dinah and Tamar - Two Rapes and Two Avenging Brothers]]. **The page is nonetheless left as
  `Tamar.md`**, because `AGENTS.md` disambiguates on an actual collision between pages and only one
  Tamar page exists. **The judgment call is recorded here and in the log** so the rename is not a
  surprise: when it comes, both become qualified — `Tamar (daughter-in-law of Judah)` and
  `Tamar (daughter of David)` — and the inbound links are fixed in the same pass, exactly as `Shelah`
  was here.
- **On what was created, and the judgment calls in it.**
  - **[[Tamar]], [[Er]], [[Onan]], [[Shelah (son of Judah)]], [[Perez]], [[Zerah]]** — all six are
    named, all six are in the genealogies at Genesis 46:12, Numbers 26:19–22, and 1 Chronicles 2:3–5,
    and **four of the six carry a name the chapter plays on.** None was a close call.
  - **[[Shua's Daughter]]** — a page for a woman named only as her father's daughter. **The wiki's
    practice is established**: [[Lot's Wife]], [[Lot's Daughters]], [[Abraham's Servant]],
    [[Job's Children]]. 1 Chronicles 2:3 calls her *Bath-shua*, which is the same words read as a
    name, and the page is filed under the designation Genesis actually uses.
  - **[[Hirah the Adullamite]]** — qualified from the start rather than bare, because *the Adullamite*
    is how Genesis identifies him both times after the first and because **Hiram of Tyre is one
    vowel away** in a book the wiki will reach. The patronymic-style qualifier costs nothing now and
    prevents a silent collision later.
  - **Four place pages** — [[Adullam]], [[Chezib]], [[Timnah]], [[Enaim]]. **Chezib and Enaim are each
    named once**, which is thinner than most, but both names are load-bearing for the chapter's
    wordplay and **the wiki already has pages for [[Esek]] and [[Sitnah]]**, which are named once each
    and are wells.
  - **Four connection pages**: [[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]]
    (explicitly promised by the Genesis 37 ingest),
    [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]],
    [[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]], and
    [[Two Sets of Twins - Jacob and Esau, Perez and Zerah]].
- **On what was deliberately not created.**
  - **No page for the midwife (38:28–29)**, though she ties the thread and speaks the name. Same
    footing as the midwife of 35:17 and the man in the field at 37:15.
  - **No page for Shua**, Judah's father-in-law. He is named twice and is a patronymic both times.
  - **No page for the four women of Matthew's genealogy as a group.** Rahab, Ruth, and Bathsheba are
    all in books not yet ingested, and **a page written now would be three-quarters forward
    reference.** The material that is available — Tamar in Matthew 1:3, Ruth 4:12, and the Canaanite
    marriage thread — is on
    [[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]] and
    [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] instead.
  - **No connection page for the *ʿērābôn* / *ἀρραβών* loanword**, though it is one of the more
    striking word-histories in Scripture. It is **a lexical fact with one Genesis occurrence and three
    Pauline ones**, and it fits on the passage page and [[Oaths and Vows]]; a page would be a
    dictionary entry.
  - **No `Prostitution` theme page.** The chapter's material distributes to [[Marriage]],
    [[Sin]], and [[Language and Speech]] — the *zônā*/*qədēšâ* shift being a fact about how the errand
    was described — and **a theme page would be built almost entirely out of books not yet ingested**
    (Leviticus, Deuteronomy, Hosea, Proverbs). It is worth opening when Hosea arrives.
- **A contradiction flagged and not resolved: Tamar's nationality.** **Genesis does not say.** It names
  Judah's wife as a Canaanite in the same breath as her father, and gives Tamar no father, no town, and
  no people at all — **the only person in the chapter whose origin is withheld.** Later tradition
  supplies both answers (Canaanite, in Jubilees 41:1; Israelite, in several rabbinic readings), and
  **the wiki adopts neither** and notes that Matthew 1:3 names her without comment in a genealogy that
  also names a Moabite and a Canaanite.
- **A tension recorded, not resolved: the levirate against Leviticus.** Judah orders Onan to go in to
  his brother's wife (38:8); **Leviticus 18:16 and 20:21 forbid exactly that and attach childlessness
  as the penalty.** Deuteronomy 25:5–10 commands it where the brothers *"dwell together"* and one dies
  without a son. **The wiki sets the three texts beside each other on**
  [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]] **without harmonising them**, per the
  schema.
- **On Onan, and what the verse does and does not say.** Genesis states his motive in the same sentence
  as the act — *"lest he should give offspring to his brother"* — and 38:10 calls *"the thing which he
  did"* evil. **The wiki reports that the reason the text gives is the defrauded brother**, notes that
  the act named is coitus interruptus rather than what the English word derived from his name means,
  and **does not adjudicate the later moral tradition built on the verse.** Recorded on [[Onan]].
- **On the two executions, recorded as a fact about the text.** *"Yahweh killed him"* (38:7) and
  *"he killed him also"* (38:10) are **the only occasions in Genesis where the narrator reports Yahweh
  killing a named individual.** The wiki states this and **does not argue from it**; the book's other
  divine killings are collective, and the two here are stated without theological comment by a narrator
  who supplies a charge for one of them and none for the other.
- **On the chapter's silence about Joseph and Jacob.** Neither is named in thirty verses, and the
  chapter sits between the two halves of a sentence about Joseph. **The wiki records the placement as
  an observation about the book's arrangement** — with the *hakker-nāʾ* pairing as the strongest
  internal evidence that it is deliberate — **and does not adopt either of the standard explanations**
  (an independent Judah tradition inserted here; a deliberate foil to Genesis 39). Both are on
  [[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]].
- **On stale forward-reference markers cleared elsewhere in the wiki.** *(not yet ingested)* markers
  pointing at Genesis 38 were cleared on [[Judah]] and
  [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]].
- **What was left for later pericopes.** *"Yahweh was with Joseph"* and Potiphar's wife (39:1–23);
  Judah's surety and his speech (43:8–9; 44:18–34); the Genesis 46:12 roster; and the sceptre at
  49:8–12. **All are given as plain scripture references rather than wikilinks, so none is a dangling
  link.**
