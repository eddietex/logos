# Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 35:1–29

## Summary

**Four things are put in the ground in twenty-nine verses, and one child is born.** No other chapter
in Genesis buries as much: the household's foreign gods under the oak at Shechem (35:4), Deborah
under the oak below Bethel (35:8), Rachel on the road to Ephrath (35:19), and Isaac at Machpelah by
implication (35:29). **The one birth is [[Benjamin]], the twelfth son**, and it is the birth that
kills his mother.

**The chapter completes the house and empties it in the same breath.** By 35:26 Genesis can say *"the
sons of Jacob were twelve"* and list them by mother — **the first complete roster of the tribes
anywhere in the Bible** — and by 35:29 the man has lost his wife, his mother's nurse, and his father,
and his firstborn has disqualified himself in a single verse the narrator breaks off mid-sentence.
**It is the last chapter in which [[Jacob]] is the acting centre of the book.** Genesis 36 belongs to
[[Esau]]; Genesis 37 opens on [[Joseph]].

**A vow twenty years overdue is finally kept, and it takes a second command and a massacre to get it
kept.** At [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]] Jacob had vowed *"this stone, which I have set up for a
pillar, will be God's house"* (28:22). At 31:13 God quoted the vow back to him — *"I am the God of
Bethel, where you anointed a pillar, where you vowed a vow to me"* — as the reason to leave Paddan
Aram. **He then went to Succoth, bought a field at Shechem, and stayed** (33:17–19), and what happened
next was [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]]. **35:1 is the second command, and this time he moves
the same day.**

**And the fear Jacob stated at 34:30 is answered without his asking for it.** *"I am few in number.
They will gather themselves together against me and strike me"* — and here, *"a terror of God was on
the cities that were around them, and they didn't pursue the sons of Jacob"* (35:5). **The chapter
that never names God is followed by a chapter that opens with God speaking**, and the protection
Jacob had calculated he lost is supplied in one clause.

### 35:1–4 — a second command, and a purge nobody was told to make

> **1** God said to Jacob, “Arise, go up to Bethel, and live there. Make there an altar to God, who
> appeared to you when you fled from the face of Esau your brother.”
> **2** Then Jacob said to his household, and to all who were with him, “Put away the foreign gods
> that are among you, purify yourselves, change your garments.
> **3** Let us arise, and go up to Bethel. I will make there an altar to God, who answered me in the
> day of my distress, and was with me on the way which I went.”
> **4** They gave to Jacob all the foreign gods which were in their hands, and the rings which were in
> their ears; and Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem.

**God identifies himself by an event rather than by a name** — *"who appeared to you when you fled
from the face of Esau your brother"* — **and the identification is a reproach with no reproachful word
in it.** Bethel is named as the place Jacob was running for his life when he last stood in it. Jacob
repeats the formula back in his own version at 35:3, and **his is warmer and vaguer**: *"who answered
me in the day of my distress, and was with me on the way which I went."* God dates the debt; Jacob
paraphrases it as a kindness.

**Nothing in the command mentions foreign gods. The purge is Jacob's own addition.** God says *arise,
go up, live there, build an altar* — four imperatives, none of them about idols — and Jacob's reply to
his household begins with a fifth that God did not give. **Genesis does not say what prompted it**,
and it is the only religious reform any patriarch conducts.

***"Put away the foreign gods … purify yourselves, change your garments."*** **Three commands in the
exact vocabulary Exodus will use to prepare Israel for Sinai**: *"consecrate them today and tomorrow,
and let them wash their garments"* (Exodus 19:10, 14). **Here the same rite is performed by a
householder for a household, with no priest, no tabernacle, and no law** — the pattern this wiki has
tracked since Abel. See [[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]] and [[Clean and Unclean]].

***"The foreign gods which were in their hands."*** **Genesis does not say whose they were, and it
never says what became of [[Rachel]]'s *teraphim*.** She stole them at 31:19, hid them in a camel's
saddle, and defeated her father's search (31:34–35); the narrator has not mentioned them since.
**The book sets a purge of household gods four chapters after a theft of household gods and declines
to connect them.** The wiki records the silence rather than closing it; see
[[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]] and [[Idolatry]].

***"And the rings which were in their ears."*** ***Nəzāmîm*** — **and this is the word's third
appearance in Genesis and its most loaded.** It is the ring [[Abraham's Servant]] put on
[[Rebekah]]'s face at the well (24:22, 47), the gift that sealed the betrothal of the woman whose
nurse is buried six verses from here. **And it is the word of Exodus 32:2–3**: *"Break off the golden
rings which are in the ears of your wives … and all the people broke off the golden rings which were
in their ears, and brought them to Aaron"* — **who makes a calf out of them.** The earrings that come
off at Bethel here are the earrings that go on at Sinai there, and the calf that results is the calf
Jeroboam will eventually install **in this town** (1 Kings 12:28–29). Genesis draws none of these
lines. See [[Bethel from Jacob's Pillar to Jeroboam's Calf]].

***"Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem."*** ***Wayyiṭmōn*** — *hid*, not *destroyed*,
not *burned*, not *ground to powder*, which is what Moses does to the calf (Exodus 32:20) and Josiah
to Bethel's altar (2 Kings 23:15). **They are buried intact, on ground Jacob owns**, at the oak that
was already a landmark when Abram reached it (12:6) and will still be there when Joshua sets a
covenant stone under it (Joshua 24:26). See [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]] and
[[The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem]].

### 35:5–7 — a terror nobody prays for, and an altar named for a place

> **5** They traveled, and a terror of God was on the cities that were around them, and they didn’t
> pursue the sons of Jacob.
> **6** So Jacob came to Luz (that is, Bethel), which is in the land of Canaan, he and all the people
> who were with him.
> **7** He built an altar there, and called the place El Beth El; because there God was revealed to
> him, when he fled from the face of his brother.

***"A terror of God."*** ***Ḥittat ʾĕlōhîm*** — **the phrase occurs nowhere else in the Bible**, and
the root *ḥ-t-t* is the one the conquest texts run on: *"I will send my terror before you"* (Exodus
23:27), *"the dread of you and the fear of you"* (Deuteronomy 11:25), *"all the inhabitants of the
land melt away before you"* (Joshua 2:9). **Here it is spent on a single family in flight from a town
its own sons have just sacked.** Nobody asks for it, nobody is told about it, and Genesis reports it
in a subordinate clause. **It is the exact answer to 34:30**, and [[Jacob]] is never shown learning
that it happened. See [[The Fear of God]].

***"Luz (that is, Bethel)."*** **The gloss the reader has been waiting for since 12:8.** The narrator
has used the later name from Abram's first visit onward, and admitted the anachronism once before at
28:19 — *"the name of the city was Luz at the first"*. See [[Bethel]].

***"He built an altar there, and called the place El Beth El."*** ***ʾĒl Bêt-ʾĒl***, *God of the house
of God* — **an altar name, in the pattern of *El Elohe Israel* at Shechem two chapters earlier**
(33:20). **Jacob is the only patriarch who names his altars**; Abraham and Isaac build them and call
on the name of Yahweh at them, but never title them. See [[The Names of God]] and
[[Names and Naming]].

**And note what the name does.** At 28:17 Jacob called the site *bêt ʾĕlōhîm*, God's house, in
astonishment at a place. **Here he attaches ʾĒl to it as a title** — the house of God is no longer the
thing he marvels at, it is the address he now knows God by. [[Bethel]] is the only place-name that
becomes a divine title anywhere in Scripture (31:13).

### 35:8 — a nurse is named at her funeral, and a tree is named for the weeping

> **8** Deborah, Rebekah’s nurse, died, and she was buried below Bethel under the oak; and its name
> was called Allon Bacuth.

**One verse, dropped between two theophanies, for a woman the Bible has mentioned once before and
never named.** At 24:59 *"They sent away Rebekah, their sister, with her nurse"* — **an anonymous
attachment to a bride's baggage, eleven chapters and roughly a century ago.** She is the only nurse
in the Hebrew Bible given a personal name, and **Genesis gives it to her at her burial and not at her
service.** See [[Deborah (Rebekah's nurse)]] and [[A Wife for Isaac]].

***ʾAllôn Bākût***, **oak of weeping** — **the only tree in Scripture named for a funeral**, and
Genesis does not say who wept. The weeping is in the place-name and nowhere in the narration; no
mourner is identified, no duration is given, nothing like the seventy days for Jacob or the thirty for
Aaron. See [[Burial and the Grave]] and [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]].

**Two questions the verse raises and does not answer, and the wiki answers neither.**

1. **How is she in Jacob's household at all?** She left Paddan Aram for Canaan with Rebekah;
   Jacob is coming the other way after twenty years. Either she made the return journey — [[Rebekah]]
   said at 27:45 *"I will send, and get you from there"*, and Genesis never reports her sending
   anyone — or she attached to Jacob's camp after Rebekah's death, which Genesis also never reports.
   **Both readings require an event the book withholds.**
2. **Why does she get this and Rebekah does not?** **Genesis never narrates Rebekah's death.** The
   matriarch who received the oracle, engineered the blessing, and sent her son away is disposed of in
   a subordinate clause of Jacob's deathbed inventory fourteen chapters later — *"there they buried
   Isaac and Rebekah his wife"* (49:31). **Her nurse gets a death notice, a named burial site, and a
   tree. She gets a list entry.** The wiki flags the asymmetry and does not explain it.

### 35:9–13 — the name given a second time, and the promise it was missing

> **9** God appeared to Jacob again, when he came from Paddan Aram, and blessed him.
> **10** God said to him, “Your name is Jacob. Your name shall not be Jacob any more, but your name
> will be Israel.” He named him Israel.
> **11** God said to him, “I am God Almighty. Be fruitful and multiply. A nation and a company of
> nations will be from you, and kings will come out of your body.
> **12** The land which I gave to Abraham and Isaac, I will give it to you, and to your offspring
> after you will I give the land.”
> **13** God went up from him in the place where he spoke with him.

***"Again."*** **The adverb is the chapter's admission that it is doing something twice.** Jacob was
renamed at the Jabbok three chapters ago (32:28) by a man who would not give his own name and who
attached **no promise whatever** to the new one. **Here God does it directly, in daylight, without a
fight, and hands over everything the first naming withheld.** The wiki treats the two as a pair; see
the new connection page
[[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

**The renaming formula is not identical.** At 32:28 the reason is stated — *"for you have fought with
God and with men, and have prevailed"*. **Here no reason is given at all.** The name arrives as a bare
decree, and **the narrator adds a clause it did not add at the Jabbok**: *"He named him Israel"*
(35:10b), making God the agent of the naming rather than the wrestler.

***"I am God Almighty."*** ***ʾĂnî ʾĒl Šadday*** — **the fourth and last time the title is spoken in
Genesis by God himself**, after 17:1 to Abram and 28:3 in Isaac's mouth. **And this is the verse that
answers 28:3 word for word.** Isaac had petitioned: *"May ʾĒl Šadday bless you, and make you fruitful,
and multiply you, that you may be a company of peoples"* (28:3). **God grants the petition in the same
divine name, using the same verbs, seven chapters later** — see [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]],
which flagged this verse in advance, and
[[God Almighty - El Shaddai in Genesis and Exodus]].

***"Be fruitful and multiply."*** ***Pərû ûrəbû*** — **the creation blessing** of 1:28 and 9:1 and 9:7,
spoken to humanity and then to the survivors of the flood, **now spoken to one man with twelve sons.**
The command that opened the world is narrowed onto a household. See [[Creation]] and [[Blessing]].

***"A nation and a company of nations … and kings will come out of your body."*** ***Gôy ûqəhal
gôyim***, and ***məlākîm mēḥălāṣêkā*** — **the kings-promise, third and last of its three occurrences
in Genesis**: 17:6 to Abraham, 17:16 to Sarah, and here. **All three are made to people who will never
see a king**, and the promise sits unredeemed for eight hundred years. See
[[Kings Will Come Out of You]], [[Kingship and Empire]], and [[The Nations]].

***"The land which I gave to Abraham and Isaac."*** **The perfect tense — *gave*, not *will give* —
about ground on which all three of them were tenants**, and about a man who has just paid cash for one
field (33:19) and is about to bury his father in a cave his grandfather also paid cash for. See
[[Land and Sojourning]] and [[Covenant]].

***"God went up from him in the place where he spoke with him."*** ***Wayyaʿal*** — **the same verb the
angels used on the stairway in this same place** (28:12), and the only time in Genesis the narrator
describes God's departure as an ascent. See [[Angels Ascending and Descending - Genesis 28.12 in John 1.51]].

### 35:14–15 — the fourth pillar, and the Bible's first drink offering

> **14** Jacob set up a pillar in the place where he spoke with him, a pillar of stone. He poured out
> a drink offering on it, and poured oil on it.
> **15** Jacob called the name of the place where God spoke with him “Bethel”.

***Maṣṣēbâ*** — **the fourth and second-to-last of Jacob's standing stones** (28:18; 31:45; 35:14;
35:20), **and he is the only person in the Bible who raises them.** Two of the four are at this site,
and the second is set on the ground where the first one stood. **Deuteronomy 16:22 will forbid the
practice by name** — *"neither shall you set yourself up a sacred stone, which Yahweh your God
hates"* — and Genesis reports it without a flicker. See
[[The Pillar Jacob Raised and the Pillars the Law Forbids]].

***"He poured out a drink offering on it."*** ***Nesek*** — **the first drink offering in Scripture.**
The word will become a fixed element of the sacrificial system (Exodus 29:40; Numbers 15:5–10), always
wine, always attached to a burnt offering, always handled by a priest. **Here it is poured by a
herdsman onto a rock, four hundred years early, with no altar mentioned in the verse and nothing said
about what was in it.** Genesis supplies the oil from 28:18 as well, so **the stone gets both the old
anointing and a rite the book has never used before.** See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].

***"Jacob called the name of the place … Bethel."*** **He names it again.** He named it at 28:19 and
he names it here, and Genesis records the second naming without noting that it is one. **Three
namings in this pericope repeat or overturn an earlier one** — Bethel renamed, Israel re-given,
Ben Oni overruled — and the chapter is, on that reading, a chapter about second attempts. See
[[Names and Naming]] and [[Oaths and Vows]]: the vow of 28:20–22 is discharged in this verse and the
one before it, and **Genesis does not say so.** Nobody in the text observes that the pillar is now
God's house, that the tenth was ever paid, or that a promise made by a frightened fugitive has been
kept by a rich old man.

### 35:16–20 — a prayer answered, and it kills her

> **16** They traveled from Bethel. There was still some distance to come to Ephrath, and Rachel
> travailed. She had hard labor.
> **17** When she was in hard labor, the midwife said to her, “Don’t be afraid, for now you will have
> another son.”
> **18** As her soul was departing (for she died), she named him Benoni, but his father named him
> Benjamin.
> **19** Rachel died, and was buried on the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem).
> **20** Jacob set up a pillar on her grave. The same is the Pillar of Rachel’s grave to this day.

***"Some distance."*** ***Kibrat hāʾāreṣ*** — **an unknown unit, used three times in the Bible and all
three about this same stretch of road** (35:16; 48:7; 2 Kings 5:19). Nobody knows how far it is. **The
one measurement Genesis gives for the place Rachel dies is a word the language did not keep.**

***"Don't be afraid, for now you will have another son."*** **The midwife is quoting Rachel's own
prayer back to her at the moment it is answered.** At [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] Rachel named
Joseph *"May Yahweh **add** another son to me"* (30:24) — ***yōsēp***, the name built on the request.
**She gets the son she named her first son for, and the getting is what ends her.** The midwife's
*don't be afraid* is the last comfort offered to her and it is factually correct and useless, in the
register of Jacob's *"Am I in God's place?"* at 30:2. See
[[Am I in God's Place - Jacob at 30.2 and Joseph at 50.19]] and [[Prayer]].

***"As her soul was departing (for she died), she named him Benoni, but his father named him
Benjamin."*** **The only naming in Genesis that is overruled**, and the only one performed by a dying
woman.

***Ben-ʾônî*** **is genuinely ambiguous and both senses are live.** ***ʾÔn*** means **vigour, virility,
strength** — it is the word in *"Reuben, you are my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my
**strength**"* (49:3), spoken about the son whose one act is seven verses below this one. And *ʾāwen*,
from a homonymous root, means **trouble, sorrow, iniquity** — the sense nearly every translation takes
here, including the WEB's implicit one. **So the name is either *son of my strength* or *son of my
sorrow*, and the man who overrules it is the man who will use the other sense of the word about
Reuben.** Genesis glosses neither.

***Bin-yāmîn***, **son of the right hand** — or **son of the south**, since *yāmîn* is the ordinary
Hebrew for both, and the tribe's territory is the southern edge of the northern kingdom. **He is the
only one of the twelve sons named by his father.** Eleven were named by women; the twelfth is named by
[[Jacob]], over his wife's body, against the name she used her last breath on. See
[[Names and Naming]] and [[Benjamin]].

***"Rachel died, and was buried on the way to Ephrath."*** **She is the one matriarch not carried to
Machpelah.** Sarah, Abraham, Rebekah, Isaac, Leah, and Jacob all go into the cave; **Rachel is buried
where she falls**, and Jacob marks it with the last of his pillars rather than the family tomb. **The
loved wife is the one left on the roadside and the unloved one is buried with the patriarchs.** See
[[Buried at Machpelah - The Six Graves of Genesis]], [[Machpelah]], and [[Leah]].

***"(also called Bethlehem)"*** **is a gloss, and it is the most disputed three words in the
chapter.** 1 Samuel 10:2 puts Rachel's tomb *"in the border of Benjamin at Zelzah"*, ten miles north
of Bethlehem and on the other side of Jerusalem; Jeremiah 31:15 hears her weeping at **Ramah**, in the
same northern district; and Matthew 2:18 applies Jeremiah's verse to the infants of **Bethlehem**.
**The wiki lays out the three locations and adopts none.** See the new connection page
[[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]], and [[Bethlehem]].

***"To this day."*** **A narrator's aside to a reader standing somewhere later than the story**, the
same device as *"the name of the city was Luz at the first"* (28:19) and *"the Canaanite was then in
the land"* (12:6). It tells us the marker outlived the man who set it, and nothing about where it was.

### 35:21–22a — a tower, and a verse the text breaks off

> **21** Israel traveled, and spread his tent beyond the tower of Eder.
> **22** While Israel lived in that land, Reuben went and lay with Bilhah, his father’s concubine, and
> Israel heard of it. Now the sons of Jacob were twelve.

***Migdal-ʿĒder***, **tower of the flock** — a shepherds' watchtower, named as a landmark and then
dropped. **Micah 4:8 addresses it as a person**: *"You, tower of the flock, the hill of the daughter
of Zion, to you it will come, yes, the former dominion will come"* — and later tradition, Jewish and
Christian, located it near Bethlehem and read the shepherds of Luke 2:8 against it. **Genesis says
only that Jacob camped past it.** See [[The Tower of Eder]].

**Then one sentence, with no preparation, no scene, and no consequence.**

***"Reuben went and lay with Bilhah, his father's concubine, and Israel heard of it."*** **The verb is
the one from the last chapter** — *wayyišmaʿ*, *he heard*, exactly as at 34:5 where *"Jacob heard that
he had defiled Dinah"* and *held his peace*. **Twice in two chapters Genesis tells us what Jacob heard
and refuses to tell us what he thought.** He says nothing for fourteen chapters, and then, from his
deathbed: *"Boiling over like water, you shall not excel; because you went up to your father's bed, then
defiled it"* (49:4). See [[Reuben]], [[Bilhah]], and [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**The act is not only a sexual offence; it is a move on the succession.** Taking a ruler's concubine is
how the Bible's later politics announces a claim: Abner with Saul's concubine and Ish-bosheth's
accusation (2 Samuel 3:7–8), Absalom with David's ten on the palace roof *"in the sight of all
Israel"* on Ahithophel's explicit advice (2 Samuel 16:21–22), and Adonijah asking for Abishag, which
Solomon reads as *"ask for him the kingdom also"* and executes him for (1 Kings 2:22). **The firstborn
of Jacob does in one verse what three later men do as policy**, and the wiki treats them as one
pattern; see the new connection page
[[Lying with the Father's Concubine - A Claim on the Succession]].

***"Now the sons of Jacob were twelve."*** **The Masoretic Text breaks the verse in half here.** A
*piska bĕʾemṣaʿ pasuq* — a section break in the middle of a verse — falls after *"Israel heard of
it"*, one of a very small number in the Hebrew Bible, **and it is the scribal tradition's way of
marking a sentence it will not finish.** The Septuagint fills the gap with *καὶ πονηρὸν ἐφάνη
ἐναντίον αὐτοῦ*, *and it appeared evil before him*. **The wiki records the Greek as a reading of the
break, not as the missing clause**, and notes that the Hebrew's refusal to complete the sentence is
the same refusal the narrative has already made twice about this man's silences.

**And the count that follows is a non sequitur on purpose.** The verse that reports the firstborn
disqualifying himself is the verse that announces the house is complete. **Genesis puts the roster
immediately after the ruin of the man at the top of it.**

### 35:22b–26 — the first complete list of the twelve

> **23** The sons of Leah: Reuben (Jacob’s firstborn), Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun.
> **24** The sons of Rachel: Joseph and Benjamin.
> **25** The sons of Bilhah (Rachel’s servant): Dan and Naphtali.
> **26** The sons of Zilpah (Leah’s servant): Gad and Asher. These are the sons of Jacob, who were
> born to him in Paddan Aram.

**The Bible's first complete list of the twelve, and it is organized by mother.** Not by age, not by
later tribal geography, not by the order of the births as Genesis 29–30 narrated them. **Wives first,
slaves second; within each mother, birth order.** Strict birth order would run Reuben, Simeon, Levi,
Judah, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Joseph, Benjamin — **this list moves Joseph from
eleventh to seventh and Dan from fifth to ninth.** Every later list in Scripture reorders it again, and
no two agree. See [[The Twelve Sons and the Lists That Reorder Them]].

***"Reuben (Jacob's firstborn)."*** **The title is given, in parentheses, in the verse after the verse
that costs him it.** Genesis states the rank and the disqualification back to back and comments on
neither. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

***"Bilhah (Rachel's servant)"*** and ***"Zilpah (Leah's servant)"*** — **named as mothers of four of
the twelve, with their status attached.** [[Bilhah]] is called a *concubine* four verses earlier and a
*wife* at 37:2 and a *servant* here, **and Genesis reconciles none of the three.** The wiki has flagged
this since [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and continues to adopt none of them.

**[[Dinah]] is not in the list.** It is a list of sons; she was named at her birth (30:21) and given a
chapter of her own (34), and the roster that closes the household does not count her. **Genesis
records the omission without remark**, as it does with Serah, Asher's daughter, at 46:17.

***"These are the sons of Jacob, who were born to him in Paddan Aram"*** — **and one of them was not.**
**Benjamin was born on the road to Ephrath, in Canaan, seven verses above this one.** The contradiction
is flat, internal to a single chapter, and unresolved in the text.

**Three readings, and the wiki adopts none.**

1. **A generalizing summary.** The formula describes the list as a whole — eleven of twelve were born
   there — in the loose way a colophon often does, and the exception is beneath the summary's
   resolution. This is the traditional harmonization, and it is what the rabbinic commentators offer.
2. **Composite sources.** The roster belongs to a strand that did not contain the Benjamin birth
   narrative of 35:16–20, and the two were joined without reconciliation — the standard critical
   account, which also notes that 46:15–25 repeats the same by-mother structure with the same
   Paddan Aram attribution.
3. **A deliberate rounding**, marking the household as a unit constituted in exile and brought home
   whole, with the last son's birthplace subordinated to the point being made.

**What is not in doubt is that Genesis does not notice.** The book sets the two statements seven
verses apart and moves on, exactly as it sets Bilhah's three job titles four chapters apart and moves
on.

### 35:27–29 — Jacob reaches his father, and buries him

> **27** Jacob came to Isaac his father, to Mamre, to Kiriath Arba (which is Hebron), where Abraham
> and Isaac lived as foreigners.
> **28** The days of Isaac were one hundred eighty years.
> **29** Isaac gave up the spirit, and died, and was gathered to his people, old and full of days.
> Esau and Jacob, his sons, buried him.

***"Jacob came to Isaac his father."*** **The vow of 28:21 is completed in four words** — *"so that I
come again to my father's house in peace"* — **and it has taken him from 28:22 to 35:27 to arrive.**
33:18 gave him *šālēm*, in peace, at Shechem; **the destination itself waits until here.** He does not
speak to his father, and Genesis records no word between them. **[[Isaac]]'s last recorded speech was
28:1–5, the sending.** See [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]].

***"To Mamre, to Kiriath Arba (which is Hebron), where Abraham and Isaac lived as foreigners."***
**Three names for one place and a verb for what they did there.** ***Gār*** — *sojourned*, the
sojourner's verb, used of the two generations who owned exactly one cave between them. See
[[Mamre (place)]], [[Hebron]], [[Land and Sojourning]], and
[[A Stranger and a Foreigner - The Sojourner's Confession]].

***"The days of Isaac were one hundred eighty years."*** **The longest life of the three patriarchs**
— Abraham 175, Jacob 147 — **and the least eventful.** Isaac is on stage for parts of nine chapters,
speaks fewer lines than his wife, and is the only patriarch who never leaves Canaan.

***"Gave up the spirit, and died, and was gathered to his people, old and full of days."*** **The
three-verb formula, in its third of four Genesis occurrences** (25:8 Abraham, 25:17 Ishmael, here,
49:33 Jacob) — **and the gathering is again listed as a separate event from the dying and placed before
the burial.** See [[Gathered to His People]] and
[[Buried in a Good Old Age - The Promise at Genesis 15.15 and Its Fulfilment]].

***"Esau and Jacob, his sons, buried him"*** — **the elder named first.** At Abraham's grave it was
*"Isaac and Ishmael his sons"* (25:9), **covenant son before firstborn**; here the order is reversed,
against 28:5's *"Jacob and Esau"* and against everything the book has done with these two since 25:23.
**The last thing the brothers do together in Genesis is a funeral**, and the verse gives Esau the
precedence the whole cycle took from him. See [[Esau]] and
[[The Elder Will Serve the Younger - Genesis 25.23 in Malachi and Romans]].

**And the death is out of order.** On the book's own arithmetic Isaac is alive through the selling of
Joseph and dies roughly ten years before the descent into Egypt: Isaac is 60 at the twins' birth
(25:26), so he dies when Jacob is 120, and Jacob is 130 at 47:9. **Genesis closes the Isaac account
here because it is finished with the Isaac account** — exactly as it finished Ishmael at 25:17 before
turning to Jacob, and as it will give Esau's *toledot* the whole of chapter 36 before opening the
Joseph cycle. See [[The Other Line First - The Order of the Toledot in Genesis]].

**Rebekah is not mentioned.** Jacob returns to the house she sent him from; **the book does not say
whether she was alive, does not report her death, and does not record them meeting.** *"Let me not be
bereaved of you both in one day"* (27:45) is the last thing she says in Scripture, and she is never
shown getting either of them back.

## People

- [[Jacob]] — **obeys on the first line and buries on the last.** He adds a purge to a command that did
  not ask for one, builds and names an altar, receives the name Israel a second time and the promise
  the first time withheld, sets his fourth pillar and pours the Bible's first drink offering,
  overrules his dying wife's naming of their son, hears what his firstborn has done and says nothing,
  and reaches his father's house seven chapters after vowing to
- [[God]] — **speaks twice, acts once without speaking.** He commands the return by naming the flight
  it repays (35:1), puts a terror on the cities without being asked (35:5), and in the chapter's one
  extended speech gives the name, the title *ʾĒl Šadday*, the creation blessing, a company of nations,
  kings, and the land (35:9–12) — then *"went up from him"*
- [[Rachel]] — **dies getting the son she named her first son for.** She travails, is told by a midwife
  not to be afraid, names the child *Ben Oni* as her soul departs, is overruled, and is buried where
  she fell under the only one of Jacob's pillars set over a person. **She is the one matriarch not at
  Machpelah**, and the only woman in Genesis whose grave is given a name
- [[Benjamin]] — **new page.** The twelfth son and the last; named twice in one verse, by a dying
  mother and by a father who overrides her; **the only one of the twelve named by Jacob**, and the only
  one born in Canaan — though 35:26 says otherwise seven verses later
- [[Isaac]] — **dies at a hundred eighty, silent, in the house he never left.** Jacob reaches him and
  no word passes between them on the page. The three-verb death formula, the longest patriarchal life,
  and a burial performed by both sons with the elder named first
- [[Deborah (Rebekah's nurse)]] — **new page.** Named at her funeral and nowhere else; the anonymous
  nurse of 24:59, buried under an oak below Bethel which is renamed for the weeping. **The only nurse
  in the Hebrew Bible with a name**
- [[Reuben]] — **one verse, and it costs him the birthright.** He lies with his father's concubine; his
  father hears and says nothing for fourteen chapters; and the verse that reports it is cut off
  mid-sentence by the Masoretic scribes and completed by nobody
- [[Bilhah]] — **acted upon, unspeaking, and given a third job title.** *Concubine* at 35:22,
  *servant* at 35:25, *wife* at 37:2. **The Bible never records a word she said**
- [[Esau]] — **returns for one clause**, to bury his father alongside the brother who took his
  blessing, and is named first in it
- [[Rebekah]] — **named once, as a possessive**: *"Deborah, Rebekah's nurse"*. She is the reason the
  verse exists and she is not in the chapter, and Genesis never narrates her death
- [[Leah]], [[Zilpah]] — named in the roster as mothers of six and two of the twelve; neither speaks
- [[Simeon]], [[Levi]], [[Judah]], [[Issachar]], [[Zebulun]], [[Joseph]], [[Dan (person)]],
  [[Naphtali]], [[Gad]], [[Asher]] — listed at 35:23–26 in the Bible's first complete roster of the
  twelve, ordered by mother rather than by age
- [[Abraham]] — named twice: in the land promise God repeats (35:12) and in the sojourning at Hebron
  (35:27)
- [[Dinah]] — **absent from the roster by construction.** Named at her birth and given chapter 34; not
  counted among the twelve

## Places

- [[Bethel]] — **the destination, the altar, the second naming, and the fourth pillar.** The vow of
  28:20–22 is discharged here, twenty years and two divine commands after it was made. The chapter also
  supplies the town's old name a second time — *"Luz (that is, Bethel)"*
- [[Bethlehem]] — **new page.** Named at 35:19 as a gloss on Ephrath, in the verse that buries Rachel;
  the town Micah 5:2, Ruth, and Matthew 2 will build on, and the site whose identification with
  Rachel's grave 1 Samuel 10:2 contradicts
- [[The Tower of Eder]] — **new page.** *Migdal-ʿĒder*, tower of the flock; a landmark Jacob camps
  beyond in one clause, and the name Micah 4:8 addresses as a person
- [[Shechem (city)]] — the oak the foreign gods are buried under; Jacob's own field, bought at 33:19
- [[Mamre (place)]], [[Hebron]] — the three-named site Jacob finally reaches, where Abraham and Isaac
  *sojourned*, and where Isaac dies. *Kiriath Arba* is glossed to *Hebron* in the same clause
- [[Machpelah]] — not named in the chapter, and the place Isaac is buried (49:31) and Rachel is not
- [[Canaan]] — named at 35:6 as where Bethel is; the land of the promise repeated at 35:12
- [[Aram Naharaim]] — *Paddan Aram*, named at 35:9 and 35:26 as where Jacob came from and where the
  twelve were born

## Themes

- [[Idolatry]] — the one religious purge any patriarch conducts, added to a command that did not ask
  for it, and Rachel's *teraphim* never accounted for
- [[Names and Naming]] — **five namings, three of them second attempts**: *El Beth El*, *Allon Bacuth*,
  *Israel* (again), *Bethel* (again), and *Ben Oni* overruled to *Benjamin*
- [[Burial and the Grave]] — four things put in the ground in one chapter; the only tree in Scripture
  named for a funeral; the one matriarch not at Machpelah
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — the vow of 28:20–22 kept at 35:14–15, with no character in the text remarking
  on it
- [[Covenant]] — the land promise repeated in the perfect tense to a man who owns one field
- [[Blessing]] — *"be fruitful and multiply"*, the creation blessing narrowed onto one household
- [[The Names of God]] — *ʾĒl Šadday* spoken by God for the last time in Genesis, and *ʾĒl Bêt-ʾĒl*,
  a place turned into a title
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — an altar, and the Bible's first *nesek*, poured on a stone by a man with
  no priesthood
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — *purify yourselves, change your garments*, the Sinai preparation performed by
  a householder four centuries early
- [[The Fear of God]] — *ḥittat ʾĕlōhîm*, a phrase found nowhere else, spent on one family
- [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] — Reuben's one verse, and the parenthesis *"Jacob's firstborn"*
  three verses later
- [[Sin]] — the act at 35:22, which the Hebrew declines to finish the sentence about
- [[Kingship and Empire]], [[The Nations]] — *a company of nations*, and *kings will come out of your
  body*
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — *gār*, what Abraham and Isaac did at Hebron for two hundred years
- [[Life and Death]] — three deaths and one birth, and the birth is the cause of one of the deaths
- [[Prayer]] — 30:24's request, granted at 35:17 in the words of a midwife

## Notes

- **On the boundary, which was used as drawn.** The plan's box read *Return to Bethel; the Deaths of
  Rachel and Isaac — Genesis 35:1–29*, and it needed no revision. **35:1 opens on a direct command from
  God**, the thing chapter 34 conspicuously lacked, and **35:29 closes the Isaac account**, with 36:1
  opening the formula *"Now this is the history of the generations of Esau"* on a different subject
  entirely. **A three-way split was considered and rejected** — 1–15 (Bethel), 16–22a (Rachel and
  Reuben), 22b–29 (the roster and Isaac) — because the chapter's unity is not its plot but its
  ledger: four burials and a birth, and each of the three parts loses its force read apart from the
  other two. **Genesis 35 is also unusually well-behaved as a chapter break**, which is not true of
  most of this cycle.
- **On the title, kept from the plan.** *Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac* names the
  three things a reader would search for. **Alternatives considered and rejected**: *Four Burials and
  a Birth* (the wiki's own reading of the shape, but an interpretive claim in a title, and it hides
  Bethel); *The Closing of the Jacob Cycle* (true and unsearchable); *El Beth El* (names five verses).
  The semicolon is deliberate — the chapter is two arrivals and three departures and no single
  conjunction joins them honestly. **No other book has a passage page by this title**, so no `(Book)`
  qualifier is needed.
- **On what was created, and the judgment calls in it.**
  - **[[Benjamin]]** — a person page was unavoidable; he is born, named twice, and listed here.
  - **[[Deborah (Rebekah's nurse)]]** — created under the qualifier [[A Wife for Isaac]]'s own notes
    specified in advance, so that Deborah the prophetess and judge (Judges 4–5) can take the bare
    name or her own qualifier when Judges is reached. **A bare `Deborah.md` would have been a silent
    basename collision**, which `AGENTS.md` treats as the exact failure the qualifier rules exist for.
  - **[[Bethlehem]]** — a place page, titled by the name that carries the rest of Scripture rather
    than by *Ephrath*, which is put in `also_known_as` alongside *Ephrathah*. This follows [[Bethel]],
    which holds *Luz* the same way.
  - **[[The Tower of Eder]]** — a thin place page, created on the precedent of [[Esek]] and [[Sitnah]]:
    a named site that occurs once, that later Scripture picks up (Micah 4:8), and that would otherwise
    have nowhere to hang.
  - **Three connection pages**: [[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]],
    [[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]], and
    [[Lying with the Father's Concubine - A Claim on the Succession]].
- **On what was deliberately not created.** ***Allon Bacuth* is not a place page.** It is a tree, not a
  settlement, it occurs once, and [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]] already tracks it alongside the oak
  of Moreh and the tamarisk at Beersheba; a page would duplicate that thread and orphan itself.
  ***El Beth El* is not a place page either** — it is the name of an altar, like *El Elohe Israel* at
  33:20, and altar names live on [[The Names of God]]. **The midwife of 35:17 gets no page**: she is
  unnamed, speaks one line, and the wiki's practice for anonymous single-line figures (the Egyptian
  midwives are still ahead) is to leave them on the passage page. **And no `Paddan Aram.md` was
  created**, per the standing instruction on [[A Wife for Isaac]]: it is [[Aram Naharaim]] under a
  second designation.
- **A contradiction flagged and not resolved: Benjamin's birthplace.** 35:16–19 has him born in Canaan
  on the road to Ephrath; 35:26 says all twelve *"were born to him in Paddan Aram."* Three readings
  are set out in the 35:22b–26 section above — generalizing summary, composite sources, deliberate
  rounding — **and the wiki adopts none.** This is an internal contradiction within a single chapter,
  which is rarer than the cross-chapter kind the wiki usually records.
- **A contradiction carried forward, not newly created: Bilhah's status.** *Concubine* (35:22),
  *servant* (35:25), *wife* (30:4; 37:2). [[Bilhah]] and [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] already
  flag it; this chapter supplies two of the three terms four verses apart and still does not
  reconcile them.
- **A geographical contradiction flagged and not resolved: where Rachel is buried.** Genesis 35:19 and
  48:7 gloss *Ephrath* as *Bethlehem*, six miles south of Jerusalem; 1 Samuel 10:2 puts the tomb
  *"in the border of Benjamin at Zelzah"*, and Jeremiah 31:15 hears her at Ramah — both ten or more
  miles north, on the other side of the city. **The new connection page lays out all three and adopts
  none**; the wiki notes only that Matthew 2:18 reads the southern identification and that the gloss
  is the kind of aside 12:6 and 28:19 have already shown this narrator making for a later reader.
- **On the Masoretic break at 35:22, recorded as a scribal fact.** The *piska bĕʾemṣaʿ pasuq* after
  *"Israel heard of it"* is one of the few mid-verse section breaks in the Hebrew Bible, and the
  Septuagint's *"and it appeared evil before him"* is an ancient completion of it. **The wiki reports
  the break and the Greek reading and does not treat the Greek as recovering a lost Hebrew clause**,
  on the same footing it gives the Septuagint's *a field* for Abraham's tamarisk at 21:33.
- **On the chronology of Isaac's death, reported but not adopted as a problem.** By Genesis's own
  numbers Isaac outlives the selling of Joseph by about twelve years. **The wiki does not treat this
  as an error**; it is the *toledot* structure doing what it does at 25:17 with Ishmael — finishing one
  account before starting the next. See
  [[The Other Line First - The Order of the Toledot in Genesis]].

  pointing at Genesis 35 were cleared on [[Bethel]], [[Shechem (city)]], [[Mamre (place)]],
  [[Rachel]], [[Jacob]], [[Isaac]], [[Esau]], [[Reuben]], [[Bilhah]], [[Zilpah]], [[Issachar]],
  [[Zebulun]], [[Dan (person)]], [[Naphtali]], [[Gad]], [[Asher]], [[Names and Naming]],
  [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]], and [[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]].
- **What was left for later pericopes.** Esau's *toledot* (36:1–43); Bilhah called a *wife* again
  (37:2); Jacob's own retelling of Rachel's death (48:7); Reuben's disinheriting, Rebekah's only
  named burial, and Jacob's gathering (49:3–4, 31, 33). All are given as plain scripture references
  rather than wikilinks, so none is a dangling link.
