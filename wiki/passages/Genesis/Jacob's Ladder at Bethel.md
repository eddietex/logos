# Jacob's Ladder at Bethel

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 28:10–22

## Summary

**God speaks to Jacob for the first time, and says nothing about chapter 27.**

That is the fact the passage is built on. The man asleep on the ground has lied to his blind father,
taken a blessing under a disguise, and is walking north because his brother intends to kill him.
**There is no rebuke, no condition, and no reference to any of it.** What arrives instead is the
Abrahamic promise — land, offspring, the nations — spoken to him directly for the first time in his
life, with three clauses attached that neither Abraham nor Isaac ever received: ***I am with you***,
***I will keep you***, ***I will bring you back.*** **Those three exist because Jacob is the first
bearer of the promise to leave the land carrying it.**

**And he is not looking for God when it happens.** *"He came to a certain place, and stayed there all
night, because the sun had set."* The verb is ***pāgaʿ***, to **light upon, run into, be met by** —
he does not choose the site, he collides with it. **The whole ancient practice of sleeping at a
shrine to solicit a dream is available to this story and Genesis refuses it**: Jacob stops because it
is dark, sleeps on a stone, and finds out afterwards what he was lying on. ***"Surely Yahweh is in
this place, and I didn't know it."***

**What he sees is a construction project running the other way.** The *sullām* has *"its top reached
to heaven"* — and the last thing in this book with its top reaching heaven was the tower of
[[The Tower of Babel]] at Genesis 11:4, built by men *"lest we be scattered abroad."* Here the
structure is already standing, nobody built it, the traffic on it starts from the ground and goes up,
and the promise attached to it is that Jacob **will** be scattered — west, east, north, and south.
**Jacob then names the place *the gate of heaven*, which is what the name Babel was popularly taken
to mean.** Genesis puts the two scenes at opposite ends of the same image and makes no comment.

**Then he answers, and the answer is the most calculating speech in the passage.** God has promised
presence, protection, and return, unconditionally. Jacob repeats all three back **as conditions** —
*"If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go … so that I come again to my
father's house in peace"* — adds two items God never mentioned, **bread and clothing**, and leaves
out the land, the offspring, and the nations entirely. **He asks for less than he was given, and asks
for it if.** It is the first vow in the Bible.

### The shape

| verses | what happens | who speaks |
|---|---|---|
| **10–11** | an itinerary, a sunset, a stone, and a place he stumbles into | nobody |
| **12** | the *sullām*, its top in heaven, and the angels going **up** first | nobody |
| **13–15** | Yahweh names himself and gives the promise — **land, offspring, nations, and presence** | **God**, to Jacob, for the first time |
| **16–17** | *"I didn't know it"*, then fear: **God's house** and **the gate of heaven** | **Jacob** |
| **18–19** | the pillow becomes a pillar, oil is poured, and Luz becomes **Bethel** | nobody |
| **20–22** | the vow: four conditions, one god, one house, one tenth | **Jacob** |

### 28:10–11 — a man walking north in the dark

> **10** Jacob went out from Beersheba, and went toward Haran.
> **11** He came to a certain place, and stayed there all night, because the sun had set. He took one
> of the stones of the place, and put it under his head, and lay down in that place to sleep.

**The itinerary is two place names and no companions.** [[Beersheba]] is where 26:23–33 settled the
household and where the previous pericope's blessing was given; [[Haran (city)]] is the town
[[Laban]] is actually found in, which is why 28:10 gives it where 28:5 gave the regional name
**Paddan Aram**. **The book uses the two registers side by side without reconciling them**, and this
verse is the seam: the sending is to Paddan Aram, the walking is toward Haran. See
[[Aram Naharaim]].

***"Because the sun had set."*** ***Kî-bāʾ haššemeš.*** **Genesis frames the entire twenty years with
the sun.** It sets on Jacob here as he leaves, and at 32:31, on the morning after the fight at the
ford, *"the sun rose on him as he passed over Peniel, and he limped."* **Out in the dark, back in the
light and lame** — and both nights end in a naming: *Bethel*, *Peniel*.

***"He came to a certain place."*** **The Hebrew is stranger than the English.** ***Wayyipgaʿ
bammāqôm*** — *he ran into the place*, with the definite article. ***Pāgaʿ*** is the verb of
unintended encounter; it is what happens when you meet someone on the road, and elsewhere in
Scripture it is what an avenger of blood does to a killer. **Jacob does not find a holy site. A holy
site happens to him.**

***Māqôm*** — **the word runs six times through twelve verses** (three times in this verse alone,
then 16, 17, 19) and it is the standing Hebrew term for a **sanctuary site**, the word Deuteronomy
uses for *"the place which Yahweh your God shall choose."* **The narrator is calling it a shrine
before Jacob knows it is one**, and the repetition is how he says so.

***"One of the stones of the place, and put it under his head."*** ***Mērʾašōtāyw*** — literally *at
his head-places*; whether it is a pillow, a headrest, or a barrier laid around the head against
animals is genuinely undecided. **The wiki notes only what the text needs**: it is an ordinary stone,
picked at random from a field, and it is the same stone that stands up at 28:18 and is promised as
God's house at 28:22. **Everything sacred in this passage started out as gravel.**

### 28:12 — the stairway, and which way the traffic goes

> **12** He dreamed. Behold, a stairway set upon the earth, and its top reached to heaven. Behold,
> the angels of God ascending and descending on it.

***Sullām.*** **The word occurs here and nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible** — a true hapax
legomenon — so its meaning is reconstructed rather than known. The usual derivation is from
***sālal***, *to heap up, to cast up a roadway*, which yields **a ramp or a stairway** rather than a
portable ladder; the WEB's *stairway* follows that, against the KJV's *ladder*, which came in through
the Vulgate's *scala*. **The picture most often set beside it is the monumental staircase of a
Mesopotamian ziggurat**, built so that a god could come down it to the city — which is worth knowing
mainly because of what this passage does with the idea.

***"Its top reached to heaven."*** ***Wərōʾšô maggîaʿ haššāmāymâ.*** **This is Babel's sentence.**
Genesis 11:4 has *"a tower whose top reaches to the sky"*, and it is the only other place in the book
where something's *head* is said to reach the sky. **Set the two scenes together and every term
inverts:**

| | **Babel, Genesis 11** | **Bethel, Genesis 28** |
|---|---|---|
| **who builds** | *"let's build ourselves"* — brick, tar, labour | nobody; it is simply *set upon the earth* |
| **top reaching heaven** | a tower, to make a name | a stairway, in a dream |
| **direction of traffic** | God has to **come down to see** (11:5) | angels **go up** first, then down |
| **the name** | *"let's make a name for ourselves"* | God gives the name of his fathers; Jacob names the place |
| **scattering** | feared — *"lest we be scattered"* | promised — *"you will spread abroad"* to four points |
| **what it is called** | *Babel*, popularly *the gate of god* | *"this is the gate of heaven"* |

**The wiki does not claim Genesis 28 was written as a reply to Genesis 11.** What it records is that
the two passages share an image, an idiom, and a name, and stand at opposite ends of every one of
them. See [[Babel and the Gate of Heaven]] and
[[A Name for Ourselves and a Great Name]].

***"Ascending and descending."*** ***ʿŌlîm wəyōrədîm*** — **up before down**, which is the wrong
order for visitors from heaven and has been noticed for as long as the text has been read. Either the
angels were already here, or the sequence is simply how you describe traffic on a stair seen from the
bottom. **Genesis does not explain the order and neither does this page.**

***"On it."*** ***Bô*** is masculine, and so is ***sullām*** — but so is Jacob. **The pronoun can
mean *on it* or *on him***, and the ambiguity is real in the Hebrew rather than manufactured; the same
is true of *"Yahweh stood above it"* (***niṣṣāb ʿālāyw***) in the next verse, which can equally be
*stood beside him*. The WEB takes both as the stairway. **The rabbinic tradition preserved the other
reading**, and John 1:51 will put the angels on a person. See
[[Angels Ascending and Descending - Genesis 28.12 in John 1.51]].

***Malʾăkê ʾĕlōhîm*** — **the angels of God, plural**, and this is not the singular
[[The Angel of Yahweh]] who has spoken to Hagar and stopped a knife on Moriah. **These say nothing,
do nothing, and are never mentioned again.** They are traffic; the point of them is that there is
traffic.

**And it is a dream.** Not the first in Genesis — [[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]] got one at 20:3 —
but **the first given to anyone inside the covenant line**, and the family becomes the dream family
after it: Jacob again at 31:10–13, Laban at 31:24, Joseph twice, a cupbearer, a baker, and a Pharaoh.
See [[Prophecy and Vision]].

### 28:13–15 — the promise, given to him and not about him

> **13** Behold, Yahweh stood above it, and said, “I am Yahweh, the God of Abraham your father, and
> the God of Isaac. The land whereon you lie, to you will I give it, and to your offspring.
> **14** Your offspring will be as the dust of the earth, and you will spread abroad to the west, and
> to the east, and to the north, and to the south. In you and in your offspring will all the families
> of the earth be blessed.
> **15** Behold, I am with you, and will keep you, wherever you go, and will bring you again into
> this land. For I will not leave you, until I have done that which I have spoken of to you.”

**Everything God has said about Jacob until now, he has said to somebody else.** The oracle of 25:23
went to [[Rebekah]] while Jacob was unborn. Isaac's blessing at 28:3–4 asked *ʾĒl Šadday* to act on
his behalf. **This is the first time in Jacob's life that God addresses him**, and the address is a
self-introduction.

***"I am Yahweh, the God of Abraham your father, and the God of Isaac."*** *Father* here is
***ʾāb*** in its wide sense — Abraham is his grandfather — and the formula is being assembled in
front of us. **It reaches its final form at Exodus 3:6**, *"the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and
the God of Jacob"*, which is the name God gives Moses at the bush and the sentence Jesus uses to
argue for the resurrection: *"God is not the God of the dead, but of the living"* (Matthew 22:32).
**The third name in that formula is being earned in this verse.** See
[[The Hope of Resurrection in the Old Testament]] and [[The Names of God]].

***"The land whereon you lie."*** **This is the smallest land grant in Genesis and the largest, at
once.** Abram was told to *"lift up your eyes, and look … northward and southward and eastward and
westward"* and then to *"walk through the land in its length and in its width"* (13:14–17). **Jacob is
horizontal, unconscious, and in the dark**, and the grant is measured by the patch of dirt under his
body. He owns, at this moment, a stone he did not buy in a field he does not know the name of. See
[[Lift Up Your Eyes - Two Ways of Seeing the Land]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].

***"As the dust of the earth."*** ***Kaʿăpar hāʾāreṣ*** — **the same image, in the same words, as
13:16 to Abram**, and not the stars of 15:5 or the sand of 22:17. **Genesis gives Jacob the dust
promise while he is lying in the dust.** See
[[Dust, Stars, and Sand - The Offspring Promises]].

***"You will spread abroad."*** ***Ûpāraṣtā*** — *to burst through, to break out* — with four
directions after it, and the sequence here (**west, east, north, south**) is not the sequence of
13:14 (north, south, east, west). **Babel's men feared exactly this and built to prevent it**, though
their word was ***pûṣ*** and this one is ***pāraṣ***: two different roots for one idea, and the wiki
notes the difference rather than smoothing it into a pun.

***"In you and in your offspring will all the families of the earth be blessed."*** **This is the
fifth and last statement of the Abrahamic blessing-of-the-nations in Genesis** — 12:3, 18:18, 22:18,
26:4, and here — and it is the only one after the first to use the first one's exact noun.
**12:3 and 28:14 both say *the families of the earth*, ***mišpəḥōt hāʾădāmâ***; 22:18 and 26:4 say
*the nations of the earth*.** The promise ends where it started, verbally, in the mouth of the
grandson. See
[[All the Families of the Earth - Genesis 12.3 in the New Testament]] and [[The Nations]].

***"Behold, I am with you."*** **Here the promise stops being inherited and starts being new.** Four
clauses, and **none of them was ever said to Abraham**:

- ***Hinnēh ʾānōkî ʿimmāk*** — *I am with you.* Said to Isaac twice (26:3, 26:24) and to Jacob again
  at 31:3; it becomes the standing formula of every commission in Scripture — Moses at the bush
  (Exodus 3:12), Joshua (Joshua 1:5), Gideon, Jeremiah — and the last sentence of Matthew's Gospel.
- ***Ûšmartîkā*** — *and I will keep you.* The verb of the priestly blessing (*"Yahweh bless you and
  keep you"*, Numbers 6:24) and of Psalm 121, which is a psalm about a road.
- ***Wahăšibōtîkā ʾel-hāʾădāmâ hazzōʾt*** — *and I will bring you back to this land.*
- ***Lōʾ ʾeʿĕzobkā*** — *I will not leave you.*

**They exist because of Jacob's specific problem.** Abraham was called *into* the land and told never
to send Isaac out of it; Isaac was forbidden to go down to Egypt in a famine (26:2). **Jacob is the
first bearer of the promise to leave the land carrying it**, and everything added at 28:15 is about
absence: presence *wherever you go*, and a return. See
[[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].

***"Until I have done that which I have spoken of to you."*** ***ʿAd ʾăšer*** has bothered readers who
hear an expiry date in it. **The ordinary reading is that the *until* fixes the guarantee to the
completion of the thing promised, not that the keeping stops when the promise lands** — the same
construction and the same worry attach to Psalm 110:1. **The wiki records the question and takes the
ordinary reading**, noting that Genesis itself has God repeat the whole promise to the same man at
35:9–12, long after the return.

**And there is no condition anywhere in the speech.** No *if*, no requirement, no reference to the
goatskins or the lie or the brother. **The most one-sided speech God makes in the Jacob cycle is made
over the least deserving man in it, on the night he is running away**, and Genesis does not soften it
with a reproach or explain why. See [[Call and Election]] and [[Favor and Grace]].

### 28:16–17 — waking up

> **16** Jacob awakened out of his sleep, and he said, “Surely Yahweh is in this place, and I didn’t
> know it.”
> **17** He was afraid, and said, “How dreadful is this place! This is none other than God’s house,
> and this is the gate of heaven.”

***"And I didn't know it."*** ***Wəʾānōkî lōʾ yādāʿtî.*** **The man whose whole career so far has run
on knowing more than the person in front of him says, first thing on waking, that he did not know.**
Chapter 27 turned on Isaac's ignorance and Jacob's information. **Here the ignorance is his**, and
the wiki notes the reversal as a fact of the wording rather than as a moral the text draws.

***"How dreadful is this place!"*** ***Mah-nôrāʾ hammāqôm hazzeh*** — ***nôrāʾ*** is *awe-inspiring,
fear-causing*, from ***yārēʾ***, and the WEB's *dreadful* is the old sense, *full of dread*, not
*bad*. **His first response to the promise is fear**, and Genesis states it as the sentence before he
says anything true about the place. See [[The Fear of God]].

***"God's house … the gate of heaven."*** ***Bêt ʾĕlōhîm*** — **he says the name before he gives it**,
because *Bethel* is *bêt-ʾēl*, house of God, and 28:19 will only make the naming official.
***Šaʿar haššāmayim***, *the gate of heaven*, is the other half, and it is the half that connects to
Babel: **Babylonian *bāb-ili* was understood as *gate of god*, and Genesis 11:9 has already refused
that etymology in favour of *bālal*, to confuse.** The gate the tower-builders claimed for a city on
a plain turns up over a man asleep on a ridge.

**And nothing is here except a stone.** No temple, no altar yet, no priest, no ark. **The gate of
heaven is a field in the dark**, and it is identified as such by a fugitive who found it by accident.

### 28:18–19 — the pillar, the oil, and the name

> **18** Jacob rose up early in the morning, and took the stone that he had put under his head, and
> set it up for a pillar, and poured oil on its top.
> **19** He called the name of that place Bethel, but the name of the city was Luz at the first.

***Maṣṣēbâ*** — **a standing stone**, from ***nāṣab***, *to stand*: the same root as
***niṣṣāb*** in 28:13, where Yahweh *stood*. **What stood over the stairway in the dream is answered
by a stone standing on the ground.** Jacob sets up four of them in this book (here, 31:45 at the
boundary heap, 35:14 back at Bethel, 35:20 over Rachel's grave), and he is the only patriarch who
does. **Abraham built altars; Jacob raises stones.**

**The law will later hate them.** *"Neither shall you set yourself up a pillar, which Yahweh your God
hates"* (Deuteronomy 16:22); *"you shall not rear up for yourselves … a standing stone"* (Leviticus
26:1); and the conquest commands are to smash the Canaanite ones (Exodus 23:24; 34:13). **And Moses
sets up twelve at Sinai (Exodus 24:4), and Joshua stands one up as a witness (Joshua 24:26–27), and
Isaiah 19:19 foresees one to Yahweh in the middle of Egypt.** The tension is genuine and this wiki
does not resolve it. See
[[The Pillar Jacob Raised and the Pillars the Law Forbids]].

***"Poured oil on its top."*** **This is the first oil in the Bible.** ***Šemen*** occurs here for the
first time in Scripture, and what it is used for is **anointing a stone** — before any priest, king,
tabernacle, or vessel is anointed, and by a man with no office. **Whether he was carrying oil or the
narrator is not interested in the question, the text does not say.** Genesis 31:13 will have God
himself call it that: *"I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar."* See
[[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]] and [[Priesthood]].

***"He called the name of that place Bethel."*** **The book has been calling it Bethel for sixteen
chapters.** Abram pitched *"having Bethel on the west, and Ai on the east"* at 12:8 and came back to
the same altar at 13:3–4 — **at a site whose name, this verse says, was still Luz.** It is the same
narrator's convenience as *Ur of the Chaldees*, and Genesis flags it here rather than hiding it. See
[[Bethel]].

**And Jacob names a place his grandfather had already worshipped at, without knowing it.** Genesis
does not say whether Abram's altar was still standing, whether Jacob recognised anything, or whether
the family had ever told him. **The book puts the two visits a hundred and sixty verses apart and
lets the reader notice.**

### 28:20–22 — the first vow in the Bible

> **20** Jacob vowed a vow, saying, “If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go,
> and will give me bread to eat, and clothing to put on,
> **21** so that I come again to my father’s house in peace, and Yahweh will be my God,
> **22** then this stone, which I have set up for a pillar, will be God’s house. Of all that you will
> give me I will surely give a tenth to you.”

***Wayyiddar Yaʿăqōb neder*** — **the first *neder* in Scripture**, and the cognate accusative
(*vowed a vow*) is the formula the law will legislate in Numbers 30 and Deuteronomy 23:21–23, and
that Ecclesiastes 5:4–5 will warn about. **The institution enters the Bible in the mouth of a man
negotiating.** See [[Oaths and Vows]].

**And the conditions are God's own promises, handed back with an *if* on them.** Set 28:15 beside
28:20–21:

| **God said, 28:15** | **Jacob says, 28:20–21** |
|---|---|
| *"I am with you"* | *"**If** God will be with me"* |
| *"and will keep you, wherever you go"* | *"and will keep me **in this way that I go**"* |
| *"and will bring you again into this land"* | *"so that I come again to **my father's house** in peace"* |
| — | *"and will give me **bread to eat, and clothing to put on**"* |
| *land, offspring as the dust, all the families of the earth* | **absent** |

**Three things about that table.** He **narrows** each promise — *wherever you go* becomes *this way
that I go*, and *this land* becomes *my father's house*. He **adds** the only two items in the
exchange that are about staying alive, **bread and clothing**, which 1 Timothy 6:8 will make the
measure of contentment: *"having food and clothing, we will be content with that."* And he **drops
everything that cannot be verified inside his own lifetime** — the land, the dust, the nations. **The
vow is the promise reduced to what a frightened man can check.**

**The wiki does not read this as unbelief and does not read it as faith.** What it records is that
Genesis puts an unconditional promise and a conditional reply on the same page, gives the promise no
rebuke and the vow no commendation, and then — at 35:1 — sends him back to keep it.

***"And Yahweh will be my God."*** **Where the sentence breaks is a genuine translation crux.** The
protasis is *if*, the apodosis is *then*, and Hebrew marks neither: **the WEB puts this clause inside
the condition** (*if … and Yahweh will be my God, then this stone …*), while the KJV and many others
begin the apodosis here (*then shall the LORD be my God*). **The theological weight is large and the
grammar is genuinely undetermined**: on one reading Jacob makes Yahweh's being his God a term he is
requiring; on the other, it is the first thing he promises. **The wiki records both and adopts
neither**, noting only that on either reading it is **the first time Jacob says *my* God.** At 27:20,
lying to his father, he had said *"Yahweh **your** God gave me success"* — **the pronoun that kept him
outside the relationship he was borrowing.** See [[Jacob]] and [[Deception]].

***"This stone … will be God's house."*** ***Bêt ʾĕlōhîm*** again, and now it is a promise rather
than an exclamation. **The pillow, the pillar, and the house of God are one object**, tracked across
twelve verses from *one of the stones of the place*.

***"I will surely give a tenth to you."*** ***ʿAśśēr ʾăʿaśrennû lāk*** — infinitive absolute, *tithing
I will tithe it.* **This is the Bible's second tithe and its second pre-Mosaic one**, and it differs
from Abram's at every point: Abram gave a tenth of war spoil to a priest, unprompted, after a
victory; **Jacob promises a tenth of everything he has not got yet, to God directly, with no priest
and no sanctuary, as the back half of a bargain.** Genesis never records him paying it. See
[[The Tithe Before the Law]] and [[Tithing]].

### What happens to the vow

**Genesis closes the loop three times and the wiki notes it here because the passage is unfinished
without it.**

- **31:13** — the angel of God, in a dream in Paddan Aram: *"I am the God of Bethel, where you
  anointed a pillar, where you vowed a vow to me. Now arise, get out from this land."* **God
  identifies himself by Jacob's own stone**, and uses the vow as the ground of the command to leave.
- **35:1** — *"Arise, go up to Bethel, and live there. Make there an altar to God, who appeared to
  you when you fled from the face of Esau your brother."* **He is sent back**, and what he does first
  is bury the household's foreign gods under an oak (35:2–4) — which is Genesis telling us, twenty
  years late, what *"Yahweh will be my God"* had left open.
- **35:14–15** — *"Jacob set up a pillar in the place where he spoke with him … He poured out a drink
  offering on it, and poured oil on it."* **The same act, done again on purpose**, with a libation
  added and no condition attached.

**And at the end of his life he says it plainly** — *"the God who has fed me all my life long to this
day, the angel who has redeemed me from all evil"* (48:15–16). **Bread was the thing he asked for
conditionally at Bethel and the thing he names first when he is dying.**

### What the pericope is doing

**It converts an inheritance into a relationship.**

Everything Jacob had held until this night was second-hand: a birthright bought from his brother, a
blessing taken off his father in the dark, a covenant handed on by a man reciting his own father's
formulas. **Nothing had come to him from God.** At Bethel it does, in his own name, unasked, and with
the one clause the patriarchs before him never needed — *I am with you wherever you go.*

**And it does it without cleaning him up first.** There is no confession in this passage, no altar of
repentance, no acknowledgement that anything happened in chapter 27. **The promise lands on the man
as he is, and his response is to negotiate.** Genesis lets both stand: the God who commits to
everything and the man who commits to a tenth, if.

**What it also does is plant a bomb under the northern kingdom.** The place is real, the appearance is
real, the name is real — and **eight hundred years later Jeroboam will put a golden calf in the house
of God on the strength of exactly that** (1 Kings 12:28–29), and Amos will be thrown out of the
sanctuary for saying so, and Hosea will rename the town **house of trouble.** **Genesis 28 is the
warrant they all appeal to**, and nothing in Genesis 28 authorises any of it. See
[[Bethel from Jacob's Pillar to Jeroboam's Calf]].

## People

- [[Jacob]] — **spoken to by God for the first time in his life.** He walks out of Beersheba alone,
  stops because it is dark, and is met by a promise he did not seek and is not asked to earn. His
  first words on waking are a confession of ignorance — *"I didn't know it"* — from the man whose
  whole method has been knowing more than the person in front of him; his second response is fear;
  and his third is a vow that hands God's own promises back with an *if* on them, narrows each one,
  adds bread and clothing, and drops the land and the nations. **He says *my God* for the first time
  here**, against the *your God* he used to his father while lying at 27:20. He raises the first of
  his four pillars, pours the first oil in the Bible, and makes the first vow in it.
- [[God]] — **unconditional, and uninterested in the last chapter.** He introduces himself by the
  names of Abraham and Isaac, grants the ground Jacob is lying on, restates the dust and the families
  of the earth in the exact words of 12:3, and then adds what neither patriarch before him
  received — *I am with you*, *I will keep you*, *I will bring you back*, *I will not leave you*.
  **No condition, no rebuke, no reference to the deception.** He stands *above it* — or *beside him*;
  the Hebrew allows both — and he says nothing about the vow that answers him until 31:13, when he
  identifies himself by it.
- [[Abraham]] — named first in the self-introduction, and the source of everything in 28:13–14 except
  the presence clauses. **He worshipped at this site and Jacob does not know it** (12:8; 13:3–4). The
  formula being assembled over his grandson's head reaches its final form at the burning bush.
- [[Isaac]] — named second, and alive; his own *"I am with you"* from 26:3 and 26:24 is the phrase
  God now gives his son. **His blessing at 28:3–4 asked *ʾĒl Šadday* to do exactly this**, and four
  verses later God does it directly.
- [[The Angel of Yahweh]] — **not this.** The *malʾăkê ʾĕlōhîm* of 28:12 are plural, silent, and
  anonymous, and the singular figure who spoke to Hagar and stopped Abraham's knife does not appear.
  The two are kept distinct here deliberately; 31:11–13 will have *the angel of God* speak in a dream
  and identify himself as *the God of Bethel*.
- [[Esau]] — **named nowhere in these verses**, and the reason for every one of them. Genesis 35:7
  supplies the gloss this passage withholds: God appeared to Jacob *"when he fled from the face of
  his brother."*

## Places

- [[Bethel]] — **named in this passage and by this man**, though the book has used the name since
  12:8. *Bêt ʾĕlōhîm*, the house of God, and *the gate of heaven*: a field with one stone in it. Its
  earlier name was **Luz**, and its later career runs from Abram's altar through Jacob's pillar to
  Jeroboam's calf and Josiah's bonfire.
- [[Beersheba]] — the point of departure, and the household [[Isaac]] settled at 26:23–33. Jacob will
  not see it again; his father's house, when he returns, is at Mamre (35:27).
- [[Haran (city)]] — the stated destination, where [[Laban]] is actually found, against 28:5's
  regional **Paddan Aram**. **The two registers sit in adjacent verses.**
- [[Aram Naharaim]] — the region under its other names; the country Jacob is walking to and will not
  leave for twenty years.
- [[Canaan]] — *"this land"* of 28:15, promised to a man on his way out of it, with a return attached.

## Themes

- [[Covenant]] — the Abrahamic promise transmitted to the third generation **directly**, without a
  human intermediary, and enlarged by the presence clauses.
- [[Call and Election]] — the promise arrives on a deceiver in flight, with no condition and no
  reproach; the passage is the wiki's clearest instance of election running ahead of character.
- [[Favor and Grace]] — what 28:13–15 is, structurally: an entirely one-sided speech at the point
  where the recipient has least claim.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — *"the land whereon you lie"*: the grant narrowed to a body's width and
  guaranteed to a man leaving the country.
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — the first dream inside the covenant line, and the start of the family
  Genesis will characterise by dreams.
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — a dream instead of a vision, an unrecognised holy place, and *"I didn't
  know it"*; the reverse of Abram at 13:14, who was told to look.
- [[The Fear of God]] — ***nôrāʾ***: his first reaction to the promise is dread, before anything else
  he says is true.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — the first *neder* in Scripture, conditional, and the institution the Torah
  will regulate and Ecclesiastes will warn about.
- [[Tithing]] — the second pre-Mosaic tenth, promised rather than paid, with no priest to receive it.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — a pillar and oil instead of an altar and a victim; **nothing is killed
  in this passage.**
- [[Priesthood]] — the first anointing in the Bible, performed on a stone by a man with no office.
- [[The Names of God]] — *Bêt-ʾĒl*, *ʾĕlōhîm*, and *Yahweh*, all three in twelve verses, plus the
  formula *the God of Abraham … and the God of Isaac* that Exodus 3 completes.
- [[The Nations]] — *"all the families of the earth"*, in the wording of 12:3, for the last time in
  Genesis.
- [[Remembrance]] — a stone set up so that a night has a monument, and a name that outlives the
  event by a thousand years.
- [[Blessing]] — received here from God, four verses after receiving it from Isaac, and it is the
  same blessing with more in it.

## Notes

- **On the boundary, which the previous pass drew.** The plan's original box read *Jacob's Ladder at
  Bethel — Genesis 27:46–28:22*; the previous ingest split it at 28:9/10 and left this half unchecked
  as the next in the queue. **The split was used as drawn and needed no revision**: 28:10 opens with a
  fresh itinerary formula and 28:22 ends on the vow, with 29:1 restarting on *"Then Jacob went on his
  journey"*. Twelve verses, one night, one place.
- **On the title.** *Jacob's Ladder at Bethel* was kept from the plan, though the page argues
  throughout that ***sullām*** is more probably a **stairway or ramp** than a ladder and the WEB
  translates it so. **The traditional English title was retained because it is the name the episode
  has in every index a reader would come from**, and because the alternative — *The Stairway at
  Bethel* — buys precision at the cost of findability. The philological point is made in the body
  rather than in the filename. No other book has a passage page by this title.
- **On *sullām* as a hapax.** Recorded as a distribution fact: the noun occurs once in the Hebrew
  Bible. The derivation from ***sālal*** is the usual one and is given as *usual*, not as settled.
- **On the Babel parallel, which is new on this page.** The claim made is narrow and checkable —
  Genesis 11:4 and 28:12 are the only two places in the book where something's *top* is said to reach
  the sky, and Jacob's *"gate of heaven"* stands beside the popular etymology of *Babel* that 11:9
  itself declines. **The wiki does not claim Genesis 28 was composed as a reply to Genesis 11**, and
  the page says so. The scattering verbs are noted as **different roots** (*pûṣ* at 11:4, *pāraṣ* at
  28:14) rather than made into a pun.
- **On the two grammatical ambiguities, both recorded and neither resolved.** ***Bô*** at 28:12
  (*on it* / *on him*) and ***ʿālāyw*** at 28:13 (*above it* / *beside him*) are genuine in the
  Hebrew; the WEB takes both as the stairway, the rabbinic tradition preserved the other reading, and
  John 1:51 puts the angels on a person. **Both readings are on the page.**
- **On where the vow's apodosis begins.** *"And Yahweh will be my God"* (28:21b) sits inside the
  condition in the WEB and begins the promise in the KJV and others. **The Hebrew does not mark it**,
  the theological difference is large, and **the wiki adopts neither**, recording only what is true on
  both readings: it is the first time Jacob says *my God*.
- **On *ʿad ʾăšer* at 28:15.** The *until* is read in the ordinary way — the guarantee is fixed to the
  completion of the promise, not terminated by it — and the reading is stated as a choice rather than
  smuggled in, with 35:9–12 noted as Genesis's own answer.
- **On oil.** 28:18 is recorded as **the first occurrence of *šemen* in Scripture**, verified against
  `raw/bible/Genesis.md` rather than asserted from memory. Nothing theological is built on it beyond
  the observation that the Bible's first anointing is of a stone, by a layman.
- **On what was created.** **Four new connection pages**, all of them threads this passage opens and
  no existing page carried:
  [[Angels Ascending and Descending - Genesis 28.12 in John 1.51]],
  [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]],
  [[The Pillar Jacob Raised and the Pillars the Law Forbids]],
  [[Bethel from Jacob's Pillar to Jeroboam's Calf]], and
  [[Babel and the Gate of Heaven]] — five, counting the Babel page, which was made separately from
  the Jeroboam one because they run in opposite directions: one backwards into chapter 11, one
  forwards into Kings.
- **On what was deliberately not created.** **No page for *Luz***, which is Bethel's earlier name and
  is carried in that page's `also_known_as`, on the same decision recorded for **Paddan Aram** at
  [[A Wife for Isaac]]. **No page for the angels of 28:12** — they are plural, silent, and
  unindividuated, and [[The Angel of Yahweh]] now carries the distinction between them and the
  singular figure. **No new theme page**; *presence* and *dreams* were considered and both are
  already covered, by [[Covenant]] and [[Walking with God]] on the one hand and
  [[Prophecy and Vision]] on the other.
- **What was left for later pericopes.** The angel's citation of the vow (31:11–13); the second night

  pillar (35:1–15); and Jacob's deathbed verdict on the bread (48:15–16). All are referenced and none
  is developed. **No dangling links were left.**
