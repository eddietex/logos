# The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 50:1–26

## Summary

**The book that opens with God creating the heavens and the earth ends with a man in a box in
Egypt — and the last thing anyone says in it is that they are leaving.**

Genesis 50 is **the only chapter in the book with two funerals in it**, and it spends its
twenty-six verses getting one body out of Egypt and failing to get the other one out. **50:1–14 is
the burial of [[Jacob]]** — embalming, seventy days of Egyptian weeping, a negotiated leave of
absence, a state cortege with chariots, seven days of mourning at a threshing floor east of the
river, the cave at [[Machpelah]], and the return. **50:15–21 is the last conversation between
[[Joseph]] and his brothers**, who wait until their father is dead to ask whether they are
forgiven. **50:22–26 is Joseph's old age and death**, a hundred and ten years, three generations
of great-grandchildren, an oath about his bones, and a coffin.

**The chapter's organising verb is *ʿālâ*, *to go up*.** It appears seven times in twenty-six
verses — *let me go up and bury my father* (5), *go up and bury* (6), *Joseph went up* (7),
*there went up with him* (9), *all that went up with him* (14), *bring you up out of this land*
(24), *carry up my bones* (25). **The first five are about a corpse and the last two are about a
nation**, and nothing in between explains how one becomes the other. See
[[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].

**The land is entered once in this chapter and only to dig a grave.** Sixty-six verses earlier,
Abram was promised the country in four directions (13:14–17); at the end of the book his family
owns a cave, a field, and a strip outside [[Shechem (city)]], **and lives four hundred miles
away**. **Genesis closes with the promise entirely unfulfilled and entirely unrevoked**, which is
the arrangement it has run on since chapter 12. See [[Land and Sojourning]] and [[Covenant]].

**The last word of the book in Hebrew is *bəmiṣrāyim* — *in Egypt*.**

## Key verses

### The body

> **1** Joseph fell on his father's face, wept on him, and kissed him. **2** Joseph commanded his
> servants, the physicians, to embalm his father; and the physicians embalmed Israel. **3** Forty
> days were fulfilled for him, for that is how many the days it takes to embalm. The Egyptians wept
> for him for seventy days.

**Jacob has already died** — 49:33 ended the previous pericope with him drawing his feet into the
bed and being *"gathered to his people"*. **Chapter 50 opens on a corpse**, and its first sentence
is a promise being kept. At 46:4, at [[Beersheba]], on the night Jacob was told it was safe to go
down, God said *"I will also surely bring you up again, and Joseph will close your eyes"*
(in [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]]). **The closing of the eyes is not narrated; the falling on
the face is.** See [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].

***"The physicians embalmed Israel."*** ***Ḥānaṭ***, **to embalm, occurs three times in the whole
Hebrew Bible and all three are in this chapter** — twice here and once of Joseph at 50:26. **There
are exactly two mummies in Scripture and they are the last two men in Genesis.** Filed as
[[Embalmed and Put in a Coffin - The Only Two Mummies in Scripture]].

**Note which name the verse uses.** Joseph commands the embalming of ***his father***; what the
physicians embalm is ***Israel***. **The covenant name is the one that goes under the Egyptian
knife**, and the narrator, who has alternated *Jacob* and *Israel* deliberately for twenty chapters,
puts them in the same sentence here. See
[[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

***Forty days … seventy days.*** **Two numbers, two peoples, and the text separates them.** Forty
is the embalming; seventy is *"the Egyptians wept for him"* — **and seventy days of national
mourning is a king's due.** **A patriarch buried out of Egypt receives Egyptian royal honours for a
death Egypt has no stake in.** Compare the thirty days Israel will mourn Aaron (Numbers 20:29) and
Moses (Deuteronomy 34:8): **Jacob is wept over more than twice as long as the man who brings the
nation out.** See [[Burial and the Grave]].

### Asking a king for three weeks off

> **4** When the days of weeping for him were past, Joseph spoke to the house of Pharaoh, saying,
> "If now I have found favor in your eyes, please speak in the ears of Pharaoh, saying, **5** 'My
> father made me swear, saying, "Behold, I am dying. Bury me in my grave which I have dug for myself
> in the land of Canaan." Now therefore, please let me go up and bury my father, and I will come
> again.'"

**The second man in Egypt cannot get an audience with the first.** He speaks **to the house of
Pharaoh**, asks them to **speak in the ears of Pharaoh**, and opens with ***"if now I have found
favor in your eyes"*** — ***ḥēn***, **the word that has tracked him since [[Potiphar]]'s house**
(39:4, 21) **and the word the brothers used on him when they had no standing at all** (47:25).
**The man who holds the signet ring (41:42) petitions through intermediaries.**

**Genesis gives no reason and two are available.** A mourner in Egypt would be unshaven and
ritually unfit for the royal presence — **and the book has already shaved Joseph once to get him
in front of this king** (41:14). Or the request is delicate on its own terms: **the vizier who has
just bought the country for the crown** (47:20, in [[Joseph and the Famine]]) **is asking leave to
take the entire Egyptian administration abroad to bury a foreigner.** The wiki notes both and
prefers neither.

***"I will come again."*** **He says it before he is asked.** The whole of Exodus is latent in that
clause, and Joseph is the one who volunteers it.

### The grave Jacob dug for himself

***"Bury me in my grave which I have dug for myself in the land of Canaan"*** (50:5). **This is not
what Jacob said**, and the wiki flags the difference rather than smoothing it.

| where | what Jacob is recorded saying |
|---|---|
| **47:29–31**, [[Joseph and the Famine]] | *"don't bury me in Egypt, but … lie with my fathers"* — a hand under the thigh, and an oath |
| **49:29–32**, [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]] | *"bury me with my fathers in the cave that is in the field of Ephron the Hittite … which Abraham bought"* |
| **50:5**, Joseph quoting him | *"my grave **which I have dug for myself**"* |

***Kārîtî*** **is the verb.** In its ordinary sense it is ***I dug***, **and then Genesis has Jacob
claiming as his own excavation a cave [[Abraham]] bought from [[Ephron the Hittite]] and which
already holds five bodies** (49:31). **In a second and well-attested sense the same root means
*to acquire by purchase*** — the sense it plainly carries at Hosea 3:2, where the prophet *kārâ*'s
a woman for fifteen pieces of silver — **and on that reading the clause says exactly what 49:30 and
23:17–20 say.**

**A third reading is that the difference is Joseph's.** **A private grave a man prepared for
himself is a family matter; a purchased holding in Canaan, entered in the record as a permanent
possession, is a land claim** — and a vizier asking a foreign king for leave has reason to phrase
it the first way. **Genesis, which reports both versions eleven verses apart, does not adjudicate,
and neither does this page.** See [[Deception]] and [[Machpelah]].

### The cortege

> **6** Pharaoh said, "Go up, and bury your father, just like he made you swear." **7** Joseph went
> up to bury his father; and with him went up all the servants of Pharaoh, the elders of his house,
> all the elders of the land of Egypt, **8** All the house of Joseph, his brothers, and his father's
> house. Only their little ones, their flocks, and their herds, they left in the land of Goshen.
> **9** There went up with him both chariots and horsemen. It was a very great company.

***"Just like he made you swear."*** **Pharaoh's whole answer is five words in Hebrew and the
operative one is the oath.** **A king who has just been told his vizier swore something to a dead
foreigner does not argue with it** — and the oath is the same instrument that got [[Abraham]]'s
servant to Aram (24:2–9) and will get Joseph's own bones out of Egypt eighteen verses later.
**Genesis treats a sworn word as the one thing a king does not overrule.** See
[[Oaths and Vows]] and [[Put Your Hand Under My Thigh]].

***"Chariots and horsemen."*** **The first chariots in Scripture were the one Joseph rode into
office in** (41:43) **and the one he went up to meet his father in** (46:29). **These are the
third**, and the next time Egyptian chariots go up out of Egypt after a company of Israelites
they are not an escort (Exodus 14:6–9). ***Maḥăneh kābēd məʾōd***, ***a very heavy camp***, **is
military vocabulary**, the same noun Jacob divided in fear of Esau (32:7).

***"Only their little ones, their flocks, and their herds, they left in the land of Goshen."***
**This is the arrangement Moses will refuse.** **Pharaoh's third offer in Exodus 10:8–11 is that
the men may go and worship and the little ones stay; his fourth, at 10:24, is that everyone may go
and the flocks and herds stay.** **Moses answers *"we will go with our young and with our old …
with our flocks and with our herds"* and will not move** — **because he knows what Genesis 50:8
is: sureties.** **Here Israel leaves its children in Egypt voluntarily and comes back, and the
verse is the only reason Pharaoh lets anyone go at all.** Filed as
[[Our Little Ones Stayed in Goshen - Genesis 50.8 and the Bargaining of Exodus 10]].

### A threshing floor east of the river

> **10** They came to the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan, and there they
> lamented with a very great and severe lamentation. He mourned for his father seven days.
> **11** When the inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites, saw the mourning in the floor of Atad,
> they said, "This is a grievous mourning by the Egyptians." Therefore its name was called Abel
> Mizraim, which is beyond the Jordan.

***ʾĀṭād*** **is the bramble** — **the thornbush that is offered the kingship in Jotham's fable and
accepts it** (Judges 9:14–15). **Genesis stages the mourning for Israel on a threshing floor named
for a thorn**, which is the plant the ground was cursed to grow at 3:18. See [[Curse]].

***Seven days.*** **This is the Bible's first week of mourning**, and it is the length that becomes
standard — the men of Jabesh Gilead fast seven days for Saul (1 Samuel 31:13), and the rabbinic
*shivah* is named for the number. **Genesis 50 therefore records the origin of the Jewish mourning
week inside an Egyptian state funeral.**

***"Beyond the Jordan"*** — ***bəʿēber hayyardēn*** — **is the chapter's geographical problem, and
the narrator says it twice** (50:10, 11) **as though it mattered.** **The phrase ordinarily means
the east bank, spoken by someone standing on the west.** **But the road from Egypt to [[Hebron]]
does not cross the Jordan, in either direction, by any route.** **A cortege that reaches Machpelah
by way of Transjordan has gone around three sides of a square.**

**The wiki records the puzzle and does not resolve it.** Three readings are live: that the phrase
is being used loosely of the Jordan valley generally; that Atad was a west-bank site and *ʿēber*
is written from an eastern standpoint; **or that the detour is deliberate and theological — that
Jacob's body enters the land on the road Moses will not be allowed to take and Joshua will, from
the east, across the river.** **The third is the most interesting and the least provable.**
See [[The Jordan]].

***"This is a grievous mourning by the Egyptians."*** **The Canaanites get it exactly wrong and
name the place for their mistake.** ***ʾĒbel Miṣrayim***, ***mourning of Egypt***, **on a pun with
*ʾābēl*, *meadow*** — the shape of a dozen place names west of the river. **The people who live in
the land watch the funeral of the man to whom the land was promised, and what they see is
Egyptians.** See [[Names and Naming]] and [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

### The sixth grave

> **12** His sons did to him just as he commanded them, **13** for his sons carried him into the
> land of Canaan, and buried him in the cave of the field of Machpelah, which Abraham bought with
> the field, as a possession for a burial site, from Ephron the Hittite, near Mamre.

***"His sons did to him just as he commanded them … for his sons carried him."*** **Twice in two
verses, and in both the subject is the twelve.** **The Egyptian state got him to the river; his
sons carry him the rest of the way.** **It is the only act in Genesis the twelve brothers perform
together and get right** — the last time they acted in concert they were stripping a coat off the
eleventh of them (37:23, in [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]) — **and the narrator does not point it
out.** See [[Brotherhood]].

**Verse 13 is a quotation of a deed.** *"The cave of the field of Machpelah, which Abraham bought
with the field, as a possession for a burial site, from Ephron the Hittite, near [[Mamre (place)]]"*
**reproduces the conveyancing language of 23:17–20 almost word for word**, seventy-seven years and
twenty-seven chapters later, in [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]]. **Genesis states the
title one last time because the cave is the only thing in Canaan the family owns**, and because
**burying Jacob in it is what makes the purchase mean anything.**

**Machpelah now holds six**: [[Sarah]], [[Abraham]], [[Isaac]], [[Rebekah]], [[Leah]], and Jacob.
**[[Rachel]] is not among them**, and Jacob explained why at 48:7 without being asked. Filed as
[[Buried at Machpelah - The Six Graves of Genesis]].

***"Joseph returned into Egypt — he, and his brothers, and all that went up with him"*** (50:14).
**The verse goes out of its way to say that everybody came back.** **The family stands inside the
promised land, in the one plot it legally owns, and leaves.**

## The brothers, seventeen years later

> **15** When Joseph's brothers saw that their father was dead, they said, "It may be that Joseph
> will hate us, and will fully pay us back for all the evil which we did to him."

***Lû yiśṭəmēnû*** — ***should Joseph hate us***. **The verb is *śāṭam*, to bear a grudge, and
Genesis has used it once before**: *"Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing"* (27:41, in
[[The Stolen Blessing]]) — **a grudge that waited for exactly this moment**, *"the days of mourning
for my father are at hand; then I will kill my brother Jacob."* **The brothers are not imagining
the danger; they are quoting the precedent.** See [[Vengeance]] and [[Retribution]].

***"Will fully pay us back"*** — ***wəhāšēb yāšîb***, **infinitive absolute**, *returning he will
return*, **the same emphatic construction Joseph himself will use of God nine verses later**
(*pāqōd yipqōd*, 50:24). **What they expect to be repaid, he says God will visit.**

**Seventeen years have passed.** Jacob came down at 47:9 and lived seventeen years in Egypt (47:28)
— **the same number of years Joseph was in his father's house before they sold him** (37:2).
**For seventeen years these men have eaten food Joseph gave them** (47:12) **and believed the whole
time that it was for Jacob's sake.** **The reconciliation scene of chapter 45 did not take.** See
[[Guilt and Conscience]].

### A message with a dead man's name on it

> **16** They sent a message to Joseph, saying, "Your father commanded before he died, saying,
> **17** 'You shall tell Joseph, "Now please forgive the disobedience of your brothers, and their
> sin, because they did evil to you."'" Now, please forgive the disobedience of the servants of the
> God of your father." Joseph wept when they spoke to him.

**They send rather than come**, and **what they send is a quotation from a man who cannot be
asked.**

**Genesis records nothing of the sort.** **Jacob's last instructions run for two full chapters** —
the adoption of [[Ephraim]] and [[Manasseh]] and the crossed hands (48), twelve sayings and a
burial order (49) — **and not one clause of it concerns Joseph's brothers or their forgiveness.**
**Either Jacob said it off-page, or they made it up.** **The book that has just given the old man
more deathbed dialogue than anyone else in it does not corroborate them**, and the wiki leaves the
gap open rather than filling it in either direction. See
[[Truly We Are Guilty - The Brothers' Confession and the Gap in Genesis 37]].

**If it is invented, the symmetry is exact and brutal.** **These are the men who took a coat,
dipped it in goat's blood, and brought it to their father with a question they knew the answer to**
(37:31–33). **Thirty-odd years later they carry a message in the opposite direction — from their
father, to the brother — and it is the same instrument.** See [[Deception]].

***"Please forgive."*** ***Śāʾ nāʾ*** — **literally *lift, please*** — ***pešaʿ*** (**rebellion,
the strongest of the Hebrew sin-words**) **and *ḥaṭṭāʾt* (sin).** **This is the first time in the
Bible that one human being asks another for forgiveness.** **The idiom — *to lift* a
transgression — is the one the Torah will give God at Exodus 34:7**, *"forgiving iniquity,
disobedience, and sin"*, **three nouns of which two are in this verse.** See [[Forgiveness]].

***"The servants of the God of your father."*** **They do not appeal as brothers.** **They appeal
on the covenant, in the third person, standing as far outside the family as the grammar allows** —
**and they call themselves *servants*, which is the word they will say out loud in the next
verse.**

***"Joseph wept when they spoke to him."*** **The seventh and last time Joseph weeps in Genesis**
(42:24; 43:30; 45:2, 14–15; 46:29; 50:1; 50:17). **The narrator does not say why**, and the obvious
reading is the saddest one available: **he weeps because after seventeen years they are still
afraid of him, and because they think forgiveness is a thing you have to get a dead man to
request.**

### The dream, finally

> **18** His brothers also went and fell down before his face; and they said, "Behold, we are your
> servants."

**Genesis 37:7 has been waiting thirteen chapters for this sentence.** *"Behold, your sheaves came
around, and bowed down to my sheaf."* **The brothers have bowed three times already** (42:6; 43:26,
28) — **but always as strangers, always with grain in view, and never all eleven at once.** **Here
it is all of them, by name, in the book's last scene, and they say the word:** ***ʿăbādîm***,
***servants***. Filed at [[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]].

**And it is the wrong fulfilment, and Joseph refuses it.** **[[Judah]] offered exactly this at
44:16** — *"behold, we are my lord's servants"* — **and was told no** (44:17, in
[[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]]). **They offer it again with nothing at stake but their lives,
and are told no again.** **The dream comes true in the only form Joseph will not accept.** See
[[Slavery and Servitude]].

### *"Am I in the place of God?"*

> **19** Joseph said to them, "Don't be afraid, for am I in the place of God? **20** As for you, you
> meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring to pass, as it is today, to save many
> people alive. **21** Now therefore don't be afraid. I will nourish you and your little ones." He
> comforted them, and spoke kindly to them.

***Hă·taḥat ʾĕlōhîm ʾānî.*** **His father said the identical sentence to [[Rachel]] at 30:2** —
*"Am I in God's place, who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?"* — **and it was a
refusal.** **Jacob used the idiom to get out of helping a desperate woman; Joseph uses it to get
out of judging desperate men.** **The same four words, forty years and a whole moral universe
apart.** Filed as [[Am I in God's Place - Jacob at 30.2 and Joseph at 50.19]].

***"Don't be afraid"*** — ***ʾal-tîrāʾû*** — **twice, at 50:19 and 50:21.** **In Genesis this is
God's line**: to Abram at 15:1, to Hagar's son at 21:17, to Isaac at 26:24, to Jacob at 46:3.
**Joseph is the first human being in the book to say it to anybody**, and he says it in the same
breath as denying that he stands where God stands. See [[The Fear of God]].

### *You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good*

***Ḥāšab* is the verb in both clauses and it is the same word twice**: *ʾattem ḥăšabtem ʿālay
rāʿâ, ʾĕlōhîm ḥăšābāh ləṭōbâ*. **Not *you did evil and God repaired it* — one act, reckoned two
ways, by two parties, at the same time.** **Genesis does not say the sale was good, and does not
say the brothers were instruments; it says their intention and God's intention were both real and
attached to the same event**, and leaves the two standing.

***Ḥāšab*** **is also the verb of 15:6** — *"he believed in Yahweh, who **credited** it to him for
righteousness"* — **the most theologically loaded word in the Abraham cycle, in
[[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]. The verb that reckons righteousness to a man is the verb that
reckons intention here.**

***Ləhaḥăyōt ʿam-rāb***, ***to save many people alive***. **The hiphil of *ḥāyâ*, to keep alive,
is a rare and pointed choice: its next appearance of any weight is Exodus 1:17–18, where the
Hebrew midwives *"saved the boy babies alive"* against a king's order.** **The phrase that
summarises Joseph's whole career is the phrase Exodus gives to two women who disobey Pharaoh.**

**This is the sentence the New Testament reaches for whenever it has to say how a crime and a
purpose occupy the same event** — Acts 2:23, Acts 3:13–15, Romans 8:28 — **and it is the fullest
statement of providence in the Hebrew Bible.** Filed as
[[You Meant Evil Against Me - The Two Intentions at Genesis 50.20]]. See [[Providence]].

**It is also the second time he has answered the question**, and the answers are not identical.
**At 45:5–8, five days after the disclosure, he said *"God sent me before you to preserve life"*
and the brothers' act nearly vanished from the sentence** (see
[[God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale]]). **At 50:20, seventeen years later,
with their father dead and nothing left to protect, he names it: *you meant evil*.** **The second
forgiveness is the harder and the more complete, because it forgives something he is finally
willing to call by its name.**

***"I will nourish you and your little ones."*** ***Kalkēl*** — **the verb from 45:11 and 47:12**,
*"there I will nourish you"*. **The last promise Joseph makes his brothers is the first promise he
made them, repeated verbatim on the day the reason for it died.**

***"He comforted them, and spoke kindly to them"*** — ***wayḏabbēr ʿal-libbām***, **literally
*spoke upon their heart*.** **Genesis has used the idiom once, of [[Shechem (son of Hamor)]] to
[[Dinah]] after he had raped her** (34:3, in [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]]) — **its one
previous use in the book is an abuser's**. **Its future is better**: it is what Boaz does for Ruth
(Ruth 2:13) and what God commands be done to Jerusalem at Isaiah 40:2. See
[[Compassion and Mercy]].

## A hundred and ten years

> **22** Joseph lived in Egypt, he, and his father's house. Joseph lived one hundred ten years.
> **23** Joseph saw Ephraim's children to the third generation. The children also of Machir, the son
> of Manasseh, were born on Joseph's knees.

**A hundred and ten is the Egyptian ideal lifespan.** **It is the number Egyptian wisdom texts use
for a perfectly completed life, the way Hebrew uses seventy** (Psalm 90:10) — **and Genesis gives
it to the one patriarch who lived and died as an Egyptian official.** **He is also the shortest-lived
of them**: [[Abraham]] 175, [[Isaac]] 180, [[Jacob]] 147, Joseph 110. **The book's lifespans have
been falling since 930** (5:5) **and they stop falling here.**

***"Ephraim's children to the third generation."*** **Seeing your descendants to the third or
fourth generation is the Bible's standard picture of a finished life** — Job gets four (Job 42:16),
Psalm 128:6 makes it the blessing of the man who fears Yahweh. **Joseph gets it in Egypt.**

***"The children also of [[Machir]], the son of [[Manasseh]], were born on Joseph's knees."***
***ʿal-birkê yōsēp.*** **The adoption idiom, and Genesis uses it exactly twice** — here, and at
30:3, where [[Rachel]] gives [[Bilhah]] to Jacob *"that she may bear on my knees"*
(in [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]]). **The book that opened its Jacob cycle with a barren woman
adopting a slave's child closes it with a great-grandfather taking a great-grandson onto his lap.**
See [[Adoption]].

**Machir is the last person named in Genesis**, and what he becomes is not incidental: **his clan
takes Gilead by force** (Numbers 32:39–40) **and holds the Transjordan** (Joshua 17:1). **The final
name in a book about a promised land belongs to the family that will settle outside it.**

## *God will surely visit you*

> **24** Joseph said to his brothers, "I am dying, but God will surely visit you, and bring you up
> out of this land to the land which he swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob." **25** Joseph
> took an oath from the children of Israel, saying, "God will surely visit you, and you shall carry
> up my bones from here." **26** So Joseph died, being one hundred ten years old, and they embalmed
> him, and he was put in a coffin in Egypt.

***Pāqōd yipqōd ʾĕlōhîm ʾetḵem*** — ***visiting, God will visit you*** — **twice, at 50:24 and
50:25.** ***Pāqad*** **is the verb of Genesis 21:1, *"Yahweh visited Sarah as he had said"***, and
**it is the password of the exodus**: Moses is told to tell the elders *"I have surely visited
you"* (Exodus 3:16), **and at Exodus 4:31 the people hear that God *had visited* them and bow their
heads and worship.** **The phrase Joseph says twice on his deathbed is the phrase that will
identify a deliverer four hundred years later.** See
[[Yahweh Visited Sarah - Paqad and the Visitations of God]].

**Notice what he does not say.** **He does not say *when*, he does not say *how*, and he does not
say *I*.** **He is the only patriarch who dies having been told nothing by God directly** —
Genesis records no theophany to Joseph, no covenant speech, no night vision of his own — **and the
promise he repeats is one he has only ever been told secondhand.** **He names the three men it was
sworn to and is not one of them.** See [[Faith]] and [[Hope]].

***"You shall carry up my bones from here."*** ***ʿAṣmōtay.*** **Jacob was carried to Canaan
within the year; Joseph asks to wait.** **He could have had what his father had — the cortege
existed, the road was open, the king had said yes once — and he chooses instead to be left in
Egypt with the people until the people leave.** **It is the one decision in his life that costs
him something and buys him nothing.**

**Hebrews 11:22 credits Joseph with exactly one act of faith and it is this one**: *"By faith
Joseph, when his end was near, made mention of the departure of the children of Israel, and gave
instructions concerning his bones."* **Not the dreams, not the famine policy, not the
forgiveness — the coffin.** **Exodus 13:19 records that Moses took the bones on the night of the
departure; Joshua 24:32 buries them at [[Shechem (city)]], in the parcel Jacob bought from Hamor's
sons and gave to Joseph at 48:22.** **It takes four hundred years and two books to answer this
verse.** Filed as [[Carry Up My Bones - Joseph's Oath and Its Keeping]] and
[[Bought, Sacked, or Taken by the Sword - How Jacob Got Shechem]].

***"He was put in a coffin in Egypt."*** ***ʾĀrôn***, **coffin — and it is the same noun the Torah
will use, three hundred-odd times, for the ark of the covenant.** **The first *ʾārôn* in Scripture
holds a dead man in Egypt; the second carries the presence of God through the wilderness.** **No
verse connects them and the word does.**

**The book's last three words in Hebrew are *wayyîśem bāʾārôn bəmiṣrāyim* — *and he was put in a
coffin in Egypt*.** **Genesis begins with God creating the heavens and the earth and ends with an
embalmed Hebrew in an Egyptian box**, **and the last direct speech in it is a promise that the
box will be moved.** **The Torah's other bookend matches**: **Deuteronomy ends with Moses buried in
a valley in Moab, *"no man knows of his tomb to this day"*** (34:6). **Neither of the two men the
Torah ends on is buried in the land.** See [[Burial and the Grave]] and
[[The Hope of Resurrection in the Old Testament]].

## People

- [[Joseph]] — **the chapter is his**, from the first verb to the last. He weeps on the body, orders
  the embalming, petitions a king through courtiers, leads the cortege, returns to Egypt, refuses to
  stand in God's place, refuses his brothers' offer of servitude for the second time, renews the
  promise to feed them, sees three generations of great-grandchildren, and dies at a hundred and ten
  asking to be carried out. **He is the only patriarch to whom God never speaks and the only one who
  dies with the promise word-perfect on his lips**
- [[Jacob]] — **dead before the chapter opens**, and its subject for fourteen verses. Embalmed as
  *Israel*, wept over by Egypt for seventy days, carried by his own twelve sons into Canaan, and laid
  in Machpelah as the sixth of six. **The oath he extracted at 47:29–31 is what pries the cortege out
  of Egypt**
- [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] — **his last appearance, and it is a single sentence of assent**
  (50:6). **He lets the second man in the kingdom, the whole court, and the elders of the country
  leave for a foreign funeral on the strength of an oath to a dead shepherd.** The Pharaoh of
  Exodus 1:8, *"who didn't know Joseph"*, is the mirror of this verse
- The eleven brothers — [[Reuben]], [[Simeon]], [[Levi]], [[Judah]], [[Dan (person)]],
  [[Naphtali]], [[Gad]], [[Asher]], [[Issachar]], [[Zebulun]], [[Benjamin]] — **who carry their
  father's body themselves** (50:12–13) **and then, within two verses, decide their brother has been
  waiting seventeen years to kill them.** They send a message rather than come, quote a command from
  Jacob that Genesis never records, and fall down offering themselves as slaves
- [[Manasseh]] — named once, as [[Machir]]'s father; his line is the one Genesis follows to the end
- [[Ephraim]] — named once, whose children Joseph sees *"to the third generation"*
- [[Machir]] — **the last person named in Genesis**; his children are born on Joseph's knees, and his
  clan will take Gilead
- [[Abraham]] — named in the deed at 50:13 as the buyer of the field, and in the oath at 50:24 as one
  of the three the land was sworn to
- [[Isaac]] — named in the oath formula at 50:24
- [[Ephron the Hittite]] — named at 50:13, twenty-seven chapters after the sale, because the title
  still runs in his name
- [[God]] — **does not speak, appear, or act in this chapter.** He is the subject of three clauses and
  all three are in other people's mouths: *God meant it for good* (20), *God will surely visit you*
  (24, 25). **Genesis ends with God spoken about and silent**

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — **where the whole chapter begins and ends.** Its physicians embalm Israel, its people
  weep seventy days, its court grants leave, its elders and chariots attend the funeral, and its soil
  keeps Joseph's coffin. **The last word of Genesis**
- [[Goshen]] — where the little ones, the flocks and the herds are left (50:8)
- [[Abel Mizraim]] — **the threshing floor of Atad**, *beyond the Jordan*, renamed by the Canaanites
  for what they thought they were watching
- [[The Jordan]] — named twice in two verses as the reference point for Atad, and the chapter's
  standing geographical puzzle
- [[Canaan]] — entered once, for a burial, and left in the same verse-cluster
- [[Machpelah]] — **the sixth burial**, and the only real property the family holds in the land at the
  end of the book
- [[Mamre (place)]] — the location clause carried over verbatim from the deed of 23:17
- [[Shechem (city)]] — not named here, but the destination of the bones Joseph swears them to
  (Joshua 24:32)

- [[Hope]] — *God will surely visit you*, said twice by a man who will not see it

## Notes

**On the boundary.** The book page's plan gave this pericope as Genesis 50:1–26 and the text
supports it exactly: 49:33 closes on Jacob's death and 50:1 opens on Joseph's reaction to it, and
nothing after 50:26 exists to run into. **The chapter is three scenes — burial, forgiveness, death —
and splitting them would have separated 50:20 from the fear that provokes it and the death that
follows it.** The boundary stands as planned.

**On what Genesis does not do at the end.** **There is no summary, no benediction, and no narrator's
verdict.** **The last independent clause in the book is passive and has no named subject:** *he was
put in a coffin*. **A book that has been willing to say *"it was very good"* (1:31) and *"the thing
which he did was evil in Yahweh's sight"* (38:10) ends on a piece of furniture.** The wiki does not
supply the missing sentence.

**On the seventeen years.** The arithmetic is the book's own: Jacob tells Pharaoh he is a hundred and
thirty at 47:9, and 47:28 gives him seventeen years in Egypt and a hundred and forty-seven in total.
**Joseph was seventeen when his brothers sold him** (37:2). **The number of years he spent in his
father's house before the pit equals the number of years his brothers spent in his house before this
chapter**, and Genesis, which never once comments on a number, does not comment on this one.

**On the completion of Genesis.** **This pericope closes the book** — fifty-nine of fifty-nine, from
[[The Creation of the World]] to here. The book page's status moves to `complete`. **The next
`/ingest` goes to [[Job]] and not to Exodus**: `wiki/books/index.md` still carries Job as
`in-progress` at six of thirty-two, and an open book outranks an earlier unopened one.
