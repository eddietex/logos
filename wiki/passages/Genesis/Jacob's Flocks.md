# Jacob's Flocks

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 30:25–43

## Summary

**A man asks to go home, is talked into staying for wages, and then takes the flock apart.**

Nineteen verses, six years (31:41), and **the most technical passage in Genesis outside the ark's
specifications and the brickmaking at Babel.** It contains a resignation, a counter-offer, a contract
with an audit clause, a pre-emption, and **seven verses on animal husbandry** — peeled rods in
watering troughs, faces turned toward streaked animals, and a selective-breeding scheme that gives
the strong stock to one man and the feeble to the other.

**God does not appear in it.** He is named three times and every one of them is in somebody's mouth:
Laban says *"Yahweh has blessed me for your sake"* (30:27), Jacob says *"Yahweh has blessed you
wherever I turned"* (30:30), and **that is the whole of the theology in the pericope.** No dream, no
altar, no vow, no speech. **The chapter that follows will say the increase was God's doing** — an
angel in a dream, at 31:10–12, and *"God has taken away your father's livestock and given them to
me"* at 31:9 — **but that explanation is not in these verses.** What is in these verses is poplar
rods.

**And the passage is where the promise starts paying out.** Jacob was told at Bethel *"you will
spread abroad"* — ***ûpāraṣtā*** (28:14) — and the last verse here is ***wayyipṛōṣ hāʾîš məʾōd
məʾōd***, ***the man burst out exceedingly*** (30:43). **Genesis uses the promise's own verb for a
livestock inventory, and does not say that is what it is doing.** See [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]].

### The shape

| verses | what happens | who is in control |
|---|---|---|
| **25–26** | Jacob resigns: *send me away* — the trigger is Joseph's birth | Jacob asks |
| **27–28** | Laban refuses to release him: *I have divined that Yahweh has blessed me for your sake* | Laban |
| **29–30** | Jacob's audit: *it was little… and it has increased to a multitude* | Jacob states the case |
| **31–33** | The contract: **no wage at all**, only the odd-coloured animals, with a standing audit | Jacob sets terms |
| **34–36** | Laban agrees — and **removes the animals himself**, three days away, into his sons' hands | Laban pre-empts |
| **37–39** | The rods: poplar, almond, plane; peeled white; set in the troughs | Jacob |
| **40–42** | The separation, and the breeding of the strong only | Jacob |
| **43** | ***The man burst out exceedingly*** | the verb of 28:14 |

**Two moves each, and Jacob has the last one.**

### 30:25–26 — the resignation, and what triggers it

> **25** When Rachel had borne Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, “Send me away, that I may go to my own
> place, and to my country.
> **26** Give me my wives and my children for whom I have served you, and let me go; for you know my
> service with which I have served you.”

***"When Rachel had borne Joseph."*** **The clause is a date, and Genesis chooses this one.** Eleven
sons and a daughter have been born; the book waits for the twelfth-to-last, **the first child of the
loved wife**, and makes that the moment the household is complete enough to leave. **Nothing else has
changed.** See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] and [[Rachel]].

***"Send me away."*** ***Šalləḥēnî.*** **Piel of *šālaḥ*, and it is the verb of the Exodus** —
*šallaḥ ʾet-ʿammî*, *let my people go*. **A man who came with nothing, worked fourteen years for two
wives, and now asks to be released with his family is asking in the vocabulary Moses will use to
Pharaoh**, and Genesis puts the request in his mouth rather than God's. **He is not commanded to go
until 31:3.** He asks first, and is refused. See
[[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].

***"To my own place, and to my country."*** ***ʾEl-məqômî ûlʾarṣî.*** **The land is named by a
possessive pronoun and nothing else** — no *Canaan*, no *the land I will show you*, no reference to
the promise. **And it is the Bethel vow's own clause coming due**: *"so that I come again to my
father's house in peace"* (28:21). **Jacob's vow had four conditions, and this verse is him moving to
collect on the last of them**, six years before God tells him to. See [[Oaths and Vows]] and
[[Land and Sojourning]].

***"For whom I have served you… my service with which I have served you."*** ***ʿĀbadtîkā***,
***ʿăbōdātî***, ***ʿăbadtîkā*** — **three forms of *ʿābad* in one verse.** The root ran **seven
times** through the marriage contract in [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]]; here it runs three times in
twenty-odd Hebrew words, and **the object of the service is not a wage but two women**. *"Give me my
wives and my children"* is a man asking his employer to release the goods he has already paid for.
See [[Work]].

### 30:27–30 — the counter-offer, and a foreigner's use of the divine name

> **27** Laban said to him, “If now I have found favor in your eyes, stay here, for I have divined
> that Yahweh has blessed me for your sake.”
> **28** He said, “Appoint me your wages, and I will give it.”
> **29** He said to him, “You know how I have served you, and how your livestock have fared with me.
> **30** For it was little which you had before I came, and it has increased to a multitude. Yahweh
> has blessed you wherever I turned. Now when will I provide for my own house also?”

***"If now I have found favor in your eyes."*** ***ʾIm-nāʾ māṣāʾtî ḥēn bəʿênêkā.*** **The formula of
a petitioner to a superior** — Abraham to the three visitors (18:3), Lot to the angels (19:19),
Jacob himself to Esau (33:8, 15) — **and Laban is using it on his own hired man.** He has just been
handed a resignation and answers it as a supplicant. **The employer asks a favour of the employee**,
which is the passage's first indication that the balance has moved. See [[Favor and Grace]].

***"I have divined."*** ***Niḥaštî.*** **From *nāḥaš*, the root of augury and omen-reading**, and
**this is its first appearance in the Bible.** The verb is later prohibited outright — *"you shall
not practice divination"* (Leviticus 19:26), *"there shall not be found with you… one who practices
divination"* (Deuteronomy 18:10) — **and Genesis reports it without a word of comment**, from a man
whose household will turn out to keep *teraphim* (31:19). **The only other use of the root in Genesis
is of Joseph's cup** (44:5, 15). See
[[Divination in Genesis - Laban's Nahash and Joseph's Cup]] and [[Prophecy and Vision]].

**What he divined is orthodox.** *"Yahweh has blessed me for your sake"* — ***biglālekā***. **An
Aramean says the covenant name and reads his own prosperity as somebody else's blessing spilling
over onto him**, which is [[The Call of Abram]]'s stated purpose (12:3) stated by a beneficiary
rather than by God. **He joins the run of foreigners in Genesis who hold the right theology** —
[[Melchizedek]], [[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]], [[Abimelech (of Isaac's time)]], and now Laban —
**and he is the only one who says it while refusing to let the blessing's carrier go.** See
[[The Nations]] and [[Blessing]].

**And the same idiom will govern the house of Potiphar**: *"Yahweh blessed the Egyptian's house **for
Joseph's sake**"* (39:5), ***biglal yôsēp***. **Two foreign households in Genesis are enriched by a
man of this family, and the book uses the same preposition for both.** See
[[Blessed for His Sake - Laban's House and Potiphar's]].

***"Appoint me your wages."*** ***Noqbâ śəkārəkā ʿālay*** — *name your price against me.* **The
second time Laban has opened a wages negotiation with this exact move**; the first was 29:15, *"Tell
me, what will your wages be?"*, and **it cost Jacob seven years.** The root ***śākār*** is the one
[[Leah]] used to hire her own husband for a night at 30:16. **Three wage negotiations in two
chapters, and Jacob has never once set the price and been paid it.**

***"It was little which you had before I came, and it has increased to a multitude."*** **Jacob's
audit, and Laban does not dispute a word of it.** *"Yahweh has blessed you **wherever I turned**"* is
literally ***lərag̱lî***, ***at my foot*** — *the blessing followed my footsteps.* **Both men in this
exchange attribute the growth of Laban's estate to Yahweh acting through Jacob**, and neither of them
mentions Bethel, the promise, or the dust of the earth. **The theology is complete and entirely
transactional.**

***"Now when will I provide for my own house also?"*** ***Bêtî*** — *my house.* **The first time
Jacob calls anything his own in the Jacob cycle.** He has wives he was given, children he did not
name, and a flock that belongs to his uncle; **the sentence that changes the chapter is a man
noticing that after twenty years he owns nothing.**

### 30:31–34 — the contract nobody could cheat

> **31** He said, “What shall I give you?” Jacob said, “You shall not give me anything. If you will do
> this thing for me, I will again feed your flock and keep it.
> **32** I will pass through all your flock today, removing from there every speckled and spotted one,
> and every black one among the sheep, and the spotted and speckled among the goats. This will be my
> hire.
> **33** So my righteousness will answer for me hereafter, when you come concerning my hire that is
> before you. Every one that is not speckled and spotted among the goats, and black among the sheep,
> that might be with me, will be considered stolen.”
> **34** Laban said, “Behold, let it be according to your word.”

***"You shall not give me anything."*** ***Lōʾ-titten-lî məʾûmâ.*** **A refusal of wages from a man
who has just complained he has none**, and **the formula is Abram's to the king of Sodom**: *"I will
not take a thread nor a sandal strap nor anything that is yours, lest you should say, 'I have made
Abram rich'"* (14:23). **Both men refuse payment from a patron so that the increase cannot be
credited to the patron**, and **only one of them then engineers the increase himself.** See
[[Not a Thread nor a Sandal Strap]] and [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

***"Feed your flock and keep it."*** ***ʾErʿeh… ʾešmōr*** — **shepherd and *keep*.** *Šāmar* is
Eden's second verb (2:15) and the one Cain refuses at 4:9. **Jacob offers to go on being the keeper
of somebody else's animals**, and it is the last thing in the pericope he offers.

***"Every speckled and spotted one, and every black one among the sheep."*** ***Nāqōd***,
***ṭālûʾ***, ***ḥûm.*** **The terms matter because of the animals they describe.** Near Eastern sheep
are predominantly white and goats predominantly dark; **Jacob has asked for the sheep that are not
white and the goats that are not plain** — **the minority in both herds, and by ordinary breeding a
small and self-limiting one.** **On its face he has asked for almost nothing**, which is why Laban's
*"let it be according to your word"* comes back in five Hebrew words with no haggling.

***"So my righteousness will answer for me."*** ***Wəʿāntâ-bî ṣidqātî.*** ***Ṣədāqâ*** is the noun
of 15:6 — *"he believed in Yahweh, and he reckoned it to him for righteousness"* — and of 18:19,
*"to do righteousness and justice."* **Here it means something narrower and wholly practical: my
honesty will be visible in the colouring of the animals.** **Jacob designs a wage that audits
itself** — any plain animal in his fold is *stolen*, by his own prior admission — **and he is the
man who once put on his brother's clothes to be mistaken for him.** **The one who made his living
by making things look like what they were not has proposed a contract in which appearance is
proof.** See [[Righteousness]] and [[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]].

### 30:35–36 — Laban moves first

> **35** That day, he removed the male goats that were streaked and spotted, and all the female goats
> that were speckled and spotted, every one that had white in it, and all the black ones among the
> sheep, and gave them into the hand of his sons.
> **36** He set three days’ journey between himself and Jacob, and Jacob fed the rest of Laban’s
> flocks.

**Jacob said *I will pass through all your flock today, removing from there* (30:32). The next verse
says *that day, he removed* — and hands the animals to *his sons*.** **The Hebrew subject is
unstated, and the standing reading is Laban**, because the animals go to *his* sons and because a
Jacob who kept them would have no reason for anything that follows. **The wiki adopts that reading
and flags that Genesis does not spell it out.**

**What it amounts to is not theft and not a breach.** Laban does exactly what the contract said
would happen — the odd-coloured animals come out of the flock — **and then puts them three days'
journey away in his own sons' custody**, so that **Jacob is left tending a herd from which every
animal that could produce his wage has been removed.** **He is shepherding a flock that has been
engineered to owe him nothing**, and Genesis reports the manoeuvre in one verse without a verb of
judgment. **Nobody says a word about it**, then or later; **31:7's *"changed my wages ten times"* is
the nearest the book comes to a verdict, and it is Jacob's.**

***"Three days' journey."*** ***Derek šəlōšet yāmîm.*** **The measure of separation in Genesis 22:4**
(Moriah) **and of the release Moses will ask Pharaoh for** (Exodus 5:3). Here it is a firebreak
between an employer and the man he has just contracted with. **Laban will cover the same ground in
seven days when he pursues** (31:23).

### 30:37–39 — the rods

> **37** Jacob took to himself rods of fresh poplar, almond, plane tree, peeled white streaks in
> them, and made the white appear which was in the rods.
> **38** He set the rods which he had peeled opposite the flocks in the gutters in the
> watering-troughs where the flocks came to drink. They conceived when they came to drink.
> **39** The flocks conceived before the rods, and the flocks produced streaked, speckled, and
> spotted.

**Three trees, and the first of them is his father-in-law's name.**

***"Fresh poplar."*** ***Libneh laḥ.*** ***Libneh*** — the storax or white poplar — **is built on the
root *l-b-n*, *white*, and ***Lābān*** is that adjective exactly.** The verse then uses the root
twice more: he peels ***pəṣālôt ləbānôt***, ***white*** streaks, and *"made the ***white***
appear"*, ***maḥśōp hallābān***. **Three occurrences of *lābān* in one verse, in the operation that
strips Laban of his flock.** **Genesis does not gloss the pun and does not need to.** See
[[Names and Naming]] and [[Laban]].

***"Almond."*** ***Lûz*** — **and *Luz* is the name Genesis gave for Bethel before Jacob renamed
it**: *"the name of that city was Luz at the first"* (28:19). **The man who slept at Luz and vowed a
tenth there is now cutting rods of *lûz***; the book supplies the coincidence and says nothing about
it. See [[Bethel]] and [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]].

***"Plane tree."*** ***ʿErmôn***, whose consonants are those of ***ʿārûm***, *crafty* — the serpent's
adjective at 3:1 — **and of *ʿārôm*, *naked*, which 2:25 pairs with it.** **The wiki records the
resemblance and presses nothing on it**, because *ʿermôn* is simply the name of a tree and Genesis
draws no line between them. See [[The Serpent]].

***"He peeled… and made the white appear."*** **The technique is peeling bark to expose pale
sapwood, so the rods are striped.** **It is a piece of ancient folk husbandry resting on maternal
impression** — the belief that what a dam sees at conception marks her young — **and Genesis
describes it in the flattest procedural prose in the book.** **It offers no theory, no source, and no
verdict.** It does not say Jacob was taught this, that it worked, or that it was superstition; **it
says he did it and reports what the flocks produced.**

***"In the gutters in the watering-troughs."*** ***Bārəhāṭîm bəšiqătôt hammāyim.*** **The trough is
where this family's marriages happen.** Rebekah emptied her jar into the ***šōqet*** for ten camels
(24:20); Jacob rolled the stone off the well and watered Laban's flock the hour he arrived (29:10).
**The place where a wife was won for Isaac and where Jacob first met Rachel is the place he now uses
to breed his employer's stock away from him.** See [[The Betrothal at the Well]] and
[[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]].

### 30:40–43 — the separation, and the promise's verb

> **40** Jacob separated the lambs, and set the faces of the flocks toward the streaked and all the
> black in the flock of Laban: and he put his own droves apart, and didn’t put them into Laban’s
> flock.
> **41** Whenever the stronger of the flock conceived, Jacob laid the rods in front of the eyes of the
> flock in the gutters, that they might conceive among the rods;
> **42** but when the flock were feeble, he didn’t put them in. So the feebler were Laban’s, and the
> stronger Jacob’s.
> **43** The man increased exceedingly, and had large flocks, female servants and male servants, and
> camels and donkeys.

***"Set the faces of the flocks toward the streaked."*** **Verse 40 is among the most obscure
sentences in Genesis**, and the wiki says so rather than smoothing it. The Hebrew is compressed
enough that translations diverge on **whose** animals are being faced toward **whose**; the WEB's
reading is that Jacob positioned Laban's ewes so that the streaked and dark animals were in their
line of sight. **What all readings agree on is the outcome clause**: *he put his own droves apart,
and didn't put them into Laban's flock.* **Jacob is now running two herds and only one of them is
his employer's.**

***"Whenever the stronger of the flock conceived."*** ***Hamquššārôt*** against ***hāʿăṭupîm***
(30:42). **The pair is read either as *vigorous* against *feeble* or as *early-breeding* against
*late-breeding*, and the wiki adopts neither**, because both give the same result: **Jacob used the
rods only on the better animals and withheld them from the worse.** **This is the sharpest thing in
the passage and it has nothing to do with maternal impression** — it is **selective breeding**,
applied so that the odd-coloured young (his) come from the strong stock and the plain young (Laban's)
from the weak. **The folk technique may or may not do anything. The selection unquestionably does**,
and Genesis narrates them in the same breath without distinguishing them.

***"So the feebler were Laban's, and the stronger Jacob's."*** **The contract is honoured to the
letter and gutted in substance.** **Every animal in Jacob's fold is one the contract awarded him**;
he simply arranged which animals would be born. **The wiki records that the passage contains no lie,
no theft, and no broken term** — and that it is nonetheless **the third act of this man's life in
which an inheritance changes hands by a manoeuvre nobody can quite call fraud** (25:29–34, 27:1–29).
See [[Deception]] and [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

***"The man increased exceedingly."*** ***Wayyipṛōṣ hāʾîš məʾōd məʾōd.*** **Two things at once.**

- ***Pāraṣ*, *burst out, spread*, is the verb of the Bethel promise**: *"you will **spread abroad**
  to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south"* (28:14). **It is used again here
  for the first time in Jacob's life, and its subject is livestock.** The wiki states the verbal link
  and states equally that **no character makes it** — nobody in these verses mentions Bethel, the
  vow, the ladder, or the promise. See [[Dust, Stars, and Sand - The Offspring Promises]].
- ***"The man"*** — ***hāʾîš***, not *Jacob.* **Genesis called Isaac *the man* at the exact same
  moment**: *"**the man** became great, and grew more and more until he became very great"* (26:13),
  **immediately before *"the Philistines envied him"* (26:14) and the expulsion.** **The same noun,
  the same enrichment abroad, and the same sequel**: 31:1–2 opens with Laban's sons complaining and
  Laban's face turning. See [[Isaac in Gerar]] and
  [[Mightier Than We - Isaac in Gerar and Israel in Egypt]].

***"Large flocks, female servants and male servants, and camels and donkeys."*** **The inventory is
the book's standing list for a patriarch enriched in a foreign country** — Abram leaving Egypt with
*"sheep, cattle, male donkeys, male servants, female servants, female donkeys, and camels"* (12:16),
Abraham *"blessed… greatly"* with the same catalogue at 24:35, Isaac at 26:14. **Three generations,
three foreign patrons, and the same six nouns.** See [[Wealth and Possessions]] and
[[Abram in Egypt]].

### What the pericope is doing

**It is the first time Jacob wins something and the text does not tell you what to think about it.**

The birthright was bought from a starving man; the blessing was taken in a disguise with a flat lie
in it; **this is a contract Jacob proposed, Laban accepted, Laban immediately hollowed out, and Jacob
then out-engineered.** **Nothing in the nineteen verses is illegal and nothing is condemned.** The
narrator does not call it craft, does not call it justice, and does not call it theft.

**The passage's central problem is that Genesis gives the increase two causes and never reconciles
them.** Here it is rods and selective breeding, described in seven verses of procedure with no
divine actor anywhere in them. **One chapter later Jacob will tell his wives that an angel of God
showed him the streaked goats in a dream and that *"God has taken away your father's livestock and
given them to me"* (31:9–12)** — **the same result, attributed to revelation, by the man who cut the
rods.** **The wiki does not choose between them**, and does not assume that Jacob's later account
supersedes the narrator's earlier one. See
[[Two Explanations for One Flock - The Rods and the Dream]].

**What the passage does say plainly is that the blessing is contagious and the carrier is not
released.** **Laban's own diagnosis at 30:27 is correct** — his estate grew because Jacob was in it —
**and his response to the diagnosis is to keep him.** [[Egypt]] will do the same thing to this man's
descendants for four hundred years. **The pattern the book keeps returning to is a foreign household
that discovers the blessing works and then declines to let it leave**, and this pericope is its first
full statement.

**And the promise is being kept without being mentioned.** *You will spread abroad*; **the man burst
out exceedingly.** *I am with you and will keep you wherever you go*; **the man is not recorded
praying once.** **Genesis lays the fulfilment of 28:13–15 across two pericopes of household
arithmetic and animal husbandry, and lets nobody in either of them notice.**

## People

- [[Jacob]] — **the first pericope in his life he ends up in charge of.** He resigns, states an audit
  no one contests, refuses a wage, designs a contract that audits itself, absorbs a pre-emption
  without a word, and then out-breeds the man who made it. **He speaks four times and every sentence
  is about property or release**; he does not pray, build, vow, or mention the promise, and the verb
  Genesis uses of his prosperity in the last verse is the one God used of his offspring at Bethel.
  **Genesis passes no verdict on any of it.**
- [[Laban]] — **he diagnoses the blessing correctly and responds by refusing to release its carrier.**
  He petitions his own employee with the formula of a supplicant, admits he has been divining, opens
  a wages negotiation with the same move that cost Jacob seven years at 29:15, agrees to the terms in
  five words — and **strips every animal that could produce those wages out of the flock the same
  day**, three days' journey off, into his sons' hands. **Genesis reports the manoeuvre and never
  calls it anything.** He is the only man in the book to name Yahweh while holding a man against his
  will.
- [[Rachel]] — **named once, in a dating clause.** *"When Rachel had borne Joseph"* is the trigger for
  the whole passage: the birth of the loved wife's first son is the moment the household is judged
  complete enough to go home.
- [[Joseph]] — **named once, and it is his birth that starts the departure.** The child of 30:24 is
  the hinge of 30:25, and **the household whose blessing spills over onto its foreign patron will do
  it again in his generation, in Egypt** (39:5).
- [[God]] — **named three times, always by a human being, never as the subject of a narrated act.**
  *Yahweh has blessed me for your sake* (Laban); *Yahweh has blessed you wherever I turned* (Jacob).
  **He does not speak, appear, promise, or command in the pericope**, and the increase that fills its
  second half is credited to him only in the next chapter, by Jacob (31:9–12).
- **Laban's sons** — **unnamed, and the passage's quietest problem.** The odd-coloured flock is put
  *"into the hand of his sons"* (30:35), which means Laban has grown sons, three days away, with
  property of their own — and **it is their complaint that opens Genesis 31**: *"Jacob has taken away
  all that was our father's."* No page is created for them; they are carried on [[Laban]].

## Places

**No place is named in these nineteen verses.** The setting is Laban's country — [[Aram Naharaim]],
Paddan Aram, the land Jacob reached at 29:1 — and the only geography the passage supplies is a
distance: ***three days' journey*** between two flocks (30:36). **The place Jacob wants is named by
a pronoun**, *my own place and my country* (30:25), which is [[Canaan]]; **the place he keeps cutting
rods from is named by a tree**, *lûz*, which is what [[Bethel]] was called before he renamed it
(28:19).

## Themes

- [[Work]] — **the only sustained description of a trade in Genesis.** Seven verses of husbandry, a
  wage contract with an audit clause, and the third negotiation in two chapters in which this man's
  labour is priced by somebody else. ***ʿĀbad*** three times in 30:26; ***śākār*** in 30:28, 32, 33.
- [[Blessing]] — **the blessing observed from outside and treated as an asset.** *Yahweh has blessed
  me for your sake* is Genesis 12:3's stated purpose, spoken by its beneficiary, and used as an
  argument for detaining the man it works through.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — **the patriarch enriched abroad, for the third generation running**,
  in the book's standing inventory of flocks, slaves, camels and donkeys, and with the host's
  resentment following in the next verse.
- [[Deception]] — **the pericope in which the family's method is used with no lie in it.** Laban
  keeps the letter of the contract and empties it; Jacob keeps the letter of the contract and
  engineers it. **Genesis condemns neither and describes both.**
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — ***nāḥaš***, divination, **enters the Bible here**, in the mouth of a
  man who got the right answer by a means the law will forbid.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — *"send me away, that I may go to my own place, and to my country"*: **the
  first time Jacob asks to go back**, six years before God commands it (31:3), and on the strength of
  a vow he made at Bethel rather than a promise he was given there.
- [[Favor and Grace]] — ***ḥēn bəʿênêkā*** used **downward**, by a master to a servant, for the only
  time in Genesis.
- [[The Nations]] — an Aramean names Yahweh, reads providence correctly, and divines. **The book's
  outsiders keep turning out to know things**, and it never explains how.
- [[Names and Naming]] — ***libneh***, ***ləbānôt***, ***hallābān*** — **three plays on *Lābān* in
  the one verse that begins his undoing**, and *lûz*, the old name of Bethel, cut as a rod.
- [[Righteousness]] — ***ṣidqātî***, 30:33: the noun of 15:6 and 18:19, here meaning *my books will
  bear inspection.*
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — the Bethel vow's fourth clause, *"so that I come again to my father's house in
  peace"*, is what 30:25 is a move to collect.
- [[Envy]] — not yet in the passage, and **two verses away**: 31:1–2 gives Laban's sons the
  complaint and Laban the changed face, exactly as 26:14 followed 26:13.
- [[Strife and Peacemaking]] — **the whole exchange is conducted in perfect courtesy**, with a
  petition formula, an accepted contract, and no raised voice, and it ends with two herds three days
  apart and a pursuit coming.

## Notes

- **On the boundary, which was used as drawn.** The plan's box read *Jacob's Flocks — Genesis
  30:25–43*, and it needed no revision. **30:25 is a clean break** — new subject, new interlocutor,
  and an explicit dating clause (*"when Rachel had borne Joseph"*) closing the birth sequence — and
  **30:43 ends the unit on a summary verse**, with 31:1 opening on a new complaint from new speakers.
  **A boundary at 31:1 was considered and rejected**: 31:1–2 belongs with the flight it motivates,
  and the plan's next box already covers 31:1–55.
- **On the title.** *Jacob's Flocks* was kept from the plan. **Alternatives considered and
  rejected**: *The Speckled and the Spotted* (memorable, but it names only the second half),
  *Jacob's Wages* (accurate, and nearly a duplicate of the wage material already titled under
  [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]]), and *The Rods in the Troughs* (names the one element of the passage
  the text itself declines to explain). **No other book has a passage page by this title.**
- **On the subject of 30:35, which is a real ambiguity and is flagged rather than resolved.** Jacob
  says at 30:32 *"I will pass through all your flock today, removing from there"*; 30:35 says *"that
  day, **he** removed"* and gives the animals to *"his sons"*. **The Hebrew supplies no name.** The
  page adopts the standing reading — **Laban is the remover** — on the two grounds the text does
  supply (the sons are Laban's, and nothing in 30:37–42 makes sense if Jacob already held the
  odd-coloured animals), **and says explicitly that Genesis does not spell it out.**
- **On 30:40, which is left obscure.** *"Set the faces of the flocks toward the streaked and all the
  black in the flock of Laban"* is among the hardest sentences in the book, and translations differ
  on whose animals face whose. **The page states the difficulty, gives the WEB's reading as the
  WEB's, and rests the paragraph on the clause all readings share** — *he put his own droves apart.*
- **On the two mechanisms in 30:37–42, which are distinguished here for the first time in the wiki.**
  The rods (30:37–39) are **maternal impression**, a folk belief the text neither endorses nor
  mocks. The selection (30:41–42) is **selective breeding**, and it works. **Genesis narrates them as
  one continuous procedure and draws no line between them**; the page draws the line and says that
  the book does not.
- **On the *lābān* pun, which is the page's main philological claim.** ***Libneh*** (poplar/storax),
  ***ləbānôt*** (white, of the peeled streaks), and ***hallābān*** (the white) all sit in Genesis
  30:37, and ***Lābān*** is the ordinary adjective *white*, as the [[Laban]] page has recorded since
  Genesis 24. **The page states the wordplay as wordplay and makes no argument from it**, because
  Genesis glosses none of it.
- **On the two explanations, which are the reason a connection page exists rather than a verdict.**
  This pericope credits nothing to God; Genesis 31:9–12 credits everything to him, in Jacob's own
  mouth. **The wiki does not harmonise them, does not treat the later speech as the narrator's view,
  and does not treat the earlier silence as a denial.** See
  [[Two Explanations for One Flock - The Rods and the Dream]].
- **On what was created.** **One theme page**, [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Genesis has tracked
  livestock, slaves, silver, and gold since 12:5 and 13:2, three patriarchs have grown rich in
  foreign countries, and the wiki had no page for the subject; this pericope is the third instance
  and the most detailed. **Three connection pages**:
  [[Blessed for His Sake - Laban's House and Potiphar's]],
  [[Two Explanations for One Flock - The Rods and the Dream]], and
  [[Divination in Genesis - Laban's Nahash and Joseph's Cup]].
- **On what was deliberately not created.** **No page for Laban's sons** — they are given a hand to
  hold animals in and a complaint in 31:1, and they are carried on [[Laban]] until that pericope
  arrives. **No place page**, because the passage names none. **No connection page for *šalləḥēnî*
  and the Exodus**: the proto-exodus shape of the Laban years (a service, a release refused, a
  departure with plunder, a pursuit) only completes in Genesis 31, and the material is carried here
  and on [[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]] until then.
- **What was left for later pericopes.** Laban's sons and Laban's face (31:1–2); *"return to the land
  of your fathers"* (31:3); the wages changed ten times and the six years (31:7, 41); the angel and
  the dream (31:9–12); the *teraphim* (31:19, 30–35); the seven-day pursuit (31:23); the flocks
  counted out for Esau (32:13–21); Potiphar's house (39:5); and Joseph's cup (44:5, 15). **All are
  given as plain scripture references rather than wikilinks, so none is a dangling link.**
