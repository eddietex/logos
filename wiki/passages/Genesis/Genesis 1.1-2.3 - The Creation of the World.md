# Genesis 1:1–2:3 — The Creation of the World

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 1:1–2:3

## Summary

God creates the heavens and the earth out of a formless, empty, dark, water-covered state, and
orders it over six days by speaking. The account is built on a deliberate symmetry: days 1–3
form three empty realms, days 4–6 fill each of them in the same order.

| Forming | | Filling |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 — light, separated from darkness | → | Day 4 — sun, moon, stars to rule day and night |
| Day 2 — the expanse, separating waters above and below | → | Day 5 — birds of the sky, creatures of the sea |
| Day 3 — dry land, seas, vegetation | → | Day 6 — land animals, then humanity, fed by vegetation |

A refrain carries each day: *God said* → *and it was so* → *God saw that it was good* → *evening
and morning*. Creation happens by speech alone; there is no conflict, no rival, no struggle
against the deep. Humanity arrives last, on the sixth day, marked off from everything before it
by three things: a shift to deliberative speech (*"Let us make man…"* rather than *"Let there
be…"*), creation in God's own image as male and female, and a mandate to be fruitful and to rule
the creatures of the other five days. Then the verdict changes: not merely *good* but *very
good*.

The seventh day breaks the pattern. It has no evening and morning, no creative act, and no
creature made in it. What God does instead is rest, bless the day, and make it holy — the first
thing in the Bible called holy is a span of time, not a place or an object.

## Key verses

> **1:1** In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.

> **1:2** The earth was formless and empty. Darkness was on the surface of the deep and God’s
> Spirit was hovering over the surface of the waters.

> **1:26** God said, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have
> dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the birds of the sky, and over the livestock, and
> over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth."

> **1:27** God created man in his own image. In God’s image he created him; male and female he
> created them.

> **1:31** God saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. There was evening
> and there was morning, a sixth day.

> **2:3** God blessed the seventh day, and made it holy, because he rested in it from all his
> work of creation which he had done.

## People

- [[God]] — the only actor in the passage; every verb of creation is his.
- Humanity — made in God's image, male and female, on the sixth day. Not yet named as
  individuals; [[Adam]] and [[Eve]] are named in the next pericope.

## Places

None named. The passage's geography is cosmic — heavens, earth, sky, seas, dry land — and the
named locations of Genesis begin at [[Eden]] in the next pericope.

## Themes

- [[Creation]] — creation by speech, from formlessness to order, declared good.
- [[Image of God]] — humanity's defining status and the ground of its dominion.
- [[Blessing]] — the first three blessings in Scripture: sea creatures and birds (1:22),
  humanity (1:28), and the seventh day (2:3).
- [[Sabbath]] — the seventh-day rest, blessed and made holy before any law commands it.

## Notes

- **"In the beginning"** (Hebrew *bərē'šît*) can be read as an absolute statement ("In the
  beginning God created…", as WEB, KJV, ESV) or as a temporal clause ("When God began to
  create…", as NJPS and the NRSV footnote). The first reading supports creation out of nothing;
  the second describes God ordering pre-existing formless matter. The text carries both; the
  doctrine of *creatio ex nihilo* rests more on passages like Hebrews 11:3 than on this verse
  alone.
- **"Let us make man"** (1:26) — the plural is variously read as the divine council of heavenly
  beings, a plural of deliberation or majesty, or (in Christian tradition) an intimation of the
  Trinity. Verse 27 immediately reverts to the singular: *"God created man in his own image."*
- **The lights of day 4 are unnamed.** Where surrounding cultures named sun and moon as deities,
  Genesis calls them only "the greater light" and "the lesser light" and assigns them a job —
  timekeeping. The stars get half a clause: *"He also made the stars."*
- **Order without violence.** The Enuma Elish and other ancient Near Eastern cosmogonies build
  the world from the corpse of a defeated sea-monster. Here the deep is inert, the "large sea
  creatures" (1:21) are simply creatures God makes and calls good, and the whole work proceeds by
  speech.
- **The diet given in 1:29–30 is vegetarian**, for humans and animals alike. Meat is not granted
  until after the flood (Genesis 9:3).
- **Days.** Whether the six days are 24-hour days, an age each, or a literary framework is
  disputed and the text does not settle it; note that the sun is not made until day 4, and that
  day 7 has no closing "evening and morning".
