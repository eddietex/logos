# Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 41:1–57

## Summary

**Thirteen years of Joseph's life are undone in a single morning, and Genesis spends fifty-seven verses
on the day.**

**It is the longest chapter in the Joseph cycle and the longest in Genesis after chapter 24.** Between
verse 14, where Joseph is *"brought hastily out of the dungeon"*, and verse 43, where he rides in
Pharaoh's second chariot, **nothing intervenes but a conversation.** He is shaved, dressed, questioned,
believed, and given a country — **and the whole transaction is conducted between two men, in one room,
with no witness the text names.**

**What makes the chapter strange is how little it credits.** It is the hinge of the entire Joseph
narrative, the moment the dreams of 37:7 become inevitable, and **the narrator never once says that God
did any of it.**

### God is named nine times and the narrator says none of them

***ʾĔlōhîm*** occurs at **41:16, 25, 28, 32 (twice), 38, 39, 51, and 52** — **nine occurrences, and every
single one is inside quotation marks.** Seven are Joseph's; two are Pharaoh's. ***Yahweh* does not occur
in the chapter at all.**

| who speaks | where | what is claimed |
|---|---|---|
| **Joseph** | 41:16 | *"God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace"* |
| **Joseph** | 41:25, 28 | *"What God is about to do he has declared / shown to Pharaoh"* |
| **Joseph** | 41:32 | *"established by God, and God will shortly bring it to pass"* |
| **Pharaoh** | 41:38 | *"a man in whom is the Spirit of God"* |
| **Pharaoh** | 41:39 | *"Because God has shown you all of this"* |
| **Joseph** | 41:51, 52 | *"God has made me forget"* · *"God has made me fruitful"* |
| **the narrator** | — | **nothing** |

**This continues the count established at [[The Cupbearer and the Baker]] and it is not an accident of
one chapter.** The eight *Yahweh*s of [[Joseph in Potiphar's House]] stop at the chapter break and do not
return; **from 40:1 to the end of Genesis the covenant name occurs exactly once** (49:18). **What the
Joseph cycle does instead of narrating providence is let its characters assert it** — and then reports,
without comment, that they were right. See [[The Names of God]] and [[Prophecy and Vision]].

### The dream is told twice, and not the same way

**Genesis gives the dream in the narrator's voice (41:1–7) and then again in Pharaoh's (41:17–24), and
the two accounts differ.** The differences are not scribal noise; **they are the chapter's method.**

| | narrator (1–7) | Pharaoh (17–24) |
|---|---|---|
| **where he stands** | *"he stood by the river"* | *"I stood on the brink of the river"* |
| **the thin cattle** | *"ugly and thin"*, and they *"stood by the other cattle on the brink"* | *"poor and very ugly and thin, **such as I never saw in all the land of Egypt for ugliness**"*; the standing-beside is dropped |
| **after they eat** | *"the ugly and thin cattle ate up the seven sleek and fat cattle"* | **and *"it couldn't be known that they had eaten them, but they were still ugly, as at the beginning"*** |
| **the second dream** | *"seven heads of grain, thin and blasted with the east wind"* | *"withered, thin, and blasted with the east wind"* |
| **the experts** | *"there was no one who could interpret them"* | *"I told it to the magicians, but there was no one who could **explain** it to me"* |

**The important addition is verse 21, and it is the one the interpretation turns on.** Pharaoh volunteers
that the eating left no trace — ***lōʾ nôdaʿ***, *it could not be known* — **a detail the narrator did not
report**, and **Joseph hands it straight back to him four verses later**: *"the plenty will **not be
known** in the land by reason of that famine which follows"* (41:31), ***lōʾ-yiwwādaʿ***, **the same verb
in the same stem.**

**The dreamer supplies the hardest datum in his own dream without knowing what it is for.** Genesis is
not describing a man decoding a symbol set; **it is describing a message whose addressee has been
carrying the decisive clause around for a fortnight.** Compare 40:5's *"each man according to the
interpretation of his dream"* — **the meaning is a property of the dream, not of the reader.**

### The chapter's Egypt is the most detailed in Scripture

**Nothing else in the Hebrew Bible looks this hard at an Egyptian court from the inside**, and much of
the vocabulary is Egyptian rather than Hebrew.

- ***Yeʾōr*** (41:1, 2, 3, 17, 18) — **the Nile**, from Egyptian *itrw*, *river*. **The word enters
  Scripture here** and becomes the standard biblical term for the Nile. See [[The Nile]].
- ***Ḥarṭummîm*** (41:8, 24) — the **magicians**, probably from Egyptian *ḥry-tp*, *chief lector priest*.
  **It occurs in the Hebrew Bible only in Genesis 41, Exodus 7–9, and Daniel 1–2.** See
  [[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]].
- ***Wayəgallaḥ*** (41:14) — **he shaved himself.** Egyptians shaved; Semites did not. **The detail is
  four letters long and it is a change of nationality.**
- ***Šēš*** (41:42) — **fine linen**, an Egyptian loanword, and the fabric of the tabernacle throughout
  Exodus 25–39.
- ***ʾAbrēk*** (41:43) — **a hapax legomenon**, and nobody knows what it means. The WEB's *"Bow the
  knee!"* reads it from Hebrew *bārak*, *to kneel*; **the competing proposals are Egyptian *ib r.k*,
  *attention!*, and Akkadian *abarakku*, a high steward.** Genesis leaves an Egyptian word untranslated
  in the middle of a Hebrew sentence.
- ***Ṣāpənat Paʿnēaḥ***, ***ʾĀsənat***, ***Pôṭî Pheraʿ***, ***ʾŌn*** (41:45) — **four Egyptian proper
  nouns in one verse**, which is more than the rest of Genesis contains put together.

### Joseph's owner and Joseph's father-in-law have the same name

***Pôṭîpar*** (37:36; 39:1) and ***Pôṭî Pheraʿ*** (41:45, 50) are **the same Egyptian name** — *pȝ-dỉ-pȝ-rʿ*,
***he whom Ra has given*** — **the first a contracted form of the second.** Genesis spells them
differently and never remarks on it, and **there is no indication in the text that the two men are the
same man** (one is a *sārîs* and captain of the guard at Memphis or wherever the court sat; the other is a
priest at On). **Later Jewish tradition identified them and made Asenath the daughter of Potiphar's
wife**, which is a way of tying the humiliation to the reward; **the text supports none of it.**

**What the coincidence does establish is that Genesis knows what Egyptian names look like**, and that
**both of the households Joseph enters in Egypt are named for the sun god.** See [[Potiphar]],
[[Potiphera]], and [[Idolatry]].

**The hinge is verses 33–36**, and it is the one paragraph nobody asked for. **Everything before it is
Joseph doing what he was fetched to do; everything after it is the consequence of his doing more.**

### 41:1–8 — two dreams, and a professional apparatus that fails

> **1** At the end of two full years, Pharaoh dreamed: and behold, he stood by the river.
> **2** Behold, there came up out of the river seven cattle, sleek and fat, and they fed in the marsh
> grass.
> **3** Behold, seven other cattle came up after them out of the river, ugly and thin, and stood by the
> other cattle on the brink of the river.
> **4** The ugly and thin cattle ate up the seven sleek and fat cattle. So Pharaoh awoke.
> **5** He slept and dreamed a second time: and behold, seven heads of grain came up on one stalk,
> healthy and good.
> **6** Behold, seven heads of grain, thin and blasted with the east wind, sprung up after them.
> **7** The thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven healthy and full ears. Pharaoh awoke, and behold,
> it was a dream.
> **8** In the morning, his spirit was troubled, and he sent and called for all of Egypt’s magicians and
> wise men. Pharaoh told them his dreams, but there was no one who could interpret them to Pharaoh.

***"At the end of two full years."*** ***Miqqēṣ šənātayim yāmîm*** — literally ***two years of days***,
the idiom for **two full years and not a part of a third.** **Genesis measures the interval exactly and
says nothing about what filled it.** 40:23 ended on a man forgotten; **this verse resumes the sentence
and the subject has changed.** Psalm 105:19 — *"until the time that his word happened"* — **remains the
only text in Scripture that offers an account of the delay.** See [[Hope]] and
[[Sold for a Servant - Joseph's Sale in Psalm 105 and Acts 7]].

***"He stood by the river."*** ***Hayʾōr*** — **the Nile, and the chapter's first Egyptian word.** **The
cattle come *up out of the river***, which is not how cattle behave and is exactly how the Nile behaves:
**the inundation is the whole of Egyptian agriculture**, and a dream in which fatness and famine both
climb out of the water is a dream about the flood gauge. **The cow was also the form of Hathor and a
standing Egyptian image of fertility** — **so the dream is composed in the dreamer's own religious
vocabulary**, which is worth holding beside the fact that **its interpreter credits it to a God the
dreamer has never heard of.**

***"Blasted with the east wind."*** ***Šədûpōt qādîm*** — **the sirocco**, the desert wind that arrives
hot and dry and strips a crop in a day. **It is the Bible's standing instrument of agricultural ruin**
and it recurs at the two hinges of the Exodus: **it brings the locusts** (Exodus 10:13) and **it divides
the sea** (Exodus 14:21). See also Hosea 13:15 and Jonah 4:8.

***"Pharaoh awoke, and behold, it was a dream."*** ***Wəhinnēh ḥălôm*** — **the narrator's only comment
on either dream**, and it is the observation that it was one. **Genesis does not say the dream came from
God.** It does not say so here, it did not say so of [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]'s two dreams at 37:5–11,
and **in this cycle the attribution is always made by a human being inside the story** (40:8; 41:16, 25,
28, 32). **The narrator declines the claim every time.**

***"His spirit was troubled."*** ***Wattippāʿem rûḥô*** — **a violent verb**, *to beat, to strike*; the
same form describes Nebuchadnezzar at Daniel 2:1, **whose court will also fail him and who will also send
for a Hebrew.** See
[[Do Not Interpretations Belong to God - Joseph and Daniel]].

***"All of Egypt's magicians and wise men."*** ***Ḥarṭummê Miṣrayim*** and ***ḥăkāmeyhā*** — **the state
apparatus, summoned in full**, and **this is the point of the verse.** Egypt had manuals: **the Chester
Beatty III dream papyrus is essentially a two-column lookup table**, *if a man sees himself doing X, it
means Y.* **A dream of seven cows and seven ears is not an obscure symbol set** — the numbers are plain
and the agriculture is theirs. **What the chapter claims is not that the dream was hard but that the
professionals were irrelevant**, and it claims it by summoning every one of them and reporting a blank.
**In chapter 40 the prison took the experts away; here they are in the room.** See [[Wisdom]] and
[[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]].

### 41:9–13 — the answer to 40:14, two years late

> **9** Then the chief cup bearer spoke to Pharaoh, saying, “I remember my faults today.
> **10** Pharaoh was angry with his servants, and put me in custody in the house of the captain of the
> guard, with the chief baker.
> **11** We dreamed a dream in one night, he and I. We dreamed each man according to the interpretation
> of his dream.
> **12** There was with us there a young man, a Hebrew, servant to the captain of the guard, and we told
> him, and he interpreted to us our dreams. To each man according to his dream he interpreted.
> **13** As he interpreted to us, so it was. He restored me to my office, and he hanged him.”

***"I remember my faults today."*** ***ʾEt-ḥăṭāʾay ʾănî mazkîr hayyôm*** — **the verb of 40:14 and 40:23,
in the causative, and he uses it about himself.** Joseph asked to be *caused to be remembered*
(*wəhizkartanî*); **the cupbearer now says *I am causing to be remembered* — and the object is not
Joseph, it is his own offences.** See [[Remembrance]] and
[[Remember Me - Genesis 40.14 and the Prayers of the Condemned]].

**Whose faults, and against whom?** ***Ḥăṭāʾay*** is **the same root as 40:1**, where the two officers
*"offended their lord"*. **The natural reading is that he means his old offence against Pharaoh** — he is
raising, in a throne room, the episode that got him jailed, which requires a reason. **The other reading
is that he means his failure to speak for Joseph.** Genesis does not say, **and the ambiguity is
productive**: the word puts the two failures in the same vocabulary and leaves the reader to decide
whether the man knows what he is confessing. **The wiki records both and adopts neither.**

***"A young man, a Hebrew, servant to the captain of the guard."*** **Three descriptions, and all three
are disqualifications.** ***Naʿar*** — a youth, a subordinate. ***ʿIbrî*** — **the word
[[Potiphar's Wife]] used as a slur at 39:14 and 39:17**, and which Joseph had turned into the name of a
country at 40:15. ***ʿEbed*** — **a slave, and the property of a named official.** **The cupbearer does
not say his name.** He is introduced to Pharaoh as a category three times over, **and he has been in
Egypt thirteen years.**

***"As he interpreted to us, so it was."*** **This is 40:22's *"as Joseph had interpreted to them"*
repeated in the first person**, and it is **the only testimonial Joseph ever receives in Genesis.**
**It is offered by the man who owed it two years earlier**, in a sentence whose second half is
***"and he hanged him"*** — **the cupbearer's evidence for the interpreter's reliability is the corpse of
his colleague.**

### 41:14–16 — out of the pit, shaved, and the disclaimer repeated

> **14** Then Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and they brought him hastily out of the dungeon. He shaved
> himself, changed his clothing, and came in to Pharaoh.
> **15** Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I have dreamed a dream, and there is no one who can interpret it. I
> have heard it said of you, that when you hear a dream you can interpret it.”
> **16** Joseph answered Pharaoh, saying, “It isn’t in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace.”

***"Out of the dungeon."*** ***Min-habbôr*** — ***the pit*, for the third and last time.** 37:24's
waterless cistern, 40:15's *"they should put me into the dungeon"*, and **this verse, which is the only
one of the three in which he is coming out.** **The WEB renders the same Hebrew word *pit* once and
*dungeon* twice**; the Hebrew does not change. **Joseph's thirteen years have one name in this book and
his brothers gave it to him.**

***"They brought him hastily."*** ***Wayərîṣuhû*** — **they made him run.** After two years in which
nothing happened, **the chapter's pace changes inside a single verb**, and the man who is late by two
years is now required to hurry.

***"He shaved himself, changed his clothing."*** **The third garment change in Joseph's story, and the
first one he performs himself.** His brothers stripped the *kətōnet passîm* (37:23); he left the *beged*
in [[Potiphar's Wife]]'s hand and ran (39:12); **here he takes off the prison clothes and puts on
something fit for a court.** See [[A Garment Used as Evidence - The Coat and the Cloak]] and
[[Nakedness and Covering]]. **And the shaving is not grooming, it is assimilation** — a Semite entering
an Egyptian presence has to stop looking like a Semite. **Verse 45 will finish the job with a name and a
wife.**

***"It isn't in me."*** ***Bilʿāday*** — **literally *apart from me*, and it is 40:8 said again in a
palace.** **The construction is a denial that the faculty is his**, not a modest preface: *God* is the
subject of the clause that follows, and *Pharaoh* is its indirect object. **Joseph tells a king to his
face that the answer is coming from somewhere else.**

***"God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace."*** ***ʾĔlōhîm yaʿăneh ʾet-šəlôm Parʿōh*** — **and the
sentence is odd in Hebrew**: God will answer *the peace of Pharaoh*. **The promise is not that the answer
will be good news** — Joseph is about to announce seven years of famine — **but that it will be an answer
that leaves Pharaoh whole.** *Šālôm* here is nearer to *welfare* than to *good tidings*.

**And he says *ʾĕlōhîm*.** **Joseph never speaks the covenant name to an Egyptian anywhere in Genesis** —
not to [[Potiphar's Wife]] at 39:9, not to two prisoners at 40:8, not to a king here. **The narrator, who
spent eight *Yahweh*s on a slave in chapter 39, does not supply it either.**

### 41:17–24 — the dreamer tells it his own way

> **17** Pharaoh spoke to Joseph, “In my dream, behold, I stood on the brink of the river:
> **18** and behold, there came up out of the river seven cattle, fat and sleek. They fed in the marsh
> grass,
> **19** and behold, seven other cattle came up after them, poor and very ugly and thin, such as I never
> saw in all the land of Egypt for ugliness.
> **20** The thin and ugly cattle ate up the first seven fat cattle,
> **21** and when they had eaten them up, it couldn’t be known that they had eaten them, but they were
> still ugly, as at the beginning. So I awoke.
> **22** I saw in my dream, and behold, seven heads of grain came up on one stalk, full and good:
> **23** and behold, seven heads of grain, withered, thin, and blasted with the east wind, sprung up
> after them.
> **24** The thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven good heads of grain. I told it to the magicians,
> but there was no one who could explain it to me.”

***"Such as I never saw in all the land of Egypt for ugliness."*** **Pharaoh editorialises.** The
narrator said *"ugly and thin"*; **the king adds a superlative, a comparison, and a territory** — and the
territory is the one about to starve. **Nothing in the retelling is closer to the interpretation than
this clause**, and it is the dreamer's own embellishment.

***"It couldn't be known that they had eaten them."*** ***Wəlōʾ nôdaʿ kî-bāʾû ʾel-qirbenāh*** — **the
detail the narrator withheld**, and **the one Joseph will quote back at 41:31.** The famine will not
merely follow the plenty; **it will consume the plenty without showing any sign of having eaten.**
**The dreamer has been carrying the interpretation's decisive clause for two nights and hands it over as
an aside.**

***"But they were still ugly, as at the beginning."*** ***Ûmarʾêhen raʿ kaʾăšer battəḥillâ*** — the
horror of the image is that **the meal changed nothing.** Seven fat years go in and the country looks
exactly as hungry as before. **It is the most precise description of a subsistence economy anywhere in
Genesis.**

***"There was no one who could explain it to me."*** ***Maggîd*** — **and Pharaoh has changed the verb.**
Verse 8 said no one could *interpret* (*pōtēr*); **Pharaoh says no one could *tell* him** — *nāgad*, the
ordinary verb for declaring. **The king does not use the technical term**, which is a small sign that
the chapter's specialist vocabulary belongs to Joseph and the narrator rather than to the court.

### 41:25–32 — one dream, seven years, and why it came twice

> **25** Joseph said to Pharaoh, “The dream of Pharaoh is one. What God is about to do he has declared to
> Pharaoh.
> **26** The seven good cattle are seven years; and the seven good heads of grain are seven years. The
> dream is one.
> **27** The seven thin and ugly cattle that came up after them are seven years, and also the seven empty
> heads of grain blasted with the east wind; they will be seven years of famine.
> **28** That is the thing which I spoke to Pharaoh. What God is about to do he has shown to Pharaoh.
> **29** Behold, there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt.
> **30** There will arise after them seven years of famine, and all the plenty will be forgotten in the
> land of Egypt. The famine will consume the land,
> **31** and the plenty will not be known in the land by reason of that famine which follows; for it will
> be very grievous.
> **32** The dream was doubled to Pharaoh, because the thing is established by God, and God will shortly
> bring it to pass.

***"The dream of Pharaoh is one."*** ***Ḥălôm Parʿōh ʾeḥād hûʾ*** — **and he says it twice, at 25 and at
26.** **The first move of the interpretation is to refuse the premise of the question**: Pharaoh came
with two dreams; Joseph tells him he has one. **The magicians were not given the wrong answer, they were
given the wrong number of problems.**

***"What God is about to do he has declared to Pharaoh."*** **Said twice in four verses** (25, 28), and
**both times the object of the revelation is Pharaoh himself.** **Joseph does not tell a foreign king
that the God of Abraham has spoken to a Hebrew about Egypt**; he tells him **God has spoken to *him*, in
his own bed, in his own imagery.** See [[The Nations]].

***"The plenty will be forgotten."*** ***Wəniškaḥ*** — ***šākaḥ***, **the verb of 40:23**, *"but forgot
him."* **Genesis uses the chapter's governing word for what famine does to abundance**, and it is the
same word for what a courtier did to a prisoner. **Forgetting is this cycle's standing form of loss.**

***"The plenty will not be known in the land."*** ***Lōʾ-yiwwādaʿ*** — **41:21 returned to its owner.**
See the summary table above: **this is the interpretation's load-bearing clause and Pharaoh supplied the
words for it.**

***"The dream was doubled to Pharaoh, because the thing is established by God."*** **The Joseph cycle
explaining its own device, in one verse, at the third of its three dream-pairs.** Joseph had two dreams
(37:5–11), the cupbearer and baker had two (40:5), **and only now does anyone say why.** ***Nākôn***,
*established, fixed, made firm*, **and *māhar*, *shortly* — the doubling certifies both the certainty and
the imminence.** See
[[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]].

**It is also, quietly, a claim about Joseph's own two dreams.** **If doubling means God has fixed the
thing, then the sheaves and the stars were fixed too** — and the men who swore to disprove them
(37:20) were arguing with something already settled. **Joseph does not make that application and the
narrator does not make it for him.**

### 41:33–36 — the paragraph nobody asked for

> **33** “Now therefore let Pharaoh look for a discreet and wise man, and set him over the land of Egypt.
> **34** Let Pharaoh do this, and let him appoint overseers over the land, and take up the fifth part of
> the land of Egypt’s produce in the seven plenteous years.
> **35** Let them gather all the food of these good years that come, and lay up grain under the hand of
> Pharaoh for food in the cities, and let them keep it.
> **36** The food will be for a store to the land against the seven years of famine, which will be in the
> land of Egypt; that the land not perish through the famine.”

***"Now therefore."*** ***Wəʿattâ*** — **the chapter's turn, and the point at which Joseph stops doing
what he was fetched for.** **He was summoned to interpret and the interpretation ended at verse 32.**
Verses 33–36 are **policy advice from a foreign slave to a head of state who did not ask for any**, and
**Genesis attaches no divine warrant to them at all**: no *God has shown*, no *thus says*, nothing. **The
four verses that change his life are the four in which he is speaking entirely for himself.**

**Is he angling for the job?** **It is the oldest question about the chapter and the text does not
answer it.**

- **The suspicious reading**: *"let Pharaoh look for a discreet and wise man"* is a job description, and
  Pharaoh fills it with the man who wrote it four verses later using **Joseph's own two adjectives**
  (41:39: *"there is no one so discreet and wise as you"*). **A prisoner who has spent two years waiting
  for someone to mention his name does not waste an audience.**
- **The reading the text supports as easily**: **a true prediction of a seven-year famine is useless
  without a policy**, the advice is sound, it is addressed to *Pharaoh* and not to Joseph's advantage in
  any stated way, and **it names no candidate.** Joseph's stated interest in chapter 40 was to *"bring me
  out of this house"*, not to run a country.

**The wiki sets out both and adopts neither**, consistent with its posture on *did Potiphar believe his
wife?* and *is 40:14 a failure of faith?*

***"A discreet and wise man."*** ***ʾÎš nābôn wəḥākām*** — **the standard Israelite wisdom pair**, and
**it is being recommended as a qualification for high office in Egypt.** The same two words describe
Solomon's gift at 1 Kings 3:12 and are what Moses asks of the tribes at Deuteronomy 1:13. **Joseph is
Scripture's first wise courtier and the pattern every later one is cut from.** See [[Wisdom]].

***"Overseers."*** ***Pəqidim***, from ***pāqad*** — **the verb this wiki has tracked since 21:1**,
*visited, mustered, appointed, put in charge*. It was God visiting Sarah, it was the captain of the guard
assigning prisoners at 40:4, **and here it is a civil service.** See
[[Yahweh Visited Sarah - Paqad and the Visitations of God]].

***"Take up the fifth part."*** ***Wəḥimmēš*** — **a verb coined from the number five**, and **it is not
used this way anywhere else in the Hebrew Bible.** **Twenty per cent is a tax, and a steep one** —
the ordinary rate is not stated anywhere in Genesis, but **47:24–26 makes this same fifth the permanent
law of Egypt after the famine**, which is the chapter's long shadow. **Genesis reports the whole
programme as competent administration and passes no judgment on it** — see [[Wealth and Possessions]]
and [[Kingship and Empire]]. **A reader who finds 47:13–26 troubling should notice that it begins
here**, in advice given before Joseph had any power at all.

***"That the land not perish through the famine."*** ***Wəlōʾ-tikkārēt hāʾāreṣ*** — **literally *that the
land not be cut off*.** ***Kārat*** is **the covenant verb** — *to cut* a covenant — **and its passive is
the standard term for a line being extinguished.** **The stated purpose of the policy is the survival of
a country that is not the covenant country**, and **the man proposing it has no idea yet that it will
also be the survival of his father's house.** 45:7 and 50:20 are where he says what it was for.

### 41:37–45 — a slave is dressed as a king's deputy

> **37** The thing was good in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in the eyes of all his servants.
> **38** Pharaoh said to his servants, “Can we find such a one as this, a man in whom is the Spirit of
> God?”
> **39** Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Because God has shown you all of this, there is no one so discreet and
> wise as you.
> **40** You shall be over my house, and according to your word will all my people be ruled. Only in the
> throne I will be greater than you.”
> **41** Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Behold, I have set you over all the land of Egypt.”
> **42** Pharaoh took off his signet ring from his hand, and put it on Joseph’s hand, and arrayed him in
> robes of fine linen, and put a gold chain about his neck,
> **43** and he made him to ride in the second chariot which he had. They cried before him, “Bow the
> knee!” He set him over all the land of Egypt.
> **44** Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I am Pharaoh, and without you shall no man lift up his hand or his foot
> in all the land of Egypt.”
> **45** Pharaoh called Joseph’s name Zaphenath-Paneah; and he gave him Asenath, the daughter of
> Potiphera priest of On as a wife. Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.

***"A man in whom is the Spirit of God."*** ***ʾÎš ʾăšer rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm bô*** — **and this is the highest
thing anyone says about anyone in the book of Genesis.**

**Three things about it.**

1. ***Rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* has occurred once before in Genesis**, at 1:2, *"God's Spirit was hovering over the
   surface of the waters"* — **over an unformed world.** **Its second occurrence is a person**, and see
   [[The Creation of the World]] for the first.
2. **It is the first time in Scripture that the phrase is used of a human being**, and the people it is
   used of afterwards are a short list: **Bezalel**, filled with the Spirit of God for the tabernacle
   (Exodus 31:3; 35:31), **Joshua** (Numbers 27:18), **Balaam** (Numbers 24:2), and **Daniel**, of whom
   a Babylonian king says almost exactly this sentence (Daniel 4:8; 5:11, 14). See
   [[A Man in Whom Is the Spirit of God]].
3. **A pagan king says it.** **Joseph does not claim the Spirit; Pharaoh attributes it to him** — and
   **Pharaoh, not Joseph, is the character in this chapter who draws the theological conclusion.**
   Genesis has a habit of putting its clearest confessions in foreign mouths:
   [[Melchizedek]] at 14:19, [[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]] at 20:4–5, [[Laban]] at 30:27. **This is
   the strongest of them.**

***"Only in the throne I will be greater than you."*** **A ruler in the Ancient Near East does not say
this.** **Genesis has no interest in making Pharaoh reluctant** — the elevation is instant, total, and
apparently unopposed: *"in the eyes of **all his servants**"* (41:37). **The one clause reserved is the
throne**, and Joseph never approaches it.

***"According to your word will all my people be ruled."*** ***ʿAl-pîkā yiššaq kol-ʿammî*** — **literally
*by your mouth shall all my people ——*, and the verb is a crux.** ***Nāšaq*** ordinarily means **to
kiss**; the WEB's *"be ruled"* follows a proposed second root meaning *to be equipped* or *to order
themselves*. **Translations have been giving it different verbs for two thousand years** and Genesis
gives no help. **What is not in doubt is *ʿal-pîkā*, *at your mouth*** — the same idiom Joseph has just
earned, **a man whose authority is that his words come true.**

***"His signet ring … robes of fine linen … a gold chain."*** **Three items of investiture, and all
three are attested Egyptian practice** — the gold collar (*šbyw*) awarded for service is on tomb walls,
and the transfer of a seal is the transfer of the power to sign. **The identical three-part ceremony
appears twice more in Scripture, both times for a Jew at a foreign court**: Mordecai at Esther 6:11 and
8:2, 15 — **where the king's signet passes to him in almost these words** — and Daniel at Daniel 5:29.
See [[The Signet Ring and the Second Chariot - Joseph, Mordecai, and Daniel]].

***"The second chariot."*** ***Mirkebet hammišneh*** — **the vehicle of the second man**, and *mišneh*
is the word that will be used of Mordecai as *second to king Ahasuerus* (Esther 10:3). **Chariots put
this scene in the Second Intermediate Period or later**, since the chariot reaches Egypt with the Hyksos;
**Genesis supplies no date and the wiki declines to import one** (see
[[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]]).

***"Bow the knee!"*** ***ʾAbrēk*** — **a word that occurs once in the Bible and is not translated so much
as guessed at.** **Whatever it means, the scene means one thing**: **Egypt is made to bow to Joseph
before his brothers do.** The dream of 37:7 had sheaves bowing and the dream of 37:9 had the sun, moon
and eleven stars; **the first fulfilment of either is a crowd of Egyptians shouting an untranslatable
word at a chariot.**

***"Zaphenath-Paneah."*** ***Ṣāpənat Paʿnēaḥ*** — **an Egyptian name, most often explained as
*ḏd-pȝ-nṯr-iw.f-ʿnḫ*, *the god speaks and he lives*.** **Joseph is renamed by the power that owns him**,
which is what happens to Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah at Daniel 1:7, to Eliakim at
2 Kings 23:34, and to Mattaniah at 2 Kings 24:17. **Genesis then never uses the name again** — **not
once, not even in Pharaoh's mouth** — and the narrator goes on calling him Joseph for nine more chapters.
See [[Zaphenath-Paneah - Renaming at a Foreign Court]] and [[Names and Naming]].

***"Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera priest of On."*** **An Egyptian wife, a priest's daughter, and a
priest of the sun cult at that.** **[[On]]** is **Heliopolis**, the centre of the worship of Ra.

**And Genesis says nothing about it.** **This is the book that made Abraham swear an oath about Isaac's
wife** (24:3), **that records Esau's Hittite marriages as a grief to his parents** (26:35), **and that
has Isaac repeat the charge to Jacob almost word for word** (28:1). **Joseph marries into an Egyptian
priesthood and the narrator does not pause.** **No comment, no defence, no note that it was arranged
rather than chosen.** See [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]], [[Marriage]], and [[Idolatry]].

**The silence is the observation.** It is not a licence and it is not a condemnation; **it is a gap
where the reader expects the book's own standing concern, and the wiki records it as a gap.**

### 41:46–52 — thirty years old, and two sons named for what he lost

> **46** Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh king of Egypt. Joseph went out from the
> presence of Pharaoh, and went throughout all the land of Egypt.
> **47** In the seven plenteous years the earth produced abundantly.
> **48** He gathered up all the food of the seven years which were in the land of Egypt, and laid up the
> food in the cities: the food of the field, which was around every city, he laid up in the same.
> **49** Joseph laid up grain as the sand of the sea, very much, until he stopped counting, for it was
> without number.
> **50** To Joseph were born two sons before the year of famine came, whom Asenath, the daughter of
> Potiphera priest of On, bore to him.
> **51** Joseph called the name of the firstborn Manasseh, “For”, he said, “God has made me forget all my
> toil, and all my father’s house.”
> **52** The name of the second, he called Ephraim: “For God has made me fruitful in the land of my
> affliction.”

***"Joseph was thirty years old."*** **He was seventeen at 37:2.** **Genesis gives the reader the
arithmetic and never does it aloud: thirteen years** — a slave, a convict, and a prisoner — **between the
coat and the chariot.** **Nothing in the book fills them except two chapters.** The book's other precise
figures for this stretch come later: seven years of plenty and two of famine before the brothers arrive
(45:6), and Jacob's arrival when Joseph is thirty-nine.

***"Grain as the sand of the sea."*** ***Kəḥôl hayyām*** — **and this is a covenant phrase.** *"I will
multiply your offspring as the stars of the sky, and as the sand which is on the seashore"* — the oath at
[[The Binding of Isaac]] (22:17), repeated to Jacob at 32:12. **Genesis has used *sand of the sea* three
times of the promised offspring and uses it here of Egyptian grain**, in a chapter with no promise in it.
See [[Dust, Stars, and Sand - The Offspring Promises]] and [[Blessing]].

***"Until he stopped counting, for it was without number."*** ***ʿAd kî-ḥādal lispōr kî-ʾên mispār*** —
**the same idiom, doubled.** **The man who will one day count out grain to his own brothers stops
counting it here.**

***"Manasseh … God has made me forget all my toil, and all my father's house."*** ***Mənaššeh***, from
***nāšâ***, *to forget* — **and it is the hardest sentence Joseph speaks in Genesis.**

**He names his firstborn son for the erasure of his family.** Not for his deliverance, not for his
office, not for the God who gave him either — **but for the fact that he no longer remembers the house
he was stolen from.** And ***ʿămālî***, *my toil*, **is the same category**: the thirteen years and the
father are put in one clause and forgotten together.

**Three things sit against it and Genesis reconciles none of them.**

1. **He thanks God for the forgetting.** *"God has made me forget"* is a piel with God as subject; **it
   is offered as a mercy.**
2. **He has not forgotten.** At 42:9 *"Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed about them"*; at
   43:27 he asks after *"your father, the old man of whom you spoke"*; at 45:3 the first thing he says
   after disclosing himself is ***"Does my father still live?"*** **The name is falsified by the man who
   gave it, three chapters later.**
3. **He never sends word.** **He is the second most powerful man in Egypt for seven years and Canaan is
   a nine-day journey**, and Genesis records no message. **This is the cycle's largest unexplained
   silence** and the text offers no motive for it — not protection, not resentment, not obedience to a
   plan. **The wiki flags it and proposes nothing**; see [[Joseph]].

***"Ephraim … God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction."*** ***ʾEprayim***, from ***pārâ***,
*to be fruitful* — **the verb of 1:22 and 1:28, and of the promise to Abraham at 17:6 and to Jacob at
35:11.** **Joseph applies the creation-and-covenant word to two sons born to an Egyptian mother in a
country he has just called *the land of my affliction*.**

***ʿOnyî*** — **and that is the chapter's last word on Egypt from the inside.** **He says it at the peak
of his power**, with a new name, a wife, a chariot and a country, **and he still calls the place an
affliction.** **The root is *ʿānâ*, and it is the word the Exodus is built on**: *"I have surely seen the
affliction of my people who are in Egypt"* (Exodus 3:7), **and the same root at 15:13, where Abram was
told his offspring would be afflicted in a land not theirs for four hundred years.** See
[[Suffering]], [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]], and
[[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].

**The two names together are the whole of Joseph's inner life as Genesis reports it**: **one son for what
was taken and one for what was given, and both speeches make God the agent.** They are also **the only
naming speeches in Genesis given by a father** rather than a mother, apart from Jacob's overruling of
[[Rachel]] at 35:18 and Lamech's at 5:29. See [[Names and Naming]].

### 41:53–57 — the plenty ends and the world arrives

> **53** The seven years of plenty, that were in the land of Egypt, came to an end.
> **54** The seven years of famine began to come, just as Joseph had said. There was famine in all lands,
> but in all the land of Egypt there was bread.
> **55** When all the land of Egypt was famished, the people cried to Pharaoh for bread, and Pharaoh said
> to all the Egyptians, “Go to Joseph. What he says to you, do.”
> **56** The famine was over all the surface of the earth. Joseph opened all the store houses, and sold
> to the Egyptians. The famine was severe in the land of Egypt.
> **57** All countries came into Egypt, to Joseph, to buy grain, because the famine was severe in all the
> earth.

***"Just as Joseph had said."*** ***Kaʾăšer ʾāmar Yôsēp*** — **the chapter's verdict on him, one clause
long, exactly as 40:22 was.** **Genesis authenticates its prophets by reporting the weather.**

***"There was famine in all lands, but in all the land of Egypt there was bread."*** **The sentence that
makes the rest of the book possible.** Egypt has food when everyone else does not — **which is what Egypt
was, geographically and permanently**, and which is why [[Abraham]] went down at 12:10 and why
[[Isaac]] was forbidden to at 26:2. See [[Egypt]] and
[[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].

***"The people cried to Pharaoh for bread."*** ***Wayyiṣʿaq*** — **the cry-verb.** It is what Esau does
at 27:34, what Sodom's outcry is at 18:20–21, **and what Israel will do in Egypt at Exodus 2:23**, where
the same root brings God down. **Here it brings a referral.**

***"Go to Joseph. What he says to you, do."*** ***Ləkû ʾel-Yôsēp ʾăšer-yōʾmar lākem taʿăśû*** — **a king
directing his own starving people to a foreigner he bought out of a prison.** **It is the completion of
41:40: *according to your word*.**

**The Greek of this clause and the Greek of John 2:5 are all but identical** — Mary at Cana: *"Whatever
he says to you, do it."* **The Septuagint has *ho eàn eípē hymîn poiḗsate* and John has *hó ti àn légē
hymîn poiḗsate*.** **The wiki records the echo and claims nothing from it**: it is a natural sentence in
Greek, the contexts share only a shortage and a direction to obey, **and no New Testament writer draws
the link.** Noted here rather than made into a connection page — see the Notes below.

***"All countries came into Egypt, to Joseph, to buy grain."*** ***Wəkol-hāʾāreṣ bāʾû Miṣraymâ*** —
**and the last two words of the chapter's action are the first two of the next.** 42:1 has Jacob see that
there is grain in Egypt and send ten sons. **The men who sold a brother to a caravan going to Egypt will
now go to Egypt to buy from him**, and **the sheaves of 37:7 bow in 42:6.** See
[[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]].

## People

- [[Joseph]] — **the chapter in which everything reverses and he says less about it than his employer
  does.** He comes out of the *bôr* for the last time, shaves off his nationality, **repeats 40:8's
  disclaimer to a king** (*"It isn't in me"*), reads one dream out of two, **quotes the dreamer's own
  added clause back to him**, explains why dreams double — **and then gives four verses of policy advice
  nobody asked for**, which is what gets him the country. He is **thirty years old, thirteen years after
  the coat**; he takes an Egyptian name he is never called by, an Egyptian wife, and a fifth of the
  harvest; and **he names his sons for forgetting his father's house and for being made fruitful in
  *the land of my affliction*.** **He does not send word to Canaan, and Genesis never says why**
- [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] — **the chapter in which he becomes a character, and he is the most
  attractive foreign ruler in Genesis.** He dreams, is troubled, summons his whole professional
  establishment, listens to a cupbearer's confession, sends for a prisoner, **retells his own dream with
  two details the narrator omitted**, hears seven years of famine announced, and **hands the country to
  the man who announced it before the day is out.** He says ***"a man in whom is the Spirit of God"*** —
  **the highest confession in the book, and a pagan king makes it** — and *"only in the throne I will be
  greater than you."* **He is never deceived, never punished, and never asked for anything**
- [[The Chief Cupbearer]] — **he keeps his word two years late and in a self-serving frame.** *"I
  remember my faults today"* uses the causative of the very verb Joseph gave him at 40:14, **with his own
  offences as its object rather than Joseph.** He names Joseph to Pharaoh as ***a young man, a Hebrew, a
  slave*** — **three disqualifications and no name** — and his testimonial's second half is *"and he
  hanged him."* **He is the only person in Genesis who moves Joseph from the prison to the throne room**,
  and Scripture never mentions him again
- [[The Chief Baker]] — **present only as the other half of the evidence** (41:13). **The demonstration
  that persuades a king depends on the fact that one of the two interpretations was fatal and came
  true**
- [[Potiphar]] — **unnamed, and referred to twice by office**: the cupbearer's *"the house of the captain
  of the guard"* (41:10) and *"servant to the captain of the guard"* (41:12). **The last mention of him
  in Genesis is a description of Joseph as his property**, in the sentence that ends Joseph's slavery.
  His name and that of Joseph's father-in-law are **the same Egyptian name in two forms** — see
  [[Potiphera]]
- [[Asenath]] — **new page.** Daughter of a priest of the sun cult at [[On]], given to Joseph by Pharaoh,
  **the mother of two tribes of Israel**, and **the only woman in the chapter.** She has no speech, no
  age, no consent recorded, and **her name is very likely *she belongs to Neith*, after an Egyptian
  goddess.** **Genesis offers no comment on the marriage whatever**
- [[Potiphera]] — **new page.** *Priest of On*, which is Heliopolis, **the centre of the worship of Ra**.
  His name means ***he whom Ra has given*** and is the full form of [[Potiphar]]'s. **Joseph's
  father-in-law is an Egyptian priest and the book does not flinch**
- [[Manasseh]] — **new page.** The firstborn, named ***God has made me forget all my toil, and all my
  father's house*** — **a thanksgiving for amnesia, given by a man who demonstrably has not forgotten.**
  Adopted by Jacob as his own at 48:5, and **displaced by his younger brother in the same scene**
- [[Ephraim]] — **new page.** The second son, named ***God has made me fruitful in the land of my
  affliction*** — **the creation verb *pārâ* applied to two half-Egyptian boys**, in a sentence that
  calls Joseph's kingdom an affliction. **He receives the right hand at 48:14** and becomes the name of
  the northern kingdom
- **Egypt's magicians and wise men** — **no page**, per the standing practice on unnamed functionaries
  with no recorded speech (the keeper of the prison, the midwife of 35:17). **They are a plural
  institution and the chapter's use for them is a blank**: summoned in full, given two plain dreams from
  their own agriculture, and unable to say anything. See
  [[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]]
- [[God]] — ***ʾĕlōhîm* nine times, every one of them inside quotation marks.** He does not speak,
  appear, or act on the page; **the narrator does not mention him once.** Seven of the nine are Joseph's
  and two are Pharaoh's, **and between them they credit him with the dream, its meaning, its certainty,
  its timing, the interpreter's gift, and both of the children.** **Everything in the chapter is claimed
  for God by somebody and narrated by nobody**
- [[Jacob]] — **named only inside his son's grief**, as *"all my father's house"* (41:51), **the thing
  Joseph thanks God for forgetting.** He does not know his son is alive and will not for seven more years

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — **the chapter is Scripture's closest look at how the country actually worked**: the
  inundation, the sirocco, a dream-interpretation profession with state standing, a shaved court, linen,
  a signet, chariots, granary cities, and a twenty per cent levy. **It is also the chapter in which Egypt
  becomes the world's granary** — *"there was famine in all lands, but in all the land of Egypt there was
  bread"* (41:54) — **which is the condition every subsequent chapter of Genesis depends on.** The name
  occurs **fourteen times**
- [[The Nile]] — **new page.** ***Hayʾōr***, an Egyptian loanword entering Scripture at 41:1 and
  **the word the Bible uses for the Nile ever after.** Both dreams begin with it: **the fat cattle and
  the lean both come up out of the river**, which is a precise image of a country whose entire food
  supply is one annual flood
- [[On]] — **new page.** **Heliopolis**, the ancient centre of the sun cult of Ra, and **the home of
  Joseph's wife and father-in-law.** It appears three times in Genesis (41:45, 50; 46:20) and **the
  prophets return to it to announce its destruction**

## Themes

- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — **the cycle's third and last dream pair, and the only one whose device is
  explained**: *"the dream was doubled to Pharaoh, because the thing is established by God"* (41:32).
  **The interpretation is disclaimed in the sentence before it is delivered** (41:16 = 40:8 in a throne
  room), **the dream is told twice and differently**, and **the narrator never once attributes the dream
  to God** — every attribution in the chapter is in a character's mouth
- [[Famine and Plenty]] — **new page.** Genesis's third famine and the one that organises the rest of
  the book. **Seven years of abundance are consumed so completely that *"it couldn't be known that they
  had eaten them"***, and the remedy is **storage, a levy, and a bureaucracy** — the Bible's first
  instance of a state feeding a population out of a plan
- [[Wisdom]] — ***nābôn wəḥākām***, the Israelite wisdom pair, **recommended as a civil service
  qualification and then applied to a foreign slave by a king** (41:33, 39). **Egypt's entire
  professional establishment is summoned and produces nothing**, which is the chapter's claim about where
  wisdom comes from. **Joseph is the first of Scripture's wise men at a foreign court**
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — **an investiture scene in full**: signet, linen, gold chain, second chariot,
  a herald, a throne-name, a dynastic marriage, and *"only in the throne I will be greater than you."*
  **And a twenty per cent levy proposed by the man who will collect it**, which 47:20–26 makes permanent
- [[Names and Naming]] — **three namings in one chapter, which is more than any other chapter in
  Genesis**: [[Zaphenath-Paneah - Renaming at a Foreign Court]], **a name Genesis never uses again**;
  [[Manasseh]], *forgetting*; and [[Ephraim]], *fruitfulness*. **Two of the three are etymologised in the
  text and the Egyptian one is not**
- [[Remembrance]] — **the answer to 40:23, and it is grudging.** *"I remember my faults today"* (41:9)
  puts *zākar* in the causative with the speaker's own offences as its object. **And the chapter's other
  memory verb is *šākaḥ*, twice**: the plenty *"will be forgotten"* (41:30) and Joseph thanks God for
  making him forget his father's house (41:51)
- [[The Nations]] — **a pagan king makes the strongest theological confession in Genesis**
  (*"a man in whom is the Spirit of God"*, 41:38), **an Egyptian court receives a true revelation in its
  sleep**, and **all countries come to a foreigner for bread** (41:57). **The blessing of 12:3 operating
  before anyone in the chapter knows it is a promise**
- [[Favor and Grace]] — ***ḥēn* is not in this chapter**, which is worth noting: the formula of 39:4 and
  39:21 does not recur. **What replaces it is Pharaoh's own judgment** — *"the thing was good in the eyes
  of Pharaoh"* — and the credit Pharaoh gives is to God, not to Joseph's charm
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — **a slave is introduced to a king as *"a young man, a Hebrew, servant to
  the captain of the guard"*** (41:12) **and is running the country forty verses later.** Genesis never
  says he was manumitted; **it says he was promoted**
- [[Work]] — **the longest description of administrative labour in the Torah.** He *"went throughout all
  the land"*, gathered, laid up city by city, and **stopped counting**. The competence is the point:
  **the chapter's proof that the interpretation was true is seven years of successful logistics**
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — ***grain as the sand of the sea***, the covenant simile for offspring,
  spent on an Egyptian harvest; **and a fifth of a nation's produce transferred to the crown by a policy
  its beneficiary proposed.** Genesis reports the whole thing as good government
- [[Hope]] — **two full years, measured to the day, between a correct prediction and any consequence
  from it** (40:23; 41:1). **The chapter opens on the interval and never explains it**
- [[Suffering]] — ***"the land of my affliction"*** (41:52), **said by the second man in Egypt, with a
  wife, a chariot and a country.** **The root is the Exodus's own** (Exodus 3:7; Genesis 15:13)
- [[Marriage]] — **Joseph is given a wife by his employer**, a priest's daughter of a foreign cult, **and
  Genesis records no negotiation, no consent, no bride-price and no objection** — the book that spent a
  whole chapter on finding a wife for Isaac disposes of this one in half a verse
- [[Priesthood]] — **the Bible's first named foreign priesthood outside [[Melchizedek]]**, and **Joseph
  marries into it.** [[Potiphera]]'s office at [[On]] is stated flatly, twice (41:45, 50), with no
  qualification of any kind
- [[Idolatry]] — **the theme's loudest silence.** The chapter names the cult centre of Ra, makes a priest
  of it Joseph's father-in-law, and gives Joseph a throne-name that probably contains a god. **Genesis
  does not comment on any of it** — and the same book condemned Esau's Hittite wives in a clause
- [[Life and Death]] — ***"that the land not perish through the famine"*** (41:36), ***kārat***, *be cut
  off* — **the covenant verb used of a nation outside the covenant.** 45:5 and 50:20 will say what the
  policy was for
- [[Language and Speech]] — **a chapter built on words nobody can construe**: *ʾabrēk* (a hapax),
  *yiššaq* (a crux), *Ṣāpənat Paʿnēaḥ* (untranslated), and *ḥimmēš* (a verb coined from a numeral).
  **Genesis lets four Egyptian words stand in a Hebrew sentence without explaining any of them**
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — **the advocacy of 40:14 finally arrives**, from a man confessing a fault,
  in a speech that does not use Joseph's name. **And Joseph becomes the mediator himself** — *"Go to
  Joseph. What he says to you, do"* (41:55)
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — **a king who *"never saw"* anything so ugly in all Egypt** (41:19), **a
  thing *"good in the eyes of Pharaoh"*** (41:37), and **an eating that *"couldn't be known"*** (41:21).
  The chapter's verbs of perception carry its argument
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — **Egypt is *the land* forty times over in this chapter and Canaan is not
  named once.** The man who calls it *"the land of my affliction"* is its governor
- [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] — ***the firstborn*** is named at 41:51 and **the text will spend
  chapter 48 taking the position away from him.** [[Manasseh]] is introduced as the elder in the verse
  that makes the reversal possible
- [[Blessing]] — ***pārâ***, *made me fruitful* (41:52), **the verb of 1:22, 1:28, 17:6 and 35:11**,
  spoken over two sons of an Egyptian mother; and **grain *as the sand of the sea***, the offspring
  simile of 22:17, spent on a harvest

## Cross-references

- [[The Cupbearer and the Baker]] — Genesis 40:1–23; **the chapter this one completes in every
  particular.** 40:8's *"don't interpretations belong to God?"* becomes 41:16's *"it isn't in me"*;
  40:14's *zākar* becomes 41:9's *mazkîr*; 40:22's *"as Joseph had interpreted"* becomes 41:13's *"as he
  interpreted to us, so it was"*; **and the *bôr* of 40:15 is the *bôr* he is pulled out of at 41:14.**
  ***Pātar*** and ***pitrôn*** **finish their fourteen occurrences here and never appear again in the
  Hebrew Bible**
- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:2, 5–11; **the arithmetic** (seventeen there, thirty here),
  **the dreams that are now certified by 41:32's rule**, and ***"Bow the knee!"*** — **the first bowing
  the book delivers**, five years before the brothers do it
- [[Joseph in Potiphar's House]] — Genesis 39:1–23; **the divine-name count**, and the third and last of
  Joseph's garment changes. **The prospering formula of 39:2–6 and 39:21–23 is not used here**; the

## Notes

- **On the boundary, which was used as drawn.** The plan's box read *Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise —
  Genesis 41:1–57*, and **the chapter is sealed at both ends by a time marker**: 41:1's *"at the end of
  two full years"* and 42:1's shift of scene and cast to Jacob in Canaan. **A split at 41:36 or 41:45 was
  considered and rejected** — the interpretation and the elevation are one causal chain, and the narrative
  gives them no seam. **A merger with 42 was also rejected**: 41:57 and 42:1 report the same fact
  (the world comes to Egypt for grain) from two countries, and **the change of viewpoint is the
  boundary.**
- **On the title, kept from the plan.** *Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise* names both halves of the
  chapter and is where a reader would look. **Alternatives considered and rejected**: *Joseph Before
  Pharaoh* (covers verses 14–45 and drops the famine); *Seven Years of Plenty and Seven of Famine*
  (describes the dream's content, not the chapter's event); *Zaphenath-Paneah* (a name Genesis itself
  never uses again). **No other book has a passage page by this title**, so no `(Book)` qualifier is
  needed.
- **On what was created, and the judgment calls in it.**
  - **[[Asenath]], [[Potiphera]], [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]] all got pages** — **all four are named,
    which is the wiki's ordinary threshold**, and three of them recur (46:20; 48:1–20). Asenath is named
    twice in this chapter and again at 46:20.
  - **[[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]] are unqualified.** **Both names later belong to tribes, to a
    territory, and — for Manasseh — to a king of Judah**, so a collision is certain once the wiki reaches
    Kings. **It is not a collision yet**, and `AGENTS.md`'s rule is to qualify in the pass that creates
    the second page, not in anticipation. **Flagged here so the later pass knows to rename these two and
    fix their inbound links.**
  - **[[The Nile]] got a page.** ***Yeʾōr*** enters Scripture in this chapter and is the setting of both
    dreams; the wiki already gives pages to [[The Euphrates]] and [[The Jabbok]], **so a river that
    carries a national food supply and five occurrences in one chapter clears the bar easily.**
  - **[[On]] got a page** as a named settlement with a named institution, on the same footing as
    [[Enaim]] or [[Chezib]].
  - **[[Famine and Plenty]] is a new theme page**, and it is the one call here that could have gone the
    other way. **The wiki declined a `Dreams` page last pericope because [[Prophecy and Vision]] already
    held that material.** **Nothing holds famine**: it is mentioned in passing on
    [[Land and Sojourning]], [[Wealth and Possessions]], [[Work]], [[Blessing]], [[Priesthood]] and
    [[Favor and Grace]], **and owned by none of them** — and it is about to be the organising fact of
    nine consecutive chapters. **A page was created rather than a sixth passing mention added.**
  - **Four connection pages**, all of them threads that start or turn here rather than thematic
    groupings: *rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm* of a person, the foreign-court renaming, the investiture regalia, and
    *ḥarṭummîm*. **The last three all point the same way** — at Daniel and Esther — **and they are kept
    separate because they are three different kinds of evidence** (a name, a ceremony, a professional
    guild) and a reader chasing any one of them should not have to read the other two.
- **On what was deliberately not created.**
  - **No page for Egypt's magicians and wise men.** They are a plural institution with **no recorded
    speech and no individual named**, which is the standing test. The material is on
    [[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]].
  - **No connection page for John 2:5.** The Septuagint of 41:55 and the Greek of Mary's sentence at Cana
    are all but identical, **and the observation is recorded on this page** because a reader will notice
    it. **It was not made a page because there is no thread to trace**: the words are an unremarkable
    Greek sentence, the situations share only a shortage, **and no New Testament writer draws the link.**
    **This is the opposite call from the one made on
    [[The Two Birthdays in the Bible - Pharaoh's Feast and Herod's]]** last pericope, and the difference
    is that the two birthdays are **a closed set in the Hebrew Bible** — a reader can check the claim and
    find it exhaustive — whereas **a common Greek idiom is not a set at all.**
  - **No connection page for the Egyptian Famine Stela**, the Ptolemaic inscription on Sehel Island that
    recounts a seven-year famine under Djoser relieved by a royal decree. **The parallel is real and is
    worth knowing**, but it is **roughly fifteen hundred years later than any date proposed for Joseph**,
    it is itself a piece of temple propaganda about a much older king, and `AGENTS.md` defines a
    connection page as a link *within* Scripture. **Same reasoning as the Chester Beatty III dream
    papyrus last pericope and the *Tale of Two Brothers* before that.**
- **Ambiguities flagged and not resolved.**
  - **Is Joseph angling for the job in 41:33–36?** He appends unrequested policy to a commissioned
    interpretation, describes the ideal appointee in two words, and is appointed in those exact two words
    six verses later. **Against that**: a famine prediction without a policy is useless, he names no
    candidate, and nothing in the advice is to his stated advantage. **Genesis attaches no divine warrant
    to the paragraph and passes no verdict on it. Both readings set out; neither adopted.**
  - **Whose faults does the cupbearer mean at 41:9?** *Ḥăṭāʾay* is the root of the officers' offence at
    40:1, so the plain reading is his old offence against Pharaoh, raised to explain how he comes to know
    a prisoner. **The other reading is that he is confessing his two-year silence.** **The chapter lets
    both stand and the wiki adopts neither.**
  - **What does *ʾabrēk* mean?** Hebrew *bārak* (*kneel*), Egyptian *ib r.k* (*attention*), Akkadian
    *abarakku* (*steward*). **All three recorded, none adopted.** Likewise ***yiššaq*** at 41:40, where
    the WEB's *"be ruled"* is one of several defensible guesses at a verb that ordinarily means *kiss*.
  - **Why does Joseph not send word to Canaan?** **He has absolute authority over Egypt for seven years
    of plenty and Hebron is nine days away.** Genesis records no message, no attempt, and no reason —
    **and it does not present the silence as a problem either.** Proposed motives (protecting himself
    from his brothers, waiting for the dreams to fulfil themselves, a grief too large to reopen) are all
    imported. **The wiki records the silence as a silence.**
  - **Is Potiphera the same man as Potiphar?** **The names are the same Egyptian name in two forms**, and
    later Jewish tradition made the identification and gave Asenath to Potiphar's wife as a daughter.
    **Genesis offers not one word of support**: different spellings, different offices, different cities.
    **Recorded as a name coincidence; the identification is not adopted.**
