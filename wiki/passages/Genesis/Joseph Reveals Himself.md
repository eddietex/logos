# Joseph Reveals Himself

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 45:1–28

## Summary

**[[Joseph]] stops acting, names the crime once, and then spends four verses taking it away from the
men who committed it and giving it to God.**

The chapter has three movements and they are governed by three different people. **The first**
(45:1–15) is Joseph's: the room cleared, the disclosure, the providence speech, and the weeping that
finally has nobody to hide from. **The second** (45:16–24) is [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]]'s — he
hears the news secondhand, is pleased, and issues an invitation more generous than the one Joseph had
just issued, with wagons attached. **The third** (45:25–28) is [[Jacob]]'s, and it is eleven words of
disbelief followed by four of surrender.

**What the chapter does not contain is as load-bearing as what it does.** Joseph never says he
forgives. The brothers never say they are sorry — **they do not speak at all between 45:3 and 45:15,
and when they finally do speak, at 45:26, it is to their father and it is a half-report**: *"Joseph is
still alive, and he is ruler over all the land of Egypt."* **Neither clause is the one that matters,
and the one that matters waits until 50:15–17**, twenty-odd years later, when they are still afraid.

**God is named four times in twenty-eight verses and every one of them is in Joseph's mouth.** The
narrator, who has said nothing about God since 39:23, says nothing here either. **Genesis's most
explicit statement of providence is spoken by a character and left uncorroborated**, which is the
book's method rather than an omission. See [[Providence]].

### The verb that fails

***Wayyitʾappaq*** — *he forced himself* — **held at 43:31**, when Joseph washed his face and came
back out to serve lunch. **The first clause of this chapter is that verb negated.**

| | text | |
|---|---|---|
| **43:31** | *"He washed his face, and came out. He controlled himself"* | ***wayyitʾappaq*** |
| **45:1** | *"Then Joseph couldn't control himself"* | ***wəlōʾ-yākōl… ləhitʾappēq*** |

**The hithpael of *ʾpq* is a rare verb and Genesis uses it exactly twice, once in each direction.**
Elsewhere in Scripture it is what Saul does before offering the sacrifice he should not offer
(1 Samuel 13:12) and what Haman does walking past the man at the king's gate who will not bow
(Esther 5:10) — **a man holding down something that is going to come out anyway.** **What breaks it
here is not the cup, not the search, and not Benjamin. It is a speech about somebody else's
father.** See [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]].

### The two approaches, and the verb they share

***Wayyiggaš ʾēlāyw yəhûdâ*** — *"Then Judah came near to him"* (44:18) — **opened the longest speech
in Genesis.** ***Gəšû-nāʾ ʾēlay*** — *"Come near to me, please"* (45:4) — **is the same root turned
around**, and it is the first thing Joseph says after the brothers fail to answer him.

**The chapter's whole reversal is in those two verses.** At 44:18 one man crosses a floor toward a
throne, apologising in advance for the sound of his own voice. **At 45:4 the throne asks all eleven to
come closer, and then repeats the sentence they could not answer** — *"I am Joseph, your brother, whom
you sold into Egypt"* — **with the crime added.** He says it twice because the first version (45:3)
was a title and the second is a relationship with a history attached.

### Sent, three times, by someone who was not in the room

**The providence speech is four verses long and it is built by swapping one verb for another.**

> **4** …I am Joseph, your brother, **whom you sold** into Egypt. **5** Now don't be grieved, nor
> angry with yourselves, **that you sold me here**, for **God sent me** before you to preserve life.
> … **7** **God sent me** before you to preserve for you a remnant in the earth, and to save you
> alive by a great deliverance. **8** So now **it wasn't you who sent me here, but God**…

***Mākar***, *to sell*, **is what they did, and Joseph says it twice** (45:4, 5) **before he says
anything else about it.** ***Šālaḥ***, *to send*, **is what God did, and Joseph says it three times**
(45:5, 7, 8). **The second verb does not replace the first. It is laid over it**, and 45:8's
*"it wasn't you"* is a statement about agency and not about guilt — **the sale is still a sale, and
45:4 has already called it one.**

**Genesis 50:20 will say the same thing without the softening**: *"You meant evil against me, but God
meant it for good."* **There the two intentions are both named and both stand.** Here the emphasis is
pastoral rather than forensic — *"don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves"* — **which is why 45:5
is the more comforting verse and 50:20 is the more honest one.** See
[[God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale]] and
[[Am I in God's Place - Jacob at 30.2 and Joseph at 50.19]].

**The same English verb carries three more times in the chapter, and Joseph is the subject of all
three**: he *sent* provisions to his father (45:23), *sent* his brothers away (45:24), and had *sent*
the wagons (45:27). **A man who spends four verses saying he was sent spends the rest of the chapter
sending.**

### A remnant, and the word the prophets will take

***Šəʾērît*** — *"a remnant in the earth"* (45:7) — **appears here for the first time in Scripture**,
and it appears in a sentence about a famine and a family of seventy. **Every later use of it is about
what survives a judgment**: Isaiah's *"a remnant will return"* (10:21–22), Amos's *"it may be that
Yahweh… will be gracious to the remnant of Joseph"* (5:15), Micah 2:12, Ezra 9:8, and Paul's *"even so
at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace"* (Romans 11:5).

**The paired noun is *pəlêṭâ*** — *"to save you alive by a great deliverance"*, ***liplêṭâ gədōlâ***,
literally *for a great escape*. **Genesis has used its masculine form once before**: *"One who had
escaped came and told Abram, the Hebrew"* (14:13), ***happālîṭ***. **The vocabulary of survival enters
the Bible here, in a private room, about eleven men and an old man in Canaan.** See
[[To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11]].

### Father to Pharaoh

***"He has made me a father to Pharaoh, lord of all his house, and ruler over all the land of
Egypt"*** (45:8). **Three titles, and the first one is the strange one.**

***ʾĀb ləparʿōh*** is an office — the vizier as the king's counsellor, a usage Isaiah gives to Eliakim
(*"he will be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem"*, Isaiah 22:21) — **and Genesis puts it in a
sentence whose next word but one is *my father*.** **Joseph is a father to the king of Egypt and a son
to a man in Canaan who thinks he is dead**, and the chapter says both inside four verses. ***Father*
appears eleven times in twenty-eight verses**, and only one of those eleven is Pharaoh's. See
[[Kingship and Empire]].

### The proof he offers, and what it settles

> **12** Behold, your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth that speaks
> to you.

**The evidence for the claim is that they can hear him.** **Genesis 42:23 established an interpreter
between Joseph and his brothers — *"for there was an interpreter between them"* — and it was the wall
the disguise stood on.** **Here he offers his own mouth as the proof**, and the wiki reads it as the
wall coming down: **he is speaking their language, in front of them, and the language is the
argument.** See [[Language and Speech]] and [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

***"And the eyes of my brother Benjamin."*** **Benjamin is singled out and is not addressed.** He is
mentioned as a second witness, embraced two verses later, and **says nothing in this chapter as he
says nothing in every other one.**

### Five changes of clothing

> **22** He gave each one of them changes of clothing, but to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of
> silver and five changes of clothing.

**This is the chapter's most uncomfortable verse and Genesis passes it without a word.**

| | the thing given | to whom |
|---|---|---|
| **37:3** | *a coat of many colors* | **one son of [[Rachel]], and the brothers *hated him*** |
| **43:34** | **five times** anyone else's portion | **the other son of Rachel, at the table** |
| **45:22** | one change of clothing each | **the ten** |
| **45:22** | **five** changes, and **three hundred pieces of silver** | **the other son of Rachel** |

**The garment that started the cycle is handed out again, in the chapter that ends it, by the man it
was taken from — and the son of the loved wife gets five.** **Three hundred pieces of silver is
fifteen times the twenty Joseph was sold for** (37:28). **Then, in the next verse but one, he tells
them not to quarrel on the road.**

**Genesis offers no comment, and the wiki declines to supply one**: this is either a man
unselfconsciously reproducing his father's favouritism, or a man testing nothing and simply loving his
full brother, or a deliberate and final inversion — **the same provocation as 37:3, delivered to men
who this time have to carry it home without acting on it.** See
[[Five Changes of Clothing - The Coat Given Again]], [[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]]
and [[Envy]].

### The wagons, not the words

**Jacob is told the truth and does not believe it. Then he sees the carts.**

> **26** They told him, saying, "Joseph is still alive, and he is ruler over all the land of Egypt."
> His heart fainted, for he didn't believe them. **27** They told him all the words of Joseph, which
> he had said to them. When he saw the wagons which Joseph had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob,
> their father, revived.

***ʿĂgālôt*** — **wagons — were [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]]'s idea, not Joseph's** (45:19), **and
they are the only thing in the chapter that changes Jacob's mind.** The brothers' report fails
(45:26); Joseph's words, relayed in full, are not said to have worked either; **the verse turns on
*"when he saw"*.** **A man who was persuaded of his son's death by a bloodied coat is persuaded of his
son's life by a cart** — **an object in both cases, and testimony in neither.** See
[[A Garment Used as Evidence - The Coat and the Cloak]] and [[Faith]].

### 45:1–2 — a privacy that fails in the next sentence

> **1** Then Joseph couldn't control himself before all those who stood before him, and he cried,
> "Cause everyone to go out from me!" No one else stood with him, while Joseph made himself known to
> his brothers. **2** He wept aloud. The Egyptians heard, and the house of Pharaoh heard.

***"Cause everyone to go out from me!"*** **He clears the room and the room is an Egyptian court.**
**The disclosure is arranged to be private** — *"no one else stood with him"* — **and the very next
sentence reports that it was not.** ***Wayyittēn ʾet-qōlô bibkî***, *he gave his voice in weeping*:
**the Egyptians hear, and the palace hears, and the news reaches Pharaoh by 45:16 without Joseph
telling him.**

**The clearing of the room is nevertheless the chapter's first act of mercy**, and it is for the
brothers. **What he is about to say — *whom you sold into Egypt* — is a confession of their crime made
in a foreign court**, and he makes it with no Egyptian in the room to hear the charge. **Compare the
opposite instinct at 42:7**, where the disguise existed to keep them exposed. See [[Compassion and Mercy]].

**This is the third of Joseph's seven weepings** (42:24, 43:30, **45:2**, 45:14–15, 46:29, 50:1,
50:17) **and the first that is not hidden.** The first was behind a turned back; the second behind a
door and a washed face; **this one takes down a wall.**

### 45:3 — a question he already knows the answer to

> **3** Joseph said to his brothers, "I am Joseph! Does my father still live?" His brothers couldn't
> answer him; for they were terrified at his presence.

***ʾĂnî yôsēp*** — **two words, and there is no preparation for them.** **He has asked after his
father twice already in the cycle** (43:7 by report, 43:27 in person) **and has just heard seventeen
verses about him**, in which Judah said *"your servant my father"* nine times and described his death
in advance. ***"Does my father still live?"* is therefore not a request for information.** It is the
only thing he wants to say to men he has not spoken to as a brother in twenty-two years, **and the
whole speech to come is built on the answer he already has.**

***"His brothers couldn't answer him"*** — ***wəlōʾ-yāḵəlû ʾeḥāyw laʿănôt ʾōtô.*** **The chapter's
first verb of inability belongs to Joseph** (*couldn't control himself*, 45:1) **and its second to
them.** ***Nibhălû*** — *terrified, dismayed, thrown into confusion* — **is a strong word**, the
reaction of men in front of a theophany or a catastrophe, **and it is what a brother's face produces.**

**They are silent for twelve verses.** Genesis gives them no reply, no protest, no confession — **and
the confession is the conspicuous absence.** They made one at 42:21 among themselves and another at
44:16 about the wrong crime; **here, to the man himself, with every fact on the table, they say
nothing.** See [[Truly We Are Guilty - The Brothers' Confession and the Gap in Genesis 37]] and
[[Guilt and Conscience]].

### 45:4–8 — the crime named once and then reassigned

> **4** Joseph said to his brothers, "Come near to me, please." They came near. "He said, I am Joseph,
> your brother, whom you sold into Egypt. **5** Now don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that
> you sold me here, for God sent me before you to preserve life.

***Gəšû-nāʾ ʾēlay*** — **the root of [[Judah]]'s approach at 44:18, now issued as an invitation.**
**The men who were on the floor in torn clothes one verse-block ago are asked to stand closer to the
throne.**

***"Whom you sold into Egypt."*** **He says it, and it is the only accusation in the chapter.**
**Joseph does not describe the pit, the stripping, the caravan, the price, [[Potiphar]]'s house, the
false charge, or the two years the cupbearer forgot him.** *The land of the Hebrews* and *I have done
nothing* (40:15) were said to a fellow prisoner; **to the men responsible he says four words.**

***"Don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves"*** — ***ʾal-tēʿāṣəbû wəʾal-yiḥar bəʿênêkem.***
***ʿĀṣab*** is the verb of the sons of Jacob at Shechem — *"the men were grieved, and they were very
angry"* (34:7) — **and here it is the state Joseph is telling them not to enter about themselves.**
**He is managing their guilt for them**, which is generous and is also the reason 50:15 exists: **a
guilt somebody else has disposed of on your behalf does not stay disposed of.**

> **6** For these two years the famine has been in the land, and there are yet five years, in which
> there will be no plowing and no harvest. **7** God sent me before you to preserve for you a remnant
> in the earth, and to save you alive by a great deliverance.

**Verse 6 is the only piece of hard information in the speech and it is the reason for the hurry.**
**Two years down, five to go** — **the seven of 41:29–30 with a date attached** — **and five years is
long enough that a family in Canaan with donkeys and a finite amount of silver does not survive it.**
See [[Famine and Plenty]].

***"A remnant in the earth"*** — ***šəʾērît*** — **the word's first appearance in Scripture**, and
***"a great deliverance"*** — ***pəlêṭâ gədōlâ*** — **its companion.** **Joseph is describing the
survival of a household of seventy in the language the prophets will use for the survival of a
nation**, and Genesis puts it in the mouth of a man who has not been home in twenty-two years. See
[[To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11]].

> **8** So now it wasn't you who sent me here, but God, and he has made me a father to Pharaoh, lord
> of all his house, and ruler over all the land of Egypt.

***"It wasn't you who sent me here, but God."*** **The sentence is the high point of the theme and it
is also the chapter's one piece of overstatement** — **they did send him, by selling him, and 45:4 has
already said so.** **The wiki reads the two verses together rather than choosing between them**: 45:4
is the fact and 45:8 is the frame, and **Genesis 50:20 restates the pair in a form that concedes
nothing** — *"you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good."* See [[Providence]].

### 45:9–13 — a message composed for a man who will not believe it

> **9** Hurry, and go up to my father, and tell him, 'This is what your son Joseph says, "God has made
> me lord of all Egypt. Come down to me. Don't wait. **10** You shall dwell in the land of Goshen…

***"Hurry… Don't wait."*** **Twice in one verse**, and the reason is verse 6. **Joseph's message is
almost entirely imperative**: *hurry, go up, tell him, come down, don't wait, you shall dwell, tell my
father, hurry and bring my father down here.* **He has been a governor for nine years and it is
audible.**

***"Your son Joseph."*** **He dictates a self-identification, because he knows the claim is not
credible** — **and 45:26 proves him right.**

**[[Goshen]] enters Scripture here** (45:10). **It is named without introduction, as though Joseph
expects his father to know where it is**, and it will be the address of Israel for four hundred years
and the staging ground of the Exodus. **The chapter that reunites the family also chooses the country
it will have to be brought out of.** See [[Land and Sojourning]].

> **11** There I will nourish you; for there are yet five years of famine; lest you come to poverty…
> **13** You shall tell my father of all my glory in Egypt, and of all that you have seen. You shall
> hurry and bring my father down here."

***"I will nourish you"*** — ***wəkilkaltî*** — **the same verb as 47:12**, discharged in
[[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]], and
**the reversal of the cycle's opening economics**: **the brothers came to Egypt with silver to buy
grain** (42:3), **and now the grain is a gift and the silver goes home with them.**

***"All my glory in Egypt"*** — ***kəbôdî.*** **He wants his father told about the rank.** It is the
one vain sentence in the speech, **and it is also practical** — **an old man who will not believe his
son is alive may believe a report about a vizier** — **and it does not work either.** **What works is
a cart.**

### 45:14–15 — the neck, the kiss, and twelve verses of silence ending

> **14** He fell on his brother Benjamin's neck, and wept, and Benjamin wept on his neck. **15** He
> kissed all his brothers, and wept on them. After that his brothers talked with him.

***Wayyippōl ʿal-ṣawwəʾrê binyāmin ʾāḥîw*** — **the idiom of 33:4**, where [[Esau]] *"ran to meet
[[Jacob]], embraced him, fell on his neck, kissed him, and they wept."* **Genesis uses it for the two
reconciliations of the book and for nothing else**, and Luke 15:20 gives it to the father of the
prodigal. **The brother who was wronged does the falling both times.** See
[[Fell on His Neck and Wept - The Running Embrace in Genesis and Luke]].

**The order matters and Genesis reports it in two verses.** **Benjamin first** — the full brother, the
one who did nothing — **and the ten second**, by kiss. ***Wayyənaššēq ləkol-ʾeḥāyw***: **the kiss goes
to all of them**, including the four who are not named anywhere in the cycle, **and the weeping goes
onto them rather than in front of them.**

***"After that his brothers talked with him."*** **The chapter's quietest sentence.** **Genesis does
not record one word of the conversation** — **what they said, whether anybody apologised, what he told
them about the intervening years.** **It is the reunion the reader has waited nine chapters for and
the text gives it four words and moves to Pharaoh's household.** **The silence is the same one the
book keeps over the pit** (37:24–25, filled in only at 42:21).

### 45:16–20 — a king who is pleased, and an invitation that outbids Joseph's

> **16** The report of it was heard in Pharaoh's house, saying, "Joseph's brothers have come." It
> pleased Pharaoh well, and his servants.

***Wayyîṭab bəʿênê parʿōh*** — **the formula of 41:37**, when Joseph's famine policy *"was good in
Pharaoh's eyes, and in the eyes of all his servants."* **Genesis uses it twice and both times about
the same man**, and here it is applied to a piece of domestic news. **The court is glad the vizier has
a family.**

> **18** Take your father and your households, and come to me, and I will give you the good of the
> land of Egypt, and you will eat the fat of the land.' **19** Now you are commanded: do this. Take
> wagons out of the land of Egypt for your little ones, and for your wives… **20** Also, don't concern
> yourselves about your belongings, for the good of all the land of Egypt is yours."

**Pharaoh raises every term.** **Joseph offered [[Goshen]]** (45:10); **Pharaoh offers *"the good of
the land of Egypt"* and *"the fat of the land"*** (45:18, 20). **Joseph said nothing about transport;
Pharaoh commands wagons** (45:19). **Joseph said *don't wait*; Pharaoh says *don't concern yourselves
about your belongings*** — **leave the furniture.**

**Two readings and the wiki holds both.** **A generous king**, which is how the chapter reads it. **And
a shrewd one**: **a vizier whose family is resident in Egypt at the king's invitation and at the king's
expense is a vizier with no country to go back to**, seven years into a famine that has made Pharaoh
the owner of Egypt by 47:20 *(not yet ingested)*. **Genesis says only that it pleased him.** See
[[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Favor and Grace]].

**Note also who is speaking.** **This is the most Pharaoh has said since 41:44**, and **he speaks to
Joseph about Joseph's brothers without ever being shown meeting them** — that comes at 47:1–10 *(not
yet ingested)*. **The invitation to Israel to enter Egypt is issued sight unseen.**

### 45:21–24 — the gifts, and one sentence about the road

> **21** The sons of Israel did so. Joseph gave them wagons, according to the commandment of Pharaoh,
> and gave them provision for the way. **22** He gave each one of them changes of clothing, but to
> Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver and five changes of clothing.

***"The sons of Israel did so."*** **The narrator's name for them changes here** — *bənê yiśrāʾēl*,
**the phrase that will name a nation** — **and it arrives in a verse about loading carts.**

***"According to the commandment of Pharaoh."*** **The same construction of exact obedience the
narrator used of [[Joseph's Steward]] at 43:17 and 44:2**, *"he did according to the word that Joseph
had spoken"* — **and here Joseph is the one executing somebody else's word.**

**On the five changes of clothing, see the summary above**, and
[[Five Changes of Clothing - The Coat Given Again]]. **Three hundred pieces of silver against the
twenty he was sold for** (37:28) **is the fourth and last movement of silver in the cycle**, and it
runs the opposite way: [[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]].

> **23** He sent the following to his father: ten donkeys loaded with the good things of Egypt, and
> ten female donkeys loaded with grain and bread and provision for his father by the way. **24** So he
> sent his brothers away, and they departed. He said to them, "See that you don't quarrel on the way."

**Twenty donkeys, ten and ten**, **for the man who at 43:11 scraped together a little balm, honey,
spices, myrrh, nuts and almonds — *"the choice fruits of the land"* — as a present for this
same governor.** **The gift goes back up the road multiplied.** See
[[Two Presents to a Feared Brother - Jacob's Minchah to Esau and to Egypt]].

***ʾAl-tirgəzû badderek*** — ***"See that you don't quarrel on the way."*** **The verb *rāgaz* means
to tremble, to be agitated, to be stirred up**, and it will carry either reading: ***don't be
afraid***, or ***don't fall out with each other.*** **WEB takes the second and the wiki follows it**,
because of what is in the carts: **ten men with one garment apiece, one man with five and a bag of
silver, and three hundred miles to explain to their father what happened to Joseph.** **It is the last
thing he says to them in the chapter and it is a warning about themselves.** See [[Brotherhood]].

### 45:25–28 — a heart that faints, and a name that changes

> **25** They went up out of Egypt, and came into the land of Canaan, to Jacob their father. **26**
> They told him, saying, "Joseph is still alive, and he is ruler over all the land of Egypt." His
> heart fainted, for he didn't believe them.

***"Joseph is still alive, and he is ruler over all the land of Egypt."*** **Eleven men deliver a
two-clause report and neither clause is *we sold him*.** **The lie of 37:32 — a coat, a question, and
no statement at all — is not retracted here or anywhere.** **Genesis never records the brothers
telling Jacob what they did**, and the omission is why 50:15 is possible: **after their father dies
they assume Joseph's kindness was for his sake.** See
[[Truly We Are Guilty - The Brothers' Confession and the Gap in Genesis 37]] and [[Deception]].

***Wayyāpāg libbô*** — ***"his heart fainted"*** — **from *pûg*, a rare verb for going numb or
slack**, used of a hand that fails and of a law that has gone slack (Habakkuk 1:4). **It is not joy
and it is not doubt; it is a system stopping.** ***"For he didn't believe them."*** **The last time
they brought him news about Joseph it was a bloodied coat and he believed it instantly** (37:33). See
[[Suffering]].

> **27** They told him all the words of Joseph, which he had said to them. When he saw the wagons
> which Joseph had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived. **28** Israel said,
> "It is enough. Joseph my son is still alive. I will go and see him before I die."

***Wattəḥî rûaḥ yaʿăqōb ʾăbîhem*** — ***"the spirit of Jacob… revived."*** **The idiom is a returning,
not a cheering up**: the same construction describes the Egyptian slave revived by figs and water in
1 Samuel 30:12 and Samson at the spring in Judges 15:19. **Genesis has been describing Jacob as a man
going down to Sheol since 37:35**; **this is the sentence that turns him around.** See
[[The Spirit of Jacob Revived - Ruach Returning]] and [[Life and Death]].

***"The spirit of **Jacob** … revived. **Israel** said."*** **The name changes across the verse
boundary and Genesis does not remark on it.** **The book has alternated the two names since 32:28
without a stated rule**; here the alternation lands exactly on the recovery, **the man who bargains
and mourns becoming the man who decides.** See
[[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

***Rab*** — ***"It is enough."*** **One syllable, and it is a word about quantity** — *much, plenty,
sufficient.* **He does not say he believes them.** **He says the amount of evidence has become
sufficient**, and then names the only thing he wants: ***"I will go and see him before I die."***

**Against 37:35** — *"I will go down to Sheol to my son mourning"* — **and 42:38 and 44:29, where the
same journey is the one his sons will send him on with sorrow.** **Three times he has said he is
going down to Sheol to reach Joseph. Here he says he is going down to Egypt**, and the verb is *see*.
See [[Sheol]] and [[Hope]].

## People

- [[Joseph]] — **the chapter is his and he speaks in it more than in any other in Genesis.** The verb
  that held him at 43:31 fails in its first clause (45:1); he clears the room, weeps loud enough for
  the palace to hear, and says ***"I am Joseph"*** twice — once as a name (45:3) and once with the
  crime attached (45:4). **He names the sale and then reassigns it three times to God** (45:5, 7, 8),
  claims the titles *father to Pharaoh, lord of all his house, ruler over all the land of Egypt*
  (45:8), and dictates a message that is nine imperatives long. **He never says he forgives, and he
  never asks for an apology** — he tells them not to be grieved, which is not the same transaction.
  Then he falls on [[Benjamin]]'s neck, kisses the ten, **gives the son of [[Rachel]] five changes of
  clothing and three hundred pieces of silver in front of the men who once solved that problem with a
  caravan**, and tells them not to quarrel on the road
- [[Jacob]] — **absent for twenty-five verses, the subject of nearly all of them, and given the last
  word.** *Father* runs eleven times through the chapter and most of them are him. He is told the
  truth by eleven sons and **his heart faints** — ***wayyāpāg*** — *"for he didn't believe them"*
  (45:26); **the words do not move him and the wagons do** (45:27). His spirit *revives*, the
  narrator's name for him switches to **Israel** in the next verse, and he says four things: *it is
  enough*, *Joseph my son is still alive*, *I will go*, *before I die*. **The man who said three times
  that he would go down to Sheol mourning** (37:35; 42:38; 44:29) **says here that he will go down to
  Egypt to look at his son**
- [[Benjamin]] — **embraced, wept over, named as a witness, and silent.** *"The eyes of my brother
  Benjamin"* (45:12) makes him the second pair of eyes on the proof; 45:14 gives him the chapter's
  only mutual action — ***"Benjamin wept on his neck"***, **the one thing he is recorded doing in the
  whole Joseph cycle.** **He receives five changes of clothing and three hundred pieces of silver**
  (45:22) **and says nothing about that either.** The pledge [[Judah]] made for him at 43:9 and called
  in at 44:32 is discharged here without being mentioned
- [[Judah]] — **not named in the chapter, and the chapter is the answer to his speech.** *"Then Joseph
  couldn't control himself"* (45:1) is the next sentence after *"lest I see the evil that will come on
  my father"* (44:34). **He is the reason the disclosure happens where and when it does**, and Genesis
  gives him no line, no reaction, and no acknowledgement. See
  [[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]]
- [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] — **speaks for the first time since 41:44, and outbids his own
  vizier.** He hears the news secondhand through a palace that overheard the weeping (45:2, 16), is
  *pleased* in the exact formula of 41:37, **and issues an invitation more generous than Joseph's** —
  *the good of the land of Egypt*, *the fat of the land*, wagons by royal command, and *"don't concern
  yourselves about your belongings"* (45:18–20). **He has not met any of them.** Genesis reports his
  pleasure and assigns him no motive; **the invitation he issues here is the one Exodus 1:8 will be
  written against**
- [[God]] — **named four times in twenty-eight verses and every time by [[Joseph]]** (45:5, 7, 8, 9).
  **The narrator says nothing about God in this chapter**, as he has said nothing since 39:23, and
  will not until 46:2. **Genesis's most explicit statement of divine agency is therefore a character's
  claim that the book neither confirms nor disputes**
- [[Rachel]] — **unnamed and structurally everywhere.** *"My brother Benjamin"* (45:12), the neck wept
  on first (45:14), the five changes of clothing and the silver (45:22): **the chapter's asymmetries
  are all along the line of her two sons**, exactly as 37:3 was
- [[Joseph's Steward]] — **absent.** The man who executed every order in chapters 43 and 44 is not in
  the room at 45:1, because *"cause everyone to go out from me"* included him
- [[Simeon]], [[Levi]], [[Reuben]], [[Dan (person)]], [[Naphtali]], [[Gad]], [[Asher]],
  [[Issachar]], [[Zebulun]] — **the nine, plus Judah, who are addressed as a group and never
  individuated.** They cannot answer (45:3), come near when told (45:4), are kissed and wept on
  (45:15), *"did so"* (45:21), take one garment each, and deliver a report to their father that leaves
  out the only part of it they would have to confess (45:26). **Genesis records no word of theirs in
  Egypt and one sentence of theirs in Canaan**

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — **named ten times in twenty-eight verses, more than in any chapter since 41.** It is
  where Joseph was *sold* (45:4), what he is *ruler over* (45:8, 26), the *good* and the *fat* of which
  are being offered (45:18, 20), and the place a family in Canaan is now going to move to. **The
  chapter is the hinge between Genesis's Egypt — a granary and a refuge — and Exodus's**
- [[Goshen]] — **enters Scripture at 45:10** and is named without explanation. Joseph assigns it before
  Pharaoh has offered anything, and Pharaoh's counter-offer (*"the good of the land of Egypt"*, 45:18)
  is wider; **the family ends up in Goshen anyway** (47:6). **It is where Israel
  will live for four hundred years and where the plagues will not fall**
- [[Canaan]] — **where the chapter's last movement happens and where its stakes have been the whole
  time.** The brothers *go up out of Egypt* (45:25) and arrive to find a man who does not believe them.
  **It is the land of the promise, and the chapter's business is the family's departure from it**
- [[Sheol]] — **not named in the chapter and the chapter is aimed at it.** 45:28's *"before I die"* is
  the counter-sentence to 37:35, 42:38 and 44:29

## Themes

- [[Providence]] — **the theme's explicit statement in Genesis, and the chapter that the whole Joseph
  cycle has been withholding it for.** ***"God sent me before you"*** three times (45:5, 7, 8), against
  ***"you sold me"*** twice (45:4, 5). **The narrator is still silent**, so the doctrine arrives as a
  character's testimony rather than an authorial verdict; 50:20 will restate it in a harder form that
  concedes the brothers' intention rather than dissolving it
- [[Brotherhood]] — ***"I am Joseph, your brother"*** (45:4). **The word runs twelve times through the
  chapter**, and the reunion it produces is asymmetric in every detail: **one brother speaks, one
  brother is embraced first, one brother gets five garments, and nine are kissed as a set.** *"See that
  you don't quarrel on the way"* (45:24) is the theme's last word here
- [[Guilt and Conscience]] — **the chapter disposes of the brothers' guilt without their participation
  in it.** *"Don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves"* (45:5) is Joseph absorbing the thing they
  have not yet confessed, **and it does not hold**: they say nothing at 45:3–15, leave the sale out of
  their report to Jacob at 45:26, and are still frightened at 50:15
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — **the room is cleared so that a confession of their crime can be made
  where no Egyptian will hear it** (45:1). **The mercy in this chapter is structural rather than
  stated** — a cleared room, a message dictated for an old man, wagons, provisions for the road
- [[Famine and Plenty]] — **the chapter's engine and its date-stamp.** *"These two years the famine has
  been in the land, and there are yet five years"* (45:6): the seven of 41:30 with the clock started.
  **Everything urgent in the chapter is urgent because of verse 6**
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — **[[Goshen]] is offered, and the family that was promised Canaan moves to
  Egypt to survive.** *"Come down to me. Don't wait"* (45:9) is an invitation out of the land of the
  promise, issued by the one member of the family who has not been living in it
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — ***"a father to Pharaoh, lord of all his house, and ruler over all the land
  of Egypt"*** (45:8). **The vizier's power is exercised in the chapter as hospitality**, and the king
  above him raises every term of it. **The chapter is the friendliest Egypt ever is to Israel**
- [[Life and Death]] — ***to preserve life*** (45:5), ***to save you alive*** (45:7), *"Joseph is still
  alive"* (45:26, 28), *"the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived"* (45:27), *"before I die"* (45:28). **Five
  statements about being alive in a chapter whose premise is a man everyone believed dead**
- [[Deception]] — **the twenty-two-year deception ends in 45:1–4 and a second one is left standing.**
  Joseph's disguise comes off; **the brothers' account of 37:32 does not**, and Genesis never records
  them telling Jacob what they did
- [[Language and Speech]] — ***"it is my mouth that speaks to you"*** (45:12) **is offered as the proof
  of identity**, and it is the answer to the interpreter of 42:23. The chapter is also **the
  book's densest run of imperatives**: *come near, don't be grieved, hurry, go up, tell him, come down,
  don't wait, tell my father, hurry and bring him, don't quarrel*
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — *"your eyes see"* (45:12), *"of all that you have seen"* (45:13),
  *"pleased Pharaoh well"* — literally *good in the eyes of* (45:16), *"when he saw the wagons"*
  (45:27), *"I will go and see him"* (45:28). **The chapter that ends the concealment is built out of
  eyes**, and **the sight that finally persuades is of a cart, not a face**
- [[Envy]] — **the cause of chapter 37 is reproduced in the chapter that resolves it.** Five changes of
  clothing to one brother and one apiece to the rest (45:22), from the man whose own coat did this.
  **Genesis records it without comment and the wiki flags it rather than explaining it away**
- [[Favor and Grace]] — **Pharaoh's, unprompted and unearned by the recipients** (45:16–20). *The good
  of the land*, *the fat of the land*, *don't concern yourselves about your belongings* — **the most
  generous speech any foreign king makes in Genesis**
- [[Hope]] — ***"It is enough"*** (45:28). **The chapter's last movement is a man deciding that the
  evidence has become sufficient**, and the decision is a journey rather than a belief
- [[Suffering]] — **twenty-two years of it, summarised by its subject in four words** — *"whom you sold
  into Egypt"* — **and then explained away as an errand.** The page reads 45:5–8 as pastoral rather
  than complete, on the evidence of 50:15–21
- [[Remembrance]] — **the chapter contains no act of remembering and one of not being believed.** The
  brothers are not asked to recall anything; Jacob is asked to accept a fact. **Nobody in the chapter
  recites the past except Joseph, and he recites it in order to close it**

## Cross-references

- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:18–34; **this chapter is its next sentence.**
  Judah's *"lest I see the evil that will come on my father"* (44:34) is followed immediately by
  *"Then Joseph couldn't control himself"* (45:1), **and the *wayyiggaš* of 44:18 is answered by the
  *gəšû-nāʾ* of 45:4.** Judah is not named again
- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:3, 28, 32, 35; **every element of the first chapter comes
  back inverted.** A coat given to one son becomes **five changes of clothing given to one son**
  (45:22); twenty pieces of silver becomes **three hundred** (45:22); *"Jacob tore his clothes"* and
  *"I will go down to Sheol to my son mourning"* (37:34–35) becomes *"the spirit of Jacob… revived"*
  and *"I will go and see him before I die"* (45:27–28). **The one thing that does not come back is the
  lie**
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:7–8, 23, 24; **the disguise that was built
  there is dismantled here.** *"acted like a stranger to them"* (42:7) becomes *"Joseph made himself
  known to his brothers"* (45:1); **the interpreter of 42:23 is answered by *"it is my mouth that
  speaks to you"*** (45:12); and the weeping that had to be hidden at 42:24 is heard by the house of
  Pharaoh at 45:2
- [[The Second Journey]] — Genesis 43:30–31, 34; ***wayyitʾappaq*** **is the link.** The verb that held
  at 43:31 fails at 45:1, **and the fivefold portion of 43:34 becomes five changes of clothing at
  45:22** — the same number, to the same brother, twice in three chapters
- [[Providence]] — **the theme page's *(not yet ingested)* marker on 45:5–8 is discharged by this
  page.** The three sendings, the *šəʾērît*, and the narrator's continuing silence
- [[God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale]] — **new page.** ***Mākar*** twice and
  ***šālaḥ*** three times in five verses (45:4–8), with Psalm 105:17 (*"he sent a man before them"*),
  Acts 7:9–10, and Genesis 50:20 — **four texts that put a human act and a divine purpose on the same
  event, and differ on whether the human one survives the reframing**
- [[To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11]] — **new page.** ***Šəʾērît***
  (45:7) **in its first appearance in Scripture**, with ***pəlêṭâ*** beside it, through Isaiah
  10:20–22, Amos 5:15, Micah 2:12, Ezra 9:8 and Romans 11:5 — **the survival of a household becoming
  the vocabulary for the survival of a nation**
- [[Five Changes of Clothing - The Coat Given Again]] — **new page.** Genesis 37:3, 43:34 and 45:22:
  **a garment to one son, five portions to one son, five garments to one son** — **the same
  favouritism, the same mother's child, and the third instance performed by the victim of the first**
- [[The Spirit of Jacob Revived - Ruach Returning]] — **new page.** ***Wattəḥî rûaḥ*** (45:27) with
  Judges 15:19 and 1 Samuel 30:12 — **three men brought back by a physical thing**, and the one case
  where the thing is a cart
- [[Fell on His Neck and Wept - The Running Embrace in Genesis and Luke]] — **Genesis 45:14 is the
  idiom's second occurrence**, after [[Esau]] at 33:4 and before Luke 15:20. **In all three the wronged
  party initiates and no apology is completed first** — at 33:4 Jacob's speech is cut off, at 45:14 the
  brothers have not spoken, at Luke 15:21 the son's rehearsed confession is interrupted
- [[Truly We Are Guilty - The Brothers' Confession and the Gap in Genesis 37]] — **the third confession
  is the one that does not happen.** 42:21 was private, 44:16 was about the wrong crime, **and 45:3–15
  gives them the opportunity and records silence** — after which 45:26's report to Jacob omits the sale
  entirely
- [[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]] — **the dreams are not mentioned**, for
  the fourth pericope running, in the chapter where their content is finally acknowledged out loud by
  the dreamer. *"God has made me lord of all Egypt"* (45:9) **is the sheaf and the star image stated as
  a fact and never connected to them**
- [[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]] — **silver's last movement in the cycle**: out
  at twenty (37:28), back in the sacks (42:25), doubled in hand (43:12), returned with the cup (44:1),
  **and three hundred pieces given to Benjamin** (45:22) — **fifteen times the sale price, to the
  brother who was not sold**
- [[Am I in God's Place - Jacob at 30.2 and Joseph at 50.19]] — **45:5–8 is the first of Joseph's two
  verdicts** and the softer one. *"It wasn't you who sent me here, but God"* against *"you meant evil
  against me, but God meant it for good"* — **the same doctrine with and without the concession**
- [[Sold for a Servant - Joseph's Sale in Psalm 105 and Acts 7]] — **Psalm 105:17 reads the sale as a
  sending**, ***šālaḥ***, **which is Joseph's own word at 45:5, 7 and 8.** Acts 7:13 renders 45:1 as
  *"Joseph was made known to his brothers"*, and Stephen's version adds a detail Genesis does not —
  *"Joseph's race was revealed to Pharaoh"*
- [[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]] — **the pledge is
  discharged in this chapter and nobody says so.** Benjamin goes up with his brothers (45:24), which is
  exactly what Judah asked for at 44:33, **and Judah is not named in Genesis 45 at all**
- [[A Garment Used as Evidence - The Coat and the Cloak]] — **the fourth object that testifies, and the
  first that tells the truth.** A coat dipped in blood (37:31–33), a cloak in a hand (39:12–18), a cup
  in a sack (44:2), **and wagons in a courtyard** (45:27) — **and the wagons persuade a man that words
  could not**
- [[Two Presents to a Feared Brother - Jacob's Minchah to Esau and to Egypt]] — **the present comes
  back down the road.** Jacob's *"a little balm, a little honey, spices and myrrh, nuts, and almonds"*
  (43:11) is answered by **twenty donkeys loaded with the good things of Egypt** (45:23)
- [[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]] — **45:27–28 switches names across a
  verse boundary at the exact moment of the revival**: *"the spirit of **Jacob**, their father,
  revived. **Israel** said, 'It is enough.'"* **Genesis does not remark on it**
- [[If I Am Bereaved, I Am Bereaved - Jacob and Esther]] — **the bereavement Jacob accepted in advance
  at 43:14 does not arrive.** Both sons come back, and so do the wagons
- [[Zaphenath-Paneah - Renaming at a Foreign Court]] — **the Egyptian name is not used in this
  chapter.** He is *Joseph* to his brothers, *your son Joseph* to his father, and *"Joseph's brothers"*
  in Pharaoh's house (45:16) — **the court refers to him by the Hebrew name in the one chapter where
  his family is present**

## Notes

**On what Joseph does not say.** **There is no word for forgiveness in Genesis 45.** He tells them not
to grieve and not to be angry with themselves (45:5), which addresses their feeling about the act
rather than the act. **The nearest thing to a pardon in the Joseph cycle is 50:19–21**, and it is
prompted by the brothers' fear after Jacob's death — **which is the evidence that 45:5–8 did not
settle it.** The wiki reads the chapter as a reconciliation that is real and incomplete, **and notes
that Genesis itself supplies the evidence for the incompleteness five chapters later.**

**On *"it wasn't you who sent me here, but God"* (45:8).** **Read flatly it contradicts 45:4**, which
says they sold him. **The wiki does not harmonise it by weakening either verse.** Two readings are
available and the text supports both: **the idiom is a Hebrew comparative** — *not so much you as God*
— **or Joseph is deliberately overstating, in a room where eleven terrified men need to be able to
stand up.** **Genesis 50:20's *"you meant evil"* is the control**, and it shows that the
reassignment of agency was never meant to be a denial of the deed.

**On the WEB's quotation marks at 45:4.** The raw text reads *"Come near to me, please." They came
near. "He said, I am Joseph…"* — **the opening quotation mark is misplaced in the WEB's typesetting**,
and the sense is *They came near. He said, "I am Joseph…"*. **Quoted verbatim above** as it stands in
`raw/bible/Genesis.md`; the misplacement is the edition's, not a textual variant.

**On the counts.** *Father* appears **eleven times**, *Egypt* **ten**, *brother(s)* **twelve**, *God*
**four**, *sent* **six** (three with God as subject, three with Joseph), and *wept* **four**, all in
twenty-eight verses. **These are counted from the WEB text in `raw/bible/Genesis.md`** and are offered
as the shape of the chapter rather than as a Hebrew concordance figure.

**On Pharaoh's motive.** Genesis says *"it pleased Pharaoh well, and his servants"* (45:16) and
assigns no reason. **The page notes the political reading** — a vizier whose family is settled in Egypt
at royal expense is a vizier without an exit — **and does not adopt it**, because the text gives no
support for it beyond the shape of the situation. **Exodus 1:8's *"there arose a new king over Egypt,
who didn't know Joseph"* is the verse that makes the reading available in retrospect**, and it is a
different king.

**On the seven weepings.** The wiki counts Joseph's weepings as seven: 42:24, 43:30, **45:2**,
**45:14–15**, 46:29, 50:1, 50:17. **This chapter holds the third and fourth**, and 45:14–15 is counted
once though the verb appears three times in two verses.

**Links left for later pericopes.** [[Goshen]] as the family's actual settlement (46:28–47:6), Jacob
before Pharaoh (47:7–10), *"I will nourish you"* fulfilled (47:12), the brothers' fear after Jacob's
death and *"you meant evil against me"* (50:15–21) are named above in prose and marked
*(not yet ingested)* rather than linked — **their passage pages do not exist yet.**
