# Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 46:1–47:12

## Summary

**The family leaves the land of the promise, and God tells them to.**

The pericope is the descent itself: **[[Jacob]] stops at [[Beersheba]] and sacrifices** (46:1), **God
speaks to him there in the night** (46:2–4), **the household is counted as it crosses** (46:8–27),
**[[Joseph]] meets his father in [[Goshen]]** (46:28–30), **five brothers and then the old man are
presented to [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]]** (47:1–10), **and the family is given land and fed**
(47:11–12). **Sixty-six people go down a road; seventy are in Egypt when the counting is done.**

**Genesis 46:2–4 is the only time God speaks in the Joseph cycle.** The narrator has said nothing
about God since 39:23 and the characters have done all the theology — four mentions in Joseph's
mouth in chapter 45 alone. **Here, for four verses, the book's usual voice returns, and what it says
is *go*.** After 46:4 it is silent again through the deathbed chapters to the end of Genesis. See
[[Providence]] and [[Prophecy and Vision]].

**The chapter is therefore the hinge the whole Bible turns on, and it is written as a travel
itinerary with a family register in the middle.** *"I will go down with you into Egypt. I will also
surely bring you up again"* (46:4) **is the Exodus promised before the descent has finished
happening**, and 47:11 parks the family in *"the land of Rameses"* — **the name of the store-city
Israel will build as slaves and the place it will march out from**. **Genesis names the address of
the bondage and calls it *the best of the land*.** See [[Land and Sojourning]] and
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

### Beersheba, and the God he sacrifices to

> **1** Israel traveled with all that he had, and came to Beersheba, and offered sacrifices to the
> God of his father, Isaac.

**He stops at the last town before the desert, and the stop is the point.** **[[Beersheba]] is where
[[Abraham]] *"planted a tamarisk tree… and called there on the name of Yahweh, the Everlasting God"*
(21:33), where [[Isaac]] built an altar (26:25), and where Jacob himself was standing when he left
the land the first time** — *"Jacob went out from Beersheba, and went toward Haran"* (28:10).
**Genesis has him leave the land twice and start from the same place both times.**

***"To the God of his father, Isaac."*** **Not the God of Abraham, and this is the pericope's first
deliberate choice.** **Abraham went down to Egypt in a famine without asking anyone** (12:10) and the
episode cost him his wife's safety. **Isaac, in the next famine, was forbidden** — *"Don't go down
into Egypt. Live in the land I will tell you about"* (26:2). **Jacob, in the third, sacrifices to the
God who issued that prohibition, at the place where He renewed the promise to the man He issued it
to**, and waits. See
[[Don't Go Down to Egypt - The Three Patriarchs and the Same Road]] and
[[Sacrifice and Offering]].

### The same place, the same hour, the same opening — and the opposite instruction

**Genesis 26:24 and Genesis 46:2–3 are built from the same four parts.**

| | [[Isaac]], 26:24 | [[Jacob]], 46:2–3 |
|---|---|---|
| **where** | Beersheba (26:23) | Beersheba (46:1) |
| **when** | *"the same night"* | *"in the visions of the night"* |
| **opening** | *"I am the God of Abraham your father"* | *"I am God, the God of your father"* |
| **reassurance** | *"Don't be afraid, for I am with you"* | *"Don't be afraid to go down into Egypt"* |

**The frame is identical and the content is reversed.** **Isaac is told not to be afraid because he
is staying; Jacob is told not to be afraid because he is leaving.** **The land that was the whole
substance of the promise to both of them is, for the first time, the thing God tells a patriarch to
walk out of.** See [[Covenant]] and [[The Fear of God]].

### *Jacob, Jacob* — a doubled name and an answer of one word

> **2** God spoke to Israel in the visions of the night, and said, "Jacob, Jacob!" He said, "Here I
> am."

***"God spoke to Israel… and said, 'Jacob, Jacob!'"*** **The narrator calls him Israel and God calls
him Jacob, in one verse, and Genesis does not remark on it** — as it did not remark on the switch
across 45:27–28. **The name alternates four times in five verses**: *Israel traveled* (46:1), *God
spoke to Israel* (46:2), *Jacob rose up* (46:5), *the sons of Israel carried Jacob* (46:5). See
[[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

**The doubled vocative is rare and always urgent.** Genesis has used it once before — *"Abraham,
Abraham!"* at the knife (22:11) — **and both times the man answers with one Hebrew word,
*hinnēnî*, *Here I am*.** **It recurs at every hinge of the story after this**: *Moses, Moses*
(Exodus 3:4), *Samuel, Samuel* (1 Samuel 3:10), *Martha, Martha* (Luke 10:41), *Simon, Simon* (Luke
22:31), *Saul, Saul* (Acts 9:4). See [[Jacob, Jacob - The Doubled Name and Hinneni]].

### Four verses that contain the Exodus

> **3** He said, "I am God, the God of your father. Don't be afraid to go down into Egypt, for there
> I will make of you a great nation. **4** I will go down with you into Egypt. I will also surely
> bring you up again. Joseph will close your eyes."

**Three promises and a detail, and the detail is the one that would have mattered most to the man
hearing it.**

***"There I will make of you a great nation."*** ***Gôy gādôl*** — **the exact phrase of 12:2,
*"I will make of you a great nation"*, spoken to Abram on the way *into* Canaan.** **Here it is
relocated.** **The nation is not going to be made in the land of the promise; it is going to be made
in Egypt and then brought back** — which is what 15:13–16 said in advance, *four hundred years* and
a *fourth generation*, without naming the country. **This verse names it.**

***"I will go down with you… I will also surely bring you up again."*** ***Yārad*** and ***ʿālâ***,
**down and up, are the Exodus's two verbs**, and Genesis puts them in a single sentence in the mouth
of God before the descent is complete. **The promise of the going-out is made to the man doing the
going-in.** **It is also, strictly, a promise made to a corpse**: Jacob dies in Egypt (49:33) and is
carried up to Machpelah (50:13), **and Joseph's bones make the trip four centuries later** (50:25;
Exodus 13:19). **The *you* of 46:4 is both the man and the nation he is about to become.** See
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]] and [[Covenant]].

***"Joseph will close your eyes."*** **The only tender sentence God speaks in the Joseph cycle.**
**It is not a promise about a nation; it is a promise that a particular son will be standing at a
particular bedside**, and it is discharged at 50:1, where *"Joseph fell on his father's face, wept
on him, and kissed him."* **A man who spent twenty-two years believing he would *"go down to Sheol
to my son mourning"*** (37:35) **is told, in one clause, how he will actually die.** See [[Life and Death]]
and [[Compassion and Mercy]].

### Seventy

**The register at 46:8–27 is the Bible's first roll of Israel, and it is taken at a border.**

| mother | sons and grandsons named | count given |
|---|---|---|
| **[[Leah]]** (46:9–15) | Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, and [[Dinah]] | **thirty-three** |
| **[[Zilpah]]** (46:16–18) | Gad, Asher | **sixteen** |
| **[[Rachel]]** (46:19–22) | Joseph, Benjamin | **fourteen** |
| **[[Bilhah]]** (46:23–25) | Dan, Naphtali | **seven** |
| | | **= seventy** |

**Three of the four subtotals can be reproduced exactly by counting the names in them. Leah's
cannot.** Her section lists **thirty-two** living people — six sons, [[Dinah]], and twenty-five
grandsons and great-grandsons, with [[Er]] and [[Onan]] explicitly subtracted at 46:12 as having
*"died in the land of Canaan"* — **and the verse says thirty-three.** **The one person present at
Beersheba whom the register has not yet counted is [[Jacob]] himself**, and 46:8 has already put him
in the list — *"the children of Israel, who came into Egypt, Jacob and his sons"*. **Counting him
into Leah's line, as the father of it, makes the arithmetic close on the nose**, and the two totals
that follow confirm it:

- **46:26 — *"all the souls who came with Jacob into Egypt, who were his direct offspring, besides
  Jacob's sons' wives, all the souls were sixty-six."*** Seventy minus Jacob, minus [[Joseph]],
  [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]], **who did not come with him because they were already there**.
- **46:27 — *"the sons of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt, were two souls. All the souls of
  the house of Jacob, who came into Egypt, were seventy."*** Sixty-six on the road, **plus the three
  waiting in Egypt, plus the old man**.

**The number is not neutral.** **Genesis 10 counted the nations of the world at seventy**; here a
household of seventy goes into one of them. **Exodus 1:5 and Deuteronomy 10:22 both repeat the
figure**, and Deuteronomy adds what it is for — seventy persons went down, and the God of Israel has
since made them as numerous as the stars. **Acts 7:14 says seventy-five**, following the Septuagint.
See
[[Seventy Souls - The Count of Jacob's House in Genesis, Exodus and Acts]] and
[[Seventy Nations - Genesis 10 in Deuteronomy 32 and Luke 10]].

### What the register says about women, and what it does not

**Four women are named and all four are named as mothers**; **one granddaughter is named**; **and the
wives who are actually walking the road are counted out of the total by name.**

- ***"Besides Jacob's sons' wives"*** (46:26). **Eleven or more women are on this journey and the
  register's job is to say that they are not in the number.** **The seventy is a male descent line
  plus [[Dinah]]**, not a census of travellers.
- ***"With his daughter Dinah"*** (46:15). **She is in the count**, eleven chapters after Shechem,
  and Genesis gives her no husband, no child, and no further sentence. **The last thing the book
  does with her is include her.**
- ***"Serah their sister"*** (46:17). **[[Serah]] is the only granddaughter in seventy names**, and
  the text gives no reason. **She is listed again at Numbers 26:46 and 1 Chronicles 7:30, which is
  more attention than most of the men in this list ever get.**
- ***"The sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife"*** (46:19). ***[[Rachel]] has been dead since 35:19***, **and
  she is the only one of the four called *wife* here.** **[[Leah]], [[Zilpah]] and [[Bilhah]] are
  identified by who bore them or who handed them over** — *"whom Laban gave to Leah, his daughter"*
  (46:18), *"whom Laban gave to Rachel, his daughter"* (46:25). **The favouritism that drove chapter
  37 is still operating inside the sentence structure of a genealogy.** See [[Love]] and [[Envy]].

### Three foreign mothers, three different treatments

**The register flags one, honours another, and passes the third in silence.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **46:10** | *"Shaul the son of a Canaanite woman"* | **named by her nationality and by nothing else** |
| **46:20** | *"Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On"* | **named, with her father and his office** |
| **46:12** | Er, Onan, Shelah, Perez, Zerah | **all of them born to [[Shua's Daughter]], a Canaanite** (38:2), **who is not mentioned** |

**[[Shaul (son of Simeon)]] is singled out for a parentage that half the men in the list share.**
**[[Asenath]] is Egyptian, the daughter of a pagan priest, and she is given a full pedigree.**
**[[Judah]]'s Canaanite wife is the mother of the line the register spends its longest paragraph on
— through [[Perez]] to [[Hezron (son of Perez)]] and [[Hamul]], which is the line to David and to
Matthew 1 — and the register does not say so.** **Genesis offers no rule that accounts for all
three**, and the wiki does not supply one. See
[[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] and [[The Nations]].

### The three Levites, and a name that is not Job's

***"The sons of Levi: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari"*** (46:11). **Three names in one clause, and they
are the whole administrative structure of the tabernacle.** [[Gershon]], [[Kohath]] and [[Merari]]
become the three Levitical houses of Numbers 3–4; **[[Kohath]] is the grandfather of Moses and
Aaron** (Exodus 6:16–20). **Genesis records them as a list item on a journey and says nothing about
any of it.** See [[Priesthood]].

***"The sons of Issachar: Tola, Puvah, Iob, and Shimron"*** (46:13). **The WEB's *Iob* is a
transcription of a Hebrew name (*yôb*, elsewhere *Jashub*, Numbers 26:24) and is not the *Job* of the
book of Job**, whose name has a different consonant. **The wiki keeps [[Job (person)]] separate from
this name deliberately**, as it keeps him separate from the Edomite king at 36:33. See
[[Jobab of Bozrah and the Septuagint's Job]].

### Judah sent ahead

> **28** He sent Judah before him to Joseph, to show the way before him to Goshen, and they came into
> the land of Goshen.

**The man who said *"What profit is it if we kill our brother…? Come, and let's sell him"* (37:26–27)
is the one sent ahead to find him.** **Genesis gives no explanation and needs none**: chapter 44 was
twenty verses of Judah speaking for the family, and the family now sends him. **He is the fourth son
and he has been functioning as the first since 43:3.** See
[[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]] and
[[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

***"To show the way before him"*** — ***ləhôrōt ləpānāyw***, **from *yārâ*, to point or to
instruct.** **It is the root behind *tôrâ*.** **Its first use in Genesis is Judah pointing out the
road into Egypt**, which is a coincidence of vocabulary rather than a doctrine, and the wiki notes it
as such.

### The reunion, and a verb Genesis normally reserves for God

> **29** Joseph prepared his chariot, and went up to meet Israel, his father, in Goshen. He presented
> himself to him, and fell on his neck, and wept on his neck a good while. **30** Israel said to
> Joseph, "Now let me die, since I have seen your face, that you are still alive."

***"He presented himself to him"*** — ***wayyērāʾ ʾēlāyw***, **the Niphal of *rāʾâ*, *he appeared to
him*.** **Every other occurrence of that verb-form in Genesis has God as its subject**: *"Yahweh
appeared to"* Abram (12:7; 17:1), to him by the oaks of Mamre (18:1), to Isaac twice (26:2, 24), *"God
appeared to Jacob again"* (35:9), and *"God Almighty appeared to me at Luz"* (48:3); **35:1 has the
same verb in a relative clause, also of God.** **Here it is a son getting down from a chariot.** The
wiki records the
usage and declines to build on it — **the Niphal means only *was seen* and Hebrew narrative uses it
of men elsewhere** — **but it is the one verse in Genesis where the theophany verb describes a
family.** See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

***"Wept on his neck a good while."*** **The fifth of [[Joseph]]'s seven weepings** (42:24, 43:30,
45:2, 45:14–15, **46:29**, 50:1, 50:17), **and the longest.** **The idiom — falling on the neck — is
33:4 and 45:14 again**, and **as in both of those, only one of the two men is said to weep.**
**Genesis does not say that [[Jacob]] wept.** It gives him a sentence instead, and the sentence is
about dying. See
[[Fell on His Neck and Wept - The Running Embrace in Genesis and Luke]].

***"Now let me die, since I have seen your face, that you are still alive."*** **He said at 45:28
that he would go and see Joseph *before I die*; he has now seen him and the clause comes back
inverted.** **It is a release, not a complaint** — the same shape as Simeon's *"now you are releasing
your servant… for my eyes have seen your salvation"* (Luke 2:29–30). **Both men make a lifetime's
waiting conditional on one act of sight, and neither dies in the scene.** See
[[Now Let Me Die, Since I Have Seen - Jacob and Simeon]] and [[Hope]].

### The briefing, and the brothers who depart from it

**Joseph coaches them** (46:31–34) **and they do not say what he told them to say.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **46:34, Joseph's script** | *"Your servants have been **keepers of livestock** from our youth even until now, both we, and our fathers"* |
| **47:3, what they said** | *"Your servants are **shepherds**, both we, and our fathers"* |

***"For every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians"*** (46:34). **That is the reason for the
script** — ***tôʿēbâ***, **the same word as 43:32**, where *"the Egyptians don't eat bread with the
Hebrews, for that is an abomination to the Egyptians."* **Joseph's plan is to get the word said in a
form that will make an Egyptian court want them out of sight and in [[Goshen]], which is exactly
where he wants them.** **The brothers use the forbidden word instead** — ***rōʿēh ṣōʾn***, the plain
term — **and add a request Joseph never gave them**: *"Now therefore, please let your servants dwell
in the land of Goshen"* (47:4). **It works.** See
[[An Abomination to the Egyptians - Separate Tables in Genesis and Exodus]] and [[Work]].

**Note what the strategy amounts to.** **Joseph's care is to place his family where Egypt will not
assimilate them**, and the instrument is Egyptian contempt. **The separation that keeps Israel Israel
for four hundred years is engineered, in this pericope, out of a prejudice.** See
[[Land and Sojourning]].

### Two audiences with a king

**Pharaoh sees the brothers** (47:1–6) **and then the father** (47:7–10), **and the two scenes are
written in opposite registers.**

**With the five brothers it is an interview.** *"What is your occupation?"* — **the question Joseph
predicted verbatim** (46:33) — **answered, a request made, and a grant given: *"the land of Egypt is
before you… let them dwell in the land of Goshen"*** (47:6). **And an offer nobody asked for**:
*"If you know any able men among them, then put them in charge of my livestock."* **The men whose
trade is an abomination are offered the royal herds in the same breath in which they are quarantined
for practising it.** See [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Favor and Grace]].

**With Jacob there is no interview.** **The old man is brought in, and before Pharaoh has said a word
he blesses him** (47:7), **answers the one question he is asked with a lament** (47:9), **blesses him
again, and leaves** (47:10). **Pharaoh asks him nothing about sheep.**

### Jacob blesses Pharaoh, twice

> **7** Joseph brought in Jacob, his father, and set him before Pharaoh, and Jacob blessed Pharaoh.
> … **10** Jacob blessed Pharaoh, and went out from the presence of Pharaoh.

**The verb brackets the scene and the narrator reports it flatly both times, with no content.**
**Genesis does not say what he said.**

**A hundred-and-thirty-year-old Aramean herdsman, in the country on famine relief, with no land, no
army and no standing, blesses the most powerful man on earth** — **and the book states it as a fact
of protocol.** **It is 12:3 operating in plain sight**: *"I will bless those who bless you… All the
families of the earth will be blessed through you."* **Hebrews 7:7 supplies the principle the scene
runs on** — *"without any dispute, the lesser is blessed by the greater"* — **and Genesis, having put
the greater in the sandals, does not comment.** See
[[Jacob Blessed Pharaoh - The Lesser Blessed by the Greater]] and [[Blessing]].

### "Few and evil"

> **8** Pharaoh said to Jacob, "How many are the days of the years of your life?" **9** Jacob said to
> Pharaoh, "The days of the years of my pilgrimage are one hundred thirty years. Few and evil have
> been the days of the years of my life, and they have not attained to the days of the years of the
> life of my fathers in the days of their pilgrimage."

**It is the only time in Genesis that a patriarch is asked to assess his own life, and the assessment
is bleak.**

***"My pilgrimage"*** — ***məgûray***, *my sojournings*, **from the root Genesis uses for the
patriarchs' whole tenure in Canaan**: *the land of your sojournings* (17:8, 28:4), *"Jacob lived in
the land of his father's travels"* (37:1). **Asked how old he is, he answers with a word that means
he has never been anywhere he belonged**, and he applies it to his fathers too.

***"Few and evil."*** **A hundred and thirty years is *few* only against [[Abraham]]'s hundred and
seventy-five and [[Isaac]]'s hundred and eighty** (25:7; 35:28), **and he says so.** He is right on
the arithmetic — **he will die at a hundred and forty-seven** (47:28) — **and the reader, who has
just come through the flight from [[Esau]], the twenty years with [[Laban]], the death of
[[Rachel]], Shechem, and twenty-two years of believing [[Joseph]] torn to pieces, is in no position
to correct him on the rest.** **The book lets the complaint stand.** See [[Suffering]] and
[[A Stranger and a Foreigner - The Sojourner's Confession]].

**The sentence he does not say is *God has been good to me*.** **He said something like it twice
before** — *"the God who has fed me all my life long to this day"* (48:15, *not yet ingested*) — and
**he will say it again on the deathbed.** **Here, in front of a king, in the first month of his
rescue, with the son he had buried standing in the room, his verdict on his own life is that it has
been short and hard.** **Genesis records both verdicts and reconciles neither.** See [[Faith]].

### The address

> **11** Joseph placed his father and his brothers, and gave them a possession in the land of Egypt,
> in the best of the land, in the land of Rameses, as Pharaoh had commanded. **12** Joseph nourished
> his father, his brothers, and all of his father's household, with bread, according to their
> families.

***"A possession"*** — ***ʾăḥuzzâ***, **the word Genesis has used exactly twice before, both times
for the cave at Machpelah** (23:9, 20) — ***"a possession for a burial place."***
**The only land the family has ever owned in Canaan is a tomb.** **The first freehold Genesis gives
them is in Egypt**, and it is given by a foreign king's command rather than by promise. See
[[Land and Sojourning]] and [[Burial and the Grave]].

***"In the land of Rameses."*** **The name is an anachronism inside Genesis and the book uses it
anyway.** **[[Rameses]] is one of the two store-cities Israel builds under the lash**
(Exodus 1:11, with Pithom) **and the town the Exodus departs from** (Exodus 12:37, the first stage of
the march, to Succoth). **Genesis settles them,
in *the best of the land*, at the exact coordinates of the slavery and of the going-out.**

***"Joseph nourished"*** — ***wayəkalkēl***, **the verb of 45:11**, *"There I will nourish you."*
**The promise made to persuade an old man to come is discharged in the last clause of the
pericope.** **The same verb returns at 50:21**, *"I will nourish you and your little ones"*, **spoken
to the brothers after Jacob's death** — **the bracket around everything that is left of Genesis.**

### 46:5–7 — the loading, and a phrase that is not quite true

> **5** Jacob rose up from Beersheba, and the sons of Israel carried Jacob, their father, their
> little ones, and their wives, in the wagons which Pharaoh had sent to carry him. **6** They took
> their livestock, and their goods, which they had gotten in the land of Canaan, and came into
> Egypt—Jacob, and all his offspring with him, **7** his sons, and his sons' sons with him, his
> daughters, and his sons' daughters, and he brought all his offspring with him into Egypt.

***"The wagons which Pharaoh had sent."*** **They appear a fourth time** (45:19, 21, 27, **46:5**).
**The object that persuaded Jacob is the object that carries him**, and Genesis mentions its
provenance every single time.

***"They took their livestock, and their goods, which they had gotten in the land of Canaan."***
**Against 45:20** — *"don't concern yourselves about your belongings, for the good of all the land of
Egypt is yours."* **Pharaoh told them to leave the furniture and they brought the furniture.** **The
flocks turn out to matter** (46:32; 47:3), **and the wiki reads the disobedience as prudence rather
than defiance**: a family that arrives with its own herds is a family that can leave with them.

***"His daughters, and his sons' daughters."*** **Plural, twice, in a register that will name one of
each.** **Genesis says the women are there and then counts them out** (46:26). See
[[Names and Naming]].

### 46:8 — the first *bənê yiśrāʾēl* register

> **8** These are the names of the children of Israel, who came into Egypt, Jacob and his sons:
> Reuben, Jacob's firstborn.

***"These are the names of the children of Israel"*** — ***wəʾēlleh šəmôt bənê yiśrāʾēl***.
**Exodus opens by repeating this sentence almost word for word** (Exodus 1:1) **and takes its Hebrew
name, *Šəmôt*, *Names*, from it.** **Genesis 46:8 is where the second book of the Bible starts its
sentence**, and the register is given twice because the four hundred years between the two printings
are the point.

***"Reuben, Jacob's firstborn."*** **The title is given in full and it is the last time it is given
straight.** **49:3–4 will keep the word and remove the thing** — *"Reuben, you are my firstborn…
Boiling over like water, you shall not excel"* — and [[Judah]] is already walking the road ahead of him
(46:28). See [[Reuben]] and [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

### 46:12 — the paragraph that goes furthest

> **12** The sons of Judah: Er, Onan, Shelah, Perez, and Zerah; but Er and Onan died in the land of
> Canaan. The sons of Perez were Hezron and Hamul.

**It is the only branch in the register that runs to a fourth generation**, and **the only one that
stops to report deaths.** **[[Er]] and [[Onan]] are named in a list of people going into Egypt in
order to say that they are not** — the register's one backward glance, **and it lands on chapter 38,
the chapter the Joseph story was interrupted for.**

**[[Hezron (son of Perez)]] and [[Hamul]] are two names with no story attached**, and the line
through the first of them is Ruth 4:18–22 and Matthew 1:3. **Genesis puts the ancestry of David and
of Jesus in a subordinate clause about a man's grandsons**, in the middle of a travel document. See
[[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]] and
[[Two Sets of Twins - Jacob and Esau, Perez and Zerah]].

### 46:31–47:6 — the same speech, three times, with two edits

**Joseph rehearses what he will tell Pharaoh** (46:31–32), **then tells him** (47:1), **and the two
versions differ.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **46:31–32, the plan** | *"My brothers, and my father's house, who were in the land of Canaan, have come to me. These men are shepherds…"* |
| **47:1, the delivery** | *"My father and my brothers, with their flocks, their herds, and all that they own, have come out of the land of Canaan; and behold, they are in the land of Goshen."* |

**He drops the word *shepherds* from his own mouth and adds their location.** **By the time Pharaoh
hears about them they are already in [[Goshen]]** — **the grant at 47:6 ratifies a fait accompli**,
which is what *"they are in the land of Goshen"* is for. **Joseph has run his family's immigration
the way he ran the famine**: the outcome arranged first, the authorisation obtained after. See
[[Wisdom]].

***"From among his brothers he took five men"*** (47:2). **Genesis does not say which five and no
tradition in the text explains the number.** **The register has just named all eleven; the court sees
five.**

## People

- [[Jacob]] — **the pericope is his journey and it gives him three utterances: an answer, a lament,
  and a blessing said twice with no content reported.** He sacrifices at [[Beersheba]] *"to the God
  of his father, Isaac"* (46:1), **is called by his old name in a doubled vocative and answers
  *"Here I am"*** (46:2), and is told the one thing he has needed since 37:35 — **that he will not
  die alone and that his family will come back out.** He is *carried* in Egyptian wagons (46:5),
  says *"Now let me die"* on seeing Joseph's face (46:30), and tells a king that his hundred and
  thirty years have been ***few and evil*** (47:9). **He blesses Pharaoh on the way in and on the way
  out** (47:7, 10)
- [[Joseph]] — **stage-manages the whole arrival and is never said to consult anyone.** He harnesses
  his own chariot (46:29) — the second chariot of Egypt (41:43) — **weeps on his father's neck *a
  good while*, and then immediately starts briefing his brothers on what to say to the king**
  (46:31–34). **He edits his own script between the rehearsal and the delivery** (47:1), presents
  five of the eleven, brings his father in, **and settles them in [[Rameses]] and feeds them**
  (47:11–12), discharging the promise of 45:11 in the pericope's last clause
- [[God]] — **speaks once, for three verses, and it is the only divine speech in the Joseph cycle**
  (46:2–4). **Identifies himself as *the God of your father*** — Isaac, the patriarch forbidden to
  go to Egypt — **relocates the *great nation* promise of 12:2 to Egyptian soil, promises the
  descent and the return in one sentence, and ends with a detail about a deathbed.** **After 46:4 the
  narrator's silence resumes and is not broken again in Genesis**
- [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] — **meets the family at last, having invited them sight unseen at
  45:17–20.** Asks the one question Joseph predicted (47:3), **grants [[Goshen]] and offers the
  brothers charge of the royal livestock** (47:6), **asks the old man his age and is told a
  complaint**, and **is blessed twice by a foreigner on relief** (47:7, 10). **Genesis assigns him no
  reaction to any of it**
- [[Judah]] — **sent ahead to find the way** (46:28), which is the family's own verdict on who he has
  become; **his branch of the register runs a generation further than anyone else's** (46:12), to
  [[Hezron (son of Perez)]] and [[Hamul]]. He is one of the five men before Pharaoh only if the text
  is guessing, which it is not — **Genesis does not say who the five were**
- [[Leah]] — **the largest line, thirty-three including Jacob**, and the only one whose arithmetic
  requires him. **She is named as the mother and never as the wife**; the title goes to [[Rachel]],
  who is eleven years dead
- [[Rachel]] — ***"the sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife"*** (46:19). **Fourteen souls, the smallest of
  the four lines except Bilhah's, and the only one whose mother is given a status rather than a
  provenance.** She is dead and buried at 35:19–20 and **still the wife of record in the register her
  sons are listed in**
- [[Zilpah]] and [[Bilhah]] — **named as what [[Laban]] gave** (46:18, 25), **thirty years and a
  border later**. Between them they account for **twenty-three of the seventy**
- [[Dinah]] — **counted** (46:15). **It is the last mention of her in Scripture**, and it is a number
- [[Serah]] — ***"Serah their sister"*** (46:17), **the only granddaughter named in the register**,
  with no explanation given here or anywhere else in the Bible
- [[Shaul (son of Simeon)]] — ***"the son of a Canaanite woman"*** (46:10). **The register's one
  ethnic annotation**, attached to a man about whom nothing else is said, **in a list containing
  [[Judah]]'s five Canaanite-born sons and [[Joseph]]'s two Egyptian-born ones**
- [[Gershon]], [[Kohath]], [[Merari]] — **the three sons of [[Levi]]** (46:11), **named in Scripture
  for the first time here**, and the three houses that will carry the tabernacle. **[[Kohath]]'s line
  produces Moses and Aaron**
- [[Hezron (son of Perez)]] and [[Hamul]] — ***"the sons of Perez"*** (46:12). **The register's
  fourth generation, and the line to David and to Matthew 1**
- [[Er]] and [[Onan]] — **listed and then subtracted**: *"but Er and Onan died in the land of
  Canaan"* (46:12). **The only deaths the register reports**
- [[Reuben]] — ***"Jacob's firstborn"*** (46:8), **stated plainly for the last time**
- [[Simeon]], [[Levi]], [[Issachar]], [[Zebulun]], [[Gad]], [[Asher]], [[Dan (person)]],
  [[Naphtali]], [[Benjamin]] — **each named with his sons and nothing else.** [[Dan (person)]] has
  one son to [[Benjamin]]'s ten; **the tribe that will be the larger of the two in Numbers gets the
  shorter entry here**
- [[Perez]], [[Zerah]], [[Shelah (son of Judah)]] — **the survivors of chapter 38**, carried into the
  register as a matter of course
- [[Manasseh]] and [[Ephraim]] — **the two who were already in Egypt** (46:20, 27), **counted into
  the seventy from the far side of the border**
- [[Asenath]] and [[Potiphera]] — **the Egyptian mother and the pagan priest**, given a full pedigree
  inside Israel's founding register (46:20) **without a word of comment**
- [[Isaac]] — **not present and named twice** (46:1, and behind *"the God of your father"* at 46:3).
  **The whole Beersheba scene is addressed to the fact that he was told not to do this**
- [[Shua's Daughter]] — **unnamed here and the mother of five of the seventy.** The register flags
  [[Shaul (son of Simeon)]]'s Canaanite mother and passes hers

## Places

- [[Beersheba]] — **the last stop in the land and the place Genesis keeps returning to at
  departures.** [[Abraham]]'s tamarisk and *the Everlasting God* (21:33), [[Isaac]]'s altar and night
  theophany (26:23–25), **[[Jacob]]'s own departure for Haran** (28:10), **and now his departure for
  Egypt.** **It is the site of the only divine speech in the Joseph cycle**
- [[Egypt]] — **entered, this time to stay.** Named eleven times in the pericope. **The family that was
  promised [[Canaan]] takes up a legal possession here** (47:11), by a king's command, **with God's
  explicit permission and an explicit promise of return** (46:4)
- [[Goshen]] — **occupied.** Named seven times: Joseph assigns it (46:28), the family arrives before the
  king has granted it (46:28; 47:1), the brothers ask for it (47:4), **and Pharaoh grants it** (47:6).
  **The instrument that secures it is Egyptian contempt for shepherds** (46:34)
- [[Rameses]] — **enters Scripture at 47:11** as the district the family is settled in, ***"the best
  of the land."* Exodus 1:11 makes it a store-city built by slaves and Exodus 12:37 makes it the
  starting line of the Exodus.** **Genesis gives the address of the bondage as a gift**
- [[Canaan]] — **left.** *"Their goods, which they had gotten in the land of Canaan"* (46:6); *"Er and
  Onan died in the land of Canaan"* (46:12); *"the famine is severe in the land of Canaan"* (47:4).
  **The land of the promise appears three times in the pericope and every appearance is in the past
  tense or a complaint**
- [[On]] — [[Potiphera]]'s city and priesthood, carried into the register at 46:20
- [[Machpelah]] — **not named, and the reason *"a possession"* at 47:11 is a loaded word.** The only
  ***ʾăḥuzzâ*** Genesis has recorded before this is a grave (23:9, 20)

## Themes

- [[Providence]] — **the theme's only direct divine statement in the Joseph cycle** (46:2–4). **Where
  chapter 45 gave the doctrine as a character's testimony, this gives it as God's own speech**, and
  what it authorises is the descent into the country of the bondage. **The providence of Genesis 45
  was retrospective; this is prospective, and it is a command**
- [[Covenant]] — ***"there I will make of you a great nation"*** (46:3) **relocates 12:2 to foreign
  soil**, and *"I will also surely bring you up again"* (46:4) **makes the return a covenant term
  rather than a hope.** **The promise survives the family's departure from the land it was about**
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — **the pericope's central tension.** The family leaves [[Canaan]] with
  divine permission, receives an ***ʾăḥuzzâ*** in Egypt (47:11) when the only one it holds in Canaan
  is a tomb, and **its head describes his entire life as *my pilgrimage*** (47:9)
- [[Blessing]] — ***"Jacob blessed Pharaoh"*** twice (47:7, 10), **content unreported.** **The
  Abrahamic promise operating without ceremony**, with the powerless party doing the blessing
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — ***"in the visions of the night"*** (46:2), ***marʾōt hallaylâ***, **a
  phrase used nowhere else in the Torah.** **Genesis's last theophany**, and the only one in the book
  that authorises leaving the land
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — **[[Jacob]] sacrifices at [[Beersheba]] before the crossing** (46:1),
  and **the sacrifice is the question**: the answer comes that night. **It is the last sacrifice in
  Genesis**
- [[Famine and Plenty]] — **the engine, still running underneath.** *"The famine is severe in the land
  of Canaan"* (47:4) is the brothers' own reason for the request, **and 47:12's bread *according to
  their families* is a ration**
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — **the family meets the throne.** A grant of land, an offer of employment,
  **and a king who is blessed by an old shepherd and says nothing about it**. **Egypt is at its most
  hospitable here and the address it hands over is [[Rameses]]**
- [[Life and Death]] — ***"Joseph will close your eyes"*** (46:4); *"Now let me die"* (46:30); *"few
  and evil have been the days"* (47:9); **and [[Er]] and [[Onan]] subtracted from a list of the
  living** (46:12). **Four deaths handled in a pericope where nobody dies**
- [[Names and Naming]] — **seventy of them, and the register's whole method is the listing.** ***"These
  are the names of the children of Israel"*** (46:8) **is the sentence Exodus takes its Hebrew title
  from.** The women present are named collectively and counted out (46:7, 26)
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — ***"he presented himself to him"*** (46:29), **the verb of theophany**;
  *"since I have seen your face"* (46:30); **and a court that sees five brothers out of eleven**
  (47:2). **The pericope in which the family is finally looked at by Egypt**
- [[Suffering]] — ***"few and evil have been the days of the years of my life"*** (47:9). **The one
  verdict a patriarch passes on his own life in Genesis, delivered to a foreign king, and the book
  does not correct it**
- [[Faith]] — **[[Jacob]] sacrifices and waits for an answer before crossing a border he has already
  decided to cross** (45:28; 46:1). **The obedience is real and it is also belated**, and Genesis
  reports the sequence without comment
- [[Work]] — ***"What is your occupation?"*** (47:3). **The family's trade is the thing that gets it
  land and the thing that makes it unclean**, and both facts operate in the same two verses.
  [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] offers *"charge of my livestock"* to men whose livestock work he
  finds abominable
- [[The Nations]] — **three foreign mothers in one register** (46:10, 12, 20), **handled three
  different ways**, and **an Israelite household formally taking up residence inside an empire**
- [[Brotherhood]] — **eleven men travel together, five are chosen to stand before a king, and Genesis
  says nothing about how the other six took it.** [[Judah]] leads the way in; the text does not say
  whether [[Reuben]] noticed
- [[Priesthood]] — **[[Gershon]], [[Kohath]] and [[Merari]] enter Scripture as three names in a list**
  (46:11), **and [[Potiphera]], *priest of On*, is in the same register** (46:20). **The only priest
  named in the pericope is Egyptian**
- [[Hope]] — ***"I will also surely bring you up again"*** (46:4). **The sentence the rest of the
  Pentateuch is the working-out of**, spoken to a man who will not live to see it

## Notes

**On the arithmetic of the seventy.** The four subtotals are **33 + 16 + 14 + 7 = 70** (46:15, 18, 22,
25, 27). **Zilpah's, Rachel's and Bilhah's reproduce exactly from the names printed.** **Leah's does
not**: her section names thirty-two living people once [[Er]] and [[Onan]] are removed as 46:12
directs. **The wiki's reading is that [[Jacob]] is the thirty-third**, on the strength of 46:8, which
puts him inside the register's own scope — *"the children of Israel, who came into Egypt, Jacob and
his sons"* — **and on the strength of 46:26–27, whose sixty-six and seventy only reconcile if he is
counted once and only once.** **The alternative, that a name has dropped out of Leah's list in
transmission, is possible and unprovable**, and the page does not adopt it. **The arithmetic is set
out in full on**
[[Seventy Souls - The Count of Jacob's House in Genesis, Exodus and Acts]].

**On *"he presented himself to him"* (46:29).** The Hebrew is ***wayyērāʾ ʾēlāyw***, and **every other
occurrence of that verb-form in Genesis has God as its subject** — the eight *appeared to* verses
listed above. **The wiki records the datum and
builds nothing on it.** The Niphal of *rāʾâ* means *was seen* or *showed himself* and is ordinary
Hebrew for a person becoming visible; **a coincidence of form is not an allusion**, and Genesis gives
no other signal here. **It is noted because the coincidence is exact and because the WEB's
*"presented himself"* conceals it.**

**On who the subject of 46:29 is.** WEB assigns the chariot, the going up, the appearing, the falling
and the weeping all to [[Joseph]], and the Hebrew is consistent with that throughout. **An old reading
takes [[Jacob]] as the subject of the weeping** and supplies him a recitation of the Shema instead;
**it has no support in the text** and the wiki follows the WEB.

**On *Iob* at 46:13.** **The WEB's spelling invites a collision with the book of Job and there is
none.** The name is *yôb* (the parallel list at Numbers 26:24 reads *Jashub*); **[[Job (person)]] is
*ʾiyyôb*, a different word.** **This is the second false lead the wiki has had to disarm around that
name**, after the Septuagint's identification of [[Jobab]] with Job at 36:33.

**On the five brothers (47:2).** **Genesis does not say which five, and does not say why five.** The
page records the fact and the silence. **No attempt is made to reconstruct the list.**

**On the Hebrew *tôʿēbâ* and the strategy built on it.** **46:34's *"every shepherd is an abomination
to the Egyptians"* is the narrator's clause, not Joseph's** — the WEB punctuates it inside the
quotation, but it reads as the reason supplied to the reader either way. **Egyptian sources do not
attest a general revulsion at herdsmen**, and the wiki does not defend the statement historically;
**what it notes is the use Joseph makes of it.** He wants his family in [[Goshen]] and out of the
court's sight, **and the fastest route to that is to make the court want it too.**

**On Jacob's verdict at 47:9.** **The page takes *"few and evil"* at face value** rather than reading
it as a pious formula or as ingratitude. **Genesis has given the reader every one of the evils in
question** and gives Jacob a longer and better-documented grievance than any other character in the
book. **That he says it in the month he gets his son back, to a king, unprompted, is the point**, and
**Genesis neither rebukes it nor endorses it.**

**On the counts.** In the forty-nine verses of the pericope, *Egypt* appears **eleven times**,
*Pharaoh* **sixteen**, *Jacob* **twenty**, *Joseph* **thirteen**, *Israel* **six**, *Goshen*
**seven**, *land* **seventeen**, *souls* **eight**, and *blessed* **twice**. **Counted from the WEB
text in `raw/bible/Genesis.md`**, and offered as the shape of the passage rather than as a Hebrew
concordance figure. **The register is responsible for most of the *sons*, and the name *Israel* is
outnumbered by *Jacob* better than three to one in the pericope where the nation is first counted.**

**On the names given no page.** **The register contains roughly fifty individuals who appear here and
in the parallel lists of Numbers 26 and 1 Chronicles 2–8 and nowhere else in Scripture** — Hanoch,
Pallu, Carmi, Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar, Tola, Puvah, Sered, Jahleel, Ziphion, Haggi, Shuni,
Ezbon, Eri, Arodi, Areli, Imnah, Ishvah, Ishvi, Beriah, Heber, Malchiel, Becher, Ashbel, Gera,
Naaman, Ehi, Rosh, Muppim, Huppim, Ard, Hushim, Jahzeel, Guni, Jezer, Shillem, and the rest. **They
are listed on this page and given no pages of their own**, because a page whose whole content is *"a
son of Gad, named at 46:16"* is a page that makes the wiki harder to read rather than richer. **Seven
of the register's names were given pages**, on the grounds that Scripture itself develops them:
[[Gershon]], [[Kohath]], [[Merari]], [[Serah]], [[Hezron (son of Perez)]], [[Hamul]] and
[[Shaul (son of Simeon)]]. **Note also that *Hezron* and *Bela* and *Elon* each occur twice in
Genesis with two different referents**, which is why the page created here is qualified.

**Links left for later pericopes.** Joseph's land policy and the sale of Egypt (47:13–26), Jacob's
oath about his burial (47:29–31), *"the God who has fed me all my life long"* (48:15), the blessings
of 49, the death and the carrying-up (49:33–50:14), and *"I will nourish you and your little ones"*
(50:21) **are named above in prose and marked or dated rather than linked** — their passage pages do
not exist yet.
