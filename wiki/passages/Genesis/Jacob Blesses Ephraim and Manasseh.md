# Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 48:1–22

## Summary

**A blind man crosses his hands on purpose, and two boys born to an Egyptian priest's daughter become
tribes of Israel.**

The pericope does two distinct legal things and one narrative thing. **48:5–6 is an adoption** —
[[Joseph]]'s two sons are transferred into [[Jacob]]'s own sonship, *"even as [[Reuben]] and
[[Simeon]]"*, with any later sons left to Joseph. **48:13–20 is a blessing given in the wrong order on
purpose** — the right hand goes to the younger, the father of the boys objects, and the grandfather
refuses to move it. **Around both sits a deathbed scene**: the summons (1–2), the recital of the
[[Bethel]] promise (3–4), an abrupt sentence about [[Rachel]]'s grave (7), the question *"Who are
these?"* (8–12), and two closing verses of legacy — God's presence, and a portion taken *"with my
sword and with my bow"* (21–22).

**The chapter is the fourth time Genesis puts a younger son ahead of an elder, and the first time it
is done in the open.** [[Isaac]] over [[Ishmael]] came by a divine instruction and a dismissal;
[[Jacob]] over [[Esau]] came by a sold birthright and a stolen blessing; [[Perez]] over [[Zerah]]
came out of the womb by an accident of a scarlet thread. **Here there is no deceit, no purchase, no
struggle, and nobody is disinherited** — ***"He also will become a people, and he also will be
great"*** (48:19). See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**And it is a deliberate rerun of Genesis 27 with the deception removed.** An old man whose eyes have
failed, a blessing, the younger son under the hand that should have covered the elder, a protest, and
a refusal to revise — **every element of the theft of [[Esau]]'s blessing is present, and the one
thing missing is the lie.** ***"Guiding his hands knowingly"*** (48:14) **is the narrator's insurance
against the obvious reading**: Jacob cannot see the boys and is not mistaken about them. Filed as
[[Two Blind Fathers - Genesis 27 and Genesis 48]].

### The name switch inside one verse

> **2** Someone told Jacob, and said, "Behold, your son Joseph comes to you," and Israel strengthened
> himself, and sat on the bed.

**Genesis has two names for this man and has used them loosely for thirteen chapters. Here it uses
both in one sentence and the division of labour is exact.** ***Jacob*** **is told he has a visitor
(48:2) and recites what God once said to him (48:3). *Israel* does everything else** — strengthens
himself and sits up (2), sees (8), cannot see (10), speaks of what God has let him see (11), holds
out the right hand (14), and pronounces the future (20, 21). **The invalid is Jacob; the patriarch is
Israel**, and at 48:20 the name has stopped being a man at all: *"In you will **Israel** bless."* See
[[Renamed and Still Called Jacob - The Two Namings of Israel]].

**Two mentions of *Jacob* against nine of *Israel* in twenty-two verses**, which inverts the ratio of
the surrounding chapters.

### *"God Almighty appeared to me at Luz"*

> **3** Jacob said to Joseph, "God Almighty appeared to me at Luz in the land of Canaan, and blessed
> me, **4** and said to me, 'Behold, I will make you fruitful, and multiply you, and I will make of
> you a company of peoples, and will give this land to your offspring after you for an everlasting
> possession.'"

**He is quoting 35:11–12, and the quotation is not exact.** Set side by side:

| Genesis 35:11–12 | Genesis 48:4 |
|---|---|
| *"I am God Almighty"* | *"God Almighty appeared to me"* |
| *"Be fruitful and multiply"* — **an imperative** | *"I will make you fruitful, and multiply you"* — **a promise** |
| *"A nation and a company of nations will be from you"* | *"I will make of you a company of peoples"* |
| *"and kings will come out of your body"* | **dropped** |
| *"The land which I gave to Abraham and Isaac, I will give it to you"* | *"will give this land to your offspring after you for an everlasting possession"* |

**Jacob turns a command into a guarantee, drops the kings, and adds *for an everlasting possession***
— ***ʾăḥuzzat ʿôlām***, **which is the phrase from [[Abraham]]'s covenant** (17:8) **and not from his
own.** **The wiki records the paraphrase as a paraphrase**: Genesis lets a dying man quote God from
memory and does not correct him. See [[Covenant]] and [[Remembrance]].

***"At Luz."*** **He names the place by the name it had before he renamed it.** Bethel is his own
coinage — *"he called the name of that place Bethel, but the name of the city was Luz at the first"*
(28:19) — **and here, recalling the second and greater appearance there, he reverts to the Canaanite
name**, exactly as the narrator did at 35:6 (*"Jacob came to Luz (that is, Bethel)"*). See [[Bethel]]
and [[Names and Naming]].

***El Shaddai.*** **The fourth of five occurrences in Genesis** (17:1; 28:3; 35:11; 48:3; 49:25),
**and the third that Jacob is personally attached to.** See
[[God Almighty - El Shaddai in Genesis and Exodus]] and [[The Names of God]].

### The adoption

> **5** Now your two sons, who were born to you in the land of Egypt before I came to you into Egypt,
> are mine; Ephraim and Manasseh, even as Reuben and Simeon, will be mine. **6** Your issue, whom you
> become the father of after them, will be yours. They will be called after the name of their
> brothers in their inheritance.

**This is the only adoption formula spoken by an adopter anywhere in the Torah, and it is four words
long in Hebrew**: ***lî-hēm***, ***they are mine***, said twice.

**Four things in it are load-bearing.**

**1. The comparison names the two sons Jacob is about to curse.** *"Even as [[Reuben]] and
[[Simeon]]"* — **the firstborn and the second-born**, so the rank conferred is first and second. **But
Reuben has lain with [[Bilhah]]** (35:22) **and Simeon has sacked [[Shechem (city)]]** (34:25), **and
one chapter later both are told so to their faces**: *"you shall not excel"* (49:4), *"cursed be their
anger"* (49:7). **Jacob picks as his measure precisely the two positions that are about to be
vacated**, and 1 Chronicles 5:1–2 reads the chapter that way: *"his birthright was given to the sons
of Joseph."* Filed as [[Two Tribes in Place of One - The Adoption of Ephraim and Manasseh]].

**2. The boys are Egyptian on their mother's side and the text says so in the same breath as the
adoption.** *"Who were born to you in the land of Egypt."* **Their mother is [[Asenath]], daughter of
[[Potiphera]], priest of [[On]]** (41:45, 50). **The two most populous tribes of the northern kingdom
begin as the half-Egyptian grandsons of a man who would not let his own sons marry Canaanites.** See
[[The Nations]] and [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]].

**3. *Ephraim and Manasseh* — in that order, seven verses before the crossed hands.** **Genesis has
named them elder-first twice** (41:51–52; 46:20) **and reverses the order here, in the adoption
sentence, before the blessing scene that is supposed to establish it.** **48:20 then says it outright:
*"He set Ephraim before Manasseh."*** The reversal is announced, enacted, and summarised, in that
order.

**4. Clause 6 never activates.** *"Your issue, whom you become the father of after them, will be
yours"* — **and Scripture records no further sons of Joseph.** **The provision is a legal tidy-up for
a contingency that does not arise**, and it is the clearest sign that 48:5 is meant as a transfer of
two specific persons and not as a general policy.

### Verse 7, and why it is not a digression

> **7** As for me, when I came from Paddan, Rachel died by me in the land of Canaan on the way, when
> there was still some distance to come to Ephrath, and I buried her there on the way to Ephrath
> (also called Bethlehem).

**Read as narration this is the worst non-sequitur in Genesis.** A man adopts two grandsons and then,
without transition, mentions a death sixty years old. **Read as part of the legal instrument it is the
premise.**

**[[Rachel]] bore two sons and died bearing the second** (35:16–19). **[[Leah]] bore six.** **The
double portion Jacob is in the act of handing to Rachel's firstborn is the share she did not live to
produce herself**, and 48:7 is the sentence that supplies the reason — ***"Rachel died by me"***,
*ʿālay*, **on me, to my cost.** **The verse is an explanation, and Genesis, as usual, does not label
it one.** The wiki records the reading and marks it as a reading.

**The geography carries a second point.** **Four verses earlier he asked to be carried to [[Machpelah]]**
(47:29–30); **four verses later he will be given a portion at Shechem** (48:22); **and in between he
says that the wife he loved is buried by a road** — *"there was still some distance to come to
Ephrath"* — **in neither place.** **49:31 will list five people in the cave and she is not among
them.** See [[Burial and the Grave]],
[[Buried at Machpelah - The Six Graves of Genesis]] and
[[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]].

***"When I came from Paddan."*** **Everywhere else the book gives the region its full name, *Paddan
Aram*** (25:20; 28:2; 31:18; 35:9; 46:15); **this is the only time it is shortened, and the shortening
is in direct speech, which suits it.** See [[Aram Naharaim]].

### *"Who are these?"*

> **8** Israel saw Joseph's sons, and said, "Who are these?" **9** Joseph said to his father, "They
> are my sons, whom God has given me here." He said, "Please bring them to me, and I will bless them."
> **10** Now the eyes of Israel were dim for age, so that he couldn't see. He brought them near to
> him; and he kissed them, and embraced them. **11** Israel said to Joseph, "I didn't think I would
> see your face, and behold, God has let me see your offspring also." **12** Joseph brought them out
> from between his knees, and he bowed himself with his face to the earth.

***"Who are these?"*** — ***mî-ʾēlleh***, **and it is the question [[Esau]] asked Jacob about his
children at 33:5.** **Jacob's answer then was *"The children whom God has graciously given your
servant"*; Joseph's answer now is *"my sons, whom God has given me here."*** **The same question, the
same theology, one generation apart, and the wiki notes that Jacob is on the other side of it.** See
[[Seeing and Being Seen]].

**The question is asked by a man who cannot see and who has already named both boys in verse 5.**
**It is not ignorance about who they are; it is the formal identification of the parties**, which is
why Joseph answers with a legal statement (*they are my sons*) rather than with names. **Genesis puts
the medical note immediately after, in verse 10, as if to forestall the other reading.**

***"Dim for age"*** — ***kābedû mizzōqen***, **the eyes were heavy.** **[[Isaac]]'s were *kāhâ* at
27:1**, a different verb for the same condition. **Both patriarchs go blind in the verse that begins
their last blessing**, and only one of them is fooled by it.

***"He kissed them, and embraced them"*** — **the order is inverted from every other embrace in the
book** (29:13; 33:4; 45:15 all embrace first). **A blind man finds a face before he holds it.**

***"From between his knees."*** **Whose knees the WEB leaves open, and the Hebrew does too.** **If
they are Jacob's, the verse records the adoption gesture itself** — **compare [[Rachel]] at 30:3,
*"that she may bear on my knees, and I also may obtain children by her"***, **the only other knee-idiom
of parentage in Genesis, and also spoken by a parent claiming children not born to them.** **The
Hebrew for *knees*, *birkayim*, shares its three consonants with *bārak*, to bless**, which is the
word this pericope uses six times. See [[Adoption]].

***"He bowed himself with his face to the earth."*** **[[Joseph]] bows — the fifth time someone bows
in this family's story and the second in two pericopes** (47:31). **The man the sheaves and the stars
bowed to** (37:7–10) **is on the floor in front of his father**, and again Genesis declines to
mention the dream. See [[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]].

### The crossed hands

> **13** Joseph took them both, Ephraim in his right hand toward Israel's left hand, and Manasseh in
> his left hand toward Israel's right hand, and brought them near to him. **14** Israel stretched out
> his right hand, and laid it on Ephraim's head, who was the younger, and his left hand on Manasseh's
> head, guiding his hands knowingly, for Manasseh was the firstborn.

**Verse 13 is staged with unusual care, and the care is Joseph's.** **He positions the boys so that
the old man's right hand will fall on the elder without having to be aimed** — Ephraim to Israel's
left, Manasseh to Israel's right. **It is the arrangement a son makes for a father who cannot see.**
**Verse 14 undoes it in one movement.**

***Śikkēl ʾet-yādāyw*** — **WEB *"guiding his hands knowingly"* — is the interpretive crux of the
chapter and the verb occurs nowhere else in this sense in the Bible.** **The root is *śēkel*,
*prudence*, *insight*** — the noun of 41:33, 39, where Pharaoh looks for a man *discreet and wise*.
**Older versions render it *crossed*, which is what the hands must have done and not what the word
says.** **What the word says is that the crossing was an act of understanding**, and it is placed in
the verse immediately before the clause *"for Manasseh was the firstborn"*, **so that the narrator
states the fact Jacob is overriding in the same breath as the overriding.**

**This is the first time in Scripture that anyone lays a hand on a head to bless.** **The gesture will
become the ordination of Joshua** (Numbers 27:18–23), **the transfer of the Levites' office**
(Numbers 8:10), **and the way Jesus blesses children** (Mark 10:16). **Its first appearance is a
grandfather's, and it is performed wrong on purpose.** Filed as
[[Hands on the Head - The First Blessing by Touch]].

### The three names of God

> **15** He blessed Joseph, and said, "The God before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, the
> God who has fed me all my life long to this day, **16** the angel who has redeemed me from all
> evil, bless the lads, and let my name be named on them, and the name of my fathers Abraham and
> Isaac. Let them grow into a multitude upon the earth."

**Three designations, one verb, and a grammatical oddity.** *"He blessed **Joseph**"* — **and what
follows is a prayer over the boys.** **Joseph is blessed in the blessing of his sons and receives no
other blessing in the pericope**, which is the adoption restated as grammar.

**The three titles are Jacob's own summary of his life, and none of them is a standard formula.**

***"The God before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked."*** ***Hithallēk***, **the verb of
[[Enoch (son of Jared)]]** (5:22, 24) **and [[Noah]]** (6:9), **and the command given to Abram in the
one other verse that pairs it with El Shaddai**: *"I am God Almighty. Walk before me, and be
blameless"* (17:1). **Jacob claims the verb for his fathers and does not claim it for himself.** See
[[Walking with God]].

***"The God who has fed me all my life long to this day."*** **The Hebrew is *hārōʿeh ʾōtî* — *the one
shepherding me* — and this is the first time in the Bible that God is the subject of that verb.**
**The man who says it spent twenty years as a hired shepherd and itemised the job under oath**: *"That
which was torn by animals I didn't bring to you … of my hand you required it"* (31:39). **He has one
professional metaphor and he applies it to God on his deathbed**, and one chapter later he will use
the noun — *"from there is the shepherd, the stone of Israel"* (49:24). **Psalm 23:1 and John 10:11
are downstream of this verse.** Filed as
[[The God Who Has Fed Me - The First Time God Is a Shepherd]].

***"The angel who has redeemed me from all evil."*** ***Haggōʾēl*** — **and *gāʾal* occurs nowhere
else in Genesis.** **The word that will carry the exodus** (*"I will redeem you with an outstretched
arm"*, Exodus 6:6), **the law of the kinsman** (Leviticus 25:25, 47–49), **Boaz** (Ruth 4), **Job's
*"I know that my Redeemer lives"* (Job 19:25) **and the whole prophetic title** (*"your Redeemer, the
Holy One of Israel"*, Isaiah 41:14) **enters Scripture here, in a blessing over two children, with an
angel as its subject.** See [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]].

***"The angel"*** **is set in apposition to *"the God"* twice over and governs the same singular
imperative, *bless*.** **Jacob has done this before** — *"the angel of God said to me in the dream …
I am the God of Bethel"* (31:11, 13) — **and he has wrestled with something he afterwards called
God** (32:28–30). **Genesis stacks the three titles and supplies no doctrine of their relation.** See
[[The Angel of Yahweh]].

***"From all evil"*** — ***mikkol-rāʿ***, **and the same word is his own verdict on his years**:
*"few and evil have been the days of the years of my life"* (47:9). **He calls the days evil to
Pharaoh and calls himself redeemed from evil to God**, eleven verses apart, and Genesis does not
reconcile the two. See [[Suffering]] and [[Providence]].

***"Let my name be named on them."*** **The adoption of verse 5 is completed as a naming** —
*yiqqārēʾ šemî bāhem* — **and the name conferred is not only Jacob's but *"the name of my fathers
Abraham and Isaac"*.** **Two half-Egyptian boys are written into a three-generation line by a
sentence.** See [[Names and Naming]] and [[Call and Election]].

### *"I know, my son, I know"*

> **17** When Joseph saw that his father laid his right hand on the head of Ephraim, it displeased
> him. He held up his father's hand, to remove it from Ephraim's head to Manasseh's head. **18**
> Joseph said to his father, "Not so, my father; for this is the firstborn; put your right hand on
> his head." **19** His father refused, and said, "I know, my son, I know. He also will become a
> people, and he also will be great. However, his younger brother will be greater than he, and his
> offspring will become a multitude of nations."

***Yādaʿtî bənî yādaʿtî.*** **Three words, and they settle what *śikkēl* had already implied.** **The
protest is not rejected on the merits; it is answered with the statement that the fact it rests on
was never in doubt.**

**Compare the same scene in chapter 27.** **[[Isaac]] *"trembled violently"* when he learned what he
had done and could not undo it** — *"I have blessed him? Yes, he will be blessed"* (27:33). **Jacob
does not tremble, because nothing has been done to him.** **The irrevocability is the same and the
ignorance is gone.** See [[Yes, He Will Be Blessed - The Irrevocable Word]].

***"He also will become a people, and he also will be great."*** **Nobody is cursed in this chapter.**
**Manasseh loses a position and keeps a future**, which distinguishes 48:19 from 27:39–40, where
Esau's blessing is a description of subjection. **The wiki notes the difference as the difference
between a reversal and a disinheritance.**

***"A multitude of nations"*** — ***məlōʾ haggôyim***, **literally *the fullness of the nations***,
**and the phrase occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible.** **Romans 11:25's *"until the fullness of the
Gentiles has come in"* is the same two words in Greek.** **The wiki records the verbal identity and
does not claim that Paul is quoting Genesis 48:19**, because he does not say so and the phrase was
available to him from the Septuagint's ordinary vocabulary. See [[The Nations]] and
[[To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11]].

### The formula

> **20** He blessed them that day, saying, "In you will Israel bless, saying, 'God make you as Ephraim
> and as Manasseh'" He set Ephraim before Manasseh.

**The pericope's one sentence about the future of the blessing itself.** **Not what the boys will
receive, but what other people will say when they want to bless someone.**

**The Old Testament preserves exactly one other *make-you-like* blessing** — *"Yahweh make you like
[[Rachel]] and like [[Leah]], who both built the house of Israel … let your house be like the house of
[[Perez]]"* (Ruth 4:11–12) — **and one *make-you-like* curse**, Jeremiah 29:22, *"Yahweh make you like
Zedekiah and like Ahab."* **All three name a pair.** Filed as
[[God Make You as Ephraim and as Manasseh - The Blessing Formula]]; see
[[Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel]].

***"He set Ephraim before Manasseh."*** **The narrator's closing note, and the third statement of the
same fact** — the adoption order at 48:5, the right hand at 48:14, and this. **Genesis is making sure
the reader cannot mistake it for an accident.**

### *"I am dying, but God will be with you"*

> **21** Israel said to Joseph, "Behold, I am dying, but God will be with you, and bring you again to
> the land of your fathers. **22** Moreover I have given to you one portion above your brothers, which
> I took out of the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow."

***"God will be with you, and bring you again"*** — **the promise of 46:4 handed on**, *"I will go
down with you into Egypt. I will also surely bring you up again."* **[[Joseph]] will repeat this
sentence almost word for word as his own last words**: *"I am dying, but God will surely visit you,
and bring you up out of this land"* (50:24). **A dying father tells a son that God will bring the
family home; the son says it to his brothers fifty-five years later; neither of them lives to see
it.** See [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]] and
[[Don't Go Down to Egypt - The Three Patriarchs and the Same Road]].

***"One portion above your brothers"*** — ***šəkem ʾeḥād***, **and the noun is also the name of the
town.** **Read as a common noun it is *one shoulder*, a share; read as the place name it is *one
Shechem*, and Joshua 24:32 buries Joseph's bones there.** **The double meaning is almost certainly
intended and the WEB translates the common noun.**

***"Which I took out of the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow."*** **Genesis narrates
no such conquest by Jacob.** **What it narrates at Shechem is a purchase** — *"he bought the parcel of
ground … for one hundred pieces of money"* (33:19) — **and a massacre he disowned on grounds of
safety** (34:30). **Here, on his deathbed, he claims the ground by right of arms.** **Three accounts,
and the book retracts none of them.** Filed already as
[[Bought, Sacked, or Taken by the Sword - How Jacob Got Shechem]]; see [[War]] and
[[Land and Sojourning]].

## People

- [[Jacob]] — **blind, propped up on a bed, and legally busy.** He recites a promise (48:3–4),
  **adopts two grandsons as sons** (48:5), explains himself by way of a sixty-year-old grave (48:7),
  asks who the boys are and is told (48:8–9), **crosses his hands knowingly** (48:14), names God three
  ways (48:15–16), **refuses his son's correction in three words** (48:19), coins a blessing formula
  (48:20), and **claims ground at Shechem by the sword** (48:22). **He is called *Jacob* twice and
  *Israel* nine times**, and the switch is not random
- [[Joseph]] — **the only person in the chapter who is wrong about anything.** He brings his sons
  (48:1), answers the identification (48:9), bows to the ground (48:12), **arranges the boys correctly
  and is overruled** (48:13–14, 17–18), and is himself the grammatical object of the blessing he does
  not receive (48:15). ***"It displeased him"*** — ***wayyēraʿ bəʿênāyw***, **the same construction
  Genesis uses of Yahweh at 38:10** — **and he acts on it physically, holding his father's hand up to
  move it.** **The vizier of Egypt handles an old man's arm and loses**
- [[Ephraim]] — **the younger, named first in the adoption, given the right hand, set before his
  brother, and told his offspring will be *a multitude of nations*** (48:5, 14, 19, 20). **He says
  nothing and does nothing; the whole chapter happens to him.** **The name will belong to the leading
  tribe of the north and then to the northern kingdom itself in Hosea, Isaiah and Jeremiah**
- [[Manasseh]] — **called *the firstborn* by the narrator in the verse that demotes him** (48:14) **and
  by his father in the protest** (48:18). **He is not cursed and not disinherited**: *"He also will
  become a people, and he also will be great"* (48:19). **The only elder brother in Genesis who is
  passed over and loses nothing but precedence.** See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]]
- [[Rachel]] — **dead for decades and the hinge of the legal argument** (48:7). **Two sons against
  [[Leah]]'s six, and a double portion arriving through the elder of them.** **Buried on a road, not
  in [[Machpelah]]**, by the husband who four verses earlier demanded [[Machpelah]] for himself
- [[Reuben]] and [[Simeon]] — **named once, as the measure of the rank conferred** (48:5), **and the
  next chapter takes that rank off both of them** (49:3–7). **The comparison is the strongest internal
  evidence that Genesis 48 and Genesis 49 are meant to be read together**
- [[God]] — **named seven times in twenty-two verses and titled three ways in one sentence** — *El
  Shaddai* who appeared at [[Bethel]] (48:3), the God *before whom* the fathers walked, the God *who
  has fed me*, and *the angel who has redeemed me* (48:15–16). **Also the subject of both other men's
  sentences**: *"whom God has given me here"* (48:9), *"God has let me see your offspring"* (48:11)
- [[The Angel of Yahweh]] — **invoked as a blessing-giver alongside God and governing the same verb**
  (48:16). **The first *gōʾēl* in Scripture, and it is an angel**
- [[Abraham]] and [[Isaac]] — **not present and named twice each**, both times as a pair and both times
  in the blessing: *"before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked"* and *"the name of my fathers
  Abraham and Isaac"* (48:15–16). **The adoption writes two boys into their line explicitly, by name**
- **Asenath** — **not named, and the reason the chapter is remarkable.** *"Your two sons, who were born
  to you in the land of Egypt"* (48:5) **is as close as Genesis comes here to mentioning that the
  mother of these two tribes was [[Asenath]], daughter of a priest of [[On]]** (41:45)

## Places

- [[Bethel]] — **called *Luz*, by the man who renamed it** (48:3). **The place of both of Jacob's
  theophanies**, and the one he cites is the second (35:9–13), which is where *El Shaddai*, the
  fruitfulness and the company of nations all come from
- [[Canaan]] — **twice, and both times at a distance.** It is where God appeared *"in the land of
  Canaan"* (48:3) and where [[Rachel]] died *"in the land of Canaan on the way"* (48:7). **The land of
  the promise appears in this pericope only as memory: a theophany and a grave**
- [[Egypt]] — **twice, in one verse, as the boys' birthplace and the narrator of the adoption's
  timestamp**: *"born to you in the land of Egypt before I came to you into Egypt"* (48:5). **The two
  half-tribes of Joseph are the only tribes of Israel born outside the land**
- [[Bethlehem]] — **as *Ephrath*, twice in one verse, with the later name supplied in a parenthesis
  for the reader** (48:7), exactly as at 35:19
- [[Aram Naharaim]] — **as *Paddan*, the only time Genesis shortens *Paddan Aram*** (48:7)
- [[Shechem (city)]] — **not named and unmistakably meant.** *"One portion"* is *šəkem ʾeḥād* (48:22),
  **the ground of 33:19 and 34:25–30**, and Joshua 24:32 will bury Joseph in it
- **The land of the Amorite** — 48:22. **Genesis has used *Amorite* for the pre-Israelite population
  since 14:13** and never for the men of Shechem, who are Hivites (34:2). **Jacob's deathbed
  vocabulary is the conquest's, not the narrative's**

## Themes

- [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] — **the theme's clearest and cleanest instance.** *"Manasseh was
  the firstborn"* (48:14) is stated by the narrator inside the sentence that overrides it, and
  repeated by Joseph in protest (48:18). **Fourth reversal in Genesis, and the only one with no victim**
- [[Adoption]] — **the Bible's first adoption, and its only formula spoken by the adopting parent**:
  *"are mine … will be mine"* (48:5). **The knees of 48:12, the naming of 48:16, and the comparison to
  [[Reuben]] and [[Simeon]] are the three mechanisms by which it is accomplished**
- [[Blessing]] — ***bārak*** **six times in twenty-two verses**, in four different senses: what God did
  to Jacob at Luz (48:3), what Jacob intends for the boys (48:9), what he does to Joseph by blessing
  them (48:15), and **what Israel as a nation will do with their names for ever** (48:20)
- [[Covenant]] — **the promise handed down by quotation rather than by a new theophany.** God does not
  speak in this pericope; **Jacob speaks God's words from memory, imprecisely, and the transmission is
  the event** (48:3–4, 21)
- [[Names and Naming]] — **four operations on names in one chapter**: a town called by its old name
  (48:3), two boys put in a new order (48:5, 20), *"let my name be named on them"* (48:16), and **a
  blessing formula that turns two personal names into liturgy** (48:20)
- [[Providence]] — ***"the God who has fed me all my life long to this day"*** (48:15) **is the whole
  of Genesis 25–48 in nine words, spoken by the man who lived it**, and it makes no distinction between
  the years at Laban's, the years mourning a son he believed dead, and the seventeen in Goshen
- [[Walking with God]] — **the verb is granted to [[Abraham]] and [[Isaac]] and not claimed for
  himself** (48:15). **Compare 17:1, the only other verse where *walk before me* and *El Shaddai* stand
  together**
- [[The Names of God]] — **four designations in four verses**: *El Shaddai* (48:3), *the God before
  whom my fathers walked*, *the God who has fed me*, *the angel who has redeemed me* (48:15–16).
  **Three of the four are relational participles rather than titles**
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — **the blessing is predictive and specific**: *a multitude of nations*, *his
  younger brother will be greater than he* (48:19), *God will … bring you again to the land of your
  fathers* (48:21). **None of it is introduced as a revelation; a dying man simply says it**
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — **three claims on ground in two verses.** God gave the land *for an
  everlasting possession* (48:4); God will *bring you again* to it (48:21); and Jacob personally *took*
  a portion of it *with my sword and with my bow* (48:22). **The man making all three is dying in Egypt**
- [[Burial and the Grave]] — **48:7 is the only thing Jacob says about [[Rachel]] in the whole
  pericope, and it is about where she is lying**. **Set against 47:29–30 and 49:29–32 it is the one
  grave in the family Jacob does not ask to share**
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — **a blind man's chapter.** *"Israel saw Joseph's sons"* (48:8) and *"the
  eyes of Israel were dim … so that he couldn't see"* (48:10) are two verses apart; **and between them
  he says *"God has let me see your offspring also"*** (48:11), which is the only kind of seeing the
  pericope credits him with
- [[The Nations]] — ***məlōʾ haggôyim***, *a multitude of nations*, **promised to the half-Egyptian
  younger son of an Egyptian mother** (48:19), **against *"a company of peoples"* promised to Jacob
  himself** (48:4). **The two most populous tribes of Israel start as foreigners on their mother's side**
- [[Suffering]] — ***"redeemed me from all evil"*** (48:16) **against *"few and evil have been the days
  of the years of my life"*** (47:9). **The same word, eleven verses and one chapter division apart,
  and the wiki records both without harmonising them**
- [[Remembrance]] — **the pericope is almost entirely recollection**: a theophany at Luz, a death on the
  road to Ephrath, a lifetime of being shepherded, a rescue from all evil, a fight with an Amorite the
  book never narrates. **Genesis lets him remember and does not check his work**
- [[Faith]] — **Hebrews 11:21 selects this scene, and only this scene, from Jacob's whole life**: *"By
  faith, Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph."* **The New Testament's verdict
  on Jacob rests on Genesis 48.** See
  [[On the Bed's Head or on His Staff - Genesis 47.31 in Hebrews 11.21]]

## Notes

**On reading 48:7 as a legal premise rather than a digression.** The verse has been called an
interpolation for two centuries, on the ground that a note about [[Rachel]]'s death has no business
between an adoption and a blessing. **The wiki files it as the premise of the adoption** — Rachel bore
two sons and died, [[Leah]] bore six, and the double portion Jacob is handing to Rachel's firstborn is
the share she did not live to bear — **and flags that this is a reading.** Genesis supplies the verse
and no connective. **The competing readings** (a dying man's free association; a note explaining why
Joseph and not Jacob will have to bury him in [[Canaan]]; an editorial seam) **are all defensible and
none is stated in the text.**

**On whose knees (48:12).** *"Joseph brought them out from between his knees"* is ambiguous in the
Hebrew as in the English. **If the knees are Jacob's, the verse records the adoption gesture**, and
[[Rachel]]'s *"that she may bear on my knees"* (30:3) is the only other place in Genesis where knees
do that work. **If they are Joseph's, the boys have simply been standing in front of their father
while he knelt or bowed.** **The wiki prefers the first and says so**, because 48:5 has just performed
an adoption and the gesture is attested elsewhere in the ancient Near East; **it does not treat the
preference as settled.**

**On *śikkēl* (48:14).** The verb is used of the hands and nowhere else in the Bible in this
construction. **Its root is the root of *discreet and wise* at 41:33, 39.** WEB's *"guiding his hands
knowingly"* renders the sense; the King James *"guiding his hands wittingly"* and the older
*"crossed"* render the effect. **The choice matters because the whole chapter turns on whether the
crossing was an error**, and the narrator uses a word about insight in the same verse in which he
tells the reader that Manasseh was the firstborn. **It is a deliberate act, and Genesis says so twice
more** (48:19, 20).

**On *məlōʾ haggôyim* and Romans 11:25.** The Hebrew phrase at 48:19 occurs once in the Old Testament.
Its Greek equivalent, *plērōma tōn ethnōn*, occurs once in the New, at Romans 11:25. **The wiki records
the coincidence and declines the inference.** Paul does not cite Genesis 48, his argument in Romans 11
runs through Isaiah and Deuteronomy, and *plērōma* is ordinary Greek. **The echo is worth noting and is
not evidence of a quotation.**

**On what Jacob did to the Amorite (48:22).** Genesis records no battle fought by Jacob. It records a
purchase at [[Shechem (city)]] (33:19), a massacre carried out by [[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] which Jacob
condemned as a danger to the household (34:30) and cursed on his deathbed (49:5–7), and this claim.
**The wiki lists the three and adopts none**, per
[[Bought, Sacked, or Taken by the Sword - How Jacob Got Shechem]]. **It notes only that 48:22 is the
one version in Jacob's own voice, and that he uses *Amorite*** — the Torah's blanket term for the
pre-Israelite inhabitants — **where the narrative said Hivite** (34:2).

**On the counts.** In the twenty-two verses: *Joseph* **twelve** times, *hand* or *hands* **eleven**
(of which *right hand* **five**), *father* or *fathers* **eleven**, *Israel* **nine** (one of them the
nation, 48:20), *Ephraim* **eight**, *Manasseh* **eight**, *God* **seven**, *head* **six**, *bless* in
all forms **six**, *son* or *sons* **six**, *land* **five**, *name* **three**, *know* **three**
(including *knowingly*, 48:14), *firstborn* **two**, *Jacob* **two**. **Counted from the WEB text in
`raw/bible/Genesis.md`.** ***Hand*** **is the chapter's subject as surely as *land* was the last
one's.**

**On the pericope boundary.** The book page queued 48:1–22 and **the boundary was kept.** Chapter 48
is a self-contained scene with its own cast; 49:1 starts a new one by summoning all twelve sons, and
the natural temptation — to run 48 and 49 together as one deathbed — **would produce a fifty-five-verse
pericope in which the adoption and the crossed hands disappear into the tribal poem.** **They are kept
separate and cross-linked instead**, with 48:5's *"even as Reuben and Simeon"* flagged as the hinge on
both pages once 49 is ingested.

**Links left for later pericopes.** Genesis 49:3–7 (Reuben's and Simeon's demotions), 49:22–26
(Joseph's blessing and *the shepherd, the stone of Israel*), 49:29–32 (the instruction naming
[[Machpelah]] and the five buried there), 50:13 (the burial), 50:24–25 (Joseph repeating 48:21) and
Joshua 24:32 (Joseph buried at [[Shechem (city)]]) **are cited above by reference and not linked** —
their passage pages do not exist yet.
