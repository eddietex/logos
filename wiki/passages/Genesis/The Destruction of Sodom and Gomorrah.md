# The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 19:1–29

## Summary

The verdict chapter 18 refused to give, delivered as a crowd count: *"all the people from every
quarter"* (19:4). There were not ten.

**The chapter is built as chapter 18 run again in a worse town, and the correspondence is close
enough to be deliberate.** Two of the three visitors walk down from Mamre and arrive at Sodom the
same day. A man sitting in a doorway sees them, rises, bows to the ground, presses food and a bed
on them, and bakes bread. Then the second half diverges: at Mamre the guests were fed and the host
walked them out to the ridge; at Sodom the entire male population comes to the door for them.

***"The two angels came to Sodom."*** **19:1 is the first time the narrative calls them
angels.** Chapter 18 said *"three men"* eleven times over and never once *malʾāk*; here the word
arrives in the opening clause — and then the chapter drops it again. For the rest of the scene they
are *"the men"* (19:5, 8, 10, 12, 16), exactly as before. The seam that runs through
[[The Angel of Yahweh]] is not resolved by chapter 19; it is only labelled once, in passing, and
then left where it was.

### *"Lot sat in the gate of Sodom"*

**The drift that began in chapter 13 finishes in this clause.** Genesis has moved him one step at a
time and never narrated a single one of the moves:

| | |
|---|---|
| 13:11 | *"So Lot chose the Plain of the Jordan for himself"* — he leaves Canaan |
| 13:12 | *"moved his tent as far as Sodom"* — outside the walls |
| 14:12 | *"Lot, Abram's brother's son, who lived in Sodom"* — inside them |
| 19:1 | *"Lot sat in the gate of Sodom"* — in its civic centre |

**The gate is where a town does its business.** Elders sit in it (Ruth 4:1–2), lawsuits are brought
to it (Deuteronomy 21:19; 22:15), a man is known by it (Proverbs 31:23), and the prophets attack
what is done in it (Amos 5:10, 12, 15). Whether Lot held any office there Genesis does not say — and
the townsmen's own answer at 19:9 says he did not: *"This one fellow came in to live as a foreigner,
and he appoints himself a judge."* *Gēr*, **resident alien**, the standing legal category for
someone living where he has no kin and no rights, and the class Israel's law will single out for
protection precisely because a town can do this to it (Exodus 22:21; 23:9; Leviticus 19:33–34).

**And the mob's complaint is made with Abraham's word.** *Wayyišpōṭ šāpôṭ*, *he appoints himself a
judge*, is the root **š-p-ṭ** — the root of *"Shouldn't the **Judge** of all the earth do right?"*
(18:25). Four verses of narrative separate the two uses. See
[[Shall Not the Judge of All the Earth Do Right]].

### The hospitality, and what it costs

***"They said, 'No, but we will stay in the street all night.'"*** They intend to sleep in the
square, and Lot *"urged them greatly"* — *wayyipṣar bām məʾōd*, a verb of hard pressing. **He knows
what a night in the street of Sodom is.** That is the narrator's most economical statement of what
the city is, and it is made without a word of description, three verses before the mob arrives.

***"He made them a feast, and baked unleavened bread."*** *Maṣṣôt* — the flat bread of haste, and
the word that will carry the exodus (Exodus 12:39). The wiki records the resonance and marks it as
reception, not as anything Genesis is doing: a household eating unleavened bread on the night a
judgment passes over it is the shape of Passover, and Genesis draws no line to it at all.

***"both young and old, all the people from every quarter"*** (19:4). *Mi-qāṣeh*, *from the
edge* — the whole town, with nobody left out. **This is Abraham's answer**, and the narrator gives
it as a headcount rather than a verdict. Chapter 18 ended without saying whether ten righteous were
in the city; 19:4 says how many men are at the door, and the number is all of them.

***"Bring them out to us, that we may have sex with them"*** (19:5). The Hebrew verb is *yādaʿ*,
**know** — the verb of 4:1 (*"The man knew Eve his wife"*) and of 18:19 (*"For I have known him"*),
and the WEB renders the sense the context requires. The mid-century proposal that it means only *get
acquainted with* (D. S. Bailey, 1955) has to account for the same verb three verses later — the
Hebrew of 19:8 is *ʾăšer lōʾ-yādəʿû ʾîš*, *who have not known a man*, which the WEB renders
*"two virgin daughters"* — and for Lot's counteroffer, which answers a sexual demand with a sexual
substitute or answers nothing at all. The wiki records the proposal and does not adopt it.

***"See now, I have two virgin daughters. Please let me bring them out to you"*** (19:8). **Genesis
passes no judgment on this verse, and neither will the wiki.** The book is consistent about Lot:
it shows him choosing, drifting, offering, lingering, and haggling, and it grades none of it. What
the chapter supplies instead of an evaluation is the reason he gives — *"they have come under the
shadow of my roof"*, *bəṣēl qōrātî*, the guest-protection obligation in its baldest ancient Near
Eastern form — and then the fact that the offer is refused, so that the daughters are never in the
mob's hands and the crime the chapter actually narrates is the one attempted on the visitors.
Judges 19 will run the same scene without that mercy. See
[[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]] and [[Lot]].

***"They struck the men who were at the door of the house with blindness"*** (19:11). *Sanwērîm* —
a rare word, not the ordinary *ʿiwwārôn*, and it occurs in exactly one other place in the Hebrew
Bible: Elisha's prayer over the Aramean army at Dothan (2 Kings 6:18). Both are a dazzling laid on
an armed crowd surrounding a house, and in both the crowd is left alive. It does not stop them:
*"they wearied themselves to find the door"* — struck blind at the threshold and still groping for
it.

### The extraction

***"he seemed to his sons-in-law to be joking"*** (19:14). ***Kim-ṣaḥēq.*** **The laughter root
again.** *Ṣ-ḥ-q* is Abraham's laugh at 17:17, Sarah's at 18:12, the name of the son who is coming,
and here a man taken for a comedian on the last night of his city. Three consecutive chapters, three
valences, and the wiki notes that Genesis never marks the repetition. See
[[Laughter and the Name Isaac]].

*Lōqḥê bənōtāyw* is ambiguous — *who had taken his daughters* or *who were taking them*, i.e.
married or betrothed. The WEB takes the betrothal reading (*"who were pledged to marry his
daughters"*), which is what 19:8's *virgin daughters* and 19:15's *"your two daughters who are
here"* require; the alternative would give Lot four daughters, two of them married and left behind,
and the chapter would have to be told differently.

***"But he lingered"*** (19:16). *Wayyitmahmāh.* **The rescue is carried out over the resistance of
the man being rescued**, and Genesis says so in the same breath as the reason it happens anyway:
*"the men grabbed his hand, his wife's hand, and his two daughters' hands, **Yahweh being merciful
to him**; and they took him out."* Four hands, physically taken. *Bəḥemlat YHWH ʿālāyw* — in
Yahweh's **sparing** of him. The verse gives the cause of the rescue, and the cause is not anything
Lot does.

### The second negotiation

**Chapter 18 had a man bargaining with God on a ridge; chapter 19 has a man bargaining with God's
agents in the dark, and the terms have changed.**

| | Abraham, 18:23–33 | Lot, 19:18–22 |
|---|---|---|
| Bargains for | a city he has no tie to | himself |
| On the ground of | *"Shouldn't the Judge of all the earth do right?"* | *"I can't escape to the mountain, lest evil overtake me"* |
| Opens with | *"Will you consume the righteous with the wicked?"* | *"Oh, not so, my lord"* — a refusal of the terms of his own rescue |
| Concessions | six, all granted, none resisted | one, granted: *"I have granted your request concerning this thing also"* |
| Result | the city is destroyed | a city is spared |

**Abraham argued that a city be spared for the sake of righteous men inside it and did not get it.
Lot asks that a city be spared because the hills are too far, and does.** Genesis reports the
asymmetry without commenting on it.

***"isn't it a little one?"*** (19:20). *Hălōʾ miṣʿār hîʾ* — and then, in the verse that grants it,
*"Therefore the name of the city was called **Zoar**"* (19:22), *Ṣōʿar*, *littleness*. One of Genesis's naming etiologies, hung on a
word Lot uses twice while pleading. The town was called Bela at 14:2 and 14:8; it is still a real
place in Deuteronomy 34:3, Isaiah 15:5, and Jeremiah 48:34, centuries after the plain is supposed
to have burned. See [[Zoar]].

***"Hurry, escape there, for I can't do anything until you get there"*** (19:22). **The executioner
says he is unable to act while the man is outside shelter.** It is the nearest thing the chapter
gives to an answer to 18:25: the principle Abraham could only assert — that the Judge of all the
earth does not sweep the righteous away with the wicked — turns up here as an operational constraint
on the one carrying out the sentence. Stated by the agent, in a hurry, about one man.

### The overthrow

***"Then Yahweh rained on Sodom and on Gomorrah sulfur and fire from Yahweh out of the sky"***
(19:24). ***Wa-YHWH himṭîr … mēʾēt YHWH min-haššāmāyim.* The divine name appears twice in one
clause**, and the doubling is really in the text. Two explanations are old. The ordinary
grammatical one is that Biblical Hebrew commonly uses a repeated proper name where English would
use a reflexive or resumptive pronoun (compare 1 Kings 8:1; Zechariah 2:11 [Heb.]), so the sense is
*from himself*. The other is that there are two figures, which is how Justin Martyr reads it against
Trypho (*Dialogue* 56, 129) and how some rabbinic responses to that reading are framed. The wiki
takes the idiom as the reading and records the second as reception — as it does with the three
visitors of chapter 18 — because the doubling is a known Hebrew habit and the chapter shows no
interest in the question.

***Gopriṯ wā-ʾēš*, sulfur and fire — the pair's first occurrence in Scripture**, and after this the
standing vocabulary of judgment: Deuteronomy 29:23, Psalm 11:6, Isaiah 30:33, 34:9, Ezekiel 38:22,
Luke 17:29, and five times in Revelation. See [[Fire and Sulfur from the Sky]].

***"He overthrew those cities"*** (19:25). *Hāpak* — the verb behind ***mahpēkâ*, the Overthrow**,
which becomes the event's technical name in the prophets (Deuteronomy 29:23; Isaiah 1:7; 13:19;
Jeremiah 49:18; 50:40; Amos 4:11). It is also the verb of Jonah 3:4, *"Yet forty days, and Nineveh
will be overthrown"* — a sentence in the vocabulary of Sodom, pronounced over a city that then is
not.

***"and that which grew on the ground"*** (19:25). *Ṣemaḥ hā-ʾădāmâ*. The plain Lot saw as
*"well-watered everywhere … like the garden of Yahweh, like the land of Egypt"* (13:10) loses its
vegetation along with its people. The Eden simile is closed off in the same verse as the cities, and
what is left is the salt country of 19:26. Compare [[The Flood as Un-Creation]], where judgment also
runs backwards through what was made.

***"But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt"*** (19:26). **One
clause.** She has no name, no line, no stated motive, and no prior appearance; the whole of her is a
verb and its consequence. The verb is *nābaṭ*, and it is the verb of the prohibition she breaks —
*"Don't look behind you"* (19:17), *ʾal-tabbîṭ ʾaḥăreykā* — so the sentence is written to match the
command word for word. *Nəṣîb melaḥ*, a **pillar of salt**, in the one landscape in the region where
salt stands up in columns. Josephus says the pillar was still there and that he had seen it
(*Antiquities* 1.11.4); Wisdom 10:7 calls it *"a pillar of salt standing as a monument to an
unbelieving soul"*; and Jesus makes her the shortest citation in the Gospels — *"Remember Lot's
wife"* (Luke 17:32). See [[Lot's Wife]] and [[Remember Lot's Wife]].

### The coda

***"Abraham got up early in the morning to the place where he had stood before Yahweh"*** (19:27).
He goes back to the spot on the ridge where 18:22 left him standing, and looks. *"the smoke of the
land went up as the smoke of a furnace"* — *kəqîṭōr hakkibšān*, the furnace-word that Exodus 19:18
will use of Sinai.

**And then he goes home without being told anything.** Nobody reports to him. He does not learn
whether ten were found, does not learn that Lot is alive, and does not speak again in the chapter or
about it ever. **The outcome of Scripture's first intercession is disclosed to the reader and
withheld from the intercessor.**

***"When God destroyed the cities of the plain, God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the
middle of the overthrow"*** (19:29). **The summary verse, and it hands the credit to the man who
never mentioned Lot's name.**

- The subject is ***ʾĕlōhîm***, not *Yahweh* — the only two occurrences of *God* in the chapter, in
  its last verse, in a chapter that has said *Yahweh* throughout. It is the standard mark of the
  Priestly source, and 19:29 reads as P's one-sentence summary appended to a J narrative.
- ***Wayyizkōr ʾĕlōhîm*, God remembered** — the formula of 8:1 (*"God remembered Noah"*), 30:22
  (*"God remembered Rachel"*), and Exodus 2:24 (*"God remembered his covenant"*). In Genesis it
  never means *recalled*; it is the word for the moment God acts on an obligation already standing.
  See [[Remembrance]].
- **The reason given for the rescue is Abraham, not Lot.** Genesis nowhere says Lot was among any
  ten, or was righteous, or was spared for his own sake. It says God remembered somebody else. The
  New Testament will say otherwise, and the wiki flags the difference rather than harmonizing it:
  see [[Righteous Lot - 2 Peter's Verdict and Genesis's Silence]].

**So did the intercession work?** The chapter supports both answers and settles neither. Read one
way it failed: Abraham asked that the place be spared and the place is ash. Read the other way it
was answered past its terms — the righteous were taken out rather than left in, one more city was
spared on top, and 19:29 says plainly that what moved God was Abraham. What the text will not do is
say which, and it withholds the information from Abraham as well as from the reader.

## People

- [[Lot]] — the chapter he has been walking toward since 13:11. He sits in the gate, bows to the
  ground, presses two strangers into his house, shuts the door behind him against his neighbours,
  offers them his daughters instead, is dragged out of the city by the wrist, argues that the hills
  are too far, and is spared for his uncle's sake. Genesis grades none of it.
- [[Lot's Wife]] — one clause, no name, no dialogue: she looks back in the verb she was forbidden to
  look in, and becomes salt. Jesus's shortest citation of anyone.
- [[Lot's Daughters]] — offered to the mob at 19:8, taken by the hand at 19:16, and the only members
  of the household still with him at Zoar. Their own scene begins at 19:30.
- [[Abraham]] — off-stage for twenty-six verses, then back on the ridge at dawn looking at smoke.
  He is told nothing, and 19:29 names him as the reason the rescue happened.
- [[God]] — who sends two agents to do what he said at 18:21 he would come down to see for himself,
  who is *"merciful"* to a man who will not move, who grants the one thing asked of him in the
  chapter, and who is named twice in the same clause at 19:24.
- [[The Angel of Yahweh]] — the two visitors, called *angels* in 19:1 and *men* in every verse
  after. They speak as *"we will destroy this place"* (19:13) and as *"I will not overthrow the
  city"* (19:21), plural and singular in nine verses.

## Places

- [[Sodom]] — the city whose *cry* came up at 18:20, whose whole male population is at the door by
  19:4, and which is ash by 19:25. Its name leaves geography here and becomes the Bible's standing
  word for a place under sentence.
- [[Gomorrah]] — named with Sodom in the rain of 19:24 and the sight of 19:28, and, as always, given
  no scene of its own.
- [[Zoar]] — Bela of 14:2, renamed in 19:22 out of the word Lot used to plead for it, and the one
  city of the plain left standing. He leaves it in the next pericope anyway.
- [[The Plain of the Jordan]] — the *kikkar* Lot chose in 13:10–11 for being *"like the garden of
  Yahweh"*, destroyed entire at 19:25 down to what grew on it.
- [[The Salt Sea]] — the country the pillar of 19:26 stands in, and the region that supplies the
  chapter its salt, its bitumen, and its geology.
- [[Hebron]] and [[Mamre (place)]] — where Abraham gets up early and walks back to the ridge he
  stood on at 18:22, some forty miles from the smoke he is looking at.

## Themes

- [[Hospitality]] — the theme's founding pair completed. Abraham's reception at Mamre is answered
  point for point by Lot's at the gate, and then by a town that comes to the door for the guests.
  Luke 10:10–12 makes exactly this the measure of a town, and it is why Sodom is the comparison
  there rather than anywhere else.
- [[Judgment]] — investigated before it is executed (18:21), deferred while one household is walked
  out, constrained by the presence of a man outside shelter (19:22), and then total. The chapter is
  the Old Testament's template for the thing, down to its vocabulary.
- [[Remembrance]] — *"God remembered Abraham"* (19:29): the formula of 8:1 and Exodus 2:24, and the
  chapter's own explanation of why anyone survived it.
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — the answer to chapter 18, and not in the terms it was asked in. The
  place is not spared; the man the intercessor never named is carried out of it.
- [[Righteousness]] — the word chapter 18 used seven times in eleven verses does not occur once
  here. Genesis never applies it to Lot; 2 Peter 2:7 does, three times in two verses.
- [[Sin]] — *"the outcry against them has grown so great before Yahweh"* (19:13): the *cry* of 18:20
  repeated by the agents as their warrant, so that the sentence is grounded in what was done to
  people, not only in what was done before God.
- [[Favor and Grace]] — *"your servant has found favor in your sight, and you have magnified your
  loving kindness"* (19:19). *Ḥēn* and *ḥesed* in the mouth of a man in the act of refusing the
  terms of his own rescue.
- [[City and Civilization]] — Genesis's cities run Enoch, Babel, Sodom: built by a fratricide,
  abandoned in confusion, burned. Lot's arc from tent to gate is the book's one portrait of somebody
  moving into one.
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — the chapter is organized around looking. *"Don't look behind you"*
  (19:17), *"his wife looked back"* (19:26), *"Abraham … looked toward Sodom"* (19:28). Two people
  look back at the same fire and one of them is standing forty miles off.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — *"This one fellow came in to live as a foreigner"* (19:9): the *gēr*
  charge, thrown at Lot by the town, and the status Israel's law will protect because of scenes like
  this one.
- [[Life and Death]] — *"lest you be consumed in the iniquity of the city"* (19:15). The problem
  Abraham posed at 18:25 — the righteous perishing with the wicked — solved here by removal rather
  than by sparing.
- [[Deception]] — its absence, oddly: this is one of the few Genesis narratives in which nobody
  lies. What the sons-in-law disbelieve is the plain truth.

- Deuteronomy 34:3 — Moses shown *"the plain of the valley of Jericho … to Zoar"*, a Zoar still
  standing.
- Judges 19:15–30 — a traveller taken in off the square, a town at the door demanding him, a
  daughter offered, and no angels.
- Isaiah 1:9–10 — Judah addressed as Sodom and told it would have *been* Sodom but for a remnant.
- Ezekiel 16:49–50 — the Old Testament's one explicit statement of what Sodom's sin was, and it is
  about the poor and needy.
- Amos 4:11 — *"as when God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and you were like a burning stick plucked
  out of the fire"*.
- Zephaniah 2:9 — Moab and Ammon, Lot's own descendants, sentenced to become *"as Sodom"*.
- Jonah 3:4 — *hāpak* pronounced over Nineveh, and not carried out.
- Matthew 10:15; 11:23–24; Luke 10:12 — Sodom as the lighter sentence, in every case attached to a
  town that refused messengers.
- Luke 17:28–33 — the day of Lot, the fire and sulfur, *"Remember Lot's wife"*, and *"whoever seeks
  to save his life will lose it"*.
- Romans 9:29 — Paul quoting Isaiah 1:9: *"we would have become like Sodom."*
- 2 Peter 2:6–9 — the cities made *"an example to those who would live in an ungodly way"*, and
  *"righteous Lot"* delivered.
- Jude 7 — Sodom and Gomorrah *"set forth as an example, suffering the punishment of eternal fire."*
- Revelation 11:8 — the great city *"which spiritually is called Sodom and Egypt, where also their
  Lord was crucified."*
- Wisdom 10:6–7 — the deuterocanonical retelling: the righteous man rescued, and *"a pillar of salt
  standing as a monument to an unbelieving soul."*

## Notes

- **On where the pericope ends.** 19:29 is the close, and the seam is clean: it is a summary verse,
  it returns to Abraham, it names God rather than Yahweh, and 19:30 opens a new scene with a new
  location (*"Lot went up out of Zoar"*). A reader could argue for running 19:1–38 together on the
  grounds that 19:30 immediately cancels the concession Lot won at 19:21, and the wiki records that
  argument in the next pericope's cross-references rather than merging the two: what happens in the
  cave is a different kind of scene, and 19:29 is written to end something.
- **What the chapter's charge is, and the reception it acquired.** The English word *sodomy* comes
  from this passage, and the reading it names is one of three that Scripture itself supports. The
  wiki records the range on [[Sodom]] and adds one observation here: what Genesis 19 narrates is the
  attempted gang rape of guests by the entire male population of a town, which is at once a sexual
  crime, a total collapse of the guest obligation, and a demonstration of the *cry* that 18:20 said
  was already rising before the visitors ever arrived. Each of the three standard readings — Ezekiel
  16:49–50's injustice and arrogance, Luke 10:10–12's inhospitality, Jude 7's sexual immorality —
  takes one of those and is textually grounded in doing so. Reading the chapter as *only* one of
  them requires setting aside parts of it.
- **The chapter is not an account of a private sin.** Whatever else is disputed, the text is
  emphatic on one point and it is the point most often lost: the crowd is *"both young and old, all
  the people from every quarter"* (19:4), the demand is made to the whole street, and the offence is
  against strangers under a roof. It is a description of a town, not of individuals, which is why
  the sentence falls on a town.
- **Lot is not exonerated and not condemned.** Genesis's method here is what it has been since
  chapter 13: report and decline to grade. He honours the guest obligation at a price no later law
  would permit, has to be dragged out by the hand, and haggles over his own rescue. 2 Peter 2:7–8
  calls him righteous three times in two verses and narrates nothing. Both are canonical and they
  are not obviously the same portrait. See
  [[Righteous Lot - 2 Peter's Verdict and Genesis's Silence]].
- **Source criticism, briefly.** Genesis 18–19 is assigned to J by near-consensus and reads as one
  continuous composition; 19:29, with its *ʾĕlōhîm*, its formulaic *God remembered*, and its
  summarizing view of the whole event, is the standard example of a P notice appended to close a J
  narrative. As the book stands, 19:29 is the verse that ties the chapter back to the intercession,
  and nothing in the wiki's reading depends on the analysis.
- **Geology and location.** Unresolved, and the wiki endorses no identification; the arguments for
  a southern site under the shallows below the Lisan and for Tall el-Hammam in the north, and the
  criticism of the 2021 airburst proposal, are set out on [[Sodom]]. What the region uncontroversially
  supplies is the chapter's furniture: an active transform fault, bitumen seeps (*"tar pits"*, 14:10),
  and salt domes that stand up in pillars.
- **Josephus and the pillar.** *Antiquities* 1.11.4 reports the pillar of salt as still standing and
  claims autopsy — *"for I have seen it, and it remains at this day"*. It is worth recording as the
  earliest evidence of the chapter being read against a visible landscape, and worth nothing more
  than that: the Lisan marl throws up salt columns continually, and Josephus makes similar claims
  elsewhere.
