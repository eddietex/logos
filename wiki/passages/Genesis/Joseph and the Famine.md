# Joseph and the Famine

**Book:** [[Genesis]]
**Reference:** Genesis 47:13–31

## Summary

**Egypt is sold into servitude and Israel prospers, and Genesis puts the two sentences next to each
other without a word.**

The pericope has two unequal halves. **47:13–26 is an administrative document**: [[Joseph]] takes
Egypt's money (13–15), then its livestock (16–17), then its land and its people (18–21), exempts the
priests (22), issues seed against a fifth of the harvest (23–24), is thanked for it (25), and makes
the fifth permanent law (26). **47:27–31 is three sentences of family business**: Israel settles and
multiplies (27), [[Jacob]] reaches a hundred and forty-seven (28), and the old man makes his son
swear not to bury him in Egypt (29–31).

**The join is the point.** *"They said, 'You have saved our lives! … we will be Pharaoh's servants'"*
(47:25) is followed immediately by *"Israel lived in the land of Egypt, in the land of Goshen; and
they got themselves possessions therein, and were fruitful, and multiplied exceedingly"* (47:27).
**A nation becomes property in one verse and a household becomes a nation in the next**, and the same
man is responsible for both. See [[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[Providence]].

**Genesis does not editorialise, and the omission is conspicuous.** The book has not been shy about
verdicts — *"the thing which he did was evil in Yahweh's sight"* (38:10), *"Yahweh was with Joseph"*
(39:2) — and here, where a man of God transfers the freehold and the freedom of a whole country to a
king, **the narrator reports the transaction, records the people's gratitude, and stops.** The wiki
records the silence and does not fill it. See [[Wisdom]] and [[Kingship and Empire]].

### The instalments

**The famine is collected in four payments, in descending order of what a person can spare.**

| verses | what is taken | what is given | what is left |
|---|---|---|---|
| **47:14–15** | **all the money** in Egypt and Canaan | grain | *"our money fails"* |
| **47:16–17** | **the livestock** — horses, flocks, herds, donkeys | bread, *"for that year"* | *"the herds of livestock are my lord's"* |
| **47:18–21** | **the land, and the people with it** | bread | *"nothing left … but our bodies, and our lands"* |
| **47:23–26** | **a fifth of every harvest, for ever** | seed | four parts, and the statute |

**Nothing in the sequence is coerced and nothing in it is refused.** **Each instalment is proposed by
the people themselves except the second**, which Joseph proposes — *"Give me your livestock"*
(47:16) — **and the third and largest is their own idea, offered in their own words**: *"Buy us and
our land for bread, and we and our land will be servants to Pharaoh"* (47:19). **Genesis stages the
enslavement of Egypt as a petition.** See [[Famine and Plenty]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]].

***"Joseph brought the money into Pharaoh's house"*** (47:14). **Not into a treasury, not into a
relief fund — into the king's household.** The verse says twice where the silver came from, *"the
land of Egypt, and … the land of Canaan"*, **which means the family in [[Goshen]] paid into this
system before it was fed out of it** (42:25, 35; 43:12, 21–23 — the brothers' sacks). **The money
Joseph secretly returned to his brothers is in Pharaoh's house by 47:14, because everyone else's
is.**

### *"Why should we die before your eyes?"*

> **18** When that year was ended, they came to him the second year, and said to him, "We will not
> hide from my lord how our money is all spent, and the herds of livestock are my lord's. There is
> nothing left in the sight of my lord, but our bodies, and our lands. **19** Why should we die
> before your eyes, both we and our land? Buy us and our land for bread, and we and our land will be
> servants to Pharaoh. Give us seed, that we may live, and not die, and that the land won't be
> desolate."

***ʿăbādîm***, ***servants*** — ***slaves*** — **is the word the previous four chapters were
saturated with**, and it arrives here from the other direction. **At 44:16 eleven Hebrews offered
themselves to Joseph as *"my lord's slaves"* and were refused; at 47:19 a nation offers itself to his
master and is accepted.** **The man who was *sold for a servant* (Psalm 105:17) buys a country.** See
[[Sold for a Servant - Joseph's Sale in Psalm 105 and Acts 7]] and
[[Buy Us and Our Land - Egypt Sold into Servitude and Israel After It]].

***"Why should we die before your eyes?"*** — **the second time in three verses** (47:15, 19).
**The appeal is not to justice or to mercy but to the vizier's sightline**, and it works both times.
**Compare the brothers' *"that we may live, and not die"*** (42:2; 43:8), **the same clause, said by
the family that is being fed for nothing.** See [[Life and Death]] and [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

### The fifth

> **24** It will happen at the harvests, that you shall give a fifth to Pharaoh, and four parts will
> be your own, for seed of the field, for your food, for them of your households, and for food for
> your little ones.

**A twenty per cent levy was Joseph's proposal to a king thirteen years earlier**, as an emergency
measure for the seven good years — *"let him appoint overseers … and take up the fifth part of the
land of Egypt's produce in the seven plenteous years"* (41:34). **Here the emergency measure becomes
the constitution.** ***"Joseph made it a statute concerning the land of Egypt to this day"*** (47:26).

**The rate is worth stating plainly, because it is the number by which every later king in the Bible
is measured.** **When Israel asks for a king, Samuel's warning of what a king does is a shorter
version of Genesis 47** — he will take your fields and your vineyards, *"he will take the tenth of
your seed"*, *"and you shall be his servants"* (1 Samuel 8:14–17). **Joseph's Egypt runs at twice
Samuel's rate and the people call it salvation.** Filed as
[[A Fifth to Pharaoh - Joseph's Tax and the Rights of the King]].

***"To this day."*** **The seventh and last time the narrator of Genesis says it** — after Moab and
Ammon (19:37–38), Yahweh Will Provide (22:14), [[Beersheba]] (26:33), the sinew of the hip (32:32)
and [[Rachel]]'s pillar (35:20). **Six of the seven explain a name or a custom. The seventh explains
a tax.** See [[Remembrance]].

### The one class that keeps its land

> **22** Only he didn't buy the land of the priests, for the priests had a portion from Pharaoh, and
> ate their portion which Pharaoh gave them. That is why they didn't sell their land.

***A portion*** — ***ḥōq***, **an allotted due**, **and it is the word the Torah will use of the
priests' share of the offerings** (Leviticus 6:18; 7:34; 10:13–14). **The Egyptian priesthood is the
only body in the country that emerges from the famine owning what it owned before**, because it was
already on the state payroll.

**Israel's arrangement is the exact negative of this and Genesis does not draw the contrast.** **The
priests of Egypt keep land because they are fed by the crown; the priests of Israel are fed by the
offerings because they are given no land at all** — *"You shall have no inheritance in their land …
I am your portion and your inheritance among the children of Israel"* (Numbers 18:20; Deuteronomy
10:9; Joshua 13:14, 33). **One priesthood is exempted from a nation's dispossession; the other is
dispossessed so that the nation is not.** Filed as
[[The Priests Who Kept Their Land and the Levites Who Got None]]. See [[Priesthood]].

**Note also who the priests of Egypt are in this book.** [[Potiphera]], *priest of [[On]]*, is
Joseph's father-in-law (41:45; 46:20). **The class Joseph exempts is the class he married into**, and
Genesis, which mentioned the marriage twice, says nothing about the exemption.

### *"You have saved our lives"*

> **25** They said, "You have saved our lives! Let us find favor in the sight of my lord, and we will
> be Pharaoh's servants."

***Heḥĕyitānû*** — ***you have kept us alive*** — **is the Hiphil of *ḥāyâ*, and it is the verb of
[[Joseph]]'s own theology.** *"God sent me before you to preserve life"* (45:5, ***ləmiḥyâ***); *"to
save you alive by a great deliverance"* (45:7); *"to save many people alive, as it is today"*
(50:20). **The doctrine Joseph states twice about himself is stated back to him once, by the people
who paid for it with their land and their persons**, and Genesis puts the two in the same
vocabulary. See
[[God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale]] and
[[To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11]].

***"Let us find favor in the sight of my lord"*** — ***nimṣāʾ-ḥēn***, **the courtesy formula of the
whole Joseph cycle** (39:4; 47:29; and back through 18:3, 30:27, 32:5, 33:8). **A nation uses it in
the act of selling itself**, and four verses later [[Jacob]] uses it to ask his own son for a grave:
*"If now I have found favor in your sight"* (47:29). **The same words, from a country and from a
father, to the same man, in the same paragraph.** See [[Favor and Grace]].

### 47:21, and one consonant

> **21** As for the people, he moved them to the cities from one end of the border of Egypt even to
> the other end of it.

**The WEB follows the Masoretic Hebrew, which reads *heʿĕbîr*, *he transferred* — a population moved
into the towns**, presumably to be nearer the granaries and away from fields that would not be sown
until the seed of 47:23 arrived. **The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint and the Vulgate read
*heʿĕbid*, *he made them servants*** — **one consonant different, *r* for *d*, and in the old script
a very easy exchange** — which gives *"he reduced them to servitude, from one end of Egypt to the
other."*

**The variant does not change the substance**, because 47:19–20 and 47:23 have already said in plain
Hebrew that the people and the land were bought. **It changes the emphasis**: the Masoretic verse
records a relocation and the versions record an enslavement, **and the modern translations divide on
it.** The wiki follows the WEB and records the reading alongside it, without adopting it.

### The hinge

> **27** Israel lived in the land of Egypt, in the land of Goshen; and they got themselves possessions
> therein, and were fruitful, and multiplied exceedingly.

**Every clause is loaded.**

***"They got themselves possessions therein"*** — ***wayyēʾāḥăzû***, **from the root of *ʾăḥuzzâ*,
the word at 47:11** and, before that, the word for the cave at [[Machpelah]] and nothing else in
Genesis (23:9, 20). **Egypt has just lost its freehold to Pharaoh in the previous sentence** (47:20)
**and Israel takes hold of one in this one.** See [[Land and Sojourning]].

***"Were fruitful, and multiplied exceedingly"*** — ***wayyiprû wayyirbû məʾōd***, **the creation
blessing** (1:22, 28; 8:17; 9:1, 7) **and the patriarchal promise** (17:6; 28:3; 35:11; 48:4)
**reported for the first time as an accomplished fact — in Egypt.** **Exodus 1:7 will say it again in
five verbs instead of two**, *"fruitful, and increased abundantly, and multiplied, and grew
exceedingly mighty"*, **and the very next verse is *"there arose a new king over Egypt, who didn't
know Joseph."*** **Genesis 47:27 is the first sentence of the bondage narrative**, and it is written
as a blessing. Filed as
[[They Got Possessions and Multiplied - Genesis 47.27 and Exodus 1.7]]. See [[Covenant]] and
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

### Seventeen years

> **28** Jacob lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years. So the days of Jacob, the years of his
> life, were one hundred forty-seven years.

**[[Joseph]] was seventeen when his brothers sold him** — *"Joseph, being seventeen years old, was
feeding the flock with his brothers"* (37:2). **[[Jacob]] gets seventeen years with him at the end.**
**Genesis states both numbers and never places them side by side**, and the wiki records the symmetry
as a symmetry rather than a doctrine: the book does not say the second seventeen is compensation for
the first.

**A hundred and forty-seven is the number he predicted himself.** At 47:9 he told Pharaoh his hundred
and thirty years had been ***few and evil*** and had *"not attained to the days of the years of the
life of my fathers"*; **he is right — [[Abraham]] died at a hundred and seventy-five (25:7) and
[[Isaac]] at a hundred and eighty (35:28) — and the seventeen good years are the ones he did not know
were coming when he said it.** See [[Suffering]] and [[Hope]].

### The oath

> **29** The time came near that Israel must die, and he called his son Joseph, and said to him, "If
> now I have found favor in your sight, please put your hand under my thigh, and deal kindly and
> truly with me. Please don't bury me in Egypt, **30** but when I sleep with my fathers, you shall
> carry me out of Egypt, and bury me in their burying place." He said, "I will do as you have said."

**Three formulae, all of them significant, all of them in one sentence.**

***"Put your hand under my thigh."*** **The gesture occurs twice in the Bible and both times in
Genesis** — [[Abraham]] to his servant about a wife (24:2, 9) and [[Jacob]] to [[Joseph]] about a
grave. **Both times an old man near death binds a subordinate to something he will not live to see**,
and **both times the oath is about a family refusing to be absorbed by the country it lives in.** See
[[Put Your Hand Under My Thigh]] and [[Oaths and Vows]].

***"Deal kindly and truly with me"*** — ***ḥesed we-ʾemet***, **the pair the servant used twice in
that same chapter** (24:27, 49) **and the pair God will use of himself at Exodus 34:6.** **Its three
earliest occurrences in Scripture are all inside the two scenes that use this gesture.**

***"When I sleep with my fathers"*** — ***wəšākabtî ʿim-ʾăbōtay***, **and this is the idiom's first
appearance in the Bible.** **It becomes the standing formula for a king's death** —
Deuteronomy 31:16, 2 Samuel 7:12, and then nearly every reign in Kings and Chronicles. **Genesis has
used *gathered to his people* for [[Abraham]] (25:8), [[Ishmael]] (25:17) and [[Isaac]] (35:29), and
will use it of Jacob at 49:33.** **Here he invents a different one**, and it is the one that means a
grave with other people already in it. See [[Gathered to His People]] and [[Burial and the Grave]].

**What he asks for is the only thing his family owns in [[Canaan]].** *"Their burying place"* is
[[Machpelah]], the field [[Abraham]] bought from the Hittites for four hundred shekels (23:16), where
Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebekah and Leah are already lying (49:31). **The man who has just been given
a freehold in the best of Egypt** (47:11) **spends his last strength arranging not to be buried in
it.** See [[Buried at Machpelah - The Six Graves of Genesis]].

**And it is the first time since 46:4 that anyone has acted as though Egypt were temporary.** God
said *"I will also surely bring you up again"*; **47:27 has the family settling in and multiplying;
47:29 has its head extracting a sworn promise that at least one of them is going back.** See
[[Don't Go Down to Egypt - The Three Patriarchs and the Same Road]].

### *"Israel bowed himself on the bed's head"*

> **31** He said, "Swear to me," and he swore to him. Israel bowed himself on the bed's head.

**The last clause of the pericope is four Hebrew words and it is one of the best-known textual
divisions in the Old Testament.** **The consonants are מטה.** **Pointed *miṭṭâ* it is a bed; pointed
*maṭṭeh* it is a staff**, and the Hebrew text as written does not distinguish them. **The Masoretes
read *bed*, which the WEB follows. The Septuagint read *staff*** — *"and Israel bowed upon the top of
his staff"* — **and Hebrews 11:21 quotes the Septuagint**: *"By faith, Jacob, when he was dying,
blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff."*

**Neither reading is a variant in the letters; both are in the pointing**, which is why it cannot be
settled by manuscript evidence. **The wiki follows the WEB and records the other**, and notes that
Hebrews has also attached the gesture to the blessing of chapter 48 rather than to the oath of
chapter 47. Filed as
[[On the Bed's Head or on His Staff - Genesis 47.31 in Hebrews 11.21]].

***"Bowed himself"*** — **whichever object he leaned on, the verb is *šāḥâ*, the verb of the sheaves
and the stars.** **[[Joseph]]'s second dream was *"the sun and the moon and eleven stars bowed down
to me"* (37:9), and [[Jacob]] rebuked him for it** — *"Will I and your mother and your brothers
indeed come to bow ourselves down to you to the earth?"* (37:10). **Genesis never stages that scene.
The closest it comes is this verse**, where an old man bows at the head of a bed after his son has
promised to bury him properly, **and the text gives no sign that it means the dream.** The wiki
records the vocabulary and declines the identification. See
[[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]].

## People

- [[Joseph]] — **the pericope is his administration, and it is the only extended account in Scripture
  of how he actually governed.** He takes the money into Pharaoh's house (47:14), proposes the
  livestock himself (47:16), **accepts the people's offer of their own bodies and land** (47:19–20),
  moves the population into the cities (47:21), **exempts the priesthood he married into** (47:22),
  issues seed against a permanent fifth (47:23–24), and legislates (47:26). **He is thanked.**
  Then, in the same breath, **he is asked by his father to swear that he will carry a body four
  hundred miles**, and answers in five words: *"I will do as you have said"* (47:30)
- [[Jacob]] — **has three verses and uses them on his funeral.** He lives seventeen years in Egypt and
  dies at a hundred and forty-seven (47:28), **the figure he had implicitly forecast to Pharaoh at
  47:9.** *"The time came near that Israel must die"* (47:29) **is the narrator's, not his**, and
  what he does with the time is administer an oath with the gesture [[Abraham]] used (24:2) —
  ***ḥesed we-ʾemet***, *don't bury me in Egypt*, *carry me out* — **and bow.** **The narrator calls
  him Israel throughout this scene and Jacob only in the death-notice of 47:28**
- [[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]] — **named eleven times in nineteen verses and does not appear.**
  The money comes to his house, the livestock becomes his, the land becomes his, the people become
  his servants, and **the fifth is his in perpetuity** — **all of it transacted by someone else, and
  he says nothing and is not said to have ordered any of it.** **The king who handed a foreign
  ex-convict his signet ring** (41:42) **receives a country back for it.** See
  [[The Signet Ring and the Second Chariot - Joseph, Mordecai, and Daniel]]
- **The Egyptians** — **given no page and three speeches**, which is more direct speech than most
  named characters in Genesis get. They say *"Give us bread, for why should we die in your
  presence?"* (47:15), *"Buy us and our land for bread"* (47:19), and *"You have saved our lives!"*
  (47:25). **They are the collective protagonist of the first half and the wiki treats them as one**,
  because the text does
- **The priests of Egypt** — **the one class whose property survives** (47:22, 26), **because
  Pharaoh already fed them.** Not given a page; [[Potiphera]], Joseph's father-in-law, is the member
  of it Genesis names

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — **bought.** Named twelve times in nineteen verses. **The country changes owner inside
  the pericope**: at 47:13 it *faints*, at 47:20 *"the land became Pharaoh's"*, at 47:26 it has a
  permanent tax code. **And at 47:30 it becomes, for the first time, a place a member of this family
  refuses to be buried in**
- [[Goshen]] — **where the other story is happening.** One mention (47:27), and it is the verse in
  which Israel takes possession and multiplies **while the country around it is being sold**
- [[Canaan]] — **mentioned three times and all three are about absence**: it *faints* with Egypt
  (47:13), its money goes into Pharaoh's house (47:14), **and it is where Jacob wants his body taken**
  (47:30, *"their burying place"*). **The land of the promise is a famine zone, a revenue source, and
  a graveyard, in that order**
- [[Machpelah]] — **not named and unmistakably meant.** *"Bury me in their burying place"* (47:30) is
  the cave [[Abraham]] bought at 23:16, **the family's only freehold in [[Canaan]]**, and 49:29–31
  will name it explicitly
- **The cities of Egypt** — 47:21, *"from one end of the border of Egypt even to the other end of
  it."* **Genesis names none of them.** The relocation is the pericope's one piece of pure
  administration and the text gives it half a verse

## Themes

- [[Famine and Plenty]] — **the theme's terminal statement.** The seven years announced at 41:30 are
  worked out to their end here, **and what they produce is not starvation but a change in the
  ownership of a country.** *"The famine was very severe"* (47:13) is the last time Genesis says it
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — **a nation volunteers for it.** *"Buy us and our land for bread, and we
  and our land will be servants to Pharaoh"* (47:19), **repeated as gratitude at 47:25.** **The theme
  has followed one Hebrew into Egypt as property; here it turns and takes the Egyptians**, and
  Exodus 1 will turn it back
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — **the clearest description in the Bible of how an absolute monarchy is
  actually assembled**: a crisis, a monopoly on grain, four instalments, and a statute. **Samuel's
  warning at 1 Samuel 8:11–18 is this passage in the future tense**, and at half the rate
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — **the pericope is a ledger.** Money (six mentions), livestock (five),
  land (twenty-three), seed (three), *a fifth* (two). **Everything an Egyptian owns is enumerated as
  it is transferred**, and the only property Genesis reports being *acquired* is Israel's (47:27)
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — **two freeholds move in opposite directions in two verses.** Egypt's
  passes to Pharaoh (47:20); Israel's is *taken hold of* in Goshen (47:27). **And the head of the
  household with the new freehold spends his last scene refusing to be buried on it**
- [[Providence]] — **stated by the beneficiaries and not by the narrator.** *"You have saved our
  lives"* (47:25) is [[Joseph]]'s own doctrine (45:5–7; 50:20) **in the mouths of people who paid
  their liberty for it.** **Genesis records the verdict and does not endorse or withdraw it**
- [[Priesthood]] — **one class keeps its land because the crown feeds it** (47:22), **which is the
  mirror image of the arrangement the Torah will make for [[Levi]]** (Numbers 18:20). **The only
  priesthood in Genesis is Egyptian and it is the only institution that comes through the famine
  intact**
- [[Wisdom]] — ***ḥākām wənābôn*** was Pharaoh's verdict at 41:39. **This is what it looked like in
  practice over seven years**, and the text supplies neither praise nor blame for it. **The wiki
  notes that the same administrative genius that saved the region from starvation ended its
  freedom**, and that Genesis declines to rank the two
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — **the last oath in Genesis before the deathbed chapters**, and **the second and
  final use of the hand-under-the-thigh gesture in Scripture** (47:29; cf. 24:2, 9). *"Swear to me,"
  and he swore to him* (47:31)
- [[Burial and the Grave]] — ***"Please don't bury me in Egypt"*** (47:29). **The theme's pivot from
  record-keeping to claim-making**: every burial in Genesis so far has been reported after the fact,
  **and this one is negotiated in advance, under oath, by the man whose body it concerns**
- [[Life and Death]] — **five occurrences of *die* in nineteen verses**, four of them Egyptian
  (47:15, 19 twice) and the fifth Jacob's (47:29). **The country begs not to die and sells itself;
  the patriarch knows he will and arranges the freight**
- [[Favor and Grace]] — ***nimṣāʾ-ḥēn*** **twice in four verses, from opposite ends of the world**:
  a nation selling itself (47:25) and a father asking a son for a grave (47:29). **Both address
  [[Joseph]]**
- [[Work]] — **seed, sowing, and four parts out of five** (47:23–24). **The Egyptians go back to the
  same fields they owned last year as tenants**, and the terms Joseph gives them — food for their
  households and their little ones — **are the terms he gave his own family for nothing** (47:12)
- [[Covenant]] — ***"were fruitful, and multiplied exceedingly"*** (47:27) **is the language of 1:28
  and 35:11 discharged on foreign soil**, exactly as 46:3 said it would be: *"there I will make of you
  a great nation"*
- [[Hope]] — **the oath is an act of it.** A hundred-and-forty-seven-year-old in the richest province
  of the world's greatest power makes his son swear to carry him out of it, **which only makes sense
  if 46:4 is true**
- [[The Nations]] — **an entire foreign nation, addressed collectively, given three speeches and no
  names.** **It is the largest non-Israelite population Genesis has put on the page since chapter
  10**, and it is on it to be bought
- [[Remembrance]] — ***"to this day"*** (47:26), **the narrator's last**, and the only one in Genesis
  attached to a law rather than a name or a custom

## Notes

**On the wiki's refusal to grade Joseph's policy.** Readers have taken 47:13–26 as a model of
statesmanship and as an indictment for at least two thousand years, and **Genesis supplies no
adverb.** The passage records that the plan worked, that nobody starved, that the people initiated
three of the four instalments, that they thanked him, and that a free peasantry ended the seven years
as Pharaoh's tenants at twenty per cent. **All five of those are in the text and no evaluation is.**
**The wiki states them and stops**, and notes that the book's silence here is louder than usual
because Genesis is elsewhere willing to say *evil in Yahweh's sight* (38:10) about a much smaller
matter.

**On the exemption of the priests, and whether it is self-dealing.** [[Joseph]] is married to the
daughter of a priest of [[On]] (41:45), **and the priests are the only Egyptians who keep their
land** (47:22). **The text gives a reason that has nothing to do with that** — they were on a royal
stipend and so never had to sell — **and it is a sufficient reason.** **The wiki records the
coincidence and does not allege the motive**, because Genesis does not.

**On 47:21.** The Masoretic *heʿĕbîr*, *he removed them to the cities*, and the Samaritan, Greek and
Latin *heʿĕbid*, *he made them servants*, **differ by one consonant and cannot both be original.**
**The WEB follows the Masoretic text and so does this page.** **The choice does not change what the
pericope reports**, since the purchase of the people is stated outright at 47:19–20 and 47:23 in
every witness. **It is recorded because the two readings have produced two noticeably different
English traditions** and a reader comparing translations will meet it immediately.

**On *"to this day"* (47:26).** The clause is the narrator's, not Joseph's, and it dates the telling
rather than the event — **a tax still in force at the time of writing.** **Herodotus and later
Hellenistic sources describe an Egyptian land regime broadly of this kind, with temple estates
exempt**, and the wiki mentions this as a point of contact and not as corroboration: **Genesis is not
offering a date and the correspondence cannot supply one.**

**On the seventeen years.** [[Joseph]] is seventeen at 37:2 and [[Jacob]] lives seventeen years with
him at 47:28. **The symmetry is real and the text never mentions it.** **Medieval commentators made a
good deal of it and the wiki records it as arithmetic**, not as design — Genesis gives ages
constantly and some of them will coincide.

**On the counts.** In the nineteen verses of the pericope, *land* appears **twenty-three** times,
*Egypt* **twelve**, *Pharaoh* **eleven**, *Joseph* **ten**, *money* **six**, *bread* **five**,
*livestock* **five**, *die* **five**, *Canaan* **three**, *Israel* **three**, *seed* **three**,
*priests* **three**, *Jacob* **two**, *servants* **two**, and *the fifth* **two**. **Counted from the
WEB text in `raw/bible/Genesis.md`**, and offered as the shape of the passage rather than as a Hebrew
concordance figure. ***Land*** **outnumbers every proper noun in the pericope combined with room to
spare**, which is the correct summary of what it is about.

**On the pericope boundary.** The book page queued this unit as Genesis 47:13–31 and **the boundary
was kept**, although 47:27–31 is a coda rather than part of the famine narrative. **Splitting it
would leave five verses with nowhere to go**: 48:1 opens a new scene with a new cast, *"someone said
to Joseph, 'Behold, your father is sick'"*, and 47:27's report of Israel multiplying belongs with the
famine it survived, not with the blessing of Ephraim and Manasseh. **The page treats 47:27 as the
hinge between the two halves and says so.**

**Links left for later pericopes.** Genesis 48:4 (*"I will make you fruitful"*), 49:29–32 (the
instruction naming [[Machpelah]]), 49:31 (the five already buried there), 49:33 (*gathered to his
people*), 50:5–14 (the funeral convoy), 50:20 (*"to save many people alive"*) and 50:25 (Joseph's own
oath about his bones) **are named above in prose and dated rather than linked** — their passage pages
do not exist yet.
