# One Event Comes to All

**Book:** [[Ecclesiastes]]
**Reference:** Ecclesiastes 9:1–12

## Summary

The righteous, the wise, and their works are in God's hand, yet the person does not know
love or hatred (1). The Preacher expands the common end across moral and religious
categories: righteous and wicked, clean and unclean, those who sacrifice and those who
do not, those who take an oath and those who fear one (2). He calls the shared event an
evil and adds that human hearts contain evil and madness before they go to the dead (3).

Life nevertheless has an advantage: the living have hope and know that they will die;
a living dog is better than a dead lion (4–5). The dead know nothing, lose reward and
remembrance, and no longer have a portion in what is done under the sun (5–6). The answer
is a sequence of commands: eat and drink joyfully, wear white garments and oil, enjoy life
with the wife you love, and do your work with all your might (7–10). God has already
accepted your works (7); life remains vanity, but enjoyment is a portion within it (9).
Sheol has no work, plan, knowledge, or wisdom (10).

The final observation rules out mastery through ability. Speed, strength, wisdom,
understanding, and skill do not secure the expected prizes: time and chance happen to
all (11). People do not know their time; sudden evil catches them like fish in a net
or birds in a snare (12). Urgent action and present enjoyment are commended without
promising control over the result.

## Key verses

The complete unit is copied verbatim from immutable stored WEB Ecclesiastes 9.

> **1** For all this I laid to my heart, even to explore all this: that the righteous, and the wise, and their works, are in the hand of God; whether it is love or hatred, man doesn’t know it; all is before them.

> **2** All things come alike to all. There is one event to the righteous and to the wicked; to the good, to the clean, to the unclean, to him who sacrifices, and to him who doesn’t sacrifice. As is the good, so is the sinner; he who takes an oath, as he who fears an oath.

> **3** This is an evil in all that is done under the sun, that there is one event to all: yes also, the heart of the sons of men is full of evil, and madness is in their heart while they live, and after that they go to the dead.

> **4** For to him who is joined with all the living there is hope; for a living dog is better than a dead lion.

> **5** For the living know that they will die, but the dead don’t know anything, neither do they have any more a reward; for their memory is forgotten.

> **6** Also their love, their hatred, and their envy has perished long ago; neither have they any more a portion forever in anything that is done under the sun.

> **7** Go your way—eat your bread with joy, and drink your wine with a merry heart; for God has already accepted your works.

> **8** Let your garments be always white, and don’t let your head lack oil.

> **9** Live joyfully with the wife whom you love all the days of your life of vanity, which he has given you under the sun, all your days of vanity: for that is your portion in life, and in your labor in which you labor under the sun.

> **10** Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might; for there is no work, nor plan, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in Sheol, where you are going.

> **11** I returned, and saw under the sun, that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, neither yet bread to the wise, nor yet riches to men of understanding, nor yet favor to men of skill; but time and chance happen to them all.

> **12** For man also doesn’t know his time. As the fish that are taken in an evil net, and as the birds that are caught in the snare, even so are the sons of men snared in an evil time, when it falls suddenly on them.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 9:1–12 unit is retained. The common event, the living person's
remaining portion, and the uncertainty of outcomes form a connected reflection. Verse 13
opens a particular story about wisdom and belongs to the next unit, which remains un-ingested.

**What is the one event?** Verses 3–6 and 10 identify death as the horizon of the repeated
comparison. Verse 2's general language is not a claim that everyone has an identical
biography: 8:14 has already described different and reversed outcomes. Shared mortality
does not erase the moral and ritual distinctions that the list itself names.

**God's hand and unknown love or hatred.** Verse 1 places righteous people and their work
in God's hand, then denies human knowledge of love or hatred. The WEB does not explicitly
identify whose love or hatred is meant. Divine favor is a plausible contextual reading;
human experience is another possible scope. Neither is presented as a settled translation
of the Hebrew. Verse 7's assurance that God has already accepted the addressee's works is
retained beside this uncertainty, without extending acceptance to every act of every person.
The evil hearts of verse 3 remain evil. The passage reports the Preacher's assurance,
not a new oracle spoken directly by God.

**Explicit tension: whether life is better.** Ecclesiastes 4:2 praises the dead above the
living because of oppression; 6:3–6 ranks a stillborn child above abundance without good;
9:4 ranks even a living dog above a dead lion because the living have hope. Circumstances
help explain the differing judgments, but the text does not announce that one cancels
the others. The animal comparison supplies no basis for identifying particular people.

**What the dead lack.** The categorical statements of 5 and 10 must not be softened into
merely being less productive. The dead know nothing; Sheol has no work, plan, knowledge,
or wisdom. Verse 6 explicitly limits their lost portion to what is done under the sun.
These statements describe death from the present inquiry's horizon; they do not supply
a detailed account of resurrection or stages after death. Within the same stored book,
12:7 affirms the spirit's return to God and 12:14 affirms judgment of every work. Those
verses are verified comparisons, still awaiting their own ingestion, and should neither
be suppressed nor used to rewrite 9:5. A complete harmonization is not given here.

**Joy and effort without control.** Bread, wine, white garments, oil, and the loved wife
make enjoyment concrete. Reading the clothing and oil as glad living fits the sequence;
no ritual or resurrection symbolism is expressly supplied. The wife is not identified,
and the command is not an autobiographical claim about the speaker. Vanity remains the
description of the days. Work with might follows because opportunity ends at Sheol,
not because enough effort will guarantee bread, riches, or favor.

**Time and chance.** The five reversals of expected success (11) deny a reliable equation
between ability and outcome. They do not say that ability is useless. Verse 12 pictures
sudden evil with caught fish and birds; death is the unit's horizon, but every evil time
need not be reduced to the instant of death. The juxtaposition of God's hand (1) and
time and chance (11) remains explicit. The passage gives neither a theory of godless
randomness nor a disclosed providential cause for each disaster.
