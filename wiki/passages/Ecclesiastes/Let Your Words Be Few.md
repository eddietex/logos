# Let Your Words Be Few

**Book:** [[Ecclesiastes]]
**Reference:** Ecclesiastes 5:1–7

## Summary

The Preacher stops observing and starts commanding. Until now Ecclesiastes has reported what
he saw and tested under the sun; here, for the first time in the book, he addresses the reader
in a run of imperatives, and the subject is worship. The setting is *"God’s house"* (1), and
every instruction concerns what the worshipper does with feet, ears, mouth, and promises there.

Verse 1 sets the priority. Guard your steps when you go; *"to draw near to listen is better than
to give the sacrifice of fools,"* who do evil without knowing it. Verse 2 gives the reason for
restraint in speech: *"God is in heaven, and you on earth. Therefore let your words be few."*
Verse 3 compares a fool's speech to a dream: as many cares produce a dream, many words produce
a fool's voice.

Verses 4–6 turn to vows. A vow made to God is to be paid without delay, *"for he has no pleasure
in fools"* (4). Not vowing is better than vowing and not paying (5). The mouth must not lead
the body into sin, and the worshipper must not excuse an unpaid vow *"before the messenger"* as a
mistake; the question that follows is why God should be angry and destroy the work of the
speaker's hands (6).

Verse 7 closes the unit. Many dreams and many words contain vanities, *"but you must fear God."*
The vanity refrain, used so far for labor, pleasure, wisdom's limits, and popularity, is here
applied to religious talk, and the remedy named is not silence alone but the fear of God.

## Key verses

All seven verses are copied verbatim from stored WEB Ecclesiastes 5:1–7.

> **1** Guard your steps when you go to God’s house; for to draw near to listen is better than to give the sacrifice of fools, for they don’t know that they do evil.

> **2** Don’t be rash with your mouth, and don’t let your heart be hasty to utter anything before God; for God is in heaven, and you on earth. Therefore let your words be few.

> **3** For as a dream comes with a multitude of cares, so a fool’s speech with a multitude of words.

> **4** When you vow a vow to God, don’t defer to pay it; for he has no pleasure in fools. Pay that which you vow.

> **5** It is better that you should not vow, than that you should vow and not pay.

> **6** Don’t allow your mouth to lead you into sin. Don’t protest before the messenger that this was a mistake. Why should God be angry at your voice, and destroy the work of your hands?

> **7** For in the multitude of dreams there are vanities, as well as in many words: but you must fear God.

## People

- [[The Preacher (Ecclesiastes)]] — the speaker, here giving direct instruction for the first
  time in the book.
- [[God]] — named in every verse but 3 and 5: the one whose house is entered, who is in heaven,
  who takes no pleasure in fools, whose anger is warned of, and who is to be feared.

*The messenger* (6) is unidentified; see the notes. The wiki creates no page for him. The fools
of verses 1 and 4 are a type, not named people.

## Places

None named. *God’s house* (1) is not identified with any building. The book's royal setting is
[[Jerusalem]] (1:1, 12), and readers commonly assume the temple there, but this unit does not say
so, and the wiki does not link it to a place page.

## Themes

- [[The Fear of God]] — the unit's closing command, *"you must fear God"* (7).
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — pay the vow, and better not to vow than to vow and not pay (4–6).
- [[Language and Speech]] — the rash mouth, few words, and the many words of the fool (2–3, 6–7).
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — the sacrifice of fools ranked below drawing near to listen (1).
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — *God’s house* on earth, and *"God is in heaven"* (1–2).
- [[Prayer]] — speech uttered *before God* in his house, to be few and unhurried (2).
- [[Folly]] — fools who sacrifice without knowing they do evil (1), whose speech is many words
  (3), and in whom God has no pleasure (4).
- [[Sin]] — the mouth leading the flesh into sin, and the excuse of *a mistake* (6).
- [[Vanity]] — pronounced over many dreams and many words (7).

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 5:1–7 unit is retained. Verse 1 opens with a command after 4:16's
vanity refrain, and verse 7 closes with *"you must fear God."* Verse 8 turns back to observation,
the oppression of the poor in a province, which begins the next pericope.

**Chapter numbering.** The English 5:1 is 4:17 in the Hebrew text, and English 5:2–7 are Hebrew
5:1–6 (Mechon-Mamre's Masoretic text of Ecclesiastes 4 and 5, read 2026-10-27,
`https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt3104.htm` and `pt3105.htm`). The wiki follows the WEB's
numbering throughout. Read in Hebrew, the instruction to guard one's step closes chapter 4; the
WEB places it with the vow sayings it introduces.

**Steps or feet.** The WEB's *"Guard your steps"* renders *šəmōr raḡləḵā*, *guard your foot*. In
that Mechon-Mamre text the written form is the plural *raḡlêḵā*, *your feet*, and the reading
tradition gives the singular. The sense does not change: watch how you walk into God's house.

**Listening above sacrifice.** Verse 1 does not forbid sacrifice. It ranks *qārôḇ lišmōaʿ*, *to
draw near to listen* (same Mechon-Mamre text), above the offering of fools, and gives the fools'
fault as ignorance: *"they don’t know that they do evil."* That is the ranking of Proverbs 21:3,
27 and of 1 Samuel 15:22, where the verb *listen* also stands against sacrifice in the WEB. The
Hebrew of 1 Samuel has not been checked here, so the wiki makes no claim that the same Hebrew
verb is used. *To listen* can mean to hear instruction or to obey it; the verse does not say
which, and both fit what follows, where the mouth is to be restrained.

**God is in heaven.** Verse 2's reason is distance, not hostility: the worshipper stands on
earth, God in heaven, and words should be few in proportion. The phrase *yihyû ḏəḇārêḵā
məʿaṭṭîm*, *let your words be few* (Hebrew 5:1, same Mechon-Mamre text), is the source of this
page's title. The verse limits speech before God; it does not forbid prayer.

**Dreams.** Verses 3 and 7 both pair dreams with many words. In verse 3 the dream is an analogy:
much *ʿinyān*, *business* or *care*, produces a dream, as many words produce a fool's voice
(Hebrew 5:2). *ʿInyān* is the word the Preacher used for the *"burden"* God gave the sons of men
at 1:13 and 3:10 in the WEB; that link is drawn from the WEB's English and the Mechon-Mamre text
of 5:2 only, and the Hebrew of 1:13 and 3:10 was not rechecked in this pass. In verse 7 the
Hebrew is compressed — *kî ḇəroḇ ḥălōmôṯ wahăḇālîm ūḏəḇārîm harbēh* (Hebrew 5:6) — and the WEB's
*"in the multitude of dreams there are vanities, as well as in many words"* is one way of
construing it. The word *hăḇālîm*, *vanities*, is the plural of the book's keyword *heḇel*. The
dreams here are not revelatory dreams of the kind given to Joseph or Pharaoh in Genesis; nothing
in the unit treats them as messages from God.

**The vow.** Verses 4–5 restate the law's position: a vow is voluntary, but once made it must be
paid without delay, and not vowing is no sin. The WEB of Deuteronomy 23:21–23 (verified at
bible-api.com, 2026-10-27; not stored) says the same, almost clause for clause; see
[[Pay What You Vow - Deuteronomy 23.21-23, Proverbs 20.25, and Ecclesiastes 5.4-6]]. The motive
the Preacher gives is his own: God *"has no pleasure in fools"* (4). A vow left unpaid is
treated as folly, not only as a breach of law.

**The messenger and the mistake.** Verse 6's *"before the messenger"* renders *lip̄nê
hammalʾāḵ* (Hebrew 5:5, same Mechon-Mamre text). *Malʾāḵ* is the ordinary word for a messenger
and also the word used for an angel. Readers have taken this figure as a priest or temple
official who received or recorded vows, as an angel, or as a stand-in for God; the text does
not identify him, and the wiki leaves him unidentified. The excuse *"this was a mistake"* renders
*šəḡāḡâ*, the word for an unintentional sin (same text). The verse forbids calling a deliberate
vow inadvertent in order to escape it; the sacrificial law's provisions for unintentional sin
are not quoted or ingested here, so the connection is noted by vocabulary only.

**Fear God.** Verse 7's command, *kî ʾeṯ-hāʾĕlōhîm yərāʾ* (Hebrew 5:6, same Mechon-Mamre text),
is the book's first imperative to fear God. 3:14 said God's work endures *"that men should fear
before him"*; here the fear becomes an instruction to the reader, and 12:13 will make it the
conclusion. Against the many words and dreams that the Preacher calls vanities, the fear of God
is the one thing in the unit that is not.
