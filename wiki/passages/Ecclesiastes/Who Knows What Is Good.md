# Who Knows What Is Good

**Book:** [[Ecclesiastes]]
**Reference:** Ecclesiastes 6:10–12

## Summary

The Preacher turns from wealth and unsatisfied appetite to the limits surrounding human
life. Whatever has been was named long ago; what humanity is is known, and a person cannot
contend with one mightier than himself (10). The stronger party is not named in this verse.
Reading him as God fits the book's account of enduring divine work and limited human power,
but remains an interpretation of this sentence, not an explicit identification.

Many words create vanity, and the question of human profit returns (11). More speech does
not itself supply the advantage the inquiry has been seeking. Two questions close the unit:
who knows what is good during a life spent like a shadow, and who can tell someone what
will be after him under the sun (12)? Neither receives an answer here. The question about
good carries forward the inquiry of 2:3 and the unsatisfied life of 6:1–9, while the question
about what follows recalls 3:22.

## Key verses

All three verses are copied verbatim from immutable stored WEB Ecclesiastes 6:10–12.

> **10** Whatever has been, its name was given long ago; and it is known what man is; neither can he contend with him who is mightier than he.

> **11** For there are many words that create vanity. What does that profit man?

> **12** For who knows what is good for man in life, all the days of his vain life which he spends like a shadow? For who can tell a man what will be after him under the sun?

## Notes

**Boundary.** Retain the planned three-verse unit, 6:10–12. It closes the preceding
inquiry with a statement of limits and questions of profit, good, and the future. The
better-than sayings beginning at 7:1 remain the next pericope; none is ingested here.

**Named long ago.** The WEB states that the name was given; it does not identify the
namer or narrate a new naming ceremony. Reading naming as an established order fits the
movement toward human limits, but it does not establish a complete theory of determinism.
The English text does not identify the man as the individual Adam. No Hebrew wordplay,
source-language grammar, or etymology is asserted without verification.

**The mightier party.** God is a plausible contextual reading, given 3:14's enduring
work and 6:2's dependence on his gift. The designation alone does not explicitly name
God. On that reading the inability to contend expresses an unequal relation, not a
narrated prohibition against every prayer, complaint, or question. Job 9:14–19, 32–35
likewise describes the inequality but leads into direct complaint in 10:1–2. Job 13:3,
15 continues the desire to argue a case. These voices must not be flattened into a
single rule that the afflicted may never speak.

**Words and profit.** The reading here follows the stored WEB's many words in 6:11.
No claim is made about all possible translations of the underlying term. Ecclesiastes
5:2–7 supplies a close comparison, but 6:10–12 does not repeat its temple setting or
vow instructions. The question concerns what the multiplication achieves; it is not a
blanket denial of worthwhile teaching or a command for permanent silence.

**Who knows, beside I know.** There is a real tension between 6:12's question and the
Preacher's positive judgments in 3:12–13 and 5:18: rejoicing, doing good, and enjoying
labor are commended. One contextual reading distinguishes a present good received from
God from comprehensive knowledge of what makes a whole life good. That distinction is
interpretive; 6:12 itself does not add a qualification resolving the tension. The wiki
retains both the affirmative statements and the unanswered question.

**After him under the sun.** The stated horizon is what will happen under the sun after
a person, not an explicit description of that person's experience in an afterlife.
The shadow image emphasizes passing days. Neither an exact lifespan nor a doctrine of
postmortem consciousness is supplied. Compare 3:22's inability to see what follows.
