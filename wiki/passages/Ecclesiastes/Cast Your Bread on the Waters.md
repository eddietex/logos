# Cast Your Bread on the Waters

**Book:** [[Ecclesiastes]]
**Reference:** Ecclesiastes 11:1–6

## Summary

After the sayings about rulers and fools in chapter 10, the Preacher turns to commands. The unit
opens with two of them: *"Cast your bread on the waters; for you shall find it after many days"*
(11:1), and *"Give a portion to seven, yes, even to eight"* (2). The first gives a reason in
hope, a return after many days. The second gives a reason in ignorance: *"you don’t know what
evil will be on the earth."*

Two observations about the natural world follow (3). Full clouds empty themselves on the earth,
and a fallen tree lies where it falls, whether toward the south or the north. Some things happen
by their own course, and once they have happened they stay as they are. The next saying turns
the observation against hesitation: *"He who observes the wind won’t sow; and he who regards the
clouds won’t reap"* (4). Whoever waits for certainty about the weather never works.

Verse 5 names the limit behind it. You do not know the way of the wind, nor how the bones grow in
the womb of a pregnant woman; *"even so you don’t know the work of God who does all."* The
unit closes with a final command that follows from that ignorance: sow in the morning, and do
not withhold your hand in the evening, *"for you don’t know which will prosper, whether this or
that, or whether they both will be equally good"* (6).

The unit does not ask the reader to stop working because the outcome is unknown. It argues the
opposite. Because no one knows what will prosper, the right response is to act widely and
persistently, spreading the risk across seven or eight portions and across morning and evening.
*You don't know* appears four times (2, 5 twice, 6), and what the unit draws from it is a
command, not the vanity verdict the book has so often given.

## Key verses

The complete unit is copied verbatim from immutable stored WEB Ecclesiastes 11.

> **11:1** Cast your bread on the waters; for you shall find it after many days.

> **11:2** Give a portion to seven, yes, even to eight; for you don’t know what evil will be on the earth.

> **11:3** If the clouds are full of rain, they empty themselves on the earth; and if a tree falls toward the south, or toward the north, in the place where the tree falls, there shall it be.

> **11:4** He who observes the wind won’t sow; and he who regards the clouds won’t reap.

> **11:5** As you don’t know what is the way of the wind, nor how the bones grow in the womb of her who is with child; even so you don’t know the work of God who does all.

> **11:6** In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening don’t withhold your hand; for you don’t know which will prosper, whether this or that, or whether they both will be equally good.

## People

- [[The Preacher (Ecclesiastes)]] — the implied speaker of the commands. He does not speak in the
  first person in this unit, and reports nothing he has seen.
- [[God]] — *"who does all"* (11:5); his work is what the reader does not know.

The sower, the observer of the wind, and the pregnant woman are figures of general sayings. None
is identified with a historical individual, and none receives a person page.

## Places

No named place appears. The *south* and *north* of 11:3 are directions, not places.

## Themes

- [[Work]] — Sowing and reaping, morning and evening; the hand not withheld.
- [[Providence]] — The work of God who does all, unknown in its course (11:5).
- [[Wisdom]] — Wisdom as acting well without knowing the outcome, not as foreknowledge.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Bread sent on the waters and a portion given to seven or eight.
- [[Creation]] — Rain from full clouds, the fallen tree, the wind, the bones in the womb.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 11:1–6 unit is retained. 11:1 begins a run of imperatives after the
sayings of chapter 10, which ended with the warning about cursing the king (10:20). 11:6 closes
with a final *you don't know*, and 11:7 turns to a new subject: *"Truly the light is sweet"*,
the opening of the call to rejoice in youth and remember the Creator. The planned title is kept.

**Bread on the waters: charity or trade.** The WEB's *"Cast your bread"* renders *šallaḥ
laḥməḵā ʿal-pənê hammāyim* (MAM, read 2026-10-27), literally *send your bread on the face of the
waters*. Two readings are common. One takes it as generosity: give freely, and the gift returns.
The other takes it as maritime trade: send goods overseas, and the return comes after a long
voyage. Verse 2 suits both: giving to *"seven, yes, even to eight"* can mean generosity to many,
or spreading an investment across several ventures, because *"you don’t know what evil will be
on the earth."* The text does not decide between them. What it does say is that a return is
expected, but only after *"many days"*, and that it is not guaranteed for any single portion.
Neither reading is adopted here.

**Seven, yes, eight.** *ləšiḇʿâ wəḡam lišmônâ* (MAM) is a graded numerical saying, the form
already seen in Proverbs 6:16, 30:15–31, and Job 5:19. Unlike those, it introduces no list. The
second number simply means *more than enough*. Micah 5:5 uses the same pair of numbers, *"seven
shepherds, and eight leaders of men"* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com on 2026-10-27; the Hebrew of
Micah was not checked).

**What falls stays.** The verb of 11:3, *yippôl* (*falls*), appears twice (MAM). It is the same
form as *"may fall"* in 10:8, where the digger may fall into his pit. Here it describes not a
hazard but a settled fact: the tree lies where it fell. The pair *south* and *north* (*dārôm*,
*ṣāp̄ôn*) is the same pair as the wind's course in 1:6. There the wind went round and round;
here the tree simply stays down.

**Wind or spirit.** The Hebrew word in 11:4 and 11:5 is the same, *rûaḥ* (MAM), which can mean
*wind*, *breath*, or *spirit*. The WEB translates both as *wind*. Some readers take 11:5 as
*the way of the spirit* into the bones of the unborn child, linking the two halves of the
verse. The WEB keeps them as two parallel mysteries, and that reading is followed here. In 11:5
the Hebrew has no verb corresponding to the WEB's *"grow"*. It reads *kaʿăṣāmîm bəḇeṭen
hamməlēʾâ*, *like bones in the womb of the full one* (MAM); the WEB's *"how the bones grow"* and
*"her who is with child"* make that sense explicit. No lexicon was consulted.

**The work of God who does all.** 11:5's *maʿăśēh hāʾĕlōhîm ʾăšer yaʿăśeh ʾet-hakkōl* repeats a
phrase from earlier passages: *hammaʿăśeh ʾăšer-ʿāśâ hāʾĕlōhîm* in 3:11 and *kol-maʿăśēh
hāʾĕlōhîm* in 8:17 (MAM). In both earlier passages the point was that human beings cannot find
out God's work. Here the same ignorance leads to the opposite conclusion from despair: work in
the morning and in the evening.

**Prospering and skill.** 11:6's *yiḵšar* (*will prosper*) shares its root, *k-š-r*, with
*hakšêr* in 10:10 (MAM), where the WEB has *"skill brings success"*. In 10:10 wisdom gives an
advantage when it is applied in time. In 11:6 no one knows which effort will succeed, so the
advantage lies in sowing more than once. The shared root is a verbal link; whether the author
intended it is not determined here.

**Do not withhold your hand.** 11:6's *ʾal-tannaḥ yāḏeḵā* (MAM) is literally *do not let your
hand rest*. It sits against the fool's folded hands of 4:5 and the idle hands of 10:18 (see
[[Folding the Hands - Proverbs 6.10, 24.33, and Ecclesiastes 4.5]]), and beside 9:10's
*"Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might"*.

**Not a reversal of the vanity verdict.** This unit does not say that labor yields lasting
gain, and the next verses (11:8) return to *"All that comes is vanity."* What changes is the
response to uncertainty. Earlier, ignorance of what follows led to enjoying the present portion
(3:22) or to unanswered questions (6:12). Here it leads to diligent, diversified work. The two
responses stand together.
