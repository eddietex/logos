# The Wicked Buried and the Work Beyond Finding

**Book:** [[Ecclesiastes]]
**Reference:** Ecclesiastes 8:10–17

## Summary

The Preacher sees the wicked buried and, in the stored WEB, forgotten in the city where
they acted (10). Delayed execution of sentence encourages people to commit evil (11).
A sinner may commit crimes a hundred times and live long, yet the speaker insists that
it will be better with those who fear God; it will not be well with the wicked (12–13).
He immediately reports the opposite distribution of outcomes: righteous people receive
what fits the wicked, and wicked people what fits the righteous. This too is vanity (14).

The response is to commend eating, drinking, and joy as a companion in labor during the
life God gives (15). The commendation does not narrate the correction of injustice.
The Preacher then describes his application to wisdom and the activity of the earth,
including sleeplessness day and night (16). His finding is a limit: people cannot find
out God's work, however much they labor to search, and a wise person's claim to comprehend
it does not overcome that limit (17).

## Key verses

The complete unit is copied verbatim from immutable stored WEB Ecclesiastes 8.

> **10** So I saw the wicked buried. Indeed they came also from holiness. They went and were forgotten in the city where they did this. This also is vanity.

> **11** Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil.

> **12** Though a sinner commits crimes a hundred times, and lives long, yet surely I know that it will be better with those who fear God, who are reverent before him.

> **13** But it shall not be well with the wicked, neither shall he lengthen days like a shadow; because he doesn’t fear God.

> **14** There is a vanity which is done on the earth, that there are righteous men to whom it happens according to the work of the wicked. Again, there are wicked men to whom it happens according to the work of the righteous. I said that this also is vanity.

> **15** Then I commended mirth, because a man has no better thing under the sun, than to eat, and to drink, and to be joyful: for that will accompany him in his labor all the days of his life which God has given him under the sun.

> **16** When I applied my heart to know wisdom, and to see the business that is done on the earth (for also there is that neither day nor night sees sleep with his eyes),

> **17** then I saw all the work of God, that man can’t find out the work that is done under the sun, because however much a man labors to seek it out, yet he won’t find it. Yes even though a wise man thinks he can comprehend it, he won’t be able to find it.

## Notes

**Boundary and title.** The planned 8:10–17 boundary is retained: an observation about the
wicked opens the unit, and the failure to comprehend God's work closes it. The provisional
queue title, *The Wicked Honored and the Work Beyond Finding*, is revised because the stored
WEB says buried and forgotten, not honored. This is a title correction to match the source,
not a conclusion about another translation or the underlying Hebrew.

**The difficult opening.** The WEB's “came also from holiness” (10) is retained verbatim.
The sequence of coming, going, burial, and forgetting is compressed and difficult to
reconstruct. This page follows the WEB's continuing plural subject without inventing a
funeral procession, a named city, or an identified temple. No source-language edition was
consulted, and no claim about the Hebrew wording or textual variants is made.

**Delay and its consequences.** Verse 11 describes delay as encouraging evil, but does not
identify the sentencing authority or say why execution is delayed. A human court fits the
preceding royal context; divine judgment fits the wider inquiry. Neither identification
is explicit in this verse. Delay should not be called divine patience on this verse alone,
and the observation is not a detailed prescription for a criminal justice system.

**Explicit tension: long life and recompense.** Verse 12 allows a persistent sinner to live
long while affirming that those who fear God will fare better. Verse 13 denies well-being
and lengthened days to the wicked, and verse 14 reports the righteous and wicked receiving
outcomes corresponding to the other's work. These are competing assertions within the
same speaker's unit. An ultimate reckoning may reconcile them in a larger theology, but
neither a date nor an afterlife setting is stated here. Reading the assurance as a
traditional saying being tested is also an interpretation: the WEB introduces it as what
the speaker knows, without marking a separate speaker or withdrawing the claim.

**Explicit tension: mirth commended.** Verse 15 follows the verdict on injustice without
reporting its repair. Its eating, drinking, and joy belong to life and labor given by God.
This stands beside 2:1–2's verdict on mirth and 7:2–4's preference for mourning. Present
enjoyment can be distinguished from a project to secure lasting profit, but that reading
must not make the contrary evaluations disappear or turn pleasure into moral indifference.

**Searching without sleep.** Verse 16's parenthesis describes someone who sees no sleep
day or night, without expressly identifying that person. It can intensify the picture of
human activity and inquiry; it does not establish a personal medical history for the
Preacher. Verse 17 denies that either effort or a wise person's confidence can secure
comprehension. It does not retract every claim to knowledge: 8:12's assurance and 8:15's
commendation remain alongside it.
