# Love Better Than Wine

**Book:** [[Song of Solomon]]
**Reference:** Song of Solomon 1:1–4

## Summary

The heading associates the song with Solomon (1). The first speaking voice immediately
asks for kisses and values the beloved's love above wine (2). His pleasing oils become
a comparison for his name: poured-out fragrance expresses the attraction that also
draws the virgins to him (3). The request to be taken away gives the opening urgency;
the statement about the king's rooms moves into intimacy, followed by plural rejoicing
and praise of love above wine, and a final affirmation of that love (4).

This is desire expressed by the woman herself, not a father's instruction about how a
son should love. Wine and fragrance supply positive measures of delight; surpassing
wine does not make wine evil. The repetition in verses 2 and 4 frames the opening with
the same valuation. The movement between singular and plural also keeps love from
being voiced only in private: other voices affirm it, although their identities and
the exact distribution of the lines are not established by a narrated scene.

The heading names Solomon, but it does not identify the woman, explicitly name the
beloved in verses 2–4, or explain whether the king and beloved are one person. Reading
the whole opening as a scene from Solomon's life is possible, but is an interpretation,
not additional biographical information supplied by these verses.

## Key verses

> **1** The Song of songs, which is Solomon’s.

> **2** Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth; for your love is better than wine.

> **3** Your oils have a pleasing fragrance. Your name is oil poured out, therefore the virgins love you.

> **4** Take me away with you. Let us hurry. The king has brought me into his rooms. Friends We will be glad and rejoice in you. We will praise your love more than wine! Beloved They are right to love you.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The heading and 1:2–4 form the opening unit, ending with the renewed wine
comparison and affirmation. Verse 5 begins self-description and addresses the daughters
of Jerusalem. That transition supplies the next boundary; only this first unit is ingested.

**Speaker cues and the raw source.** The fetched bible-api.com WEB includes the words
Friends and Beloved within verse 4. They are preserved exactly above and treated here
as the supplied edition's speaker cues, not personal names or dialogue spoken aloud.
The role pages are a practical way of following the literary voices; they do not establish
historical identities, a particular dramatic cast, or certainty about every speaker change.
No claim about the wording of a Hebrew edition is made.

**What the opening does not settle.** No wedding or marriage status is stated here, and
no particular wife of Solomon is identified. The request for kisses is not an adultery
scene merely because the wiki has previously met erotic language in Proverbs' warnings.
The poem does not identify the lovers as God and a people, or as Christ and the church;
such a theological reading would require a further interpretive argument.

**Existing synthesis corrected.** The Love page previously described the whole Song as
having no command or ranking. This opening already contains requests and ranks love
above wine; later stored verses contain injunctions and comparisons (2:7; 5:9–10; 6:9).
The earlier blanket statement is explicitly withdrawn there. The positive comparison
does not itself reproduce Genesis's conflict over preferred family members.
