# Make Haste, My Beloved

**Book:** [[Song of Solomon]]
**Reference:** Song of Solomon 8:13–14

## Summary

The Song ends with a request to hear a voice and a summons to the beloved. One speaker
addresses someone dwelling in the gardens, with friends in attendance (8:13). The
reply calls the beloved away and pictures him as a gazelle or young stag on mountains
of spices (8:14). Reading the man as the first speaker and the woman as the respondent
fits the earlier exchanges, but the stored WEB supplies no speaker labels here.

The closing words return to the voice, animal comparison, and mountains of
[[Rise Up, My Love]] (2:14–17), and to the fragrant heights of [[You Are All Beautiful]]
(4:6). Gardens and spices recall [[A Garden Locked and Opened]]. These recurrences
make the ending recognizable without providing a final narrated reunion. The woman
has the last invitation on this reading; no account follows of what he does.

## Key verses

> **13** You who dwell in the gardens, with friends in attendance, let me hear your voice!

> **14** Come away, my beloved! Be like a gazelle or a young stag on the mountains of spices!

## People

- [[The Man (Song of Solomon)]] — likely asks to hear her voice in 8:13, then is addressed.
- [[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]] — likely the garden-dweller and the voice of 8:14.
- Friends — an unnamed group in attendance (8:13); not securely identified with the
  daughters of Jerusalem or any earlier company. No separate person page.

## Places

- Gardens and mountains of spices — poetic settings without a named geographical
  location. The mountains recall 2:17 and 4:6 but are not explicitly identified with
  Bether, Lebanon, or a literal estate; no new place page.

- [[Language and Speech]] — hearing the beloved's voice is itself desired.
- [[Joy and Enjoyment]] — animal movement and fragrant landscape express anticipated delight.

## Notes

**Boundary and title.** The planned two-verse closing exchange is retained. The inherited
queue title, *Make Haste, My Beloved*, is an editorial title, not a quotation from the
stored WEB. That text reads “Come away, my beloved!” No claim about the exact Hebrew
verb or an alternative edition is made here.

**An open ending.** The summons does not establish either permanent separation or a
completed journey. Likewise, friends in attendance need not mean a wedding ceremony.
The garden imagery can evoke intimacy without fixing the lovers' location or turning
every garden into the woman's body. The earlier union in 4:16–5:1 and the continuing
summons here are different poetic moments, not mutually exclusive historical claims.

**Sources.** Both verses above are copied from the stored WEB Song of Solomon 8:13–14.
Comparisons at 2:8–17; 4:6, 12–16; 5:1 were read in the same raw file. No external
quotation or new source-language assertion is used. Speaker assignments remain
