# I Opened, but He Had Gone

**Book:** [[Song of Solomon]]
**Reference:** Song of Solomon 5:2–8

## Summary

The woman is asleep, *"but my heart was awake"* (5:2). She hears her beloved knocking, and she
reports his words: *"Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled"*. He gives the reason he
wants to come in: his head is wet with dew and his hair with the damp of the night. He is outside in
the dark, and she is inside.

She does not open at once. *"I have taken off my robe. Indeed, must I put it on? I have washed my
feet. Indeed, must I soil them?"* (5:3). The two questions are her excuse, and they are the only
words she says to him in the unit, if she says them aloud at all. Then he puts his hand in through
the opening of the latch, and her heart pounds for him (5:4).

She gets up to open. Her hands drip with myrrh, her fingers with *"liquid myrrh, on the handles of
the lock"* (5:5). The verse slows down at the door: the hands, the fingers, the myrrh, the handles.
Then the turn comes in one line: *"I opened to my beloved; but my beloved left; and had gone away"*
(5:6). The delay of 5:3 has cost her the meeting. Her heart *"went out when he spoke"*, and she does
what she did at 3:1–2: she looks for him and does not find him. This time she also calls him, and
he does not answer.

The watchmen who go about the city find her, as they did at 3:3. This time they do not let her
pass. *"They beat me. They bruised me. The keepers of the walls took my cloak away from me"* (5:7).
She is out at night, struck by the men who keep the city, and stripped of her outer garment. The
poem gives no reason for what they do, and she says nothing to them.

The unit ends with an adjuration, like 2:7 and 3:5, but the request is new. She puts the daughters
of Jerusalem under oath, not to keep from awakening love, but to carry a message: *"If you find my
beloved, that you tell him that I am faint with love"* (5:8). *Faint with love* is what she said of
herself at 2:5, in the banquet hall, with him beside her. Here she says it with him gone.

The unit is the second of the Song's two night searches, and it repeats the first almost phrase by
phrase, except at the end. In [[Seeking Him by Night]] the watchmen only found her, and she found him
*"scarcely"* after (3:4). Here the watchmen hurt her, and she never finds him. The comparison is laid
out on [[The Two Night Searches - Song of Solomon 3.1-5 and 5.2-8]].

It also comes straight after the Song's scene of fullest union, [[A Garden Locked and Opened]], where
she invited him in and he came (4:16–5:1). Here he asks to come in and she hesitates. The poem does
not say how the two scenes are related in time. Set side by side, they show the same door opened
twice, once in time and once too late.

The woman speaks the whole unit. The man's only words are quoted by her (5:2). He knocks, reaches in,
and goes, and he says nothing when she calls.

## Key verses

> **2** I was asleep, but my heart was awake. It is the voice of my beloved who knocks: “Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled; for my head is filled with dew, and my hair with the dampness of the night.”

> **3** I have taken off my robe. Indeed, must I put it on? I have washed my feet. Indeed, must I soil them?

> **4** My beloved thrust his hand in through the latch opening. My heart pounded for him.

> **5** I rose up to open for my beloved. My hands dripped with myrrh, my fingers with liquid myrrh, on the handles of the lock.

> **6** I opened to my beloved; but my beloved left; and had gone away. My heart went out when he spoke. I looked for him, but I didn’t find him. I called him, but he didn’t answer.

> **7** The watchmen who go about the city found me. They beat me. They bruised me. The keepers of the walls took my cloak away from me.

> **8** I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, If you find my beloved, that you tell him that I am faint with love.

## People

- [[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]] — the only speaker. She hears him, delays, opens, searches, calls,
  is beaten by the watchmen, and adjures the daughters (5:2–8).
- [[The Man (Song of Solomon)]] — *"my beloved"*, six times in the WEB of 5:2–8. He knocks, asks to
  come in, puts his hand through the latch opening, and is gone when she opens (5:2–6). His only words
  are the request she quotes.
- [[The Watchmen (Song of Solomon)]] — *"who go about the city"*, the same men as 3:3; here they beat
  and wound her, and *"the keepers of the walls"* take her cloak (5:7).
- [[The Daughters of Jerusalem]] — adjured a third time, with a new request: to tell him she is faint
  with love (5:8). They answer in the next unit (5:9).

## Places

- [[Jerusalem]] — named only in the address *"daughters of Jerusalem"* (5:8).
- *The city* and its walls (5:7) are not named, as at 3:2–3. The wiki does not identify them with
  Jerusalem.
- The house with the door and the lock (5:2–6) is not located, and whose house it is is not said.

## Themes

- [[Love]] — the knock and the request to come in, the delay, the absence, and *"I am faint with
  love"* (5:2–8).
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — the third adjuration, *"I adjure you"*, with a request instead of a
  prohibition (5:8).
- [[City and Civilization]] — the watchmen who go about the city and the keepers of its walls (5:7).
- [[Light and Darkness]] — the dew and *"the dampness of the night"* on his hair (5:2); the search at
  night.
- [[Suffering]] — beaten and wounded by the watchmen, for no stated reason (5:7).
- [[Nakedness and Covering]] — the robe she has taken off (5:3) and the cloak taken from her (5:7).

## Notes

**Boundary.** The unit runs from her sleep (5:2) to the adjuration (5:8). The planned boundary held,
and no box was redrawn. The Masoretic text divides differently: Sefaria's *Miqra according to the
Masorah* marks a section break (*{ס}*) after 5:1 and the next one after 6:3, with none after 5:8. So
the Masoretic section is 5:2–6:3, the whole movement from losing him to *"My beloved is mine"*. The
wiki keeps 5:2–8 and 5:9–6:3 apart because 5:9 is a new voice, the daughters' question, and begins
the description of the man. The two units are tied: the daughters ask *"that you do so adjure us?"*
(5:9), answering 5:8 directly.

**Who speaks.** The whole unit is the woman's first-person narration. The forms are feminine: *ʾănî
yǝšēnâ*, *I* (fem.) *am asleep* (5:2), and the man is *dôḏî*, *my beloved*, throughout (Sefaria,
*Miqra according to the Masorah*). The man's words in 5:2 are reported by her, as his invitation was
at 2:10–14.

**"Asleep, but my heart was awake."** *ʾĂnî yǝšēnâ wǝlibbî ʿēr* (5:2; Sefaria). *ʿĒr*, *awake*, has
the consonants of the verb of the refrain, *ʿ-w-r*, *to awaken* (*tāʿîrû … tǝʿôrǝrû*, 2:7; Sefaria).
The wiki takes *ʿēr* as a form of that root, the usual reading; it has not been checked in a
lexicon here. If so, the unit begins by saying that her heart was already *awake*, the word the
daughters were charged not to do to love. Many readers take the line as describing a dream, as with
3:1–4; others as half-sleep, or as a waking night. The text does not decide, and neither does the
wiki.

**"The voice of my beloved."** *Qôl dôḏî* (5:2) is exactly how 2:8 begins, *"The voice of my
beloved!"* (Sefaria for both). There he came leaping on the mountains; here he is already at the
door, knocking (*dôp̄ēq*).

**Four names.** *ʾĂḥōtî raʿyātî yônātî tammātî*: *my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled* (5:2;
Sefaria). *Sister* has come before only as *"my sister, my bride"* (4:9, 10, 12; 5:1 in the stored
WEB); here *bride* is gone, and in the stored WEB it does not return. *My dove* was his word for her
at 2:14. *Tammātî*, *my perfect one*, *my undefiled*, from *t-m-m*, *to be complete*, returns with
*my dove* at 6:9, *yônātî tammātî* (Sefaria). Compare *"There is no spot in you"* (4:7).

**Dew.** He is wet with *ṭal* and his locks, *qǝwuṣṣôṯay*, with *rǝsîsê lāyǝlâ*, *drops of the night*
(5:2; Sefaria). *Qǝwuṣṣôt* is the word for *his hair* again at 5:11, where she describes it *"black
as a raven"* (Sefaria; stored WEB, not yet ingested).

**Her excuse.** *Kuttāntî*, *my robe* (5:3; Sefaria), is a *kǝtōnet*, the garment-word of the
*"coats of animal skins"* God made at Genesis 3:21 (*kāṯǝnôṯ ʿôr*; Sefaria). *ʾÊḵāḵâ*, *how?*, opens
both of her questions (5:3). They are rhetorical: she has undressed and washed her feet, and getting
up would undo both. The poem does not say whether she says this to him or to herself.

**His hand.** *Dôḏî šālaḥ yādô min-haḥōr*, *my beloved sent his hand from the hole* (5:4; Sefaria).
The WEB's *latch opening* is an interpretation of *ḥōr*, *hole*: an opening in the door through which
a bolt could be reached. Many readers also hear the line, and the myrrh of 5:5, as sexual imagery.
The text supports the literal scene at the door and does not explain an image beyond it, so the wiki
records the reading and does not adopt it.

**"My heart pounded for him."** The Hebrew is *ûmēʿay hāmû ʿālāyw*, literally *my inward parts
stirred* (or *moaned*) *for him* (5:4; Sefaria). *Mēʿîm* is the belly, the bowels, the inner body as
the seat of feeling. The same verb and noun are said by God of Ephraim at Jeremiah 31:20, *hāmû mēʿay
lô*, rendered in the WEB *"my heart yearns for him"* (Sefaria; WEB checked at bible-api.com). In 5:14
the same noun, *mēʿāyw*, is *his body* in her description of him (Sefaria; WEB *"His body"*).

**Myrrh on the lock.** Her hands *nāṭǝp̄û-mōr*, *dripped myrrh*, and her fingers *mōr ʿōḇēr*,
*flowing myrrh*, on *kappôt hammanʿûl*, *the handles of the lock* (5:5; Sefaria). The same three
words describe his lips at 5:13, *nōṭǝp̄ôt mōr ʿōḇēr*, *"dropping liquid myrrh"* (Sefaria; stored
WEB). Whether the myrrh was on her hands already, or was left on the lock by him, is not said. Myrrh
belongs to the lovers throughout: the sachet of 1:13, the mountain of 4:6, the garden of 4:14, the
myrrh he gathered at 5:1. See
[[Myrrh, Aloes, and Cinnamon - Proverbs 7.17, Song of Solomon 4.14, and Psalm 45.8]].

**"Had gone away."** *Wǝḏôḏî ḥāmaq ʿāḇār*, *and my beloved had turned away, had passed on* (5:6;
Sefaria). *ʿĀḇār*, *passed on*, is the same root as the *ʿōḇēr*, *flowing*, of the myrrh in the
verse before: the myrrh *passes* over her fingers and he *passes* on. The wiki notes the repetition;
it does not claim the poet intended a pun. *Ḥāmaq* is rare; this page has not checked its other
occurrences.

**"My heart went out when he spoke."** *Napšî yāṣǝʾâ ḇǝḏabbǝrô*, literally *my soul went out at his
speaking* (5:6; Sefaria). *Napšî* is the word of *"him whom my soul loves"* (3:1–4). The phrase *her
soul going out* is used of Rachel's death at Genesis 35:18, *bǝṣēʾt napšāh*, *"As her soul was
departing"* (Sefaria; stored WEB). Here it means something like *I nearly fainted*. The timing is a
puzzle: he spoke in 5:2, before she delayed, but the line comes after he is gone. It may look back
to his words at the door, or mean that she remembers them now. The text does not say, and the
WEB's *"when he spoke"* keeps the puzzle.

**Seeking and calling.** *Biqqaštîhû wǝlōʾ mǝṣāʾtîhû qǝrāʾtîw wǝlōʾ ʿānānî*, *I sought him and did not
find him; I called him and he did not answer me* (5:6; Sefaria). The first half repeats 3:1–2 with
the same verbs. The second half is new. Its verbs, *q-r-ʾ* and *ʿ-n-h*, are those of Wisdom's warning
at Proverbs 1:28, *ʾāz yiqrāʾunǝnî wǝlōʾ ʾeʿĕneh*, *"Then will they call on me, but I will not
answer"* (Sefaria; stored WEB). Proverbs' verb for *seek* there is a different one, *š-ḥ-r*. See
[[Seeking in the Streets and Squares - Song of Solomon 3.1-4 and Proverbs 1, 7, and 8]]. The wiki
does not claim the Song alludes to Proverbs.

**The watchmen.** *Mǝṣāʾunî haššōmǝrîm hassōḇǝḇîm bāʿîr*: the line of 3:3, word for word (Sefaria).
Then *hikkûnî pǝṣāʿûnî*, *they struck me, they wounded me* (5:7). *P-ṣ-ʿ* is the *wound* of Exodus
21:25, *peṣaʿ taḥaṯ pāṣaʿ*, *"wound for wound"*, and of Lamech's boast at Genesis 4:23, *"I have slain
a man for wounding me"* (Sefaria; stored WEB). It is a word for real injury. The second group, *šōmǝrê
haḥōmôt*, *the keepers of the walls*, is the same verb *š-m-r* in a new phrase. Whether they are the
same men as the watchmen or a second group is not said; this wiki keeps them on one page. Readers have
explained the violence as the city's punishment of a woman out alone at night, as a nightmare, or as
an image of what love's absence costs. The text gives no reason.

**Her cloak.** *Rǝḏîḏî*, *my cloak* or *my shawl* (5:7; Sefaria). The word is rare. It appears at
Isaiah 3:23 among the finery of Jerusalem's women, *hārǝḏîḏîm*, rendered *"the shawls"* in the WEB
(Sefaria; WEB checked at bible-api.com). It is not the *kǝtōnet* of 5:3. She took off one garment
herself to sleep; the other is taken from her.

**The third adjuration.** *Hišbaʿtî ʾetḵem bǝnôt yǝrûšālāim* begins exactly as 2:7 and 3:5, without
the roes and the hinds (5:8; Sefaria). Then it changes: *ʾim-timṣǝʾû ʾet-dôḏî mah-taggîdû lô
šeḥôlat ʾahăḇâ ʾānî*. Word for word this is *if you find my beloved, what will you tell him? — that I
am sick with love*. The WEB makes it one request, *"that you tell him"*. The final clause is the
same as 2:5, *kî-ḥôlat ʾahăḇâ ʾānî*, *"For I am faint with love"*, with *še-* in place of *kî* (Sefaria
for both). At 2:5 she was faint in his presence; here, in his absence. The daughters' reply in 5:9,
*šekkāḵâ hišbaʿtānû*, *"that you do so adjure us"*, uses the same verb (Sefaria). The third refrain at
8:4 also has *mah* where 2:7 and 3:5 have *ʾim* (Sefaria; see [[Seeking Him by Night]]); the two uses of
*mah* need not mean the same thing.

**Rejection and return.** Many readers, Jewish and Christian, have read 5:2–6 allegorically: God or
Christ knocking, the beloved people or soul slow to open, the beloved withdrawing, and a search that
costs the seeker. The New Testament's *"I stand at the door and knock"* (Revelation 3:20) is often set
beside the scene. The wiki records the reading and the parallel on
[[The Beloved at the Door - Song of Solomon 5.2-6 and Revelation 3.20]]; it does not claim
Revelation quotes the Song.

**Pages decided.** [[The Watchmen (Song of Solomon)]] gets a page now. At 3:3 the decision was put
off until 5:2–8, when they act again; they recur, act, and are named as a group, the same grounds as
[[The Daughters of Jerusalem]]. *The keepers of the walls* are kept on that page, not split off. No
page for the house, the door, or the city. No new theme page; [[Suffering]] and
[[Nakedness and Covering]] take this unit as short entries.
