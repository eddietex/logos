# Delight and the Unawakened Love

**Book:** [[Song of Solomon]]
**Reference:** Song of Solomon 1:9–2:7

## Summary

The unit is an exchange of praise. A voice that says *"my love"* compares the woman to a
steed in Pharaoh's chariots and admires her cheeks and neck with their jewels (1:9–10). A
plural voice promises her earrings of gold with studs of silver (11). The woman answers
with fragrance. While the king sat at his table, her perfume spread. Her beloved is a
sachet of myrrh between her breasts and a cluster of henna blossoms from the vineyards of
En Gedi (12–14).

Then the two lovers trade the same words. *"Behold, you are beautiful, my love"* — her eyes
are doves (15). *"Behold, you are beautiful, my beloved"* — and their couch is green, their
house beamed with cedar and fir (16–17). She calls herself a rose of Sharon, a lily of the
valleys (2:1). He outdoes the image: she is a lily among thorns, set apart from the other
daughters (2). She outdoes it back: he is an apple tree among the trees of the wood. She sits
in his shadow and finds his fruit sweet (3).

The last movement is hers. He has brought her to the banquet hall, and *"his banner over me
is love"* (4). She asks to be strengthened with raisins and apples, because she is faint with
love (5). She describes his embrace (6). Then she turns to the daughters of Jerusalem and puts
them under oath, by the roes and the hinds of the field, not to stir up or awaken love *"until
it so desires"* (7).

Two things carry the unit. The first is mutuality. Each compliment is answered, often in the
same words, and neither lover is only the object of the other's praise. The second is the
turn at the end. After the fullest picture of fulfilled closeness so far, the woman does not
urge anyone toward love. She tells the daughters to leave it alone until it wakes by itself.
Delight and restraint stand side by side.

The setting does not stay still. A king at his table (1:12), a green couch and a house
of cedar beams (1:16–17), a wood of trees (2:3), and a banquet hall (2:4) follow one another
without a narrated move. The house may be a real house, an outdoor bower pictured as a
house, or both; the poem does not say. As in [[Dark and Lovely]], royal and rural images
stand together without being joined into one plot.

## Key verses

> **9** I have compared you, my love, to a steed in Pharaoh’s chariots.

> **12** While the king sat at his table, my perfume spread its fragrance.

> **13** My beloved is to me a sachet of myrrh, that lies between my breasts.

> **14** My beloved is to me a cluster of henna blossoms from the vineyards of En Gedi.

> **15** Behold, you are beautiful, my love. Behold, you are beautiful. Your eyes are doves.

> **16** Behold, you are beautiful, my beloved, yes, pleasant; and our couch is verdant.

> **2:1** I am a rose of Sharon, a lily of the valleys.

> **2:2** As a lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters.

> **2:3** As the apple tree among the trees of the wood, so is my beloved among the sons. I sat down under his shadow with great delight, his fruit was sweet to my taste.

> **2:4** He brought me to the banquet hall. His banner over me is love.

> **2:5** Strengthen me with raisins, refresh me with apples; For I am faint with love.

> **2:6** His left hand is under my head. His right hand embraces me.

> **2:7** I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, or by the hinds of the field, that you not stir up, nor awaken love, until it so desires.

## People

- [[The Man (Song of Solomon)]] — the voice that says *"my love"* (1:9, 15; 2:2), and the
  *"beloved"* the woman describes (1:13–14, 16; 2:3–6). This is the first unit in which a male
  voice plainly praises her. The WEB gives no speaker cues here; see Notes on how the
  speakers are assigned.
- [[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]] — praised, she answers with praise of her own; she
  describes the embrace and gives the adjuration (2:7).
- [[The Daughters of Jerusalem]] — put under oath in the first refrain (2:7).
- *The king* (1:12) — named only by title. Whether he is the beloved, and whether either is
  [[Solomon]], is not stated. Solomon is not named in this unit.
- *Pharaoh* (1:9) — named only in the comparison *"Pharaoh's chariots"*. No particular
  Pharaoh is meant, and none of the wiki's Pharaoh pages applies.
- *We* (1:11) — an unnamed plural voice promising jewelry. It may be the man speaking for
  himself and others, or the Friends of 1:4. The text does not say.
- *The daughters* (2:2) and *the sons* (2:3) — other young women and men, a foil for the
  lovers, not a group with a role.

## Places

- [[En Gedi]] — its vineyards supply the henna blossoms of 1:14. A comparison, not a setting.
- [[Sharon]] — *"a rose of Sharon"* (2:1). Again a comparison, not a setting.
- [[Jerusalem]] — named in the address to its daughters (2:7). Nothing happens in the city.
- [[Egypt]] is evoked by Pharaoh's chariots (1:9) but not named; its page is not changed.
- The table, couch, house, wood, and banquet hall are unnamed and unlocated.

## Themes

- [[Love]] — mutual praise, the embrace, faintness with love, and the charge not to awaken
  love before its time (1:15–2:7).
- [[Joy and Enjoyment]] — fragrance, fruit, raisins, and the banquet hall, with *"great
  delight"* in his shadow (1:12–14; 2:3–5).
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — *"Behold, you are beautiful"*, said by each lover to the other
  (1:15–16), and the eyes that are doves.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — *"I adjure you"* (2:7), an oath laid on others, sworn by wild animals.
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — the king at his table (1:12) and Pharaoh's chariots (1:9) as
  images inside a love poem.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The unit runs from the first compliment, *"I have compared you, my love"*
(1:9), to the first refrain (2:7). 2:8 begins a new scene with *"The voice of my beloved!"*
The planned boundary held, and no box was redrawn. The unit crosses a chapter break, and
the break does not mark a change: 1:17 and 2:1 belong to the same exchange.

**Who speaks.** Unlike 1:4, the stored WEB gives no speaker cues in 1:9–2:7. The assignment
above follows the forms of address. *"My love"* is said to the woman (1:9, 15; 2:2) and
*"my beloved"* of the man (1:13–14, 16; 2:3). The Hebrew supports the alternation in
1:15–16: *hinnāk yāpâ* (feminine) answers *hinnəkā yāpeh* (masculine), read from Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*. Who says 1:11 and 1:17 is not fixed by either form.

**A steed in Pharaoh's chariots.** The Hebrew word in 1:9 is *ləsusātî*, which has the
feminine form of *sûs*, horse (Sefaria, *Miqra according to the Masorah*). Many translations
therefore give *mare*; the WEB gives *steed*. Why a mare among Pharaoh's chariots is a
compliment is debated. Some readers take the point to be splendor: a royal horse in fine
harness, which fits the jewelry of 1:10–11. Others propose that a mare among chariot horses
would cause a stir, so the image is about her effect on men. That second reading depends on
claims about ancient chariotry that the text does not make. The wiki records both and
adopts neither.

**The banquet hall.** The WEB's *banquet hall* (2:4) renders *bêt hayyāyin*, literally
*house of wine* (Sefaria, *Miqra according to the Masorah*). That links the line with the
wine comparisons of 1:2 and 1:4. What *"his banner over me is love"* pictures — a military
standard, a sign over a hall, or something else — is not settled, and the page makes no
lexical claim about *banner*.

**Rose and lily.** The WEB's *rose of Sharon* names a flower that is not securely identified.
The page follows the WEB and makes no botanical claim. The woman's self-description may be
modest (a common wildflower) or proud (the flower of a famed region). The man's reply takes it
as praise either way: she stands out from the other daughters like a lily among thorns.

**The adjuration.** *"I adjure you"* renders *hišbaʿtî* (Sefaria), the oath verb, so the woman
places the daughters under oath. It is sworn *"by the roes, or by the hinds of the field"*,
not by God. The Hebrew for those animals, *biṣbāʾôt* and *bəʾaylôt haśśādeh*, has often been
heard as a play on divine titles (*ṣəbāʾôt*, *hosts*; *ʾēl* and *šadday*). That is an
interpretation of the sound, not something the text states, and this page does not adopt it.
The same noun for *hind* appears in Proverbs 5:19 as the *"loving doe"* (*ʾayyelet ʾăhābîm*,
Sefaria), where it describes the wife.

**What the refrain asks.** The verse forbids stirring up or awakening love *"until it so
desires"*. Two readings are common. One is that love must not be forced or hurried before its
proper time. The other is that the lovers, now embraced, must not be disturbed. The first fits
the refrain's return at 3:5 and 8:4, after different scenes. The second fits the embrace just
described in 2:6. The text supports both, and the wiki notes the question without settling
it. The refrain recurs at 3:5 (identical in the WEB) and 8:4 (without the roes and hinds),
and 8:3 repeats 2:6 as a wish. 5:8 uses *"I adjure you"* with *"faint with love"*. All are
verified in the stored WEB and not yet ingested.

**No wedding stated.** The couch, the house, and the embrace show closeness. They do not
state a marriage, and the page does not add one. The word *bride* first appears at 4:8
(stored WEB, not yet ingested).

**Pages decided.** [[En Gedi]] and [[Sharon]] get place pages, following the
[[Kedar]] precedent for named places that appear only in comparisons; both recur elsewhere
in Scripture. No Pharaoh page is created, because 1:9 names a title in an image and no person
acts. The plural *we* of 1:11 and the daughters and sons of 2:2–3 get no pages.
