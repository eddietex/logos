# Come into the Fields, My Beloved

**Book:** [[Song of Solomon]]
**Reference:** Song of Solomon 7:10–8:4

## Summary

The woman declares that she belongs to her beloved and that his desire is toward her.
She invites him to the fields and villages, then to inspect the flowering vineyards
early, where she promises her love. Fragrant mandrakes and fruits stored for him
extend the invitation. She wishes he were like a brother so that she could kiss him
outside without contempt, lead him into her mother's house, and offer him spiced wine.
The imagined embrace leads into the recurring charge to the daughters of Jerusalem
not to stir up love before it desires.

## Key verses

Complete pericope, copied from the stored WEB:

> **7:10** I am my beloved’s. His desire is toward me.

> **7:11** Come, my beloved, let us go out into the field. Let us lodge in the villages.

> **7:12** Let’s go early up to the vineyards. Let’s see whether the vine has budded, its blossom is open, and the pomegranates are in flower. There I will give you my love.

> **7:13** The mandrakes produce fragrance. At our doors are all kinds of precious fruits, new and old, which I have stored up for you, my beloved.

> **8:1** Oh that you were like my brother, who nursed from the breasts of my mother! If I found you outside, I would kiss you; yes, and no one would despise me.

> **8:2** I would lead you, bringing you into my mother’s house, who would instruct me. I would have you drink spiced wine, of the juice of my pomegranate.

> **8:3** His left hand would be under my head. His right hand would embrace me.

> **8:4** I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you not stir up, nor awaken love, until it so desires.

## Notes

**Invitation, not an itinerary.** The movement from fields to the mother's house
belongs to invitation and wish. The page does not supply an intervening journey,
a marriage ceremony, or a historical identity for either lover.

**A brother-like beloved.** In 8:1 she wishes he were *like* her brother and explains
why: she could kiss him outside without contempt. This does not identify him as an
actual sibling or make a general rule about every ancient society. It illuminates
the affectionate sister language of [[A Garden Locked and Opened]] without turning
that language into a family tree.

**The mother's instruction.** The WEB reads the mother as the one who would instruct
her (8:2). This page follows that English rendering and makes no claim to settle the
source-language syntax. The mother remains part of the woman's literary-role page,
as in the earlier mother's-house scene, rather than receiving a new unnamed-person page.

**Boundaries and sources.** The planned 7:10–8:4 unit is retained: the belonging
declaration starts the invitation, and the adjuration closes it. All quotations here
come from the immutable stored WEB. Internal comparisons were checked in the stored
Song; the mandrake comparison was checked in stored Genesis 30:14–24. No new Hebrew
claim, outside quotation, or allegorical identification is introduced.
