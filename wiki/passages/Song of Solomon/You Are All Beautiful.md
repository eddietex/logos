# You Are All Beautiful

**Book:** [[Song of Solomon]]
**Reference:** Song of Solomon 4:1–7

## Summary

The man speaks to the woman, and only he speaks. He begins with the words of 1:15, *"Behold, you are
beautiful, my love. Behold, you are beautiful"*, and ends with a summary, *"You are all beautiful, my
love. There is no spot in you"* (4:1, 7). Between them he describes her, part by part, from the eyes
down to the breasts.

Each part gets a picture. Her eyes are doves behind her veil. Her hair is a flock of goats coming down
from Mount Gilead. Her teeth are a flock of newly shorn sheep come up from the washing, every one with a
twin and none bereaved. Her lips are a scarlet thread, her mouth is lovely, and her temples are a slice
of pomegranate behind the veil (4:1–3). Her neck is David's tower, built for an armory, with a thousand
shields hung on it, *"all the shields of the mighty men"* (4:4). Her breasts are two fawns, twins of a
roe, feeding among the lilies (4:5).

The pictures do not ask to be seen literally. A neck is not shaped like a tower hung with shields, and
hair does not look like a hillside of goats. Each comparison carries something else: colour, movement,
pairing, height, strength, or the worth of the thing compared. Readers often note that many of the
pictures are drawn from the land, its flocks, and its landmarks, so that praising her body also names
the country.

Then he breaks off the list and says what he will do. *"Until the day is cool, and the shadows flee
away, I will go to the mountain of myrrh, to the hill of frankincense"* (4:6). The first half of the
verse is the woman's line from 2:17, word for word in the Hebrew. There she sent him away to the
mountains of Bether until evening; here he says where he will go, and the mountain is one of spices.
Many readers take the mountain of myrrh as a picture of the woman herself, since she called him a
sachet of myrrh between her breasts at 1:13. The verse does not say so.

The unit closes on the summary of 4:7. The word for *spot* is *mûm*, a *defect* or *blemish*, the word
used in Leviticus for a priest or an animal unfit to be offered. Here it is praise, not a ritual
judgment: nothing about her is wanting. See Notes.

This is the Song's first long description of a body. The man praised her briefly at 1:9–11 and 1:15,
and the woman praised him at 1:13–14 and 2:3. The full form returns three times: the woman describes
him at 5:10–16, the man repeats part of this description at 6:4–7, and someone describes the woman
from the feet up at 7:1–5 (all stored WEB, not yet ingested). See
[[Your Hair Is Like a Flock of Goats - Song of Solomon 4.1-7, 6.4-7, and 7.1-5]].

The unit follows [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]]. The man does not call her *bride* here; that word
first appears at 4:8, in the next unit. Whether the praise belongs to the wedding of 3:11 is left as
open as the wedding itself.

## Key verses

> **1** Behold, you are beautiful, my love. Behold, you are beautiful. Your eyes are doves behind your veil. Your hair is as a flock of goats, that descend from Mount Gilead.

> **2** Your teeth are like a newly shorn flock, which have come up from the washing, where every one of them has twins. None is bereaved among them.

> **3** Your lips are like scarlet thread. Your mouth is lovely. Your temples are like a piece of a pomegranate behind your veil.

> **4** Your neck is like David’s tower built for an armory, whereon a thousand shields hang, all the shields of the mighty men.

> **5** Your two breasts are like two fawns that are twins of a roe, which feed among the lilies.

> **6** Until the day is cool, and the shadows flee away, I will go to the mountain of myrrh, to the hill of frankincense.

> **7** You are all beautiful, my love. There is no spot in you.

## People

- [[The Man (Song of Solomon)]] — the only speaker. Masculine speech to a feminine *you*: every
  suffix on her body parts is feminine (Sefaria). He is not named and not called king.
- [[The Woman (Song of Solomon)]] — addressed and described; she does not speak.
- [[David]] — named only in *"David's tower"* (4:4), a landmark used as a comparison. No event of
  David's life is in view.
- *The mighty men* (4:4) — the owners of the shields; the same word, *gibbōrîm*, as Solomon's escort
  in 3:7. No page.

## Places

- [[Gilead]] — *"Mount Gilead"* (4:1), the hill country east of the Jordan, here a slope of goats.
  Its first appearance in the Song.
- *David's tower* (4:4) — not identified elsewhere in the wiki's sources by this name. It may be a
  real tower in Jerusalem or a poetic landmark. No place page.
- *The mountain of myrrh* and *the hill of frankincense* (4:6) — not geographical places. No page.

## Themes

- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — a description that moves over her body part by part, framed by

- [[Love]] — *"my love"*, *raʿyāṯî*, twice (4:1, 7); and *"You are all beautiful"* (4:7).

## Notes

**Boundary.** The unit runs from *"Behold, you are beautiful"* (4:1) to *"You are all beautiful"*
(4:7). The two phrases frame it. Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* marks a section break
(*{ס}*) after 4:7, and the previous unit ended with one after 3:11. At 4:8 a new address begins,
*"Come with me from Lebanon, my bride"*. The planned boundary held, and no box was redrawn.

**The frame.** 4:1 opens *hinnāḵ yāpâ raʿyāṯî hinnāḵ yāpâ ʿênayiḵ yônîm*, the same six words as 1:15
(Sefaria for both). 4:1 adds *mibbaʿaḏ lǝṣammāṯēḵ*, *behind your veil*. 4:7 closes with *kullāḵ
yāpâ raʿyāṯî*, *all of you is beautiful, my love*. *Raʿyâ*, *my love* or *my companion*, is the man's
usual word for her in the Song. *Yāpâ*, *beautiful*, opens and closes the unit.

**The veil.** *Ṣammâ* (4:1, 3; Sefaria) is usually translated *veil*, as in the WEB. At Isaiah 47:2,
virgin Babylon is told *gallî ṣammāṯēḵ*, *"Remove your veil"* (Sefaria; WEB checked at bible-api.com).
Some translators render the Song's word *locks* or *hair* instead. The word is rare. This page has
not counted its uses with a concordance and does not decide the meaning. In the WEB the eyes and the
temples are both seen *behind* it, so whatever it is, it partly hides her face.

**The flocks.** The hair is *kǝʿēḏer hāʿizzîm šeggālǝšû mēhar gilʿāḏ*, *like a flock of goats that
stream down from Mount Gilead* (4:1; Sefaria). The verb *gālaš* is commonly said to appear only here
and at 6:5; this page has not verified that by a concordance. Black goats moving down a slope suggest
dark hair falling in waves. The teeth are *kǝʿēḏer haqqǝṣûḇôṯ*, a flock of *shorn* ewes (4:2), white
from the washing. *Šekkullām maṯʾîmôṯ wǝšakkulâ ʾên bāhem* (4:2) is literally *all of them bearing
twins, and a bereaved one is not among them*. The picture is a full set of matching teeth, with none
missing. *Šakkulâ*, *bereaved*, is the word for an animal or a mother that has lost its young.

**Scarlet thread.** *Kǝḥûṭ haššānî śip̄ṯôṯayiḵ* (4:3; Sefaria). The same phrase, *ḥûṭ haššānî*, is
Rahab's cord at Joshua 2:18, *"this line of scarlet thread"* (Sefaria; WEB checked at bible-api.com).
At Genesis 38:28 the midwife ties *šānî*, *scarlet*, on Zerah's hand (stored WEB; Sefaria). The words
are the same, but nothing in the Song points to either story. The wiki records the shared phrase and
does not read a link into the verse. *Midbārēḵ*, *your mouth* (4:3), appears to be formed from the
root *d-b-r*, *to speak*, so it may mean *your speech* as well. This is a common observation; this page
has not verified it in a lexicon. It is a different word from *midbār*, *wilderness*, in 3:6, though
it is spelled with the same consonants.

**David's tower.** *Kǝmiḡdal dāwîḏ ṣawwāʾrēḵ bānûy lǝṯalpiyyôṯ* (4:4; Sefaria). *Talpiyyôṯ* is the WEB's
*"for an armory"*. The word is commonly said to occur only here, and its meaning is uncertain:
proposals include *for weapons*, *in courses* (of stone), and *for a lookout*. This page has not
verified either the uniqueness or any lexicon entry, and it follows the WEB. A thousand
*māḡēn*-shields hang on the tower, *kōl šilṭê haggibbōrîm*, *all the shields of the mighty men*. The
second word for shield, *šeleṭ*, is used at 2 Samuel 8:7, where *"David took the shields of gold"*
from Hadadezer's servants and brought them to Jerusalem (Hebrew *šilṭê hazzāhāḇ*, Sefaria; WEB checked
at bible-api.com). At Ezekiel 27:11 the men of Arvad *"hung their shields on your walls all
around"*, and Ezekiel adds *"They have perfected your beauty"* (Hebrew *šilṭêhem tillû … kālǝlû
yop̄yēḵ*, Sefaria; WEB checked at bible-api.com). So shields hung on a wall belong to the picture of a
beautiful city. The Song does not name Jerusalem here. This page knows of no other verse that calls a tower
David's, but it has not checked with a concordance; whether the poet meant a real tower is not known.

**The fawns.** *Šǝnê ʿŏp̄ārîm tǝʾômê ṣǝḇiyyâ hārôʿîm baššôšannîm* (4:5; Sefaria). *ʿŌp̄er*, *fawn*, is
the young deer of 2:9, 17, and *ṣǝḇiyyâ* is the feminine of *ṣǝḇî*, the roe of 2:7, 9, 17. *Hārôʿîm
baššôšannîm*, *grazing among the lilies*, is the phrase the woman used of the man at 2:16, *hārōʿeh
baššôšannîm*, in the plural. At 7:3 (7:4 in Hebrew) the first half of 4:5 returns without the lilies
(stored WEB; Sefaria).

**4:6 and 2:17.** *ʿAḏ šeyyāp̄ûaḥ hayyôm wǝnāsû haṣṣǝlālîm* is identical in both verses (Sefaria). At
2:17 it is followed by *sōḇ*, *turn*, addressed to him; here by *ʾēleḵ lî*, *I will go*. The WEB
renders *yāp̄ûaḥ* as *is cool*; it means *breathes* or *blows*. Whether it means the evening breeze or
the morning breeze is disputed, so whether *until* means *until evening* or *until dawn* is open. The
mountain is *har hammôr*, the hill *giḇʿaṯ hallǝḇônâ*. Myrrh and frankincense are the pair that
perfumed Solomon's carriage at 3:6 (*mōr ûlǝḇônâ*, Sefaria). Some readers hear *Moriah* in *har
hammôr*. That is a wordplay readers propose, not something the text marks; the wiki does not adopt it.

**No blemish.** *Ûmûm ʾên bāḵ*, *and a blemish there is not in you* (4:7; Sefaria). *Mûm* is the
disqualifying defect in Leviticus 21:17–18 for a priest and Leviticus 22:20–21 for an offering (Sefaria;
WEB *"defect"*, checked at bible-api.com). It is said of Absalom's beauty at 2 Samuel 14:25, *"From the
sole of his foot even to the crown of his head there was no defect in him"*, and of the young men chosen
for the king's palace at Daniel 1:4 (both Sefaria and WEB at bible-api.com). In those verses it is a
beauty or fitness word as well as a ritual one, and the Song uses it that way. The wiki does not read a
sacrificial meaning into 4:7. See
[[No Blemish in You - Song of Solomon 4.7, Leviticus 22.21, and 2 Samuel 14.25]].

**The form.** Scholars often call a poem that describes a body part by part a *wasf*, from an Arabic
word for *description*. The term comes from modern study of Arabic wedding songs and is applied to the
Song by analogy. The wiki uses it as a label only and does not depend on the comparison.

**Pages decided.** No new person or place page. David's tower, the mountain of myrrh, and the hill of
frankincense are images, not places with a page. The mighty men are unnamed. Two new connection pages:
one for the Song's repeated descriptions, one for *mûm*.
