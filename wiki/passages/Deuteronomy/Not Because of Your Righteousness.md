# Not Because of Your Righteousness

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 9:1–6

## Summary

Moses calls Israel to hear: ahead lie the Jordan, stronger nations, sky-high fortifications,
and the tall Anakim whose reputation seems to rule out resistance (1–2). Yahweh himself goes
before Israel as a devouring fire; his action enables their commanded action against the nations (3).

The reassurance turns immediately into a warning against moral self-congratulation. Israel must
not explain possession by its righteousness or upright heart. The nations’ wickedness and
Yahweh’s sworn word to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob explain the gift (4–5). The final repetition
makes the rebuke personal: the recipients are a stiff-necked people (6).

## Key verses

Complete unit, copied from stored WEB:

> **9:1** Hear, Israel! You are to pass over the Jordan today, to go in to dispossess nations greater and mightier than yourself, cities great and fortified up to the sky,

> **9:2** a people great and tall, the sons of the Anakim, whom you know, and of whom you have heard say, “Who can stand before the sons of Anak?”

> **9:3** Know therefore today, that Yahweh your God is he who goes over before you as a devouring fire. He will destroy them, and he will bring them down before you. So you shall drive them out, and make them perish quickly, as Yahweh has spoken to you.

> **9:4** Don’t say in your heart, after Yahweh your God has thrust them out from before you, saying, “For my righteousness Yahweh has brought me in to possess this land”; because Yahweh drives them out before you because of the wickedness of these nations.

> **9:5** Not for your righteousness, or for the uprightness of your heart, do you go in to possess their land; but for the wickedness of these nations Yahweh your God does drive them out from before you, and that he may establish the word which Yahweh swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.

> **9:6** Know therefore, that Yahweh your God doesn’t give you this good land to possess for your righteousness; for you are a stiff-necked people.

## Notes

**Fear and pride are both addressed.** The same people who fear the enemy may later claim moral
credit for victory. Moses answers both through Yahweh’s action, without denying the enemies’
strength or excusing Israel’s stubbornness. The earlier wealth warning (8:17–18) prepares this
warning about righteousness: possession must not become a verdict of self-approval.

**Two stated grounds.** Verse 5 gives both judgment on the nations and fidelity to the ancestral
oath. Genesis 15:16, 18 already places the Amorites’ iniquity beside the land promise. The text
here describes destructive conquest, not merely inward spiritual victory; its stated explanation
does not authorize an unrestricted claim to other peoples’ territory.

**Tensions retained.** Deuteronomy 6:25 calls obedience righteousness; 9:4–6 denies righteousness
as the reason for this gift. Conduct remains significant, while the gift is not proof of merit.
Deuteronomy 7:22 describes gradual removal; 9:3 says the nations perish quickly. Swift victories
within a gradual occupation are a possible distinction, not an explicit harmonization in the text.
See the connections above for both comparisons.

**Scope and sources.** Israel is the collective addressee, not a new individual person. The
Anakim have an existing collective page; no separate biography of Anak is inferred from this
unit. The cities are unnamed. Canaan identifies the land from context. The planned 9:1–6
boundary is retained, ending before the detailed remembrance of rebellion begins at 9:7.
Quotations are copied from stored WEB Deuteronomy 9; comparisons use stored Deuteronomy
4:24, 6:25, 7:7–8, 7:22, 8:17–18 and Genesis 15:16–18. No new original-language claim or
quotation from an unstored book is introduced.
