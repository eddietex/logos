# The Word Is Near and the Choice Is Life

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 30:11–20

## Summary

Moses turns from what Yahweh will do after exile (30:1–10) back to the present and the command
itself. *This commandment which I command you today is not too hard for you or too distant* (11). It
is not in heaven, so that someone must go up and bring it down, and not beyond the sea, so that
someone must cross over and fetch it, before Israel can hear and do it (12–13). *But the word is very
near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may do it* (14).

Then comes the closing appeal of the covenant in Moab. *Behold, I have set before you today life and
prosperity, and death and evil* (15). Life follows loving Yahweh, walking in his ways, and keeping his
commandments, statutes, and ordinances: Israel will *live and multiply*, blessed in the land (16).
Death follows a heart that turns away, will not hear, is *drawn away*, and worships other gods (17):
*you will surely perish* and will not prolong its days in the land across the Jordan (18). Moses calls
*heaven and earth to witness* that he has set before Israel *life and death, the blessing and the
curse*, and gives the command the whole book has been building toward: *Therefore choose life, that
you may live, you and your descendants* (19). Choosing life means *to love Yahweh your God, to obey his
voice, and to cling to him; for he is your life, and the length of your days*, in the land sworn to
Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (20).

The unit closes the address that began at 29:1 and, with it, the long speech of commands. What
follows in chapter 31 is succession, writing, and song. The pairing of a blessing and a curse first
set before Israel at 11:26–28 returns here widened to *life and death*; see
[[A Blessing and a Curse Set Before You - Deuteronomy 11.26-28 and 30.15-20]]. Paul reads verses 12–14
in Romans 10:6–8; see [[The Word Is Near You - Deuteronomy 30.11-14 in Romans 10.6-8]].

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, Deuteronomy 30:11–20. Full unit copied verbatim.

> **30:11** For this commandment which I command you today is not too hard for you or too distant.

> **30:12** It is not in heaven, that you should say, “Who will go up for us to heaven, and bring it to us, and proclaim it to us, that we may do it?”

> **30:13** Neither is it beyond the sea, that you should say, “Who will go over the sea for us, and bring it to us, and proclaim it to us, that we may do it?”

> **30:14** But the word is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may do it.

> **30:15** Behold, I have set before you today life and prosperity, and death and evil.

> **30:16** For I command you today to love Yahweh your God, to walk in his ways, and to keep his commandments, his statutes, and his ordinances, that you may live and multiply, and that Yahweh your God may bless you in the land where you go in to possess it.

> **30:17** But if your heart turns away, and you will not hear, but are drawn away, and worship other gods, and serve them;

> **30:18** I denounce to you today, that you will surely perish. You will not prolong your days in the land where you pass over the Jordan to go in to possess it.

> **30:19** I call heaven and earth to witness against you today, that I have set before you life and death, the blessing and the curse. Therefore choose life, that you may live, you and your descendants;

> **30:20** to love Yahweh your God, to obey his voice, and to cling to him; for he is your life, and the length of your days; that you may dwell in the land which Yahweh swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, to give them.

## People

- [[God]] — The one to be loved, obeyed, and clung to (16, 20); he blesses in the land (16) and swore it to the fathers (20). The unit's strongest statement about him is *he is your life, and the length of your days* (20).
- [[Moses]] — The speaker. He commands *today* (11, 16), sets life and death before Israel (15, 19), *denounce[s]* the outcome of apostasy (18), and calls heaven and earth as witnesses (19).
- [[Abraham]], [[Isaac]], [[Jacob]] — Named as the fathers to whom Yahweh swore the land (20). Ancestral remembrance, not presence.

## Places

- [[The Jordan]] — The river Israel will *pass over* to possess the land (18). The crossing is still ahead.
- [[Canaan]] — Not named; *the land where you go in to possess it* (16, 18) and *the land which Yahweh swore to your fathers* (20) is the promised land in context.

## Themes

- [[Life and Death]] — The unit's subject. Life and death are set before Israel (15, 19), life is commanded (*choose life*, 19), and Yahweh himself is called Israel's life (20).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The command is *not too hard* and *very near … that you may do it* (11, 14). Obedience is defined as love, walking, and keeping (16), and as obeying his voice (20).
- [[Love]] — *To love Yahweh your God* heads both the description of life (16) and the meaning of *choose life* (20), picking up 6:5 and the promise of a heart able to love in 30:6.
- [[Covenant]] — The closing appeal of the covenant in Moab (29:1), with heaven and earth as witnesses (19) and the oath to the fathers as its ground (20).
- [[Blessing]] — *Yahweh your God may bless you in the land* (16); *the blessing* is one of the two set before Israel (19).
- [[Curse]] — *The curse* is the other (19); its content here is perishing and short days in the land (18).
- [[Idolatry]] — The path to death is a heart that turns away and is *drawn away*, to *worship other gods, and serve them* (17).
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Life is life *in the land* (16, 20); death is not prolonging days there (18). The land is still across the Jordan.
- [[Walking with God]] — *To walk in his ways* (16) and *to cling to him* (20).
- [[Call and Election]] — Yahweh chooses throughout Deuteronomy; here Israel is told to choose (*choose life*, 19). See Notes.
- [[Language and Speech]] — The word is *in your mouth and in your heart* (14); speech and inner disposition together.
- [[Faith]] — Not used in the passage. Added because Paul reads verses 12–14 as *the righteousness which is of faith* (Romans 10:6–8); see the connection page.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 30:11–20 boundary and title are kept. Verse 11 opens with *For* and turns from
the promise of 30:1–10 to *this commandment which I command you today*. Verse 20 ends the speech;
31:1 begins a narrative frame, *Moses went and spoke these words to all Israel*.

**Not too hard, not too far.** Verse 11's two denials are *niplēʾt*, *too hard* (*pālāʾ*, Strong's
6381), and *rəḥōqâ*, *distant* (7350). Verse 14 answers the second with *qārôb*, *near* (7138). The
first word is the one Deuteronomy 17:8 uses of the legal case *too hard* for local judges, and that
Genesis 18:14 uses in *Is anything too hard for Yahweh?*; see
[[The Case Too Hard - Exodus 18, Deuteronomy 1.17, and Deuteronomy 17.8]]. Lemmas are from the Open
Scriptures WLC (morphhb) Strong's tags for Deuteronomy 30:11–20.

**Heaven and sea.** Verses 12–13 name the two farthest reaches a person might imagine having to travel
to, upward and across. The verbs are *ʿālâ*, *go up* (5927), and *ʿābar*, *go over* (5674), by the
same WLC tags. The second verb returns in verse 18, *where you pass over the Jordan*. No one has to
cross the sea to get the word; Israel will cross the Jordan to enter the land. That contrast is an
observation about this edition's tagging, not an argument the text makes. Job 11:8–9 and the passages
gathered in [[Higher Than Heaven, Deeper Than Sheol - The Measure of the Unsearchable]] use heaven,
the deep, and the sea for what is beyond human reach; verses 12–13 say the command is not like that.

**In your mouth and in your heart.** Verse 14 places the word *in your mouth and in your heart*,
*bəpîkā ûbilbābkā*. The nearest earlier echo is 6:6–7, *These words … shall be on your heart* and
*you shall talk of them*. This passage does not say how the word got there: by being taught and
recited (6:7), or by the heart-circumcision promised in 30:6. Read with 30:6, the nearness of the word
can be heard as part of that promise; read with 6:6–7, as the result of teaching. The wiki records
both readings without choosing.

**Drawn away and driven.** Verse 17's *drawn away* is *nidaḥtā*, from *nāḏaḥ* (5080), by the WLC tags.
The same verb is *driven* in 30:1 and *outcasts* in 30:4, and the same form appears in 4:19, *you are
drawn away and worship them*. The English hides it: in this chapter Israel is first *drawn away* to
other gods and then *driven* among the nations. The connection is lexical; this passage does not
spell it out.

**Life and prosperity, death and evil.** The Hebrew of verse 15 is *haḥayyîm wəʾet-haṭṭôb* and
*hammāwet wəʾet-hārāʿ*, literally *the life and the good* and *the death and the evil* (WLC). The WEB
renders *ṭôb* as *prosperity*. *Good and evil* (*ṭôb wārāʿ*) is the same pair as Genesis 2:9's tree; no
allusion is claimed here, and the WEB's choice is a fair reading of a pair whose sense is outcome
rather than moral quality.

**Choose.** *Ûbāḥartā*, *choose* (977), is the verb Deuteronomy uses constantly of Yahweh choosing
Israel and *the place Yahweh will choose*. Israel as the chooser is rare in the book; the other case
the wiki has recorded is the runaway slave of 23:16. See
[[Where It Pleases Him Best - The Runaway Slave and the Place Yahweh Chooses]].

**Who acts.** The previous unit promised that Yahweh will circumcise the heart *that you may live*
(30:6). This one commands Israel to *choose life, that you may live* (19). The same purpose clause
closes both. The wiki recorded the question of order — human turning or divine renewal first — at
[[Return Restoration and the Circumcised Heart]]; the two units side by side make it sharper and do not
settle it.

**Witnesses.** *I call heaven and earth to witness* (*haʿîdōtî*, 5749) repeats 4:26 word for word in the
WEB, and 31:28 invokes the same witnesses (see [[The Book Beside the Ark]]). At 4:26 the witnesses attend a
warning of perishing and short days in the land across the Jordan; verse 18 repeats that warning
almost exactly. Here, for the first time, the witnessed statement is also an offer.

**Cling.** *Ûlədābqâ bô*, *to cling to him* (*dābaq*, 1692), is the same phrase as 11:22 by the WLC
tags, and the verb also appears at 10:20 and 13:4 (WLC 13:5). Genesis 2:24 uses it of a man joining his
wife; that echo is noted, not argued.

**He is your life.** *Hûʾ ḥayyêkā*, *he is your life* (20). Deuteronomy 32:47 will say of the words of
the law, *it is your life, and through this thing you shall prolong your days in the land* (stored WEB,
not yet ingested). Here it is Yahweh who is Israel's life; there it is his word. The two are left side
by side for the ingest of 32:47.

**Romans 10.** Paul quotes verses 12–14 in Romans 10:6–8 and applies them to *the word of faith,
which we preach*. He keeps the ascent into heaven, replaces going over the sea with *descend into the
abyss*, and reads both of Christ. The WEB of Romans 10:6–8 was verified at bible-api.com, and the Greek
verbs (*anabainō* and *katabainō*, *abyssos*) were checked against the SBLGNT (MorphGNT). Whether
Paul's wording draws on a Greek translation of Deuteronomy or on another tradition is not checked here.
See [[The Word Is Near You - Deuteronomy 30.11-14 in Romans 10.6-8]].
