# What Yahweh Requires of Israel

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 10:12–22

## Summary

Moses asks what Yahweh requires: fear, love, wholehearted service, and obedience for Israel’s
good (12–13). The owner of heaven and earth has loved and chosen the ancestors’ offspring,
who must now abandon stubbornness (14–16). Divine supremacy means impartial justice and care
for vulnerable people; Israel must love foreigners as God does, remembering its own foreignness
in Egypt (17–19). Loyalty and praise close the unit, with the growth from seventy ancestors
to a multitude like the stars as evidence of God’s work (20–22).

## Notes

### Restoration calls for a response

The question follows [[New Tablets and the Journey Onward]], where Israel survives through heard
intercession. The requirements do not purchase that survival. Nor does the phrase “for your good”
(13) reduce the command to private benefit: the following verses require care for people outside
the chosen ancestry. Israel is the collective addressee, not the patriarch Jacob acting here.
The fathers and the seventy are remembered collectively; no individual ancestor is named.

### Election and impartiality

Choice of the fathers’ offspring (15) and refusal of partiality (17) are both explicit. They
concern different relationships: a particular people is chosen, but justice cannot be bought or
bent in its favor. Love of foreigners is commanded immediately afterward. This complicates any
reading of election as permission to despise all outsiders; it does not erase the distinct
commands about the nations in [[A People Chosen in Love]]. The tension between those commands
and this ethic should remain visible rather than be flattened into one universal rule.

### A heart that responds

Circumcising the heart is paired with ceasing to be stiff-necked (16), answering the diagnosis
in [[Not Because of Your Righteousness]]. The passage does not reject the bodily sign of
Genesis 17. Its contrast with the promise of divine heart-circumcision in Deuteronomy 30:6
is discussed in [[Circumcision of the Heart]]; that later text is now ingested in
[[Return Restoration and the Circumcised Heart]]. Neither verse explicitly supplies a theory resolving
human responsibility and divine action.

### Remembering Egypt in two ways

Former foreignness becomes an obligation to care (19); the small ancestral household’s growth
becomes a reason to praise (22). The seventy agrees with the stored WEB Genesis 46:27 and
Exodus 1:5. It is a retrospective total, not a detailed passenger list or a new census.
Egypt is a remembered location, not the setting of this speech. Heaven and earth express the
scope of divine ownership rather than new geographical sites requiring place pages.
