# The People's Request for a Mediator

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 5:23–33

## Summary

The tribal heads and elders approach Moses after hearing God from the burning mountain.
They acknowledge that God has spoken to humans who survived, yet fear that further hearing
will kill them. They ask Moses to approach, hear, and transmit the words, promising to obey.
Yahweh approves their words and expresses the desire that their hearts remain fearful of him
and obedient always. The people return to their tents; Moses is to remain beside God to receive
instruction for life in the land. The closing exhortation binds hearing to an unwavering walk.

## Key verses

> **5:24** and you said, “Behold, Yahweh our God has shown us his glory and his greatness, and we have heard his voice out of the middle of the fire. We have seen today that God does speak with man, and he lives.

> **5:27** Go near, and hear all that Yahweh our God shall say, and tell us all that Yahweh our God tells you; and we will hear it, and do it.”

> **5:28** Yahweh heard the voice of your words, when you spoke to me; and Yahweh said to me, “I have heard the voice of the words of this people, which they have spoken to you. They have well said all that they have spoken.

> **5:29** Oh that there were such a heart in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always, that it might be well with them, and with their children forever!

> **5:31** But as for you, stand here by me, and I will tell you all the commandment, and the statutes, and the ordinances, which you shall teach them, that they may do them in the land which I give them to possess it.”

> **5:33** You shall walk in all the way which Yahweh your God has commanded you, that you may live, and that it may be well with you, and that you may prolong your days in the land which you shall possess.

## Notes

### Survival and fear of further hearing

Verses 24–26 hold together two claims: the people have heard and survived, and they fear
hearing more. The second is their prediction, not the narrator's report that the divine
voice has killed them. Their request is not a refusal to receive instruction: they promise
to hear and do what Moses relays (27). God explicitly approves what they say (28).

### Good words and an enduring heart

Approval is followed by a wish for lasting fear and obedience (29). Correct words at the
mountain do not by themselves establish enduring fidelity. The concern includes the children
and their future well-being. This is not a narrated new rebellion, nor does this unit explain
how the wished-for heart will be secured.

### A mediator who remains to hear

The contrast between the people's tents and Moses' place beside God (30–31) authorizes the
teaching that follows. Moses receives instruction to teach, not a private revelation exempting
the people from responsibility. The public speech's limit in 5:22 therefore does not end God's
instruction. Mediation here transmits commands; no sacrifice or petition for pardon occurs.

### Fear and obedience in the parallel

Exodus 20:18–21 tells of the people's distance and Moses' approach into darkness. Moses there
tells them not to fear while explaining that God's fear is to prevent sin. Deuteronomy supplies
the representatives' fuller speech and God's approving reply. These are differing emphases,
not an explicit contradiction. Neither account warrants labeling the request itself apostasy.

The life and prolonged days of verse 33 concern Israel's walk in the land. The verse does not
offer a case-by-case explanation of every individual's lifespan. The right-and-left image
requires fidelity to the commanded way, not merely compliance with whichever commands are easy.
