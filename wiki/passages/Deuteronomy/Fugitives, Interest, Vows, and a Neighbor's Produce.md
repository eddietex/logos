# Fugitives, Interest, Vows, and a Neighbor's Produce

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 23:15–25

## Summary

Five short laws, with no heading and no narrative between them. A slave who has escaped to Israel
is not handed back, and lives where he chooses (15–16). No Israelite woman or man is to be a
*qǝdēšâ* or *qādēš*, and a prostitute's hire may not pay a vow at Yahweh's house (17–18). No
interest is taken from a brother, though it may be from a foreigner (19–20). A vow is optional, but
once spoken it must be paid without delay (21–23). A passer-by may eat a neighbor's grapes and pluck
his grain by hand, but may not fill a container or put in a sickle (24–25).

In the Hebrew this is WLC 23:16–26, one verse ahead of the WEB throughout (see Notes).

**The runaway slave (23:15–16).** *"You shall not deliver to his master a servant who has escaped
from his master to you"* (15). The verb for the master's loss is a niphal of *nāṣal*, *to be
snatched away, escape*. A verse earlier the same verb was Yahweh's own purpose in the camp: he walks
there *"to deliver you"* (14), a hiphil of *nāṣal*. That is the infinitive of Exodus 3:8, *"I have
come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians"*. In the Torah the niphal occurs only
here and at Genesis 32:30, where Jacob says *"my life is preserved"* after Peniel (see Notes). The
WEB's *"deliver"* in verse 15 is another verb, *sāgar* in the hiphil, *to hand over, shut in*. Moses
uses it again in 32:30, *"Yahweh had delivered them up"*.

*"He shall dwell with you, among you, in the place which he shall choose within one of your gates,
where it pleases him best. You shall not oppress him"* (16). Two phrases here belong to the rest of
Deuteronomy. *"The place which he shall choose"*, *bammāqôm ʾăšer yibḥar*, is the formula of
chapters 12–18 for the sanctuary. *"Within one of your gates"*, *bǝʾaḥad šǝʿārêkā*, is built like
12:14's *"in one of your tribes"*. In Deuteronomy the form *yibḥar*, *he will choose*, has Yahweh as
its subject every time but this one. Here the fugitive chooses (see Notes and
[[Where It Pleases Him Best - The Runaway Slave and the Place Yahweh Chooses]]). *"Oppress"* is
*yānâ* in the hiphil. Exodus 22:21 and Leviticus 19:33 use it of the *gēr*, the resident alien:
*"You shall not wrong an alien"*. Leviticus 25:14, 17 uses it of neighbors in a sale. The runaway is
given the alien's protection.

The law does not say whose slave this is. *"Has escaped from his master to you"* and *"within one of
your gates"* read most naturally as a slave from outside arriving in Israel's towns, and most readers
take it that way. But the text never says the master is a foreigner, and it never says the law does
not apply to an Israelite's slave. The wiki adopts neither reading. Several existing pages read
23:15–16 as forbidding what Genesis 16:9 commands Hagar, *"Return to your mistress"*. That holds if
the law covers every runaway. It is weaker if the law is about fugitives crossing into Israel. See
[[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[The Two Flights of Hagar]].

**The *qǝdēšâ* and the *qādēš* (23:17–18).** *"There shall be no prostitute of the daughters of
Israel, neither shall there be a sodomite of the sons of Israel"* (17). WEB translates two words from
the root *qdš*, *holy*: the feminine *qǝdēšâ* and the masculine *qādēš*. They come immediately after
*"your camp shall be holy"*, *qādôš* (14). In the Torah *qǝdēšâ* occurs elsewhere only in Genesis
38:21–22. There Hirah looks for *"the prostitute"* Judah used, and is told *"There has been no
prostitute here"*; the narrator had called her a *zônâ* (38:15). *Qādēš* occurs in the Torah only
here (see Notes). Whether the words mean cultic prostitutes or another class of sanctuary personnel
is disputed, as [[Judah and Tamar]] records. The wiki leaves it unsettled. The WEB's *"sodomite"* is
an interpretation; the word has nothing to do with the name Sodom.

*"You shall not bring the hire of a prostitute, or the wages of a male prostitute, into the house of
Yahweh your God for any vow; for both of these are an abomination to Yahweh your God"* (18). *"The
hire of a prostitute"* is *ʾetnan zônâ*: the ordinary word for prostitute, not *qǝdēšâ*. *"The wages
of a male prostitute"* is *mǝḥîr keleb*, literally *the price of a dog*. That *"dog"* names the male
prostitute of verse 17 is the usual reading, and the WEB takes it. The text does not say so (see
Notes). The verse mentions a vow, which links it to 21–23: a vow paid with money earned this way is
refused. *"Abomination"* is *tôʿăbat YHWH*, the phrase of 7:25,
12:31, 17:1, 18:12, and 22:5 (see Notes).

**Interest (23:19–20).** *"You shall not lend on interest to your brother; interest of money, interest
of food, interest of anything that is lent on interest. You may lend on interest to a foreigner"*
(19–20). Exodus 22:25 and Leviticus 25:35–37 had forbidden interest to a poor brother. Deuteronomy
drops the word *poor*. It widens the goods to *"anything"*, and it names an exception, the *nokrî*:
the foreigner of 15:3, *"Of a foreigner you may require it"*, not the *gēr* (see
[[Lending Without Interest - Exodus 22.25 and the Poor Brother]]). The reason given is blessing:
*"that Yahweh your God may bless you in all that you put your hand to"* (20), the phrase of 12:7,
12:18, and 15:10 (WLC; see Notes). The lender who gives up the profit is promised more than the profit.

**Vows (23:21–23).** *"When you vow a vow to Yahweh your God, you shall not be slack to pay it; for
Yahweh your God will surely require it of you; and it would be sin in you. But if you refrain from
making a vow, it shall be no sin in you"* (21–22). The vow is optional; delay is sin. *"Will surely
require it"* is *dārōš yidrǝšennû*. That is *dāraš*, the verb Yahweh uses of the man who will not
listen to the prophet in 18:19, *"I will require it of him"*. In 23:6 it was *"You shall not seek
their peace"*. *"You shall observe and do that which has gone out of your lips"* (23). The noun is
*môṣāʾ*, *what goes out*. Numbers 30:12 uses the same phrase for a woman's vows, *"whatever
proceeded out of her lips"*. Deuteronomy 8:3 uses the noun for *"every word that proceeds out of
Yahweh's mouth"*. A vow binds the way Yahweh's word does (see Notes and
[[Pay What You Vow - Deuteronomy 23.21-23, Proverbs 20.25, and Ecclesiastes 5.4-6]]).

**The neighbor's vineyard and field (23:24–25).** *"When you come into your neighbor's vineyard, then
you may eat of grapes your fill at your own pleasure; but you shall not put any in your container.
When you come into your neighbor's standing grain, then you may pluck the ears with your hand; but you
shall not move a sickle to your neighbor's standing grain"* (24–25). The line falls between eating and
taking away. A hand and a mouth are allowed; a container and a sickle are not. *"Your fill"*, *śobaʿ*,
is the word of Leviticus 25:19 and 26:5, where the land gives its fruit and Israel eats *"your fill"*.
*"Sickle"* is *ḥermēš*, which occurs in the Torah only here and at 16:9, where the count to the
Feast of Weeks begins *"From the time you begin to put the sickle to the standing grain"*. The law
makes no mention of the poor or the foreigner. It is given to anyone who *"comes into"* a neighbor's
field, and it protects the owner as much as it feeds the passer-by. Leviticus 19:9–10 had left the
corners and the gleanings *"for the poor and for the foreigner"*. Deuteronomy's gleaning law comes in
24:19–21. In the Gospels Jesus' disciples pluck grain going through the fields, and the Pharisees
object only that it is the Sabbath (Matthew 12:1–2; Mark 2:23–24; Luke 6:1). See
[[Plucking a Neighbor's Grain - Deuteronomy 23.24-25 and the Sabbath Grain Fields]].

**How the passage fits together.** The five laws have no shared subject, and the text gives no
heading. Two words tie it to what precedes it. *Nāṣal* runs from Yahweh who walks in the camp *"to
deliver you"* (14) to the slave who *"has escaped"* (15). The root *qdš* runs from *"your camp shall be
holy"* (14) to the *qǝdēšâ* and *qādēš* (17). Within the passage, the vow connects 18 to 21–23. The
brother and the foreigner divide 19–20. The *"neighbor"* is named four times in 24–25. Each law
draws a line around what an Israelite may take from someone else: a slave's freedom, a body's price,
a brother's need, a word already spoken, a neighbor's crop. In each, the line leaves the weaker party
with more than the stronger could have taken. The text does not say so; the wiki records it as an
observation.

## Notes

**Verse numbering.** WEB 23:15–25 is WLC 23:16–26. Verse numbers in this page are the WEB's unless
marked WLC. The boundaries are the plan's, kept as drawn. 23:15 turns from the camp to the runaway,
and 24:1 begins the divorce law.

**"Escaped" and "to deliver you."** *Yinnāṣēl* (WLC 23:16), niphal of *nāṣal* (lemma 5337). In the
Torah the niphal occurs only here and at Genesis 32:31 (WEB 32:30), *wattinnāṣēl napšî*, *"my life is
preserved"* (WLC morphology search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). *Lǝhaṣṣîlkā* (WLC 23:15) is the hiphil
infinitive with suffix, the form of *lǝhaṣṣîlô* in Exodus 3:8 (WLC). That the law-giver chose the verb
to echo verse 14 is not claimed. The recurrence is what is recorded.

**"Deliver to his master."** *Lōʾ-tasgîr* (WLC 23:16), hiphil of *sāgar* (lemma 5462). The hiphil in
the Torah is otherwise Leviticus 13–14, the priest *shutting up* a diseased person or house, and
Deuteronomy 32:30, *hisgîrām*, WEB *"delivered them up"* (WLC).

**"The place which he shall choose."** *Bammāqôm ʾăšer-yibḥar bǝʾaḥad šǝʿārêkā* (WLC 23:17). In the
WLC of Deuteronomy, *yibḥar* (qal imperfect 3ms of *bāḥar*, lemma 977) occurs 23 times: 12:5, 11, 14,
18, 21, 26; 14:23, 24, 25; 15:20; 16:2, 6, 7, 11, 15, 16; 17:8, 10, 15; 18:6; 23:17; 26:2; 31:11.
Every one but 23:17 has Yahweh as the chooser (WLC lemma search; subjects read from stored WEB). In
the book the other non-divine chooser is Israel, *ûbāḥartā*, *"choose life"* (30:19).

**"Where it pleases him best."** *Baṭṭôb lô* (WLC 23:17), *in what is good to him*.

**"Oppress."** *Lōʾ tônennû* (WLC 23:17), hiphil of *yānâ* (lemma 3238). In the Torah otherwise Exodus
22:20 (WEB 22:21), Leviticus 19:33, and Leviticus 25:14, 17 (WLC lemma search). Deuteronomy 24:14's
*"You shall not oppress a hired servant"* uses a different verb, *ʿāšaq*; that is reported from
general knowledge and was not checked against the WLC in this pass.

**"Prostitute" and "sodomite."** *Qǝdēšâ* (lemma 6948) and *qādēš* (lemma 6945) (WLC 23:18). In the
Torah *qǝdēšâ* occurs otherwise only at Genesis 38:21 (twice) and 38:22. *Qādēš* occurs only here
(WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). *Qādôš* (lemma 6918), *holy*, is WLC 23:15. That the three
share the root *qdš* is the standard lexical view. 1 Kings 14:24 and Hosea 4:14 are the passages
usually cited for *qādēš* and *qǝdēšâ* outside the Torah; neither was checked in this pass.

**"The hire of a prostitute … the wages of a male prostitute."** *ʾEtnan zônâ ûmǝḥîr keleb* (WLC
23:19). *ʾEtnan* (lemma 868) and *mǝḥîr* (lemma 4242) occur in the Torah only here. *Zônâ* is the qal
participle of *zānâ* (lemma 2181), the verb of 22:21 and 31:16 in Deuteronomy. *Keleb* (lemma 3611),
*dog*, is otherwise in the Torah only at Exodus 11:7 and 22:30 (WEB 22:31), where the word means the
animal (WLC). That *keleb* here means a male prostitute is the usual reading, reported from general
knowledge. Some readers take it literally, as the price of a dog; the verse alone does not decide it.

**"Interest."** *Nešek* (lemma 5392) three times and the verb *nāšak* (lemma 5391 b) in the hiphil
and qal (WLC 23:20–21). In the Torah the noun occurs otherwise only at Exodus 22:24 (WEB 22:25) and
Leviticus 25:36–37. **The morphhb tagging lists the verb of interest as a separate lemma (5391 b)
from the verb *to bite* (5391 a: Genesis 49:17; Numbers 21:6, 8, 9).** That separation is morphhb's
own classification. The common derivation of *nešek* from *bite* is therefore not supported by this
edition's lemmatization, though it does not refute it either. [[Lending Without Interest - Exodus 22.25 and the Poor Brother]]
had left that derivation unchecked.

**"Foreigner."** *Nokrî* (lemma 5237) (WLC 23:21). In Deuteronomy otherwise 14:21, 15:3, 17:15, and
29:21 (WEB 29:22). It is not *gēr*.

**"All that you put your hand to."** *Bǝkōl mišlaḥ yādekā* (WLC 23:21). *Mišlaḥ* (lemma 4916) occurs in
Deuteronomy otherwise at 12:7, 18; 15:10; and 28:8, 20 (WLC).

**"Be slack" and "refrain."** *Lōʾ tǝʾaḥēr* (WLC 23:22), piel of *ʾāḥar*, *to delay*. *Teḥdal*
(WLC 23:23), qal of *ḥādal*, *to cease, leave off*: the verb of 15:11, *"the poor will never cease"*
(WLC).

**"Require."** *Dārōš yidrǝšennû* (WLC 23:22), infinitive absolute with the imperfect of *dāraš*
(lemma 1875). *Dāraš* also occurs in 18:19, *ʾedrōš*, and in 23:7 (WEB 23:6), *lōʾ-tidrōš* (WLC).

**"That which has gone out of your lips."** *Môṣāʾ śǝpātêkā* (WLC 23:24). Numbers 30:13 (WEB 30:12)
has *môṣāʾ śǝpātêhā*, the same construct with a feminine suffix. Numbers 30:3 (WEB 30:2) has a
different construction, the participle *hayyōṣēʾ mippîw*, *what goes out from his mouth*.
Deuteronomy 8:3 has *môṣāʾ pî-YHWH* (WLC lemma 4161). *"Freewill offering"* is *nǝdābâ* (lemma 5071),
the noun of 12:6, 17 and 16:10. Here it describes the vow, so the phrase joins the two categories
Leviticus 7:16 and 22:18–23 keep distinct; the WEB's *"as a freewill offering"* is one way to take
the syntax.

**"An abomination to Yahweh your God."** *Tôʿăbat YHWH ʾĕlōhêkā* (WLC 23:19). The construct *tôʿăbat*
followed directly by *YHWH* occurs in Deuteronomy at 7:25; 12:31; 17:1; 18:12; 22:5; 23:19 (WLC);
25:16; and 27:15 (WLC lemma 8441 search).

**"Your fill," "ears," "sickle."** *Śobaʿ* (lemma 7648, WLC 23:25) occurs in the Torah otherwise at
Exodus 16:3 and Leviticus 25:19; 26:5. *Mǝlîlōt* (lemma 4425), *ears of grain*, occurs only here in
the Torah. *Ḥermēš* (lemma 2770) occurs only here and at 16:9. *Qāmâ* (lemma 7054), *standing grain*,
occurs only here, at 16:9, and at Exodus 22:5 (WEB 22:6), the field burned by a spreading fire (WLC
lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy).

**What the passage does not settle.** Whether 23:15–16 covers an Israelite's slave or only one fleeing
into Israel. What the *qǝdēšâ* and *qādēš* were. Whether *keleb* means a male prostitute. Why the
*nokrî* may be charged interest, beyond the contrast of terms. Whether the vineyard law was meant for
travellers, labourers, or the poor. Each is recorded, none adopted.
