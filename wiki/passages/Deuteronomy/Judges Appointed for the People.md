# Judges Appointed for the People

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 1:9–18

## Summary

Moses turns from Yahweh's word at Horeb to his own word *"at that time"*. Israel had become too
many for one man: Yahweh had multiplied them until they were *"as the stars of the sky for
multitude"*, and Moses, breaking off to pray that they become a thousand times more, asks how he
alone can carry their *"problems"*, *"burdens"*, and *"strife"*. He tells the people to take wise,
understanding, well-known men from their tribes; the people agree; he makes them heads — captains
of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens, and officers. Then he charges the judges: hear cases
between brothers and with the resident foreigner, judge righteously, show no partiality, hear small
and great alike, fear no man, *"for the judgment is God's"*, and bring the case too hard for them to
Moses. A closing line says he commanded *"all the things which you should do"* at that time.

The unit sits between the command to leave Horeb (1:6–8) and the march to Kadesh Barnea (1:19). In
the order of the speech, Israel is organized and its courts are charged before it sets out — so the
refusal at Kadesh that follows is the failure of a people that had been fully equipped.

## Key verses

> **9** I spoke to you at that time, saying, “I am not able to bear you myself alone.

> **10** Yahweh your God has multiplied you, and behold, you are today as the stars of the sky for multitude.

> **11** Yahweh, the God of your fathers, make you a thousand times as many as you are, and bless you, as he has promised you!

> **12** How can I myself alone bear your problems, your burdens, and your strife?

> **13** Take wise men of understanding and well known according to your tribes, and I will make them heads over you.”

> **14** You answered me, and said, “The thing which you have spoken is good to do.”

> **15** So I took the heads of your tribes, wise men, and known, and made them heads over you, captains of thousands, and captains of hundreds, and captains of fifties, and captains of tens, and officers, according to your tribes.

> **16** I commanded your judges at that time, saying, “Hear cases between your brothers, and judge righteously between a man and his brother, and the foreigner who is living with him.

> **17** You shall not show partiality in judgment; you shall hear the small and the great alike. You shall not be afraid of the face of man, for the judgment is God’s. The case that is too hard for you, you shall bring to me, and I will hear it.”

> **18** I commanded you at that time all the things which you should do.

## People
- [[Moses]] — The speaker, recalling his own words (9–13), his appointment of the heads (15), and his charge to the judges (16–17). He keeps the hard case for himself (17).
- [[God]] — Yahweh has multiplied Israel (10), is prayed to for a thousandfold increase and blessing (11), and is the owner of the judgment the courts give (17).
- [[Jethro]] — Absent. Exodus 18 credits the plan to him; this retelling never mentions him. See the Notes.

## Places
- [[Horeb]] — Not named in these verses, but *"at that time"* (9, 16, 18) follows directly from the departure command at Horeb (1:6–8); the speech places the appointment there.

## Themes
- [[Judgment]] — The charge to the judges (16–17): righteous, impartial, unafraid, and grounded in the claim that *"the judgment is God's"*.
- [[Wisdom]] — The qualification for office is to be *"wise men of understanding and well known"* (13, 15).
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — Moses cannot carry the people alone (9, 12) and shares the work, while keeping the case too hard for the others (17).
- [[Blessing]] — Moses' interjected prayer for a thousandfold increase and blessing *"as he has promised you"* (11).
- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — *"The foreigner who is living with him"* stands in the same court as the brother (16), and *"the small"* are heard like *"the great"* (17).
- [[The Fear of God]] — *"You shall not be afraid of the face of man, for the judgment is God's"* (17).
- [[Remembrance]] — The unit is Moses recalling what he said and did *"at that time"* (9, 16, 18).

## Notes

**Whose plan.** Exodus 18 tells the same institution as Jethro's counsel: Moses sits alone from
morning to evening, his father-in-law says *"the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to
perform it yourself alone"* (Exodus 18:18), and Moses *"listened to the voice of his father-in-law,
and did all that he had said"* (18:24). Deuteronomy gives the sentence to Moses himself — *"I am not
able to bear you myself alone"* (9) — gives the proposal to him too, and adds the people's consent:
*"The thing which you have spoken is good to do"* (14). Jethro is not mentioned. The wiki does not
harmonize the two; the readings (one event retold to a new generation, or more than one appointment
compressed) are set out at
[[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]]. That page's table of the
three accounts, including its Hebrew comparison, is not repeated here.

**Who chooses.** In Exodus Moses *"chose able men out of all Israel"* (18:25). Here the people are
told to *"take"* men *"according to your tribes"* and Moses makes them heads (13, 15). The
qualifications differ as well: Exodus asks for men who *"fear God: men of truth, hating unjust
gain"* (18:21), Deuteronomy for men *"wise … of understanding and well known"* (13). The fear of God
does appear in Deuteronomy's charge, but as its reverse — the judges are not to be afraid of man,
*"for the judgment is God's"* (17).

**The blessing in the middle of the complaint.** Verse 10 states Israel's size as the reason Moses
cannot carry them; verse 11 immediately prays that they become *"a thousand times as many as you
are"*. The burden is not presented as a problem with the promise. Moses wishes the burden larger and
asks for help to carry it. Verse 10's *"as the stars of the sky for multitude"* is the star image of
Genesis 15:5 and 22:17 reported as fulfilled; Deuteronomy 10:22 says it again, counting from the
*"seventy persons"* who went down to Egypt. See
[[Dust, Stars, and Sand - The Offspring Promises]].

**Small and great.** Exodus 18:22 sorts *cases*: *"every great matter they shall bring to you, but
every small matter they shall judge themselves."* Deuteronomy 1:17 uses the same pair for
*persons*: *"you shall hear the small and the great alike."* The division of work by case size
remains — *"the case that is too hard for you, you shall bring to me"* — but the words *small* and
*great* are now spent on the parties, who must be heard equally. This is an observation on the WEB
wording of both passages; no claim about the underlying Hebrew is made here.

**The foreigner in court.** The judges hear cases *"between a man and his brother, and the foreigner
who is living with him"* (16). The resident foreigner is placed inside Israel's court, not outside
it, in line with Exodus 22:21 and 23:9 and Leviticus 19:33–34, which forbid wronging him and give
Israel's own time *"in the land of Egypt"* as the reason. Deuteronomy 10:18–19 will say Yahweh
*"loves the foreigner"* and command Israel to do the same.

**"The judgment is God's."** The reason given for impartiality is not efficiency or public order but
ownership: the verdict belongs to God, so the judge has no one else to fear and nothing of his own to
dispense. Deuteronomy 10:17 grounds the same rule in God's character — he *"doesn't respect persons,
nor takes reward"* — and 16:18–19 repeats the charge for judges *"in all your gates"*: *"You shall not
show partiality. You shall not take a bribe."* Proverbs 24:23 condenses it into a wisdom saying:
*"To show partiality in judgment is not good."* See
[[The Judgment Is God's - Exodus 23, Leviticus 19.15, and Deuteronomy 1.17]].

**The hard case.** *"The case that is too hard for you, you shall bring to me"* (17) matches Exodus
18:26, where the judges *"brought the hard causes to Moses."* Deuteronomy 17:8–9 provides for the
same need after Moses: *"If there arises a matter too hard for you in judgment"*, the parties go up
to the place Yahweh chooses, to the Levitical priests and *"the judge who shall be in those days"*.
The function Moses keeps here is the one the law later hands to an office.

**Verse 18.** *"I commanded you at that time all the things which you should do"* closes the Horeb
section of the retrospect. It is general; no specific laws are listed.
