# Leave Horeb and Possess the Land

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 1:6–8

## Summary

Moses' direct speech begins. His first words are not a law but a quotation: what Yahweh said to
Israel at Horeb. The people have stayed long enough at the mountain; they are to turn, set out,
and go to the hill country of the Amorites and its whole surrounding country, which Moses lists
region by region out to Lebanon and the Euphrates. Yahweh has set the land before them. They
are to go in and possess what he swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and to their offspring.

The unit is three verses: a command to leave, a map, and an oath. Everything Moses goes on to
recount in chapter 1 — the judges, the spies, the refusal — happens between this command and its
fulfilment, so the speech opens by setting the standard the rest of the retrospect measures
against.

## Key verses

Complete unit copied verbatim from raw/bible/Deuteronomy.md (WEB).

> **6** “Yahweh our God spoke to us in Horeb, saying, ‘You have lived long enough at this mountain.

> **7** Turn, and take your journey, and go to the hill country of the Amorites, and to all the places near there, in the Arabah, in the hill country, in the lowland, in the South, by the seashore, in the land of the Canaanites, and in Lebanon, as far as the great river, the river Euphrates.

> **8** Behold, I have set the land before you. Go in and possess the land which Yahweh swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, to give to them and to their offspring after them.’”

## People
- [[God]] — Yahweh is the speaker Moses quotes (6–8). He ends the stay at Horeb, names the destination, sets the land before Israel, and is himself the one who swore it to the fathers.
- [[Moses]] — The speaker of the whole address; here he quotes Yahweh rather than issuing a command in his own name (6). He says *us*, placing himself inside the people who were addressed at Horeb.
- [[Abraham]] — First of the three fathers to whom the land was sworn (8). The oath is recalled, not re-narrated.
- [[Isaac]] — Second of the three fathers named in the oath (8).
- [[Jacob]] — Third of the three fathers named in the oath (8). The formula uses the name Jacob, not Israel.

## Places
- [[Horeb]] — Where Yahweh spoke and where Israel had *lived long enough* (6). Deuteronomy uses the name Horeb; the wiki files Horeb and Sinai on one page.
- [[Canaan]] — *The land of the Canaanites* is one of the regions in the list (7), and the whole is the land sworn to the fathers (8).
- [[Arabah]] — Named again, now as a region of the land to be entered (7), not as the setting of the address as in 1:1.
- [[The Negev]] — The WEB's *the South* (7), filed under the Negev page as elsewhere in the wiki.
- [[Lebanon]] — The northern reach of the list (7); the first time Lebanon is named in the stored Torah.
- [[The Euphrates]] — *The great river, the river Euphrates* (7), the same formula as Genesis 15:18.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The land is set before Israel and is to be possessed (7–8); the sojourning of the fathers is answered by an oath to give it to them and their offspring.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — The land is the land Yahweh *swore* (8). The speech grounds the command in God's sworn word, not in Israel's readiness.
- [[Covenant]] — The oath to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob is invoked as the basis of the journey (8).
- [[Remembrance]] — Moses' address opens by recalling a word spoken decades earlier (6). Recollection is the speech's first act.
- [[The Wilderness]] — The stay at the mountain is ended by command (6); the wilderness is the time between that command and the land.

## Notes

**The first words are Yahweh's.** Verse 5 says Moses *began to declare this law*, and what follows
is a quotation inside a quotation: Moses' speech opens with Yahweh's speech. In the WEB the
nesting is marked by double and single quotation marks. Before Moses says anything in his own
voice, he places his authority under a word already spoken at Horeb.

**"Spoke to us."** The WEB has Moses say Yahweh spoke *to us* and addresses the hearers as *you*.
Numbers 26:64–65 says that by the census in Moab not a man of those counted at Sinai remained
except Caleb and Joshua. The audience in Moab is therefore mostly not the generation that stood
at Horeb, yet the address treats Israel as one people across the generations. This is an
observation about the speech's form; the verses themselves do not comment on it.

**A long stay ended.** *You have lived long enough at this mountain* treats the time at Horeb as
complete, not as wasted. Numbers 10:11–13 narrates the same departure in the second month of the
second year, when the cloud is taken up and Israel sets out *according to the commandment of
Yahweh by Moses*. Exodus 33:1 records an earlier command to *depart, go up from here* toward the
land sworn to the three fathers, given in the aftermath of the golden calf. Deuteronomy does not say
which of these moments it quotes; it compresses the command into a single remembered word. See
[[Depart from the Mountain - Exodus 33, Numbers 10, and Deuteronomy 1.6-8]].

**The map.** Verse 7 lists the land by kinds of country more than by borders: hill country, the
Arabah, the lowland, the South, the seashore, Canaanite land, Lebanon, the Euphrates. *The hill
country of the Amorites* heads the list; the wiki has no page for the Amorites as a people, and they
are not given one here. *The lowland* is commonly identified with the Shephelah, the foothills
between the central hills and the coast; the stored WEB does not use that name, so no page is
created for it. *By the seashore* names no sea. The northern and eastern reach — Lebanon and the
Euphrates — repeats the largest form of the promise from Genesis 15:18, not the narrower survey of
Numbers 34. See [[From the River of Egypt to the Euphrates]].

**Set before you.** *Behold, I have set the land before you* is spoken as already done; *go in and
possess* is what remains. The pairing of a gift already given with an act still required is the
pattern the rest of chapter 1 tests: the spies and the refusal at Kadesh Barnea (1:19–46) are the
next units of the plan.

**The oath formula.** *To Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob* names all three fathers as recipients.
The stored WEB has the same three-name oath at Genesis 50:24, Exodus 6:8, Exodus 33:1, and
Numbers 32:11. Here, unlike Numbers 32:11, it is spoken as a promise to be entered rather than as a
sentence excluding a generation from it.

All verse quotations are copied from stored WEB Deuteronomy, Numbers, Exodus, and Genesis. No
Hebrew or Greek wording is asserted; *the South* is filed under the Negev page as an existing wiki
convention, not on a new lexical claim.
