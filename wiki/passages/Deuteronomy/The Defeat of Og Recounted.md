# The Defeat of Og Recounted

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 3:1–11

## Summary

Moses recalls Og’s attack at Edrei and Yahweh’s assurance that his defeat will follow Sihon’s.
Israel takes sixty fortified cities and many villages, destroys the inhabitants, and retains
livestock and goods. The account summarizes the two kings’ territory from the Arnon to Hermon,
glosses Hermon’s names, and identifies Og with the Rephaim remnant, pointing to his iron bedstead
in Rabbah.

## Key verses

Complete unit, copied verbatim from stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:1–11.

> **1** Then we turned, and went up the way to Bashan. Og the king of Bashan came out against us, he and all his people, to battle at Edrei.

> **2** Yahweh said to me, “Don’t fear him; for I have delivered him, with all his people, and his land, into your hand. You shall do to him as you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived at Heshbon.”

> **3** So Yahweh our God delivered into our hand Og also, the king of Bashan, and all his people. We struck him until no one was left to him remaining.

> **4** We took all his cities at that time. There was not a city which we didn’t take from them; sixty cities, all the region of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.

> **5** All these were cities fortified with high walls, gates, and bars; besides a great many villages without walls.

> **6** We utterly destroyed them, as we did to Sihon king of Heshbon, utterly destroying every inhabited city, with the women and the little ones.

> **7** But all the livestock, and the plunder of the cities, we took for plunder for ourselves.

> **8** We took the land at that time out of the hand of the two kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan, from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon.

> **9** (The Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir.)

> **10** We took all the cities of the plain, and all Gilead, and all Bashan, to Salecah and Edrei, cities of the kingdom of Og in Bashan.

> **11** (For only Og king of Bashan remained of the remnant of the Rephaim. Behold, his bedstead was a bedstead of iron. Isn’t it in Rabbah of the children of Ammon? Nine cubits was its length, and four cubits its width, after the cubit of a man.)

## Notes

### Fortifications, assurance, and destruction

The cities’ high walls, gates, and bars emphasize the kingdom’s strength (4–5), yet the account
attributes victory to Yahweh’s delivery (2–3). The earlier refusal to advance and later
presumptuous advance in Deuteronomy 1 provide a contrast: the decisive issue in this narrative is
the divine word, not the absence of formidable opponents.

Verse 6 explicitly includes women and little ones. Verse 7 distinguishes the retained animals and
goods from the destroyed inhabitants. No catalogue of civilian offenses is supplied; the moral
difficulty should remain visible rather than being replaced with an account of soldiers alone.

### Names and the bedstead

The Sirion/Senir gloss (9) preserves different peoples’ names for Hermon. Song 4:8 pairs Senir and
Hermon; the existing tension is retained in the linked comparison rather than resolved by an
unverified geographical reconstruction.

The WEB describes an iron bedstead, nine cubits long and four wide (11). These dimensions are not
Og’s stated height. The question pointing to Rabbah presents an object as available to the text’s
audience; it is not independent modern confirmation of that object. The text does not explain how
it reached Rabbah. No claim about the Hebrew term or the object’s archaeological identity is made.

### Scope

The planned boundary is retained: conquest and explanatory notices through verse 11 form this
unit; tribal allotments beginning at verse 12 remain next. Argob, Salecah, and Rabbah receive place
pages. Sirion and Senir remain aliases under Hermon. The Rephaim retain their existing collective
page; Israel, Amorites, and Sidonians here are peoples, not newly appearing individual ancestors.
Rabbah’s location in Ammon does not imply conquest of the territory protected in Deuteronomy 2.
