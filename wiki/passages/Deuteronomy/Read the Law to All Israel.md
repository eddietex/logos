# Read the Law to All Israel

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 31:9–13

## Summary

Moses writes the law and delivers it to the priests descended from Levi, who bear the covenant
ark, and to all Israel’s elders (9). Leadership succession is accompanied by a provision for
continued instruction: the written law must be heard by the people.

At the end of every seven years, during the year of release and the feast of tents, the law is
to be read at the place Yahweh will choose (10–11). The assembly expressly includes men, women,
little ones, and resident foreigners (12). The goal moves from hearing to learning, fearing
Yahweh, and doing the law. Children who have not known must also hear and learn, throughout
Israel’s life in the land beyond the Jordan (13). This is a command for future practice, not
an account of a reading already performed.

## Key verses

> **31:9** Moses wrote this law, and delivered it to the priests the sons of Levi, who bore the ark of Yahweh’s covenant, and to all the elders of Israel.

> **31:12** Assemble the people, the men and the women and the little ones, and the foreigners who are within your gates, that they may hear, and that they may learn, and fear Yahweh your God, and observe to do all the words of this law;

> **31:13** and that their children, who have not known, may hear, and learn to fear Yahweh your God, as long as you live in the land where you go over the Jordan to possess it.”

## Notes

- The text attributes writing to Moses, but does not define the boundaries of “this law.”
  These verses alone do not establish the extent or final composition of the Pentateuch.
- The priests and elders are both recipients. The passage does not identify a future king,
  high priest, or Joshua as the single reader. The elders are not identified with the seventy
  of Exodus 24 or Numbers 11, so no link equating those groups is made.
- The chosen place remains unnamed. Jerusalem is not supplied as though named in this unit.
- The feast’s inclusive assembly complements the male pilgrimage duty in 16:16; that duty
  did not say women or children were forbidden. Household teaching in 6:6–9 also means the
  seven-year gathering is not the only occasion for learning.
