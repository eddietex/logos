# Refuge for the Unwitting Killer

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 19:1–13

## Summary

The list of offices is finished (16:18–18:22), and the law code turns to the protection of life. The
first case is the man who has killed someone and did not mean to.

**Three cities in the land (19:1–7).** *"When Yahweh your God cuts off the nations"* and Israel lives
*"in their cities and in their houses"* (1), Israel is to *"set apart three cities for yourselves in the
middle of your land"* (2), *"prepare the way"*, and divide the land into three parts, *"that every man
slayer may flee there"* (3). The case is defined twice. In general terms it is the one *"who kills his
neighbor unawares, and didn't hate him in time past"* (4). As an example it is two neighbours chopping
wood in the forest: the ax head *"slips from the handle and hits his neighbor so that he dies"* (5).
That man *"shall flee to one of these cities and live"*. The reason is the distance and the anger of
the family: *"the avenger of blood might pursue the man slayer while his heart is hot and overtake him,
because the way is long, and strike him mortally; even though he was not worthy of death"* (6). Hence
the command repeated: *"set apart three cities for yourselves"* (7).

**Three more if the border grows (19:8–10).** *"If Yahweh your God enlarges your border, as he has
sworn to your fathers"*, and if Israel keeps *"all this commandment … to love Yahweh your God, and to
walk ever in his ways"*, *"then you shall add three cities more for yourselves, besides these three"*
(8–9). The purpose is stated as the land's, not the killer's: *"so that innocent blood will not be shed
in the middle of your land … leaving blood guilt on you"* (10). If the unwitting killer is caught and
killed on a long road, the blood that is shed is innocent, and the guilt for it falls on Israel.

**No refuge for the murderer (19:11–13).** The opposite case: a man who *"hates his neighbor, lies in
wait for him, rises up against him, strikes him mortally"* and then flees to one of the cities (11).
*"The elders of his city shall send and bring him there, and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of
blood, that he may die"* (12). *"Your eye shall not pity him, but you shall purge the innocent blood from
Israel that it may go well with you"* (13).

**How the passage fits together.** The section turns on one distinction, hatred beforehand, stated three
times: *"didn't hate him in time past"* (4, 6) and *"hates his neighbor"* (11). What makes a killing
murder is not the death but what came before it. The law protects the innocent killer from the avenger,
and it protects the avenger's right against the guilty killer; both are named as *"innocent blood"* —
the blood of the unwitting killer that must not be shed (10), and the blood of the victim that must be
purged (13). Refuge is not an escape from justice but the condition for it: it keeps a man alive long
enough for the difference to be found.

**What Deuteronomy assumes and does not say.** Numbers 35 has already set the system out in full: six
cities among the Levites' forty-eight, a hearing before *"the congregation"* (35:12, 24–25), residence
until *"the death of the high priest"* (35:25), and no ransom for a murderer (35:31). Deuteronomy 19
mentions none of these. It does not use the phrase *"city of refuge"* either (WLC: *miqlāṭ*, lemma 4733,
is absent from Deuteronomy; morphhb, eleven times in Numbers). In their place it has the town's
**elders** (12), who send for a murderer and hand him over, and it has the **way**: Israel is to
*"prepare the way"* (3), because *"the way is long"* (6). The two laws are compatible — the elders'
action in 19:12 presumes the murderer is known to be one, and Numbers says how that is decided — but
they are not the same text, and the wiki does not merge them. See
[[A Place to Flee - Exodus 21 and Numbers 35]].

**Three, six, or nine?** Moses had already set apart three cities east of the Jordan (4:41–43). These
three are *"in the middle of your land"* (2), the land west of the Jordan Israel has yet to enter. That
makes six, the number of Numbers 35:13–14, and Joshua 20:7–8 records six: Kedesh, Shechem, and Kiriath
Arba west of the Jordan, Bezer, Ramoth, and Golan east of it (WEB, checked at bible-api.com). The
*"three cities more"* of 19:9 then makes nine, which no later text records. Readers have taken this in
more than one way:

- *Nine in principle.* Three east, three west, and three more for a border enlarged to the full extent
  of the promise to the fathers. The condition — keeping *"all this commandment"* and the enlarged border
  — was not met, and the three were never needed.
- *Six in all.* The three of 19:2 are the western cities, and the *"three more"* are the eastern three
  already named in 4:41–43, read as added to the land when its border took in the east. This sits
  awkwardly with 19:8–9, which puts the addition in the future, after Moses had named the eastern cities.

The wiki records both readings and adopts neither. The text itself fixes only that the number grows
with the land, so that no one in Israel is too far from a refuge.

## Key verses

> **19:1** When Yahweh your God cuts off the nations whose land Yahweh your God gives you, and you succeed them and dwell in their cities and in their houses;

> **19:2** you shall set apart three cities for yourselves in the middle of your land, which Yahweh your God gives you to possess it.

> **19:3** You shall prepare the way, and divide the borders of your land which Yahweh your God causes you to inherit into three parts, that every man slayer may flee there.

> **19:4** This is the case of the man slayer who shall flee there and live. Whoever kills his neighbor unawares, and didn’t hate him in time past;

> **19:5** as when a man goes into the forest with his neighbor to chop wood and his hand swings the ax to cut down the tree, and the head slips from the handle and hits his neighbor so that he dies, he shall flee to one of these cities and live.

> **19:6** Otherwise, the avenger of blood might pursue the man slayer while his heart is hot and overtake him, because the way is long, and strike him mortally; even though he was not worthy of death, because he didn’t hate him in time past.

> **19:7** Therefore I command you to set apart three cities for yourselves.

> **19:8** If Yahweh your God enlarges your border, as he has sworn to your fathers, and gives you all the land which he promised to give to your fathers;

> **19:9** if you keep all this commandment to do it, which I command you today, to love Yahweh your God, and to walk ever in his ways; then you shall add three cities more for yourselves, besides these three.

> **19:10** This is so that innocent blood will not be shed in the middle of your land which Yahweh your God gives you for an inheritance, leaving blood guilt on you.

> **19:11** But if any man hates his neighbor, lies in wait for him, rises up against him, strikes him mortally so that he dies, and he flees into one of these cities;

> **19:12** then the elders of his city shall send and bring him there, and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of blood, that he may die.

> **19:13** Your eye shall not pity him, but you shall purge the innocent blood from Israel that it may go well with you.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, who cuts off the nations and gives the land (1–2), who may enlarge the border as he
  swore to the fathers (8), and whom Israel is to love and walk with (9).
- [[Moses]] — the speaker: *"I command you"* (7, 9).
- The man slayer, the neighbour, the avenger of blood, the murderer, and the elders of his city — legal
  roles, not individuals. No pages.

## Places

- The land Yahweh gives (1–3, 8, 10) — divided into three parts for the three cities; unnamed. No page.
- The three cities (2, 7) and the three more (9) — unnamed here. Joshua 20:7 names the western three;
  they get pages when Joshua is ingested.

## Themes

- [[Vengeance]] — the avenger of blood, restrained from the innocent killer (6) and given the guilty
  one (12).
- [[Blood]] — *"innocent blood"* not to be shed (10), and purged (13); *"blood guilt on you"* (10).
- [[Judgment]] — the line between the unwitting killer and the murderer, drawn by prior hatred (4, 6, 11).
- [[Life and Death]] — *"flee there and live"* (4–5); *"not worthy of death"* (6); *"that he may die"* (12).
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — refuge for the man who did not mean it, and *"your eye shall not pity him"*
  for the man who did (13).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — the three further cities depend on keeping *"all this commandment"* (9).
- [[City and Civilization]] — cities set apart for a legal purpose, and the town's elders acting for it.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — the land divided into three, its border enlarged, and its guilt (2–3, 8, 10).
- [[Love]] — *"to love Yahweh your God"* (9), set against the hatred that makes murder (11).

## Notes

**"Man slayer."** *Rōṣēaḥ* (19:3, 4, 6, WLC), from *rāṣaḥ*, the verb of *"You shall not murder"*, *lōʾ
tirṣāḥ* (Deuteronomy 5:17, WLC; lemma 7523 in morphhb). In Deuteronomy the root occurs only at 4:42, 5:17,
19:3–6, and 22:26 (morphhb lemma search). The same word that names the forbidden act in the Ten Words is
used here of a man who killed by accident, so the word by itself does not decide guilt; the law has to
add *"unawares"* and *"didn't hate him"*. WEB's *"man slayer"* keeps the word neutral, as the Hebrew is in
this chapter.

**"Unawares."** *Biblî-daʿat*, literally *without knowledge* (19:4, WLC), the same phrase as 4:42. Numbers
35:11, 15 has instead *bišgāgâ* (WLC), WEB *"unwittingly"* — the noun the wiki's Leviticus 4 pages
associate with the sin offering (taken from those pages, not re-checked against WLC for this one).
Deuteronomy's phrase is about what the killer knew; Numbers' word is about error. They describe the same
case. See [[Sinning Unwittingly - Integrity, Ignorance, and Guilt]] and
[[Unwitting and Presumptuous - Numbers 15, Psalm 19, and Hebrews 10]].

**"In time past."** *Mittəmōl šilšōm* (19:4, 6, WLC), literally *from yesterday and the day before*: the
usual Hebrew way of saying *formerly*. Hatred is a history, not a mood at the moment of the blow.

**The ax head.** *Wənāšal habbarzel min-hāʿēṣ* (19:5, WLC), literally *the iron slips off the wood*. The
verse uses *ʿēṣ* three times — the *"wood"* chopped, *"the tree"* cut down, and the *"handle"* the iron
flies from — so WEB's three English words translate one Hebrew one.

**"While his heart is hot."** *Kî-yēḥam ləbābô* (19:6, WLC). The verb *yāḥam*, *be hot*, occurs only here
in Deuteronomy (morphhb). The law takes the avenger's anger as a fact to plan around, not a sin to forbid.
It does not condemn him for pursuing; it shortens the road.

**"Not worthy of death."** *Wəlô ʾên mišpaṭ-māwet* (19:6, WLC), literally *and to him there is no judgment
of death*. *Mišpāṭ* is the word for a legal ruling; the unwitting killer is not under a death sentence,
and an avenger who kills him sheds *"innocent blood"* (10).

**"Lies in wait."** *Wəʾārab lô* (19:11, WLC); lemma 693 occurs in Deuteronomy only here (morphhb). It is
the opposite of the forest accident: the murderer chose the place. Exodus 21:13–14 draws the same line
with other words — *"God allows it to happen"* against *"schemes and comes presumptuously"* (stored WEB).

**The elders, not the city of refuge.** The murderer is fetched by *"the elders of his city"* (12) — his
own town, not the city he fled to. Deuteronomy gives the town's elders other cases ahead: the unsolved
killing (21:1–9), the rebellious son (21:19), the slandered bride (22:15), and the brother-in-law who
refuses (25:7–9; stored WEB). Numbers 35:24 gives the judging to *"the congregation"*; Joshua 20:4 has the
fugitive state his case to *"the elders of that city"*, the city of refuge (WEB, checked at bible-api.com).
The three texts give the decision to different bodies. They can be harmonized — local elders, the larger
assembly, and the elders of the receiving city at different stages — but none of them describes the
stages, and the wiki records the difference rather than resolving it.

**"Your eye shall not pity him."** *Lōʾ-tāḥôs ʿêneka ʿālāyw* (19:13, WLC). *Ḥûs*, *pity*, *spare*, with
*eye*, occurs in Deuteronomy at 7:16 (the nations), 13:9 (13:8 in English; the relative who entices to
other gods), 19:13 and 19:21 (the false witness), and 25:12 (morphhb lemma 2347). In every case it shuts
off a mercy the hearer might be tempted to show.

**"Purge the innocent blood."** *Ûbiʿartā dam-hannāqî miyyiśrāʾēl* (19:13, WLC). The verb is *bāʿar*,
the verb of *"So you shall remove the evil from among you"* (13:5; 17:7, 12), and its object here is not
the evil but *"the innocent blood"* — the victim's. WEB renders the same verb *"put away"* at 21:9, where
the object is again *"the innocent blood"*. See
[[Remove the Evil from Among You - Deuteronomy 13.5 and 1 Corinthians 5.13]].

**"Innocent blood" twice.** *Dām nāqî* (10) and *dam-hannāqî* (13, WLC; lemma 5355 a, which occurs in
Deuteronomy at 19:10, 13; 21:8, 9; 24:5; 27:25, morphhb). In 10 it is the unwitting killer's blood that
must not be shed; in 13 it is the murdered man's blood that must be purged. The same phrase describes
the two people the law is protecting.

**Not resolved here.** Whether there were to be six cities or nine; who decides between the killer and the
avenger — the elders, the congregation, or both; whether the avenger is a duty, a right, or only a fact;
how the high priest's death in Numbers 35 relates to a law that does not mention it. Each is recorded,
none adopted.
