# First Fruits and the Tithe Declaration

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 26:1–15

## Summary

The laws of chapters 12–25 end with two rites, and both are spoken. The first is a yearly gift at
the chosen place: the farmer brings the first of the ground's fruit in a basket and recites Israel's
story over it, from a perishing father to a land of milk and honey (1–11). The second comes every
third year: the farmer gives the tithe to the four landless groups in his own town, then declares
before Yahweh that he has done it, and done it cleanly, and asks Yahweh to bless (12–15). The law
code opened at 12:5–7 with *"the place which Yahweh your God shall choose"*, with tithes, and with
rejoicing; it closes on the same things.

In the Hebrew, verses 1–15 have the same numbers as the WEB.

**"When you have come in to the land" (26:1).** The rite is for after the conquest: *"possess it,
and dwell in it"*. *"Take some of the first of all the fruit of the ground … put it in a basket,
and shall go to the place which Yahweh your God shall choose, to cause his name to dwell there"*
(2). The phrase *"to cause his name to dwell there"* (*lǝšakkēn šǝmô šām*) is the formula of 12:11,
14:23, and 16:11. *"The first"* is *rēʾšît*, not *bikkûrîm*: Deuteronomy uses *rēʾšît* for first
fruits (18:4; 26:2, 10) and never uses *bikkûrîm*, the word Exodus 23:19 pairs with it in *"The
first of the first fruits of your ground"* (WLC lemma search; see Notes). The basket, *ṭeneʾ*,
appears in the Torah only here (2, 4) and in the blessing and curse of 28:5 and 28:17: *"Your basket
and your kneading trough shall be blessed"* and *"… will be cursed"*. The basket brought to the
altar is the one the next chapters put under blessing or curse.

**"I profess today" (26:3–4).** Before the gift, a sentence to the priest: *"I profess today to
Yahweh your God, that I have come to the land which Yahweh swore to our fathers to give us."* The
verb is *higgadtî*, hiphil of *nāgad*, *I declare*. The same form with *hayyôm* returns at 30:18,
*"I denounce to you today, that you will surely perish"*: there Moses declares, and the thing
declared is that Israel will perish; here the farmer declares that he has arrived (WLC; see Notes).
The farmer speaks in the singular, *"I have come"*, of a promise made *"to our fathers"*. Whoever
brings the basket, in whatever generation, says he is the one who came in. The priest sets the
basket *"before Yahweh your God's altar"* (4).

**The confession (26:5–10).** *"You shall answer and say before Yahweh your God"* (5), and what
follows is a fixed prayer for an ordinary Israelite to say, which the Torah rarely provides. It runs in four
movements.

*The father.* *"My father was a Syrian ready to perish."* The Hebrew is three words, *ʾărammî ʾōbēd
ʾābî*. *ʾĂrammî* is the gentilic of Bethuel and Laban, *"the Syrian"* (Genesis 25:20; 28:5; 31:20,
24), and occurs nowhere else in Genesis–Deuteronomy (WLC lemma search). *ʾŌbēd* is the qal
participle of *ʾābad*, the verb of *"you will … utterly perish"* (4:26) and *"you shall surely
perish"* (8:19; 30:18). The WEB's *"ready to perish"* takes it that way. Other translations take it
as *wandering*; the same verb is used of a brother's property *"which he has lost"* (22:3) (the
*wandering* rendering is reported from general knowledge). Who the father is the text does not
say. Jacob is the usual reading, since he went down into Egypt with the family and spent twenty
years in Aram; Abraham is another (both reported from general knowledge as current readings). A third reading, attested in later Jewish tradition, takes
*ʾōbēd* as transitive, *an Aramean (Laban) sought to destroy my father* (reported from general
knowledge; not checked against a particular edition). The wiki follows the WEB and records the
others. On what the line does with the family's Aramean past, see [[Laban (person)]],
[[Aram Naharaim]], and [[Jegar Sahadutha - The Only Aramaic in the Torah]].

*Down, few, many.* *"He went down into Egypt, and lived there, few in number. There he became a great,
mighty, and populous nation"* (5). *"Lived there"* is *wayyāgor*, *sojourned* as a foreigner, the
verb of Abram in Egypt (Genesis 12:10). *"Few in number"*, *bimǝtê mǝʿāṭ*, returns at 28:62, *"You
will be left few in number"*; 4:27 has the same English for a slightly different phrase, *mǝtê
mispār* (WLC). The confession
starts where the curse of 28:62 will end. *"A great, mighty, and populous nation"*: *gôy gādôl* is
the promise to Abram, *"I will make of you a great nation"* (Genesis 12:2), and *ʿāṣûm*, *mighty*,
is the word of Exodus 1:9, where Pharaoh says the people are *"more and mightier than we"*. Deuteronomy
10:22 has the same arc in one verse: *"Your fathers went down into Egypt with seventy persons; and now
Yahweh your God has made you as the stars of the sky for multitude."*

*Oppression and cry.* *"The Egyptians mistreated us, afflicted us, and imposed hard labor on us"* (6).
The farmer now says *us*: the father's story has become his own. Each of the three verbs comes
from Exodus 1. *"Afflicted"* is *wayǝʿannûnû*, the piel of *ʿānâ* of Exodus 1:11–12 and of
Genesis 15:13, *"They will afflict them four hundred years"*. *"Hard labor"* is *ʿăbōdâ qāšâ*, a
phrase the Torah uses only at Exodus 1:14 (*"hard service"*), Exodus 6:9 (*"cruel bondage"*), and
here (WLC; see Notes). *"Then we cried to Yahweh, the God of our fathers. Yahweh heard our voice,
and saw our affliction, our toil, and our oppression"* (7). *"Our affliction"* (*ʿonyēnû*) and
*"our oppression"* (*laḥăṣēnû*) are the two things Yahweh *sees* at the bush, *"I have surely seen
the affliction of my people"* (Exodus 3:7) and *"I have seen the oppression with which the Egyptians
oppress them"* (3:9). *Laḥaṣ* occurs in the Torah only at Exodus 3:9 and here. *"Our toil"*
(*ʿămālēnû*) is the word Joseph uses at Manasseh's naming, *"God has made me forget all my toil"*
(Genesis 41:51) (WLC lemma search). The cry, *wanniṣʿaq*, is the verb of Exodus 22:23 (WLC 22:22), where Yahweh
promises that if the widow or fatherless *"cry at all to me, I will surely hear their cry"*. What
Yahweh did for Israel he says he will do for the widow and orphan; the second half of this chapter
asks Israel to feed them.

*Out and in.* *"Yahweh brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand, with an outstretched arm, with great
terror, with signs, and with wonders"* (8). The five phrases repeat 4:34, *"by signs, by wonders, by
war, by a mighty hand, by an outstretched arm, and by great terrors"*. *"And he has brought us into
this place, and has given us this land, a land flowing with milk and honey"* (9). *Brought out* and
*brought in* are the same pair as the child's answer in 6:21–23: *"he brought us out from there, that
he might bring us in"*. *"This place"*, *hammāqôm hazzeh*, here means the land; the same chapter used
*hammāqôm* in verse 2 for the place Yahweh chooses.

The confession turns on *wǝʿattâ*, *"Now"* (10). *"Behold, I have brought the first of the fruit of
the ground, which you, Yahweh, have given me."* Yahweh *brought* (*wayǝbiʾēnû*, 9) and the farmer
*has brought* (*hēbēʾtî*, 10): the same verb, hiphil of *bôʾ*. For the first time the speaker turns
from speaking *about* Yahweh to speaking *to* him: *"you, Yahweh"*. The gift is the land's first
yield, and the farmer calls it something Yahweh gave him.

**"And worship before Yahweh your God" (26:10).** *Wǝhištaḥăwîtā*, *bow down*. In Deuteronomy this
verb occurs eight times, and this is the only one where the one bowed to is Yahweh. The other seven
are warnings against bowing to other gods or the host of heaven: 4:19; 5:9; 8:19; 11:16; 17:3;
29:26 (WLC 29:25); 30:17 (WLC lemma search; see Notes). Deuteronomy says *do not bow* seven times, and
here, with a basket of fruit, it says *bow*.

**"You shall rejoice" (26:11).** *"You shall rejoice in all the good which Yahweh your God has given to
you, and to your house, you, and the Levite, and the foreigner who is among you."* The rejoicing at
the place, shared with the landless, is the pattern of 12:7, 12, 18; 14:26; and 16:11, 14. See
[[Rejoice Before Yahweh - The Feast Meals of Deuteronomy]]. Here two of the four landless groups are
named, the Levite and the foreigner. The foreigner who joins in has no Israelite father to recite
about, and is at the meal anyway. The text does not say whether he also says the words.

**The third-year tithe (26:12).** *"When you have finished tithing all the tithe of your increase in
the third year, which is the year of tithing, then you shall give it to the Levite, to the foreigner,
to the fatherless, and to the widow, that they may eat within your gates, and be filled."* This is
the law of 14:28–29 seen from its end. There the tithe *"at the end of every three years"* was stored
*"within your gates"*, and the four were to *"eat and be satisfied"*. Here it is done, and the
farmer must say so. *"Be filled"*, *wǝśābēʿû*, is the verb of 14:29 (*"be satisfied"*) and of the
warning in 8:10–12, *"you shall eat and be full"*, with its danger of forgetting. On how this tithe
relates to Leviticus 27 and Numbers 18, see
[[Three Tithe Laws - Leviticus 27, Numbers 18, and Deuteronomy 14]] and [[Tithes for Feasting and Provision]].

**The declaration (26:13–14).** *"You shall say before Yahweh your God"* (13), and what he says is a
list of things done and things not done.

*"I have put away the holy things out of my house."* *"Put away"* is *biʿartî*, the piel of
*bāʿar*. It is the verb of Deuteronomy's refrain *"So you shall remove the evil from among you"*
(13:5 (WLC 13:6); 17:7, 12; 19:19; 21:21; 22:21, 22, 24; 24:7), and of *"you shall put away the
innocent blood from among you"* (21:9; compare 19:13, *"purge the innocent blood"*) (WLC lemma search; see Notes). Everywhere else in
the book it removes guilt from the community. Here the same verb removes something holy from a
house: the tenth that belongs to others is to be out of the farmer's house as completely as evil is
to be out of Israel. The wiki notes the shared verb; the text does not draw the comparison.

*"And also have given them to the Levite, to the foreigner, to the fatherless, and to the widow,
according to all your commandment."* The four are named in full, in the order of 14:29 and 26:12.
See [[Within Your Gates - The Levite, the Foreigner, the Fatherless, and the Widow]]. *"I have not
transgressed any of your commandments, neither have I forgotten them."* *Lōʾ šākāḥtî*, *I have not
forgotten*: the chapter before ended with the command *lōʾ tiškāḥ*, *"You shall not forget"* (25:19),
and this is the answer in the first person.

Then three negatives (14). *"I have not eaten of it in my mourning, neither have I removed any of it
while I was unclean, nor given of it for the dead."* The holy portion has not touched death in any
form. Contact with the dead makes a person unclean (Numbers 19:11), and the unclean may not
eat holy things (Leviticus 7:20; 22:4). Hosea 9:4 calls the sacrifices of a faithless Israel *"the
bread of mourners; all who eat of it will be polluted"* (WEB at bible-api.com). *"Nor given of it for
the dead"* is not explained. It may mean food set out for the dead, or food given to a mourning
household, or offerings in a cult of the dead; the text does not say, and the wiki adopts none of
these (reported as current readings from general knowledge). Deuteronomy 14:1 has already forbidden
the mourning rites of cutting and shaving *"for the dead"*. The tithe that feeds the living poor
must not be used in any of the rites for the dead.

*"I have listened to Yahweh my God's voice. I have done according to all that you have commanded
me."* The word *bǝʾōnî*, *"in my mourning"*, is uncertain: morphhb assigns it the lemma *ʾāwen*,
*trouble* or *iniquity*, which it shares with Numbers 23:21. The WEB's *"mourning"* reads it as the
noun of Benoni, *"son of my sorrow"* (Genesis 35:18). The wiki follows the WEB and records the
lexical question (see Notes).

**"Look down from your holy habitation" (26:15).** The declaration becomes a prayer, the first
imperative the farmer speaks to God: *"Look down from your holy habitation, from heaven, and bless
your people Israel, and the ground which you have given us, as you swore to our fathers, a land
flowing with milk and honey."* *"Look down"* is *hašqîpâ*, hiphil of *šāqap*, *look down from a
height*. The only other verse in the Torah where Yahweh is named as the one who looks down is at the sea,
where he *"looked out on the Egyptian army … and confused the Egyptian army"* (Exodus 14:24) (WLC
lemma search; Genesis 18:16 has *"the men"* as subject). See
[[The Crossing of the Sea]]. There Yahweh looked down to rout an army; here Israel asks him to look
down to bless a field. *Māʿôn*, *"habitation"*, occurs only here in Genesis–Deuteronomy (WLC lemma
search). The book has located Yahweh's name at the chosen place on earth (2), and here locates
Yahweh himself *"from heaven"*. Both are in the same rite, and the text does not reconcile them;
see [[The Dwelling of God]].

The prayer asks for exactly what the third-year tithe gave away: food from the ground. The farmer
has given the tenth to people with no land, and asks Yahweh to bless the land that produced it.
14:29 had promised the same, *"that Yahweh your God may bless you in all the work of your hand which
you do."* That is the link the law makes, not a reading imposed on it.

**What the two rites share.** Each is something the farmer *says*, with the formula *"you shall
answer and say"* (5) or *"you shall say"* (13) *"before Yahweh your God"*. The first speaks of
what Yahweh did; the second, of what the farmer did. The first ends with a gift brought; the second
with a request made. The first has *"we cried … Yahweh heard"*; the second has *"I have listened to
Yahweh my God's voice"*, *šāmaʿtî bǝqôl* (14), the same verb as *wayyišmaʿ … ʾet-qōlēnû* in 7.
Yahweh heard Israel's voice; Israel says it has heard Yahweh's. This pairing is offered as a reading
of the shared verb, not as a structure the text announces. 26:16 turns from the farmer to the whole
people, *"Today Yahweh your God commands you"*, and the boundary is the plan's, kept as drawn.

## People

- [[Moses]] — the speaker, giving Israel the words to say.
- [[Jacob]] — most readers' identification of *"my father … a Syrian ready to perish"* (5); the text
  does not name him.
- The farmer, the priest *"who shall be in those days"* (3), the Levite, the foreigner, the
  fatherless, and the widow: categories, not individuals. No pages.

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — *"He went down into Egypt"* (5); *"Yahweh brought us out of Egypt"* (8).
- [[Aram Naharaim]] — not named, but behind *ʾărammî*, *"a Syrian"* (5).
- The place Yahweh will choose (2) is unnamed, as throughout Deuteronomy. *"This land, a land flowing
  with milk and honey"* (9, 15) is Canaan, not named here.

## Notes

**Verse numbering.** WEB 26:1–15 is WLC 26:1–15. The boundary is the plan's, kept as drawn.

**"First."** *Mērēʾšît* (WLC 26:2), *rēʾšît* (26:10), lemma 7225. In Deuteronomy: 11:12; 18:4; 21:17;
26:2, 10; 33:21. *Bikkûrîm* (lemma 1061) occurs in Genesis–Deuteronomy at Exodus 23:16, 19; 34:22, 26;
Leviticus 2:14; 23:17, 20; Numbers 13:20; 18:13; 28:26, and never in Deuteronomy (WLC lemma search).

**"Basket."** *Ṭeneʾ* (lemma 2935): Deuteronomy 26:2, 4; 28:5, 17; nowhere else in Genesis–Deuteronomy.

**"I profess."** *Higgadtî hayyôm* (WLC 26:3), hiphil perfect 1cs of *nāgad* (lemma 5046); the same form
with *hayyôm* at 30:18.

**"A Syrian ready to perish."** *ʾĂrammî ʾōbēd ʾābî* (WLC 26:5). *ʾĂrammî* (lemma 761) in
Genesis–Deuteronomy: Genesis 25:20 (twice); 28:5; 31:20, 24; Deuteronomy 26:5. *ʾŌbēd* is parsed by morphhb as qal participle of
*ʾābad* (lemma 6). The *wandering* sense and the Laban reading are reported from general knowledge;
the Laban reading is widely associated with the Passover Haggadah (not checked).

**"Lived there," "few," "mighty."** *Wayyāgor* (lemma 1481). *Bimǝtê mǝʿāṭ* (lemmas 4962, 4592) at
Deuteronomy 26:5; 28:62; Deuteronomy 4:27 has *mǝtê mispār* (WLC). *ʿĀṣûm* (lemma 6099) at Exodus 1:9 and in Deuteronomy at 4:38;
7:1; 9:1, 14; 11:23; 26:5.

**"Mistreated … afflicted … hard labor."** *Wayyārēʿû* (WLC 26:6), hiphil of *rāʿaʿ* (lemma 7489); the
same form at Numbers 20:15. *Wayǝʿannûnû*, piel of *ʿānâ* (lemma 6031), as at Genesis 15:13 and
Exodus 1:11–12. *ʿĂbōdâ qāšâ* (lemmas 5656, 7186): Exodus 1:14; 6:9; Deuteronomy 26:6. In Genesis, Leviticus, and
Numbers *qāšâ* occurs only at Genesis 42:7, 30, of Joseph's *"roughly"* spoken words (WLC lemma
search).

**"Cried … heard … saw."** *Wanniṣʿaq* (lemma 6817), *wayyišmaʿ … qōlēnû*: Numbers 20:16 has the same
two forms. *ʿOnyēnû* (lemma 6040); *ʿămālēnû* (lemma 5999), in Genesis–Deuteronomy only Genesis 41:51;
Numbers 23:21; Deuteronomy 26:7; *laḥăṣēnû* (lemma 3906), only Exodus 3:9 and Deuteronomy 26:7 (WLC
lemma search). Numbers 23:21 pairs *ʿāmāl* with *ʾāwen*, the lemma morphhb gives to *bǝʾōnî* in 26:14;
the WEB renders them there *"perverseness"* and *"iniquity"*.

**"Great terror."** *Ûbǝmōrāʾ gādōl* (WLC 26:8), lemma 4172; in Deuteronomy at 4:34; 11:25; 26:8;
34:12.

**"Worship."** *Wǝhištaḥăwîtā* (WLC 26:10), lemma 7812. In Deuteronomy: 4:19; 5:9; 8:19; 11:16; 17:3;
26:10; 29:25 (WEB 29:26); 30:17. All but 26:10 concern other gods or the host of heaven (stored WEB of each verse read).

**"Rejoice."** *Wǝśāmaḥtā* (lemma 8055) in Deuteronomy: 12:7, 12, 18; 14:26; 16:11, 14; 24:5; 26:11;
27:7; 33:18 (WLC lemma search).

**"Tithe."** *Maʿăśēr* (lemma 4643) in Deuteronomy: 12:6, 11, 17; 14:23, 28; 26:12 (twice, *maʿśar* and
*hammaʿăśēr*). *Laʿśēr*, piel infinitive of *ʿāśar* (lemma 6237).

**"Put away."** *Biʿartî* (WLC 26:13, 14), piel of *bāʿar* (lemma 1197). The piel in Deuteronomy:
13:6 (WEB 13:5); 17:7, 12; 19:13, 19; 21:9, 21; 22:21, 22, 24; 24:7; 26:13, 14. The qal participle
*bōʿēr*, *burning*, at 4:11; 5:23; 9:15 is the same lemma in morphhb (WLC lemma search).

**"In my mourning."** *Bǝʾōnî* (WLC 26:14), lemma 205 in morphhb, which elsewhere in
Genesis–Deuteronomy is assigned only at Numbers 23:21. Genesis 35:18's *Ben-ʾônî* is tagged as a proper
name (lemma 1126). The WEB's *"mourning"* is a translation choice, and the lexical identification is
not settled here.

**"Look down."** *Hašqîpâ* (WLC 26:15), hiphil imperative of *šāqap* (lemma 8259). In
Genesis–Deuteronomy: Genesis 18:16; 19:28; 26:8; Exodus 14:24 (hiphil); Numbers 21:20; 23:28 (niphal);
Deuteronomy 26:15. *Mimmǝʿôn qodšǝkā*: *māʿôn* (lemma 4583), only here in Genesis–Deuteronomy.

**"Bless."** *Ûbārēk* (WLC 26:15), piel imperative of *bārak* (lemma 1288). The only other imperative
of the lemma in Deuteronomy is 33:11, *"Yahweh, bless his skills"*, in Moses' blessing of Levi (morphhb
parsing *Vpv2ms*; WLC lemma search).

**What the passage does not settle.** Who *"my father"* is, and whether *ʾōbēd* means perishing or
wandering. Whether the foreigner who rejoices (11) also recites. What *"given of it for the dead"*
refers to. How the tithe of 14:22–27, eaten at the place, relates to the third-year tithe given in
the towns. How the name at the chosen place (2) and the habitation in heaven (15) relate. Each is
recorded, none adopted.
