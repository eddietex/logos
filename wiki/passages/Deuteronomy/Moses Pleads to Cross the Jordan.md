# Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 3:23–29

## Summary

Moses recalls begging Yahweh to let him cross the Jordan and see the good land, the fine mountain,
and Lebanon. His appeal begins with the greatness and strong hand already shown to him: no god
in heaven or earth can perform comparable works (23–25).

Yahweh refuses. Moses says the anger was for the people's sakes; he is told to speak no more
about this request. He may ascend Pisgah and look in all four directions, but may not cross the
Jordan. Instead he must commission, encourage, and strengthen Joshua, who will bring the people
into the inheritance Moses will see (26–28). The unit closes with Israel staying in the valley
near Beth Peor (29).

The contrast is between the leader's denied entry and the people's continuing future. Permission
to see does not revoke exclusion, and exclusion does not end Moses' responsibility for Israel.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, Deuteronomy 3:23–29. Full unit copied verbatim.

> **23** I begged Yahweh at that time, saying,

> **24** “Lord Yahweh, you have begun to show your servant your greatness, and your strong hand. For what god is there in heaven or in earth that can do works like yours, and mighty acts like yours?

> **25** Please let me go over and see the good land that is beyond the Jordan, that fine mountain, and Lebanon.”

> **26** But Yahweh was angry with me for your sakes, and didn’t listen to me. Yahweh said to me, “Let this satisfy you. Speak no more to me of this matter.

> **27** Go up to the top of Pisgah, and lift up your eyes westward, and northward, and southward, and eastward, and see with your eyes; for you shall not go over this Jordan.

> **28** But commission Joshua, and encourage him, and strengthen him; for he shall go over before this people, and he shall cause them to inherit the land which you shall see.”

> **29** So we stayed in the valley near Beth Peor.

## People
- [[God]] — Yahweh’s incomparable power grounds Moses’ appeal (24), but he refuses the crossing and forbids further petition on this matter (26–27). He provides for Israel’s future by ordering Moses to strengthen Joshua (28).
- [[Moses]] — Moses asks to cross and see the good land (23–25), but receives permission to see from Pisgah without crossing (26–27). He attributes Yahweh’s anger to the people’s sakes; Numbers 20:12 instead stresses his and Aaron’s failure to believe and sanctify Yahweh. Both emphases remain explicit. He must prepare Joshua to lead (28).
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — Yahweh commands Moses to commission, encourage, and strengthen Joshua, who will cross before the people and cause them to inherit the land (28). This is a command, not a narrated commissioning ceremony; Numbers 27:22–23 supplies such a ceremony.

## Places
- [[The Jordan]] — The river marks the crossing Moses requests and is refused (25–27). Joshua will lead the people over (28); no crossing happens within this unit.
- [[Pisgah]] — Moses is commanded to ascend its top and look west, north, south, and east, while remaining forbidden to cross the Jordan (27). The command is not an account of an ascent already performed.
- [[Lebanon]] — Lebanon is named as part of the desired prospect beyond the Jordan (25). The verse does not identify the unnamed fine mountain with a particular later sanctuary.
- [[Canaan]] — The good land beyond the Jordan is what Moses longs to enter (25). It will be inherited under Joshua while Moses only sees it (27–28). Canaan is the wider narrative identification, not a name used in these verses.
- [[Beth Peor]] — The closing notice locates Israel’s stay in the valley near Beth Peor (29). It does not say that the people entered the settlement or identify the valley with a particular modern site.

## Themes
- [[Prayer]] — Moses begins with praise of Yahweh’s incomparable works and asks for personal entry into the land (23–25). The refusal is explicit, including a prohibition on further petition about this matter (26). This is a particular denied request, not a rule that prayer is futile.
- [[Judgment]] — Moses’ exclusion stands despite his plea (26–27). His explanation of divine anger for the people’s sakes differs in emphasis from Numbers 20:12, which charges the leaders with failure to believe and sanctify Yahweh; neither account is silently replaced.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Seeing the land and entering it are separated: Moses receives the prospect, Joshua the charge to bring the people into their inheritance (25–28). The people’s future outlasts their present leader’s permitted journey.
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — The mediator petitions for himself here (23–25), unlike his request for a successor in Numbers 27:15–17. His denied request leaves him responsible for encouraging that successor (28). Mediation does not grant exemption from judgment.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The answer gives Moses limits and work: cease this petition, ascend and look, and commission and strengthen Joshua (26–28). These are reported commands; their performance is not narrated here.
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — Moses asks to cross and see, but is commanded to look from Pisgah without crossing (25–27). Sight grants a prospect of the promised land without granting possession.
- [[Call and Election]] — Joshua’s appointment is attached to a defined task: crossing before the people and causing them to inherit (28). Moses’ service continues in preparing the successor who will enter where he cannot.

## Notes

**A tension retained.** Deuteronomy 3:26 repeats the explanation in 1:37 that Yahweh was angry
with Moses for the people's sakes. Numbers 20:12 grounds exclusion in Moses and Aaron's failure
to believe and sanctify Yahweh, and Numbers 27:14 recalls rebellion at Meribah. The people's
provocation and the leaders' responsibility can coexist, but this is a possible synthesis,
not an explanation offered in these seven verses. The different emphases remain visible.

**A refused prayer with an answer.** Not listening in verse 26 means not granting the request;
Yahweh immediately speaks. The command to stop concerns this matter, not every future prayer.
The passage does not report Moses' emotional response or claim that the refusal is reversed.

**Seeing, then succession.** The four-direction view in verse 27 is commanded, not performed
within this unit. Verse 28 likewise commands commissioning without narrating the ceremony.
Numbers 27 narrates a public commission with Eleazar and laying on of hands; Deuteronomy 3
does not repeat those details. Its emphasis is the encouragement Moses must give Joshua.

**Geographical restraint.** The fine mountain of verse 25 remains unnamed here. No identification
with Jerusalem or another later sanctuary is asserted. Beth Peor receives a place page distinct
from [[Peor]], the height named in Numbers 23:28; similar names alone do not establish identical
sites. The valley notice closes this unit without narrating Moses' ascent or death.

**Sources and scope.** The planned boundary and title are retained. Quotations come only from
stored WEB. Comparisons use stored Numbers 20 and 27 and Deuteronomy 1 and 3. No claim about
exact Hebrew wording or quotation from an unstored book is introduced.
