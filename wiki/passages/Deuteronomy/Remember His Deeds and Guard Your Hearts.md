# Remember His Deeds and Guard Your Hearts

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 11:1–17

## Summary

Love and obedience are again commanded (1). Moses then addresses the generation that saw Yahweh's
deeds, not its children: the signs against Pharaoh and Egypt, the drowning of Egypt's chariots in
the Red Sea, the wilderness care, and the earth swallowing Dathan and Abiram (2–7). What they saw
grounds obedience for strength, possession, and long life in the land of milk and honey (8–9).
That land is unlike Egypt, where seed was watered "with your foot"; it drinks rain from the sky,
and Yahweh's eyes are on it all year (10–12). Listening brings the early and late rain, grain,
wine, oil, and grass (13–15). A deceived heart that serves other gods brings anger, a shut sky,
and quick loss of the good land (16–17).

## Notes

### Witnesses, not heirs of a story

Verse 2 reverses the household pattern of [[Hear Israel and Love Yahweh]] and
[[Do Not Forget When You Are Full]], where the children are to be taught. Here Moses insists he
is *not* speaking to the children, but to those whose "eyes have seen" (7). The speech does not
reconcile this with its own frame. Deuteronomy 2:14–16 says "all the generation of the men of
war" died in the wilderness, so many of those addressed would have been children or young at
the sea. The text does not say how many eyewitnesses remained, and the wiki should not
supply a number. It treats the assembly as one people whose memory is a duty. The children
come back into view in the next unit, Deuteronomy 11:19.

"Chastisement" (2) names the discipline already described in
[[Remember the Wilderness and the Giver of Wealth]], where hunger and manna teach as a father
disciplines a son. The sequence that follows counts judgment on Egypt and on rebels inside
Israel as part of the same instruction.

### Dathan and Abiram without Korah

Verse 6 names only Dathan and Abiram, the Reubenites, with their households and tents. Korah,
who leads the rebellion in [[The Rebellion of Korah]], is absent. Numbers 26:10 explicitly
places Korah among those the earth swallowed. Numbers 16:32 is less clear: it lists "all of
Korah’s men", not Korah himself, and 16:35 has fire consume the two hundred fifty. Deuteronomy
adds no new claim about Korah's death; it simply leaves him out. One reading is that a speech
whose point is what *the earth* did cites the two whose fate is described only that way. The
text gives no reason, and the omission is recorded rather than explained. See
[[Korah Died but His Sons Lived - Numbers 16 and 26]].

The Reubenite pair once called Egypt itself "a land flowing with milk and honey"
(Numbers 16:13). Here, three verses after recalling them, Moses gives that phrase back to the
promised land (9) and declares it unlike Egypt (10). The connection page
[[Watered with Your Foot - Egypt and the Land That Drinks the Rain]] takes this up.

### A land that depends on the sky

The contrast in 10–12 is about how the land is watered, not about which land is richer. Egypt's
seed was watered "with your foot". The text does not explain the phrase; it is often taken to
mean foot-worked irrigation channels or devices, but that is an interpretation, not stated
fact. The promised land "drinks water from the rain of the sky" (11). Its fertility therefore
depends directly on Yahweh, whose eyes are on it "from the beginning of the year even to the
end of the year" (12). The same dependence makes it vulnerable: the sky that gives the rain in
its season (14) can be shut (17).

Blessing and curse sit inside one conditional. Listening brings rain, grain, new wine, oil, and
grass (13–15); a deceived heart turning to other gods brings a shut sky and loss of the land
(16–17). Leviticus 26:4, 19 already stated the same pair, rain in season and sky like iron. The
fuller list in Deuteronomy 28:12, 23–24 is verified in the stored WEB but not yet ingested.
Verse 13 moves from Moses' "I command" to a divine "I will give" (14–15) without marking the
change. The WEB shows the shift as it stands. Whether it is Moses quoting Yahweh or a looser
prophetic voice is not settled by the passage.

### Heart and deception

"Lest your heart be deceived" (16) places the danger inside, in what the heart takes for good,
before any outward act of worship. It echoes the warning in [[Do Not Forget When You Are Full]]
that plenty breeds forgetting. Here the plenty is the rain-fed harvest itself. The passage
does not name the gods. Later texts often link Canaanite worship with storm and fertility
deities, but Deuteronomy 11 does not, and no such identification is drawn from it here.
