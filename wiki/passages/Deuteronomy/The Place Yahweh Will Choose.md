# The Place Yahweh Will Choose

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 12:1–28

## Summary

The statutes and ordinances begin. They are for the land, *"all the days that you live on the
earth"* (1). Israel must destroy every place where the dispossessed nations served their gods —
on mountains, on hills, under every green tree — breaking altars, smashing pillars, burning
Asherah poles, cutting down images, and destroying their name from that place (2–3). *"You shall
not do so to Yahweh your God"* (4). Instead Israel is to seek the one place Yahweh will choose
out of all the tribes *"to put his name there"*, and bring there burnt offerings, sacrifices,
tithes, wave offerings, vows, freewill offerings, and firstborn animals, and eat and rejoice
before him with their households (5–7).

The present practice, *"every man whatever is right in his own eyes"*, is tolerated only because
Israel has not yet reached the rest and the inheritance. Once Yahweh gives rest from the enemies
around, everything commanded goes to the chosen place, and the whole household rejoices there,
including servants and the landless Levite (8–12). Burnt offerings may not be offered *"in every
place that you see"* (13–14).

Ordinary meat is another matter. Animals may be killed and eaten in every town, as gazelle and
deer are eaten, by clean and unclean alike — only the blood must be poured on the ground like
water (15–16). Tithes, firstborn, vows, and freewill and wave offerings may not be eaten at home;
they are eaten at the chosen place, with the Levite not forsaken (17–19). The permission is
restated for when the border is enlarged and the place is too far: eat meat *"after all the
desire of your soul"*, but not the blood, *"for the blood is the life"* (20–25). Holy things and
vows still go to the place, where the blood of sacrifices is poured out on the altar (26–27). The
unit closes by calling Israel to hear and do, *"that it may go well with you, and with your
children after you forever"* (28).

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, *"the God of your fathers"* (1), who chooses the place, puts his name there,
  gives rest from enemies, and blesses the work of Israel's hands (5–12, 15).
- [[Moses]] — the speaker. His *"we do here today"* (8) includes himself in the practice that is
  to end.
- [[Levi]] — the Levite *"within your gates"*, without portion or inheritance, who joins the
  household at the feast and must not be forsaken (12, 18–19).
- The fathers, the nations, sons and daughters, and male and female servants are collective; no
  individual is named.

## Notes

### The hinge from exhortation to statutes

Moses has spent 4:44–11:32 calling Israel to love, remember, and choose; 11:32 ended with *"all
the statutes and the ordinances which I set before you today"*, and 12:1 begins *"These are the
statutes and the ordinances"*. The law code (chapters 12–26) therefore opens not with justice or
the poor but with worship: where Yahweh is to be served, and how that differs from the way the
nations served their gods. Rival worship is the subject on both sides of this unit — the
destroyed shrines (2–3) and, in the next unit, the warning not to ask how the nations served
their gods (12:29–31).

### "You shall not do so to Yahweh your God"

Verse 4 is short and governs what follows. The nations worshiped at many sites, chosen by
height and greenery — *"on the high mountains, and on the hills, and under every green tree"*
(2). Israel is not to worship Yahweh that way. The contrast is not between images and no
images (that was 4:15–31) but between many places chosen by the worshiper and one place chosen by
God. Verse 13 makes the same point from the other side: not *"in every place that you see"*.
What the worshiper sees is set against what Yahweh chooses.

The destruction of the nations' *name* from those places (3) answers to the placing of Yahweh's
*name* at his (5, 11, 21). The wiki notes the pairing as it stands in the WEB; no claim is made
here about the Hebrew wording of the two clauses.

### The place is never named

Deuteronomy calls it only *"the place which Yahweh your God shall choose"*, six times in this
unit alone (5, 11, 14, 18, 21, 26), with the variants *"to put his name there"* (5, 21) and *"to
cause his name to dwell there"* (11). Verse 14 adds *"in one of your tribes"*. The book never
says which. Later texts identify places where the name dwelt:

- Joshua 18:1 sets up the Tent of Meeting at Shiloh once *"the land was subdued before them"*
  (WEB, verified from bible-api.com).
- Jeremiah 7:12 calls Shiloh *"my place … where I caused my name to dwell at the first"* (WEB,
  verified from bible-api.com).
- Solomon's prayer speaks of the temple as *"the place of which you have said, 'My name shall be
  there'"* (1 Kings 8:29, WEB, verified from bible-api.com).

So the books that follow treat the formula as applying first to Shiloh and later to Jerusalem.
Whether Deuteronomy itself meant one fixed site, or whichever site Yahweh chose at a given time,
is a long-standing question the text does not settle, and the wiki does not decide it. No
[[Jerusalem]] link is drawn from this page's places list, because the passage does not name it.

### Not yet the rest

Verses 8–10 contain an admission. *"The things that we do here today, every man whatever is right
in his own eyes"* describes Israel's present practice, and Moses includes himself in the
*"we"*. Deuteronomy does not say what those things are. The verse ties the change to a future
condition: rest from all the enemies around, and safety in the inheritance (10). Until then, the
centralized rule is not yet fully in force.

The same phrase becomes the refrain of Judges' closing chapters — *"In those days there was no
king in Israel. Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes"* (Judges 17:6; 21:25, WEB,
verified from bible-api.com). There it describes life in the land, long after the crossing. See
[[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]]. Against it Deuteronomy
sets *"that which is right in Yahweh's eyes"* (25) and *"good and right in Yahweh your God's
eyes"* (28).

The *"rest"* of verse 9 is also picked up later; see
[[Rise Up, Yahweh - The Ark's Cry in Numbers 10, Psalm 68, and Psalm 132]].

### Meat at home: a change from Leviticus 17

Leviticus 17:3–5 required that any bull, lamb, or goat killed *"in the camp, or … outside the
camp"* be brought to the door of the Tent of Meeting as a peace offering; otherwise *"blood shall
be imputed to that man"*. Deuteronomy 12:15 and 20–22 allow animals from the herd and flock to be
killed and eaten *"within all your gates"*, like gazelle and deer, by clean and unclean alike.
Verse 21 gives the reason: the chosen place may be *"too far from you"*.

The two laws are written for different settings — a camp gathered around the tent, and a people
spread across an enlarged land (20). Read in sequence, Deuteronomy adjusts the earlier rule for
the new setting rather than simply repeating it. What carries over unchanged is the blood:
poured on the ground *"like water"* (16, 24), as the hunter of Leviticus 17:13 poured it out and
covered it with dust. Deuteronomy's *"for the blood is the life"* (23) restates Leviticus 17:11,
14 and Genesis 9:4. Interpreters differ over whether the two laws were meant to stand in
sequence, as here, or come from different stages of Israel's law. The wiki records the
difference and the stated reason, and does not settle the compositional question. See
[[One Place or Every Place - Exodus 20.24, Leviticus 17, and Deuteronomy 12]].

The gazelle and deer comparison (15, 22) works because game was never brought to the altar.
Domestic meat eaten at home is treated like game, so the purity of the eater no longer matters
(*"the unclean and the clean may eat of it"*). Holy portions are different: they still require the
place (17–18, 26–27).

### A feast, not only an offering

Each time the offerings are listed, eating and rejoicing follow (7, 12, 18). The worshipers eat
*"before Yahweh"* with sons, daughters, male and female servants, and the Levite. The household
at the feast largely matches the one the Sabbath command of 5:14 named — son, daughter, male and
female servant — with the Levite where 5:14 had the stranger within the gates. Tithes in particular are eaten
by the people who bring them (17–18), which differs from Numbers 18:21–24, where the tithe is
given to the Levites as their inheritance. Deuteronomy 14:22–29 will develop its own tithe rules,
including a third-year tithe for the Levite, foreigner, orphan, and widow. The wiki notes the
difference here and leaves its explanation for that ingest; see [[The Tithe Before the Law]].

### The Levite

The Levite appears three times (12, 18, 19) and is defined by what he lacks: *"no portion nor
inheritance with you"* (12), as in 10:9. He lives *"within your gates"*, in the towns, not at the
sanctuary only, and he depends on the households who bring him to the feast. Verse 19's warning,
*"Be careful that you don't forsake the Levite"*, is the first of Deuteronomy's repeated care
clauses for him.

### Boundary

The planned boundary (12:1–28) is kept. Verse 28 closes with the *"go well with you"* formula
already used at 25, and verse 29 turns from the place of worship to the danger of imitating the
nations' worship, which runs into chapter 13.
