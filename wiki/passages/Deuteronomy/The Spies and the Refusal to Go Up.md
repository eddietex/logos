# The Spies and the Refusal to Go Up

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 1:19–33

## Summary

The march that Horeb commanded (1:6–8) is told in one verse: Israel crossed *"all that great and
terrible wilderness which you saw"* toward the hill country of the Amorites, *"as Yahweh our God
commanded us"*, and came to Kadesh Barnea (19). There Moses repeats the Horeb word almost exactly —
*"Behold, Yahweh your God has set the land before you. Go up, take possession"* — and adds the first
*"Don't be afraid, neither be dismayed"* of the book (20–21).

The people come near, *"everyone of you"*, and ask to send men ahead to search out the way and the
cities; Moses approves and takes twelve, one per tribe (22–23). The men go up as far as the valley of
Eshcol, bring fruit back, and say only one thing: *"It is a good land which Yahweh our God gives to
us"* (24–25). Then the turn: *"Yet you wouldn't go up, but rebelled"* (26). In their tents they say
that Yahweh brought them out of Egypt *because he hated us*, to hand them to the Amorites, and they
quote *"our brothers"* on peoples taller than they, cities *"fortified up to the sky"*, and the sons of
the Anakim (27–28).

Moses answers with three memories set against their fear: Yahweh goes before them and will fight for
them *as he did in Egypt before your eyes* (30); in the wilderness he *"bore you, as a man does bear
his son"* (31); and he went ahead on every stage of the road, *"in fire by night … and in the cloud by
day"*, to find them a place to camp (33). Between the last two stands the verdict: *"Yet in this
thing you didn't believe Yahweh your God"* (32).

This is the same event as [[The Twelve Spies]] and [[The People Refuse the Land]] (Numbers 13–14),
retold forty years later to the sons of those who refused. It is told in the second person — *you*
came near, *you* rebelled, *you* murmured — to a generation most of whom were children or unborn at
Kadesh. The sentence that follows the refusal is the next unit, 1:34–46.

## People
- [[Moses]] — The speaker. He gives the command to go up (20–21), approves the people's plan and picks the twelve (23), and answers their fear (29–31).
- [[God]] — Yahweh *"gives"* the land (20, 25), *"goes before"* the people and *"will fight for you"* (30), *"bore you, as a man does bear his son"* (31), and searched out their camps in fire and cloud (33). The people say he *"hated us"* (27).
- [[The Ten Spies]] — Not separated from the twelve here. Deuteronomy gives the twelve one united report (25), and the fear is quoted as *"our brothers"* (28), with no names and no Caleb or Joshua yet (1:36, 38).
- [[The Anakim]] — *"the sons of the Anakim"* (28), the first use of the plural form in Scripture. See that page.
- [[Caleb (son of Jephunneh)]] — Not in these verses. He is excepted from the sentence at 1:36.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — Not in these verses either. He is named at 1:38.

## Places
- [[Kadesh]] — *"Kadesh Barnea"* (19), where the command to go up is given and refused.
- [[The Valley of Eshcol]] — How far the men went (24). Again the only place they are said to reach.
- [[Horeb]] — The starting point of the march (19).
- [[Egypt]] — Where Yahweh fought for them *"before your eyes"* (30), and what the people say he brought them out of to destroy them (27).
- [[Canaan]] — *"The hill country of the Amorites"* (19–20, 24), the land the twelve searched.

## Themes
- [[Faith]] — *"In this thing you didn't believe Yahweh your God"* (32), said after a list of what they had seen.
- [[Murmuring]] — *"You murmured in your tents"* (27).
- [[Love]] — The people's *"because Yahweh hated us"* (27), answered by the father who carried his son (31).
- [[War]] — *"He will fight for you"* (30); the fortified cities and taller people (28).
- [[The Wilderness]] — *"That great and terrible wilderness"* (19), and the road on which Yahweh carried them (31).
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — *"A good land which Yahweh our God gives to us"* (25), refused (26).
- [[Remembrance]] — Moses argues from memory: Egypt *"before your eyes"* (30), the wilderness *"where you have seen"* (31).
- [[The Fear of God]] — *"Don't be afraid"* (21, 29): the fear of the peoples displaces trust in Yahweh.

## Notes

**Who began it.** Numbers 13:1–2 has Yahweh command the mission; here the people propose it and
Moses agrees: *"The thing pleased me well"* (23). The wiki does not harmonize the two. The usual
reading — the people asked and Yahweh allowed it — is set out with the comparison at
[[Who Sent the Spies - Numbers 13 and Deuteronomy 1]], and is not repeated here. What Deuteronomy adds
is the request's stated purpose: *"the way by which we must go up, and the cities to which we shall
come"* (22). The people ask for a route. They have already been told to go up (21).

**The report is good.** In Numbers 13 the twelve report a land flowing with milk and honey, then the
strength of its people, then an evil report (13:27–33). Deuteronomy gives the spies only one sentence:
*"It is a good land which Yahweh our God gives to us"* (25). The frightening facts appear, but
inside the people's own speech, as something *"our brothers"* said (28). Moses does not call the
spies' words false or the people's fear groundless — the peoples were taller and the cities were
walled. He puts the argument elsewhere: the question was never their size but who goes before Israel
(30).

**"Because Yahweh hated us."** The murmuring in Numbers 14:2–3 asks why Yahweh brings them *"to fall
by the sword"*. Deuteronomy gives the people the harshest form of it: the exodus itself was an act of
hatred, a rescue arranged *"to deliver us into the hand of the Amorites, to destroy us"* (27). Moses
will put almost the same accusation into Egypt's mouth when he intercedes after the calf: *"because he
hated them, he has brought them out to kill them in the wilderness"* (9:28, stored WEB). In both verses
the Hebrew uses the noun *śinʾâ*, hatred — *bəśinʾat* (1:27) and *miśśinʾātô* (9:28) — in the
*Miqra according to the Masorah* text read at Sefaria. Israel at Kadesh says of its God what Moses
fears the nations will say.

**Carried like a son.** Verse 31 answers *hated* with a father: Yahweh *"bore you, as a man does bear
his son, in all the way that you went"*. The verb is *nāśāʾ*, *to lift, carry* — the one Moses used
at 1:9 and 1:12, *"I am not able to bear you myself alone"*, *"How can I myself alone bear"* (Hebrew
read at Sefaria, *Miqra according to the Masorah*). So within one chapter the people are a load Moses
cannot carry and a child Yahweh has carried the whole way. Numbers 11:12 had Moses ask whether he
must carry them *"as a nurse carries a nursing infant"*. See
[[Carried as a Man Carries His Son - Numbers 11.12 and Deuteronomy 1.31]].

**"In this thing."** Verse 32 does not say that Israel did not believe in God generally. It says *"in
this thing"* — in the matter of going up. In the Sefaria Masoretic text the verb is a participle,
*ʾênəkem maʾămînim*, which the WEB renders as a past (*"you didn't believe"*); a participle can
describe an ongoing state, *you were not trusting*, but how much weight that bears is an interpretive
question, not settled here. Numbers 14:11 has Yahweh ask *"how long will they not believe in me, for
all the signs"*; Psalm 106:24 says *"They didn't believe his word"* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com).

**The argument from sight.** Every ground Moses gives is something they saw: the wilderness *"which
you saw"* (19), Egypt *"before your eyes"* (30), the carrying *"where you have seen"* (31). The spies
also saw — fruit, cities, Anakim. Deuteronomy sets two kinds of seeing against each other, and the
people believed the report of what twelve men saw over what all of them had seen.

**The cloud as scout.** Verse 33 says Yahweh went before them *"to seek out a place for you to pitch
your tents in"*. The people wanted men sent ahead to find *"the way by which we must go up"* (22).
Moses' reply is that this had already been done for forty years: they had a guide who found every
camp and showed *"by what way you should go"*. The request for spies sits between two statements that
Yahweh goes before them (30, 33). The connection with the spies' own verb in Numbers 13 is set out at
[[To Seek Out the Land - Tur in Numbers 10, 13, 15, and Ezekiel 20]].

**"He will fight for you."** Verse 30 echoes Exodus 14:14, *"Yahweh will fight for you, and you shall
be still"* (stored WEB), the word spoken at the sea; *"according to all that he did for you in Egypt
before your eyes"* makes the allusion explicit. Deuteronomy 3:22 and 20:4 repeat the promise.

**Who is "you"?** Moses addresses the people in Moab as though they had been at Kadesh. Most of them
were not, or were children: Numbers 14:29–31 condemned everyone counted from twenty years old upward
and spared the *"little ones"*, and Deuteronomy 2:14 will say that *"all the generation of the men
of war"* was consumed in the thirty-eight years after Kadesh. The
second person is deliberate. The sons are spoken to as the people who refused, so that the old
refusal becomes their own choice to make again at the next border.
