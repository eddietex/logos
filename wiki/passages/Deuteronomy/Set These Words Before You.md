# Set These Words Before You

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 11:18–32

## Summary

The words are to be laid up in heart and soul, bound on the hand and between the eyes, taught to
the children through every part of the day, and written on door posts and gates, so that the days
of the people and their children are multiplied in the sworn land (18–21). If Israel keeps the
commandments, loves Yahweh, walks in his ways, and clings to him, he will drive out nations greater
than Israel. Every place the sole of their foot treads will be theirs, from the wilderness and
Lebanon to the Euphrates and the western sea, and no one will stand before them (22–25). Moses then
sets a blessing and a curse before the people: blessing if they listen, curse if they turn from the
way to follow other gods they have not known (26–28). In the land, the blessing is to be set on
Mount Gerizim and the curse on Mount Ebal, which Moses locates beyond the Jordan, near Gilgal and
the oaks of Moreh (29–30). Israel will cross and possess the land, and is to observe all the
statutes and ordinances he sets before them today (31–32).

## Notes

### The words of chapter 6, said again

Verses 18–20 repeat [[Hear Israel and Love Yahweh]] (6:6–9) almost word for word in the WEB, with
three differences visible in English. Chapter 6 says the words "shall be on your heart"; here Israel
is to "lay up these my words in your heart and in your soul" (18), which picks up the heart and soul
of 6:5 and the "heart" of 11:13, 16. The binding on hand and forehead comes *before* the teaching
here, *after* it in chapter 6. And this time a purpose follows: "that your days may be multiplied,
and the days of your children … as the days of the heavens above the earth" (21). The repetition
frames the section: the call to love and the household teaching of chapter 6 return at the close,
just before 12:1 begins "These are the statutes and the ordinances".

The children also come back. At 11:2 Moses said he did not speak "with your children who have not
known". Seventeen verses later they are the ones the words must be taught to (19). The eyewitness
generation is made responsible for handing on what the children did not see. Whether the bound
sign and the door-post writing were meant literally is not settled by the text; later Jewish
practice of tefillin and mezuzot takes them so, and the wiki does not decide it (as on
[[When You Walk and When You Lie Down - Deuteronomy 6.6-9 and Proverbs 6.20-23]]).

### Love, walk, cling — and possession

The condition of 22 gathers terms already used: keep the commandments, love Yahweh, walk in all his
ways, and "cling to him". The same verb in English appears at 10:20 and will return at 30:20,
where clinging to Yahweh is joined to "the length of your days". No claim is made here about the
underlying Hebrew verb, which was not checked in this ingest.

The promise that follows (23–25) is military and territorial. Nations "greater and mightier than
yourselves" will be dispossessed, which picks up 7:1 and 9:1. "Every place on which the sole of
your foot treads shall be yours" (24) is given almost verbatim to Joshua: "I have given you every
place that the sole of your foot will tread on, as I told Moses" (Joshua 1:3, WEB, verified from
bible-api.com). Joshua 1:4 then repeats the border list with "the great sea toward the going down
of the sun" in place of "the western sea". The border of 24 is the maximal one of Genesis 15:18 and
Deuteronomy 1:7: wilderness to Lebanon, and the Euphrates to the sea. See
[[From the River of Egypt to the Euphrates]].

"As he has spoken to you" (25) points back to a promise already given. The closest stored match is
2:25, where Yahweh says, "Today I will begin to put the dread of you and the fear of you on the
peoples". That was said before the defeat of Sihon. The wiki identifies 2:25 as the likely
reference; the verse itself does not say which word it means.

### A blessing and a curse, today

Verse 26 reduces the whole address to two outcomes, and verses 27–28 define them by a single
verb: listen, or do not listen. The curse is specified as turning "out of the way" to "go after
other gods, which you have not known" (28). The English echoes 11:2, where the children "have not
known" the discipline of Yahweh. Gods Israel has no history with are set against the God whose
deeds the people saw. This is the wiki's reading of the WEB wording, not a stated wordplay.

The same pair returns at the end of the covenant in Moab: "I have set before you life and death,
the blessing and the curse. Therefore choose life" (30:19, stored WEB, not yet ingested). See
[[A Blessing and a Curse Set Before You - Deuteronomy 11.26-28 and 30.15-20]].

### Two mountains, and where they are

Verse 29 is the first command to stage the blessing and curse in the land. It gives no reason why
Gerizim takes the blessing and Ebal the curse, and it does not say what is to be done there. The
ceremony is filled out in Deuteronomy 27:11–13, where six tribes stand on each mountain, and carried
out in Joshua 8:33–35. See
[[Blessing on Gerizim and Curse on Ebal - Deuteronomy 11, 27, and Joshua 8]].

Verse 30 locates the mountains with a rhetorical question and five markers:

- **"Beyond the Jordan"** — here the *west* side. At 1:1 the same phrase placed Moses himself "beyond
  the Jordan in the wilderness", on the *east*. The phrase takes its bearing from the speaker or the
  narrator, and the two verses use it in opposite directions.
- **"Behind the way of the going down of the sun"** — westward.
- **"In the land of the Canaanites who dwell in the Arabah"** — the Arabah is the rift valley, while
  Gerizim and Ebal stand in the central hill country. The verse joins them without explanation.
- **"Near Gilgal"** — the hardest marker. Joshua 4:19 places the Gilgal where Israel camps after the
  crossing "on the east border of Jericho" (WEB, verified from bible-api.com), far south of the two
  mountains. Readers have either taken 11:30 as a loose orientation by way of the Jordan-valley road,
  or proposed a second Gilgal near Shechem. The text does not settle it, and the wiki records the
  difficulty without choosing. The [[Gilgal]] page is kept as one page for now and will be split if a
  separate place becomes clear.
- **"Beside the oaks of Moreh"** — the landmark at Shechem where Abram built his first altar in the
  land (Genesis 12:6–7). Genesis has the singular "oak of Moreh"; the WEB here has the plural. The
  wiki does not build anything on the difference. See [[The Call of Abram]] and
  [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]].

Shechem is never named in the verse, but the oaks of Moreh and the two mountains place the ceremony
at the town where Abram first stopped. Deuteronomy's first site in the land, then, is the
patriarchs' first site in the land.

### The hinge into the statutes

Verses 31–32 close the exhortation that began at 4:44. "Set before you today" now appears twice,
the blessing and the curse (26) and the statutes and ordinances (32). Chapter 12 opens with "These
are the statutes and the ordinances which you shall observe to do in the land" (12:1). The book
thus presents the law code to come as the content of the choice just set before Israel.
