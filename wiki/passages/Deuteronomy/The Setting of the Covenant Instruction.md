# The Setting of the Covenant Instruction

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 4:44–49

## Summary

The narrator introduces the law Moses set before Israel: testimonies, statutes, and ordinances
spoken in the setting of the exodus journey. The location is the valley opposite Beth Peor,
east of the Jordan, in Sihon’s former territory. Sihon and Og have already been defeated;
the summary extends their acquired lands from Aroer by the Arnon to Hermon and through the
eastern Arabah to its sea below Pisgah. The geographical frame connects instruction with
Israel’s recent history before Moses resumes direct speech in 5:1.

## Key verses

> **4:44** This is the law which Moses set before the children of Israel.

> **4:45** These are the testimonies, and the statutes, and the ordinances, which Moses spoke to the children of Israel, when they came out of Egypt,

> **4:46** beyond the Jordan, in the valley over against Beth Peor, in the land of Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived at Heshbon, whom Moses and the children of Israel struck, when they came out of Egypt.

> **4:47** They took his land in possession, and the land of Og king of Bashan, the two kings of the Amorites, who were beyond the Jordan toward the sunrise;

> **4:48** from Aroer, which is on the edge of the valley of the Arnon, even to Mount Sion (also called Hermon),

> **4:49** and all the Arabah beyond the Jordan eastward, even to the sea of the Arabah, under the slopes of Pisgah.

## People

- [[Moses]] — The narrator identifies Moses as the speaker who sets the law before Israel (44–45), and recalls Moses and Israel striking Sihon (46).
- [[Sihon]] — His former land contains the valley where Moses speaks.
- [[Og]] — His former kingdom of Bashan is included with Sihon’s territory in Israel’s acquired land (47).

Israel and the Amorites are collective peoples here; no new individual is introduced.

## Notes

### A heading, not another campaign

The third-person narrative identifies the instruction and its setting. The boundary 4:44–49
is retained because 5:1 begins Moses’ summons and direct speech. The victories are recalled
as accomplished events, not repeated. The phrase about coming out of Egypt in 45–46 compresses
the journey: 1:3–4 explicitly dates the address to the fortieth year, after the victories.
These are different levels of chronological detail, not competing dates for the battle.

### Names and limits

Mount Sion is expressly glossed as Hermon in the stored WEB (48), so it belongs on the Hermon
page. It is not identified here with Jerusalem’s Zion. The sea is identified by comparison
with 3:17. No modern coordinates or source-language etymologies are asserted. The title’s
covenant language describes the surrounding discourse; these verses do not report a new
covenant ceremony. No contradiction is newly established by this short framing notice.
