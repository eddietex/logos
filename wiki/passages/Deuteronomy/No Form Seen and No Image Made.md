# No Form Seen and No Image Made

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 4:15–31

## Summary

Moses draws the first command out of the memory of Horeb. Because Israel *saw no kind of form* on
the day Yahweh spoke from the fire (15), they must not corrupt themselves by making a carved image in
the form of any figure: male or female, land animal, winged bird, creeping thing, or fish in the water
under the earth (16–18). Nor may they look up at the sun, moon, and stars, *all the army of the sky*,
and be drawn away to worship and serve them. Those Yahweh *has allotted to all the peoples under the
whole sky* (19). Israel is different: Yahweh took them out of *the iron furnace*, Egypt, to be his
*people of inheritance* (20).

Moses then sets his own fate between the warning and its repetition. Yahweh was angry with him for
Israel's sake and swore that he would not cross; he must die in this land, while they go over and
possess *that good land* (21–22). So they must not forget the covenant and make an image, *for Yahweh
your God is a devouring fire, a jealous God* (23–24).

The last movement looks generations ahead. When Israel has children and grandchildren and has been
long in the land, and makes an image and provokes Yahweh, Moses calls heaven and earth as witnesses
that they will perish from the land, be scattered among the peoples, and be left few (25–27). There
they will serve gods of wood and stone, *the work of men's hands*, which neither see, hear, eat, nor
smell (28). Yet from there they will seek Yahweh and find him, if they search with all heart and
soul (29). *In the latter days*, under oppression, they will return and listen to his voice, because
Yahweh is a merciful God who will not fail them, destroy them, or forget the covenant sworn to their
fathers (30–31).

The unit thus runs from a formless voice, to forbidden forms, to gods that cannot hear, and back to a
voice Israel will listen to again. The fire that devours (24) and the mercy that remembers (31) are
both said of the same God within eight verses.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, Deuteronomy 4:15–31. Full unit copied verbatim.

> **15** Be very careful, for you saw no kind of form on the day that Yahweh spoke to you in Horeb out of the middle of the fire,

> **16** lest you corrupt yourselves, and make yourself a carved image in the form of any figure, the likeness of male or female,

> **17** the likeness of any animal that is on the earth, the likeness of any winged bird that flies in the sky,

> **18** the likeness of anything that creeps on the ground, the likeness of any fish that is in the water under the earth;

> **19** and lest you lift up your eyes to the sky, and when you see the sun and the moon and the stars, even all the army of the sky, you are drawn away and worship them, and serve them, which Yahweh your God has allotted to all the peoples under the whole sky.

> **20** But Yahweh has taken you, and brought you out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be to him a people of inheritance, as it is today.

> **21** Furthermore Yahweh was angry with me for your sakes, and swore that I should not go over the Jordan, and that I should not go in to that good land, which Yahweh your God gives you for an inheritance;

> **22** but I must die in this land. I must not go over the Jordan; but you shall go over, and possess that good land.

> **23** Be careful, lest you forget the covenant of Yahweh your God, which he made with you, and make yourselves a carved image in the form of anything which Yahweh your God has forbidden you.

> **24** For Yahweh your God is a devouring fire, a jealous God.

> **25** When you shall father children, and children’s children, and you shall have been long in the land, and shall corrupt yourselves, and make a carved image in the form of anything, and shall do that which is evil in Yahweh your God’s sight, to provoke him to anger;

> **26** I call heaven and earth to witness against you today, that you will soon utterly perish from off the land which you go over the Jordan to possess it. You will not prolong your days on it, but will utterly be destroyed.

> **27** Yahweh will scatter you among the peoples, and you will be left few in number among the nations, where Yahweh will lead you away.

> **28** There you shall serve gods, the work of men’s hands, wood and stone, which neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell.

> **29** But from there you shall seek Yahweh your God, and you shall find him, when you search after him with all your heart and with all your soul.

> **30** When you are in oppression, and all these things have come on you, in the latter days you shall return to Yahweh your God, and listen to his voice.

> **31** For Yahweh your God is a merciful God. He will not fail you, neither destroy you, nor forget the covenant of your fathers which he swore to them.

## People
- [[God]] — Spoke at Horeb out of the fire with no form seen (15). He has allotted the sky's lights to all peoples (19) but took Israel out of Egypt as his own inheritance (20). He swore Moses would not cross (21). He is *a devouring fire, a jealous God* (24), and will scatter an idolatrous Israel (27). Yet he is found by those who seek him with all heart and soul (29), and is *a merciful God* who will not forget the covenant with the fathers (31).
- [[Moses]] — Warns against images and the worship of the sky (15–19). For the third time in Deuteronomy he reports Yahweh's anger with him *for your sakes* (21; compare 1:37, 3:26), and now adds that Yahweh *swore* it. *I must die in this land* (22) is the first time Deuteronomy has him say outright that he will die east of the Jordan. He calls heaven and earth to witness (26).

## Places
- [[Horeb]] — The day Yahweh spoke from the fire is the ground of the ban on images: no form was seen there (15).
- [[Egypt]] — Called *the iron furnace* from which Yahweh took Israel to be his inheritance (20).
- [[The Jordan]] — The line Moses must not cross and Israel will (21–22); Israel's future ruin is perishing from the land *which you go over the Jordan to possess* (26).

## Themes
- [[Idolatry]] — The full Deuteronomic statement of the image ban: no likeness of human, beast, bird, creeping thing, or fish (16–18), no worship of the sun, moon, and stars (19). In exile, the gods served are wood and stone that neither see, hear, eat, nor smell (28).
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — *You saw no kind of form* (15) forbids making what was not seen. Lifting the eyes to the sky can lead to being *drawn away* (19), and exile's gods neither see nor hear (28).
- [[Creation]] — The forbidden likenesses list the creatures of Genesis 1 — male and female, beasts, birds, creeping things, fish, and the lights of the sky. See [[The Likeness of Any Figure - Deuteronomy 4.16-19, Genesis 1, and Romans 1.23]].
- [[Image of God]] — Genesis 1:27 makes humanity *male and female* in God's image; Deuteronomy 4:16 forbids an image of God *the likeness of male or female*. The image God made may not be turned into an image of God.
- [[The Nations]] — The sun, moon, and stars are what Yahweh *has allotted to all the peoples* (19), set against Israel as *a people of inheritance* (20). Exile scatters Israel among the peoples and nations (27).
- [[Covenant]] — Forgetting the covenant is equated with making an image (23). Yahweh will not forget *the covenant of your fathers which he swore to them* (31).
- [[The Names of God]] — *A devouring fire, a jealous God* (24) and *a merciful God* (31). Exodus 34:14 gives *Jealous* as a name.
- [[Judgment]] — Heaven and earth as witnesses; utter perishing from the land, scattering, and a remnant *few in number* (26–27).
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Moses dies outside it (21–22); Israel will possess it, can lose it by long-settled idolatry (25–26), and is promised return to Yahweh — not, in these verses, to the land (29–31).
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — *Yahweh your God is a merciful God* (31), the ground of the return.
- [[Hope]] — From exile Israel will seek and find (29) and return *in the latter days* (30). The promise rests on Yahweh's mercy and oath, not Israel's record (31).
- [[Remembrance]] — Israel must not forget the covenant (23); Yahweh will not forget it (31).
- [[Redemption]] — Yahweh *has taken you, and brought you out of the iron furnace* (20).
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — Moses' exclusion is *for your sakes* (21): the mediator bears a cost tied to the people.
- [[Language and Speech]] — From the voice at Horeb (15) to gods that cannot hear (28) to Israel listening *to his voice* again (30).

## Notes

**The argument from absence.** Verse 15 does not say images are forbidden because God has no form; it
says Israel *saw* no form. The ban rests on what happened at Horeb, the event 4:12 has just recalled.
An image would claim to show what Yahweh did not show. This keeps the commandment tied to revelation as
heard (12) rather than to an abstract doctrine of God; the passage itself does not say what God is or
is not like.

**Images of Yahweh, or other gods?** Verses 16–18 are framed by Horeb (15) and so most naturally forbid
images meant to represent Yahweh, the calf at Horeb being the obvious case (Exodus 32:4–5, 8, where the
calf is hailed as *your gods, Israel, which brought you up out of the land of Egypt* and a feast
*to Yahweh* is proclaimed).
Verse 19 then moves to worshiping other beings, the heavenly bodies. Verse 28 has Israel serving *gods*
in exile. The unit therefore covers both: a false image of the true God and the worship of what is not
God. It does not draw a sharp line between them, and the wiki does not either.

**The list runs backward through creation.** Male and female, land animals, birds, creeping things,
fish, then sun, moon, and stars. Genesis 1 makes the lights on day four (1:14–18), water creatures and
birds on day five (1:20–21), land animals, creeping things, and man *male and female* on day six
(1:24–27). Deuteronomy's order is close to the reverse, though not exact (birds come before creeping
things). Whether the reversal is intended is not stated; it is noted here as a resemblance of the
English lists. See [[The Likeness of Any Figure - Deuteronomy 4.16-19, Genesis 1, and Romans 1.23]].

**Allotted to the peoples.** Verse 19 says the sky's host is what *Yahweh your God has allotted to all
the peoples under the whole sky*. The verse can be read as saying the lights are given to every people
as lights (Genesis 1:14–18), or as saying the nations' worship of them is in some sense permitted to
them but not to Israel. The verse does not settle which. The contrast in verse 20 is clear either way:
the nations have the sky; Yahweh has Israel as *a people of inheritance*. Deuteronomy 32:8–9 (stored
WEB, not yet ingested) has the same pairing: the Most High gives the nations their inheritance, and
*Jacob is the lot of his inheritance*.

**The iron furnace.** Egypt is called *the iron furnace* (20). 1 Kings 8:51 uses the same phrase in
Solomon's prayer, *from the middle of the iron furnace* (WEB, verified via bible-api.com
2026-11-05). The image is of a smelting furnace, not the oven of Genesis 15:17. The Hebrew vocabulary
for that contrast is discussed on [[Smoking Furnace and Flaming Torch - Fire as Theophany]]; no Hebrew
claim is made on this page.

**Moses' third account of his exclusion.** Deuteronomy 1:37 and 3:26 report Yahweh's anger with Moses
*for your sakes*; 4:21 adds that Yahweh *swore*. Numbers 20:12 gives the reason as Moses' and Aaron's
failure to believe and sanctify Yahweh at Meribah. Deuteronomy does not name that reason here. The two
accounts give different explanations without contradicting each other; the difference is recorded on
[[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]] and not reargued here. What 4:21–22 adds is placement: Moses sets
his death between two warnings against images, so the man who will not cross is the one charging those
who will.

**Devouring fire, jealous God.** Verse 24's WEB is *a devouring fire*; Hebrews 12:29's WEB is *our God
is a consuming fire* (verified via bible-api.com 2026-11-05). The wiki's earlier fire-theophany
connection had quoted *consuming* for Deuteronomy 4:24 and is corrected in this pass. *A jealous God*
matches the image commandment's ground in Exodus 20:5 and the name *Jealous* in Exodus 34:14.

**Fire and mercy.** The same Yahweh is *a devouring fire, a jealous God* (24) and *a merciful God* who
*will not … destroy you* (31). Verse 26 had said Israel *will utterly be destroyed* from the land. The
destruction is from the land; verse 31 denies a destruction of the people. Read so, the two are not in
contradiction, but the passage does leave both on the page without explaining how they meet.

**Gods that cannot hear.** Verse 28's gods *neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell*. The verse turns
the Horeb premise around: Israel heard a God it did not see; in exile it will serve gods that can be
seen but cannot hear. Later polemics against idols make the same list of failed senses (for example
Psalm 115:4–7 and 135:15–17); they are cited here by reference only, not quoted.

**Seek and find.** Verse 29 promises that Israel *shall find him, when you search after him with all
your heart and with all your soul*. Jeremiah 29:13 gives Yahweh's promise to the exiles in close English
wording: *You shall seek me, and find me, when you search for me with all your heart* (WEB, verified via
bible-api.com 2026-11-05). This is a resemblance of WEB wording; no claim is made about the Hebrew. The
*heart and soul* pair will come back in 6:5 (stored WEB, not yet ingested).

**Is the return conditional?** Verse 29 is framed by *when you search*; verse 30 states that *you shall
return*; verse 31 rests it on Yahweh's mercy and oath. Like Leviticus 26:40–45, the text holds Israel's
seeking and Yahweh's remembering together without making one the cause of the other. See
[[If They Confess Their Iniquity - Leviticus 26.40-45, 1 Kings 8, Daniel 9, and Nehemiah 9]]. Unlike
Deuteronomy 30:1–5, these verses promise return *to Yahweh*, not
explicitly a return to the land.

**Heaven and earth as witnesses.** Moses calls them at 4:26 and again at 30:19, in
[[The Word Is Near and the Choice Is Life]], and at 31:28 (stored WEB, not yet ingested). Here they
witness a warning; at 30:19 they witness the choice between life and death, and 30:17–18 repeats this
unit's *drawn away* (19) and its threat of perishing and short days across the Jordan (26).

**Sources and scope.** The planned boundary (4:15–31) and title are kept. Verse 15's *Be very careful*
opens a new movement after the Horeb memory; verse 32's *For ask now of the days that are past* opens
the doxological close. Quotations are from stored WEB Deuteronomy 1, 3, 4, 30, 31, 32; Genesis 1; Exodus
20, 32, 34; and Leviticus 26. 1 Kings 8:51, Jeremiah 29:13, and Hebrews 12:29 were verified against
bible-api.com's WEB on 2026-11-05. Psalms 115 and 135 are cited by reference only. No claim about exact
Hebrew or Greek wording is made on this page.
