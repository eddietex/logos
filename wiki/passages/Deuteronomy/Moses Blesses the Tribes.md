# Moses Blesses the Tribes

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 33:1–29

## Summary

Moses blesses the children of Israel before his death. Yahweh’s coming from Sinai, Seir,
and Paran frames law as the assembly’s inheritance. The tribal sayings ask for survival,
help, faithful priestly service, shelter, fertility, strength, and secure possession.
Joseph’s blessing names Ephraim and Manasseh; Zebulun and Issachar share a saying; Simeon
receives none. The closing praise gathers the tribes into one people sheltered and saved
by the incomparable God, whose everlasting arms sustain them.

## Notes

**A blessing between sentence and death.** Chapter 32 ends with Moses’ exclusion from the land.
Chapter 33 does not reverse that sentence; he still blesses the people whose entry he will
not share. The chapter’s confidence also stands beside the song’s prediction of apostasy.
Neither outlook should be silently substituted for the other.

**Tribes, not living patriarchs.** The ancestral names address descendants. Jacob, Israel,
and Jeshurun designate the people. Simeon’s absence is not a narrated extinction or an
explained punishment. There are ten tribal saying units: Zebulun and Issachar are paired,
and Ephraim and Manasseh appear within Joseph. The eleven named sons of Jacob are thus
not eleven separate sayings.

**Levi and the moral tension.** Genesis 49:5–7 condemns Simeon and Levi’s cruel anger.
Here Levi’s loyalty, teaching, and altar service receive approval (8–11). Exodus 32:26–29
provides a plausible background for loyalty above kin, but this poem does not explicitly
name the calf. Its godly one and the relationship between its testing language and the
Meribah narratives remain interpretive questions.

**Poetry is not a map.** Seir and Paran accompany Sinai in a vision of divine coming.
Bashan belongs to Dan’s lion image; the mountain of verse 19 is unnamed. Neither Benjamin’s
shoulders nor Naphtali’s west and south supplies enough information to fix a sanctuary or
complete boundary. Sinai is linked under the existing Horeb page, Seir under Edom.

**Translation and limits.** These notes follow the stored WEB, including its fiery law (2),
prayer that Reuben’s men not be few (6), and bars of iron and brass (25). No source-language
edition was consulted, so no claim about exact Hebrew wording or textual variants is made.
The referent of the king in verse 5 is left open. Joseph’s blessing recalls the burning bush
(16; Exodus 3) without identifying Joseph as present at that event.
