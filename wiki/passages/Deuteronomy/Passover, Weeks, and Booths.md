# Passover, Weeks, and Booths

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 16:1–17

## Summary

Deuteronomy's festival calendar has three feasts and one rule for all of them: keep each *"in the
place which Yahweh shall choose"*.

**Passover and unleavened bread (16:1–8).** *"Observe the month of Abib, and keep the Passover to
Yahweh your God; for in the month of Abib Yahweh your God brought you out of Egypt by night"* (16:1).
The animal is *"of the flock and the herd"* and is sacrificed at the chosen place (2). No leavened
bread is eaten with it. For seven days Israel eats unleavened bread, *"the bread of affliction; for
you came out of the land of Egypt in haste"*, so that the day of leaving is remembered all of life
(3). No yeast is to be seen anywhere in Israel's borders for seven days, and none of the evening's
meat stays until morning (4). Then comes the rule this chapter adds to the older laws: *"You may not
sacrifice the Passover within any of your gates"*, only at the chosen place, *"at evening, at the
going down of the sun"* (5–6). The meal is cooked and eaten there, and *"In the morning you shall
return to your tents"* (7). Six days of unleavened bread follow, and the seventh is *"a solemn
assembly to Yahweh your God"* with no work (8).

**The feast of weeks (16:9–12).** Seven weeks are counted from the day the sickle is first put to the
standing grain (9). The feast is kept *"with a tribute of a freewill offering of your hand"*, given
*"according as Yahweh your God blesses you"* (10). Everyone rejoices before Yahweh at the place: son
and daughter, male and female servant, the Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow (11).
*"You shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt"* (12).

**The feast of tents (16:13–15).** It is kept seven days *"after you have gathered in from your
threshing floor and from your wine press"* (13), with the same household and the same dependants
rejoicing (14). Yahweh will bless all Israel's increase and work, *"and you shall be altogether
joyful"* (15).

**Summary rule (16:16–17).** *"Three times in a year all of your males shall appear before Yahweh
your God in the place which he chooses"*, at the three feasts, and *"They shall not appear before
Yahweh empty"* (16). Each gives *"as he is able, according to Yahweh your God's blessing which he has
given you"* (17).

The calendar is the fourth in the Torah, after Exodus 23:14–17, Exodus 34:18–24, and Leviticus 23,
with the offerings numbered in Numbers 28–29. It is also the shortest. It gives no day of the month
for any feast, no sheaf or loaves, no booths to live in, no eighth day, and no list of animals. What
it adds is the place, the household and its dependants at the table, and joy, which it commands
three times.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, who brought Israel out of Egypt by night (1), chooses the place where his name
  dwells (2, 6, 11), blesses the harvest and the work of Israel's hands (10, 15, 17), and before whom
  Israel rejoices and appears (11, 16).
- [[Moses]] — the speaker, unnamed in these verses; the address that began at 12:1 continues.
- The son, daughter, servants, Levite, foreigner, fatherless, and widow are categories, not named
  individuals; none gets a page. See
  [[Within Your Gates - The Levite, the Foreigner, the Fatherless, and the Widow]].

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — the place Israel came out of, *"by night"* (1) and *"in haste"* (3), and where it was a
  slave (12).
- The chosen place (2, 6, 7, 11, 15, 16) — named six times in seventeen verses and still unnamed. The
  wiki gives it no place page; see [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]].
- *"Within any of your gates"* (5) — Israel's own towns, where the Passover may not be sacrificed.

## Notes

### Abib, and no day

*Ḥōdeš hāʾābîb*, *the month of Abib* (WLC 16:1, morphhb), is named twice in the verse. *ʾĀbîb* means
the ripening ear of grain: in the Torah it is the barley *"in the ear"* struck by hail (Exodus 9:31),
the first-fruits *"grain in the ear"* of the meal offering (Leviticus 2:14), and the month at
Exodus 13:4; 23:15; 34:18 and here (WLC lemma search across Genesis–Deuteronomy, morphhb). Exodus 12:6
and Leviticus 23:5 date the Passover to the fourteenth day of the first month. **Deuteronomy names the
month and gives no day.** It gives no date for weeks or tents either. Weeks is counted from the first
cutting of the grain (9), and tents follows the gathering from threshing floor and wine press (13).
This is a calendar of the farm year, not of numbered days. The wiki does not read this as a denial of
the dates in Leviticus, since the text does not say whether it assumes them.

### By night

*"Yahweh your God brought you out of Egypt by night"* — *lāylâ* (WLC 16:1, morphhb). Exodus 12:31 has
Pharaoh call Moses and Aaron *"by night"*, and 12:42 calls it *"a night to be much observed to Yahweh
for bringing them out"*. Numbers 33:3, in the stored WEB, says *"on the next day after the Passover,
the children of Israel went out with a high hand"* — *mimmāḥŏrat happesaḥ* (WLC Numbers 33:3,
morphhb). The two can be read together: the release came at night and the march by day. Or they can
be read as two ways of telling the event. The texts do not join them, and the wiki records both.

### Of the flock and the herd

*Ṣōʾn ûbāqār* (WLC 16:2, morphhb). Exodus 12:5 limited the Passover animal to *"the sheep, or … the
goats"*. Deuteronomy adds cattle. Some readers take *the herd* here to mean the other festival
sacrifices of the seven days, not the Passover animal itself. The verse does not make that
distinction. The difference is already in the table on [[The Passover]] and is not resolved here.

### The bread of affliction

*Leḥem ʿōnî* (WLC 16:3, morphhb). The noun *ʿŏnî*, *affliction*, occurs nine times in the Torah:
Genesis 16:11 (Hagar), 29:32 (Leah), 31:42 (Jacob), 41:52 (Joseph); Exodus 3:7, 17; 4:31; here; and
Deuteronomy 26:7 (WLC lemma search across Genesis–Deuteronomy, morphhb). In six of the other eight it
is something God sees or hears, as at 26:7: *"Yahweh heard our voice, and saw our affliction"*. In the
remaining two it is a condition left behind: Joseph's *"land of my affliction"* (Genesis 41:52) and
*"the affliction of Egypt"* (Exodus 3:17). **Only here is it something Israel eats.** The verse gives the reason: *"for you came
out of the land of Egypt in haste"*. The bread is the bread of the night of leaving, when the dough
had no time to rise (Exodus 12:39). Whether *affliction* names the slavery before, or the hurried
flight itself, the verse does not say. See [[Affliction and Flight - Hagar and the Exodus]].

*Bəḥippāzôn*, *in haste*, occurs in the Torah only here and at Exodus 12:11 (WLC lemma search,
morphhb). It was already noted on [[The Passover]].

### Seven days and six days

Verse 3 commands unleavened bread *"seven days"*, and verse 4 no yeast *"seven days"*. Verse 8 says
*"Six days you shall eat unleavened bread. On the seventh day shall be a solemn assembly"*. Exodus
13:6 and Leviticus 23:6–8 count seven days with the seventh holy. Two readings are common. One counts
the Passover evening as the first day and the six as the rest. The other takes six days of eating
plus a seventh day of assembly on which unleavened bread is still eaten. The text does not settle it.

The seventh day is an *ʿăṣeret* (WLC 16:8, morphhb). The word occurs in the Torah only three times:
here, at Leviticus 23:36, and at Numbers 29:35 (WLC lemma search across Genesis–Deuteronomy, morphhb).
In both of the others it is the eighth day after the feast of booths. **Deuteronomy uses for the last
day of unleavened bread the word the other books keep for the last day of booths,** and gives booths
no eighth day at all. The WEB renders it *"solemn assembly"* in all three places.

### Cooked at the place, and home in the morning

*Ûbiššaltā wəʾākaltā*, *and you shall cook* (or *boil*) *and eat* (WLC 16:7, morphhb), uses the verb
*bāšal* that Exodus 12:9 forbids for the Passover. The WEB renders it *"roast"*. The question is set
out on [[The Passover]] and not decided here.

*"In the morning you shall return to your tents"* — *ləʾōhālêkā* (WLC 16:7, morphhb), the ordinary
word for a tent. It is not the *sukkōt* of the feast of tents in 16:13; the WEB uses *"tents"* for
both words. The phrase may mean home, as *"to your tents"* often does, or the pilgrims' lodgings near
the place. If it means home, the remaining days of unleavened bread are kept in the towns. The text
does not say which, and the reading affects how verses 7 and 8 fit together.

### The sickle and the count

*"From the time you begin to put the sickle to the standing grain"* (9). *Ḥermēš*, *sickle*, occurs
in the Torah only here and at Deuteronomy 23:25, *"you shall not move a sickle to your neighbor's
standing grain"* (WLC 16:9; 23:26 Hebrew numbering, morphhb). Leviticus 23:15 counts *"from the next
day after the Sabbath, from the day that you brought the sheaf of the wave offering"*. Deuteronomy
mentions no sheaf and no Sabbath. Its count starts in the field.

*Missat nidbat yādəkā*, *the tribute of a freewill offering of your hand* (WLC 16:10, morphhb).
*Missâ* occurs nowhere else in Genesis–Deuteronomy (morphhb lemma search). It is commonly said to
occur nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible, which this pass did not check beyond the Torah. Its meaning,
*measure* or *sufficiency*, is usually taken from the context and from Aramaic. The amount is not
set. Like the servant's gift at 15:14, it is measured by blessing: *"according as Yahweh your God
blesses you"*.

### Who rejoices

*"You shall rejoice before Yahweh your God: you, your son, your daughter, your male servant, your
female servant, the Levite …, the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow"* (11). Chapter 12 had the
household and the Levite rejoice at the place (12:12, 18). The third-year tithe of 14:29 brought the
Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow to the table in the towns. **16:11 and 14 join
the two lists.** The foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow now eat at the place itself. See
[[Within Your Gates - The Levite, the Foreigner, the Fatherless, and the Widow]].

The list names no wife. Readers commonly take the singular *"you"* (*ʾattâ*) as the household's
married head, husband and wife together. Others note that the law addresses the man, as in 16:16.
The text names son, daughter, and female servant but no wife, and does not explain the omission.

*Śāmaḥ*, *rejoice*, occurs ten times in Deuteronomy (WLC lemma search, morphhb). Eight of these are
meals or feasts before Yahweh: 12:7, 12, 18; 14:26; 16:11, 14; 26:11 at the place, and 27:7 at the
altar on Mount Ebal. The others are 24:5, the
newly married man who stays home *"to cheer his wife"*, and 33:18. Verse 15 adds the adjective
*śāmēaḥ*, *"altogether joyful"* (*ʾak śāmēaḥ*), which occurs in the Torah only here (WLC lemma
search, morphhb). See [[Rejoice Before Yahweh - The Feast Meals of Deuteronomy]].

### The feast of tents without the tents

*Ḥag hassukkōt* (WLC 16:13, morphhb). The WEB has *"feast of tents"* here and *"temporary shelters"*
at Leviticus 23:42, for the same word *sukkâ*. In the Torah the noun first names the shelters Jacob
built for his livestock at [[Succoth (Transjordan)]] (Genesis 33:17). Leviticus 23:42–43 has Israel live in the
shelters for seven days so that later generations know Yahweh *"made the children of Israel to dwell
in temporary shelters"* when he brought them out of Egypt. **Deuteronomy keeps the name and does not
mention living in shelters.** The reason it gives for the feast is the harvest: *"after you have
gathered in from your threshing floor and from your wine press"* (13). Its exodus memory is attached
to weeks instead (12). Deuteronomy 31:10–13 will make this feast, in the year of release, the time
the law is read to all Israel.

### Your males, at the place, not empty

*Kol-zəkûrəkā*, *all your males* (WLC 16:16, morphhb). *Zəkûr* occurs in the Torah only here, at
Exodus 23:17 and 34:23 in the same law, and at Deuteronomy 20:13 (WLC lemma search across
Genesis–Deuteronomy, morphhb). Exodus 23:17 and 34:23 have *"before the Lord Yahweh"*. Deuteronomy
adds *"in the place which he chooses"* and names the three feasts. The male duty to appear stands
beside the daughters, female servants, and widows who rejoice at 11 and 14. The first is a duty, the
second a welcome, and neither cancels the other.

*Wəlōʾ yērāʾeh … rêqām*, *"They shall not appear before Yahweh empty"* (WLC 16:16, morphhb), is the
*rêqām* of Exodus 23:15 and 34:20, and of the servant who is not to be sent away empty (15:13). See
[[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]]. Verse 17 sets the measure: *ʾîš kəmattənat
yādô*, *each man according to the gift of his hand*, *kəbirkat YHWH*, *according to the blessing of
Yahweh* (WLC 16:17, morphhb).

### The place six times

*"The place which Yahweh … shall choose"* comes six times in the chapter's first seventeen verses (2,
6, 7, 11, 15, 16). Three of them add *ləšakkēn šəmô šām*, *to cause his name to dwell there* (2, 6,
11), the phrase of 12:11 and 14:23 (WLC, morphhb). **The Passover is the clearest change.** Exodus 12
made it the one rite kept at every house door. Here it is the one rite expressly forbidden in the
towns: *"You may not sacrifice the Passover within any of your gates"* (5). See
[[One Place or Every Place - Exodus 20.24, Leviticus 17, and Deuteronomy 12]].

### Boundary

The planned boundary (16:1–17) is kept. The title is written *Passover, Weeks, and Booths*, with the
commas the plan's draft lacked. 16:18 turns to judges and officers in the gates, a new subject, and
16:16–17 closes the calendar with its own summary.
