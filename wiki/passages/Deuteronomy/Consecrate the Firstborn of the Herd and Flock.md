# Consecrate the Firstborn of the Herd and Flock

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 15:19–23

## Summary

*"You shall dedicate all the firstborn males that are born of your herd and of your flock to Yahweh
your God"* (15:19). Because the firstling belongs to Yahweh, the owner may not use it: no work with
the firstborn of the herd, no shearing of the firstborn of the flock. The household eats it *"before
Yahweh your God year by year in the place which Yahweh shall choose"* (20).

Then comes the exception. A firstling that is lame, blind, or has *"any defect whatever"* may not be
sacrificed (21). It is eaten at home, *"within your gates"*, by clean and unclean alike, *"as the
gazelle, and as the deer"* (22). The one limit is the limit on all meat: *"Only you shall not eat its
blood. You shall pour it out on the ground like water"* (23).

The unit is short and mostly restates. Chapter 12 already sent the firstborn to the chosen place
(12:6, 17–18), and 14:23 already set it on the household's table there. What is new here is the ban on
using the animal before it is offered, and the rule for a defective firstling. It also ends chapter 15's
run of laws about what an Israelite holds and must let go of: debts (15:1–11), servants (15:12–18), and
now the first of the herd.

## Notes

### Sanctify what is already holy

*"You shall dedicate"* is *taqdîš*, a hiphil of *qādaš*, *make holy* or *set apart* (WLC 15:19,
morphhb). Leviticus 27:26 uses the same verb with a negative: the firstborn *"no man may dedicate"*,
*lōʾ-yaqdîš ʾîš ʾōtô*, *"It is Yahweh's"* (WLC, morphhb). **One law says to dedicate the firstling and
the other says no one may.** They are likely talking about different acts. Leviticus 27 is about vows
and freewill gifts, and forbids offering as a gift what Yahweh already owns. Deuteronomy commands the
owner to treat it as Yahweh's. That reading fits the context of each, but neither verse states it.

The law covers *"males"* only — *hazzākār* (WLC 15:19, morphhb), as Exodus 13:12 has
*hazzəkārîm*, *"The males shall be Yahweh's"*. A firstborn female of the herd is not in view.

### No work, no shearing

*"You shall do no work with the firstborn of your herd"* — *lōʾ taʿăbōd bibkōr šôrekā*, *you shall not
serve with* or *work by* the firstling of your ox (WLC 15:19, morphhb). The ox's work is the plough and
the threshing floor; the sheep's yield is wool. **The owner may not take either from the firstling
before he gives it up.** No earlier firstborn law in the Torah says this.

The verb for shearing, *gāzaz*, occurs elsewhere in the Torah only at Genesis 31:19, where Laban *"had
gone to shear his sheep"*, and Genesis 38:12–13, where Judah goes up to Timnah *"to shear his sheep"*
(morphhb lemma search across Genesis–Deuteronomy). In both, shearing is the owner's harvest, and in
both it takes him away from home. The firstling is kept out of that harvest.

The rule may sit oddly beside the next verse. *"Year by year"* (20) could let a firstling grow for up
to a year before it is brought, long enough to be useful in work or wool. That may be why the ban is
needed. Exodus 22:30 and Leviticus 22:27, by contrast, speak of the eighth day, after which the animal
may be given or accepted. Whether *"year by year"* means *at the yearly pilgrimage* or sets an age
limit is not stated. The reading is offered here as an inference.

### Eaten by the household — and in Numbers, by the priest

*"You shall eat it … you and your household"* (20). Numbers 18:17–18 gives the meat of the clean
firstborn to Aaron: *"Their meat shall be yours, as the wave offering breast and as the right thigh"*.
**The two laws assign the meat differently.** The tension was first recorded at 14:23 (see the Notes
of [[Tithes for Feasting and Provision]]), and is now set out on its own page,
[[Whose Is the Firstling - Exodus 13, Numbers 18, and Deuteronomy 15]]. Numbers also says the
firstling's blood is sprinkled on the altar and its fat burned (18:17). Deuteronomy 15 says neither,
though 12:27 says the blood of sacrifices is poured on the altar at the place.

### The defective firstling

*"If it has any defect"* — *mûm*, *blemish* or *defect* (WLC 15:21, morphhb). In the Torah *mûm* is
mostly a word of Leviticus 21–22, for priests who may not offer and animals that may not be offered,
and comes back in Deuteronomy 17:1 (morphhb lemma search). The defects named are *pissēaḥ*, *lame*, and
*ʿiwwēr*, *blind*. The lame-and-blind pair occurs together in the Torah only here and at Leviticus
21:18, where a blind or lame descendant of Aaron *"shall not draw near"* (morphhb lemma search).
Then the list ends with *kōl mûm rāʿ*, *"any defect whatever"*, literally *any bad defect*. The
same phrase, with *dābār* for *mûm*, closes Deuteronomy 17:1.

The firstling is still Yahweh's; it is just not fit for his altar. Deuteronomy does not hand it back
for the owner to use, sell, or redeem. It is eaten. But it is eaten as ordinary meat, at home, by
anyone in the house. *"The unclean and the clean shall eat it alike"* (22) matters because sacrificial
meat could not be eaten by someone unclean: Leviticus 7:20–21 cuts off *"the soul who eats of the meat
of the sacrifice of peace offerings … having his uncleanness on him"*. **A defect moves the animal out
of the holy and into the common.**

### Gazelle, deer, and blood like water

*"As the gazelle, and as the deer"* — *kaṣṣəbî wəkāʾayyāl* (WLC 15:22, morphhb). The gazelle and the
deer occur in the Torah only in Deuteronomy, at 12:15, 22; 14:5, and here (morphhb lemma search). Both
are clean game, never sacrificed. In chapter 12 they were the pattern for meat killed at home now that
there was one place of sacrifice; here the defective firstling is put in that class.

Verse 23 repeats 12:16 almost word for word: *raq … lōʾ tōʾkēl ʿal-hāʾāreṣ tišpəkennû kammāyim*,
*only … you shall not eat; on the ground you shall pour it like water* (WLC 15:23; 12:16, 24, morphhb).
The only differences are grammatical number and *dāmô*, *its blood*, for *haddām*, *the blood*. Even
meat that never reaches the altar keeps the blood rule. See
[[Blood Reserved from Food - Genesis 9 and Leviticus 3]] and
[[One Place or Every Place - Exodus 20.24, Leviticus 17, and Deuteronomy 12]].

### Placement

Chapter 15's three laws each make an owner give up something he could keep: a claim on a debtor, a
servant's labor, a firstborn animal's work and wool. The first two end with blessing on the one who
lets go (15:10, 18). This one has no promise attached. It ends with the blood.

### Boundary

The planned boundary (15:19–23) is kept. 16:1 opens the calendar of feasts with Passover. Verse 16:2's
*"of the flock and the herd"* and the chosen place carry on from the firstling, but the subject and
the address (*"Observe the month of Abib"*) are new.
