# Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 24:6–22

## Summary

A run of short laws for the person who has least to bargain with. A creditor may not take a household's
millstone as a pledge (6). A man who kidnaps an Israelite and enslaves or sells him dies (7). Israel is to
follow the priests in cases of leprosy and to remember Miriam (8–9). A lender stays outside the debtor's
house to receive the pledge, and returns a poor man's pledged garment by sunset (10–13). A hired man is
paid the same day (14–15). No one is executed for a parent's or a child's sin (16). The foreigner and the
fatherless keep their right in court, and a widow's clothing is never taken as a pledge (17–18). The
forgotten sheaf, the olives left on the boughs, and the grapes left on the vine belong to the foreigner,
the fatherless, and the widow (19–22).

In the Hebrew, these verses have the same numbers as the WEB.

**The millstone (24:6).** *"No man shall take the mill or the upper millstone as a pledge; for he takes a
life in pledge."* The *"mill"* is *rēḥayim*, a dual noun, the pair of stones; the *"upper millstone"* is
*rekeb*, literally the *rider*, the same noun as Pharaoh's *"chariots"* (Exodus 14; Deuteronomy 11:4; 20:1)
(WLC). The verb is *ḥābal*, *to take in pledge*. In the Torah it occurs only here (twice), at 24:17, and in
Exodus 22:26, the garment returned before sunset (WLC lemma search; Exodus 22:26 is WLC 22:25). The reason
is three Hebrew words, *kî-nepeš hûʾ ḥōbēl*, *for a life he is pledging*. Bread was ground daily, so a
mill held overnight is a household without bread (an inference; the text gives only the reason as
stated). In the Torah the *"mill"* has appeared twice before: the firstborn *"of the female servant who
is behind the mill"* (Exodus 11:5) and the manna, which the people *"ground … in mills"* (Numbers 11:8)
(WLC lemma search). See [[The Last Plague Announced]].

**The kidnapper (24:7).** *"If a man is found stealing any of his brothers of the children of Israel, and
he deals with him as a slave, or sells him; then that thief shall die. So you shall remove the evil from
among you."* This is the law of Exodus 21:16 restated: both use the participle *gōnēb*, *one who steals*,
and both the passive of *māṣāʾ*, *found*. In Exodus the stolen man is *"found in his hand"*; here the
thief is found. Exodus has *gōnēb ʾîš*, *one who steals a man*; Deuteronomy has *gōnēb nepeš*, *one who
steals a life*, and limits the case to *"his brothers of the children of Israel"* (WLC). *"Deals with him
as a slave"* is *hitʿammēr*. In the Torah it occurs only here and at 21:14, the captive wife whom the
husband *"shall not deal with … as a slave"* (WLC lemma search). The kidnapper joins the cases closed by
*"you shall remove the evil from among you"*: see
[[Remove the Evil from Among You - Deuteronomy 13.5 and 1 Corinthians 5.13]]. Joseph said he was
*"stolen away out of the land of the Hebrews"* (Genesis 40:15), with the same verb; see
[[Kidnapping and Sale - Joseph and the Law of Exodus 21]].

**Leprosy and Miriam (24:8–9).** *"Be careful in the plague of leprosy, that you observe diligently, and do
according to all that the priests the Levites teach you. As I commanded them, so you shall observe to do.
Remember what Yahweh your God did to Miriam, by the way as you came out of Egypt."* *"The plague of
leprosy"* is *negaʿ-haṣṣāraʿat*, the phrase of Leviticus 13; *ṣāraʿat* occurs in Deuteronomy only here
(WLC). Deuteronomy does not repeat the procedure. It points to it: *"as I commanded them"*. The priests
*"teach"* (*yôrû*, hiphil of *yārâ*), the verb of 17:11, *"the law which they shall teach you"* (WLC).

Then *"Remember"*. It is *zākôr*, the infinitive absolute, used three times in Deuteronomy: 7:18,
*"remember well what Yahweh your God did to Pharaoh"*; here; and 25:17 (WLC lemma search). 24:9 and 25:17
share their frame word for word: *zākôr ʾēt ʾăšer-ʿāśâ … baddérek bǝṣēʾtǝkem mimmiṣrāyim*. In the WEB,
*"Remember what Yahweh your God did to Miriam, by the way as you came out of Egypt"*, and *"Remember what
Amalek did to you by the way as you came out of Egypt"* (WLC; stored WEB). In Numbers 12 Miriam spoke
against Moses, was *"leprous, as white as snow"* (12:10), and was *"shut up outside of the camp seven
days"* (12:15). The verse does not say what Israel is to learn from her. Two readings are current:

1. **Leprosy itself.** Even Moses' sister was put outside the camp; no one is exempt from the priests'
   ruling.
2. **Speech against Moses.** Her leprosy came from speaking against Yahweh's servant; in a law about
   obeying the priests, the example warns against contempt for the authorities Yahweh appointed.

The verse does not choose, and the wiki adopts neither. See
[[Remember What Yahweh Did to Miriam - Numbers 12, Deuteronomy 24, and Micah 6]].

**The pledge at the door (24:10–13).** *"When you lend your neighbor any kind of loan, you shall not go into
his house to get his pledge. You shall stand outside, and the man to whom you lend shall bring the pledge
outside to you."* The verb *"lend"* is *nāšâ* in the hiphil, the creditor's verb of the year of release
(15:2) (WLC). The noun *"pledge"* is *ʿăbôṭ*, and in the Torah it occurs only in these four verses
(10–13) (WLC lemma search). The creditor does not choose the pledge; the debtor brings it out. The law
keeps the lender out of the house.

*"If he is a poor man, you shall not sleep with his pledge. You shall surely restore to him the pledge when
the sun goes down, that he may sleep in his garment, and bless you."* *"Poor"* is *ʿānî*. In Deuteronomy
it occurs at 15:11 and three times here (12, 14, 15) (WLC). The *"garment"* is *śalmâ*, the word of Exodus
22:26, and the sunset deadline is the same: *"you shall restore it to him before the sun goes down, for
that is his only covering"* (Exodus 22:26–27). Two sleepers are set side by side, the lender who may not
sleep with the pledge and the debtor who sleeps in it.

*"And bless you. It shall be righteousness to you before Yahweh your God."* The blessing is the poor
man's (*ûbērǝkekkā*, he will bless you). *"Righteousness"* is *ṣǝdāqâ*. In Deuteronomy it occurs at 6:25,
*"It shall be righteousness to us, if we observe to do all this commandment before Yahweh our God"*; at
9:4–6, three times, where Israel is told the land is *not* given *"for your righteousness; for you are a
stiff-necked people"*; at 24:13; and at 33:21 (WLC). 6:25 counts the keeping of *"all this commandment"* as righteousness;
24:13 says it of one act. Genesis 15:6 has the same noun: Abram *"believed in Yahweh, who credited it to him for
righteousness"*. How 24:13 relates to 9:4–6, a returned coat counted as righteousness in a book that
denies Israel's righteousness won the land, the text does not say. The two statements stand in one
book.

**The day's wage (24:14–15).** *"You shall not oppress a hired servant who is poor and needy, whether he is
one of your brothers, or one of the foreigners who are in your land within your gates. In his day you
shall give him his hire, neither shall the sun go down on it."* The words *"oppress"* (*ʿāšaq*) and
*"hired servant"* (*śākîr*) appear together in the Torah only here and in Leviticus 19:13, *"You shall not
oppress your neighbor … The wages of a hired servant shall not remain with you all night until the
morning"* (WLC lemma search). Leviticus sets the limit at morning, Deuteronomy at sunset. The law covers
the Israelite and the *gēr* alike, *"within your gates"*.

The reason is the worker's need: *"for he is poor, and sets his heart on it"*. The Hebrew is *wǝʾēlāyw hûʾ
nōśēʾ ʾet-napšô*, *and to it he lifts up his soul* (WLC). Then the warning: *"lest he cry against you to
Yahweh, and it be sin to you"*. The last three words, *wǝhāyâ bǝkā ḥēṭʾ*, occur in Deuteronomy three
times: the man who refuses a loan as the year of release nears (15:9), the vow not paid (23:21, WLC
23:22), and here (WLC lemma search). At 15:9 the poor brother *"cry to Yahweh against you"*, as here.
Each time the sin follows from something withheld: a loan, a vow, a wage.

**The fathers and the children (24:16).** *"The fathers shall not be put to death for the children, neither
shall the children be put to death for the fathers. Every man shall be put to death for his own sin."*
The verb is the hophal of *mût*, *be put to death*, the passive of judicial execution (WLC). The noun is
*ḥēṭʾ* again, from verse 15. The law is for human courts. It sits beside 5:9, where Yahweh is *"visiting
the iniquity of the fathers on the children, and on the third and on the fourth generation of those who
hate me"*. The two are about different agents, God and the court, and Deuteronomy does not compare them.
2 Kings 14:6 quotes 24:16 as the reason Amaziah *"didn't put to death"* the children of his father's
murderers, *"according to that which is written in the book of the law of Moses"*. Ezekiel 18:20 says
*"The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father"* of God's own judgment. See
[[Visiting the Iniquity of the Fathers - Job 21.19, Jeremiah 31, and Ezekiel 18]].

**Justice and the widow's garment (24:17–18).** *"You shall not deprive the foreigner or the fatherless of
justice, nor take a widow's clothing in pledge; but you shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt."*
*"Deprive … of justice"* is *lōʾ taṭṭeh mišpaṭ*, *you shall not bend the judgment*, the words of 16:19
and Exodus 23:6 (WLC). 27:19 will curse *"he who withholds justice from the foreigner, fatherless, and
widow"* with the same verb and noun (WLC). The widow's garment uses *ḥābal* again, from verse 6, the
third time in the passage. A poor man's coat may be taken and returned at sunset (12–13); a widow's may
not be taken at all. The motive is the remembrance clause, *"you were a slave in Egypt"*. See
[[You Were a Slave in Egypt - The Remembrance Refrain of Deuteronomy]].

**The forgotten sheaf (24:19–22).** *"When you reap your harvest in your field, and have forgotten a sheaf
in the field, you shall not go again to get it. It shall be for the foreigner, for the fatherless, and for
the widow."* Three crops follow: grain, olives, grapes. *"You shall not go over the boughs again"* is the
piel of *pāʾar*, used in this sense only here in the Torah; *"you shall not glean it after yourselves"*
is the polel of *ʿālal*, the verb of Leviticus 19:10 (WLC lemma search).

*"Forgotten"* is *šākaḥ*. Most often in Deuteronomy it is a warning not to forget Yahweh or what he
has done: *"Beware lest you forget Yahweh"* (6:12), and so 4:9, 23; 8:11, 14, 19; 9:7 (WLC). Here Israel
forgets a sheaf, and the law tells it to leave it there. The sheaf is *ʿōmer*, the word for the measure of manna
(Exodus 16) and the first-fruits sheaf waved before Yahweh (Leviticus 23:10–15) (WLC lemma search).

Leviticus 19:9–10 and 23:22 leave the corners and gleanings *"for the poor and for the foreigner"*.
Deuteronomy names *"the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow"* and does not use *"the poor"* in
24:19–21; *ʿānî* is used in this chapter for the debtor and the hired man. Leviticus also leaves the
corners of the field, which Deuteronomy does not mention. The two laws overlap without matching. For the
gleaning laws and Ruth, see
[[The Forgotten Sheaf - Gleaning in Leviticus 19 and 23, Deuteronomy 24, and Ruth 2]].

Two blessings frame the passage. The poor man whose garment is returned *"bless[es] you"* (13), and
the farmer who leaves the sheaf is promised *"that Yahweh your God may bless you in all the work of your
hands"* (19). The passage closes on the refrain a second time: *"You shall remember that you were a slave
in the land of Egypt. Therefore I command you to do this thing"* (22; compare 18).

**How the passage fits together.** Three words carry across the passage. *Nepeš*, *life* or *soul*, occurs
three times: the mill is a life taken in pledge (6), the kidnapper steals a life (7), the worker lifts his
soul to his wage (15). *Ḥābal* occurs three times, for the mill (6, twice) and the widow's garment (17).
The sun sets twice, on the returned pledge (13) and the paid wage (15). The laws protect what a poor
person cannot do without for one day: bread, freedom, a coat to sleep in, the day's pay, a fair hearing,
and the grain left in the field. Two remembrances run through it: Miriam (9) and Egypt (18, 22). The
boundary is the plan's, kept as drawn, with commas added to the title. 25:1 turns to disputes in court.

**What later texts do with it.** Amos 2:8 accuses those who *"lay themselves down beside every altar on
clothes taken in pledge"*. Job 24:3, 9 lists those who *"take the widow's ox for a pledge"* and *"take a
pledge of the poor"*. Jeremiah 22:13 pronounces *"Woe to him … who uses his neighbor's service without
wages"*. Malachi 3:5 names, in one verse, *"those who oppress the hireling in his wages, the widow, and the
fatherless, and who deprive the foreigner of justice"*. James 5:4: *"the wages of the laborers who mowed
your fields, which you have kept back by fraud, cry out"*. In Matthew 20:8, *"When evening had come"*, the
lord of the vineyard pays the laborers. See
[[Before the Sun Goes Down - The Pledged Garment and the Day's Wage]]. In Ruth 2 a Moabite widow gleans in
Boaz's field, and Boaz tells his men to *"pull out some for her from the bundles, and leave it"*.

## Notes

**Verse numbering.** WEB 24:6–22 is WLC 24:6–22. The boundary is the plan's, kept as drawn: 24:5 closes
the marriage laws, and 25:1 begins the court and family laws.

**"Mill" and "upper millstone."** *Rēḥayim* (lemma 7347), dual; in the Torah at Exodus 11:5; Numbers 11:8;
Deuteronomy 24:6 (WLC lemma search). *Rekeb* (lemma 7393) is the usual Torah word for chariotry; its sense
*upper millstone* here rests on context and is the standard lexical view, reported from general knowledge.

**"Take in pledge."** *Ḥābal* (lemma 2254): Exodus 22:25 (WEB 22:26), twice; Deuteronomy 24:6, twice; 24:17
(WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). The noun *ʿăbôṭ* (lemma 5667) occurs only at Deuteronomy
24:10–13, and the verb *ʿābaṭ* (lemma 5670) at 15:6, 8 and 24:10 (same search).

**"A life."** *Nepeš* (lemma 5315) at WLC 24:6, 7, 15.

**"Stealing" and "found."** WLC Exodus 21:16 *wǝgōnēb ʾîš … wǝnimṣāʾ bǝyādô*; Deuteronomy 24:7 *kî-yimmāṣēʾ
ʾîš gōnēb nepeš*. Both are niphal of *māṣāʾ* (lemma 4672) and qal participle of *gānab* (lemma 1589).

**"Deals with him as a slave."** *Hitʿammēr* (lemma 6014): in Genesis–Deuteronomy only 21:14 and 24:7 (WLC
lemma search). The sense is disputed. *Treat as a slave*, as the WEB has it, and *treat violently* are
both offered by lexicons (reported from general knowledge).

**"Leprosy."** *Ṣāraʿat* (lemma 6883) occurs in Deuteronomy only at 24:8 (WLC lemma search). What the
Hebrew term covers medically is not settled; see [[Clean and Unclean]].

**"Remember."** *Zākôr*, qal infinitive absolute of *zākar* (lemma 2142), occurs in Deuteronomy at 7:18;
24:9; 25:17 (WLC morphology search). The shared frame of 24:9 and 25:17 was read in the WLC.

**"Righteousness."** *Ṣǝdāqâ* (lemma 6666) in Deuteronomy: 6:25; 9:4, 5, 6; 24:13; 33:21 (WLC lemma search).

**"Oppress" and "hired servant."** *ʿĀšaq* (lemma 6231) in Genesis–Deuteronomy: Leviticus 5:21, 23 (WEB 6:2,
4); 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14; 28:29, 33. *Śākîr* (lemma 7916): Exodus 12:45; 22:14; Leviticus 19:13; 22:10;
25:6, 40, 50, 53; Deuteronomy 15:18; 24:14. They occur in the same verse only at Leviticus 19:13 and
Deuteronomy 24:14 (WLC lemma search).

**"Sets his heart on it."** *Wǝʾēlāyw hûʾ nōśēʾ ʾet-napšô* (WLC 24:15). That *lift up the soul* means
*long for* is the usual reading, reported from general knowledge; its occurrences outside the Torah were
not searched.

**"It be sin to you."** *Wǝhāyâ bǝkā ḥēṭʾ* (WLC 15:9; 23:22; 24:15). *Ḥēṭʾ* (lemma 2399) also occurs in
Deuteronomy at 19:15; 21:22; 22:26; 23:23; 24:16 (WLC lemma search).

**"Put to death."** *Yûmǝtû*, hophal of *mût* (lemma 4191), three times in WLC 24:16.

**"Deprive of justice."** *Lōʾ taṭṭeh mišpaṭ* (WLC 24:17), hiphil of *nāṭâ* (lemma 5186) with *mišpāṭ*
(lemma 4941); compare WLC Exodus 23:6; Deuteronomy 16:19; 27:19.

**Harvest verbs.** *Pāʾar* in the piel (lemma 6286) at WLC 24:20 is the only occurrence in that sense in
Genesis–Deuteronomy; Exodus 8:5 (WEB 8:9) has the hithpael, *"have this honor"*. *ʿĀlal* in the polel
(lemma 5953) occurs at Leviticus 19:10 and Deuteronomy 24:21; the hithpael at Exodus 10:2 and Numbers
22:29 is a different sense (WLC lemma search). *Ḥābaṭ*, *beat* (lemma 2251), occurs only at Deuteronomy
24:20 in the Torah.

**"Forgotten."** *Šākaḥ* (lemma 7911) in Deuteronomy: 4:9, 23, 31; 6:12; 8:11, 14, 19 (twice); 9:7; 24:19;
25:19; 26:13; 31:21; 32:18 (WLC lemma search). At 4:31 Yahweh will not *"forget the covenant"*; at
26:13 the tither will say *"neither have I forgotten"* the commandments.

**"Poor."** *ʿĀnî* (lemma 6041): Deuteronomy 15:11; 24:12, 14, 15; Exodus 22:24 (WEB 22:25); Leviticus 19:10;
23:22 (WLC lemma search, Exodus, Leviticus, Deuteronomy).

**What the passage does not settle.** What Israel is to learn from Miriam. How *"righteousness"* in 24:13
relates to 9:4–6. Whether the poor man's pledge (12–13) is returned each night and retaken each morning,
as the wording implies, or held only by day. Whether *hitʿammēr* means *enslave* or *mistreat*. How 24:16
relates to 5:9. Each is recorded, none adopted.
