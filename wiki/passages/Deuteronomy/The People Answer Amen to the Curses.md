# The People Answer Amen to the Curses

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 27:11–26

## Summary

Moses prescribes the assembly’s arrangement after the Jordan crossing: six tribes stand on
Gerizim to bless and six on Ebal for the curse (11–13). The Levites must speak loudly, and all
the people must answer Amen after each curse (14–26). The text gives instructions for the
future ceremony, not a report that the people have already performed it.

The twelve curses move from secret images and dishonored parents through stolen boundaries,
misled blind people, withheld justice, forbidden sexual relations, and secret or paid killing.
The final curse gathers them under the obligation to uphold the law by doing it. Public assent
acknowledges standards that reach conduct beyond public view.

## Key verses

Complete unit, copied from stored WEB:

> **27:11** Moses commanded the people the same day, saying,

> **27:12** “These shall stand on Mount Gerizim to bless the people, when you have crossed over the Jordan: Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin.

> **27:13** These shall stand on Mount Ebal for the curse: Reuben, Gad, Asher, Zebulun, Dan, and Naphtali.

> **27:14** With a loud voice, the Levites shall say to all the men of Israel,

> **27:15** ‘Cursed is the man who makes an engraved or molten image, an abomination to Yahweh, the work of the hands of the craftsman, and sets it up in secret.’ All the people shall answer and say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:16** ‘Cursed is he who dishonors his father or his mother.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:17** ‘Cursed is he who removes his neighbor’s landmark.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:18** ‘Cursed is he who leads the blind astray on the road.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:19** ‘Cursed is he who withholds justice from the foreigner, fatherless, and widow.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:20** ‘Cursed is he who lies with his father’s wife, because he dishonors his father’s bed.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:21** ‘Cursed is he who lies with any kind of animal.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:22** ‘Cursed is he who lies with his sister, his father’s daughter or his mother’s daughter.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:23** ‘Cursed is he who lies with his mother-in-law.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:24** ‘Cursed is he who secretly kills his neighbor.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:25** ‘Cursed is he who takes a bribe to kill an innocent person.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’

> **27:26** ‘Cursed is he who doesn’t uphold the words of this law by doing them.’ All the people shall say, ‘Amen.’”

## People

- [[Moses]] — commands the ceremony.
- [[God]] — Yahweh, whose covenant standards the people acknowledge.
- Gerizim’s tribal groups: [[Simeon]], [[Levi]], [[Judah]], [[Issachar]], [[Joseph]], and [[Benjamin]].
- Ebal’s tribal groups: [[Reuben]], [[Gad]], [[Asher]], [[Zebulun]], [[Dan (person)]], and [[Naphtali]].
- These ancestral names designate communities; the patriarchs are not present. The Levites speak,
  and all the people answer; unnamed offenders are legal categories, not new named persons.

## Places

- [[Mount Gerizim]] — Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin must stand here to bless the people (12). No corresponding blessing formulas are recorded in this unit.
- [[Mount Ebal]] — Reuben, Gad, Asher, Zebulun, Dan, and Naphtali must stand here for the curse (13). All the people, not just these six tribes, must answer the Levitical curses (15–26).
- [[The Jordan]] — The tribal ceremony is commanded for after Israel crosses the river (12). The crossing itself is not narrated in this unit.

## Themes

- [[Curse]] — Twelve curses receive twelve communal responses (15–26). These are covenant sanctions Israel must acknowledge, not hostile magic directed at another nation. The last curse embraces failure to uphold the law by doing it.
- [[Blessing]] — Six tribes stand on Gerizim to bless the people (12), but this unit supplies only curse formulas (15–26). The assignment to bless must not be mistaken for a written series of matching blessings.
- [[Covenant]] — All the people are commanded to assent to each curse (15–26). Public agreement makes the community acknowledge obligations reaching secret actions as well as visible ones; the ceremony remains prospective.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The final curse concerns upholding the law by doing it (26). Recitation and assent do not replace conduct. The preceding eleven cases exemplify rather than exhaust the obligation.
- [[Idolatry]] — The first curse targets an engraved or molten image made by a craftsman and installed in secret (15). Concealment does not remove accountability to Yahweh.
- [[Sexual Sin]] — The curses name intercourse with a father’s wife, an animal, a sister, and a mother-in-law (20–23). They restate selected sexual boundaries as communal sanctions, without supplying a new trial procedure.
- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — Misleading the blind on the road and withholding justice from the foreigner, fatherless, and widow receive curses (18–19). Vulnerability must not become an opportunity for exploitation.
- [[Judgment]] — Withheld justice and payment to kill an innocent person are cursed (19, 25). The ceremony condemns these acts but does not replace the evidentiary procedures of Deuteronomy 19:15–21.
- [[Priesthood]] — The Levites must pronounce the curses loudly to all Israel (14). Levi also appears among the tribes on Gerizim (12); the text does not specify how the speaking group is positioned relative to that tribal assignment.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — The people’s repeated Amen assents to stated curses (15–26). This resembles the woman’s assent in Numbers 5:22, but here it is communal covenant acknowledgment, not a voluntary vow or an individual ordeal.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Loud Levitical proclamation is answered by the same response from all the people after each curse (14–26). Speech binds the assembly to acknowledge standards that must also be enacted.
- [[Integrity]] — Secret idolatry and secret killing are explicitly condemned (15, 24). The public response therefore acknowledges obligations even where an offender might evade ordinary observation.
- [[Blood]] — Secretly killing a neighbor and accepting a bribe to kill an innocent person are cursed (24–25). Concealment and payment do not make the taking of life permissible.
- [[Theft and Restitution]] — Removing a neighbor’s landmark is cursed (17), reinforcing the boundary protection of Deuteronomy 19:14. The curse gives no new restitution amount.
- [[Sin]] — Specific wrongs against worship, parents, neighbors, vulnerable people, and sexual boundaries culminate in failure to do the law (15–26). Communal assent is not a claim that every speaker has committed each listed act.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 27:11–26 unit is retained: a fresh command in verse 11 introduces
tribal assignments and the twelve responses. Chapter 28’s blessings remain the next pericope.

**Blessing and curse are not a moral ranking of tribes.** Both groups participate in Israel’s
ceremony, and all the people answer. The text gives no reason for the division. Levi’s placement
on Gerizim and the Levites’ speaking role coexist; dividing them into unmentioned groups or
placing them at an unstated location would be a reconstruction. Joseph appears as one group.

**Asymmetry.** Verse 12 assigns blessing, but verses 15–26 preserve only curses. No missing
blessing liturgy is reconstructed, and chapter 28 is not automatically identified with one.
The altar and rejoicing on Ebal in the preceding unit remain alongside this assignment of curse.

**Assent and action.** Amen acknowledges each sanction; it is not a confession that every
speaker has committed each offense. The closing curse expressly requires doing, so a spoken
response cannot substitute for obedience. These curses do not abolish the court’s requirements
for witnesses or supply new execution instructions.
