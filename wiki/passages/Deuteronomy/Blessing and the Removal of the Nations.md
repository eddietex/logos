# Blessing and the Removal of the Nations

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 7:12–26

## Summary

The second half of Deuteronomy 7 turns from why Israel was chosen to what follows if Israel
listens. If the people hear, keep, and do the ordinances, Yahweh will keep with them *the
covenant and the loving kindness which he swore to your fathers* (12). The blessing is concrete
and agricultural: love, multiplication, children, grain, new wine, oil, herds and flocks, in the
sworn land (13). No one, human or animal, will be barren (14). Yahweh will remove sickness and
put none of *the evil diseases of Egypt* on Israel, laying them on those who hate it instead (15).

Verse 16 returns to the nations with the same severity as 7:2. Israel is to consume the peoples
delivered to it, without pity, and must not serve their gods, *for that would be a snare to you*.

Moses then answers the fear he expects: *These nations are more than I; how can I dispossess
them?* (17). The remedy is memory. Israel must remember what Yahweh did to Pharaoh and to Egypt,
the trials, signs, wonders, mighty hand, and outstretched arm that its own eyes saw (18–19). The
same God will do the same to the peoples Israel fears. He will send the hornet, and he is
*among you, a great and awesome God* (20–21).

The conquest is to be gradual. Yahweh will clear the nations *little by little*, *lest the
animals of the field increase on you* (22), yet he will still confuse them and give their kings
into Israel's hand until they are destroyed (23–24). The chapter closes where 7:5 began, with the
images of their gods. They are to be burned, and their silver and gold must not be coveted or
taken. The metal is *an abomination to Yahweh* and *a devoted thing*, and whoever brings it home
becomes *a devoted thing like it* (25–26).

## Key verses

Complete unit, copied from the stored WEB:

> **7:12** It shall happen, because you listen to these ordinances, and keep and do them, that Yahweh your God will keep with you the covenant and the loving kindness which he swore to your fathers.

> **7:13** He will love you, bless you, and multiply you. He will also bless the fruit of your body and the fruit of your ground, your grain and your new wine and your oil, the increase of your livestock and the young of your flock, in the land which he swore to your fathers to give you.

> **7:14** You will be blessed above all peoples. There won’t be male or female barren among you, or among your livestock.

> **7:15** Yahweh will take away from you all sickness; and he will put none of the evil diseases of Egypt, which you know, on you, but will lay them on all those who hate you.

> **7:16** You shall consume all the peoples whom Yahweh your God shall deliver to you. Your eye shall not pity them. You shall not serve their gods; for that would be a snare to you.

> **7:17** If you shall say in your heart, “These nations are more than I; how can I dispossess them?”

> **7:18** you shall not be afraid of them. You shall remember well what Yahweh your God did to Pharaoh, and to all Egypt:

> **7:19** the great trials which your eyes saw, the signs, the wonders, the mighty hand, and the outstretched arm, by which Yahweh your God brought you out. So shall Yahweh your God do to all the peoples of whom you are afraid.

> **7:20** Moreover Yahweh your God will send the hornet among them, until those who are left, and hide themselves, perish from before you.

> **7:21** You shall not be scared of them; for Yahweh your God is among you, a great and awesome God.

> **7:22** Yahweh your God will cast out those nations before you little by little. You may not consume them at once, lest the animals of the field increase on you.

> **7:23** But Yahweh your God will deliver them up before you, and will confuse them with a great confusion, until they are destroyed.

> **7:24** He will deliver their kings into your hand, and you shall make their name perish from under the sky. No one will be able to stand before you, until you have destroyed them.

> **7:25** You shall burn the engraved images of their gods with fire. You shall not covet the silver or the gold that is on them, nor take it for yourself, lest you be snared in it; for it is an abomination to Yahweh your God.

> **7:26** You shall not bring an abomination into your house, and become a devoted thing like it. You shall utterly detest it, and you shall utterly abhor it; for it is a devoted thing.

## Notes

**Discussion absorbed (unattended run).** This unit is closely modelled on the end of the
Sinai covenant code, Exodus 23:25–33 (stored WEB). There, too, sickness is taken away, no one is
barren, the hornet is sent, the land is cleared *little by little*, and the peoples' gods are
*a snare*. Deuteronomy rearranges that material into an answer to fear. Its new element is the
argument from memory in 17–19: what Israel saw in Egypt is the evidence for what it will see in
Canaan.

**Conditional blessing.** Verse 12 makes Yahweh's keeping of the sworn covenant follow Israel's
listening (*because you listen … that Yahweh your God will keep*). Verses 7–8 had grounded the
exodus in love and the oath, not in anything Israel did. The two are held together here, not
merged. The rescue from Egypt is not made conditional after the fact, but the blessing in the
land is tied to obedience. Deuteronomy 28 will develop this conditional blessing at length.

**No pity, and gradual removal.** *Your eye shall not pity them* (16) repeats the severity of
7:2. It stands in the same tension, already flagged on [[Compassion and Mercy]], with the mercy
promised to a returning Israel in 4:31. Within the unit there is a second tension, between
consuming *all the peoples* (16) and *you may not consume them at once* (22). The text resolves
it as a matter of pace, not extent: the destruction is complete (23–24) but not immediate. The
reason given is the land's ecology, the wild animals, not Israel's failure. Judges 2 will later
give a different reason for peoples who remain. That tension is recorded on
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].

**The hornet.** Deuteronomy does not explain it, any more than Exodus 23:28 does. The wiki keeps
to the existing position: literal insect, figure for panic, and symbol of a foreign power are
all reported readings, and none is adopted.

**The snare is in the metal too.** At 7:16 the snare is serving the nations' gods. At 7:25 it is
coveting the silver and gold on their images. The precious metal shares the image's status. It
is an abomination and devoted, and taking it home makes the household devoted as well (26). This
is the point at which the devoted-thing law reaches private greed. The Joshua 7 story of Achan,
who takes devoted things and brings disaster on Israel, is the obvious later test case. It is
cited here by reference only; Joshua is not yet stored.

**The diseases of Egypt.** *Which you know* (15) points back to the plagues and to Exodus 15:26,
where Yahweh at Marah promises to put none of the diseases he put on the Egyptians on an obedient
Israel. Deuteronomy 28:60 (stored WEB) will reverse the promise for disobedience: *He will bring
on you again all the diseases of Egypt*. See
[[The Diseases of Egypt - The Plagues in Deuteronomy 28]].

**Boundary.** The planned 7:12–26 unit is retained. Chapter 8 opens a new exhortation on the
wilderness and the danger of forgetting in prosperity.
