# The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 3:12–22

## Summary

Moses recalls distributing the conquered eastern territory: Reuben and Gad receive land from
Aroer and part of Gilead, while half of Manasseh receives the remainder of Gilead and Bashan.
Jair’s conquest and naming of Argob and the grant to Machir specify the Manassite holdings
(12–15). The Arnon, Jabbok, Jordan, Chinnereth, Salt Sea, and Pisgah mark the allocation (16–17).

The land is Yahweh’s gift, but settled possession does not excuse the eastern warriors from
crossing with their brothers. Wives, children, and livestock may remain in the cities; the men
must serve until Yahweh gives the other tribes rest and possession too (18–20). Moses then
recalls encouraging Joshua: what he has seen happen to the two kings supplies the ground for
courage in the campaigns ahead, because Yahweh fights for Israel (21–22).

## Key verses

> **12** This land we took in possession at that time: from Aroer, which is by the valley of the Arnon, and half the hill country of Gilead, and its cities, I gave to the Reubenites and to the Gadites:

> **13** and the rest of Gilead, and all Bashan, the kingdom of Og, I gave to the half-tribe of Manasseh; all the region of Argob, even all Bashan. (The same is called the land of Rephaim.

> **17** the Arabah also, and the Jordan and its border, from Chinnereth even to the sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea, under the slopes of Pisgah eastward.

> **18** I commanded you at that time, saying, “Yahweh your God has given you this land to possess it. You shall pass over armed before your brothers the children of Israel, all the men of valor.

> **19** But your wives, and your little ones, and your livestock, (I know that you have much livestock), shall live in your cities which I have given you,

> **20** until Yahweh gives rest to your brothers, as to you, and they also possess the land which Yahweh your God gives them beyond the Jordan. Then you shall each return to his own possession, which I have given you.”

> **21** I commanded Joshua at that time, saying, “Your eyes have seen all that Yahweh your God has done to these two kings. So shall Yahweh do to all the kingdoms where you go over.

> **22** You shall not fear them; for Yahweh your God himself fights for you.”

## People
- [[God]] — Yahweh gives the eastern land and the rest still awaited by the other tribes (18–20). Moses grounds Joshua’s courage in Yahweh’s past acts and continuing warfare for Israel (21–22).
- [[Moses]] — Moses recalls allocating the eastern territory, binding its warriors to their brothers’ campaign, and encouraging Joshua from the victories already seen (12–22). His allocation administers the land that verse 18 calls Yahweh’s gift.
- [[Reuben]] — The Reubenites receive land with Gad from the Arnon through Gilead to the Jabbok and along the Jordan (12, 16–17). Their families and livestock may remain, but their warriors must help the other tribes (18–20). These are the patriarch’s descendants.
- [[Gad]] — The Gadites share the grant described with Reuben in verses 12 and 16–17. Settlement does not release their warriors from crossing with their brothers; return waits for the others’ possession (18–20). The patriarch is not a participant.
- [[Manasseh]] — The half-tribe receives the rest of Gilead and all Bashan (13). Jair and Machir specify Manassite holdings (14–15). The command following this combined allocation addresses the eastern recipients collectively (18–20); Numbers 32 had recorded the negotiation with Reuben and Gad.
- [[Machir]] — Moses recalls giving Gilead to Machir (15), within the distribution between Reuben, Gad, and Manasseh (12–16). Numbers 32:39–40 identifies the conquering children of Machir; the abbreviated grant does not require the ancestral individual to be present.
- [[Jair (son of Manasseh)]] — Jair takes Argob to the Geshurite and Maacathite border and names the territory Havvoth Jair after himself (14). Numbers 32:41 gives the village-taking and naming more briefly. The differing later account associated with a judge named Jair remains unresolved on [[Havvoth Jair]].
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — Moses appeals to what Joshua has seen happen to the two kings as grounds for confidence about the kingdoms ahead (21–22). This is a remembered exhortation, not a repetition of Numbers 27’s public laying on of hands.
- [[Og]] — His former kingdom, Bashan, is assigned to half of Manasseh (13). He is one of the two defeated kings whose fate supplies encouragement for Joshua (21), not a living participant in the allocation.
- [[Sihon]] — Although not named in this unit, he is the other of the two kings recalled in verse 21, alongside Og (compare 2:24–3:11). Their defeats become the precedent for Joshua’s future campaigns.
- [[The Rephaim]] — Bashan is called the land of Rephaim (13), continuing the notice about Og in 3:11. This is a remembered territorial designation, not a new encounter with surviving Rephaim.

## Places
- [[Aroer]] — The Arnon-valley Aroer anchors the eastern grant to Reuben and Gad (12). The joint regional grant does not settle the existing question of which Aroer Gad builds in Numbers 32:34.
- [[The Arnon]] — The valley and its middle mark the southern boundary in the land granted to Reuben and Gad (12, 16).
- [[Gilead]] — Half the hill country goes to Reuben and Gad, the rest to half of Manasseh (12–13); the grant to Machir is stated separately (15). This explains how Numbers 32 can speak of Gilead in connection with both groups without requiring identical holdings.
- [[Bashan]] — All Bashan, Og’s former kingdom and a land of Rephaim, goes to half of Manasseh (13). Jair’s taking and naming of Argob is recalled within this allocation (14).
- [[Argob]] — Jair’s acquisition extends to the border of the Geshurites and Maacathites (14), and belongs within Manasseh’s Bashan allotment (13). The verse does not claim conquest of those neighboring peoples.
- [[Havvoth Jair]] — The naming by Jair now appears in the ingested Deuteronomy account, tied to Argob and Bashan (14). The differences from the later Jair tradition already recorded below remain unresolved; this passage does not explain their relationship.
- [[The Jabbok]] — The river marks the border of the children of Ammon in the eastern grant (16). Reaching this boundary is not a grant of Ammonite territory.
- [[Ammon]] — Its border is identified with the Jabbok (16). This boundary continues the distinction between acquired land and protected Ammonite territory in Deuteronomy 2:19, 37.
- [[Arabah]] — The grant includes the Arabah and the Jordan’s border from Chinnereth to the Salt Sea beneath Pisgah’s eastern slopes (17).
- [[The Jordan]] — The river marks the eastern holdings and the crossing required of their warriors (17–20). In verse 20 the land beyond the Jordan is the western destination, showing that the expression depends on viewpoint.
- [[The Sea of Chinnereth]] — Chinnereth marks the northern end of the Jordan boundary described in verse 17. The verse uses the shorter name; Numbers 34:11 explicitly names the sea. No distinct city is identified here.
- [[The Salt Sea]] — Called the sea of the Arabah and the Salt Sea in verse 17, it marks the southern end of the Jordan boundary beneath Pisgah’s eastern slopes.
- [[Pisgah]] — Its eastern slopes supply a geographical bearing for the grant (17). Moses’ request to cross and instruction to ascend its summit belong to the following unit.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The eastern grant is already possession and rest, yet its recipients must help their brothers possess the western land before returning home (18–20). Land given to one group carries responsibility toward the whole people.
- [[Brotherhood]] — The repeated reference to brothers makes shared military service a condition of returning to settled homes (18–20). Receiving an inheritance first does not end mutual obligation.
- [[War]] — Armed men cross while families and livestock stay in the granted cities (18–20). Yahweh’s fighting for Israel (22) accompanies commanded human participation rather than replacing it.
- [[Faith]] — Joshua’s encouragement rests on victories his own eyes have seen (21–22). The call to courage is an argument from remembered divine action, not a claim that Joshua never experienced fear.
- [[Remembrance]] — Moses turns recollection of the two defeated kings into assurance for Joshua about kingdoms ahead (21–22). The past is used to equip a successor for a future Moses will not lead.
- [[Names and Naming]] — Jair names his acquired territory Havvoth Jair after himself (14). The continuing place-name preserves conquest memory; its relation to the later Jair tradition remains unresolved.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The eastern warriors are commanded to cross and may return only when their brothers also possess their land (18–20). The gift brings a defined obligation and a stated endpoint.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Moses explicitly acknowledges the eastern tribes’ many livestock and permits families and herds to remain in their cities (19). Provision for property and households accompanies continued service to the wider people.

## Notes

**The retelling selects.** Numbers 32 narrates a request, Moses’ rebuke, a negotiated promise,
and instructions to future leaders. Here Moses recalls the settled grant and command, without
repeating that negotiation. Half of Manasseh appears before the collective command (13–18),
whereas Numbers 32:1–32 gives the agreement in dialogue with Reuben and Gad. The accounts
have different scope; the later summary should not be read back as dialogue absent from Numbers.

**Rest is shared.** Verse 20 treats the eastern recipients as already enjoying the rest still
awaited by their brothers. Here rest concerns possession of land and release to return home;
it is not a command about the weekly Sabbath. Yahweh’s fighting and the warriors’ participation
are both affirmed, without explaining how divine and human action relate.

**Limits and unresolved geography.** The Geshurites and Maacathites are named as border peoples
(14); no individual ancestor, separate encounter, or conquest of them is narrated. Their names
are retained here without inventing person pages. Chinnereth is linked to the existing sea page
as the boundary landmark corresponding to Numbers 34:11, not asserted to be a distinct city.
The differing Havvoth Jair accounts remain explicitly flagged on its place page. Gilead’s
allocation to different groups is clarified by the division into half and remainder (12–16),
not silently treated as an exclusive grant of the whole region to each recipient.

**Courage and succession.** Joshua is addressed in verse 21, and the encouragement concludes
in verse 22. The next unit turns to Moses’ own plea to cross (23); that contrast is left for
its own ingestion. The planned boundary and title are retained.
