# The Year of Release and the Open Hand

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 15:1–11

## Summary

*"At the end of every seven years, you shall cancel debts"* (15:1). Every creditor releases what he
has lent to his neighbor and does not press his brother for payment, *"because Yahweh's release has
been proclaimed"* (2). A foreigner may still be required to pay; what is owed by a brother, *"your
hand shall release"* (3).

Two promises follow. *"There shall be no poor with you"*, because Yahweh will bless Israel in the
land, *"if only you diligently listen"* to his voice (4–5). Israel will lend to many nations and not
borrow, and rule over many nations and not be ruled (6).

Then the law turns to the poor man who is there after all — *"one of your brothers … within any of
your gates"*. Israel is not to harden its heart or shut its hand, but to open the hand and lend him
*"sufficient for his need"* (7–8). Moses names the calculation the release invites: *"The seventh
year, the year of release, is at hand"*, and so the eye turns evil and the poor brother gets nothing.
He will cry to Yahweh, *"and it be sin to you"* (9). Israel is to give without a grieving heart, and
Yahweh will bless the giver's work (10). The unit closes: *"the poor will never cease out of the
land"*, so open the hand to the brother, the needy, and the poor (11).

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, whose *"release has been proclaimed"* (2), who promises to bless Israel in the
  land (4, 6, 10), and who hears the poor brother's cry (9).
- [[Moses]] — the speaker, not named in these verses; *"which I command you today"* (5) and
  *"Therefore I command you"* (11).
- The creditor, the neighbor and brother, the foreigner (*nokrî*), and the poor brother are
  categories, not named individuals.

## Places

- *"The land which Yahweh your God gives you for an inheritance"* (4, 7) — the setting of every
  provision; no place is named.
- *"Within any of your gates"* (7) — Israel's own towns.

## Notes

### Release

The key word is *šəmiṭṭâ*, *release* (15:1, 2, 9), with its verb *šāmaṭ* (WLC 15:2, 3, morphhb).
Verse 2 doubles it — *šāmôṭ kol-baʿal maššēh yādô*, literally *every owner of a loan of his hand
shall release* — and gives the reason: *kî-qārāʾ šəmiṭṭâ laYHWH*, *for a release to Yahweh has been
called* (WLC 15:2, morphhb). The release is Yahweh's before it is the creditor's.

The verb has been used once before in the law, in Exodus 23:11, where the land in the seventh year is
*tišməṭennâ*, *let it rest* (WEB *"let it rest and lie fallow"*, WLC Exodus 23:11, morphhb). There
the land is released so the poor may eat from it. Here what is released is a debt. Leviticus 25:1–7
calls the same year a *šabbat* for the land and says nothing of debts. See
[[The Seventh-Year Release - Exodus 23, Leviticus 25, and Deuteronomy 15]].

**Whether the release cancels a debt outright or suspends collection for the year is not settled by
the text.** The WEB renders 15:1 *"you shall cancel debts"*; the Hebrew has only *taʿăśeh
šəmiṭṭâ*, *you shall make a release* (WLC 15:1, morphhb), and 15:2 says the creditor *"shall not
require payment"* (*lōʾ-yiggōś*). Both readings have been held. 15:9, where the approach of the
year makes a lender refuse, suggests at least that a loan made late in the cycle was at risk. The wiki
records the question and does not decide it. Later Jewish tradition is widely reported to have
devised a legal instrument, the *prozbul*, so that loans would continue to be made before the seventh
year; its sources were not checked in this pass.

### Brother and foreigner

*"Of a foreigner you may require it"* (3). The word is *nokrî* (WLC 15:3, morphhb), not *gēr*, the
resident foreigner who in 14:29 eats the third-year tithe. The release is for *"his neighbor and his
brother"* (2). Deuteronomy 23:19–20 will draw the same line for interest. A *nokrî* would, on the
usual reading, be a foreigner passing through for trade rather than a dependent living in Israel's
towns; that reading is an inference from the contrast of terms, not a statement of this verse.

### No poor, and the poor who will not cease

**Verse 4 and verse 11 say opposite things, and the unit holds them together.** *"There shall be no
poor with you"* (4) is *ʾepes kî lōʾ yihyeh-bəkā ʾebyôn* (WLC 15:4, morphhb). *"The poor will never
cease out of the land"* (11) is *kî lōʾ-yeḥdal ʾebyôn miqqereb hāʾāreṣ* (WLC 15:11, morphhb). Both
use *ʾebyôn*, *needy*. Verse 4 is conditional on listening (5) and grounded in blessing; verse 7 then
begins *"If a poor man … is with you"*, and verse 11 says he always will be. The text does not say
whether 11 expects Israel to fail the condition of 5, or whether 4 describes what the release and the
open hand are meant to bring about. The wiki records both statements as they stand.

Jesus' saying at Bethany, *"you always have the poor with you"*, is commonly read as an echo of 15:11.
See [[The Poor Will Never Cease - Deuteronomy 15 and the Poor Always with You]].

### Lender to the nations

*"You will lend to many nations, but you will not borrow"* (6). The verb is *ʿābaṭ*, to give or take a
pledge-loan (WLC 15:6, 8, morphhb), the same verb used for lending to the poor brother in 15:8. The
promise returns at 28:12 with a different verb, *lāwâ*, and its reverse at 28:44: the foreigner
*"will lend to you, and you won't lend to him"* (WLC 28:12, 44, morphhb). Lending is a sign of
blessing in Deuteronomy; the same chapter forbids using it to keep the poor in debt.

### The hand and the heart

The body words carry the law. The creditor is *baʿal maššēh yādô*, the owner of *his hand's loan* (2);
the brother's debt is released by *"your hand"* (3). Israel must not *ʾāmaṣ*, *harden*, its heart, or
*qāpaṣ*, *shut*, its hand (7) (WLC 15:7, morphhb). The verb *ʾāmaṣ* with *ləbāb* is what Yahweh did to
Sihon's heart at 2:30, *"made his heart obstinate"* (WLC 2:30, morphhb). Here Israel is warned not
to do it to itself against a brother. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

The positive command doubles the verb twice: *pātōaḥ tiptaḥ*, *you shall surely open* (8, 11), and
*haʿăbēṭ taʿăbîṭennû*, *you shall surely lend him* (8) (WLC, morphhb). The loan is measured by need,
*dê maḥsōrô ʾăšer yeḥsar lô*, *"sufficient for his need, which he lacks"* (8).

### The thought in the heart and the evil eye

Verse 9 names the excuse before anyone makes it. The *"wicked thought"* is *dābār … bəliyyaʿal*, a
*word of worthlessness* in the heart (WLC 15:9, morphhb); *bəliyyaʿal* last appeared at 13:13 for the
*"wicked fellows"* who lead a town to other gods (WLC 13:14, the Hebrew verse numbering, morphhb). The
result is that *"your eye be evil"*, *wərāʿâ ʿênəkā* (9). The same root returns in 10: *wəlōʾ-yēraʿ
ləbābkā*, *"your heart shall not be grieved"* — literally, *shall not be evil* (WLC 15:9–10,
morphhb). The evil eye toward a brother returns in the curses at 28:54.

### He will cry to Yahweh

The poor man refused *"cry to Yahweh against you, and it be sin to you"* (9). Exodus 22:23 and 27 had
God promise to hear the cry of the oppressed and of the debtor whose cloak is held. Deuteronomy 24:15
repeats this clause almost word for word for the unpaid labourer: *wəqārāʾ ʿālêkā ʾel-YHWH wəhāyâ
bəkā ḥēṭʾ* in 15:9; the same words, negated, at 24:15 (WLC, morphhb). In both places the refusal
itself is *ḥēṭʾ*, sin; no other offence is needed. See [[Sin]] and [[Prayer]].

### Blessing on the giver's work

*"Yahweh your God will bless you in all your work, and in all that you put your hand to"* (10). The
first half, *bəkol-maʿăśekā*, echoes 14:29's *bəkol-maʿăśēh yādəkā*; the second, *bəkōl mišlaḥ yādekā*, recurs at 23:20 for
the brother who lends without interest (WLC 14:29; 15:10; 23:21 Hebrew numbering, morphhb). Giving is
promised the blessing that the lender fears losing.

### Boundary

The planned boundary (15:1–11) is kept. 15:12 turns from debts to a Hebrew servant *"sold to you"*,
released in the seventh year with gifts. The two units share the seven-year count and the motive of
generosity, and the next pericope continues directly from this one.
