# A Prophet Like Moses

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 18:9–22

## Summary

The list of offices that began with the judges (16:18) and went on to the king (17:14–20) and the
priests (18:1–8) ends with the prophet. Before naming the office, the law names what it replaces.

**What the nations listen to (18:9–14).** *"When you have come into the land which Yahweh your God
gives you, you shall not learn to imitate the abominations of those nations"* (9). Nine kinds of person
*"shall not be found with you"*: *"anyone who makes his son or his daughter to pass through the fire,
one who uses divination, one who practices sorcery, or an enchanter, or a sorcerer, or a charmer, or a
consulter with a familiar spirit, or a wizard, or a necromancer"* (10–11). *"Whoever does these things
is an abomination to Yahweh"*, and these abominations are why Yahweh *"drives them out from before
you"* (12). Then a one-line command, *"You shall be perfect with Yahweh your God"* (13), and the
contrast it serves: *"these nations that you shall dispossess listen to those who practice sorcery and
to diviners; but as for you, Yahweh your God has not allowed you so to do"* (14).

**What Israel will listen to (18:15–19).** *"Yahweh your God will raise up to you a prophet from among
you, of your brothers, like me. You shall listen to him"* (15). Moses grounds the promise in Horeb. The
people asked there not to hear Yahweh's voice or see *"this great fire"* again, *"that I not die"* (16),
and Yahweh answered, *"They have well said that which they have spoken"* (17) — the same approval
recorded at 5:28. So the prophet is Yahweh's answer to the people's own request for a go-between:
*"I will raise them up a prophet from among their brothers, like you. I will put my words in his mouth,
and he shall speak to them all that I shall command him"* (18). Whoever *"will not listen to my words
which he shall speak in my name, I will require it of him"* (19).

**The prophet who was not sent (18:20–22).** A prophet who *"speaks a word presumptuously in my name,
which I have not commanded him to speak, or who speaks in the name of other gods, that same prophet
shall die"* (20). Moses anticipates the obvious question — *"How shall we know the word which Yahweh has
not spoken?"* (21) — and gives a test that can only be applied afterwards: *"if the thing doesn't follow,
nor happen, that is the thing which Yahweh has not spoken. The prophet has spoken it presumptuously. You
shall not be afraid of him"* (22).

**How the passage fits together.** The section is one contrast. The nations seek to know the hidden
things by the arts of verses 10–11; Israel is to hear a person to whom Yahweh speaks. Divination begins
from the human side and tries to get an answer; prophecy begins from Yahweh's side and is sent. That is
why the section does not end with the promise but with the counterfeit: once Israel's knowledge of
Yahweh's will comes by a human speaker, the speaker himself has to be tested. Deuteronomy 13:1–5 has
already given the other test — a sign that comes true but leads to other gods. See
[[Two Tests of a Prophet - Deuteronomy 13.1-5 and 18.20-22]].

**Who is the prophet?** The singular *"a prophet"* can be read in more than one way, and readers have
long taken it in each:

- *An office.* Yahweh will keep raising prophets in Moses' line, one after another, as he will keep
  raising judges and kings. The list of offices in 16:18–18:22 supports this, and so does the test of
  20–22, which assumes many prophets, some false.
- *One prophet still to come.* Deuteronomy 34:10 says that *"there has not arisen a prophet in Israel
  like Moses, whom Yahweh knew face to face"* — written when the line of prophets had already begun, by
  any reading of that verse's vantage point. By the time of the Gospels the phrase *"the prophet"* is
  used as a title of an expected figure distinct from the Christ and from Elijah (John 1:21, 25), and Acts
  3:22–23 and 7:37 apply 18:15 to Jesus. See
  [[A Prophet Like Me - Deuteronomy 18.15-19 in John, Acts, and the Transfiguration]].
- *Both.* The office is what the law establishes, and the unmatched prophet of 34:10 is what the office
  points toward.

The wiki records the readings and adopts none. What the passage itself fixes is the prophet's
qualifications: raised by Yahweh, an Israelite (*"of your brothers"*), and like Moses in the one respect
the text names — Yahweh's words put in his mouth.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, to whom the nine practices are an abomination (12), who drives out the nations (12),
  who raises up the prophet (15, 18), puts his words in his mouth (18), and will require it of whoever
  does not listen (19).
- [[Moses]] — the speaker, unnamed here; the prophet is to be *"like me"* (15), *"like you"* (18). He
  recalls his own role at Horeb as the people's chosen go-between (16–17).
- The prophet (15–19) — unnamed and undated. No page; see the readings above.
- The presumptuous prophet (20–22) — a type. No page.
- The nine practitioners (10–11) — types. No pages.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — *"in the day of the assembly"* (16), when the people asked not to hear the voice again.
- The land Yahweh gives (9) — unnamed. No page.

## Themes

- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — the Torah's law of the prophet: his origin, his authority, and the test of
  his word.
- [[Idolatry]] — child-burning and the arts of 10–11 as *"abominations of those nations"* (9, 12), and the
  prophet who speaks *"in the name of other gods"* (20).
- [[The Nations]] — what the nations listen to (14), and why they are driven out (12).
- [[Integrity]] — *"You shall be perfect with Yahweh your God"* (13), *tāmîm*.

  Horeb (16–18).
- [[The Fear of God]] — the fear of the fire at Horeb (16), and *"you shall not be afraid of him"*, the
  false prophet (22).
- [[Language and Speech]] — *"I will put my words in his mouth"* (18); a word spoken *"presumptuously"*
  (20, 22).
- [[The Names of God]] — the prophet speaks *"in my name"* (19–20), and the false one *"in the name of
  other gods"* (20).
- [[Brotherhood]] — the prophet is raised *"of your brothers"* (15), *"from among their brothers"* (18),
  as the king was to be (17:15).

## Notes

**Nine practices.** By the WLC, verses 10–11 list nine participles or participial phrases, set out
here with WEB's renderings:

| Hebrew (18:10–11, WLC) | WEB | Elsewhere in Genesis–Deuteronomy (WLC lemma search, morphhb) |
|---|---|---|
| *maʿăbîr bənô-ûbittô bāʾēš* | *makes his son or his daughter to pass through the fire* | *ʿābar* in the causative; Deuteronomy 12:31's *"burn their sons and their daughters in the fire"* uses a different verb, *śārap* |
| *qōsēm qəsāmîm* | *uses divination* | noun *qesem* Numbers 22:7; 23:23; verb only here and 18:14 |
| *məʿônēn* | *practices sorcery* | Leviticus 19:26; morphhb gives Genesis 9:14's *"when I bring a cloud"* the same number with a different sense letter (6049 a, against 6049 b here) |
| *mənaḥēš* | *an enchanter* | Genesis 30:27; 44:5, 15; Leviticus 19:26; noun Numbers 23:23; 24:1 |
| *məkaššēp* | *a sorcerer* | Exodus 7:11; 22:18 (22:17 in Hebrew numbering) |
| *ḥōbēr ḥāber* | *a charmer* | the noun *ḥeber* in this sense only here; the verb *ḥābar*, *join*, is used of the tabernacle curtains (Exodus 26:3 and elsewhere) |
| *šōʾēl ʾôb* | *a consulter with a familiar spirit* | Leviticus 19:31; 20:6, 27 |
| *yiddəʿōnî* | *a wizard* | Leviticus 19:31; 20:6, 27, always paired with *ʾôb* |
| *dōrēš ʾel-hammētîm* | *a necromancer* | the phrase only here |

The English words are not consistent across WEB's books: Leviticus 19:26 renders *məʿônēn*'s verb
*"practice sorcery"* and *nāḥaš* *"use enchantments"*, Leviticus 19:31 renders *ʾôbōt* *"mediums"* and
*yiddəʿōnîm* *"wizards"*, and here *ʾôb* is *"a familiar spirit"*. What each practice actually involved is
mostly a matter of etymology and comparison, not of anything the Torah describes. The connection of
*məʿônēn* with *ʿānān*, *cloud*, which morphhb's shared number for Genesis 9:14 hints at while tagging it
as a separate sense, is a possibility, not an established meaning; it was not checked against a lexicon. See
[[Diviners, Mediums, and the Dead - Deuteronomy 18.10-11 and the Torah's Forbidden Arts]].

The passing through fire stands at the head of the list. Deuteronomy 12:31 has already said the nations
*"burn their sons and their daughters in the fire to their gods"*. Whether *"pass through the fire"* in
18:10 means the same thing as 12:31 or a lesser rite of dedication is debated; the wiki's discussion is on
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]]. Its position in a list of ways of getting
knowledge has suggested to some readers a divinatory rite; the text does not say.

**"Perfect."** *Tāmîm tihyeh ʿim YHWH ʾĕlōhêkā* (18:13, WLC). *Tāmîm* is the word of Noah (Genesis 6:9),
of Abram's charge (*"Walk before me, and be blameless"*, 17:1), and of the unblemished sacrificial animal
throughout Leviticus. In Deuteronomy it occurs only here and at 32:4, of Yahweh's own work (WLC lemma
8549). The command is set in the middle of the section, between the arts and the prophet: whole-hearted
with Yahweh, and so not divided between him and the diviners. See [[Integrity]].

**"Raise up."** *Yāqîm*, *ʾāqîm* (18:15, 18, WLC), from *qûm*. 34:10 uses the same verb for what has not
happened: *wəlōʾ-qām nābîʾ ʿôd bəyiśrāʾēl kəmōšeh*, *"there has not arisen a prophet in Israel like
Moses"* (WLC; WEB). *Kāmōnî*, *like me* (15), and *kāmôkā*, *like you* (18), say the likeness twice, once
in Moses' mouth and once in Yahweh's. The verse does not say in what respect the prophet is like Moses
beyond the words put in his mouth; Numbers 12:6–8 had already said that other prophets receive by vision
and dream, not *"mouth to mouth"*. See [[Mouth to Mouth - Numbers 12.6-8, Exodus 33, and Deuteronomy 34]].

**"I will put my words in his mouth."** *Wənātattî dəbāray bəpîw* (18:18, WLC). Yahweh had said to Moses
at the bush, *"I will be with your mouth"* (Exodus 4:12, stored WEB), and of Aaron, *"you shall put the
words in his mouth"* (4:15). Jeremiah 1:9 reports the same act at that prophet's call: *"Behold, I have
put my words in your mouth"* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com).

**"I will require it of him."** *ʾĀnōkî ʾedrōš mēʿimmô* (18:19, WLC). The verb is *dāraš*, *seek*,
*inquire* — the same verb as *dōrēš ʾel-hammētîm* in 18:11, the one who *seeks* the dead. Yahweh *seeks*
from the man who would not hear. The pun is in the Hebrew and is not visible in WEB.

**"Presumptuously."** *Yāzîd* (18:20) and *bəzādôn* (18:22), from *zîd*. In Deuteronomy the root is used
of Israel's going up against the Amorites after the sentence (*"were presumptuous"*, 1:43) and of the man
who will not obey priest or judge (17:12–13, *bəzādôn* and *yəzîdûn*, WLC). The presumptuous prophet is
the third such case in the book, and like the second he dies.

**"You shall not be afraid of him."** *Lōʾ tāgûr mimmennû* (18:22, WLC). The verb is *gûr* in the sense
*be afraid*, which morphhb tags as a homonym of *gûr*, *sojourn*, the verb of the Levite who *"lives as a
foreigner"* at 18:6 (both lemma 1481, different senses). The line answers 18:16: the people feared Yahweh's
fire and asked for a prophet; they are not to fear a prophet Yahweh did not send.

**Acts 3:23.** Peter's citation of 18:15, 19 in Acts 3:22–23 ends with *"every soul that will not listen
to that prophet will be utterly destroyed from among the people"* (WEB). Deuteronomy 18:19 says *"I will
require it of him"*. The wording in Acts is closer in sense to the cutting-off formula of the Torah, as in
Leviticus 23:29, *"shall be cut off from his people"* (stored WEB). This comparison is of the WEB English;
the Greek of Acts and of the Septuagint was not checked for this page. See
[[A Prophet Like Me - Deuteronomy 18.15-19 in John, Acts, and the Transfiguration]].

**The test of 18:22 and Jeremiah 28.** Jeremiah 28:9 applies the same criterion to a prophet of peace:
*"when the word of the prophet happens, then the prophet will be known, that Yahweh has truly sent him"*
(WEB, checked at bible-api.com). Jeremiah is not yet ingested.

**Not resolved here.** Whether *"a prophet"* (18:15, 18) is an office or one figure; what each of the nine
practices was; whether *"pass through the fire"* is burning or dedication; how the test of 18:22 is applied
to a word about the far future or to a conditional threat that is withdrawn when the hearers repent. Each
is recorded, none adopted.
