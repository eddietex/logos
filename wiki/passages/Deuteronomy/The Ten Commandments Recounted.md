# The Ten Commandments Recounted

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 5:1–22

## Summary

Moses summons all Israel to hear, learn, and practice the covenant instruction. He identifies
Horeb’s covenant with the assembly alive before him and recalls both Yahweh’s direct speech and
his own mediating role. The deliverer from Egypt commands exclusive worship, reverence for his
name, shared Sabbath rest, honor for parents, and protection of the neighbor’s life, marriage,
property, and truthful standing. Desire for the neighbor’s household is itself prohibited.
The unit closes with the public voice and the two written tablets entrusted to Moses.

## Key verses

> **5:1** Moses called to all Israel, and said to them, “Hear, Israel, the statutes and the ordinances which I speak in your ears today, that you may learn them, and observe to do them.”

> **5:3** Yahweh didn’t make this covenant with our fathers, but with us, even us, who are all of us here alive today.

> **5:5** (I stood between Yahweh and you at that time, to show you Yahweh’s word: for you were afraid because of the fire, and didn’t go up onto the mountain;) saying,

> **5:14** but the seventh day is a Sabbath to Yahweh your God, in which you shall not do any work, you, nor your son, nor your daughter, nor your male servant, nor your female servant, nor your ox, nor your donkey, nor any of your livestock, nor your stranger who is within your gates; that your male servant and your female servant may rest as well as you.

> **5:15** You shall remember that you were a servant in the land of Egypt, and Yahweh your God brought you out of there by a mighty hand and by an outstretched arm. Therefore Yahweh your God commanded you to keep the Sabbath day.

> **5:21** “You shall not covet your neighbor’s wife. Neither shall you desire your neighbor’s house, his field, or his male servant, or his female servant, his ox, or his donkey, or anything that is your neighbor’s.”

> **5:22** Yahweh spoke these words to all your assembly on the mountain out of the middle of the fire, of the cloud, and of the thick darkness, with a great voice. He added no more. He wrote them on two stone tablets, and gave them to me.

## Notes

### The covenant with the living assembly

Verse 3 emphatically contrasts the fathers with those alive today. Deuteronomy 4:31 nevertheless
remembers the covenant sworn to the fathers. These are distinct statements, not permission to
delete the ancestral promises from the theology of the book. “Fathers” may refer to the patriarchs
or the preceding generation; this page leaves the referent open. Moses binds his present hearers
to Horeb corporately. That does not require asserting that every individual present in Moab
was personally at the mountain, especially after the death of the condemned generation recalled
in 2:14–16.

### Direct speech and mediation

“Face to face” in verse 4 describes the encounter, while 4:12 and 4:15 insist that no form was seen.
It should not be used to assert a visible divine body. Verse 5 places Moses between God and the
people, and verse 22 still says the whole assembly heard the words. Both claims belong to this
account. The people's fuller request and God's response are treated in
[[The People's Request for a Mediator]], Deuteronomy 5:23–33.

### Same commands, differing emphases

Exodus 20:8–11 explains Sabbath by creation; here 5:14–15 explains shared rest by remembered
slavery and deliverance. These explanations differ without commanding opposite practices.
Deuteronomy expressly says servants are to rest as the hearer does, but servants remain within
the household list of verse 21. A real protection is present alongside continuing hierarchy.

Honor for both parents carries the added well-being clause in verse 16. The wife precedes the
house in verse 21, and the field is explicitly named, unlike Exodus 20:17. The WEB uses both
“covet” and “desire”; no independent Hebrew lexical claim is made. The law addresses desire
as well as acts, so its concern cannot be reduced to outward behavior alone.

The image ban joins jealousy, generational judgment, and loving kindness (9–10). **A tension
remains with Job 21:19–21**, which objects to punishment deferred to children. Continuing hatred
and the distinction between inherited consequences and personal guilt are possible interpretive
approaches; this passage does not itself provide a complete resolution.

### The scope of the public words

Verse 22 closes the speech with the statement that Yahweh added no more and wrote the words on
two stone tablets. This is a boundary to the public declaration, not a denial that more law will
follow: 5:31 explicitly anticipates further instruction through Moses. The planned 1–22 boundary
is retained because the tablets close this recitation before the account of the people's request.
No particular traditional numbering of the commandments is imposed.
