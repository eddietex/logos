# The Portion of the Priests and Levites

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 18:1–8

## Summary

After the judges (16:18–17:13) and the king (17:14–20), the list of offices reaches the priests.
The law says what they do not receive, what they do receive, and what happens when a Levite from a
country town comes to the chosen place to serve.

**No inheritance; Yahweh is their inheritance (18:1–2).** *"The priests the Levites, all the tribe
of Levi, shall have no portion nor inheritance with Israel. They shall eat the offerings of Yahweh
made by fire and his portion"* (1). *"They shall have no inheritance among their brothers. Yahweh is
their inheritance, as he has spoken to them"* (2). The statement is the one already made at 10:9 and
behind the Levite of 12:12 and 14:27, 29, who *"has no portion nor inheritance with you"*. Here it
opens a law about what the tribe eats instead.

**The priests' due (18:3–5).** From whoever sacrifices an ox or a sheep, *"they shall give to the
priest the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the inner parts"* (3). To that are added *"the first fruits
of your grain, of your new wine, and of your oil, and the first of the fleece of your sheep"* (4). The
reason is election: *"For Yahweh your God has chosen him out of all your tribes, to stand to minister
in Yahweh's name, him and his sons for ever"* (5). The portions differ from the breast and right thigh
of Leviticus 7:31–34, and the fleece is named nowhere else in the Torah as a gift; see the notes.

**The Levite who comes to the place (18:6–8).** *"If a Levite comes from any of your gates out of all
Israel, where he lives as a foreigner, and comes with all the desire of his soul to the place which
Yahweh shall choose"* (6), *"then he shall minister in the name of Yahweh his God, as all his brothers
the Levites do, who stand there before Yahweh"* (7). *"They shall have like portions to eat, in
addition to that which comes from the sale of his family possessions"* (8). The Levite of the towns —
the one the household must not forsake (12:19; 14:27) — may come up when he wishes, serve beside the
Levites already at the place, and eat the same share. The newcomer is not to be turned away or given
less.

**What the passage settles and what it leaves open.** It settles how the tribe without land is fed at
the altar: by fixed parts of the sacrifice and by first fruits. It also secures a place at the
sanctuary for the Levite from the towns. It does not say, in words, whether every Levite may do what
Numbers reserves for Aaron's sons, the question the phrase *"the priests the Levites"* has raised
since 17:9. The verb for the country Levite's service in 18:7 is the verb used of the chosen priest
in 18:5, which has made this the main text for that question.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, who is the tribe's inheritance (2), chooses the priest *"out of all your tribes"*
  (5), and before whom the Levites stand (7).
- [[Moses]] — the speaker, unnamed here.
- [[Levi]] — *"all the tribe of Levi"* (1), defined as *"the priests the Levites"*. The patriarch is
  not in view; his tribe is.
- The priest, *"him and his sons"* (5) — unnamed. No new person page; [[Aaron]] is not named in the
  passage, and whether the priest of 18:3–5 is Aaron's line or any Levite is the question in the
  notes.
- The Levite from the towns (6–8) — a type, not a person. No page.

## Places

- The place which Yahweh shall choose (6) — unnamed, as throughout chapters 12–17. No place page;
  see [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]].

## Themes

- [[Priesthood]] — the priests' due, and the country Levite's equal right to serve and eat at the
  place.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — no portion nor inheritance with Israel (1–2); the Levite *"lives as a
  foreigner"* in the town he comes from (6).
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — the offerings by fire are the tribe's food (1), and the priest's cut of
  each ox or sheep is fixed (3).
- [[Festivals and First Fruits]] — first fruits of grain, new wine, and oil, and the first of the
  fleece, go to the priest (4).
- [[Call and Election]] — *"Yahweh your God has chosen him out of all your tribes"* (5); the place,
  too, is the one *"which Yahweh shall choose"* (6).
- [[Brotherhood]] — no inheritance *"among their brothers"* (2), and the newcomer serves *"as all his
  brothers the Levites do"* (7).
- [[The Names of God]] — the priest ministers *"in Yahweh's name"* (5, 7).

## Notes

**"His inheritance."** *ʾIššê YHWH wənaḥălātô yōʾkēlûn* (18:1, WLC). WEB renders *"the offerings of
Yahweh made by fire and his portion"*, but the noun is *naḥălâ*, *inheritance*, the same word as in
*"no portion nor inheritance"* earlier in the verse and *"Yahweh is their inheritance"* in 18:2. Whose
inheritance *his* means is not stated. It may be Yahweh's, the offerings that belong to him; or Levi's,
the tribe of the verse. Either way the verse puns on its own word: the tribe has no *naḥălâ*, and eats
a *naḥălâ*. *ʾIššê*, *offerings by fire*, occurs in Deuteronomy only here (WLC lemma 801, morphhb);
it is a common word of Leviticus (33 occurrences) and Numbers (14).

**"The priests' due."** *Mišpaṭ hakkōhănîm mēʾēt hāʿām* (18:3, WLC), literally *the judgment of the
priests from the people*. *Mišpāṭ* is the word of the courts in 16:18–17:13, here used of a right or
customary claim. 1 Samuel 2:13 opens with the same words, *ûmišpaṭ hakkōhănîm ʾet-hāʿām*, *"The custom
of the priests with the people"* (WEB), and goes on to describe Eli's sons taking whatever the fork
brought up. In the Torah the noun followed directly by *kōhănîm* occurs only at Deuteronomy 18:3 (WLC
sequence search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). See
[[The Custom of the Priests - Deuteronomy 18.3 and 1 Samuel 2.13-16]].

**Shoulder, cheeks, and inner parts.** *Hazzərōaʿ wəhalləḥāyayim wəhaqqēbâ* (18:3, WLC). *Zərōaʿ*
is the arm or foreleg; as a cut of a sacrifice it occurs elsewhere in the Torah only at Numbers 6:19,
the Nazirite's boiled shoulder. Everywhere else in Deuteronomy it is Yahweh's *"outstretched arm"*
(4:34; 5:15; 7:19; 9:29; 11:2; 26:8). *Ləḥāyayim*, the jaws, and *qēbâ*, the stomach, occur nowhere
else in Genesis–Deuteronomy (WLC lemma searches for 2220, 3895, 6896, morphhb). WEB's *"inner parts"*
is a general rendering; *qēbâ* names one organ, usually taken as the fourth stomach of a ruminant.

These are not the portions of Leviticus 7:31–34, where the waved breast and the heaved right thigh of
the peace offering go to Aaron and his sons (*ḥăzê hattənûpâ*, *šôq hattərûmâ*, WLC 7:34). The two
laws name different cuts. Several harmonizations are offered: that Deuteronomy's portions come from
animals slaughtered for food away from the altar (12:15, 21), not from peace offerings; that they are
an addition to Leviticus' portions; or that the two laws reflect different practice at different
times. Deuteronomy 18:3 itself says *"those who offer a sacrifice"*, *zōbəḥê hazzebaḥ*, which is
sacrificial language, though *zābaḥ* is also the verb of the profane slaughter of 12:15, 21. The text
does not decide between these readings, and the wiki adopts none. See
[[The Breast and the Right Thigh - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 7]].

**First fruits and fleece.** *Rēʾšît dəgānəkā tîrōšəkā wəyiṣhārekā wərēʾšît gēz ṣōʾnəkā* (18:4,
WLC). Numbers 18:12 gives Aaron the best of the oil, the new wine, and the grain, *rēʾšîtām*, *"the
first fruits of them"*, with the same three nouns in the opposite order (WLC). The fleece is new: *gēz*
occurs nowhere else in the Torah (WLC lemma 1488). Deuteronomy adds the one product of the flock that
is not food.

**"Him and his sons for ever."** *Bô bāḥar YHWH ʾĕlōhêkā mikkol-šəbāṭêkā* (18:5, WLC). The nearest
antecedent of *him* is *the priest* of verses 3–4, but *"out of all your tribes"* suggests a tribe —
Levi — rather than one family. 21:5 says the same of *"the priests the sons of Levi; for them Yahweh
your God has chosen to minister to him"* (*bām bāḥar*, WLC). So in Deuteronomy the choice falls on Levi
as priests, and Aaron is not mentioned in either verse. *Laʿămōd ləšārēt*, *to stand to minister*, is
the pair of 10:8 (*laʿămōd lipnê YHWH ləšārətô*) and 17:12 (*hāʿōmēd ləšāret šām*).

**Does the country Levite become a priest?** Verse 7 says he *wəšērēt*, *"shall minister"* (the verb
of 18:5), *"as all his brothers the Levites do, who stand there before Yahweh"*, *hāʿōmədîm šām lipnê
YHWH* (WLC). In 18:5 those two verbs describe the chosen priest; in 10:8 they describe the tribe. The
long-standing readings:

- *All Levites are eligible priests in Deuteronomy.* On this reading 18:6–8 gives any Levite the
  right to serve at the altar, and the Aaron-and-Levite distinction of Numbers 3–4 and 18 is not part
  of Deuteronomy's law. This is often joined to the view that the distinction in Numbers is later.
- *Deuteronomy assumes the distinction without stating it.* *"His brothers the Levites"* are those
  already serving in the Levites' own role, and the newcomer joins them at his own rank. The verbs
  *stand* and *minister* are also used of non-priestly Levites in Numbers (for example 16:9, stored
  WEB: *"to stand before the congregation to minister to them"*).
- *The law concerns livelihood, not rank.* Its point is verse 8: the newcomer eats *"like portions"*.

The wiki records the three and adopts none. [[Yahweh as Levi’s Inheritance - Numbers 18 and Deuteronomy 10]]
already warns against reading Deuteronomy's collective wording as abolishing Numbers' distinction; this
passage does not resolve it either way.

**"With all the desire of his soul."** *Bəkol-ʾawwat napšô* (18:6, WLC). The Levite comes because
he wants to, not because he is summoned or on a rota. The phrase echoes 12:15, 20–21, where Israel may
eat meat in the towns *"after all the desire of your soul"*; *ʾawwâ* occurs in Deuteronomy only in
those three verses and here (WLC lemma 185). The Levite *gār*, *"lives as a
foreigner"*, in his town (*gûr*, lemma 1481): the verb of the *gēr*, the sojourner, here used of an
Israelite who has no land of his own.

**"The sale of his family possessions."** *Ləbad mimkārāyw ʿal-hāʾābôt* (18:8, WLC), literally
something like *apart from his sales upon the fathers*. The phrase is obscure, and WEB's rendering is
one interpretation. Others take it as income from a sale of ancestral property, or from a family
arrangement whose details are lost, or as a textual difficulty. *Mimkār*, *sale* or *thing sold*,
occurs elsewhere in the Torah only in Leviticus 25 (25:14, 25, 27, 28, 29, 33, 50), the law of land
sale and redemption, where 25:33 speaks of the Levite's house *"that was sold"* (*mimkar bayit*, WLC).
Whatever the phrase means, it assumes the Levite may have something of his own to sell — another sign
that *"no portion nor inheritance"* means no tribal territory, not no property. See
[[The Priests Who Kept Their Land and the Levites Who Got None]].

**Josiah.** 2 Kings 23:8–9 reports that Josiah brought the priests out of the cities of Judah and
defiled the high places, *"Nevertheless the priests of the high places didn't come up to Yahweh's altar
in Jerusalem, but they ate unleavened bread among their brothers"* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com).
*Bətôk ʾăḥêhem*, *among their brothers* (WLC), echoes the *brothers* of Deuteronomy 18:7. Many readers
take 2 Kings 23:9 as a case where 18:6–8 was not applied; others note that priests of the high places
are not obviously the Levite of 18:6, who comes from a town where he sojourns, not from a rival altar.
See [[The Priests of the High Places - Deuteronomy 18.6-8 and 2 Kings 23.9]].

**Not resolved here.** Whose *"inheritance"* the tribe eats (18:1); how the cuts of 18:3 relate to the
breast and thigh of Leviticus 7; whether *"him and his sons"* (18:5) means Aaron's line or the tribe;
whether the country Levite of 18:7 serves as priest; what *"the sale of his family possessions"* (18:8)
was; and how far 2 Kings 23:9 departs from this law. Each is recorded, none adopted.
