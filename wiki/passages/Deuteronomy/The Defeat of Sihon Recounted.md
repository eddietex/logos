# The Defeat of Sihon Recounted

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 2:24–37

## Summary

Yahweh commands Israel to cross the Arnon and begin possessing Sihon’s land. Moses sends a
peaceful embassy from Kedemoth offering to stay on the highway and pay for food and water.
Sihon refuses; Moses explains that Yahweh hardened him in order to deliver him to Israel.
Sihon attacks at Jahaz, and Israel defeats him, takes his cities, destroys their inhabitants,
including women and little ones, and keeps livestock and goods as spoil. The victories extend
from Aroer to Gilead, while Ammon and the places Yahweh forbade remain untouched.

## Key verses

Complete unit, copied verbatim from stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:24–37.

> **24** “Rise up, take your journey, and pass over the valley of the Arnon: behold, I have given into your hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land; begin to possess it, and contend with him in battle.

> **25** Today I will begin to put the dread of you and the fear of you on the peoples who are under the whole sky, who shall hear the report of you, and shall tremble, and be in anguish because of you.”

> **26** I sent messengers out of the wilderness of Kedemoth to Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace, saying,

> **27** “Let me pass through your land. I will go along by the highway, I will turn neither to the right hand nor to the left.

> **28** You shall sell me food for money, that I may eat; and give me water for money, that I may drink. Just let me pass through on my feet,

> **29** as the children of Esau who dwell in Seir, and the Moabites who dwell in Ar, did to me; until I pass over the Jordan into the land which Yahweh our God gives us.”

> **30** But Sihon king of Heshbon would not let us pass by him; for Yahweh your God hardened his spirit, and made his heart obstinate, that he might deliver him into your hand, as it is today.

> **31** Yahweh said to me, “Behold, I have begun to deliver up Sihon and his land before you. Begin to possess, that you may inherit his land.”

> **32** Then Sihon came out against us, he and all his people, to battle at Jahaz.

> **33** Yahweh our God delivered him up before us; and we struck him, his sons, and all his people.

> **34** We took all his cities at that time, and utterly destroyed every inhabited city, with the women and the little ones. We left no one remaining.

> **35** Only the livestock we took for plunder for ourselves, with the plunder of the cities which we had taken.

> **36** From Aroer, which is on the edge of the valley of the Arnon, and the city that is in the valley, even to Gilead, there was not a city too high for us. Yahweh our God delivered up all before us.

> **37** Only to the land of the children of Ammon you didn’t come near; all the banks of the river Jabbok, and the cities of the hill country, and wherever Yahweh our God forbade us.

## Notes

### Peaceful terms after the command to fight

The order in the speech matters: the command to contend (24) precedes the peaceful embassy
(26). The text does not explain whether the embassy could have produced another outcome.
Sihon’s refusal and Yahweh’s hardening are both stated (30); neither should be deleted to
resolve the difficulty. The promise of dread among peoples (25) is wider than the particular
battle narrated here, not a report that Israel has conquered the whole world.

### The neighboring peoples and the apparent contradiction

Verse 29 cites Esau’s descendants and the Moabites as a precedent. Numbers 20:18–21 reports
Edom refusing passage. Restricting the comparison to the purchases in verse 28, or locating
it at another stage of the journey, offers possible readings, not a demonstrated harmony.
The earlier command to purchase in Deuteronomy 2:6 was not itself evidence of a completed
sale; this later claim adds something and must be considered separately.

### The destruction and its limits

Verse 34 explicitly includes women and little ones and reports no survivors. The narrative
credits victory to Yahweh, but supplies no individual charges against the civilians. The
wiki retains that moral difficulty rather than translating the scene into a battle against
soldiers alone. Verse 35 distinguishes living livestock and retained goods from the killed
inhabitants. No Hebrew lexical claim is made here.

Ammon remains protected (37), as commanded in 2:19. The unnamed city within the Arnon valley
is not assigned a speculative name or its own page. Seir remains under Edom; the wilderness
of Kedemoth receives a place page without an asserted modern identification. Sihon’s unnamed
sons remain in his page rather than receiving invented identities.
