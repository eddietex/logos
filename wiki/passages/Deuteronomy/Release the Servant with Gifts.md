# Release the Servant with Gifts

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 15:12–18

## Summary

*"If your brother, a Hebrew man, or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you, and serves you six years; then
in the seventh year you shall let him go free from you"* (15:12). The release is not to be bare:
*"you shall not let him go empty"* (13). The master is to *"furnish him liberally"* from flock,
threshing floor, and wine press — *"As Yahweh your God has blessed you, you shall give to him"* (14).
The reason is Israel's own history: *"You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt,
and Yahweh your God redeemed you"* (15).

One exception follows. If the servant says, *"I will not go out from you,"* because he loves the
master and his house and is well off there, the master takes an awl, thrusts it through his ear *"to
the door"*, and *"he shall be your servant forever"*. The same applies to a female servant (16–17).

The unit ends by answering the master's reluctance. Letting the servant go should not seem hard,
for he has served six years at *"double value of a hired hand"*, and Yahweh will bless the master in
all he does (18).

## Notes

### Brother first, Hebrew second

Exodus 21:2 begins *"If you buy a Hebrew servant"*. Deuteronomy begins with *"your brother"* and
only then names him *hāʿibrî ʾô hāʿibriyyâ*, *the Hebrew man or the Hebrew woman* (WLC 15:12,
morphhb). The word *brother* ties this law to the one just before it, where the release is for *"his
neighbor and his brother"* (15:2). The poor brother of 15:7–11 who needs a loan may become the brother
of 15:12 who has been sold.

The verb is *yimmākēr*, a niphal of *mākar*, *sell* (WLC 15:12, morphhb). It can be read as *is
sold* — the WEB's choice — or as *sells himself*, which is how the WEB renders the niphal at Leviticus
25:39. The verse does not say who did the selling, whether a creditor, a parent, or the man himself.

### Not empty, furnished like a necklace

*"You shall not let him go empty"* — *lōʾ təšalləḥennû rêqām* (WLC 15:13, morphhb). The verb *šillaḥ*,
*send away* or *let go*, appears four times in 15:12–18. It is the same verb as Moses' demand to
Pharaoh, *"Let my people go"*, *šallaḥ ʾet-ʿammî* (WLC Exodus 5:1, morphhb). *Rêqām*, *empty*,
occurs six times in the Torah in this sense: Genesis 31:42, where Jacob says Laban *"would have sent
me away empty"*, with the same verb *šillaḥ*; Exodus 3:21, where Israel *"shall not go
empty-handed"* from Egypt; Exodus 23:15 and 34:20 and Deuteronomy 16:16, where no one appears before
Yahweh empty; and here (WLC lemma search across Genesis–Deuteronomy, morphhb). **Israel is to do for
its released servant what God made Egypt do for Israel.** See
[[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]].

*"Furnish him liberally"* is *haʿănêq taʿănîq lô* (WLC 15:14, morphhb), a doubled verb from the root
of *ʿănāq*, *necklace*. The lemma occurs only in this verse in the Torah (morphhb lemma search). The
picture is usually explained as *put a necklace on him*, or *load his neck*; that is an inference from
the noun, and the verb's exact sense is not certain. Threshing floor and wine press are the harvest
the feast of tents waits for (16:13); the flock adds livestock. How much to give is left to the
master: *"As Yahweh your God has blessed you"* (14).

### Remember, and redeemed

Verse 15 is almost word for word the clause of 24:18: *wəzākartā kî ʿebed hāyîtā* … *wayyipdəkā YHWH
ʾĕlōhêkā* … *ʿal-kēn ʾānōkî məṣawwəkā* (WLC 15:15; 24:18, morphhb). Deuteronomy 5:15 has the same
opening for the Sabbath, but *brought you out* in place of *redeemed*; 16:12 and 24:22 have the
opening alone. The verb here is *pādâ*, *ransom* or *redeem*, the word Deuteronomy used in 7:8 and
9:26 for the exodus (morphhb). The master who releases a servant repeats on a small scale what
Yahweh did for Israel. See
[[You Were a Slave in Egypt - The Remembrance Refrain of Deuteronomy]].

Exodus 21:2–11 gives no reason for the release. Leviticus 25:42 gives one — *"they are my servants"*.
Deuteronomy gives another: Israel remembers being a slave. See
[[The Hebrew Servant Goes Free - Exodus 21, Deuteronomy 15, and Jeremiah 34]].

### The servant who stays

*"Because he loves you and your house, because he is well with you"* (16). Exodus 21:5 had the
servant say *"I love my master, my wife, and my children"*. There the master-given wife and children
are the reason for staying, since they cannot leave with him (21:4). Deuteronomy does not mention a
wife and children; the reason is the master's household and the servant's good there.

The rite is shorter. Exodus 21:6 has the master bring him *"to God"* (*ʾel-hāʾĕlōhîm*) and *"to the
door or to the doorpost"*. Deuteronomy has only *"thrust it through his ear to the door"*,
*wənātattâ bəʾoznô ûbaddelet*, literally *put it in his ear and in the door* (WLC 15:17; Exodus 21:6,
morphhb). **Deuteronomy has no "to God."** Many interpreters connect this with the law of the one
chosen place in chapter 12: if *hāʾĕlōhîm* in Exodus meant a local sanctuary, Deuteronomy would have
removed the rite from it. Others take *hāʾĕlōhîm* there as judges or household gods. Neither text
says which, so the omission is recorded here and the reason left open. The awl, *marṣēaʿ*, occurs in
the Torah only at Exodus 21:6 and here (morphhb).

*"Also to your female servant you shall do likewise"* (17). This gives the woman the same choice and
rite as the man, where Exodus 21:7 says the sold daughter *"shall not go out as the male servants do"*.
The tension is set out in
[[The Hebrew Servant Goes Free - Exodus 21, Deuteronomy 15, and Jeremiah 34]] and is not resolved
here.

*"Forever"* is *ʿebed ʿôlām*, *a servant of perpetuity* (WLC 15:17, morphhb). Leviticus 25:40–41
releases the Israelite at the Jubilee, which stands in tension with this *forever*. The tension is
recorded and not harmonized here.

### Double the hired hand

*"It shall not seem hard to you"* — *lōʾ-yiqšeh bəʿênekā*, literally *not be hard in your eyes* (WLC
15:18, morphhb). Like 15:9, the law names the owner's reluctance before it happens. The reason given
is *mišneh śəkar śākîr*, *"double value of a hired hand"* (WLC 15:18, morphhb). *Mišneh* means
*double* at Exodus 16:5, 22 and Genesis 43:12, 15, but at Deuteronomy 17:18 it means *a copy* of the
law (morphhb lemma search). So the phrase may mean the servant was worth twice a hired worker, or
that his service was *equivalent to* a hired worker's wages over six years. Both readings are held;
the wiki records the question and does not decide it. Either way, the master is told he has already
been paid.

The unit closes as 15:10 did, with blessing for the one who gives: *"Yahweh your God will bless you in
all that you do"* (18).

### Boundary

The planned boundary (15:12–18) is kept. 15:19 turns to the firstborn of herd and flock, a separate
subject, though it shares the household eating *"before Yahweh your God year by year"*.
