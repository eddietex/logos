# Hear the Statutes and Remember Horeb

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 4:1–14

## Summary

With *Now, Israel* (1) the retrospect of chapters 1–3 turns into exhortation. Moses calls Israel
to listen to the statutes and ordinances he teaches, so that they may live and go in to possess
the land. The word he commands is not to be added to or taken away from (2). The first proof is
recent and close: the men who followed Baal Peor were destroyed, while those who held fast to
Yahweh are *all alive today* (3–4).

Keeping the statutes is then called Israel's wisdom in the sight of the peoples. The nations will
hear of them and call Israel a wise and understanding people (5–6). Two rhetorical questions give
the reasons: no great nation has a god so near whenever it calls, and none has statutes and
ordinances so righteous as this law (7–8).

The second half is a warning against forgetting. Israel must keep watch over its own soul so that
what its eyes saw does not depart from its heart, and must make it known to children and
grandchildren (9). What they saw is the day at Horeb: Yahweh summoned the people to hear his words
so that they would learn to fear him and teach their children (10). They stood under a mountain
burning *to the heart of the sky*, in darkness, cloud, and thick darkness (11). Yahweh spoke from
the fire; they heard a voice but saw no form (12). He declared his covenant, the ten commandments,
and wrote them on two stone tablets (13). Moses was commanded at that time to teach the statutes
and ordinances for life in the land (14).

The unit thus ties the law Moses is about to teach to two memories: the deaths at Peor and the
voice at Horeb. Life in the land depends on obedience; the law's authority rests on an encounter
heard rather than seen.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, Deuteronomy 4:1–14. Full unit copied verbatim.

> **1** Now, Israel, listen to the statutes and to the ordinances, which I teach you, to do them; that you may live, and go in and possess the land which Yahweh, the God of your fathers, gives you.

> **2** You shall not add to the word which I command you, neither shall you take away from it, that you may keep the commandments of Yahweh your God which I command you.

> **3** Your eyes have seen what Yahweh did because of Baal Peor; for all the men who followed Baal Peor, Yahweh your God has destroyed them from among you.

> **4** But you who were faithful to Yahweh your God are all alive today.

> **5** Behold, I have taught you statutes and ordinances, even as Yahweh my God commanded me, that you should do so in the middle of the land where you go in to possess it.

> **6** Keep therefore and do them; for this is your wisdom and your understanding in the sight of the peoples who shall hear all these statutes and say, “Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.”

> **7** For what great nation is there, that has a god so near to them, as Yahweh our God is whenever we call on him?

> **8** What great nation is there, that has statutes and ordinances so righteous as all this law, which I set before you today?

> **9** Only be careful, and keep your soul diligently, lest you forget the things which your eyes saw, and lest they depart from your heart all the days of your life; but make them known to your children and your children’s children;

> **10** the day that you stood before Yahweh your God in Horeb, when Yahweh said to me, “Assemble the people to me, and I will make them hear my words, that they may learn to fear me all the days that they live on the earth, and that they may teach their children.”

> **11** You came near and stood under the mountain. The mountain burned with fire to the heart of the sky, with darkness, cloud, and thick darkness.

> **12** Yahweh spoke to you out of the middle of the fire: you heard the voice of words, but you saw no form; you only heard a voice.

> **13** He declared to you his covenant, which he commanded you to perform, even the ten commandments. He wrote them on two stone tablets.

> **14** Yahweh commanded me at that time to teach you statutes and ordinances, that you might do them in the land where you go over to possess it.

## People
- [[God]] — Yahweh gives the land (1), destroyed the followers of Baal Peor and preserved the faithful (3–4), and is *so near* whenever Israel calls (7). At Horeb he assembles the people, speaks from the fire without showing a form, declares his covenant, and writes the ten commandments on two stone tablets (10–13).
- [[Moses]] — The teacher of statutes and ordinances, as Yahweh commanded him (1, 5, 14). He stands between the voice at Horeb and the people's life in the land: Yahweh spoke to Moses about assembling the people (10) and commanded him *at that time* to teach them (14).
- [[Baal Peor]] — Named as the occasion of judgment: those who followed Baal Peor were destroyed (3), recalling Numbers 25. The verse's *because of Baal Peor* may refer to the deity or to the incident; the wiki's page treats it as the deity named in Numbers 25:3, 5.

## Places
- [[Horeb]] — The day Israel stood before Yahweh at Horeb is the memory to guard and teach (10). The mountain burns with fire to the heart of the sky, covered in darkness, cloud, and thick darkness (11). Exodus 19 calls it Mount Sinai.

## Themes
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Hearing and doing the statutes is the condition of life and possession (1, 5–6, 14). The word may not be added to or reduced (2); the law is called righteous above that of any nation (8).
- [[Remembrance]] — Israel must not forget what its eyes saw, nor let it leave its heart, and must make it known to children and grandchildren (9–10). Peor and Horeb are both summoned as seen events (3, 9).
- [[Wisdom]] — Keeping the statutes *is your wisdom and your understanding* in the nations' sight (6). Wisdom here is located in observed obedience, not in a separate body of instruction.
- [[The Nations]] — The peoples are imagined as hearers who admire Israel's statutes (6). The comparison is with every great nation's god and law (7–8), not a command to teach them.
- [[Life and Death]] — *that you may live* (1), and the faithful are *all alive today* while the followers of Baal Peor were destroyed (3–4). Living here includes entering the land.
- [[Idolatry]] — Peor is the fresh example of following another god (3). The stress that Israel saw no form at Horeb (12) prepares the prohibition of images in 4:15–31.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Israel heard *the voice of words* (12). The covenant comes as speech declared and then written (13).
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — Israel's eyes saw the judgment at Peor and the events of Horeb (3, 9), but at Horeb they saw no form (12). Sight confirms the event; hearing carries the revelation.
- [[The Fear of God]] — Yahweh assembles the people to hear his words so that they may learn to fear him all their days and teach their children (10).
- [[Covenant]] — Yahweh *declared to you his covenant*, identified with the ten commandments written on two stone tablets (13).
- [[Prayer]] — No great nation has a god so near *whenever we call on him* (7). Calling is assumed as Israel's ordinary practice, not narrated as a particular prayer.
- [[Righteousness]] — The statutes and ordinances are called righteous beyond those of any great nation (8). The claim is about the law's character; the verse does not call Israel righteous.

## Notes

**The turn from history to command.** Chapters 1–3 narrate; *Now, Israel, listen* (1) draws the
conclusion. The retrospect has been preparation for the instruction that follows, and the stay
*in the valley near Beth Peor* (3:29) makes the Peor example in 4:3 local as well as recent.

**Adding and taking away.** Verse 2 protects the command so *that you may keep* it. It is a limit
on altering the commanded word, not a statement about the extent of a later canon. Proverbs 30:6
warns only against adding; Deuteronomy forbids both directions.

**Who died at Peor.** Verse 3 says *all the men who followed Baal Peor* were destroyed; In Numbers
25:4–5 Yahweh orders the chiefs hanged and Moses tells the judges to kill those joined to Baal
Peor, and 25:9 reports twenty-four thousand dead by the plague. Deuteronomy does not separate executions from plague. Its
contrast is with the faithful who are alive today (4). The hearers are, at least in part, those
same survivors: a different group from the generation whose death in the wilderness 2:14–16 records.

**Near and righteous.** Verses 7–8 rest Israel's distinction on two things — a god near when
called, and a righteous law. The nations' admiration (6) is anticipated, not reported; nothing in
this unit describes them actually hearing.

**Horeb told and retold.** Several details differ in emphasis from Exodus. *You came near and stood
under the mountain* (11) agrees with Exodus 19:17, where the people stood at the lower part of the
mountain, but Exodus 20:18, 21 has them stay at a distance once the voice speaks. Exodus describes
smoke, quaking, thunder, lightning, and trumpet (19:16–19); Deuteronomy gives fire *to the heart of
the sky* with darkness, cloud, and thick darkness, and adds the theological point Exodus does not
make: no form was seen (12). See [[The Voice Out of the Fire - Exodus 19-20 and Deuteronomy 4]].

**Who wrote the tablets.** Verse 13 says Yahweh wrote the ten commandments on two stone tablets,
matching Exodus 31:18 and 34:1. Exodus 34:27–28 tells Moses to write *these words* and then says
*He wrote on the tablets … the ten commandments* without naming the subject. Deuteronomy settles the
writer as Yahweh for the tablets; the ambiguity of Exodus 34:28 remains Exodus's own, and is not
explicitly flagged here as a contradiction.

**Covenant and statutes.** Verse 13 identifies the covenant declared from the fire with the ten
commandments. Verse 14 distinguishes what Moses was commanded to teach — statutes and ordinances for
the land. The pairing anticipates 5:22–31, where the people ask Moses to hear the rest.

**Sources and scope.** The planned boundary (4:1–14) and title are retained. Quotations are from
stored WEB Deuteronomy 4; comparisons use stored Exodus 19, 20, 31, 34 and Numbers 25. No claim
about exact Hebrew wording is made, and no unstored book is quoted.
