# The Assembly and the Holy Camp

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 23:1–14

## Summary

Two sets of boundaries. The first (1–8) says who *"shall not enter into Yahweh's assembly"*: a man
emasculated by crushing or cutting, a person *"born of a forbidden union"*, an Ammonite or a Moabite —
and who may: the Edomite and the Egyptian, in the third generation. The second (9–14) is about the army
in the field. A man unclean from *"that which happens him by night"* goes outside the camp until
evening, and the army keeps its latrine outside the camp and covers its excrement with a trowel carried
*"among your weapons"*. The reason given for the second set is the one sentence in the passage that
names what Yahweh does: *"for Yahweh your God walks in the middle of your camp"* (14).

In the Hebrew, chapter 23 begins one verse earlier, with the father's-wife law that the WEB prints as
22:30, so WEB 23:1–14 is WLC 23:2–15 (see Notes).

**Who may not enter (23:1–2).** *"He who is emasculated by crushing or cutting shall not enter into
Yahweh's assembly"* (1). The Hebrew names two injuries, *pǝṣûaʿ-dakkâ* and *kǝrût šopkâ*, and none of
the three rare words recurs in the Torah (see Notes). The law does not say whether it means injury,
deliberate castration, or both, and it does not use *sārîs*, the word usually translated *eunuch*.
Leviticus has a related rule for the priests: a descendant of Aaron *"who has damaged testicles"*
(Leviticus 21:20) may not *"come near to the altar"*, but *"He shall eat the bread of his God"*
(21:22–23). Leviticus restricts the altar to such a man and gives him the holy food; Deuteronomy keeps
him out of the assembly, and the two rules use different Hebrew. See [[The Holiness of the Priests]].

*"A person born of a forbidden union shall not enter into Yahweh's assembly; even to the tenth generation"*
(2). *"Born of a forbidden union"* translates one word, *mamzēr*, which occurs in the Hebrew Bible only
here and at Zechariah 9:6. The text does not define it. Later Jewish law identified the *mamzēr* as the
child of a union forbidden in Leviticus 18; reading 23:2 straight after 22:30 supports that reading, but
the verse itself does not say so. The wiki reports it as the traditional reading and does not adopt it.

**Ammon and Moab (23:3–6).** *"An Ammonite or a Moabite shall not enter into Yahweh's assembly; even to
the tenth generation shall no one belonging to them enter into Yahweh's assembly forever"* (3). Two
reasons follow. The first is hospitality: *"because they didn't meet you with bread and with water on the
way, when you came out of Egypt"* (4). The second is Balaam: *"because they hired against you Balaam the
son of Beor from Pethor of Mesopotamia, to curse you"* (4). The story is [[Balak Sends for Balaam]], where
Balak of Moab sends for him; the hire is Moab's in Numbers 22, and Deuteronomy joins Ammon to it without
saying how.

The first reason has a problem inside Deuteronomy itself. In 2:28–29 Moses asks Sihon to *"sell me food
for money … and give me water for money"*, *"as the children of Esau who dwell in Seir, and the Moabites
who dwell in Ar, did to me"*. By that verse Moab did sell bread and water; by 23:4 it did not meet Israel
with them. Ammon is not in 2:29. The wiki records the tension without resolving it. One reading is that
*"meet you"* means coming out to greet with gifts, which selling at a price is not; another is that 2:29
and 23:4 remember different encounters; a third is that the two passages were never harmonized. The text
gives no reason. See [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]].

Deuteronomy 2 also protected these two nations by name. *"I have given Ar to the children of Lot for a
possession"* (2:9), and the same of Ammon (2:19). Their land is safe from Israel; their people are kept
out of Israel's assembly. Both are in the same book. See [[Around Seir and Past Moab and Ammon]] and
[[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].

*"Nevertheless Yahweh your God wouldn't listen to Balaam; but Yahweh your God turned the curse into a
blessing to you, because Yahweh your God loved you"* (5). The Numbers narrative showed the reversal three
times, in the oracles Balaam could not stop himself giving; here it is summed up in one verb, *hāpak*, *to
overturn*, and given a reason, *"because Yahweh your God loved you"* — the reason 7:8 gave for the
Exodus, *"because Yahweh loves you"*. The same verb is used of Sodom: Yahweh *"overthrew"* the cities of the plain (Genesis 19:25), and
Deuteronomy 29:23 will recall *"the overthrow of Sodom … which Yahweh overthrew in his anger"*. The
nations born from the flight out of Sodom (Genesis 19:37–38) are the ones whose curse is overturned (see
Notes). The text does not draw the link; it is in the verb.

*"You shall not seek their peace nor their prosperity all your days forever"* (6). Ezra 9:12 takes up the
same words in the mixed-marriage crisis, *"nor seek their peace or their prosperity forever"*, and
Nehemiah 13:1–3 has the law of 23:3–5 read aloud, after which *"they separated all the mixed multitude
from Israel"*. Nehemiah's paraphrase keeps the curse-into-blessing clause (13:2) and drops *"because
Yahweh your God loved you"*.

**Edom and Egypt (23:7–8).** *"You shall not abhor an Edomite; for he is your brother. You shall not abhor
an Egyptian, because you lived as a foreigner in his land"* (7). The verb is *tāʿab*, the root of
*tôʿēbâ*, *abomination* — the word Deuteronomy uses for what Israel must not tolerate (7:26; 22:5), and
the word Genesis used for what the Egyptians would not do with the Hebrews: *"the Egyptians don't eat
bread with the Hebrews, for that is an abomination to the Egyptians"* (Genesis 43:32; see Notes). The
Egyptians held the Hebrews an abomination; Israel is not to hold the Egyptian one. See
[[An Abomination to the Egyptians - Separate Tables in Genesis and Exodus]].

The reasons are remembered relationships, not conduct. Edom refused Israel passage with an army
(Numbers 20:14–21), and is still *"your brother"*; see
[[Your Brother Israel - Esau Welcomes Jacob and Edom Refuses Passage]]. Egypt enslaved Israel, and the
reason given for not abhorring the Egyptian is not the slavery but the sojourn before it, *"because you
lived as a foreigner in his land"*: *gēr*, the word of 10:19, *"love the foreigner; for you were
foreigners in the land of Egypt"*. Ammon and Moab are kept out for refusing bread and water and hiring a
curse; Egypt, which did much worse, is admitted in the third generation. The text does not explain the
proportion. *"The children of the third generation who are born to them may enter into Yahweh's assembly"*
(8).

**What "the assembly" is.** *Qǝhal YHWH*, *Yahweh's assembly*, occurs six times in 23:1–8 (WLC 23:2–9) and
in the rest of the Torah only at Numbers 16:3 and 20:4 (see Notes). Deuteronomy's own uses of *qāhāl*
before this chapter are the gathering at Horeb, *"in the day of the assembly"* (9:10; 10:4; 18:16; also
5:22). So the assembly is the body of Israel gathered before Yahweh, the people who stood at the mountain.
What *"enter"* it means in practice — worship, the assembly in its legal or military function, marriage
into Israel, full membership — the text does not say. The passage never says these people may not live
among Israel, and 23:15–16, the next pericope, has a runaway slave dwelling *"among you"*. The wiki
records the open question and adopts no reading.

**The holy camp (23:9–14).** *"When you go out and camp against your enemies, then you shall keep yourselves
from every evil thing"* (9). The setting is war: the army of chapter 20, in the field. The first case is a
man *"not clean by reason of that which happens him by night"* (10), the emission of Leviticus 15:16,
which there makes a man *"unclean until the evening"*. Deuteronomy adds what Leviticus does not: *"he shall
go outside of the camp. He shall not come within the camp"* (10), and he returns after bathing, *"When the
sun is down"* (11). The second case is ordinary: *"You shall have a place also outside of the camp where you
go relieve yourself"* (12), and *"a trowel among your weapons"*, to *"dig with it, and … cover your
excrement"* (13). *"A place"* is *yād*, literally *a hand*; the *"trowel"* is *yātēd*, elsewhere a tent
peg, the word of the tabernacle's pegs and of Jael's peg in Judges 4:21 (see Notes).

*"For Yahweh your God walks in the middle of your camp, to deliver you, and to give up your enemies before
you. Therefore your camp shall be holy, that he may not see an unclean thing in you, and turn away from
you"* (14). *"Walks"* is the participle *mithallēk*, the form of Genesis 3:8, where the man and the woman
*"heard Yahweh God's voice walking in the garden"*, and the stem of the promise of Leviticus 26:12, *"I will
walk among you"*. In the Torah those are the three places where Yahweh is the one walking (see Notes and
[[Yahweh Walks in the Camp - Genesis 3.8, Leviticus 26.12, and Deuteronomy 23.14]]).

*"An unclean thing"* is *ʿerwat dābār*, literally *the nakedness of a thing*. The phrase occurs in the
Hebrew Bible only here and at 24:1, where it is the *"unseemly thing"* a husband finds in his wife before he
writes her a bill of divorce (see Notes). Here the thing Yahweh must not see is excrement left uncovered;
the camp is to be kept so that he will not *"turn away from you"*. Holiness here is not ritual alone. It is
the latrine and the trowel, and the reason for them is that God is present among soldiers.

**How the passage fits together.** Both halves draw a line around a gathering where Yahweh is present. The
assembly has members who may not enter it; the camp has men who must step outside it for a day and things
that must be buried. The tests differ: in 1–8 the line runs by birth, body, and a people's history, and
much of it is permanent — *"to the tenth generation … forever"*; in 9–14 it is temporary, and a man who
goes out comes back *"when the sun is down"*. In both, the ground given is Yahweh's presence: in the first
as the assembly that is *Yahweh's*, in the second as the God who *"walks in the middle of your camp"*. The
passage does not say whether the exclusions of 1–8 are meant as a kind of purity like that of 9–14, or as
something else. Isaiah 56:3–7 later promises a place in Yahweh's house to the eunuch and the foreigner who
keep his covenant, in words that answer this passage; see
[[The Eunuch and the Foreigner - Deuteronomy 23.1-8 and Isaiah 56.3-7]].

## Notes

**Verse numbering.** The WLC begins chapter 23 with the verse the WEB prints as 22:30, so WEB 23:1–14 is
WLC 23:2–15. Verse numbers in this page are the WEB's unless marked WLC. The boundary is the plan's, kept
as drawn: 23:15 turns to the runaway slave, and nothing after 23:14 mentions the assembly or the camp.

**"Emasculated by crushing or cutting."** *Pǝṣûaʿ-dakkâ ûkǝrût šopkâ* (WLC 23:2), *wounded by crushing,
and cut of the organ*. *Pāṣaʿ* (lemma 6481), *dakkâ* (1795), and *šopkâ* (8212) each occur in the Torah
only in this verse (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). *Kārat*, *cut*, is the common verb; its passive
participle *kārût* is used of the cut animal that may not be offered at Leviticus 22:24 (WLC). Leviticus
21:20's *mǝrôaḥ ʾāšek*, WEB *"damaged testicles"*, uses different words (WLC). Whether *šopkâ* means the
penis or something else is not certain; *"organ"* is the usual rendering, reported here from general
knowledge.

**"Born of a forbidden union."** *Mamzēr* (WLC 23:3, lemma 4464). In the WLC it occurs only here and at
Zechariah 9:6, where WEB renders it *"Foreigners"* (*"Foreigners will dwell in Ashdod"*); the search covered
the Torah and Zechariah, and the claim that the word occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible is the standard
lexical statement, reported from general knowledge rather than a full-canon search. Its meaning is not
settled by either verse. The rabbinic definition (the child of a union forbidden under penalty of *kārēt*
or death) is reported from general knowledge of the Mishnah, tractate Yevamot, and not checked against the
text in this pass.

**"Yahweh's assembly."** *Qǝhal YHWH* (WLC 23:2, 3 twice, 4 twice, 9). In the Torah the construct phrase
occurs otherwise only at Numbers 16:3 (Korah's company, *"Why do you lift yourselves up above Yahweh's
assembly?"*) and 20:4 (WLC; stored WEB). *Qāhāl* elsewhere in Deuteronomy: 5:22 (WLC 5:19), 9:10, 10:4,
18:16, all of Horeb, and 31:30, *"the assembly of Israel"* hearing the song (WLC lemma 6951). Lamentations
1:10 uses *qāhāl* for the same exclusion of foreigners, *lōʾ-yābōʾû baqqāhāl lāk* (WLC), WEB *"should not
enter into your assembly"*, and there the nations have entered the sanctuary.

**"Meet you."** *Lōʾ-qiddǝmû ʾetkem* (WLC 23:5), the piel of *qādam*, *to come before, go to meet*. In the
Torah it occurs only here (WLC lemma 6923). Nehemiah 13:2 repeats it, *lōʾ qiddǝmû* (WLC), and Isaiah 21:14
uses it of meeting fugitives with bread (WLC; WEB not checked for that verse). That the verb implies coming
out to greet with provisions, as opposed to selling, is an inference from these uses, not a stated
definition.

**"Hired."** *Śākar* (WLC 23:5, lemma 7936). Numbers 22:7 has the elders of Moab and Midian come *"with the
rewards of divination in their hand"* (stored WEB); Numbers does not use *śākar* for it. Deuteronomy names
the transaction a hire.

**"Mesopotamia."** *ʾĂram nahărayim* (WLC 23:5), *Aram of the two rivers*; Numbers 22:5 had *"Pethor, which
is by the River"* (stored WEB). See [[Aram Naharaim]].

**"Turned the curse into a blessing."** *Wayyahăpōk … ʾet-haqqǝlālâ librākâ* (WLC 23:6). *Hāpak* is the verb
of Sodom's overthrow at Genesis 19:21, 25, 29, and of Deuteronomy 29:22 (WEB 29:23), *ʾăšer hāpak YHWH*,
*"which Yahweh overthrew"* (WLC lemma 2015; stored WEB). Nehemiah 13:2 repeats the clause with *ʾĕlōhênû*,
*"our God"*, in place of *YHWH ʾĕlōhêkā*, and without the *"loved you"* clause (WLC; WEB checked at
bible-api.com).

**"Their peace nor their prosperity."** *Lōʾ-tidrōš šǝlōmām wǝṭōbātām* (WLC 23:7). Ezra 9:12 in WEB has
*"nor seek their peace or their prosperity forever"* (checked at bible-api.com); the Hebrew of Ezra was not
checked in this pass.

**"Abhor."** *Lōʾ-tǝtaʿēb* twice (WLC 23:8), the piel of *tāʿab* (lemma 8581). In the Torah the verb occurs
otherwise only at Deuteronomy 7:26, *wǝtaʿēb tǝtaʿăbennû*, *"you shall utterly abhor it"*, of the devoted
thing (WLC; stored WEB). The noun *tôʿēbâ* (lemma 8441) is the *"abomination"* of Genesis 43:32; 46:34; and
Exodus 8:22 (WEB 8:26), all of Egyptians and Hebrews (WLC). That the noun and the verb share a root is the
standard lexical view.

**"A foreigner."** *Gēr* (WLC 23:8), the word of 10:19, *"you were foreigners in the land of Egypt"*, and of
Exodus 22:21; 23:9, WEB *"you were aliens in the land of Egypt"* (WLC Exodus 22:20; 23:9; stored WEB).

**"That which happens him by night."** *Miqqǝrēh-lāyǝlâ* (WLC 23:11). The noun *qāreh* (lemma 7137) occurs in
the Torah only here (WLC). WEB's paraphrase is the usual reading, an involuntary emission, linked to
Leviticus 15:16 by the bathing and the evening; Deuteronomy does not name semen.

**"A place."** *Wǝyād tihyeh lǝkā* (WLC 23:13), literally *a hand shall be to you*. *Yād* for *place,
marker* is also Isaiah 56:5's *yād wāšēm*, WEB *"a memorial and a name"* (WLC). That the two uses are
connected is not claimed by either text.

**"A trowel among your weapons."** *Yātēd … ʿal-ʾăzēnekā* (WLC 23:14). *Yātēd* (lemma 3489) elsewhere in the
Torah is the tent peg of the tabernacle and its court (Exodus 27:19; 35:18; 38:20, 31; 39:40; Numbers 3:37;
4:32), and in Judges the peg Jael drives through Sisera's head (4:21–22; 5:26) (WLC). *ʾĀzēn* (lemma 240),
WEB *"weapons"*, occurs only here in the Torah (WLC); its meaning is uncertain, and *"equipment"* is the
other common rendering, reported from general knowledge.

**"Walks."** *Mithallēk* (WLC 23:15), the hithpael participle of *hālak*. In the Torah the hithpael has God
as its subject only at Genesis 3:8 (*qôl YHWH ʾĕlōhîm mithallēk baggān*, the same participle), Leviticus
26:12 (*wǝhithallaktî bǝtôkǝkem*), and here (WLC morphology search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). Its other uses
are of people walking before or with God (Genesis 5:22, 24; 6:9; 17:1; 24:40; 48:15) or walking about
(Genesis 13:17; Exodus 21:19). In Genesis 3:8 the participle may modify *"voice"* or *"Yahweh God"*; the
grammar permits either.

**"An unclean thing."** *ʿErwat dābār* (WLC 23:15), *the nakedness of a thing*. The construct pair occurs in
the WLC only here and at Deuteronomy 24:1 (search of Genesis–Deuteronomy, Isaiah, Judges, Lamentations,
Nehemiah, and Zechariah; that it occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible is the standard statement, not
verified by a full-canon search). *ʿErwâ* is the *nakedness* of Leviticus 18 and of Noah (Genesis 9:22–23),
and of Joseph's charge that his brothers came *"to see the nakedness of the land"* (Genesis 42:9, 12) (WLC
lemma 6172). The nakedness of Genesis 3:7, 10 is a different word, *ʿêrōm* (WLC).

**"Turn away from you."** *Wǝšāb mēʾaḥărêkā* (WLC 23:15), *and turn back from behind you*. The same verb,
*šûb*, was used a verse earlier of the soldier who *"shall turn back"* to cover his excrement (WLC 23:14).

**"The mixed multitude."** Nehemiah 13:3's *ʿēreb* is the noun of Exodus 12:38, *ʿēreb rab*, the *"mixed
multitude"* that went up with Israel from Egypt (WLC lemma 6154; stored WEB Exodus).

**What the passage does not settle.** What *"enter into Yahweh's assembly"* means in practice. What
*mamzēr* means. Whether 23:1 concerns injury, castration, or both. How 23:4 is to be squared with 2:29.
Why Ammon is joined to Moab in the hiring of Balaam. Why Egypt is admitted in the third generation and Moab
not in the tenth. Whether the exclusions of 1–8 are a form of the purity of 9–14. Each is recorded, none
adopted.
