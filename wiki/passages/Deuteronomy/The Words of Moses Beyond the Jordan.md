# The Words of Moses Beyond the Jordan

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 1:1–5

## Summary

The narrator introduces Moses’ address to all Israel beyond the Jordan, in Moab. Geographical
bearings place the speech within the wilderness journey. An eleven-day route from Horeb to
Kadesh Barnea stands beside a date in the fortieth year. Sihon and Og have already been defeated.
Moses now begins to declare the law according to Yahweh’s command.

These five verses frame the speaker, audience, authority, place, and time. The direct address
begins in verse 6, which belongs to the next pericope.

## People
- [[God]] — Yahweh is the source of the commandments Moses speaks to Israel (3). The introduction presents Moses’ address as accountable to that commission.
- [[Moses]] — He addresses all Israel in Moab on the first day of the eleventh month of the fortieth year (1–5). His work is to declare the law after the eastern victories; the narrator introduces him before his direct speech begins.
- [[Sihon]] — His defeat precedes the address (4). He is identified as king of the Amorites, resident in Heshbon; this is retrospective dating, not a second battle.
- [[Og]] — His defeat also precedes the address (4). The WEB names Bashan, Ashtaroth, and Edrei; Numbers 21:33 locates his battle at Edrei. No new campaign is narrated here.

## Places
- [[The Jordan]] — The address is beyond the Jordan, in Moab (1, 5), hence east of the river in this setting. The river has not yet been crossed.
- [[Paran]] — Named among the geographical bearings in the opening frame (1). The verse does not narrate a new camp or give modern coordinates.
- [[Hazeroth]] — Named among the bearings in verse 1. The existing page also covers the Numbers campsite; identity of the sites is not established by this introduction alone.
- [[Horeb]] — The starting point of the eleven-day route to Kadesh Barnea by Mount Seir (2). The address itself takes place in Moab, not at Horeb.
- [[Edom]] — Mount Seir supplies the route designation between Horeb and Kadesh Barnea (2). This page retains Seir under its existing Edom aliases; no passage through Edom is newly narrated.
- [[Kadesh]] — Kadesh Barnea is the endpoint of the eleven-day journey from Horeb by Mount Seir (2). This travel measure stands beside the fortieth-year date, not a claim that the current address is at Kadesh.
- [[Heshbon]] — Named as Sihon’s residence in the retrospective notice of his defeat (4).
- [[Bashan]] — Og is identified as king of Bashan in the retrospective notice (4). The introduction assumes the victory already accomplished.
- [[Edrei]] — Named in the compressed notice of Og’s defeat (4). Numbers 21:33 explicitly makes it the battle site; this introduction does not narrate another engagement.
- [[Moab (place)]] — The explicit location where Moses begins to declare the law (5). It continues the setting of Numbers 36:13 without narrating a Jordan crossing.
- [[Arabah]] — The wilderness setting of the address includes the Arabah opposite Suf (1). The verse gives regional bearings rather than a modern map; no precise boundaries are assigned here.
- [[Suf]] — Named opposite the Arabah setting in verse 1. The stored WEB spells it Suf. Its modern identification is left open; it is not silently equated with Suphah or the Red Sea.
- [[Tophel]] — Named among the bearings for Moses’ address (1). No separate event, etymology, or secure modern identification is supplied by this verse.
- [[Laban (place)]] — Named among the geographical bearings in verse 1. It is a place, not Jacob’s relative Laban; no relationship between the name-bearers is asserted. Its modern location remains unspecified.
- [[Dizahab]] — Named with Paran, Tophel, Laban, and Hazeroth in the opening geographical frame (1). The verse supplies no separate episode or modern location.
- [[Ashtaroth]] — Named as Og’s residence in the WEB introduction (4), with Edrei also named. Numbers 21:33 identifies Edrei as the battle site. No identification with another similarly named site is imposed here.

## Themes
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Moses speaks according to Yahweh’s command and begins to declare the law (3, 5). The book presents instruction as a public address to Israel within its remembered history.
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — The narrator distinguishes the source of the command, Yahweh, from its human speaker, Moses (3). This is mediation of teaching, not a narrated act of intercession.
- [[Remembrance]] — The opening dates the address after Sihon and Og’s defeat and places an eleven-day route beside the fortieth year (2–4). The frame invites historical recollection before the speech supplies its account.
- [[The Wilderness]] — The wilderness is the setting of instruction (1), while the eleven-day route and fortieth-year date orient the reader (2–3). The introduction alone does not recount the causes of the long delay.
- [[War]] — The defeats of Sihon and Og are recalled as completed events that precede the teaching (4). This notice compresses the campaigns narrated in Numbers 21:21–35.

## Notes

**Eleven days and forty years.** Verse 2 measures a route; verse 3 dates an address.
Their juxtaposition suggests that the long history cannot be explained simply by distance.
The causes are developed later in the book, rather than supplied in these five verses.

**Narrator and speaker.** Moses is introduced in the third person, and verse 5 leads into his
speech. Attributing that speech to Moses does not by itself identify the author of the
surrounding narrative or establish a date for the book’s final composition.

**Geographical limits.** Verse 5 explicitly gives Moab. Verse 1’s cluster of bearings should
not be forced into a precisely reconstructed itinerary. Suf is kept in the stored WEB spelling;
Hazeroth’s identity with the Numbers camp remains uncertain. Seir and Kadesh Barnea use the
existing Edom and Kadesh pages. Laban the place requires both it and the existing person to be
qualified; the names do not establish a relationship.

**Victory remembered.** Verse 4 presents the battles as past. Numbers gives Israel as the
fighting community and Moses as its leader; the introductory compression is not evidence for
a separate personal combat by Moses. In the WEB, the phrasing around Ashtaroth and Edrei is
compressed; Numbers 21:33 clarifies Edrei’s role as battle site.
