# The Book Beside the Ark

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 31:24–29

## Summary

Moses finishes writing the law in a book and orders the ark-bearing Levites to deposit it
beside the covenant ark as a witness against Israel (24–26). The written record will remain
after its teacher dies, confronting the community with the commands it has received.

Moses grounds his warning in experience: Israel has rebelled while he is still alive, and
he expects worse after his death (27). He summons tribal elders and officers to hear his
words and calls heaven and earth as witnesses (28). Corruption, departure from the commanded
way, and provocation through their handiwork will bring evil upon them (29). This passage
prepares the public delivery of the song; its words begin in the next planned unit.

## Key verses

> **24** When Moses had finished writing the words of this law in a book, until they were finished,

> **25** Moses commanded the Levites, who bore the ark of Yahweh’s covenant, saying,

> **26** “Take this book of the law, and put it by the side of the ark of Yahweh your God’s covenant, that it may be there for a witness against you.

> **27** For I know your rebellion, and your stiff neck. Behold, while I am yet alive with you today, you have been rebellious against Yahweh. How much more after my death?

> **28** Assemble to me all the elders of your tribes, and your officers, that I may speak these words in their ears, and call heaven and earth to witness against them.

> **29** For I know that after my death you will utterly corrupt yourselves, and turn away from the way which I have commanded you; and evil will happen to you in the latter days; because you will do that which is evil in Yahweh’s sight, to provoke him to anger through the work of your hands.”

— Deuteronomy 31:24–29 (WEB)

## Notes

- **Book and tablets:** the book is beside the ark (26); the tablets are inside it (10:5).
  These are distinct objects with distinct placement. The scope of “this law” is not defined
  by 31:24–29 alone; completion of Moses’ writing is not itself a claim that he wrote every
  narrative sentence of the final Pentateuch.
- **Custodians:** 31:9 names priests, sons of Levi, as ark-bearers; 31:25 calls the bearers
  Levites. This passage does not settle every question of priestly and Levitical organization.
  Elders and officers are unnamed collective leaders, so no individual pages are invented.
- **Warning and choice:** the certainty of Moses’ forecast (29) stands beside the appeal to
  choose life (30:19). Both remain in the narrative; neither erases the other. No theory of
  predestination or date for the predicted calamity is supplied here.
- **Witnesses:** the book (26), heaven and earth (28), and the preceding song (31:19–21)
  preserve testimony in different forms. Heaven and earth function as witnesses, not as
  gods Israel is commanded to worship.
- **Source:** all six verses above are copied from the stored WEB. Comparisons use stored
  Deuteronomy 4:25–26; 10:1–8; 30:19; 31:9–29. No source-language assertion or quotation
  from an unstored book is introduced. The planned pericope boundary is unchanged.
