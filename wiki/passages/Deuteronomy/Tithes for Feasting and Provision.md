# Tithes for Feasting and Provision

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 14:22–29

## Summary

Moses commands a yearly tithe: *"You shall surely tithe all the increase of your seed, that which
comes out of the field year by year"* (14:22). The tithe of grain, new wine, and oil, together with
the firstborn of herd and flock, is to be eaten *"before Yahweh your God, in the place which he
chooses, to cause his name to dwell there"*. The stated purpose is *"that you may learn to fear
Yahweh your God always"* (23).

If the chosen place is too far to carry the produce, it may be turned into money. The household
binds the money in its hand, goes to the place, and spends it on *"whatever your soul desires, for
cattle, or for sheep, or for wine, or for strong drink"*. They eat it there before Yahweh, *"and you
shall rejoice, you and your household"* (24–26). The Levite *"within your gates"* is not to be
forsaken, *"for he has no portion nor inheritance with you"* (27).

*"At the end of every three years"* the whole tithe of that year's increase is stored in the towns
instead (28). The Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow come and *"eat and be
satisfied"*, so *"that Yahweh your God may bless you in all the work of your hand which you do"*
(29).

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, who chooses the place and causes his name to dwell there (23–24), who blesses
  the harvest that makes the journey hard (24), and who blesses the work of the hands of those who
  feed the needy (29).
- [[Moses]] — the speaker, not named in these verses; the address of 12:1 onward continues.
- [[Levi]] — the Levite *"within your gates"*, with *"no portion nor inheritance"* (27, 29), the
  one recipient named in both the yearly and the third-year provisions.
- The household, the foreigner (*gēr*), the fatherless, and the widow are categories, not named
  individuals.

## Places

- The chosen place (23–25) — still unnamed, as throughout Deuteronomy 12. The wiki gives it no
  place page; see [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]].
- *"Within your gates"* (27–29) — Israel's own towns in the land, where the third-year tithe is
  stored.

## Notes

### A tithe the giver eats

Leviticus 27:30 says the tithe *"is Yahweh's. It is holy to Yahweh."* Numbers 18:21 gives *"all
the tithe in Israel"* to the Levites. Here the household that tithes eats the tithe itself, at the
sanctuary, and the Levite shares in it. Every third year it goes to the Levite and the poor in the
towns. The three laws use the same word, *maʿăśēr* (WLC Deuteronomy 14:23, 28; Numbers 18:21,
morphhb), and do not say how they relate. The wiki records the difference and does not harmonize
it. See [[Three Tithe Laws - Leviticus 27, Numbers 18, and Deuteronomy 14]].

The opening verb is doubled, *ʿaśśēr təʿaśśēr*, the infinitive absolute with the finite verb
(WLC 14:22, morphhb), which the WEB renders *"You shall surely tithe"*. The same construction
stands in Jacob's vow at Bethel, *ʿaśśēr ʾăʿaśśərennû lāk* (WLC Genesis 28:22, morphhb). Jacob
vowed a tenth; here the tenth is commanded.

### To learn to fear

The tithe meal has a stated purpose: *"that you may learn to fear Yahweh your God always"* (23). The
pairing of *lāmad*, learn, with the verb *yārēʾ*, fear (*ləyirʾâ* at 14:23), recurs in Deuteronomy: the people at Horeb (4:10),
the king reading his copy of the law (17:19), and the whole assembly with its children at the
seven-year reading (31:12–13) (WLC, morphhb). In those passages the fear is learned by hearing or
reading. Here it is learned by eating at the place Yahweh chose. The text does not say how the
meal teaches it.

### Money in the hand

If the place is far, the household is to *"turn it into money"* (25). The silver is bound up in the
hand, carried, and exchanged at the place for *"whatever your soul desires"* (26). The list
explicitly includes wine and *šēkār*, *"strong drink"* (WLC 14:26, morphhb). The money rule is a
practical answer to distance, and its result is a feast.

The distance clause has a precedent two chapters back. 12:21 begins *"If the place which Yahweh
your God shall choose, to put his name there, is too far from you"*, and 14:24 has the same Hebrew
core, *kî-yirḥaq mimməkā hammāqôm*, with the same *lāśûm šəmô šām*, *to put his name there* (WLC
12:21; 14:24, morphhb). At 12:21 distance lets ordinary meat be eaten at home *"after all the
desire of your soul"*. At 14:24–26 distance does not let the tithe stay home; it changes only how
the tithe travels. Both passages use the same root for desire (*ʾawwâ* at 12:21, *ʾāwâ* at 14:26,
WLC lemmas, morphhb).

Leviticus 27:31 also lets a tithe be redeemed, but with a fifth added. Deuteronomy adds no fifth.
The two rules may answer different questions — Leviticus keeping the produce, Deuteronomy carrying
its value — and the wiki does not decide whether one replaced the other.

### Rejoicing with the household

*"You shall rejoice, you and your household"* (26) is the same command as at 12:7, 12, and 18. The
tithe feast is not given away; the giver eats it with the household and the Levite. Joy here is a
commanded part of the worship, not its by-product.

### The Levite not forsaken

*"You shall not forsake the Levite"* (27) repeats 12:19. The reason given is the same as at 10:9
and 12:12: he has *ḥēleq wənaḥălâ*, *portion and inheritance*, with no one (WLC 14:27, morphhb).
Numbers 18:21–24 answers that lack by giving the Levites the whole tithe. Deuteronomy answers it by
bringing the Levite to the tithe meal and, every third year, by storing the tithe for him in the
towns. Whether Deuteronomy's Levite also still receives Numbers' tithe is not said here.

### The third-year tithe

*"At the end of every three years"* (*miqṣēh šālōš šānîm*, WLC 14:28, morphhb) the year's tithe
stays in the towns. Deuteronomy 26:12 calls this *"the third year, which is the year of tithing"*.
The next verse in the book, 15:1, uses a related phrase for the year of release, *"at the end of
every seven years"* (*miqqēṣ šebaʿ šānîm*; the nouns *qāṣeh* and *qēṣ* are distinct but related
lemmas in WLC, morphhb). The text does not say how the three-year cycle fits within the seven, and
the wiki does not reconstruct a calendar.

The four recipients — the Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow — come, *"eat and be
satisfied"*, and the result promised is blessing on the giver's work (29). Deuteronomy 10:18 says
Yahweh himself *"does execute justice for the fatherless and widow, and loves the foreigner, in
giving him food and clothing"*. The third-year tithe puts that into a calendar. See
[[Within Your Gates - The Levite, the Foreigner, the Fatherless, and the Widow]].

### The firstborn at the meal

14:23 puts *"the firstborn of your herd and of your flock"* on the household's table at the chosen
place, as 12:6, 17–18 had already done, and 15:19–20 will repeat. Numbers 18:15–18 gives the
firstborn of cattle, sheep, and goats to the priests: their blood on the altar, their fat burned,
*"Their meat shall be yours"*, that is, Aaron's. **The two texts assign the meat of the clean
firstborn differently.** Both are recorded as they stand. Later Jewish practice resolved the tithe
differences by counting more than one tithe; the wiki notes this tradition without examining its
sources here, and records no equivalent harmonization of the firstborn.

### Boundary

The planned boundary (14:22–29) is kept. 14:22 turns from food that may not be eaten to the tithe
that is eaten before Yahweh. 15:1 begins the year of release, a new subject, though it continues the
theme of provision for the poor.
