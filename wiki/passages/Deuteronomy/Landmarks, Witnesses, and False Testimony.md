# Landmarks, Witnesses, and False Testimony

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 19:14–21

## Summary

The chapter that began with the protection of life (19:1–13) ends with the protection of two other
things a man can lose without being struck: his land, and his good name in court.

**The neighbour's landmark (19:14).** *"You shall not remove your neighbor's landmark, which they of old
time have set, in your inheritance which you shall inherit, in the land that Yahweh your God gives you to
possess."* One verse, and it stands alone between the refuge law and the law of witnesses. The landmark
was set by *"they of old time"* — those who first divided the inheritance — so moving it is not a quarrel
with a neighbour only, but an undoing of the allotment Yahweh gives. Deuteronomy 27:17 repeats it as a
curse the people answer *"Amen"* to.

**Two or three witnesses (19:15).** *"One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for
any sin, in any sin that he sins. At the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall a
matter be established."* Deuteronomy 17:6 had already said this for a capital charge of worshipping other
gods; Numbers 35:30 for murder. Here the rule is widened to *"any iniquity, or … any sin"*, so that no
single accuser can convict anyone of anything.

**The malicious witness (19:16–19).** The two-witness rule protects against one liar, but not against one
who is believed. So the law gives the case its own procedure. *"If an unrighteous witness rises up against
any man to testify against him of wrongdoing"* (16), *"both the men, between whom the controversy is, shall
stand before Yahweh, before the priests and the judges who shall be in those days"* (17). *"The judges shall
make diligent inquisition"* (18); if they find that *"the witness is a false witness, and has testified
falsely against his brother"*, then *"you shall do to him as he had thought to do to his brother. So you
shall remove the evil from among you"* (19).

**The deterrent and the measure (19:20–21).** *"Those who remain shall hear, and fear, and will never again
commit any such evil among you"* (20). *"Your eyes shall not pity: life for life, eye for eye, tooth for
tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot"* (21).

**How the passage fits together.** All three laws deal with the same kind of wrong: harm done to a neighbour
by means of a sign that others trust. The boundary stone tells everyone where one field ends and the next
begins; a witness's word tells the court what happened. Both can be moved quietly, and once moved they
deceive everyone who relies on them. So the law protects the sign. It forbids moving the stone, it will not
let one voice decide any case, and it punishes the false witness with the penalty he wanted the court to
impose.

**The penalty is what he intended, not what happened.** 19:19 measures the punishment by what the false
witness *"had thought to do"*. It does not need the innocent man to have been harmed. The witness is judged
for the sentence he tried to bring about, so if he accused someone of a capital crime, the penalty is
capital, even though the plot failed. The talion of 21 is quoted to give the scale. It is the same list as
Exodus 21:24, *"eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot"*, led by *"life for life"* as in
Exodus 21:23. It is applied here to a perjurer, who has not physically injured anyone. In Exodus 21 and
Leviticus 24 it applied to injury done by the body; here it applies to injury attempted with words.

**Who judges.** The two parties stand *"before Yahweh"*, and *"before the priests and the judges who shall be
in those days"* (17). The phrase recalls 17:9, *"the priests who are Levites, and … the judge who shall be in
those days"*, the court at the chosen place for cases too hard for the towns. Readers differ over whether
19:17 sends every disputed testimony to that central court or means a local hearing held before Yahweh. The
text does not say where the court sits. The wiki records both readings and adopts neither.

## Notes

**"Remove."** *Lōʾ tassîg* (19:14, WLC), from *nāsag*, *move back*; in Deuteronomy only here and at 27:17,
*ʾārûr massîg gəbûl rēʿēhû*, *"Cursed is he who removes his neighbor's landmark"* (morphhb lemma 5253; it
does not occur in Genesis–Numbers). *"Landmark"* is *gəbûl*, the ordinary word for *border* (14 times in
Deuteronomy, including 19:3, 8, where WEB has *"borders"* and *"border"*). The same word names the nation's
border Yahweh may enlarge (19:8) and the field-line a man may not move (19:14). *"Which they of old time have
set"* is *ʾăšer gāblû rīʾšōnîm* (WLC), with the verb from the same root; the verb *gābal* occurs only here in
Deuteronomy (lemma 1379).

**Why the landmark is here.** It interrupts two laws about courts, and the commentators have explained the
placement in different ways: as a stray law attached by the catchword *gəbûl* from 19:3, 8; as the first of
three laws about honesty in things the community relies on; or as part of a larger ordering of 19–25 by the
Ten Commandments, with 19:14 under *you shall not steal* and 19:15–21 under *false testimony*. The catchword
is in the text (WLC). The other two are readings of its arrangement. The wiki does not adopt one.

**"Rise up" three times.** The verb *qûm* opens 19:15 (*"One witness shall not rise up"*), closes it (*"shall a
matter be established"*, *yāqûm dābār*), and opens 19:16 (*"If an unrighteous witness rises up"*), WLC. In
Hebrew the witness rising against a man and the matter being *established* are the same verb. The verse says
that one witness can rise up against a man, but the charge does not stand until two or three do.

**"An unrighteous witness."** *ʿĒd-ḥāmās* (19:16, WLC), literally *a witness of violence*. It is the phrase of
Exodus 23:1, where WEB has *"a malicious witness"* (WLC, lemma 2555; in Deuteronomy only 19:16). *Ḥāmās* is the
word for the violence that filled the earth before the flood (Genesis 6:11, 13). WEB's two renderings
translate one phrase. Calling a lie told in court *violence* treats false testimony as an assault.

**"Of wrongdoing."** *Laʿănôt bô sārâ* (19:16, WLC). *Sārâ* is *turning aside*, *defection*. In Deuteronomy it
occurs only here and at 13:5 (13:6 in Hebrew), where the prophet *"has spoken rebellion against Yahweh"*
(morphhb lemma 5627). Some readers take the shared word to mean that 19:16 has a charge of apostasy in view.
Others read *sārâ* in 19:16 more generally as any wrongdoing, which is how WEB renders it. This is a lexical
observation; the wiki does not decide which reading is right.

**"False witness."** In 19:18 the witness is *ʿēd šeqer* and has *testified šeqer* (WLC), the phrase of Exodus
20:16, *ʿēd šāqer*. Deuteronomy's own form of the commandment, at 5:20, reads *ʿēd šāwʾ* instead — *worthless*,
*empty* — the word of the third commandment, *"in vain"* (5:11). In WEB 5:20 and Exodus 20:16 are identical,
*"You shall not give false testimony against your neighbor"*. So in Deuteronomy the case law at 19:18 uses
Exodus's word, not the word of Deuteronomy's own Decalogue (WLC; lemma 8267 occurs in Deuteronomy only at
19:18, lemma 7723 only at 5:11 and 5:20).

**"Diligent inquisition."** *Wədārəšû haššōpəṭîm hêṭēb wəhinnēh* (19:18, WLC): *inquire well, and behold*. The
same sequence occurs at 13:14 (13:15 in Hebrew) and 17:4, where the search finds *"if it is true, and the thing
certain"*. In 19:18 it finds the witness false. In all three, the court does not take the accusation as proved.
It investigates first.

**"As he had thought to do."** *Kaʾăšer zāmam laʿăśôt ləʾāḥîw* (19:19, WLC). *Zāmam*, *plan*, *devise*, occurs
only here in Deuteronomy (lemma 2161). The penalty follows what the witness intended. It does not depend on
what happened to the accused, so it applies only where the intention can be stated: the sentence he asked the
court to pass. The verse does not say what is done when the false charge carried no fixed penalty.

**"Brother."** The accused is the witness's *ʾāḥ*, *brother*, in 18 and 19 (WLC), as in Deuteronomy's laws on
debt and release (15:2–12). The Ten Words say *neighbour* (5:20). In this law the false witness is lying about
a fellow Israelite, a member of his own family.

**"Hear, and fear."** The deterrent of 19:20 belongs to a Deuteronomic formula: *"All Israel shall hear, and
fear"* (13:11), *"All the people shall hear, and fear, and do no more presumptuously"* (17:13), and *"All Israel
shall hear, and fear"* (21:21). In every case the public penalty is meant to teach those who see it.

**"Life for life, eye for eye."** 19:21 joins the talion with the preposition *bə*: *nepeš bənepeš, ʿayin
bəʿayin* (WLC). Exodus 21:24 and Leviticus 24:20 use *taḥat*, *in place of*: *ʿayin taḥat ʿayin* (WLC). The
English is the same in WEB. *ʿAyin bəʿayin* is also the phrase WEB renders *"face to face"* at Numbers 14:14
(WLC). And the verse begins *lōʾ tāḥôs ʿênekā* (WLC), *"your eye shall not pity"*, so the first *eye* in the
verse is the hearer's. The hearer is told not to spare the perjurer's eye.

**What this does not settle.** Whether the talion was applied literally or by payment, Deuteronomy 19 does not
say. Numbers 35:31 forbids ransom only for the murderer, and the wiki's
[[Eye for Eye - Exodus 21, Leviticus 24, and Matthew 5]] leaves the same question open for Exodus. Whether
19:17 means the central court of 17:8–13. Why the landmark law stands where it does. Each is recorded, none
adopted.
