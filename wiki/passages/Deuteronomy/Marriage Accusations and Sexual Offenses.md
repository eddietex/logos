# Marriage Accusations and Sexual Offenses

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 22:13–30

## Summary

Seven cases, each opening with *"if"* except the last, and all of them about who a woman belongs to and
what happens when that is in question. A husband charges his new wife with not being a virgin, and the
charge is false (13–19) or true (20–21). A man lies with a married woman (22). A man lies with a woman
pledged to be married, in the city (23–24) or in the field (25–27). A man seizes a virgin who is not
pledged (28–29). And a man may not take his father's wife (30). The penalties run from a fine to stoning,
and the cases are arranged so that each one turns on a single circumstance: whether the charge is true,
whether she is married or pledged or neither, whether it happened where she could be heard.

Yahweh is not named anywhere in the eighteen verses. No reason is given except the community's: *"So you
shall remove the evil from among you"* (21, 24), *"from Israel"* (22).

**The slandered bride (22:13–19).** A man *"takes a wife, and goes in to her, hates her"* (13), and then
*"accuses her of shameful things, and gives her a bad name"*, saying *"I didn't find in her the tokens of
virginity"* (14). The sequence matters: hatred comes first and the accusation after, so the law describes
the charge as the act of a man who wants out. Her father and mother answer it. They bring *"the tokens of
the young lady's virginity to the elders of the city in the gate"* (15), the father speaks — *"I gave my
daughter to this man as his wife, and he hates her"* (16) — and *"They shall spread the cloth before the
elders of the city"* (17). The cloth is the *śimlâ*, the word for the garment of 22:3 and the woman's
cloak of 22:5 (see Notes).

The man is punished three ways. The elders *"chastise him"* (18) — the verb of the parents who *"chasten"*
the rebellious son at 21:18. They *"fine him one hundred shekels of silver"* and give it to the father,
*"because he has given a bad name to a virgin of Israel"* (19). And *"She shall be his wife. He may not put
her away all his days"* (19). The man who wanted to be rid of her loses the right ever to send her away.

What the law does not do is apply its own rule for a false witness. Three chapters earlier the false
witness receives what *"he had thought to do to his brother"* (19:19), and if the charge here had been
true, the woman would have been stoned (21). The slanderer is chastised and fined, not killed. The wiki
records the difference without resolving it. One reading is that the husband is a party bringing a
complaint, not a witness testifying; another is that the law sets his penalty by the harm to her father's
house — the fine goes to the father — rather than by the harm he intended; a third is that the two laws
were simply never harmonized. The text gives no reason.

**The charge found true (22:20–21).** *"But if this thing is true"*, then *"they shall bring out the young
lady to the door of her father's house, and the men of her city shall stone her to death with stones,
because she has done folly in Israel, to play the prostitute in her father's house"* (20–21). The place of
execution is the place of the offence: she is stoned at the door of the house whose honor she is said to
have betrayed. *"Folly in Israel"* is *nǝbālâ bǝyiśrāʾēl*, the phrase of Genesis 34:7, when Jacob's sons
hear what Shechem has done to Dinah; in the Torah the noun occurs only in those two verses (see Notes and
[[Folly in Israel - Nebalah from Genesis 34 to 2 Samuel 13]]). In Genesis the *folly* was the man's; here it
is the woman's.

The procedure raises questions the text does not answer. Who keeps the cloth, and from when; what the
elders do if the parents have no cloth to bring; whether the absence of *"tokens"* proves what the law
treats it as proving. The law presents the evidence as decisive in both directions and stops there.

**The married woman (22:22).** *"If a man is found lying with a woman married to a husband, then they shall
both die, the man who lay with the woman and the woman. So you shall remove the evil from Israel."*
*"Married to a husband"* is *bǝʿulat baʿal*, the phrase God uses of Sarah to Abimelech, *"she is a man's
wife"* (Genesis 20:3; see Notes). The case is put as discovery — *"is found"* — and both die. Leviticus 20:10
gives the same sentence. Neither text names the method of death. Deuteronomy names stoning for the bride
of 22:21 and the pledged woman of 22:24, and not for this case; the wiki's [[Judgment]] page had said
that *"the ordinary penalty for adultery is stoning (Deuteronomy 22:23–24)"*, and that page now records
that 22:22 itself leaves the method unstated. See
[[No Ransom for the Adulterer - Proverbs 6.29-35, Leviticus 20.10, and Deuteronomy 22.22]].

**The pledged woman in the city (22:23–24).** A virgin *"pledged to be married to a husband"*, and *"a man
finds her in the city, and lies with her"* (23): both are stoned *"at the gate of that city"* (24). The
reasons are given separately. *"The lady, because she didn't cry, being in the city; and the man, because
he has humbled his neighbor's wife"* (24). The pledged woman is already *"his neighbor's wife"*,
*ʾēšet rēʿēhû*, the phrase of Leviticus 20:10's adultery law (see Notes); betrothal is treated as marriage
for this purpose. Her guilt is inferred from silence: in a city, a cry would have been heard.

**The pledged woman in the field (22:25–27).** *"But if the man finds the lady who is pledged to be married
in the field, and the man forces her, and lies with her; then only the man who lay with her shall die; but
to the lady you shall do nothing. There is in the lady no sin worthy of death"* (25–26). The law gives an
analogy: *"for as when a man rises against his neighbor, and kills him, even so is this matter"* (26). The
verb for *kills* is *rāṣaḥ*, the verb of the sixth commandment and of chapter 19's killer (see Notes); the
rape is put in the same category as murder, with the woman as the one struck down. And it gives her the
benefit of what no one saw: *"for he found her in the field, the pledged to be married lady cried, and
there was no one to save her"* (27). The law does not require proof that she cried. It assumes she did,
because no one was there to hear either way. *"No one to save"*, *ʾên môšîaʿ*, comes back twice in the
curses of chapter 28 (28:29, 31).

The city-and-field distinction is a rule about evidence, not a claim about women in cities. Read with the
next verse, it presumes that a woman in a city could have called for help and been heard. Whether that
presumption was meant to be rebuttable — whether a woman in a city who was gagged or threatened could show
it — the text does not say.

**The virgin not pledged (22:28–29).** *"If a man finds a lady who is a virgin, who is not pledged to be
married, grabs her, and lies with her, and they are found; then the man who lay with her shall give to the
lady's father fifty shekels of silver. She shall be his wife, because he has humbled her. He may not put her
away all his days"* (28–29). *"Grabs"* is *tāpaś*, *to seize*, the verb of Potiphar's wife catching
Joseph's garment (Genesis 39:12) and of the parents taking hold of the rebellious son (21:19). Exodus
22:16–17 has a parallel law with a different verb — a man who *"entices"* (*pātâ*) a virgin — and with a
clause Deuteronomy lacks: *"If her father utterly refuses to give her to him, he shall pay money according
to the dowry of virgins."* See [[The Unbetrothed Virgin - Exodus 22.16-17 and Deuteronomy 22.28-29]].

The law's remedy is marriage without the right of divorce. To a modern reader it binds a woman to the man
who seized her. The text does not ask her, and it does not say whether Exodus's refusal clause is assumed
here. What it does do is take from the man the one power the next chapters give husbands, the power to
send a wife away (24:1–4), and set a payment that is half the slanderer's fine. Both men of this pericope
who lose that power lose it with the same words: *"He may not put her away all his days"* (19, 29). The
wiki records the reading that the law protects a woman who would otherwise be unmarriageable, and the
reading that it protects her father's interest more than hers, and adopts neither.

**The father's wife (22:30).** *"A man shall not take his father's wife, and shall not uncover his father's
skirt."* The verse stands alone, a prohibition with no penalty; in the Hebrew it is the first verse of
chapter 23 (see Notes). Leviticus 18:8 forbids the same act — *"It is your father's nakedness"* — and 20:11
punishes it with death. Deuteronomy 27:20 will curse it: *"Cursed is he who lies with his father's wife,
because he dishonors his father's bed."* The *"skirt"* is *kānāp*, the *corner* of the garment on which
the fringes were to go in 22:12. [[Reuben]] lay with his father's concubine (Genesis 35:22) and lost the
firstborn's place for it (49:4); Paul faces the same act in Corinth, *"that one has his father's wife"*
(1 Corinthians 5:1). See [[Lying with the Father's Concubine - A Claim on the Succession]].

**How the passage fits together.** The previous pericope ended on the fringes of the *"cloak with which you
cover yourself"* (22:12); this one begins and ends with cloth — the *śimlâ* spread before the elders (17)
and the father's *kānāp* not to be uncovered (30). Chapter 21's rebellious son was brought by both parents
to the elders at the gate and stoned by the men of his city (21:19–21); the bride's parents bring her case
to *"the elders of the city in the gate"* (15), and if she is condemned the men of her city stone her (21). The
sequence of 22:22–29 runs downward by the woman's status — married, pledged, unpledged — and by penalty —
death for both, death for both or for him alone, payment and marriage. Throughout, a woman's sexual life
is treated as a matter for her father's house, her husband's, and the town's; the law protects her from a
lying husband and from a rapist in the field, and it holds her to account for what it presumes she could
have prevented. All three are in the text, and the wiki records them together.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The unit is the plan's, kept as drawn. 22:30 is WLC 23:1 and could be read as the head of
chapter 23's laws of the assembly; it is kept here because it continues 22:13–29's subject and because
the WEB places it in chapter 22.

**"Young lady."** *Naʿărâ*, fourteen times in 22:15–29 (WLC). In the consonantal text of the Torah the
singular is written without its final *he*, as *nʿr*, the spelling of the masculine *naʿar*, and read as
*naʿărâ*; in this pericope the WLC marks it as a ketiv/qere pair at each occurrence but one. The one
exception is 22:19, *hannaʿărâ*, which is written in full; a search of the WLC Torah for the lemma (5291)
finds no other singular written with the *he*. Why the spelling is irregular is not settled; that it is a
feature of the Torah's spelling rather than a statement about the woman is the usual view, reported here
from general knowledge.

**"Shameful things."** *ʿĂlîlōt dǝbārîm* (14, 17, WLC), literally *deeds of words* or *pretexts of words*.
*ʿĂlîlâ* occurs in the Torah only in these two verses (WLC lemma 5949). WEB's *"shameful things"*
interprets; the Hebrew stresses that the charge is talk.

**"A bad name."** *Wǝhôṣîʾ ʿālêhā šēm rāʿ* (14), *he brings out against her an evil name*, repeated at 19
(WLC). The verb *yāṣāʾ*, hiphil, is the same verb as the parents *"bring"* the tokens (15) and the men
*"bring out"* the young woman to be stoned (21): what is brought out decides the case.

**"Tokens of virginity."** *Bǝtûlîm* (14, 15, 17 twice, 20, WLC), an abstract plural. Outside this
pericope the Torah has it only at Leviticus 21:13, *"He shall take a wife in her virginity"*, of the high
priest (WLC lemma 1331; stored WEB). Here the word names physical evidence, and 22:17 makes it the cloth.

**"They shall spread the cloth."** *Ûpārǝśû haśśimlâ* (17, WLC). *Pāraś*, *to spread out*, is the verb of
Moses spreading his hands (Exodus 9:29, 33), of the cherubim's wings (Exodus 25:20), of the coverings
spread over the holy things (Numbers 4), and of the eagle spreading its wings (Deuteronomy 32:11) (WLC
lemma 6566). *Śimlâ* is the cloak of 22:3, 5.

**"Chastise him."** *Wǝyissǝrû ʾōtô* (18, WLC). *Yāsar* is the verb of 8:5, *"as a man disciplines his
son, so Yahweh your God disciplines you"*, and of the parents who *"chasten"* the son at 21:18 (WLC lemma
3256; stored WEB). Whether it means a beating here or a formal rebuke is not said; the later rule of forty
stripes (25:1–3) uses other words.

**"Fine him."** *Wǝʿānǝšû* (19, WLC). *ʿĀnaš* occurs elsewhere in the Torah only at Exodus 21:22, *"he
shall be surely fined"*, for injury to a pregnant woman (WLC lemma 6064; stored WEB). There the amount is
what *"the woman's husband demands"*; here the fine is fixed and paid to her father.

**"Put her away."** *Lǝšallǝḥāh* (19), *šallǝḥāh* (29), the piel of *šālaḥ* (WLC). In Deuteronomy
21–24 the same verb in the same stem is used of the captive *"let go"* (21:14), the mother bird *"let go"* (22:7), these
two wives who may not be sent away (22:19, 29), and the wife sent out with a bill of divorce (24:1, 3, 4)
(WLC lemma 7971). The verb is the same in all; the law permits it, requires it, or forbids it by case.

**"Folly in Israel."** *Nǝbālâ bǝyiśrāʾēl* (21, WLC). *Nǝbālâ* occurs in the Torah only here and at Genesis
34:7 (WLC lemma 5039). The phrase's later uses (Joshua 7:15; Judges 19–20; 2 Samuel 13:12; Jeremiah 29:23)
are recorded on [[Folly in Israel - Nebalah from Genesis 34 to 2 Samuel 13]] and were not rechecked in
this pass.

**"To play the prostitute."** *Liznôt* (21, WLC), *zānâ*. In Deuteronomy the root occurs otherwise only at
23:18 (WEB numbering; WLC 23:19) and 31:16, where the people will *"play the prostitute after the strange
gods of the land"* (WLC lemma 2181). Here it is used of a woman's unchastity before marriage, not of
payment.

**"A woman married to a husband."** *ʾIššâ bǝʿulat-baʿal* (22, WLC), the exact phrase of Genesis 20:3,
*wǝhiwʾ bǝʿulat bāʿal*, *"she is a man's wife"* (WLC; stored WEB). The participle *bǝʿulâ* occurs in the
Torah only in these two verses (WLC lemma 1166, participle forms).

**"Pledged to be married."** *Mǝʾōrāśâ* (23, 25, 27), *lōʾ-ʾōrāśâ* (28) (WLC). *ʾĀraś* occurs in the
Torah only at Exodus 22:15 (WEB 22:16), of the virgin *"who isn't pledged"*; Deuteronomy 20:7, the man
*"who has pledged to be married a wife"*; here; and 28:30 (WLC lemma 781).

**"His neighbor's wife."** *ʾĒšet rēʿēhû* (24, WLC), the phrase of Leviticus 20:10, *"he who commits
adultery with his neighbor's wife"*, and in the second person of Deuteronomy 5:21, *"You shall not covet
your neighbor's wife"* (*ʾēšet rēʿekā*) (WLC; stored WEB).

**"Didn't cry."** *Lōʾ ṣāʿăqâ* (24), *ṣāʿăqâ* (27) (WLC). *Ṣāʿaq* is the cry of the oppressed that God
hears — Israel in Egypt (Exodus 14:10; Deuteronomy 26:7) and the widow and orphan (Exodus 22:22, WLC; WEB
22:23) — and the pledged woman's cry belongs to that line (WLC lemma 6817). Potiphar's wife's *"I cried
with a loud voice"* (Genesis 39:14) uses a different verb, *qārāʾ* (WLC).

**"Forces her."** *Wǝheḥĕzîq-bāh* (25, WLC), the hiphil of *ḥāzaq*, *to seize, take hold*. In Deuteronomy
the hiphil occurs only here and at 25:11, of the wife who *"takes him by his private parts"* (WLC lemma
2388; stored WEB). WEB's *"forces"* is an interpretation of *seizes* that the context supports.

**"Kills him."** *Ûrǝṣāḥô nepeš* (26, WLC). *Rāṣaḥ* in Deuteronomy is otherwise used only of the sixth
commandment (5:17) and the killer of the refuge laws (4:42; 19:3–6) (WLC lemma 7523).

**"No one to save her."** *Wǝʾên môšîaʿ lāh* (27, WLC). *Môšîaʿ*, *savior*, occurs in the Torah only here
and at Deuteronomy 28:29, 31, both *"no one to save you"* (WLC lemma 3467, participle; stored WEB).

**"Grabs her."** *Ûtǝpāśāh* (28, WLC). *Tāpaś* is used of Potiphar's wife seizing Joseph by his garment
(Genesis 39:12), of the parents taking hold of the rebellious son (Deuteronomy 21:19), and in the passive of
the suspected wife who *"isn't taken in the act"* (Numbers 5:13) (WLC lemma 8610; stored WEB). Exodus
22:15 (WEB 22:16) has *pātâ*, *entice*, instead (WLC lemma 6601). Whether the difference in verb means a
different case — seduction there, force here — or whether the laws describe one case two ways is
disputed; the wiki records both readings.

**"Fifty shekels."** *Ḥămiššîm kāsep* (29, WLC); the word *shekels* is supplied in both 22:19 and 22:29.
Exodus 22:17 (WLC 22:16) has *kǝmōhar habbǝtûlōt*, *according to the bride-price of virgins*, and *mōhar*
occurs in the Torah only there and at Genesis 34:12, Shechem's offer (WLC lemma 4119). That fifty shekels
was the customary bride-price is a common inference from these texts; the Torah does not state it.

**"His father's skirt."** *Wǝlōʾ yǝgalleh kǝnap ʾābîw* (30; WLC 23:1). Deuteronomy 27:20 repeats the phrase,
*kî gillâ kǝnap ʾābîw* (WLC), WEB *"because he dishonors his father's bed"*. Leviticus 18:8 and 20:11 use
*ʿerwâ*, *nakedness*, instead (WLC). *Kānāp* in Deuteronomy is otherwise a bird's wing (4:17; 32:11) and the
*corners* of 22:12 (WLC lemma 3671). Ruth's request to Boaz, *"spread the corner of your garment over your
servant"* (Ruth 3:9, WEB checked at bible-api.com), is commonly read as the opposite act — covering instead
of uncovering — and as using the same noun; the Hebrew of Ruth was not checked in this pass.

**Stoning and John 8.** The scribes and Pharisees tell Jesus, *"Moses commanded us to stone such women"*
(John 8:5, WEB checked at bible-api.com), of a woman *"in adultery, in the very act"* (8:4). In the Torah,
stoning is commanded for the bride found not to be a virgin (22:21) and the pledged woman in the city
(22:24); for the married woman, 22:22 and Leviticus 20:10 say only that both die. Whether the woman of
John 8 was pledged, or whether stoning was assumed for 22:22, the texts do not say, and the wiki adopts no
reading.

**What the passage does not settle.** Why the false accuser is not given the false witness's penalty
(19:19). What happens when the parents have no cloth. Whether the city-and-field rule could be rebutted.
Whether the man of 22:28 seized or seduced, and whether Exodus's father's refusal applies. What method of
death 22:22 intends. Whether 22:30 belongs with what precedes or what follows. Each is recorded, none
adopted.
