# New Tablets and the Journey Onward

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 10:1–11

## Summary

Moses recalls the restoration of the tablets he broke: he prepares new stones and an acacia ark,
Yahweh writes the former commandments, and Moses deposits them in the ark (1–5). A parenthesis
recounts travel, Aaron’s death and Eleazar’s succession, and Levi’s appointment to service with
Yahweh as its inheritance (6–9). Moses resumes his account of forty days on the mountain:
Yahweh listened and did not destroy Israel, then ordered the journey toward the sworn land
to continue (10–11). Restoration preserves both the covenant words and a future for the people.

## Notes

### Restored words and different construction accounts

The former words, explicitly identified with the assembly’s ten commandments (4), are restored.
Deuteronomy attributes the writing to Yahweh, while Exodus 34:27–28 has distinct writing notices
whose relation requires care; see the replacement-tablets connection. Moses makes the ark here
before ascending with replacement stones. Exodus 37:1 names Bezalel as maker and Exodus 40:20
places the deposit in the tabernacle’s erection account. Commissioned workmanship or a temporary
ark are possible harmonizing proposals, but neither is stated here. The differences in agency,
detail, and narrative placement remain visible; two arks are not asserted as a textual fact.

### The parenthesis is not a seamless itinerary

The WEB places verses 6–9 in parentheses. They interrupt the first-person mountain account with
travel, succession, and tribal service. Verse 10 resumes the stay. The wording does not license
placing all these events within a single forty-day interval or dating Levi’s appointment precisely
from the adjacent death notice.

**Location tension:** Deuteronomy 10:6 says Aaron died and was buried at Moserah; Numbers 20:28
and 33:38 locate his death on Mount Hor. Numbers 33:30–33 also has Moseroth, Bene Jaakan,
Hor Haggidgad, Jotbathah, whereas this unit has Beeroth Bene Jaakan, Moserah, Gudgodah,
Jotbathah. Possible name correspondences leave a reversed first pair. No return journey,
regional identification, or editorial reconstruction is supplied as an established resolution.
Separate pages retain the differently named sites and point out their possible correspondences.

### Service and inheritance

Levi denotes the tribe, not the patriarch. Its collective duties include ark bearing, ministry,
and blessing (8); its inheritance is Yahweh (9). Numbers 18 distinguishes priestly and Levitical
responsibilities and specifies material support. Deuteronomy’s compressed wording does not
itself settle whether every member performs every task. Aaron’s death does not end the office:
Eleazar serves in his place (6).

### Heard intercession and the road ahead

Verse 10 directly ties the heard appeal to Israel’s survival; verse 11 renews the command to
journey into the land sworn to the fathers. The text is the answer to the preceding threat,
not evidence that Israel has become righteous enough to earn possession. The forty days repeat
the earlier duration, but this unit does not repeat the no-food-and-water wording of 9:9, 18.
