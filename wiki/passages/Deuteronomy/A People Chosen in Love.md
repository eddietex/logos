# A People Chosen in Love

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 7:1–11

## Summary

Moses names seven nations greater and mightier than Israel, anticipating Yahweh’s delivery of them
into Israel’s hands (1–2). Israel must destroy them, refuse treaties and intermarriage, and
dismantle their worship (2–5). The marriage prohibition is explained by the danger of turning to
other gods; Israel itself faces destruction if it turns away (4).

The reason given for separation is Israel’s holiness and chosen belonging to Yahweh (6). The
choice did not rest on numerical superiority. Yahweh’s love and commitment to the fathers’ oath
explain the exodus from Pharaoh’s power (7–8). The movement from command to explanation prevents
Israel from treating strength as its claim to election.

The conclusion identifies Yahweh as faithful, keeping covenant with those who love and obey him
and repaying those who hate him (9–10). Therefore Israel must do the commands (11). Prior rescue
and present obligation are held together; divine love is not presented as exemption from judgment.

## Key verses

Complete unit, copied from the stored WEB:

> **7:1** When Yahweh your God brings you into the land where you go to possess it, and casts out many nations before you, the Hittite, the Girgashite, the Amorite, the Canaanite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite, seven nations greater and mightier than you;

> **7:2** and when Yahweh your God delivers them up before you, and you strike them; then you shall utterly destroy them. You shall make no covenant with them, nor show mercy to them.

> **7:3** You shall not make marriages with them. You shall not give your daughter to his son, nor shall you take his daughter for your son.

> **7:4** For he will turn away your son from following me, that they may serve other gods. So Yahweh’s anger would be kindled against you, and he would destroy you quickly.

> **7:5** But you shall deal with them like this. You shall break down their altars, dash their pillars in pieces, cut down their Asherah poles, and burn their engraved images with fire.

> **7:6** For you are a holy people to Yahweh your God. Yahweh your God has chosen you to be a people for his own possession, above all peoples who are on the face of the earth.

> **7:7** Yahweh didn’t set his love on you, nor choose you, because you were more in number than any people; for you were the fewest of all peoples:

> **7:8** but because Yahweh loves you, and because he desires to keep the oath which he swore to your fathers, Yahweh has brought you out with a mighty hand, and redeemed you out of the house of bondage, from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt.

> **7:9** Know therefore that Yahweh your God himself is God, the faithful God, who keeps covenant and loving kindness with them who love him and keep his commandments to a thousand generations,

> **7:10** and repays those who hate him to their face, to destroy them. He will not be slack to him who hates him. He will repay him to his face.

> **7:11** You shall therefore keep the commandments, the statutes, and the ordinances, which I command you today, to do them.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 7:1–11 unit is retained. Verse 11 closes the charge; verse 12
begins the promised consequences of obedience, reserved for the next ingest.

**Love and destruction.** The command to show no mercy (2) is not softened into a command
only against idols: people are its objects, and worship objects receive a separate command
in verse 5. The tension with the mercy of 4:31 remains explicit on the mercy and love pages.
The text also threatens Israel itself (4); election is not immunity.

**Fewest and multiplied.** Deuteronomy 1:10 celebrates Israel’s multiplication, while 7:7
calls it the fewest of all peoples. These descriptions differ in comparison and rhetorical
purpose; this unit excludes numerical superiority as the cause of election, and supplies
no census or date with which to resolve the comparison quantitatively.
