# A Song to Witness Against Israel

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 31:14–23

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses that his death approaches and calls him and Joshua to the Tent of Meeting
for Joshua's commissioning. A pillar of cloud stands over the door (14–15). The transition
of leadership is accompanied by a disclosure of the people's future: after Moses dies,
Israel will pursue other gods and break the covenant (16).

Yahweh will respond by forsaking the people and hiding his face; their troubles will cause
them to recognize his absence (17–18). Before this occurs, a song must be written and taught,
placed in Israel's mouths as a witness against them. Their descendants will retain it even
after prosperity in the sworn land leads to betrayal (19–21).

Moses writes and teaches the song that same day (22). The closing commission charges Joshua
to be strong and courageous, promising that he will bring Israel into the land and that God
will be with him (23). Succession and preserved testimony answer Moses' approaching death
without concealing the coming covenant crisis.

## Key verses

> **15** Yahweh appeared in the Tent in a pillar of cloud, and the pillar of cloud stood over the Tent’s door.

— Deuteronomy 31:15

> **19** “Now therefore write this song for yourselves, and teach it to the children of Israel. Put it in their mouths, that this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel.

— Deuteronomy 31:19

— Deuteronomy 31:21

> **23** He commissioned Joshua the son of Nun, and said, “Be strong and courageous; for you shall bring the children of Israel into the land which I swore to them. I will be with you.”

— Deuteronomy 31:23

## Notes

- **Presence and forsaking:** the assurance in 31:6, 8 and the promise to Joshua in 23
  coexist with threatened forsaking in 17–18. The text connects withdrawal with Israel's
  abandonment of Yahweh (16). Neither unconditionalizing the encouragement nor erasing the
  encouragement in favor of the warning preserves the whole passage.
- **Who commissions Joshua?** Verse 23 begins with the WEB's pronoun “He.” The first-person
  land oath and promise of presence identify the charge as divine speech, fulfilling the
  purpose stated in 14; Moses' teaching in 22 should not turn the land oath into Moses' oath.
- **Memory without obedience:** the song persists in descendants' mouths (21). Its survival
  is not equivalent to faithfulness. The same community can know the song and stand accused
  by it. See the thematic connection above.
- **Scope:** this unit commissions and reports the teaching of the song; its full words
  belong to the planned Deuteronomy 31:30–32:47 unit. No further pericope is ingested here.
- **Locations and figures:** Moab and Canaan are contextual identifications. The Tent is
  named, but no precise campsite is given. Nun is a patronymic, and the unnamed fathers
  are not individually identified. The prostitution metaphor describes religious betrayal.
