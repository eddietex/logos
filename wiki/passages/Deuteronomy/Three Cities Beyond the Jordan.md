# Three Cities Beyond the Jordan

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 4:41–43

## Summary

The narrator records Moses setting apart three cities east of the Jordan: Bezer for the
Reubenites, Ramoth in Gilead for the Gadites, and Golan in Bashan for the Manassites.
The purpose is that a person who kills a neighbor unwittingly, without prior hatred, may
flee to one of them and live. The short notice moves from exhortation to a concrete act
of protection; it names the eastern cities left unnamed in Numbers 35:14.

## Key verses

> **4:41** Then Moses set apart three cities beyond the Jordan toward the sunrise;

> **4:42** that the man slayer might flee there, who kills his neighbor unawares, and didn’t hate him in time past; and that fleeing to one of these cities he might live:

> **4:43** Bezer in the wilderness, in the plain country, for the Reubenites; and Ramoth in Gilead, for the Gadites; and Golan in Bashan, for the Manassites.

## People

- [[Moses]] — sets the cities apart.
- [[Reuben]], [[Gad]], [[Manasseh]] — their descendants are the named tribal communities;
  the patriarchs themselves are not present.

The killer and neighbor are legal roles, not identifiable individuals.

## Places

- [[The Jordan]] — the cities lie on its eastern side.
- [[Bezer]] — in the wilderness and plain country, for the Reubenites.
- [[Ramoth]] — in [[Gilead]], for the Gadites.
- [[Golan]] — in [[Bashan]], for the Manassites.

## Themes

- [[Law and Obedience]] — Moses puts refuge provision into place.
- [[Judgment]] — unwitting killing without prior hatred is distinguished from murder.
- [[Life and Death]] — preserving the fugitive's life is the express purpose.
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — protection takes institutional form.
- [[City and Civilization]] — named cities serve a protective legal purpose.
- [[Vengeance]] — comparison with Numbers 35 explains the danger refuge restrains;
  the avenger is not named in these three verses.

## Notes

### A narrative interlude

The first-person address has given way to a third-person report about Moses. The planned
4:41–43 boundary is retained: the next unit begins a new introduction at 4:44. No trial,
arrival of a fugitive, or actual killing is narrated here.

### Protection without erasing distinctions

Verse 42 specifies both unwitting killing and absence of previous hatred. It does not
promise safety to every killer indiscriminately. Numbers 35:24–28 supplies adjudication
and residence until the high priest's death. Their omission from this short notice is
not a repeal or a contradiction. Likewise, the three eastern cities do not replace the
six-city provision of Numbers 35:13–14; they implement its eastern half. The texts do
not explain here precisely when full operation of all six cities began.

### Geography and sources

The direction toward sunrise fixes the eastern side of the Jordan. No modern site,
etymology, or identification with another similarly named town is asserted. All three
verses above are copied from stored WEB Deuteronomy. Comparisons were checked against
stored WEB Numbers 35:9–34 and Exodus 21:12–14; no external quotation or exact
source-language claim is introduced.
