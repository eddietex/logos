# Has Any People Heard God and Lived

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 4:32–40

## Summary

Moses closes the first address with a challenge to search all of history and all of space. *Ask now
of the days that are past* — back to *the day that God created man on the earth* — and *from the one
end of the sky to the other*, whether anything like *this great thing* has ever happened or been heard
of (32). He names two such things. No people ever heard *the voice of God speaking out of the middle of
the fire* and lived (33). And no god ever tried to take a nation for himself out of another nation *by
trials, by signs, by wonders, by war, by a mighty hand, by an outstretched arm, and by great terrors*,
as Yahweh did in Egypt *before your eyes* (34).

He then gives the purpose. *It was shown to you so that you might know that Yahweh is God. There is no
one else besides him* (35). Heaven and earth divide the experience: *out of heaven* Israel heard his
voice, to be instructed; *on earth* it saw his great fire and heard his words out of it (36). Behind the
exodus is love: *because he loved your fathers, therefore he chose their offspring after them*, and
brought Israel out *with his presence*. The aim was to drive out nations greater than Israel and give it
their land, *as it is today* (37–38).

The conclusion repeats the confession and ties it to obedience. *Know therefore today, and take it to
heart, that Yahweh himself is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath. There is no one else* (39).
So Israel must keep his statutes and commandments, that it may go well with them and their children, and
that they may prolong their days in the land Yahweh gives *forever* (40).

The unit answers the one before it. [[No Form Seen and No Image Made]] warned that Israel would serve
gods that *neither see, nor hear*; here the God Israel heard and saw act is the only one there is.
Chapter 4 began with *listen to the statutes* (4:1) and ends with *keep his statutes* (40).

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, Deuteronomy 4:32–40. Full unit copied verbatim.

> **32** For ask now of the days that are past, which were before you, since the day that God created man on the earth, and from the one end of the sky to the other, whether there has been anything as this great thing is, or has been heard like it?

> **33** Did a people ever hear the voice of God speaking out of the middle of the fire, as you have heard, and live?

> **34** Or has God tried to go and take a nation for himself from among another nation, by trials, by signs, by wonders, by war, by a mighty hand, by an outstretched arm, and by great terrors, according to all that Yahweh your God did for you in Egypt before your eyes?

> **35** It was shown to you so that you might know that Yahweh is God. There is no one else besides him.

> **36** Out of heaven he made you to hear his voice, that he might instruct you. On earth he made you to see his great fire; and you heard his words out of the middle of the fire.

> **37** Because he loved your fathers, therefore he chose their offspring after them, and brought you out with his presence, with his great power, out of Egypt;

> **38** to drive out nations from before you greater and mightier than you, to bring you in, to give you their land for an inheritance, as it is today.

> **39** Know therefore today, and take it to heart, that Yahweh himself is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath. There is no one else.

> **40** You shall keep his statutes, and his commandments, which I command you today, that it may go well with you, and with your children after you, and that you may prolong your days in the land, which Yahweh your God gives you, forever.

## People
- [[God]] — Spoke from the fire and let a people live (33); took a nation from within another by signs, war, and a mighty hand (34). Shown so that Israel would know *Yahweh is God* and *there is no one else* (35, 39). He spoke from heaven and showed his fire on earth (36), loved the fathers and chose their offspring (37), and gives the land (38, 40).
- [[Moses]] — The speaker. He closes the first address of Deuteronomy with a rhetorical search of all history and space (32), the confession (35, 39), and the charge to keep the statutes (40).

## Places
- [[Egypt]] — Where Yahweh acted *before your eyes* (34) and from which he brought Israel *with his presence, with his great power* (37).
- [[Horeb]] — Not named, but the scene of verses 33 and 36: the voice out of the fire, heard by a people who lived.

## Themes
- [[The Names of God]] — *Yahweh is God. There is no one else besides him* (35); *Yahweh himself is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath. There is no one else* (39). See [[There Is No One Else - Deuteronomy 4.35-39, Isaiah 45, and Mark 12.32]].
- [[Idolatry]] — The positive side of the image ban: after gods that *neither see, nor hear* (4:28), the only God there is.
- [[Signs and Wonders]] — *By trials, by signs, by wonders, by war, by a mighty hand, by an outstretched arm, and by great terrors* (34); the exodus is evidence shown *so that you might know* (35).
- [[Call and Election]] — Yahweh took *a nation for himself from among another nation* (34) and *chose their offspring* because he loved the fathers (37).
- [[Love]] — The first time Deuteronomy gives love as the reason for the election (37). Deuteronomy 7:7–8 will say it again.
- [[Life and Death]] — Hearing God's voice and living (33); prolonged days in the land (40).
- [[Language and Speech]] — The voice out of heaven *that he might instruct you* (36).
- [[Creation]] — The search runs back to *the day that God created man on the earth* (32); the confession spans *heaven above and … the earth beneath* (39).
- [[The Nations]] — No nation has had this (32–34); nations *greater and mightier* are driven out (38).
- [[Redemption]] — Yahweh brought Israel out of Egypt with his presence and power (37).
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The land given as an inheritance (38) and held *forever* by keeping the statutes (40).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Knowledge of God issues in a command: *You shall keep his statutes* (40).

## Notes

**The end of the first address.** Deuteronomy 4:41–43 is a narrative notice about cities of refuge,
and 4:44 begins a new heading. So 4:32–40 is the last part of the speech that began at 1:6. It ends
where 1:8 began, with the land *set before you*, now given *for an inheritance, as it is today* (38).

**Heard and lived.** Verse 33 makes survival the wonder. The people will say the same thing in their own
words at 5:24, *We have seen today that God does speak with man, and he lives*, and 5:26, *who is there
of all flesh, that has heard the voice of the living God speaking out of the middle of the fire, as we
have, and lived?* (stored WEB, not yet ingested). In 5:25–27 that wonder makes them ask for a mediator.
Exodus 33:20 says *man may not see me and live*, and Jacob at Peniel says *I have seen God face to face,
and my life is preserved* (Genesis 32:30). Deuteronomy 4 speaks of hearing, not seeing; this fits 4:12,
*you saw no form*.

**"Has God tried."** The WEB of verse 34 reads *has God tried to go and take a nation*. The sentence
contrasts Yahweh with any other *god* who might try the same; the WEB's capital *God* could read as
though Yahweh were contrasted with himself. English versions differ on whether to capitalize. This page
does not decide the Hebrew; it notes only that the argument of the verse is a comparison with other
claimed gods, as 4:35 makes plain.

**The list in verse 34.** *Trials, signs, wonders, war, a mighty hand, an outstretched arm, great
terrors.* Exodus 6:6 has Yahweh promise to redeem Israel *with an outstretched arm, and with great
judgments*. Deuteronomy 26:8 gives almost the same list in the first-fruits confession: *with a mighty
hand, with an outstretched arm, with great terror, with signs, and with wonders* (stored WEB, not yet
ingested). *War* in the list fits Exodus 15:3's *Yahweh is a man of war* at the sea.

**There is no one else.** Verses 35 and 39 make the strongest statement in the Torah so far that Yahweh
alone is God. Exodus 15:11 had asked *Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?*, which compares Yahweh
with other gods. Deuteronomy 4:39 says there is no one else *in heaven above and on the earth beneath*.
Whether that denies that other gods exist, or denies that any of them is like Yahweh, is debated.
Deuteronomy itself also speaks of the gods of the peoples (4:28; 6:14) and later says *There is no god
with me* (32:39, stored WEB, not yet ingested). The page records both kinds of statement without
choosing between readings. Isaiah 45 uses close English wording; see
[[There Is No One Else - Deuteronomy 4.35-39, Isaiah 45, and Mark 12.32]].

**Heaven and earth.** At 4:26 heaven and earth were witnesses against Israel. Here they are the two
places of revelation: the voice from heaven, the fire on earth (36). They are also the extent of
Yahweh's rule (39). In 4:12 and 4:33 the voice came *out of the middle of the fire*, and verse 36 keeps
that wording while assigning the voice to heaven. Exodus 20:22 has the same pairing: *I have talked with
you from heaven*, spoken at the mountain.

**Love as the reason.** Verse 37 is the first time Deuteronomy says Yahweh *loved* the fathers and that
this is why he chose their offspring. Deuteronomy 7:7–8 will make the point from the other side: not
because Israel was many, *but because Yahweh loves you, and because he desires to keep the oath which
he swore to your fathers* (stored WEB, not yet ingested). Verse 37's *with his presence* recalls Exodus
33:14–15, where Yahweh's presence going with Israel is the subject of Moses' plea.

**Knowing and keeping.** *Know therefore today, and take it to heart* (39) is followed directly by *You
shall keep* (40). The confession in 6:4–5, *Yahweh is one*, will be followed by *You shall love*. In
both places Moses moves from a statement about God to a command.

**Forever.** Verse 40 promises the land *forever*, but 4:26 has just said Israel *will not prolong your
days on it* if it makes images. The two conditions are stated side by side: long life in the land comes
by keeping the statutes, and is lost by idolatry. Not flagged as a contradiction.

**Sources and scope.** The planned boundary (4:32–40) and title are kept. Verse 32's *For ask now*
begins a new movement after the promise of return in 4:29–31, and verse 41 changes from speech to
narrative. Quotations are from stored WEB Deuteronomy 4, 5, 6, 7, 26, 32; Genesis 32; and Exodus 6, 15,
20, 33. Isaiah 45:5–6, 18, 21–22 and Mark 12:29–33 were verified against bible-api.com's WEB on
2026-11-05. No claim about exact Hebrew or Greek wording is made on this page.
