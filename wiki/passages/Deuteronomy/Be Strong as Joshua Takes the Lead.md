# Be Strong as Joshua Takes the Lead

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 31:1–8

## Summary

Moses addresses all Israel at the end of his leadership. He is 120 years old, can no longer go
out and come in, and has been forbidden by Yahweh to cross the Jordan. Israel’s future does not
end with him: Yahweh himself will go before the people, and Joshua will go before them as promised.
The destruction of Sihon and Og supplies a precedent for the nations ahead. Israel must still
carry out the command it has received.

Moses first calls the people to courage because Yahweh goes with them and will not forsake them.
He then calls Joshua before all Israel and repeats the encouragement personally: Joshua will go
with the people into the land sworn to their fathers and cause them to inherit it. The common
ground of both exhortations is Yahweh’s presence, not a claim about Joshua’s exceptional ability.

## Key verses

Stored WEB, Deuteronomy 31:1–8, quoted verbatim.

> **1** Moses went and spoke these words to all Israel.

> **2** He said to them, “I am one hundred twenty years old today. I can no more go out and come in. Yahweh has said to me, ‘You shall not go over this Jordan.’

> **3** Yahweh your God himself will go over before you. He will destroy these nations from before you, and you shall dispossess them. Joshua will go over before you, as Yahweh has spoken.

> **4** Yahweh will do to them as he did to Sihon and to Og, the kings of the Amorites, and to their land; whom he destroyed.

> **5** Yahweh will deliver them up before you, and you shall do to them according to all the commandment which I have commanded you.

> **6** Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid or scared of them; for Yahweh your God himself is who goes with you. He will not fail you nor forsake you.”

> **7** Moses called to Joshua, and said to him in the sight of all Israel, “Be strong and courageous, for you shall go with this people into the land which Yahweh has sworn to their fathers to give them; and you shall cause them to inherit it.

> **8** Yahweh himself is who goes before you. He will be with you. He will not fail you nor forsake you. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be discouraged.”

## Notes

**A public transition, not the death scene.** Moses speaks to the whole people and then to
Joshua in their sight. His death and the crossing remain ahead. The planned boundary is retained;
verse 9 turns to the written law and its public reading.

**Inability and exclusion.** Verse 2 puts age, inability to go out and come in, and the divine
prohibition together. Numbers 27:17 uses going out and coming in for a leader’s work. Deuteronomy
34:7 will describe Moses’ sight and strength as undiminished. These statements are in tension
if 31:2 is read as bodily collapse; the leadership idiom permits another reading, but the text
does not explicitly disentangle age, office, and prohibition. Neither statement is suppressed.

**Presence and forsaking.** Verses 6 and 8 assure people and leader that Yahweh will not forsake
them. Yet 31:16–18 threatens that he will forsake Israel when it abandons him for other gods.
This is an explicit tension within the chapter. Reading the reassurance in its covenant setting
preserves both the call to courage now and the warning about later apostasy; it does not turn
the reassurance into immunity from judgment.

**Places and ancestors.** Jordan alone is named here. Canaan identifies the promised destination
and Moab the continuing setting; neither name is supplied by these eight verses. The fathers
are collective, so no separate appearance of an individual patriarch is inferred.
