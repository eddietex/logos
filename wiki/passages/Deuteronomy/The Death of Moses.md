# The Death of Moses

**Book:** [[Deuteronomy]]
**Reference:** Deuteronomy 34:1–12

## Summary

Moses climbs from the plains of Moab to Mount Nebo, the top of Pisgah opposite Jericho, and
Yahweh shows him the whole land from Gilead to Dan, through Naphtali, Ephraim, Manasseh and
Judah to the western sea, the south, and the plain of Jericho as far as Zoar. Yahweh names it
as the land sworn to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and repeats the sentence: Moses has seen it but
shall not cross. Moses, *the servant of Yahweh*, dies there in Moab *according to Yahweh's
word*. He is buried in the valley opposite Beth Peor, and no one knows his tomb. He is 120, with
sight and strength undiminished. Israel weeps thirty days. Joshua, full of the spirit of wisdom
because Moses laid hands on him, is obeyed. The book closes with an epitaph: no prophet like
Moses has arisen since, whom Yahweh knew face to face, in signs, wonders, and mighty deeds.

## Notes

**The command fulfilled, and the end of the Torah.** Verse 1 carries out
[[Moses Commanded to Ascend Nebo]] almost item by item: the ascent of Nebo opposite Jericho, the
sight of the land, and death there in the land of Moab. It also completes what
[[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]] commanded at Pisgah (3:27). Nebo and Pisgah are joined in
one phrase, *Mount Nebo, to the top of Pisgah*; the verse does not explain how the two names
relate, and the wiki keeps separate pages without equating them. With this chapter Deuteronomy
closes, and with it the five books from Genesis on. The last scene of the Torah is its mediator
dying outside the land it has been heading for since Genesis 12.

**What Moses sees.** The survey runs from Gilead in the north-east to Dan in the far north,
west through the tribal lands to the sea, then south to the Negev and the plain of Jericho as
far as Zoar. Tribal names here are territories, not persons. Dan as a northern limit is a
place-name; [[Dan (city)]] already holds the same tension from Genesis 14:14, and the verse does
not say when the name was given. Verse 3 has *haNegev* and *hakkikkar*, *biqʿat Yerēḥô* (MAM via
Sefaria); WEB renders *the south* and *the Plain of the valley of Jericho*. *Kikkar* is the word
behind [[The Plain of the Jordan]], which is why the wiki files the phrase there, but the verse
does not say it means the whole basin Lot chose. Zoar, Lot's refuge (Genesis 19:22), closes the
view. Yahweh's words in verse 4 make it an oath-scene, not sightseeing: the land is the one sworn
to the three patriarchs, and its seeing is something Yahweh does — *showed him* (1),
*I have caused you to see it* (4). See [[Lift Up Your Eyes - Two Ways of Seeing the Land]].

**The servant of Yahweh.** Verse 5 calls Moses *ʿebed-YHWH*, *the servant of Yahweh* (MAM via
Sefaria). The wiki has not run a concordance search, so no claim is made about the title's
earlier use. Yahweh himself says *my servant Moses* in Numbers 12:7–8. Joshua 1:1–2
opens the next book with both titles: *"Now after the death of Moses the servant of Yahweh,
Yahweh spoke to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses’ servant, saying, ‘Moses my servant is dead.'"*
(WEB, verified at bible-api.com, 2026-11-10). *According to Yahweh's word* is literally *ʿal-pî
YHWH*, *at the mouth of Yahweh* (MAM via Sefaria); the text attaches no cause of death beyond it.

**Who buried him.** The Hebrew verb is singular, *wayyiqbōr ʾōtô*, *he buried him* (6; MAM via
Sefaria), and WEB keeps the singular *He*. The nearest named actor is Yahweh in verse 5, and
that is the natural reading, but the verse does not name the subject; an impersonal *one buried
him* is grammatically possible. The text is firm about the result: *no man knows where his tomb
is to this day*. Unlike Sarah, Abraham, Jacob, and Joseph, whose graves the Torah locates with
care, Moses' grave is deliberately unknown. *To this day* also shows a narrator looking back on
Moses' death from some later time. The verse does not say who wrote it. A rabbinic tradition,
commonly cited from Bava Batra 14b–15a, assigns these last verses to Joshua; that attribution is
recalled, not verified here. Jude 9 knows a dispute over the body; see
[[The Body of Moses - Deuteronomy 34.6 and Jude 9]].

**A contradiction with 31:2, flagged.** At 120 Moses said, *"I can no more go out and come in"*
(31:2). At 120 the narrator says, *"His eye was not dim, nor his strength gone"* (34:7; Hebrew
*lōʾ-kāhătâ ʿênô wəlōʾ-nās lēḥōh*, MAM via Sefaria). Both claims stand in the stored WEB.
*Go out … come in* is also the language of leading the people in Numbers 27:17, and 31:2 at once
adds Yahweh's prohibition, so the two can be read as office ended and body intact. Neither text
says so, and the wiki does not resolve it. The age also equals the limit in Genesis 6:3, *"his
days will be one hundred twenty years"*; Deuteronomy draws no link to it.

**Thirty days.** Israel weeps thirty days in the plains of Moab (8), the same length as for Aaron
(Numbers 20:29), and the mourning is said to end. Compare Jacob's seventy days in Egypt and seven
at Atad (Genesis 50:3, 10). See [[Aaron on Hor and Moses on Nebo - Numbers 20 and Deuteronomy 34]].

**Joshua, and a tension with Numbers 27.** Verse 9 says Joshua was *full of the spirit of wisdom*
(*mālēʾ rûaḥ ḥokmâ*, MAM via Sefaria) *for Moses had laid his hands on him*. Numbers 27:18 calls
him *"a man in whom is the Spirit"* before the hands are laid. So Numbers puts the Spirit before
the gesture, while Deuteronomy gives the gesture as the reason. Both claims are kept;
"spirit of wisdom" may name a gift for office rather than the Spirit as such, but no text
makes that distinction. Israel obeys Joshua and does *as Yahweh commanded Moses*: the
obedience is to the same command, not to a new founder. See
[[Hands on the Head - The First Blessing by Touch]].

**The epitaph.** *Since then, there has not arisen a prophet in Israel like Moses* (10) sets
the last word against [[A Prophet Like Moses]]. Deuteronomy 18 promised one; Deuteronomy 34 says
none has come yet. Numbers 12:6–8 already set Moses apart from other prophets. Verse 10 has
*pānîm ʾel-pānîm* (MAM via Sefaria), the phrase of Exodus 33:11; here it is Yahweh who *knew*
him so. Verses 11–12 sum up his career as the signs and wonders sent against Pharaoh and the
*mighty hand* and *awesome deeds* done *in the sight of all Israel*, closing the book with the
Exodus vocabulary rather than with law. See
[[Mouth to Mouth - Numbers 12.6-8, Exodus 33, and Deuteronomy 34]] and
[[A Prophet Like Me - Deuteronomy 18.15-19 in John, Acts, and the Transfiguration]].

**The New Testament.** Hebrews 3:5 says *"Moses indeed was faithful in all his house as a
servant"* (WEB, verified at bible-api.com), drawing on Numbers 12:7 and the servant title. Moses
appears again on a mountain at the Transfiguration; see
[[Shining on the Mountain - Moses and the Transfiguration]]. Those Gospel texts are not quoted
here because they are not stored.
