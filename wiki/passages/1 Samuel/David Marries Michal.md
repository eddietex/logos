# David Marries Michal

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 18:17–30

## Summary

> **18:17** Saul said to David, “Behold, my elder daughter Merab, I will give her to you as wife. Only be valiant for
> me, and fight Yahweh’s battles.” For Saul said, “Don’t let my hand be on him, but let the hand of the Philistines be
> on him.”
> **18** David said to Saul, “Who am I, and what is my life, or my father’s family in Israel, that I should be
> son-in-law to the king?”
> **19** But at the time when Merab, Saul’s daughter, should have been given to David, she was given to Adriel the
> Meholathite as wife.
> **20** Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved David; and they told Saul, and the thing pleased him.
> **21** Saul said, I will give her to him, that she may be a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be
> against him. Therefore Saul said to David, “You shall today be my son-in-law a second time.”
> **22** Saul commanded his servants, “Talk with David secretly, and say, ‘Behold, the king has delight in you, and all
> his servants love you. Now therefore be the king’s son-in-law.’”
> **23** Saul’s servants spoke those words in the ears of David. David said, “Does it seem to you a light thing to be
> the king’s son-in-law, since I am a poor man, and little known?”
> **24** The servants of Saul told him, saying, “David spoke like this.”
> **25** Saul said, “Tell David, ‘The king desires no dowry except one hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be
> avenged of the king’s enemies.’” Now Saul thought he would make David fall by the hand of the Philistines.
> **26** When his servants told David these words, it pleased David well to be the king’s son-in-law. Before the
> deadline,
> **27** David arose and went, he and his men, and killed two hundred men of the Philistines. Then David brought their
> foreskins, and they gave them in full number to the king, that he might be the king’s son-in-law. Then Saul gave him
> Michal his daughter as wife.
> **28** Saul saw and knew that Yahweh was with David; and Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved him.
> **29** Saul was even more afraid of David; and Saul was David’s enemy continually.
> **30** Then the princes of the Philistines went out; and as often as they went out, David behaved himself more wisely
> than all the servants of Saul, so that his name was highly esteemed.

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 18.)

[[Saul]] offers [[David]] his elder daughter [[Merab]], on one condition: *Only be valiant for me, and fight Yahweh's
battles*. The narrator lets the reader hear the rest: *Don't let my hand be on him, but let the hand of the
Philistines be on him* (18:17). David answers *Who am I … that I should be son-in-law to the king?* (18), and when the
time comes Merab is *given to Adriel the Meholathite as wife* (19). No reason is given.

Then *Michal, Saul's daughter, loved David* (20). Saul is told, and *the thing pleased him*: she will be *a snare to
him*, and again *the hand of the Philistines* will do what Saul will not (21). He sends his servants to David
*secretly* with a message of delight and love, *be the king's son-in-law* (22). David answers them as he answered the
king: *Does it seem to you a light thing … since I am a poor man, and little known?* (23). Saul names his price. *The
king desires no dowry except one hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be avenged of the king's enemies*, and the
narrator names his purpose, *to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines* (25). This time *it pleased David
well* (26). Before the days are up he goes with his men, kills two hundred Philistines, and brings their foreskins *in
full number to the king*, and *Saul gave him Michal his daughter as wife* (27).

The pericope ends where the last one did, with what Saul saw and what it did to him. *Saul saw and knew that Yahweh
was with David; and Michal, Saul's daughter, loved him* (28). *Saul was even more afraid of David; and Saul was David's
enemy continually* (29). Whenever the Philistine princes go out, David *behaved himself more wisely than all the
servants of Saul, so that his name was highly esteemed* (30).

**Where it happens.** At Saul's court, which the text does not place. Saul's home is [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] (10:26;
15:34, stored WEB), but 18:17–30 names no town, and the wiki does not place the betrothal there. The only place-word in
the pericope is Adriel's *Meholathite*, *hammǝḥōlāṯî* (19, WLC), a man of Meholah; it is commonly taken to mean
[[Abel Meholah]] (recalled, not checked). The raid on the Philistines (27) is not located.

**What is going on.** Saul has stopped throwing the spear and starts using his daughters. Twice the narrator opens
Saul's mind to the reader, at 17 and 21, and once more at 25, and each time the thought is the same: *the hand of the
Philistines*. The king who could not pin David to the wall (18:11) will let the enemy do it. Both offers are traps,
and the reader is told so; David is not told, and the text never says whether he knew.

The two daughters repeat the two daughters of Laban. *My elder daughter Merab*, *bittî haggǝḏôlâ* (17, WLC), is the
narrator's word for Leah, *the name of the elder was Leah* (Genesis 29:16, *haggǝḏōlâ*); 14:49 had called them *the
firstborn* and *the younger*. The elder is given, though here to another man, and the younger is loved. When Michal is
handed over, the clause is almost Laban's: *wayyitten-lô šāʾûl ʾeṯ-mîḵal bittô lǝʾiššâ*, *Saul gave him Michal his
daughter as wife* (27), and *wayyitten-lô ʾeṯ-rāḥēl bittô lô lǝʾiššâ*, *He gave him Rachel his daughter as wife*
(Genesis 29:28, WLC). Jacob's demand was *my days are fulfilled* (Genesis 29:21); here *the days were not fulfilled*
(26, WLC; the WEB's *Before the deadline*) when David brings the price. See
[[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]].

The price is the other old story. A bride-price, a marriage into a family, and a condition of foreskins come together
in one other place in the books stored: Shechem asks for Dinah, *Ask me a great amount for a dowry*, and Jacob's sons
answer *with deceit* that every male must be circumcised (Genesis 34:12–15). There the condition is a trap and it
closes; the Shechemites are killed while they are still sore. Here the condition is a trap and it fails: David pays it
twice over. See [[A Bride-Price of Foreskins - Shechem, Saul, and David]].

And the love goes on growing on one side while the fear grows on the other. Saul loved David (16:21); Jonathan loved
him (18:1, 3); *all Israel and Judah* loved him (16). Now Saul's servants are told to say *all his servants love you*
(22), and *Michal … loved David* (20, 28), twice, the second time as the last word on what Saul *saw and knew*. Saul's
fear, *afraid* (12) and *in awe* (15), becomes *even more afraid* (29), and the pericope gives it a new name: *Saul
was David's enemy*, *ʾōyēḇ* (29, WLC), *continually*. The *enemies* the bride-price was meant to avenge were the
Philistines (25); four verses later the king's enemy is the king.

**What is notable.**

- **Be valiant for me.** *Hĕyēh-llî lǝḇen-ḥayil* (17, WLC), literally *be a son of strength for me*. In 14:52, *when
  Saul saw any … valiant man*, *ben-ḥayil*, *he took him into his service* (stored WEB; WLC). He has taken this one;
  now he offers him a daughter for the same quality.
- **Yahweh's battles.** *Wǝhillāḥēm milḥămôṯ YHWH* (17, WLC). The phrase *milḥămôṯ YHWH* occurs in the Hebrew files
  checked at Numbers 21:14, *the book of the Wars of Yahweh*; here; and at 25:28, where Abigail tells David *my lord
  fights Yahweh's battles* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). Saul says it as a cover; Abigail will say it as a blessing.
  At Elah, David had said *the battle is Yahweh's* (17:47). See
  [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].
- **My hand, and the hand of the Philistines.** *ʾAl-tǝhî yāḏî bô ûṯǝhî-ḇô yaḏ-pǝlištîm* (17), said again at 21 and
  in other words at 25, *lǝhappîl … bǝyaḏ-pǝlištîm*, *to make … fall by the hand of the Philistines* (WLC). Three
  times, in Saul's thoughts, not his speech. At 19:1 he will speak it aloud, to Jonathan and to all his servants,
  *that they should kill David* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Who am I.** *Mî ʾānōḵî ûmî ḥayyay* (18, WLC). *Mî ʾānōḵî* occurs in the files checked only at Exodus 3:11, Moses
  at the bush, *Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh*; here; and at 2 Samuel 7:18, David before Yahweh, *Who am I,
  Lord Yahweh, and what is my house* (WLC; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB stored). Saul's own answer to Samuel was the
  same in substance, *Am I not a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes* (9:21, stored WEB). Here the question is put
  to a king, not to God, and the call it answers is a marriage. See [[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]].
- **Son-in-law.** *Ḥāṯān lammeleḵ* (18) and the verb *hiṯḥattēn*, *become son-in-law* (21, 22, 23, 26, 27, WLC;
  *ḥāṯan*, morphhb lemma 2859 b, in the *hitpael*). The verb occurs in the files checked five times in this pericope
  and three times elsewhere: Hamor's *Make marriages with us* (Genesis 34:9), and the commands *You shall not make
  marriages with them* (Deuteronomy 7:3) and its warning in Joshua 23:12 (stored WEB). Every other use is a marriage
  across a people's boundary. The noun *ḥāṯān* occurs in 1 Samuel only here and at 22:14, where Ahimelech defends
  David to Saul as *the king's son-in-law* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Adriel the Meholathite.** *Lǝʿaḏrîʾēl hammǝḥōlāṯî* (19, WLC). He is named in the files checked only here and at
  2 Samuel 21:8, where the WLC reads *the five sons of Michal the daughter of Saul, whom she bore to Adriel the son of
  Barzillai the Meholathite* (WLC; paraphrased). 2 Samuel 6:23 says *Michal … had no child to the day of her death*
  (WLC; paraphrased). The two verses are in tension; many translations read *Merab* at 21:8 (recalled, not checked).
  Both readings are recorded. See [[Adriel]].
- **She was given.** *Wǝhîʾ nittǝnâ* (19, WLC), the passive of *nāṯan*, *to give*: no one is named as giving her. In
  17, 21, and 27 Saul is the subject of *give*; at 19 the narrator withholds it.
- **Michal loved David.** *Watteʾĕhaḇ mîḵal bat-šāʾûl ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ* (20) and *ûmîḵal bat-šāʾûl ʾăhēḇaṯhû* (28, WLC).
  In the Hebrew files checked, *ʾāhaḇ* with a woman as subject occurs only at Genesis 25:28, *Rebekah loved Jacob*;
  Ruth 4:15, *your daughter-in-law, who loves you*, of Ruth and Naomi; and here, twice. Michal is the one woman in
  those files whose love for a husband is stated. The text does not say that David loved her. See [[Love]].
- **It pleased him.** *Wayyîšar haddāḇār bǝʿênāyw* (20), and of David, *wayyîšar haddāḇār bǝʿênê ḏāwiḏ* (26, WLC):
  *the thing was right in his eyes*, said of the father and of the groom. *Yāšar* in this sense (lemma 3474) occurs in
  the files checked at Numbers 23:27; Judges 14:3, 7, Samson's *she pleases me well* of his Philistine bride;
  1 Samuel 6:12, of the cows that *took the straight way*; and 2 Samuel 17:4 (WLC). See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **A snare.** *Ûṯǝhî-lô lǝmôqēš* (21, WLC). *Môqēš*, *snare* (lemma 4170), occurs in the files checked at Exodus
  10:7; 23:33; 34:12; Deuteronomy 7:16; Joshua 23:13; Judges 2:3; 8:27; here; and 2 Samuel 22:6. Every earlier one is
  a people, its gods, or Gideon's ephod, the things that would draw Israel from Yahweh; in Joshua 23:12–13 marriage
  with the nations is what makes them *a snare and a trap*. Saul makes his own daughter one. See
  [[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].
- **A second time.** *Bištayim tiṯḥattēn bî hayyôm* (21, WLC), literally *by two you shall become my son-in-law
  today*. The WEB's *a second time* reads it as a second offer after Merab; the Hebrew does not say which two it
  means. In this form, the dual *bištayim*, it occurs in the files checked only here, and the wiki leaves it open.
- **Secretly.** *Ballāṭ* (22, WLC), *quietly*, the word of Ruth coming *softly* to the threshing floor (Ruth 3:7) and
  of David cutting the skirt of Saul's robe *secretly* (24:4 WEB; 24:5 WLC; not yet ingested). Saul's servants carry a
  message the king wants to sound like their own.
- **Delight, and no desire.** *Ḥāp̄ēṣ bǝḵā hammeleḵ*, *the king has delight in you* (22), then *ʾên-ḥēp̄eṣ lammeleḵ
  bǝmōhar*, *the king desires no dowry* (25, WLC): verb and noun of one root, said of the king, three verses apart.
  The verb is Shechem's, *he had delight in Jacob's daughter* (Genesis 34:19), and Jonathan's, who *greatly
  delighted in David* (19:1, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The delight the servants announce is the one thing Saul does not have.
- **A poor man, and little known.** *ʾÎš-rāš wǝniqleh* (23, WLC). *Rāš*, *poor* (lemma 7326), occurs in the files
  checked only here and in Nathan's parable of *the poor man* and his one ewe lamb (2 Samuel 12:1, 3, 4, WLC;
  paraphrased), the parable whose rich man is David. *Niqleh*, *little known* (*qālâ*, lemma 7034), is Deuteronomy's
  *degraded* (25:3) and *sets light* (27:16) (stored WEB). Hannah had sung that Yahweh *raises up the poor out of the
  dust … to make them sit with princes* (2:8), with a different word for *poor*, *dal* (stored WEB; WLC). See
  [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]].
- **The dowry.** *Mōhar* (25, WLC; lemma 4119), in the files checked only at Genesis 34:12, Shechem's *ask me a great
  amount for a dowry*; Exodus 22:16–17, the *dowry of virgins* a seducer must pay; and here (stored WEB). See
  [[Marriage]].
- **Foreskins.** *Bǝmēʾâ ʿorlôṯ pǝlištîm* (25) and *ʿorlōṯêhem* (27, WLC). *ʿOrlâ* (lemma 6190) occurs in the files
  checked at Genesis 17:11–25; 34:14; Exodus 4:25; Leviticus 12:3; 19:23; Deuteronomy 10:16; Joshua 5:3; here; and
  2 Samuel 3:14. The Philistines are the *uncircumcised* of Judges 14:3, of Jonathan at 14:6, and of David at 17:26,
  36 (stored WEB); the bride-price makes the insult into a count. See [[Circumcision]].
- **To be avenged of the king's enemies.** *Lǝhinnāqēm bǝʾōyǝḇê hammeleḵ* (25, WLC). It is Saul's oath at Michmash,
  *until it is evening, and I am avenged of my enemies*, *wǝniqqamtî mēʾōyǝḇay* (14:24, stored WEB; WLC), the
  *niphal* of *nāqam* both times. The enemies are the Philistines; at 29 the *ʾōyēḇ* is Saul. See [[Vengeance]].
- **One hundred, two hundred.** Saul asks for a hundred (25); David brings two hundred (27). In 2 Samuel 3:14 David
  demands Michal back from Ish-bosheth as *my wife, whom I betrothed to me for a hundred foreskins of the Philistines*
  (WLC; paraphrased), the price asked, not the price paid. The Greek reads a hundred at 18:27 (recalled, not checked).
  The wiki records the WLC and WEB *two hundred*.
- **The days were not fulfilled.** *Wǝlōʾ mālʾû hayyāmîm* (26), then *wayǝmalʾûm lammeleḵ*, *they gave them in full
  number to the king* (27, WLC): one verb, *mālēʾ*, *fill*, for the time not yet full and the count made full. No
  deadline was stated in 25; the text assumes one. Jacob's *my days are fulfilled*, *mālʾû yāmāy* (Genesis 29:21,
  WLC), is the same phrase.
- **He and his men.** *Hûʾ waʾănāšāyw* (27, WLC). The first time David has *men* of his own in the book. They will
  be four hundred at Adullam (22:2) and six hundred after (23:13) (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Saul saw and knew.** *Wayyarʾ šāʾûl wayyēḏaʿ kî YHWH ʿim-dāwiḏ* (28, WLC). In 15 Saul *saw* that David behaved
  wisely; here he *saw and knew* what the narrator had said at 12 and 14, *Yahweh was with him*. It is the first time
  Saul is said to know it. See [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].
- **Even more afraid.** *Wayyōʾsep̄ šāʾûl lērōʾ mippǝnê ḏāwiḏ ʿôḏ* (29, WLC), *Saul added to fear David yet*: the
  fourth step after *angry* (8), *afraid* (12), and *in awe* (15). See [[The Fear of God]].
- **David's enemy continually.** *Wayǝhî šāʾûl ʾōyēḇ ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ kol-hayyāmîm* (29, WLC), *all the days*. It is the
  first time the book calls Saul David's enemy. At 19:17 Saul calls David *my enemy*, and at 24:4 David's men call
  Saul *your enemy* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **More wisely than all.** *Śāḵal dāwiḏ mikkōl ʿaḇdê šāʾûl* (30, WLC), the fourth *śāḵal* of the chapter (5, 14, 15,
  30) and in the files checked the last in 1 Samuel. See [[Wisdom]].
- **Highly esteemed.** *Wayyîqar šǝmô mǝʾōḏ* (30, WLC), *his name was precious*. *Yāqar* (lemma 3365) occurs in the
  files checked only here and at 26:21, where Saul says *my life was precious in your eyes today* (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested). David's name grows precious in Saul's war; Saul's life will be precious in David's eyes.

**How it connects.** The pericope finishes chapter 18 on the contrast that ran through it: *Yahweh was with David*,
and Saul feared him (12, 14–15, 28–29). The reward of 17:25, *the king … will give him his daughter*, is paid here,
though 18:17–27 never refers back to it, and the condition is new (see [[David Comes to the Camp]] and [[Merab]]).
Michal's love is what makes her a snare in Saul's plan, and the same love will make her David's rescuer when Saul
sends men to kill him in his house (19:11–17, stored WEB; not yet ingested); then Saul gives her to Palti son of
Laish (25:44), and David demands her back (2 Samuel 3:13–16). The plan to kill David *by the hand of the Philistines*
is the plan David will carry out against Uriah (2 Samuel 11:14–17, recalled, not checked; no 2 Samuel WEB stored).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 18:17–30 as planned: 18:17 opens the marriage offers and 18:30 closes the
chapter on David's name, and 19:1 begins Saul's open order to kill him. One new person page, [[Adriel]], because he
returns at 2 Samuel 21:8 with the reading problem attached. No page for Saul's servants, who carry messages here and
are not individuated, nor for *his men* (27), who are not yet a group with a name; both may earn pages later. No new
place page: [[Abel Meholah]] already exists, and the link to *Meholathite* is listed as recalled. One new connection,
[[A Bride-Price of Foreskins - Shechem, Saul, and David]].

**Recorded, not resolved.** Why Merab was given to Adriel (19). Whether *bištayim* (21) means a second offer, two
daughters, or something else. Whether David knew the offers were traps. 2 Samuel 21:8 (WLC *Michal*) against
2 Samuel 6:23 (Michal childless). The hundred foreskins asked (25; 2 Samuel 3:14) and the two hundred brought (27).
