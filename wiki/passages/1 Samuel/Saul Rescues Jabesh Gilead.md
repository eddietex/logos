# Saul Rescues Jabesh Gilead

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 11:1–15

## Summary

> **1** Then Nahash the Ammonite came up, and encamped against Jabesh Gilead: and all the men of Jabesh said to
> Nahash, "Make a covenant with us, and we will serve you."
> **2** Nahash the Ammonite said to them, "On this condition I will make it with you, that all your right eyes be
> gouged out. I will make this dishonor all Israel."
> **3** The elders of Jabesh said to him, "Give us seven days, that we may send messengers to all the borders of
> Israel; and then, if there is no one to save us, we will come out to you."
> **4** Then the messengers came to Gibeah of Saul, and spoke these words in the ears of the people, then all the
> people lifted up their voice, and wept.
> **5** Behold, Saul came following the oxen out of the field; and Saul said, "What ails the people that they
> weep?" They told him the words of the men of Jabesh.
> **6** God's Spirit came mightily on Saul when he heard those words, and his anger burned hot.
> **7** He took a yoke of oxen, and cut them in pieces, and sent them throughout all the borders of Israel by the
> hand of messengers, saying, "Whoever doesn't come out after Saul and after Samuel, so shall it be done to his
> oxen." The dread of Yahweh fell on the people, and they came out as one man.
> **8** He counted them in Bezek; and the children of Israel were three hundred thousand, and the men of Judah
> thirty thousand.
> **9** They said to the messengers who came, "Tell the men of Jabesh Gilead, 'Tomorrow, by the time the sun is
> hot, you will be rescued.'" The messengers came and told the men of Jabesh; and they were glad.
> **10** Therefore the men of Jabesh said, "Tomorrow we will come out to you, and you shall do with us all that
> seems good to you."
> **11** On the next day, Saul put the people in three companies; and they came into the middle of the camp in the
> morning watch, and struck the Ammonites until the heat of the day. Those who remained were scattered, so that
> no two of them were left together.
> **12** The people said to Samuel, "Who is he who said, 'Shall Saul reign over us?' Bring those men, that we may
> put them to death!"
> **13** Saul said, "No man shall be put to death today; for today Yahweh has rescued Israel."
> **14** Then Samuel said to the people, "Come, and let us go to Gilgal, and renew the kingdom there."
> **15** All the people went to Gilgal; and there they made Saul king before Yahweh in Gilgal. There they offered
> sacrifices of peace offerings before Yahweh; and there Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly.

[[Nahash (king of Ammon)]], *the Ammonite*, comes up and camps against [[Jabesh Gilead]]. The men of the town offer
to *serve* him if he will make a covenant with them (1). He names his price: every man's right eye gouged out, *I
will make this dishonor all Israel* (2). The elders ask for seven days to send messengers *to all the borders of
Israel*: *if there is no one to save us, we will come out to you* (3). The messengers come to *Gibeah of Saul*,
[[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], and the people there can only weep (4).

[[Saul]] is coming in from the field behind his oxen and asks why the people are weeping (5). When he hears,
*God's Spirit came mightily on Saul … and his anger burned hot* (6). He cuts a yoke of oxen in pieces and sends
the pieces through all Israel with a threat: whoever does not come out *after Saul and after Samuel* will have
his oxen treated the same way. *The dread of Yahweh fell on the people, and they came out as one man* (7). He
counts them at [[Bezek]]: three hundred thousand of Israel, thirty thousand of Judah (8).

The messengers go back with a promise, *Tomorrow, by the time the sun is hot, you will be rescued*, and Jabesh is
glad (9). The men of Jabesh say, *Tomorrow we will come out to you, and you shall do with us all that seems good
to you*; the text does not name whom they are speaking to (10). In the morning watch Saul's three companies break
into the Ammonite camp and strike [[Ammon]] *until the heat of the day*; the survivors scatter *so that no two of
them were left together* (11).

The people turn to [[Samuel]]: *Who is he who said, 'Shall Saul reign over us?' Bring those men, that we may put
them to death!* (12). Saul answers instead: *No man shall be put to death today; for today Yahweh has rescued
Israel* (13). Samuel calls the people to [[Gilgal]] *to renew the kingdom* (14). There *they made Saul king before
Yahweh*, offer peace offerings, and *Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly* (15).

**What is going on.** The previous pericope ended with a question: *How could this man save us?* (10:27). This
one answers it. Jabesh's elders ask for a *môšîaʿ*, *one to save* (3); Saul's promise is *təšûʿâ*, *rescue* (9);
and Saul himself credits it to Yahweh, *today Yahweh has rescued Israel* (13). Saul is twice made king before the
battle, by oil (10:1) and by lot (10:24), and the battle is what the people had asked a king for: *that our king
may … go out before us, and fight our battles* (8:20). After it, the kingdom is *renewed* at Gilgal, and for the
first time in the story everyone is glad.

The pattern is the pattern of Judges. An enemy oppresses; the people weep; the Spirit comes on a man; he musters
the tribes; the enemy is struck. Saul behaves like a judge. He is ploughing when the news comes, as Gideon was
threshing wheat when the angel came (Judges 6:11), and the verb of the Spirit is Samson's (Judges 14:6, 19; see
below). What makes it a king's story is what happens after the battle: no notice of rest, but a king made a third
time. The narrative does not say why one victory needed a third king-making, and the three scenes do not mention
one another. Many readers explain the three as separate accounts of Saul's accession combined by an editor; that
is a view about how the book was put together, recalled and not checked here, and the wiki records it without
adopting it. Read as it stands, the sequence is: Yahweh chooses in private, the lot confirms in public, and the
victory wins over the people who doubted, and only then does *all the people* make him king.

The episode also undoes the last chapters of Judges. There, a Levite from Gibeah's quarrel cut his concubine in
pieces and sent her *throughout all the borders of Israel* (Judges 19:29), and Israel came out *as one man*
(20:1) to destroy most of Benjamin; then twelve thousand were sent to destroy Jabesh Gilead because it had not
come up to Mizpah (21:8–12), under an oath that *He shall surely be put to death* (21:5). Here a man of Gibeah of
Benjamin cuts up a yoke of oxen and sends the pieces *throughout all the borders of Israel*; Israel comes out *as
one man*; and the city they come out for is Jabesh Gilead. At the end, when the people want men put to death,
the Benjamite king says *No man shall be put to death today*. The text never cites Judges; the shared words are
recorded below, and the reading is the wiki's. See
[[Jabesh Gilead and Benjamin - Judges 21, 1 Samuel 11, and 1 Samuel 31]] and
[[Cut Limb by Limb - The Burnt Offering, the Concubine, and Saul's Oxen]].

**Recorded, not resolved — when Nahash came.** In his farewell Samuel says *When you saw that Nahash the king of
the children of Ammon came against you, you said to me, 'No, but a king shall reign over us'* (12:12, stored WEB;
not yet ingested). In the order of the narrative, the elders asked for a king in chapter 8 because Samuel was old
and his sons took bribes (8:1–5), and Nahash appears only after Saul is chosen. Either Nahash's threat was already
in the background of chapter 8 without being named, or 12:12 telescopes the events, or two traditions differ. The
text does not reconcile them, and the wiki leaves both claims standing. See [[Nahash (king of Ammon)]].

**What is notable.**

- **Nahash.** *Nāḥāš hāʿammônî* (1, WLC). The name is spelled with the same letters as *nāḥāš*, *serpent*
  (Genesis 3:1, WLC), though the lexical data gives the proper name its own lemma (5176, against 5175). The text
  makes nothing of the sound. In the WLC files searched (Genesis through 2 Samuel) the name occurs at 11:1–2;
  12:12; and 2 Samuel 10:2 and 17:25, 27. 2 Samuel 10:2 has David say Nahash *showed kindness to me* (WEB checked
  at bible-api.com, 2026-11-18); whether that is the same man the text does not say.
- **Make a covenant with us, and we will serve you.** *Kərāṯ-lānû ḇərîṯ wənaʿaḇḏekkā* (1, WLC). Jabesh offers to
  become a vassal. Deuteronomy says of Ammon and Moab *You shall not seek their peace nor their prosperity all your
  days forever* (23:6, stored WEB). The narrative does not judge Jabesh's offer by that law; it lets Nahash's
  answer judge it.
- **All your right eyes gouged out.** *Binqôr lāḵem kol-ʿên yāmîn* (2, WLC). *Nāqar*, *bore out*, occurs in the
  WLC files searched only here, at Numbers 16:14 (Dathan and Abiram's *Will you put out the eyes of these men?*)
  and at Judges 16:21, where *The Philistines … put out his eyes* (Samson) (stored WEB; lemma 5365). Why the right
  eye, the text does not say. A common explanation is that it would disable a soldier who fought with a shield on
  his left; that is recalled, not checked, and the text gives no reason beyond *dishonor*.
- **I will make this dishonor all Israel.** *Wəśamtîhā ḥerpâ ʿal-kol-yiśrāʾēl* (2, WLC). *Ḥerpâ*, *reproach*, is
  the word of Gilgal's naming: *Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you* (Joshua 5:9, stored WEB).
  The story that begins with a reproach laid on all Israel ends at Gilgal. David will ask who *takes away the
  reproach from Israel* (17:26, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[The Reproach of Israel - Joshua 5.9, 1 Samuel 11.2, and 17.26]].
- **If there is no one to save us.** *Wəʾim-ʾên môšîaʿ ʾōṯānû* (3, WLC). *Môšîaʿ* is the title of the judges (for
  example Judges 3:9, 15) and is said of God at 10:19. The elders send to *all the borders of Israel*, *bəḵōl
  gəḇûl yiśrāʾēl*, the phrase of Judges 19:29, and Saul will send back by the same phrase (7).
- **Gibeah of Saul.** *Giḇʿaṯ šāʾûl* (4, WLC). The town is named after its man for the first time. See
  [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]].
- **All the people … wept.** *Wayyiśʾû ḵol-hāʿām ʾeṯ-qôlām wayyiḇkû* (4, WLC), the phrase of Bochim (Judges 2:4)
  and of Bethel after the war with Benjamin (Judges 21:2). In Judges the weeping went before Yahweh; here it is
  weeping and nothing else until Saul arrives.
- **Following the oxen out of the field.** *Bāʾ ʾaḥărê habbāqār min-haśśāḏeh* (5, WLC). The king chosen at Mizpah
  has gone back to farming. The narrator does not comment. *Bāqār*, *cattle, oxen*, returns in verse 7 for what he
  cuts up and for what he threatens.
- **God's Spirit came mightily on Saul.** *Wattiṣlaḥ rûaḥ-ʾĕlōhîm ʿal-šāʾûl … wayyiḥar ʾappô məʾōḏ* (6, WLC). The
  same verb and the same following clause, *his anger burned*, as Judges 14:19, where Samson goes down to
  Ashkelon. The verb came on Saul at 10:10 to make him prophesy; here it makes him a war leader. The written text
  (*kethiv*) has *bšmʿw*, *when he heard*, without vowels; the WLC prints it unpointed. See
  [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].
- **A yoke of oxen, cut in pieces.** *Wayyiqqaḥ ṣemeḏ bāqār wayənattəḥēhû* (7, WLC). *Nittaḥ* is the altar's word
  for cutting up a burnt offering and the word of Judges 19:29. *Ṣemeḏ*, *a yoke, a pair*, appears in the WLC
  files of Genesis through 1 Samuel 11 only at Judges 19:3, 10, *a couple of donkeys*, the Levite's (stored WEB;
  lemma 6776). Both texts pair the Levite's journey and Saul's muster with the same two words.
- **After Saul and after Samuel.** *ʾAḥărê šāʾûl wəʾaḥar šəmûʾēl* (7, WLC). The new king summons in his own name
  and in the prophet's. Whether that is deference, or the borrowing of authority he does not yet have, the text
  does not say.
- **So shall it be done to his oxen.** The threat is to property. The oath of Judges 21:5 was against life: *He
  shall surely be put to death*, *môṯ yûmāṯ* (WLC). Saul's last word in the chapter is *lōʾ-yûmaṯ ʾîš*, *No man
  shall be put to death* (13, WLC), the same verb in the same form.
- **The dread of Yahweh.** *Paḥaḏ-YHWH* (7, WLC). In the WLC files searched, *paḥad* with Yahweh's name as its
  genitive occurs only here. Elsewhere *paḥad* is *the fear of Isaac*, a name for God (Genesis 31:42, 53), and
  the dread that Yahweh puts on Israel's enemies (Exodus 15:16; Deuteronomy 2:25; 11:25) (lemma 6343). Here it
  falls on Israel, and moves them to come out. See [[The Fear of God]].
- **As one man.** *Kəʾîš ʾeḥāḏ* (7, WLC), the phrase of the muster against Benjamin (Judges 20:1, 8, 11).
- **Counted at Bezek.** *Wayyip̄qəḏēm bəḇāzeq* (8, WLC). *Bezek* appears in the WLC files searched only here and at
  Judges 1:4–5, Judah's first battle; whether it is one place, the text does not say. See [[Bezek]]. *Three
  hundred thousand … thirty thousand* separates *the men of Judah* from *the children of Israel*. The narrator
  does not explain the separate count. Readers often see in it the later division of the kingdom; that is a
  reading. The size of the numbers, and whether *ʾelep* here means *thousand* or a smaller military unit, is
  debated (recalled, not checked); the wiki gives the WEB's figures.
- **Tomorrow, by the time the sun is hot.** *Māḥār tihyeh-lāḵem təšûʿâ bəḥōm haššemeš* (9, WLC); the battle runs
  *until the heat of the day*, *ʿaḏ-ḥōm hayyôm* (11). The promise names the hour and the narrative keeps it.
  *Təšûʿâ* returns at 13 for Saul's credit to Yahweh. In the files searched it is the noun of Samson's *great
  deliverance* (Judges 15:18) and of David's victories (19:5; 2 Samuel 23:10, 12) (lemma 8668).
- **Tomorrow we will come out to you.** *Māḥār nēṣēʾ ʾălêḵem* (10, WLC). The text does not say whom they tell.
  Spoken to Nahash, it sounds like surrender, *you shall do with us all that seems good to you*; the reader knows
  that *tomorrow* is the day of rescue. *Yāṣāʾ*, *come out*, is the verb of their first offer (3) and of Israel's
  muster (7). Most readers take verse 10 as a ruse; the narrator does not call it one.
- **Three companies in the morning watch.** *Šəlōšâ rāʾšîm … bəʾašmōreṯ habbōqer* (11, WLC). Gideon divided his
  three hundred *into three companies* (Judges 7:16), and so did Abimelech (Judges 9:43); the Philistines will
  send raiders *in three companies* (13:17) (stored WEB). *The morning watch* occurs in the WLC files searched only
  here and at Exodus 14:24, *In the morning watch, Yahweh looked out on the Egyptian army … and confused the
  Egyptian army* (stored WEB; lemma 821). Gideon struck in *the middle watch* (Judges 7:19).
- **No two of them were left together.** *Wəlōʾ nišʾărû-ḇām šənayim yāḥaḏ* (11, WLC). The rout is total. Nahash's
  fate is not told.
- **Who is he who said, 'Shall Saul reign over us?'** (12). The people bring their demand to Samuel, not to Saul.
  At 10:27 the doubters' words were *How could this man save us?*; the people now quote a different question, and
  the text does not say whether they mean the same men. See [[Worthless Fellows (of Saul's time)]].
- **No man shall be put to death today; for today Yahweh has rescued Israel.** *Kî hayyôm ʿāśâ-YHWH təšûʿâ
  bəyiśrāʾēl* (13, WLC). Saul gives the victory to Yahweh and the day to mercy. He *held his peace* at 10:27; he
  speaks now, and it is to spare the men who despised him.
- **Renew the kingdom.** *Ûnəḥaddēš šām hamməlûḵâ* (14, WLC). *Ḥādaš*, *renew*, occurs in the WLC files searched
  (Genesis through 2 Samuel) only here (lemma 2318). *Məlûḵâ*, *kingship*, is the word of *the matter of the
  kingdom* (10:16) and of *the regulations of the kingdom* (10:25). What is renewed, the text does not say: Saul
  was not king before 10:24, so *renew* may mean confirm, reaffirm, or inaugurate again.
- **They made Saul king before Yahweh in Gilgal.** *Wayyamliḵû šām ʾeṯ-šāʾûl lip̄nê YHWH baggilgāl* (15, WLC).
  The subject is *all the people*. In Judges, the men of Shechem *made Abimelech king* with the same verb
  (Judges 9:6, WLC); this time the king is made *before Yahweh*.
- **Peace offerings.** *Zəḇāḥîm šəlāmîm lip̄nê YHWH* (15, WLC). At 10:8 Samuel told Saul to go down to Gilgal and
  wait seven days for him *to offer burnt offerings, and to sacrifice sacrifices of peace offerings* (stored WEB).
  This visit to Gilgal has peace offerings and no mention of waiting or of burnt offerings, and the text does not
  connect the two. The seven days are counted at 13:8 (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Gilgal]].
- **Rejoiced greatly.** *Wayyiśmaḥ šām šāʾûl wəḵol-ʾanšê yiśrāʾēl ʿaḏ-məʾōḏ* (15, WLC). The joy of Jabesh (9) has
  become the joy of the whole nation. Samuel is not named in it. The next chapter is his farewell.

**How it connects.** The pericope answers [[Saul Chosen by Lot at Mizpah]] in its own word, *save*, and closes
the accession that began in [[Israel Asks for a King]]. It reverses the end of Judges at three points: the cut
pieces sent through Israel ([[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]]), the muster *as one man*
([[The War Against Benjamin]]), and the city of Jabesh Gilead ([[Wives for Benjamin]]). It sets Saul among the
judges by the Spirit's verb and by Gideon's tactics, and it sends the reader on to Gilgal, where Saul's kingdom
will next be tested (13:8–14), and to Jabesh's own last act of loyalty, the burial of Saul's body (31:11–13,
stored WEB; not yet ingested).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 11:1–15 as planned; 12:1 begins Samuel's speech. Nahash gets a page
qualified as [[Nahash (king of Ammon)]], because 2 Samuel 17:25 names another Nahash, the father of Abigail, and
the Ammonite of 2 Samuel 10 and 17:27 may or may not be this one. The elders, the men, and the messengers of
Jabesh get no pages. One new connection, [[The Reproach of Israel - Joshua 5.9, 1 Samuel 11.2, and 17.26]], traces
*ḥerpâ* from Gilgal to Goliath. The three existing connections on Jabesh, the cut pieces, and the Spirit's rush,
which had paraphrased or quoted this chapter in advance, are updated now that it is ingested.

**Unresolved.** Why Saul was made king a third time, and what *renew* means. Whether the men of 11:12 are those of
10:27. To whom the men of Jabesh speak in 11:10. How 12:12 relates to chapter 8. What the separate count of Judah
signifies, and how large the numbers are. What Nahash's *kindness* to David (2 Samuel 10:2) has to do with this
chapter, if anything.

31:11–13. Comparisons are from stored WEB Genesis 3:1; 31:42; Exodus 14:24; 15:16; Numbers 16:14; Deuteronomy
2:25; 23:3–6; Joshua 5:9; Judges 6:11; 7:16, 19; 9:43; 16:21; 19:3, 10, 29; 21:5. 2 Samuel 10:1–2 and 17:25–27
were checked at bible-api.com (WEB), 2026-11-18, and are not stored. Hebrew is from a fresh download of the
