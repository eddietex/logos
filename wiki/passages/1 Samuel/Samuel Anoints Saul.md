# Samuel Anoints Saul

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 9:26–10:16

## Summary

> **9:26** They arose early; and about daybreak, Samuel called to Saul on the housetop, saying, "Get up, that I may
> send you away." Saul arose, and they both went outside, he and Samuel, together.
> **27** As they were going down at the end of the city, Samuel said to Saul, "Tell the servant to go on ahead of
> us." He went ahead, then Samuel said, "But stand still first, that I may cause you to hear God's message."
> **10:1** Then Samuel took the vial of oil, and poured it on his head, and kissed him, and said, "Hasn't Yahweh
> anointed you to be prince over his inheritance?
> **2** When you have departed from me today, then you will find two men by Rachel's tomb, on the border of
> Benjamin at Zelzah. They will tell you, 'The donkeys which you went to look for have been found; and behold, your
> father has stopped caring about the donkeys, and is anxious for you, saying, "What shall I do for my son?"'
> **3** "Then you will go on forward from there, and you will come to the oak of Tabor. Three men will meet you
> there going up to God to Bethel, one carrying three young goats, and another carrying three loaves of bread, and
> another carrying a bottle of wine.
> **4** They will greet you, and give you two loaves of bread, which you shall receive from their hand.
> **5** "After that you will come to the hill of God, where the garrison of the Philistines is; and it will happen,
> when you have come there to the city, that you will meet a band of prophets coming down from the high place with
> a lute, a tambourine, a pipe, and a harp before them; and they will be prophesying.
> **6** Then Yahweh's Spirit will come mightily on you, and you will prophesy with them, and will be turned into
> another man.
> **7** Let it be, when these signs have come to you, that you do what is appropriate for the occasion; for God is
> with you.
> **8** "Go down ahead of me to Gilgal; and behold, I will come down to you, to offer burnt offerings, and to
> sacrifice sacrifices of peace offerings. Wait seven days, until I come to you, and show you what you are to do."
> **9** It was so, that when he had turned his back to go from Samuel, God gave him another heart; and all those
> signs happened that day.
> **10** When they came there to the hill, behold, a band of prophets met him; and the Spirit of God came mightily
> on him, and he prophesied among them.
> **11** When all who knew him before saw that, behold, he prophesied with the prophets, then the people said to
> one another, "What is this that has come to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?"
> **12** One of the same place answered, "Who is their father?" Therefore it became a proverb, "Is Saul also among
> the prophets?"
> **13** When he had finished prophesying, he came to the high place.
> **14** Saul's uncle said to him and to his servant, "Where did you go?" He said, "To seek the donkeys. When we
> saw that they were not found, we came to Samuel."
> **15** Saul's uncle said, "Please tell me what Samuel said to you."
> **16** Saul said to his uncle, "He told us plainly that the donkeys were found." But concerning the matter of the
> kingdom, of which Samuel spoke, he didn't tell him.

At daybreak [[Samuel]] calls [[Saul]] down from the housetop to send him on his way, and walks out with him to the
edge of the city (9:26). There he has Saul send [[Saul's Servant (of the lost donkeys)]] on ahead, and tells Saul to
stand still and hear *God's message* (27). Then he takes *the vial of oil*, pours it on Saul's head, kisses him, and
asks, *Hasn't Yahweh anointed you to be prince over his inheritance?* (10:1).

Samuel then gives him three signs, in order. At [[Rachel's Tomb]], on Benjamin's border at [[Zelzah]], two men will
tell him the donkeys are found and his father is now worried about him instead (2). At [[The Oak of Tabor]], three
men going up *to God to Bethel*, carrying three young goats, three loaves, and a skin of wine, will greet him and
give him two of the loaves, and he is to take them (3–4). At [[The Hill of God]], *where the garrison of the
Philistines is*, he will meet [[Band of Prophets (of Samuel's time)]] coming down from the high place with lute,
tambourine, pipe, and harp, prophesying. *Yahweh's Spirit will come mightily on you, and you will prophesy with
them, and will be turned into another man* (5–6). When the signs come, he is to *do what is appropriate for the
occasion; for God is with you* (7). Then a separate instruction: go down ahead of Samuel to [[Gilgal]] and wait
seven days for him to come and offer burnt offerings and peace offerings and *show you what you are to do* (8).

As Saul turns to go, *God gave him another heart*, and all the signs happen that day (9). The narrative tells only
the third. At the hill the prophets meet him, *the Spirit of God came mightily on him, and he prophesied among
them* (10). Those who knew him before ask, *What is this that has come to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the
prophets?*, and a man of the place answers, *Who is their father?*; the question becomes a proverb (11–12). When he
finishes he comes to the high place (13). [[Saul's Uncle]] asks him and the servant where they went. Saul says they
looked for the donkeys and, not finding them, went to Samuel. Pressed for what Samuel said, he answers that Samuel
told them the donkeys were found, *But concerning the matter of the kingdom, of which Samuel spoke, he didn't tell
him* (14–16).

**What is going on.** Chapter 9 brought Saul to Samuel without his knowing why; this pericope tells him. Everything
in it is done in private. The servant is sent on ahead before the oil comes out; the anointing is at the edge of a
city at dawn; and the pericope ends with Saul keeping the kingdom from his own family. The people asked for a king
*like all the nations* (8:5), but the first anointing has no witness except Samuel. The public choosing by lot at
Mizpah follows in the next pericope.

The signs answer the doubt Saul voiced at 9:21. He calls himself the least, and Samuel gives him a sequence of
meetings he can check: two men with news, three men with gifts, and a band of prophets with music. The first sign
closes the errand he started on, since the donkeys are found and his father is now worried about him. The second
gives him bread on the road, after his bread had run out (9:7). The third is the change itself: the Spirit, prophecy,
and *another man*. The narrator reports that God gave him another heart *when he had turned his back to go from
Samuel*, before any of the signs came. So the change is placed in God's act, and the signs only confirm it.

Two instructions stand side by side and are not reconciled. Verse 7 gives Saul freedom: *do what is appropriate
for the occasion*, literally *do for yourself what your hand finds*. Verse 8 binds him: go to Gilgal and wait seven
days for Samuel. The first sounds like a judge's commission, the second like a prophet's control over a king.
1 Samuel 13:8 will pick up the second: *He stayed seven days, according to the time set by Samuel; but Samuel
didn't come to Gilgal* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). Between the two verses, 11:14–15 takes the people to
Gilgal to make Saul king there (stored WEB; not yet ingested). How 10:8 relates to either Gilgal scene the text
does not say, and the wiki does not decide.

**What is notable.**

- **I may cause you to hear God's message.** *Wəʾašmîʿăḵā ʾeṯ-dəḇar ʾĕlōhîm* (9:27, WLC). Samuel, who was
  called by a word in the night (3:10–11), now *makes Saul hear* a word. *Stand still first*, *ʿămōḏ kayyôm*,
  literally *stand now*, is the instruction before a revelation.
- **The vial of oil.** *Pak haššemen* (10:1, WLC). The noun *pak* occurs only here in the WLC files searched
  (lemma 6378). David's anointing uses *the horn of oil*, *qeren haššemen* (16:13, stored WEB; WLC); the flask of
  oil returns at the anointing of Jehu (2 Kings 9:1, 3; cited by reference only, not stored and not checked here).
  See [[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].
- **And kissed him.** *Wayyiššāqēhû* (10:1, WLC). In the files searched the kiss is mostly a family greeting or
  farewell (Genesis 29:11; 33:4; Exodus 4:27; 18:7; Ruth 1:9, 14, WLC lemma 5401). Whether Samuel's kiss is
  affection, homage, or part of a rite the text does not say.
- **Prince over his inheritance.** *Hălôʾ kî-məšāḥăḵā YHWH ʿal-naḥălāṯô lənāḡîḏ* (10:1, WLC). Yahweh said
  *prince over my people Israel* (9:16); Samuel says *prince over his inheritance*, the word used of Israel as
  Yahweh's portion (Deuteronomy 32:9, *Jacob is the lot of his inheritance*, stored WEB; *ḥeḇel naḥălāṯô*, WLC,
  with the *ḥeḇel* that names the band of prophets at 10:5). *Nāḡîḏ* again, not
  *meleḵ*. The question form, *Hasn't Yahweh anointed you…?*, expects yes and gives Saul no chance to answer. The
  Septuagint is commonly reported to have a much longer verse here, with an explicit first sign; that is recalled,
  not checked, and no Greek text was consulted.
- **Rachel's tomb, on the border of Benjamin at Zelzah.** *ʿIm-qəḇurat rāḥēl biḡḇûl binyāmin bəṣelṣaḥ* (10:2,
  WLC). Genesis puts the grave *on the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem)* (35:19), in Judah, south of
  Jerusalem; this verse puts it on Benjamin's border, north. The disagreement is set out, not resolved, in
  [[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]]. *Ṣelṣaḥ* occurs only here in the WLC files searched
  (lemma 6766). The first sign is given at the grave of Benjamin's mother, to the man from *the smallest of the
  tribes*.
- **Your father has stopped caring about the donkeys.** *Nāṭaš ʾāḇîḵā ʾeṯ-diḇrê hāʾăṯōnôṯ wəḏāʾaḡ lāḵem* (10:2,
  WLC). Saul's first words in the book were the fear that his father would *stop caring about the donkeys, and
  be anxious for us* (9:5). The verb for *stop caring* differs (*ḥāḏal* at 9:5, *nāṭaš* here), but *dāʾaḡ*,
  *be anxious*, is repeated. The fear has come true, and the donkeys were never the point.
- **Going up to God to Bethel.** *ʿÔlîm ʾel-hāʾĕlōhîm bêṯ-ʾēl* (10:3, WLC). The three men are pilgrims with the
  makings of an offering: three kids, three loaves, a skin of wine. Saul is to take two loaves *from their hand*
  (4). Bread brought for God is given to the anointed man. The text does not comment on it.
- **They will greet you.** *Wəšāʾălû ləḵā ləšālôm* (10:4, WLC), literally *they will ask you of peace*. The verb is
  *šāʾal*, the root of Saul's name. See [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].
- **A skin of wine; a lute.** *Nēḇel yayin* (10:3) and *nēḇel* among the instruments (10:5) are spelled alike; the
  WLC data gives them one lemma number with different senses (5035 a, b). The wiki notes the repeated word and does
  not claim a pun.
- **The hill of God, where the garrison of the Philistines is.** *Giḇʿaṯ hāʾĕlōhîm ʾăšer-šām nəṣiḇê pəlištîm* (10:5,
  WLC). The phrase *giḇʿaṯ hāʾĕlōhîm* occurs only here in the files searched (lemma 1389, a common noun *hill*,
  not the place-name lemma 1390 used for Gibeah at 10:26). Whether it is Gibeah, Geba, or another height the text
  does not say; see [[The Hill of God]]. *Nəṣîḇ* (lemma 5333) is the word for Lot's wife's *pillar of salt*
  (Genesis 19:26, WLC). Here, at 13:3–4, and at 2 Samuel 8:6, 14 it means a garrison or post, or possibly an
  officer; translations differ. The new prince is anointed to save Israel from the Philistines (9:16), and his
  first sign is fulfilled under their garrison. This is a second witness, after 9:16, against the summary at
  7:13; see [[The Philistines]].
- **A band of prophets.** *Ḥeḇel nəḇîʾîm* (10:5, 10, WLC). *Ḥeḇel* elsewhere in the files searched is a cord or a
  measured region (Deuteronomy 3:4; 32:9; Joshua 2:15; lemma 2256); its use for a group of people is found only
  here. At 19:20 the group is *lahăqaṯ hannəḇîʾîm*, *the company of the prophets*, with Samuel *standing as head
  over them* (stored WEB; WLC; not yet ingested). This is the first group of prophets in the books stored so far.
  They come *down from the high place*, the *bāmâ* of 9:12–25, with music. See
  [[Band of Prophets (of Samuel's time)]] and [[Prophecy and Vision]].
- **Prophesying.** *Mitnabbəʾîm* (10:5), *wəhitnabbîṯā* (6), *wayyiṯnabbēʾ* (10), *nibbāʾ* (11) (WLC). Outside
  1 Samuel, the verb *nāḇāʾ* (lemma 5012) occurs in the files searched only at Numbers 11:25–27, where the
  Spirit rests on the seventy elders and on Eldad and Medad *and they prophesied*. In both places the Spirit comes
  and the prophesying is something people can see. Neither text reports anything that was said. See
  [[Would That All Were Prophets - Numbers 11, Joel 2, and Acts 2]].
- **Yahweh's Spirit will come mightily on you.** *Wəṣāləḥâ ʿālêḵā rûaḥ YHWH* (10:6), *wattiṣlaḥ ʿālāyw rûaḥ
  ʾĕlōhîm* (10:10) (WLC). The verb is Samson's (Judges 14:6, 19; 15:14). There it was followed by feats of
  strength; here it is followed by prophecy. See
  [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]] and [[The Spirit of God]].
- **Turned into another man; another heart.** *Wənehpaḵtā ləʾîš ʾaḥēr* (6) and *wayyahăp̄āḵ-lô ʾĕlōhîm lēḇ
  ʾaḥēr* (9) (WLC). Both use *hāp̄aḵ*, *turn, overturn*. The promise is passive, and the fulfilment has God as
  subject. What *another heart* means for Saul's later story the text does not explain. At 9:19 Samuel promised to
  tell him *all that is in your heart*.
- **Do what is appropriate for the occasion.** *ʿĂśēh ləḵā ʾăšer timṣāʾ yāḏeḵā* (10:7, WLC), *do for yourself what
  your hand finds*. Zebul's advice to Abimelech uses the same idiom, *kaʾăšer timṣāʾ yāḏeḵā* (Judges 9:33, WLC; WEB
  *as you shall find occasion*). *For God is with you*, *kî hāʾĕlōhîm ʿimmāḵ*, echoes the angel's word to Gideon,
  *Yahweh is with you* (Judges 6:12, stored WEB).
- **Wait seven days.** *Šiḇʿaṯ yāmîm tôḥēl* (10:8, WLC). See above and 13:8.
- **All those signs happened that day.** *Wayyāḇōʾû kol-hāʾōṯôṯ hāʾēlleh bayyôm hahûʾ* (10:9, WLC). Deuteronomy's
  test of a prophet is whether *the thing* he speaks *happens* (18:22, stored WEB). Samuel passes it, as 3:19 and
  9:6 said he would. See [[Signs and Wonders]] and [[Two Tests of a Prophet - Deuteronomy 13.1-5 and 18.20-22]].
- **Is Saul also among the prophets?** *Hăḡam šāʾûl bannəḇîʾîm* (10:11, 12, WLC). The text gives this proverb two
  origins, here and at Naioth in Ramah (19:24, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]].
- **Who is their father?** *Ûmî ʾăḇîhem* (10:12, WLC). The question is not explained. It may compare Saul,
  *the son of Kish*, with prophets who have no known father, or ask who leads the band, since *father* is later a
  title for a prophet's master. Both readings are proposals, recalled and not checked. The wiki does not choose.
- **A proverb.** *Māšāl* (10:12, WLC). Before this, in the files searched, the word is used of Balaam's oracles
  (Numbers 23:7, 18; 24:3, 15, 20, 21, 23) and of Israel becoming *a byword* (Deuteronomy 28:37) (lemma 4912).
  This is the first time Scripture tells how a saying became a proverb. See [[Wisdom]].
- **Saul's uncle.** *Dôḏ šāʾûl* (10:14, WLC). He is not named. 14:50 calls Abner *the son of Ner, Saul's uncle*
  (stored WEB; not yet ingested), and it is unclear whether *uncle* there goes with Ner or Abner. Identifying this
  uncle with Ner is a common proposal, not made by the text. See [[Saul's Uncle]].
- **The matter of the kingdom.** *Dəḇar hamməlûḵâ* (10:16, WLC). *Məlûḵâ*, *kingship*, appears here for the first
  time in the files searched (lemma 4410; next at 10:25; 11:14). Samuel never said *king* to Saul. The narrator
  does, at the point where Saul keeps silent. Whether the silence is modesty, prudence, or something else the text
  does not say, and at the next public scene Saul is found hiding (10:22, stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**How it connects.** The anointing that [[Saul Comes to the Seer]] prepared now happens, and the private story of
the donkeys ends. The Spirit's coming joins Saul to Samson
([[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]]) and his prophesying to the elders of Numbers 11
([[Would That All Were Prophets - Numbers 11, Joel 2, and Acts 2]]). The first sign sends him past the disputed
grave of Benjamin's mother ([[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]]). The *vial of oil* begins the
anointings that Hannah's *his anointed* (2:10) looked toward
([[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]]). The proverb at 10:11–12 has a second origin story at
19:24 ([[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]]). The order to wait seven days at Gilgal
sets up the first break between Samuel and Saul (13:8–14). The next pericope makes public what this one keeps
secret.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 9:26–10:16 as planned. 10:17 moves to Mizpah and the lot, which belongs to
the next box. New place pages are [[Rachel's Tomb]], [[Zelzah]], [[The Oak of Tabor]] (kept apart from [[Tabor]],
the mountain in Issachar, since the text does not connect them), and [[The Hill of God]] (kept apart from
[[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], since the text uses the common noun and does not name the town). New person pages are
[[Saul's Uncle]] (unnamed and not identified with Ner) and [[Band of Prophets (of Samuel's time)]], since the group
appears again at 19:20. The two men at the tomb and the three pilgrims get no pages. The proverb is filed under
[[Wisdom]], not [[Parables]], because the Parables page keeps to told stories and sends sayings to Wisdom. New connection:
[[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]].

**Unresolved.** How *do what your hand finds* (10:7) relates to *wait seven days* (10:8), and how 10:8 relates to
the Gilgal scenes of 11:14–15 and 13:8. Where Rachel's tomb was (Genesis 35:19; 1 Samuel 10:2). Where the hill
of God was. What *Who is their father?* means. Why Saul keeps the kingdom secret. What *another heart* amounts to.
How the Philistine garrison fits 7:13. None of these is settled here.
