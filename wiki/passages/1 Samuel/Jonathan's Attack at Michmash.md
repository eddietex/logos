# Jonathan's Attack at Michmash

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 14:1–23

## Summary

> **1** Now it fell on a day, that Jonathan the son of Saul said to the young man who bore his armor, "Come, and let
> us go over to the Philistines' garrison, that is on the other side." But he didn't tell his father.
> **2** Saul stayed in the uttermost part of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree which is in Migron: and the people who
> were with him were about six hundred men;
> **3** including Ahijah, the son of Ahitub, Ichabod's brother, the son of Phinehas, the son of Eli, the priest of
> Yahweh in Shiloh, wearing an ephod. The people didn't know that Jonathan was gone.
> **4** Between the passes, by which Jonathan sought to go over to the Philistines' garrison, there was a rocky crag
> on the one side, and a rocky crag on the other side: and the name of the one was Bozez, and the name of the other
> Seneh.
> **5** The one crag rose up on the north in front of Michmash, and the other on the south in front of Geba.
> **6** Jonathan said to the young man who bore his armor, "Come, let us go over to the garrison of these
> uncircumcised. It may be that Yahweh will work for us; for there is no restraint on Yahweh to save by many or by
> few."
> **7** His armor bearer said to him, "Do all that is in your heart. Turn and, behold, I am with you according to
> your heart."
> **8** Then Jonathan said, "Behold, we will pass over to the men, and we will reveal ourselves to them.
> **9** If they say thus to us, 'Wait until we come to you!' then we will stand still in our place, and will not go
> up to them.
> **10** But if they say this, 'Come up to us!' then we will go up; for Yahweh has delivered them into our hand. This
> shall be the sign to us."
> **11** Both of them revealed themselves to the garrison of the Philistines: and the Philistines said, "Behold, the
> Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they had hidden themselves!"
> **12** The men of the garrison answered Jonathan and his armor bearer, and said, "Come up to us, and we will show
> you something!" Jonathan said to his armor bearer, "Come up after me; for Yahweh has delivered them into the hand
> of Israel."
> **13** Jonathan climbed up on his hands and on his feet, and his armor bearer after him: and they fell before
> Jonathan; and his armor bearer killed them after him.
> **14** That first slaughter, which Jonathan and his armor bearer made, was about twenty men, within as it were half
> a furrow's length in an acre of land.
> **15** There was a trembling in the camp, in the field, and among all the people; the garrison, and the raiders,
> also trembled; and the earth quaked, so there was an exceedingly great trembling.
> **16** The watchmen of Saul in Gibeah of Benjamin looked; and behold, the multitude melted away and scattered.
> **17** Then Saul said to the people who were with him, "Count now, and see who is missing from us." When they had
> counted, behold, Jonathan and his armor bearer were not there.
> **18** Saul said to Ahijah, "Bring God's ark here." For God's ark was with the children of Israel at that time.
> **19** While Saul talked to the priest, the tumult that was in the camp of the Philistines went on and increased;
> and Saul said to the priest, "Withdraw your hand!"
> **20** Saul and all the people who were with him were gathered together, and came to the battle; and behold, they
> were all striking each other with their swords in very great confusion.
> **21** Now the Hebrews who were with the Philistines before, and who went up with them into the camp, from all
> around, even they also turned to be with the Israelites who were with Saul and Jonathan.
> **22** Likewise all the men of Israel who had hidden themselves in the hill country of Ephraim, when they heard that
> the Philistines fled, even they also followed hard after them in the battle.
> **23** So Yahweh saved Israel that day; and the battle passed over by Beth Aven.

[[Jonathan (son of Saul)]] says to [[Jonathan's Armor Bearer]], *Come, and let us go over to the Philistines'
garrison*, and does not tell his father (1). [[Saul]] is sitting at the edge of [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] under the
pomegranate tree in [[Migron]] with about six hundred men (2). With him is the priest [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]],
wearing the ephod, a great-grandson of [[Eli]] through [[Phinehas (son of Eli)]] and
[[Ahitub (son of Phinehas)]], the brother of [[Ichabod]] (3). Nobody knows that Jonathan has gone.

The narrator stops to describe the ground. Between the passes stand two crags, [[Bozez]] facing [[Michmash]] on
the north and [[Seneh]] facing [[Geba]] on the south (4–5). Jonathan speaks a second time: *the garrison of these
uncircumcised*, and *It may be that Yahweh will work for us; for there is no restraint on Yahweh to save by many or
by few* (6). The armour-bearer answers, *I am with you according to your heart* (7). Jonathan sets a sign: they
will show themselves, and if the garrison says *Wait*, they stay; if it says *Come up*, they go, *for Yahweh has
delivered them into our hand* (8–10).

The Philistines see them and mock, *Behold, the Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they had hidden
themselves!*, and call down *Come up to us, and we will show you something!* (11–12). That is the sign. Jonathan
climbs *on his hands and on his feet*, the Philistines fall before him, and the armour-bearer kills them behind him:
about twenty men in half a furrow's length (13–14). Then a trembling runs through the camp, the field, the garrison,
and the raiders; *the earth quaked*, and it becomes *an exceedingly great trembling* (15).

Saul's watchmen at Gibeah see the multitude melting away (16). Saul has the people counted and finds Jonathan and
the armour-bearer missing (17). He tells Ahijah to bring *God's ark* (18), but while he is talking to the priest the
tumult grows, and he breaks off: *Withdraw your hand!* (19). Saul's men come to the battle and find the Philistines
*striking each other with their swords in very great confusion* (20). The Hebrews who had been with the Philistines
change sides (21), the men hiding in [[The Hill Country of Ephraim]] join the pursuit (22), and the narrator gives
the verdict: *So Yahweh saved Israel that day*. The battle passes over by [[Beth Aven]] (23).

**Where it happens.** On the Benjamin plateau, across the pass where the previous pericope left the Philistine
garrison (13:23). Israel is at Gibeah (2, 16), the crags face Michmash and Geba (5), the pursuit goes by Beth Aven
(23) and, in the next pericope, *from Michmash to Aijalon* (14:31, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The text gives two
names for the crags and none for the climb. Migron is named again in Isaiah's picture of an army marching on
Jerusalem through *Aiath … Migron … Michmash … the pass … Geba … Gibeah of Saul* (Isaiah 10:28–29, checked at
bible-api.com, WEB, not stored). The site of the pass is commonly identified with a modern wadi between the two
villages (recalled, not checked); the wiki does not use it.

**What is going on.** Chapter 13 ended with Israel unarmed, hiding, and down to six hundred men, and with Saul's
kingdom declared forfeit at Gilgal. Chapter 14 begins with the son, not the father. The narrator sets the two side by
side in verses 1–3: Jonathan goes; Saul *sits*. Saul's company includes the priest of a rejected house, the last
grandson of Eli named, *wearing an ephod*. Jonathan's company is one young man carrying his weapons.

The pericope turns on what Jonathan says in verse 6. He does not claim a promise. *ʾÛlay*, *It may be* (WLC), is
the word of someone who does not know. What he does claim is a truth about Yahweh: nothing restrains him *to save
by many or by few*. Then he submits the venture to a test whose terms he sets but whose outcome he does not
control (8–10). The test is the opposite of a safe one. *Come up to us* is the harder answer, a climb on hands and
feet into a garrison, and Jonathan takes that one as Yahweh's yes.

The narrator then shows Yahweh answer. The first slaughter is twenty men (14). The rest is not Jonathan's doing.
There is *ḥărāḏâ*, *trembling*, in the camp; *the earth quaked*; and the last words of verse 15 are *ḥerdaṯ
ʾĕlōhîm* (WLC), literally *a trembling of God*, which the WEB renders *an exceedingly great trembling*. The
Philistines turn their swords on one another (20). The pericope's last sentence has no human subject: *So Yahweh
saved Israel that day* (23). Jonathan's *It may be that Yahweh will work for us* (6) is answered in the narrator's
voice.

Saul's part runs the other way. He learns of the battle from his watchmen, and his first act is a roll call (17).
His second is to call for the ark, to inquire of God (18). His third is to stop the priest in the middle: *Withdraw
your hand* (19). He asks for guidance and does not wait for it, as at Gilgal he did not wait for Samuel (13:8–12).
In the next pericope he swears an oath that nearly kills Jonathan (14:24–45, stored WEB; not yet ingested).

The victory gathers in Israel's scattered people. *The Hebrews* who had been in the Philistine camp change sides
(21), and the men who had hidden in caves and pits (13:6) come out of the hill country of Ephraim to pursue (22).
The Philistines' own taunt, *the Hebrews are coming out of the holes* (11), comes true in a sense they did not
intend.

**What is notable.**

- **Two spellings of one name.** The WLC writes *yônāṯān* in verses 1, 3, 4, 12–14, 17, 21 and the long form
  *yəhônāṯān* in verses 6 and 8, the two verses where Jonathan speaks of Yahweh's saving and of the sign (morphhb
  lemmas 3129 and 3083). The long form spells out the divine name. Whether the alternation means anything is not
  settled here; the observation is only of the spelling.
- **He didn't tell his father.** *Ûlə'āḇîw lōʾ higgîḏ* (1, WLC). Saul had kept back from his uncle *the matter of the
  kingdom* with the same verb (10:16, *lōʾ higgîḏ*, WLC). The son now keeps a matter from the father.
- **Sitting under a tree.** *Yôšēḇ … taḥaṯ hārimmôn* (2, WLC). Later Saul is *sitting in Gibeah, under the tamarisk
  tree in Ramah, with his spear in his hand* (22:6, stored WEB; not yet ingested). Deborah *lived under Deborah's
  palm tree* (Judges 4:5, stored WEB) when she judged. The posture of the judge is now the posture of a king who is
  not moving.
- **Ahijah wearing an ephod.** The priest's lineage is given back to *Eli, the priest of Yahweh in Shiloh* (3), the
  house to which the man of God said *I will raise up a faithful priest* in its place (2:35, stored WEB). Ichabod,
  born the day the ark was taken, is named here only as a point in the genealogy. Ahijah's relation to *Ahimelech,
  the son of Ahitub*, the priest at Nob (22:9–20, stored WEB; not yet ingested), is commonly debated: some take them
  as brothers, others as one man under two names (recalled, not checked). The wiki keeps them apart until 1 Samuel
  21–22 is ingested.
- **The passes.** *Hammaʿbərôṯ* (4, WLC), the plural of *maʿăḇār*; the same form is *the fords* of the Jordan in the
  Ehud and Jephthah stories (Judges 3:28; 12:5–6) and in Joshua 2:7 (stored WEB; WLC, morphhb lemma 4569 b).
  Jonathan's verb throughout is *ʿāḇar*, *go over* (1, 4, 6, 8), the verb the Hebrew puns on with *ʿiḇrîm*,
  *Hebrews* (11, 21), as it did in 13:7.
- **Bozez and Seneh.** *Bôṣēṣ* and *Senneh* (4, WLC). Both names occur only here in the WLC files searched (morphhb
  lemmas 949 and 5573). They are commonly glossed *shining* and *thorny*, and *Senneh* is often compared with
  *sᵊneh*, the bush of Exodus 3:2 (recalled, not checked; the vowels of the two words differ in the WLC). See
  [[Bozez]] and [[Seneh]].
- **A rocky crag.** The *crag* of verse 5 is *māṣûq* (WLC). morphhb gives it the lemma of *məṣuqê ʾereṣ*, *the
  pillars of the earth*, in Hannah's song (2:8; lemma 4690). In the WLC files searched the word occurs only at those
  two places. Hannah had sung that Yahweh *raises up the poor out of the dust* (2:8, stored WEB).
- **These uncircumcised.** *Hāʿărēlîm hāʾēlleh* (6, WLC). The word is Samson's for the Philistines (Judges 14:3;
  15:18) and will be David's of Goliath (17:26, 36) and Saul's, at the end, of the men he fears will abuse him
  (31:4) (stored WEB; WLC). In the WLC files searched (Genesis–2 Samuel), the article-plus-noun form for the
  Philistines first appears in Judges 14:3.
- **No restraint.** *ʾÊn layhwh maʿṣôr ləhôšîaʿ bərāḇ ʾô ḇimʿāṭ* (6, WLC). *Maʿṣôr* occurs only here in Genesis–2
  Samuel (morphhb lemma 4622). The thought had been Yahweh's own at Gideon's muster: *The people who are with you are
  too many for me … lest Israel brag against me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me'* (Judges 7:2), and *By the three
  hundred men … I will save you* (7:7) (stored WEB). See
  [[My Hand Has Saved Me - Deuteronomy 8.17 and Judges 7.2]].
- **According to your heart.** *Hinnî ʿimmᵊḵā kilḇāḇeḵā* (7, WLC). The previous pericope but one had Samuel's word
  that Yahweh *has sought for himself a man after his own heart*, *kilḇāḇô* (13:14, stored WEB; WLC). The
  armour-bearer uses the same preposition and noun of Jonathan.
- **The sign.** *Wᵊzeh-lānû hāʾôṯ* (10, WLC). The terms are set in advance, like Abraham's servant's at the well
  (Genesis 24:14, without the word *sign*) and Gideon's requests (Judges 6:17, *ʾôṯ*; 6:36–40) (stored WEB; WLC).
  Unlike Gideon, Jonathan does not ask for reassurance after Yahweh has spoken; he asks before the venture and goes
  on the answer. Samuel had given Saul three signs and told him *do what is appropriate for the occasion; for God is
  with you* (10:7, stored WEB). See [[Signs and Wonders]] and [[The Fleece]].
- **Out of the holes.** *Min-haḥōrîm* (11, WLC; *ḥōr*, morphhb lemma 2356, only here in the files searched). The
  verb is *hiṯḥabbəʾû*, *hidden themselves*, the same form as 13:6, where the people *hid themselves in caves, in
  thickets, in rocks, in tombs, and in pits*, and as 14:22, *the men … who had hidden themselves* (WLC). Saul himself
  was found *hidden* among the baggage (10:22, niphal, WLC).
- **We will show you something.** *Wᵊnôḏîʿâ ʾeṯḵem dāḇār* (12, WLC), literally *we will make you know a thing*. The
  taunt is a threat; Jonathan hears it as an answer.
- **Into our hand … into the hand of Israel.** In verse 10 Jonathan says *bəyāḏēnû*, *into our hand*; in verse 12,
  *bəyaḏ yiśrāʾēl*, *into the hand of Israel* (WLC). The formula is the deliverer's, said by Ehud (Judges 3:28) and
  by Gideon after the dream (7:15) (stored WEB).
- **The trembling.** *Ḥărāḏâ* and *ḥerdaṯ ʾĕlōhîm* (15, WLC), with the verb *ḥārᵊḏû* for the garrison and the
  raiders. In Genesis–2 Samuel the noun occurs only here and at Isaac's discovery, *Isaac trembled violently*,
  *wayyeḥĕraḏ … ḥărāḏâ* (Genesis 27:33) (stored WEB; morphhb lemma 2731). The verb is the people's on Sinai (Exodus
  19:16) and Israel's at Gilgal, *all the people followed him trembling* (1 Samuel 13:7) (stored WEB; WLC). The
  trembling has crossed the pass.
- **The earth quaked.** *Wattirgaz hāʾāreṣ* (15, WLC). The verb *rāḡaz* is in the Song of the Sea: *The peoples have
  heard. They tremble. Pangs have taken hold on the inhabitants of Philistia* (Exodus 15:14, stored WEB; WLC
  *yirgāzûn*).
- **The raiders.** *Hammašḥîṯ* (15, WLC), the noun of 13:17, here among those who tremble. See [[No Smith in Israel]]
  and [[The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night]].
- **Melted away.** *Hehāmôn nāmôḡ* (16, WLC). The verb *mûḡ* occurs in Genesis–2 Samuel at Exodus 15:15, *All the
  inhabitants of Canaan have melted away*, and at Joshua 2:9, 24, Rahab's and the spies' report that *all the
  inhabitants of the land melt away* (stored WEB; morphhb lemma 4127), and then here.
- **Count now.** *Piqḏû-nāʾ* (17, WLC), the verb of 13:15, where Saul *counted the people*. His first response to
  the battle is to count again.
- **God's ark … the ephod.** The Masoretic text has *ʾărôn hāʾĕlōhîm* twice in verse 18 (WLC), and the WEB follows
  it. The ark was last placed at Kiriath Jearim in Abinadab's house for *twenty years* (7:1–2, stored WEB), and
  verse 3 has just said Ahijah was *wearing an ephod*. That the Septuagint reads *ephod* in verse 18 is commonly
  reported (recalled, not checked; no Septuagint consulted). The wiki records both the Hebrew wording and the
  tension; it does not choose. *Withdraw your hand* (19) suits a priest consulting the ephod as well as one handling
  the ark. See the open question on [[1 Samuel]].
- **Withdraw your hand.** *ʾĔsōp̄ yāḏeḵā* (19, WLC). The inquiry is begun and cut off. In the next pericope Saul asks
  again, and *he didn't answer him that day* (14:37, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Every man's sword against his fellow.** *Ḥereḇ ʾîš bərēʿēhû* (20, WLC) is the phrase of Gideon's night, where
  *Yahweh set every man's sword against his fellow* (Judges 7:22, stored WEB; WLC). There the subject is Yahweh;
  here the narrator only reports what Saul's men saw. *Məhûmâ gədôlâ məʾōḏ*, *very great confusion* (20, WLC), is
  the noun of the covenant promise *will confuse them with a great confusion* (Deuteronomy 7:23) and of the ark's
  stay in Gath and Ekron (1 Samuel 5:9, 11) (stored WEB; morphhb lemma 4103). See
  [[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].
- **The Hebrews with the Philistines.** *Kᵊʾeṯmôl šilšôm* (21, WLC), *as before*, literally *as yesterday and the
  day before*. The verse distinguishes *Hebrews* from *Israel*: Hebrews who had been with the Philistines *turned to
  be with Israel* (WLC). Later the Philistine princes will ask of David's men, *What about these Hebrews?* (29:3,
  stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Followed hard.** *Wayyaḏbəqû* (22, WLC), hiphil of *dāḇaq*, *cling*, the verb of Ruth who *clung to* Naomi
  (Ruth 1:14, stored WEB; WLC *dāḇəqâ*, qal).
- **So Yahweh saved Israel that day.** *Wayyôšaʿ YHWH bayyôm hahûʾ ʾeṯ-yiśrāʾēl* (23, WLC) is word for word the
  first half of Exodus 14:30, *Thus Yahweh saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians* (stored WEB; WLC).
  With the trembling of the peoples and their melting away from the Song of the Sea, the narrator tells Michmash in
  the language of the Exodus. See
  [[Yahweh Saved Israel That Day - The Sea and the Pass at Michmash]].
- **The battle passed over.** *Wᵊhammilḥāmâ ʿāḇᵊrâ ʾeṯ-bêṯ ʾāwen* (23, WLC). The last verb is *ʿāḇar* again. Jonathan
  *went over* to the garrison; now the battle *goes over* past Beth Aven.

**How it connects.** The pericope answers [[No Smith in Israel]]. That one ended with two swords in Israel; this one
begins with one of them going over the pass, and ends with the Philistines' swords doing the work. It answers
[[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]] too: there, Saul could not wait, and acted on what he saw (13:11); here, Jonathan
waits for a sign, and Saul again does not wait for one. The commission at Saul's anointing, *He will save my
people out of the hand of the Philistines* (9:16, stored WEB), is fulfilled by Yahweh through Saul's son. The
pattern is Gideon's: a reduced force, a sign, panic in the camp, swords turned inward, and men from the hill
country of Ephraim joining the pursuit (Judges 7:1–8:3, stored WEB). The pericope does not end the day. Saul's oath
has already been sworn (14:24, stored WEB), and the next pericope tells what it costs.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 14:1–23 as planned. Verse 23 ends it: *So Yahweh saved Israel that day* is a
narrator's closing formula, and 14:24 opens the oath with a new statement of the people's distress. Five new
pages. [[Jonathan's Armor Bearer]] gets a page as the second actor of the pericope, though unnamed; he is named by his
role, with his master in the title. [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]] and [[Ahitub (son of Phinehas)]] are qualified from
the start, since a prophet Ahijah of Shiloh and an Ahitub father of Zadok are expected later (recalled, not
checked). The places [[Bozez]], [[Seneh]], and [[Migron]] get pages; the pomegranate tree does not. *The Hebrews*
and the ark get no page, as before. One new connection:
[[Yahweh Saved Israel That Day - The Sea and the Pass at Michmash]].

**Unresolved.** Whether verse 18 originally read *ark* or *ephod*, and how the ark could be with Israel at Gibeah
while 7:2 places it at Kiriath Jearim. Whether Ahijah is Ahimelech of 22:9. The location of Migron relative to
Gibeah, since Isaiah 10:28 lists it north of Michmash, and the location of the pass. The meaning of *Bozez* and
