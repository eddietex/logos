# Michal Helps David Escape

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 19:8–17

## Summary

> **19:8** There was war again. David went out, and fought with the Philistines, and killed them with a great
> slaughter; and they fled before him.
> **9** An evil spirit from Yahweh was on Saul, as he sat in his house with his spear in his hand; and David was
> playing with his hand.
> **10** Saul sought to pin David to the wall with the spear; but he slipped away out of Saul's presence, and he stuck
> the spear into the wall. David fled, and escaped that night.
> **11** Saul sent messengers to David's house, to watch him, and to kill him in the morning. Michal, David's wife,
> told him, saying, "If you don't save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be killed."
> **12** So Michal let David down through the window. He went away, fled, and escaped.
> **13** Michal took the teraphim, and laid it in the bed, and put a pillow of goats' hair at its head, and covered it
> with clothes.
> **14** When Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, "He is sick."
> **15** Saul sent the messengers to see David, saying, "Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may kill him."
> **16** When the messengers came in, behold, the teraphim was in the bed, with the pillow of goats' hair at its head.
> **17** Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me thus, and let my enemy go, so that he is escaped?" Michal
> answered Saul, "He said to me, 'Let me go! Why should I kill you?'"

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 19.)

The oath of 19:6 does not outlast the next battle. *There was war again*, and [[David]] wins *with a great slaughter* (8). Then
the scene of 18:10–11 comes back: *An evil spirit from Yahweh was on Saul, as he sat in his house with his spear in
his hand; and David was playing with his hand* (9). [[Saul]] tries *to pin David to the wall with the spear*; David
*slipped away*, and the spear goes into the wall. *David fled, and escaped that night* (10).

Saul sends messengers to David's own house, *to watch him, and to kill him in the morning* (11). [[Michal]], *David's
wife*, warns him: *If you don't save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be killed.* She lets him down *through the
window*, and *he went away, fled, and escaped* (12). Then she makes a body in the bed: she takes *the teraphim*, lays
it in the bed, puts *a pillow of goats' hair at its head*, and covers it with clothes (13). When the messengers come
to take David, she says *He is sick* (14). Saul sends them back *to see David*: *Bring him up to me in the bed, that I
may kill him* (15). They come in, *and behold, the teraphim was in the bed* (16).

Saul asks his daughter *Why have you deceived me thus, and let my enemy go, so that he is escaped?* She answers with a
second lie, a threat she puts in David's mouth: *He said to me, 'Let me go! Why should I kill you?'* (17).

**Where it happens.** The pericope names no town. Saul *sat in his house* (9); David has a house of his own, to which
the messengers are sent (11). Saul's home is [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] (10:26; 15:34, stored WEB), and the reader may
assume both houses are there, but 19:8–17 does not say so. David's flight ends *to Samuel at Ramah* in the next verse
(19:18, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The battle of 19:8 is unplaced. The wiki gives the pericope no place page and
links no place beyond the existing one.

**What is going on.** This is the second of the four times in 19:1–20:42 that David is kept from Saul, and the second
by one of Saul's own children. Jonathan used words and was listened to (19:1–7). Michal uses a window, an image, a
bed, and two lies, and Saul's question at the end shows he has not been listened to at all. The pericope turns on the
verb *escape*. *Mālaṭ* comes four times in eight verses: David *escaped that night* (10), *If you don't save your
life* (11, the *piel*), *fled, and escaped* (12), and Saul's *so that he is escaped* (17) (WLC). It comes a fifth time
in 19:18. In the Hebrew of 1 Samuel the verb occurs in eleven verses, five of them in 19:10–18 (WLC); the rest belong to
David's fugitive years (20:29; 22:1; 23:13; 27:1) and to Abiathar (22:20) and the Amalekites (30:17).

Saul's part is all sending. *Wayyišlaḥ šāʾûl*, *Saul sent*, opens 11, 14, and 15. Michal's part is the other stem of
the same verb: Saul accuses her, *you let my enemy go*, *wattǝšallǝḥî*, and her lie has David say *Let me go*,
*šallǝḥinî* (17, WLC). Saul sends men in; Michal sends David out. The verb *kill* is shared out the same way. Saul's
messengers are sent *to kill him* (11) and are told *Bring him up … that I may kill him* (15). Michal warns David
*tomorrow you will be killed*, *māḥār ʾattâ mûmāṯ* (11), the *hophal* of the oath of 19:6, *he shall not be put to
death*, *ʾim-yûmāṯ*. Saul's oath said he would not be put to death; his daughter tells him that by morning he will be.
Then she gives the verb to David: *Why should I kill you?* (17) (WLC).

The device is a bed with something in it that is not David. It has a precedent the pericope does not name.
[[Rachel]] took her father's teraphim when [[Jacob]] fled, sat on them in a camel's saddle, and told Laban *I can't
rise up before you; for I'm having my period* (Genesis 31:19, 34–35, stored WEB). Both are daughters of the man in
power. Both use teraphim against their father on behalf of a husband in flight (Rachel's were Laban's; whose Michal's were, the text does not say). Both stop the search
with a lie about a body. In Genesis the teraphim are hidden *under* a woman; here they lie in a bed *as* a man. The
two stories share vocabulary as well as the object. Jacob *fled*, *bāraḥ* (Genesis 31:20–22), and Laban asked *Why
did you flee secretly*, *naḥbēʾṯā liḇrōaḥ*, literally *hide yourself to flee*, and said he would have *sent you
away*, *wāʾăšallēḥăḵā* (31:27). 1 Samuel 19 has David *hide yourself* (2), *fled* (12), and Michal *let … go* (17), in
the same three verbs (WLC). The wiki records the parallel and claims no dependence. See
[[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]].

The window has a precedent of its own. *Wattōreḏ mîḵal ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn* (12, WLC), *Michal let David down
through the window*, is the sentence of Joshua 2:15: [[Rahab]] *let them down by a cord through the window*,
*wattôriḏēm baḥeḇel bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn*. In the Hebrew files checked, the *hiphil* of *yāraḏ* with *window* occurs only in
those two verses and at Joshua 2:18, where the spies name *the window which you used to let us down*. Both women live
in a house that the king's men come to; both send the men out through the window and turn away the pursuit with a
false word. Rahab's words were *Where the men went, I don't know* (Joshua 2:5); Michal's are *He is sick*. See
[[Let Down Through the Window - Rahab and Michal]] and
[[The Midwives and Rahab - Deceiving the King to Save Life]].

Michal will look through a window once more. When the ark comes into the city of David, *Michal the daughter of Saul
looked out through the window*, saw David dancing, *and she despised him in her heart* (2 Samuel 6:16, WLC;
paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB stored). The phrase is the same, *bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn*. The window she saved him through is
the window she watches him from. Her love is stated at 18:20, 28; it is not stated again after this night.

**What is notable.**

- **War again.** *Wattōsep̄ hammilḥāmâ lihyôṯ* (8, WLC), *the war added to be*. The narrator does not say who began
  it. David *killed them with a great slaughter*, *makkâ ḡǝḏôlâ*. In the Hebrew files checked the phrase is
  Joshua's at Gibeon (Joshua 10:10, 20), Jephthah's (Judges 11:33), Samson's (15:8), Israel's defeat at Ebenezer
  (4:10), Beth Shemesh (6:19), here, and David's at Keilah (23:5, not yet ingested) (WLC). The success comes before
  the spear, as at 18:5–11 and 18:27–29.
- **An evil spirit of Yahweh.** *Rûaḥ YHWH rāʿâ* (9, WLC), *Yahweh's evil spirit*, in a construct with the divine name.
  The other occurrences say *an evil spirit from Yahweh*, *mēʾēṯ YHWH* (16:14), or use *ʾĕlōhîm* (16:15, 16, 23; 18:10).
  In the Hebrew files checked, only here and at 16:14 do *Yahweh*, *evil*, and *spirit* stand in one verse; at 16:14
  the same verse says that *Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul*. WEB renders both as *from Yahweh*. See
  [[An Evil Spirit from God - Abimelech, Saul, and Ahab's Prophets]] and [[The Spirit of God]].
- **His spear in his hand.** Saul *sat in his house*, *yôšēḇ*, *with his spear in his hand* (9). At 22:6 he will sit
  under the tamarisk at Gibeah *with his spear in his hand* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). In 1 Samuel the word
  *ḥănîṯ* belongs first to the Israelites who have none (13:19, 22) and to Goliath (17:7, 45, 47), and from 18:10 on it
  belongs to Saul (WLC). See [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].
- **To the wall.** *Lǝhakkôṯ baḥănîṯ bǝḏāwiḏ ûḇaqqîr* (10, WLC), *to strike with the spear at David and at the wall*,
  is Saul's own sentence from 18:11, *ʾakkeh ḇǝḏāwiḏ ûḇaqqîr*, *I will pin David even to the wall* (WLC). At 18:11
  the narrator said *David escaped from his presence twice*; this is the third throw. The fourth is at Jonathan,
  *Saul cast his spear at him to strike him* (20:33, stored WEB; not yet ingested). *Qîr*, *wall*, comes twice in
  19:10, and the spear stays in it.
- **He slipped away.** *Wayyip̄ṭar mippǝnê šāʾûl* (10, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked the verb *pāṭar* (6362)
  occurs only here. The word for David's escape is used for nothing else in those books.
- **Fled.** *Bāraḥ* (10, 12) is used of David here for the first time. It returns at 19:18; 20:1; 21:10; 27:4 (stored
  WEB; 21:11 WLC). In the files checked it is the verb of Hagar from Sarai (Genesis 16:6, 8), of Jacob from Esau and
  from Laban (27:43; 31:20–22, 27; 35:1, 7), and of Moses: *Pharaoh … sought to kill Moses. But Moses fled* (Exodus
  2:15). Saul *sought*, *wayǝḇaqqēš*, *to pin David* (10); Pharaoh *sought*, *wayǝḇaqqēš*, *to kill Moses* (WLC). See
  [[Moses Flees to Midian]].
- **That night … in the morning.** *Ballaylâ hûʾ* (10), *that night*; *babbōqer* (11), *in the morning*;
  *hallaylâ … māḥār* (11), *tonight … tomorrow* (WLC). The time words of Jonathan's warning, *in the morning* (19:2),
  come back, and this time the danger is at night, in David's own house.
- **To watch him.** *Lǝšāmrô* (11, WLC), *to guard him*. *Šāmar* is the verb of keeping and guarding; the messengers
  keep watch on David's door to kill him. At 26:15–16 David will mock Abner because he *has not kept watch over* his
  lord *Yahweh's anointed* (stored WEB; not yet ingested), the same verb.
- **David's wife.** *Mîḵal ʾištô* (11, WLC). She is called *David's wife* here and in the narrator's voice at 25:44,
  when Saul gives her to Palti (stored WEB; not yet ingested). In 19:11–17 David says nothing. He is told what to do,
  let down, and gone; the only words given him are the false ones Michal invents.
- **She told him.** *Wattaggēḏ lǝḏāwiḏ* (11, WLC), the *hiphil* of *nāḡaḏ* that Jonathan used three times in 19:2–7.
  Both of Saul's children *tell* David his danger.
- **Through the window.** *Bǝʿaḏ haḥallôn* (12, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked, *ḥallôn* occurs in eight verses:
  Noah's window (Genesis 8:6), Abimelech's (26:8), Rahab's (Joshua 2:15, 18, 21), Sisera's mother's (Judges 5:28), and
  Michal's twice (here and 2 Samuel 6:16). Rahab and Michal are the only two who let someone out through one. See
  [[Let Down Through the Window - Rahab and Michal]] and
  [[Through the Window - Proverbs 7.6, Genesis 26.8, and Judges 5.28]].
- **The teraphim.** *Hattǝrāp̄îm* (13, 16, WLC), plural in form, one object in the bed. 1 Samuel has the word twice
  more, both times in Samuel's sentence on Saul, *stubbornness is as idolatry and teraphim* (15:23). The prophet named
  them as an image of Saul's sin; Saul's daughter has them in her house. The text does not say whose they were, or why
  they were there, and passes no judgment on them or on her. See [[Idolatry]] and
  [[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]].
- **Goats' hair.** *Kǝḇîr hāʿizzîm* (13, 16, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked *kǝḇîr* (3523) occurs only in these two
  verses; *pillow* is WEB's rendering, and other translations read a net or a quilt (recalled, not checked). The
  goats' hair that dressed the false David recalls *the skins of the young goats* that dressed the false Esau on
  Jacob's hands and neck (Genesis 27:16, stored WEB). The two share the word for goats, *hāʿizzîm* (27:9, 16, WLC), and
  nothing else; the wiki records the image and claims no echo.
- **At its head.** *Mǝraʾăšōṯāyw* (13, 16, WLC). In the files checked the word occurs at Genesis 28:11, 18 (the stone
  Jacob put *under his head* at Bethel), here, and at 26:7, 11, 16, where Saul sleeps in the camp *with his spear stuck
  in the ground at his head* and David takes it, with the jar of water, *from Saul's head* (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested). In 1 Samuel the word marks two beds: here the king's men come to a bed where David is not; at 26:7 David
  comes to the bed where the king is, and spares him. See [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]].
- **He is sick.** *Ḥōleh hûʾ* (14, WLC). The participle *ḥōleh* (2470 a) is Jacob's real sickness, *your father is
  sick* (Genesis 48:1), and Saul's *none of you is sorry for me* (22:8, stored WEB; not yet ingested); the Egyptian
  slave *got sick* (30:13, qal perfect). The feigned sickness of 2 Samuel 13 uses the *hithpael*: Amnon *made himself
  sick* for Tamar, and Jonadab tells him to lie on his bed *and make yourself sick* (2 Samuel 13:2, 5, 6; WLC;
  paraphrased). Michal's is the first feigned sickness in those files, and like Amnon's it is told about a man in his
  bed.
- **Bring him up in the bed.** *Haʿălû ʾōṯô ḇammiṭṭâ ʾēlay lahămiṯô* (15, WLC). The king who swore *he shall not be put
  to death* asks for a sick man to be carried to him so that he can kill him.
- **And behold.** *Wǝhinnēh hattǝrāp̄îm ʾel-hammiṭṭâ* (16, WLC). The narrator gives the messengers' point of view, as
  at Genesis 29:25, *In the morning, behold, it was Leah!* Each time a deceived party looks in a bed and finds the
  wrong thing in it.
- **Why have you deceived me?** *Lāmmâ kāḵâ rimmîṯinî* (17, WLC). The *piel* of *rāmâ*, *deceive* (7411 b), occurs in
  the files checked at Genesis 29:25 (Jacob to Laban, *Why then have you deceived me?*), Joshua 9:22 (Joshua to the
  Gibeonites, *Why have you deceived us?*), here, 1 Samuel 28:12 (the medium to Saul, *Why have you deceived me? For
  you are Saul!*), and 2 Samuel 19:27 (Mephibosheth, *my servant deceived me*; WLC; English 19:26, paraphrased). The
  first four are one question, *lāmmâ rimmîṯ-*, *why have you deceived …?*, asked by the deceived to the deceiver's
  face after the deception has already worked; Mephibosheth's is a statement. Saul asks the question here of his
  daughter; at Endor it will be asked of him. (Morphhb also tags *rāmâ* at Numbers 15:30, *with a high
  hand*, under this lemma; that is the participle of *rûm*, a different word.) See
  [[Why Have You Deceived Me - Laban, the Gibeonites, Michal, and the Medium]] and [[Deception]].
- **My enemy.** *ʾŌyǝḇî* (17, WLC). The narrator called Saul *David's enemy continually* (18:29); now Saul says it of
  David himself, to David's wife. At 24:4 David's men will call Saul *your enemy* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Why should I kill you?** *Lāmmâ ʾămîṯēḵ* (17, WLC). Michal answers Saul's *lāmmâ* with a *lāmmâ* of her own, put in
  David's mouth. It protects her and costs David nothing, since he is gone. It also gives David the threat of killing,
  which in this pericope has belonged only to Saul. The narrator does not say whether Saul believed it.

**How it connects.** Jonathan's plea held for a verse; Michal's night holds David's life. The wiki has now seen two of
Saul's children choose David over their father, one with an argument and one with a ruse, and Saul has been
*deceived* by both his son's advocacy and his daughter's bed. The next pericope sends him after David to Naioth, where
Saul's messengers, and then Saul himself, are overcome by the Spirit (19:18–24, stored WEB; not yet ingested). His
next spear goes at Jonathan (20:33). Michal disappears from the story until 25:44, when Saul gives her to another man.
In 2 Samuel David demands her back (3:13–16), and her last scene is at a window (6:16–23) (WLC; paraphrased).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 19:8–17 as planned. 19:8 opens with *There was war again*, after the
reconciliation of 19:7; 19:17 closes on Michal's answer; 19:18 opens a new movement, *Now David fled and escaped, and
came to Samuel at Ramah*. The spear-throw (8–10) and the window (11–17) are kept together because the narrator joins
them with *that night* (10) and *tonight* (11): one night, two escapes. No new person page: Saul's *messengers*
(11, 14–16) are not individuated. No place page, because no place is named. Two new connections:
[[Let Down Through the Window - Rahab and Michal]], for a sentence the Hebrew files checked share between only those
two women, and [[Why Have You Deceived Me - Laban, the Gibeonites, Michal, and the Medium]], for the question that the
*piel* of *rāmâ* always takes in those files. The Rachel parallel was already a section of
[[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]], so it gets a new section there, not a page of its own.

**Recorded, not resolved.** Whose teraphim these were, and why there was one in David's house; the text does not say.
Whether *kǝḇîr* is a pillow, a net, or a quilt; WEB's *pillow* is kept. Whether Saul believed Michal's *Why should I
kill you?*; the text records no answer. Whether Rachel's teraphim (Genesis 31) are meant to be recalled; the shared
object and verbs are recorded, and no dependence is claimed. Whether the window of 2 Samuel 6:16 is meant to recall
this one.
