# A Man of God Against the House of Eli

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 2:27–36

## Summary

> **27** A man of God came to Eli, and said to him, "Yahweh says, 'Did I reveal myself to the house of
> your father, when they were in Egypt in bondage to Pharaoh's house?
> **28** Didn't I choose him out of all the tribes of Israel to be my priest, to go up to my altar, to burn
> incense, to wear an ephod before me? Didn't I give to the house of your father all the offerings of the
> children of Israel made by fire?
> **29** Why do you kick at my sacrifice and at my offering, which I have commanded in my habitation, and
> honor your sons above me, to make yourselves fat with the best of all the offerings of Israel my
> people?'
> **30** "Therefore Yahweh, the God of Israel, says, 'I said indeed that your house, and the house of your
> father, should walk before me forever.' But now Yahweh says, 'Far be it from me; for those who honor me
> I will honor, and those who despise me will be cursed.
> **31** Behold, the days come, that I will cut off your arm, and the arm of your father's house, that there
> will not be an old man in your house.
> **32** You will see the affliction of my habitation, in all the wealth which I will give Israel; and there
> shall not be an old man in your house forever.
> **33** The man of yours, whom I don't cut off from my altar, will consume your eyes and grieve your heart;
> and all the increase of your house will die in the flower of their age.
> **34** "'This will be the sign to you, that will come on your two sons, on Hophni and Phinehas: in one day
> they will both die.
> **35** I will raise me up a faithful priest, that will do according to that which is in my heart and in
> my mind. I will build him a sure house; and he will walk before my anointed forever.
> **36** It will happen, that everyone who is left in your house will come and bow down to him for a piece
> of silver and a loaf of bread, and will say, "Please put me into one of the priests' offices, that I may
> eat a morsel of bread."'"

An unnamed [[Man of God (of Eli's time)]] comes to [[Eli]] with a word from Yahweh (27). It opens with
history: Yahweh revealed himself to *the house of your father* in [[Egypt]], chose *him* out of all the
tribes to be priest, to go up to the altar, burn incense, and wear an ephod, and gave that house *all the
offerings of the children of Israel made by fire* (27–28). Then the charge: *Why do you kick at my
sacrifice and at my offering … and honor your sons above me, to make yourselves fat with the best of all
the offerings of Israel my people?* (29). Then the reversal: Yahweh had said Eli's house would *walk before
me forever*, *but now … Far be it from me; for those who honor me I will honor, and those who despise me
will be cursed* (30). The sentence follows. Yahweh will cut off the *arm* of Eli's house so that no one in
it reaches old age (31–32); the one man left at the altar will live to grieve (33); and the sign is that
[[Hophni]] and [[Phinehas (son of Eli)]] *in one day … will both die* (34). Yahweh will *raise me up a
faithful priest* and *build him a sure house*, who *will walk before my anointed forever* (35). Whoever is
left of Eli's house will bow to him *for a piece of silver and a loaf of bread*, begging a priest's place
*that I may eat a morsel of bread* (36).

**What is going on.** This is the judgment the previous pericope withheld. In
[[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] Eli rebuked his sons and no one acted; now Yahweh acts, through a prophet who
appears once and is never named. The oracle has the shape of a prophetic lawsuit: what Yahweh did for the
house (27–28), what the house did in return (29), the verdict (30), and the sentence with its sign (31–36).
The charge falls on Eli, not only on the sons: *you* in 29 begins plural (*tiḇʿăṭû*, *you kick*, 2mp) and
turns singular (*wattəḵabbēḏ*, *and you honor*, 2ms) before going plural again (*ləhaḇrîʾăḵem*, *to make
yourselves fat*, 2mp) (WLC). Eli is counted among those who fattened themselves. The word is repeated and
sharpened to Samuel in the next chapter (3:11–14, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and its sign is fulfilled
at Aphek, where *the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain* (4:11). Its long fulfilment runs
into Kings: *So Solomon thrust Abiathar out from being priest to Yahweh, that he might fulfill Yahweh's
word, which he spoke concerning the house of Eli in Shiloh* (1 Kings 2:27, WEB checked at bible-api.com).
See [[The Word Against the House of Eli - 1 Samuel 2.27-36 and 1 Kings 2.27]].

**What is notable.**

- **A man of God.** *ʾÎš ʾĕlōhîm* (27, WLC), without the article. In the WLC files of Genesis through 1
  Kings, the phrase is used before this only of Moses (Deuteronomy 33:1; Joshua 14:6) and of the
  messenger who came to Manoah's wife (Judges 13:6, 8). After it, 1 Samuel uses it of Samuel, whom Saul's
  servant calls *a man of God … a man who is held in honor* (9:6, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and 1
  Kings of the unnamed man of God from Judah who cries against Jeroboam's altar (1 Kings 13). This one
  speaks once, to a priest, against a priestly house. The term will be used often in Kings; here it
  introduces the book's first prophetic oracle.
- **The house of your father.** The oracle does not name *him*, the ancestor chosen *out of all the tribes
  of Israel to be my priest* (28). The description — Egypt, the altar, incense, the ephod, the offerings
  made by fire — fits [[Aaron]] and the priestly line, or Levi's house more broadly; the choice of Aaron's
  house from all the tribes is the point of [[Aaron's Rod Buds]] (Numbers 17). 1 Samuel gives Eli no
  genealogy. Later texts place his descendant Ahimelech among *the sons of Ithamar* (1 Chronicles 24:3,
  WEB checked; with 1 Samuel 14:3; 22:9, 20, stored WEB, connecting Ahimelech and Abiathar to Eli's
  house), so [[Ithamar]] rather than Eleazar is the usual reconstruction of Eli's line. That is an
  inference across books, reported, not stated in this passage.
- **Kick.** *Lāmmâ ṯiḇʿăṭû* (29, WLC). The verb *bāʿaṭ* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1
  Kings only here and at Deuteronomy 32:15: *But Jeshurun grew fat, and kicked. You have grown fat. You
  have grown thick. You have become sleek. Then he abandoned God who made him* (stored WEB). Both pair the
  kick with getting fat: the Song of Moses with *šāmēn*, and this oracle with the Hiphil of *bārāʾ*, *to
  make yourselves fat* (29, WLC), whose Hiphil occurs in those files only here. Hannah's song had already
  borrowed from the Song of Moses (2:2, 6). See
  [[Grew Fat and Kicked - Deuteronomy 32.15 and 1 Samuel 2.29]].
- **My habitation.** *Māʿôn* (29, 32, WLC). In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings it occurs
  elsewhere only at Deuteronomy 26:15, *Look down from your holy habitation, from heaven*. There it is
  Yahweh's dwelling in heaven; here it is the sanctuary at [[Shiloh]], or so the context suggests. 29's
  *which I have commanded in my habitation* is grammatically awkward in Hebrew (*ʾăšer ṣiwwîṯî māʿôn*,
  WLC, with no preposition), and many commentators emend it; the WEB's rendering is one solution.
- **Honor and heaviness.** *Wattəḵabbēḏ ʾeṯ-bāneyḵā mimmennî* (29), *you honor your sons above me*, and
  *mək̲abbəḏay ʾăḵabbēḏ*, *those who honor me I will honor* (30), are both the root *kāḇēḏ*, *be heavy*
  (WLC). Its opposite stands at the end of 30: *ûḇōzay yēqallû*, *those who despise me will be cursed*,
  where *yēqallû* is from *qālal*, *be light, be slight* (WLC). The WEB's *cursed* is a fair rendering
  (the same root is the curse of Genesis 12:3, *məqallelḵā*, WLC), but the Hebrew sets *heavy* against
  *light*. The root returns at Eli's death: he fell *for he was an old man, and heavy*, *kāḇēḏ* (4:18,
  WLC), and his grandson is named Ichabod, *The glory has departed* (4:21), *kāḇôḏ*, *glory* (stored WEB;
  WLC; not yet ingested). The wordplay is widely noticed; the text makes no comment on it.
- **Despise.** *Bōzay*, *those who despise me* (30, WLC), from *bāzâ*. In the WLC files of Genesis
  through 1 Kings, the same verb is Esau's (*So Esau despised his birthright*, Genesis 25:34), the
  high-handed sinner's (*he has despised Yahweh's word*, Numbers 15:31), and Nathan's charge to David,
  *Why have you despised Yahweh's word?* (2 Samuel 12:9, WEB as checked at bible-api.com on the
  connection page below), followed by the
  sword that will not depart from David's house (12:10). In the previous pericope the sons *despised the
  offering* with a different verb, *nāʾaṣ* (2:17, WLC). See
  [[Despised the Word of Yahweh - Numbers 15.31 and 2 Samuel 12.9]].
- **I said indeed … but now.** *ʾĀmôr ʾāmartî … wəʿattâ nəʾum-YHWH ḥālîlâ lî* (30, WLC). Yahweh quotes his
  own earlier word and withdraws it. *Walk before me forever* is the language of a standing priestly
  grant (the *covenant of an everlasting priesthood* to Phinehas, Numbers 25:13, is the nearest promise in
  the stored text, though made to Eleazar's line, not demonstrably to Eli's). *Ḥālîlâ lî*, *far be it from
  me*, in Yahweh's mouth is unusual: in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings it is otherwise always
  spoken by a human (Joseph, Genesis 44:17; Samuel, 12:23; David, 24:6 WEB; 26:11). A promise said to be
  *forever* is revoked because the house dishonoured the one who made it. How a conditional reading of
  *forever* sits with the text, the oracle does not explain; the wiki reports the tension. See
  [[Divine Grief and Relenting]].
- **Walk before me.** *Yiṯhallḵû ləp̄ānay* (30, WLC), the Hithpael of *hālaḵ*. The same form with *before
  me* is Yahweh's word to Abram, *Walk before me, and be blameless* (Genesis 17:1, stored WEB), and in 35
  it is the faithful priest's future: *he will walk before my anointed*. In 1 Samuel 12:2 Samuel will say
  *I have walked before you* to the people (WLC; not yet ingested). See [[Walking with God]].
- **No old man in your house.** *Mihyôṯ zāqēn bəḇêṯeḵā* (31, WLC), repeated in 32. Eli is *very old*
  (2:22) and will die at ninety-eight (4:15); his descendants will not. The sentence falls on a family
  defined by an old man.
- **Cut off your arm.** *Wəḡāḏaʿtî ʾeṯ-zərōʿăḵā* (31, WLC). *Gāḏaʿ* in the WLC files of Genesis through 1
  Kings is otherwise used for hewing down Asherah poles (Deuteronomy 7:5; 12:3) and for the tribe *cut
  off* from Israel (Judges 21:6). *Arm* is strength; the house will lose its power.
- **The affliction of my habitation.** *Wəhibbaṭtā ṣar māʿôn* (32, WLC), literally something like *and
  you will look on distress of dwelling*. The Hebrew of 32a is notoriously difficult, and 32a is commonly
  reported to be absent from the Septuagint and from the Qumran Samuel scroll 4QSamᵃ; recalled from
  standard commentary, not checked against either. The WEB's *affliction of my habitation* supplies the
  *my*. Many read it as a forecast of the ark's capture and Shiloh's fall (chapter 4).
- **The man of yours whom I don't cut off.** *Wəʾîš lōʾ-ʾaḵrîṯ ləḵā mēʿim mizbəḥî* (33, WLC). One
  descendant will remain at the altar, *to consume your eyes and grieve your heart*. The text does not
  name him. Readers commonly see Abiathar, the sole survivor of the massacre of the priests at Nob, *One of
  the sons of Ahimelech, the son of Ahitub, named Abiathar, escaped* (22:20, stored WEB; not yet
  ingested), who is later removed by Solomon (1 Kings 2:26–27, WEB checked). For *die in the flower of
  their age*, the Hebrew reads only *yāmûṯû ʾănāšîm*, *will die as men* (33, WLC); the Septuagint and
  4QSamᵃ are commonly reported to read *by the sword of men* (recalled, not checked).
- **The sign.** *Wəzeh-ləḵā hāʾôṯ* (34, WLC). The sign is near; the sentence is long. Hophni and Phinehas
  are named for the first time since 1:3, and they die *in one day* (34), which 4:11 fulfils. See
  [[Signs and Wonders]].
- **A faithful priest … a sure house.** *Kōhēn neʾĕmān … bayiṯ neʾĕmān* (35, WLC), the Niphal participle of
  *ʾāman* twice. In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings, *bayiṯ neʾĕmān* occurs here, in Abigail's
  word to David, *Yahweh will certainly make my lord a sure house* (25:28, stored WEB), and in Ahijah's
  word to Jeroboam, *will build you a sure house, as I built for David* (1 Kings 11:38, WEB checked);
  Nathan's oracle uses the verb, *Your house and your kingdom will be made sure forever* (2 Samuel 7:16,
  WEB checked). Moses was *faithful in all my house* (Numbers 12:7, *neʾĕmān*). Two chapters on, *all Israel
  … knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of Yahweh* (3:20), where *established* is the same
  *neʾĕmān* (WLC; stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[A Sure House - The Faithful Priest and the House of David]].
- **Who is the faithful priest?** The oracle does not say. Three readings are common. Samuel, the boy
  growing at the altar beside Eli's sons, who is called *neʾĕmān* at 3:20 and who offers sacrifice in the
  chapters ahead; but Samuel's own sons fail (8:1–3, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and his house is not
  made *sure*. Zadok, whom Solomon installs *in the place of Abiathar* (1 Kings 2:35, WEB checked), and
  whose line serves the temple; Chronicles traces Zadok to Eleazar (1 Chronicles 24:3, WEB checked, *Zadok
  of the sons of Eleazar*), so the priesthood passes from Ithamar's line to Eleazar's, if the
  reconstruction above is right. And, in Christian reading, a priest whose *house* is fulfilled in the
  Messiah. The wiki records all three and settles none; *walk before my anointed* (35) presumes a king who
  does not yet exist and so points the reading past Samuel's lifetime.
- **My anointed.** *Lip̄nê-məšîḥî* (35, WLC), the second royal *māšîaḥ* in 1 Samuel after Hannah's *his
  anointed* (2:10). A priest who walks *before* the king is a subordinate priesthood, not the
  independent sanctuary-house of Eli. See [[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].
- **A piece of silver and a loaf of bread.** *Laʾăḡôraṯ kesep̄ wəḵikkar lāḥem* (36, WLC). *ʾĂḡôrâ* occurs
  in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings only here, and is usually glossed *a small coin* or
  *payment*; the gloss is conventional, not verified. Hannah's song had said *those who were full have
  hired themselves out for bread* (2:5). The house that made itself fat on *the best of all the offerings*
  (29) will beg for *a morsel*, *pat-lāḥem* (36). *Put me into one of the priests' offices*: *səp̄āḥēnî*
  (WLC), *attach me*, a verb otherwise used in those files only at 26:19, where David complains that men
  *have driven me out today that I shouldn't cling to Yahweh's inheritance* (stored WEB; WLC; not yet
  ingested), the WEB's *cling* rendering the same verb. *Kəhunnâ*, *priesthood*, in the
  plural only here in those files.

**How it connects.** The oracle turns Hannah's song into a sentence on a particular house: *those who
honor me I will honor* is her *Yahweh … brings low, he also lifts up* (2:7), applied to priests. It
answers the sons' contempt in [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]] with Yahweh's own contempt, and it prepares
Yahweh's word to the boy Samuel, which confirms it (3:11–14). It hangs a sign on Aphek (4:11), a remnant on
Nob (22:18–20), and a fulfilment on Solomon's accession (1 Kings 2:27, 35). It places the book's first
prophetic oracle beside its first two royal uses of *anointed* (2:10, 35), so that priesthood, prophecy,
and kingship are tied together from chapter 2. And its paired *faithful priest* and *sure house* runs
through Abigail's and Nathan's words to David and Ahijah's to Jeroboam.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 2:27–36 as planned; 3:1 opens a new scene with the boy Samuel. The
unnamed prophet gets a person page qualified by period, [[Man of God (of Eli's time)]], following the
rule for unnamed figures known by a title, since 1 Kings 13 and others will need their own. Aaron,
Ithamar, and Samuel are listed as people because the oracle's content turns on them, though none is named
in it; each entry says so. Zadok and Abiathar are named in the prose but given no pages yet; their pages
belong to the pericopes where they act (1 Samuel 22; 2 Samuel 8; 1 Kings 2).

**Unresolved.** Who *him* is in 28 (Aaron, or Levi's house); whether Eli descends from Ithamar (an
inference from 1 Chronicles 24:3 with 1 Samuel 14:3 and 22:20, not stated here); the sense and text of
*my habitation* (29, 32); the reported absence of 32a and the reading *by the sword of men* in 33 from the
Septuagint and 4QSamᵃ (recalled, not checked); who the *man … whom I don't cut off* is (33); who the
*faithful priest* is (35); and how *forever* (30) is revoked. Each is reported above, not settled.
