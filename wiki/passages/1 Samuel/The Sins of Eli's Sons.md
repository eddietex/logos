# The Sins of Eli's Sons

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 2:12–26

## Summary

> **12** Now the sons of Eli were wicked men. They didn't know Yahweh.
> **13** The custom of the priests with the people was that when anyone offered a sacrifice, the priest's
> servant came while the meat was boiling, with a fork of three teeth in his hand;
> **14** and he stabbed it into the pan, or kettle, or cauldron, or pot. The priest took all that the fork
> brought up for himself. So they did in Shiloh to all the Israelites who came there.
> **15** Yes, before they burned the fat, the priest's servant came, and said to the man who sacrificed,
> "Give meat to roast for the priest; for he will not accept boiled meat from you, but raw."
> **16** If the man said to him, "Let the fat be burned first, and then take as much as your soul
> desires"; then he would say, "No, but you shall give it to me now; and if not, I will take it by force."
> **17** The sin of the young men was very great before Yahweh; for the men despised the offering of
> Yahweh.
> **18** But Samuel ministered before Yahweh, being a child, clothed with a linen ephod.
> **19** Moreover his mother made him a little robe, and brought it to him from year to year, when she came
> up with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice.
> **20** Eli blessed Elkanah and his wife, and said, "May Yahweh give you offspring from this woman for the
> petition which was asked of Yahweh." Then they went to their own home.
> **21** Yahweh visited Hannah, and she conceived, and bore three sons and two daughters. The child Samuel
> grew before Yahweh.
> **22** Now Eli was very old; and he heard all that his sons did to all Israel, and how that they slept
> with the women who served at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
> **23** He said to them, "Why do you do such things? for I hear of your evil dealings from all this
> people.
> **24** No, my sons; for it is no good report that I hear! You make Yahweh's people disobey.
> **25** If one man sins against another, God will judge him; but if a man sins against Yahweh, who will
> intercede for him?" Notwithstanding, they didn't listen to the voice of their father, because Yahweh
> intended to kill them.
> **26** The child Samuel grew on, and increased in favor both with Yahweh, and also with men.

The sons of [[Eli]], [[Hophni]] and [[Phinehas (son of Eli)]], are *wicked men* who *didn't know Yahweh*
(12). At [[Shiloh]], when someone sacrificed, the priest's servant came while the meat boiled and stabbed a
three-pronged fork into the pot, and the priest kept whatever it brought up (13–14). Worse, before the fat
was burned for Yahweh, the servant demanded raw meat to roast, and when the worshipper asked that the fat
be burned first, he answered *give it to me now; and if not, I will take it by force* (15–16). The
narrator's verdict: their sin was *very great before Yahweh*, because they *despised the offering of
Yahweh* (17). Meanwhile the boy [[Samuel]] ministers before Yahweh in a linen ephod, and every year
[[Hannah]] brings him a little robe she has made (18–19). Eli blesses [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]] and
Hannah, asking Yahweh to give them children *for the petition which was asked of Yahweh*; Yahweh visits
Hannah, and she bears three sons and two daughters, while *the child Samuel grew before Yahweh* (20–21).
Eli, very old, hears what his sons do to all Israel and that they lie with the women who served at the door
of the Tent of Meeting (22). He rebukes them: the report is not good, they make Yahweh's people
transgress, and if a man sins against Yahweh, *who will intercede for him?* (23–25). They do not listen,
*because Yahweh intended to kill them* (25). And the boy Samuel keeps growing, in favour with Yahweh and
with men (26).

**What is going on.** The chapter is built as an alternation, and the alternation is the argument. Eli's
sons (12–17), Samuel (18–21), Eli's sons (22–25), Samuel (26). Each time the narrative turns from the
sons' corruption at the altar, it turns to the boy growing at the same altar. Hannah's song had just
said that Yahweh *brings low* and *lifts up* (2:7); this pericope shows both movements beginning in the
same sanctuary at the same time. No one in the chapter yet acts against the sons. Eli speaks and is not
heard; the judgment comes in the next pericope, from a man of God (2:27–36), and is carried out at
Aphek, where *the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain* (4:11, stored WEB; not yet
ingested).

**What is notable.**

- **Wicked men who didn't know Yahweh.** *Bənê ḇəlîyāʿal lōʾ yāḏəʿû ʾeṯ-YHWH* (12, WLC). *Bəlîyāʿal* is
  the word Hannah had begged Eli not to apply to her: *Don't consider your servant a wicked woman*, *baṯ-
  bəlîyāʿal* (1:16). Eli misjudged the praying woman as worthless; his own sons are. The sequence *lōʾ
  yāḏaʿ ʾeṯ-YHWH*, *not know Yahweh*, occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth and 1 Samuel only
  three times: Pharaoh's *I don't know Yahweh* (Exodus 5:2), the generation after Joshua *who didn't know
  Yahweh* (Judges 2:10), and here. Priests at Yahweh's altar are described in the words used of Egypt's
  king and of the apostate generation. Samuel, by contrast, *didn't yet know Yahweh*, *ṭerem yāḏaʿ*
  (3:7, WLC; stored WEB; not yet ingested): not yet, rather than not. See
  [[They Didn't Know Yahweh - Pharaoh, the Generation After Joshua, and Eli's Sons]].
- **The custom of the priests.** *Ûmišpaṭ hakkōhănîm* (13, WLC) opens with the same words as the law of
  the priests' due in Deuteronomy 18:3. Whether 13–14 describe the legitimate custom that 15–16 then
  abuse, or the sons' own corrupt practice from the start, is read both ways. See
  [[The Custom of the Priests - Deuteronomy 18.3 and 1 Samuel 2.13-16]].
- **A fork of three teeth.** *Hammazlēḡ šəlōš-haššinnayim* (13, WLC). In the WLC files searched (Genesis
  through 2 Samuel, and 1 Chronicles), *mazlēḡ* elsewhere occurs only in the plural, among the bronze
  vessels of the altar (Exodus 27:3; 38:3; Numbers 4:14) and the gold vessels David lists for the temple
  (1 Chronicles 28:17). The altar's own equipment is turned on the worshippers' pots.
- **Before the fat was burned.** *Bəṭerem yaqṭirûn ʾeṯ-haḥēleḇ* (15, WLC). The law gives the fat to
  Yahweh first: *all the fat is Yahweh's* (Leviticus 3:16), and *the priest shall burn the fat on the
  altar, but the breast shall be Aaron's and his sons'* (Leviticus 7:31). The worshipper in 16 knows the
  order and asks for it; the priest's servant refuses. The sons' fault is not only greed but precedence:
  they are served before Yahweh. 1 Samuel cites no law, and the observation is the reader's.
- **Take it by force.** *Lāqaḥtî ḇəḥozqâ* (16, WLC). *Ḥozqâ* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis
  through 2 Samuel only here and at Judges 4:3, where Jabin *mightily oppressed* Israel, and Judges 8:1,
  where Ephraim *rebuked him sharply*. The priests treat Israel as Israel's oppressors did. 16 also carries
  a *ketiv/qere*: the written text has *lô*, *to him*, and the reading tradition *lōʾ*, *no* (WLC); the
  WEB's *No, but you shall give it to me now* follows the *qere*. (In 2:3 the pair ran the other way.)
- **They despised the offering.** *Kî niʾăṣû hāʾănāšîm ʾēṯ minḥaṯ YHWH* (17, WLC). *Nāʾaṣ*, *despise,
  treat with contempt*, occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth at Numbers 14:11, 23 and 16:30,
  where the people who refuse the land and Korah's company *despise* Yahweh, and at Deuteronomy 31:20 and
  32:19. Here the object is not Yahweh but his offering, and the subjects are his priests. The word next
  appears in Nathan's word to David after Uriah (2 Samuel 12:14, the same verb, doubled, WLC; the WEB of
  that verse was not checked and is not quoted here). See [[The People Refuse the Land]].
- **The young men … the men.** 17 calls the sons *hannəʿārîm*, *the young men*, then *hāʾănāšîm*, *the
  men* (WLC). The next verse calls Samuel *naʿar*, *a child*, the same noun (18, WLC), and 13 and 15 call
  the priest's servant *naʿar* too. Three kinds of *naʿar* stand at one altar. The noun is the narrator's;
  the WEB varies it as *servant*, *young men*, and *child*.
- **A linen ephod, a little robe.** *Naʿar ḥāḡûr ʾēp̄ôḏ bāḏ* (18, WLC). The exact phrase *ḥāḡûr ʾēp̄ôḏ
  bāḏ*, *girded with a linen ephod*, is used in the WLC files of Genesis through 2 Samuel only here and of
  David dancing before the ark (2 Samuel 6:14); 1 Samuel 22:18 counts the priests of Nob as men *who wore
  a linen ephod* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The *məʿîl*, *robe*, his mother makes (19) is in the Torah
  the high priest's *robe of the ephod* (Exodus 28:31; 39:22), though a *məʿîl* in Samuel is also worn by
  Jonathan, Saul, and kings' daughters (1 Samuel 18:4; 24:4, 11 WEB numbering; 2 Samuel 13:18, WLC). Samuel's robe
  returns twice: Saul tears its skirt (15:27), and the medium at Endor sees *an old man … covered with a
  robe* (28:14) (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The child's robe and the old man's are the same noun; the
  narrative does not say they are the same garment.
- **From year to year.** *Miyyāmîm yāmîmâ* (19, WLC), the phrase of the yearly visit (1:3) and of the
  lament for Jephthah's daughter (Judges 11:40). See
  [[From Year to Year - Jephthah's Daughter, the Feast at Shiloh, and Elkanah]].
- **The petition which was asked.** *Taḥaṯ haššəʾēlâ ʾăšer šāʾal laYHWH* (20, WLC). The *ask* wordplay of
  chapter 1 continues: *šəʾēlâ*, *petition*, and *šāʾal*, *ask*. The WEB's passive *was asked* renders a
  form the WLC parses as active masculine singular, *he asked* (WLC morph *Vqp3ms*). Who *he* is the
  verse does not say; some read Elkanah, some an impersonal *one asked*, and some propose that the text
  originally read *she asked*. The wiki reports the form and leaves the reading open. See
  [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].
- **Yahweh visited Hannah.** *Kî-p̄āqaḏ YHWH ʾeṯ-ḥannâ* (21, WLC), the verb of *Yahweh visited Sarah as he
  had said* (Genesis 21:1, *pāqaḏ*, WLC). Hannah gave one son and receives five more children. Her song had
  sung *the barren has borne seven* (2:5); with Samuel she has borne six. See [[The Barren Matriarchs]].
- **The child grew with Yahweh.** *Wayyiḡdal hannaʿar šəmûʾēl ʿim-YHWH* (21, WLC), literally *with*
  Yahweh; the WEB renders *before*. 26 repeats it: *hōlēḵ wəḡāḏēl wāṭôḇ gam ʿim-YHWH wəḡam ʿim-ʾănāšîm*,
  *going on and growing and good, both with Yahweh and with men* (WLC). The WEB's *increased in favor*
  translates *wāṭôḇ*, *and good*, not *ḥēn*. Luke says the same of the boy Jesus (Luke 2:52). See
  [[Favor with God and Man - Proverbs 3.4, 1 Samuel 2.26, and Luke 2.52]].
- **The women who served at the door of the Tent of Meeting.** *Hannāšîm haṣṣōḇəʾôṯ peṯaḥ ʾōhel môʿēḏ*
  (22, WLC). The participle of *ṣāḇāʾ* with women, at the door of the Tent of Meeting, occurs in the WLC
  files of Genesis through 2 Samuel only here and at Exodus 38:8, where *the mirrors of the ministering
  women who ministered at the door of the Tent of Meeting* furnish the bronze basin. The verb is the one
  used of the Levites' *service* in the tent (Numbers 4:23; 8:24) and, elsewhere, of military service
  (Numbers 31:7, 42). What these women's service was, neither text says. See
  [[Ministering Women (of Eli's time)]] and
  [[The Women Who Served at the Door - Exodus 38.8 and 1 Samuel 2.22]].
- **Half 22 is missing in some witnesses.** The clause about the women is commonly reported to be absent
  from the Septuagint and from the Qumran Samuel scroll 4QSamᵃ; recalled from standard commentary, not
  checked against either. If so, the sons' sin in the oldest form of the story may have been the
  sacrificial abuse alone. Reported, not settled.
- **Eli was very old.** *Wəʿēlî zāqēn məʾōḏ* (22, WLC). His age is given later: *ninety-eight years
  old*, his eyes set (4:15, stored WEB; not yet ingested). He *heard*; the verb *šāmaʿ* runs through 22–25:
  Eli hears (22), *I hear* (23), *the report that I hear* (24), and the sons do not *listen* (25), all
  *šāmaʿ* (WLC). The father hears and the sons will not.
- **You make Yahweh's people transgress.** *Maʿăḇirîm ʿam-YHWH* (24, WLC), from *ʿāḇar*, *cross over,
  transgress*. The WEB's *disobey* renders the causative: they cause the people to cross the line. The
  sons' sin is not private; it spreads to everyone who comes to sacrifice.
- **Who will intercede?** *Im-yeḥĕṭāʾ ʾîš ləʾîš ûp̄illô ʾĕlōhîm wəʾim laYHWH yeḥĕṭāʾ-ʾîš mî yiṯpallel-lô*
  (25, WLC). Both verbs are *pālal*: the Piel, *ûp̄illô*, *will judge* or *will mediate for him*, and the
  Hitpael, *yiṯpallel*, *intercede, pray*, which is the verb of Hannah's prayer (1:10, 12, 26–27; 2:1,
  WLC). A wrong between people has an arbiter; a wrong against Yahweh has no one above him to appeal to.
  *ʾĔlōhîm* in the first clause may mean God or, as some read it, human judges (compare Exodus 21:6;
  22:8–9, where the WEB has *God*); the wiki reports both. Eli's question is the one Job asks (Job 9:33;
  16:21). Within 1 Samuel the question gets an answer of a kind: the boy growing beside the sons becomes
  the book's intercessor, *I will pray to Yahweh for you* (7:5) and *far be it from me that I should sin
  against Yahweh in ceasing to pray for you* (12:23), both *pālal* in the Hitpael (stored WEB; WLC; not yet
  ingested). The pairing is the reader's. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].
- **They didn't listen to the voice of their father.** *Wəlōʾ yišməʿû ləqôl ʾăḇîhem* (25, WLC). The law
  of the *stubborn and rebellious son, who will not obey the voice of his father* (Deuteronomy 21:18,
  *šōmēaʿ bəqôl ʾāḇîw*, WLC) prescribes death by the city (21:21). The phrasing differs (*lə-* here, *bə-*
  there), and 1 Samuel does not cite the law; the likeness is the reader's. Eli does not bring his sons
  to the elders. 3:13 will say why the house falls: *his sons brought a curse on themselves, and he didn't
  restrain them* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[Did Not Listen to the Voice of Their Father - Deuteronomy 21.18 and 1 Samuel 2.25]].
- **Because Yahweh intended to kill them.** *Kî-ḥāp̄ēṣ YHWH lahămîṯām* (25, WLC), literally *because
  Yahweh desired to put them to death*. The same verb with the same causative of *mûṯ* is Manoah's wife's
  reasoning in Judges 13:23: *If Yahweh were pleased to kill us, he wouldn't have received a burnt
  offering*. In the WLC files of Genesis through 2 Samuel, *ḥāp̄ēṣ* with *hēmîṯ* occurs only in those two
  verses. There Yahweh's acceptance of the offering proves he does not intend death; here the men who
  despise his offering are the ones he intends to kill. The clause makes the refusal to listen part of
  the judgment, as Pharaoh's heart was hardened. That is the narrator's sentence, and how it sits with the
  sons' responsibility the text does not explain. See [[Pleased to Kill - Manoah's Wife and Eli's Sons]]
  and [[Hardening of the Heart]].

**How it connects.** The pericope opens the judgment that the song before it announced: *the bows of the
mighty men are broken* and *those who were full have hired themselves out for bread* (2:4–5) will be
spelled out to Eli's house as *a piece of silver and a loaf of bread* (2:36). It answers the sacrificial
law of Leviticus 3 and 7 and the priests' due of Deuteronomy 18 by showing the share seized and the fat
postponed. Its picture of a priest who will not restrain his sons sets up the next pericope,
[[1 Samuel]] 2:27–36, where a man of God says Eli has honored his sons above Yahweh (2:29). Its picture
of Samuel growing *with Yahweh and with men* reaches to Luke's boy Jesus (Luke 2:52). And its contrast,
*didn't know Yahweh* against *didn't yet know Yahweh*, prepares the call of Samuel in chapter 3.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 2:12–26 as planned: the alternation of sons and Samuel closes on
26, and 27 opens a new scene with the man of God. No boundary was redrawn. Hophni and Phinehas are linked
though this pericope calls them only *the sons of Eli*; 1:3 and 2:34 name them. The women of 22 receive a
group page, [[Ministering Women (of Eli's time)]], qualified by period like
[[Ministering Women (of Moses' time)]], without asserting continuity between the two groups.

**Unresolved.** Whether 13–14 describe a legitimate custom or the sons' innovation; who *he* is in *which
he asked* (20); whether *ʾĕlōhîm* in 25 means God or judges; whether the clause about the women in 22
belonged to the earliest text; and how *Yahweh intended to kill them* relates to the sons' own refusal.
Each is reported above, not settled.
