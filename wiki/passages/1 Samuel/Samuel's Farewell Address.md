# Samuel's Farewell Address

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 12:1–25

## Summary

> **1** Samuel said to all Israel, "Behold, I have listened to your voice in all that you said to me, and have
> made a king over you.
> **2** Now, behold, the king walks before you. I am old and gray-headed. Behold, my sons are with you. I have
> walked before you from my youth to this day.
> **3** Here I am. Witness against me before Yahweh, and before his anointed. Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey
> have I taken? Whom have I defrauded? Whom have I oppressed? Of whose hand have I taken a ransom to make me blind
> my eyes? I will restore it to you."
> **4** They said, "You have not defrauded us, nor oppressed us, neither have you taken anything from anyone's
> hand."
> **5** He said to them, "Yahweh is witness against you, and his anointed is witness today, that you have not found
> anything in my hand." They said, "He is witness."
> **6** Samuel said to the people, "It is Yahweh who appointed Moses and Aaron, and that brought your fathers up out
> of the land of Egypt.
> **7** Now therefore stand still, that I may plead with you before Yahweh concerning all the righteous acts of
> Yahweh, which he did to you and to your fathers.
> **8** "When Jacob had come into Egypt, and your fathers cried to Yahweh, then Yahweh sent Moses and Aaron, who
> brought your fathers out of Egypt, and made them to dwell in this place.
> **9** "But they forgot Yahweh their God; and he sold them into the hand of Sisera, captain of the army of Hazor,
> and into the hand of the Philistines, and into the hand of the king of Moab; and they fought against them.
> **10** They cried to Yahweh, and said, 'We have sinned, because we have forsaken Yahweh, and have served the Baals
> and the Ashtaroth: but now deliver us out of the hand of our enemies, and we will serve you.'
> **11** Yahweh sent Jerubbaal, Bedan, Jephthah, and Samuel, and delivered you out of the hand of your enemies on
> every side; and you lived in safety.
> **12** "When you saw that Nahash the king of the children of Ammon came against you, you said to me, 'No, but a
> king shall reign over us;' when Yahweh your God was your king.
> **13** Now therefore see the king whom you have chosen, and whom you have asked for. Behold, Yahweh has set a king
> over you.
> **14** If you will fear Yahweh, and serve him, and listen to his voice, and not rebel against the commandment of
> Yahweh, then both you and also the king who reigns over you are followers of Yahweh your God.
> **15** But if you will not listen to Yahweh's voice, but rebel against the commandment of Yahweh, then Yahweh's
> hand will be against you, as it was against your fathers.
> **16** "Now therefore stand still and see this great thing, which Yahweh will do before your eyes.
> **17** Isn't it wheat harvest today? I will call to Yahweh, that he may send thunder and rain; and you will know
> and see that your wickedness is great, which you have done in Yahweh's sight, in asking for a king."
> **18** So Samuel called to Yahweh; and Yahweh sent thunder and rain that day. Then all the people greatly feared
> Yahweh and Samuel.
> **19** All the people said to Samuel, "Pray for your servants to Yahweh your God, that we not die; for we have
> added to all our sins this evil, to ask for a king."
> **20** Samuel said to the people, "Don't be afraid. You have indeed done all this evil; yet don't turn away from
> following Yahweh, but serve Yahweh with all your heart.
> **21** Don't turn away to go after vain things which can't profit or deliver, for they are vain.
> **22** For Yahweh will not forsake his people for his great name's sake, because it has pleased Yahweh to make you
> a people for himself.
> **23** Moreover as for me, far be it from me that I should sin against Yahweh in ceasing to pray for you: but I
> will instruct you in the good and the right way.
> **24** Only fear Yahweh, and serve him in truth with all your heart; for consider what great things he has done
> for you.
> **25** But if you keep doing evil, you will be consumed, both you and your king."

[[Samuel]] speaks to *all Israel*. He has done what they asked, *I have listened to your voice*, and made them a
king, who now *walks before you*; he himself is *old and gray-headed*, his sons are *with you*, and he has walked
before them *from my youth to this day* (1–2). He puts his own career on trial: *Witness against me before
Yahweh, and before his anointed*. Whose ox, whose donkey, whom defrauded, whom oppressed, whose ransom? *I will
restore it to you* (3). The people clear him, and Samuel seals the verdict with two witnesses, Yahweh and *his
anointed*, [[Saul]]; the people answer *He is witness* (4–5).

Then he turns the trial around. *Stand still, that I may plead with you before Yahweh concerning all the
righteous acts of Yahweh* (7). He recites the history: [[Jacob]] went into [[Egypt]], the fathers cried, Yahweh
sent [[Moses]] and [[Aaron]] and brought them to *this place* (6, 8). They forgot Yahweh, and he *sold them* to
[[Sisera]] of [[Hazor]], to [[The Philistines]], and to the king of [[Moab (place)]] (9). They confessed, *We have
sinned … and have served the Baals and the Ashtaroth*, and Yahweh sent [[Gideon]] (*Jerubbaal*), [[Bedan]],
[[Jephthah]], and Samuel himself, and they lived *in safety* (10–11). But when [[Nahash (king of Ammon)]] came, the
people said *No, but a king shall reign over us*, *when Yahweh your God was your king* (12). Here is the king
*whom you have chosen, and whom you have asked for*, and Yahweh has set him over them (13). If people and king
fear Yahweh, serve him, and listen to his voice, well; if they rebel, *Yahweh's hand will be against you* (14–15).

Then a sign. *Isn't it wheat harvest today?* Samuel calls to Yahweh, and Yahweh sends *thunder and rain that
day*. *All the people greatly feared Yahweh and Samuel* (16–18). They beg him, *Pray for your servants … that we
not die; for we have added to all our sins this evil, to ask for a king* (19). Samuel answers *Don't be afraid*:
they have done evil, but they are not to turn aside after *vain things which can't profit or deliver*. *Yahweh
will not forsake his people for his great name's sake* (20–22). As for him, *far be it from me that I should sin
against Yahweh in ceasing to pray for you*; he will teach them *the good and the right way* (23). *Only fear
Yahweh … for consider what great things he has done for you. But if you keep doing evil, you will be consumed,
both you and your king* (24–25).

**Where it happens.** The chapter does not name a place. The previous verse leaves all the people at [[Gilgal]],
making Saul king and rejoicing (11:15), and the speech reads as the close of that assembly; many readers place it
there. The text itself says only *all Israel* (1).

**What is going on.** The section of the book that began with *make us a king* (8:5) ends here, and Samuel
closes it as a judge hands over office. He began the request scene displeased (8:6), was told three times to
*listen to their voice* (8:7, 9, 22), and opens now with *I have listened to your voice* (1): he has done what
Yahweh told him, and he wants that on record. The speech then has three movements, and each answers the request
for a king from another side.

The first is an audit (1–5). Samuel's sons *took bribes* (8:3) and the king *will take* (8:11–17); Samuel asks
the people to name one thing *he* took, and they cannot. The witness he calls beside Yahweh is the king. The new
order is made to certify the old one's honesty.

The second is a lawsuit (6–15). *Wəʾiššāp̄əṭâ ʾittəḵem* (7, WLC), *that I may plead with you*, uses *šāp̄aṭ*, the
judges' verb, in the Niphal: the last judge enters into judgment with Israel. The evidence is *the righteous acts
of Yahweh*, a phrase Deborah's song uses (Judges 5:11, WLC). The history he recites is the cycle of Judges in
brief — forgetting, selling, crying, confession, sending, rescue — and its point is that the cycle worked: every
time they cried, Yahweh sent a deliverer, and the last of them is the speaker. The request for a king is then
placed inside that cycle, at the moment Israel should have cried and instead asked for a king. The conditional
that follows (14–15) gives the king no exemption. He is under the same *if* as the people.

The third is a sign and a reprieve (16–25). Thunder in wheat harvest is out of season, and the people take it as
judgment: they ask for prayer *that we not die*. Samuel's answer is the theology of the whole section in brief.
The request was evil, and it will not be undone; Yahweh will not abandon them for it, *for his great name's sake*;
and the prophet will not stop praying. The king is now part of the arrangement, and the arrangement is the old
one: fear Yahweh, serve him, and do not turn aside.

**Recorded, not resolved — Nahash and the request.** In 12:12 Samuel places the request for a king after
Nahash's attack: *When you saw that Nahash … came against you, you said to me, 'No, but a king shall reign over
us'*. The narrative of chapters 8–11 has the elders ask for a king because Samuel was old and his sons corrupt
(8:1–5), and Nahash appears only in chapter 11, after Saul is chosen. The wiki has already recorded this on
[[Saul Rescues Jabesh Gilead]] and [[Nahash (king of Ammon)]], and leaves both accounts standing. Possible
readings are that an Ammonite threat stood unspoken behind chapter 8, that the speech telescopes events, or that
two traditions differ; the text does not choose.

**What is notable.**

- **I have listened to your voice … and have made a king over you.** *Šāmaʿtî ḇəqōləḵem … wāʾamlîḵ ʿălêḵem
  meleḵ* (1, WLC), the phrase of Yahweh's three commands, *šəmaʿ bəqôl hāʿām* (8:7, 9, 22). The phrase recurs
  at 14–15 in the other direction: *if you will … listen to his voice*.
- **The king walks before you … I have walked before you.** *Mithallēḵ lip̄nêḵem … hithallaḵtî lip̄nêḵem* (2,
  WLC). The same Hithpael of *hālaḵ* serves king and judge. The man of God had used it for the priestly house
  that would *walk before me forever* and the priest who *will walk before my anointed forever* (2:30, 35). See
  [[Walking with God]].
- **I am old and gray-headed. Behold, my sons are with you.** *Zāqantî wāśaḇtî* (2, WLC). *Zāqēn* is the word of
  8:1, 5, *When Samuel was old*, *you are old*, and the sons are the ones of 8:1–3. Joshua's farewell opened with
  *I am old and well advanced in years* (Joshua 23:2, stored WEB). Whether *my sons are with you* defends them or
  hands them over, the text does not say. See [[Joel (son of Samuel)]], [[Abijah (son of Samuel)]], and
  [[Not in His Ways - The Sons of Eli and the Sons of Samuel]].
- **Witness against me before Yahweh, and before his anointed.** *ʿĂnû ḇî neḡeḏ YHWH wəneḡeḏ məšîḥô* (3, WLC).
  *ʿĀnâ bə-*, *answer against*, is the legal idiom of testimony, the verb of the commandment *You shall not give
  false testimony*, *lōʾ-ṯaʿăneh … ʿēḏ šāqer* (Exodus 20:16, WLC). This is the first time in the narrative
  that a living king is called *Yahweh's anointed*. Hannah sang of *his anointed* before there was one (2:10), and
  the man of God promised a priest who would walk *before my anointed* (2:35). See [[The Song of Hannah]].
- **Whose ox … whose donkey … whom defrauded … whom oppressed … whose ransom.** Five questions, three of them with
  *lāqaḥ*, *take* (3, WLC), the verb of *the way of the king* (8:11–17). Moses, accused by Korah's company, said to
  Yahweh *I have not taken one donkey from them* (Numbers 16:15, stored WEB), with *nāśāʾ*, *carry off*, not
  *lāqaḥ* (WLC); the likeness is in the donkey and the self-defence, not the verb. *ʿĀšaq* and *rāṣaṣ*,
  *defraud* and *oppress* (3–4), are paired in Deuteronomy's curse, *You will only be oppressed and crushed always*
  (28:33, WLC). *Rāṣaṣ* is also the verb used of the Ammonites at Judges 10:8, *They troubled and oppressed the
  children of Israel* (stored WEB; WLC), which may make it pointed after chapter 11; the text does not draw the
  line.
- **A ransom to make me blind my eyes.** *Kōp̄er wəʾaʿlîm ʿênay bô* (3, WLC). *Kōp̄er* is the payment that buys
  off a penalty (Exodus 21:30; Numbers 35:31–32), here a bribe. The law gives the reason against bribes as *a
  bribe blinds the eyes of the wise* (Deuteronomy 16:19, stored WEB), though with a different noun, *šōḥaḏ*, and a
  different verb (WLC). *ʿĀlam* in the Hiphil, *hide, turn away*, with *eyes*, occurs in the files searched only
  here (WLC, Genesis–2 Samuel; lemma 5956).
- **I will restore it to you.** *Wəʾāšîḇ lāḵem* (3, WLC). Samuel offers restitution before any claim is made. See
  [[Theft and Restitution]].
- **He is witness.** *Wayyōʾmer ʿēḏ* (5, WLC): the verb is singular, *he said*, where the WEB supplies *They said*.
  Many manuscripts and versions are said to read the plural (recalled, not checked); the WLC prints the singular.
  The people's one-word reply recalls Shechem, where Joshua said *You are witnesses against yourselves* and they
  answered *We are witnesses* (Joshua 24:22, stored WEB). See
  [[Witnesses Against Yourselves - Joshua's Farewell and Samuel's]].
- **It is Yahweh who appointed Moses and Aaron.** *YHWH ʾăšer ʿāśâ ʾeṯ-mōšeh wəʾeṯ-ʾahărōn* (6, WLC): literally
  *who made Moses and Aaron*. The sentence begins with the divine name and has no main verb in Hebrew; the WEB
  supplies *It is*. Some readers take *Yahweh is witness* from verse 5 as understood (recalled, not checked).
  Joshua's history said *I sent Moses and Aaron* (Joshua 24:5, stored WEB), and verse 8 here says *Yahweh sent
  Moses and Aaron* with the same verb, *šālaḥ* (WLC).
- **Stand still, that I may plead with you.** *Hiṯyaṣṣəḇû wəʾiššāp̄əṭâ* (7, WLC). *Stand still* returns at 16,
  *hiṯyaṣṣəḇû ûrəʾû*, *stand still and see*, the words of Moses at the sea: *Don't be afraid. Stand still, and see
  the salvation of Yahweh* (Exodus 14:13, stored WEB; WLC *hiṯyaṣṣəḇû ûrəʾû*). Both speakers go on to say *Don't
  be afraid* (Exodus 14:13; 1 Samuel 12:20).
- **The righteous acts of Yahweh.** *Ṣiḏqôṯ YHWH* (7, WLC). Judges 5:11 sings *there they will rehearse Yahweh's
  righteous acts* (stored WEB), the same phrase in the WLC. See [[Righteousness]].
- **He sold them into the hand of.** *Wayyimkōr ʾōṯām bəyaḏ* (9, WLC), the verb of the Judges formula (for
  example Judges 4:2, *Yahweh sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor; the captain of
  whose army was Sisera*, stored WEB). Samuel names Sisera, not Jabin, as *captain of the army of Hazor*. The order
  of the three oppressors is not Judges' order: Eglon of Moab (Judges 3:12) came before Sisera, and the Philistines
  came last. The speech is not keeping chronology.
- **We have sinned, because we have forsaken Yahweh.** *Ḥāṭāʾnû kî ʿāzaḇnû ʾeṯ-YHWH wannaʿăḇōḏ ʾeṯ-habbəʿālîm
  wəʾeṯ-hāʿaštārôṯ* (10, WLC), close to Israel's confession under Ammon, *We have sinned against you, even because
  we have forsaken our God, and have served the Baals* (Judges 10:10, stored WEB; WLC *ḥāṭāʾnû lāḵ wəḵî ʿāzaḇnû
  ʾeṯ-ʾĕlōhênû wannaʿăḇōḏ ʾeṯ-habbəʿālîm*). *The Baals and the Ashtaroth* are what Israel put away at Mizpah (7:4).
  The written text (*kethiv*) has the singular *wyʾmr*, *he said*, and the reading tradition (*qere*) the plural
  *wayyōʾmərû* (WLC prints both). See [[Baal (deity)]] and [[Ashtoreth (deity)]].
- **Jerubbaal, Bedan, Jephthah, and Samuel.** *Yərubbaʿal* is Gideon's other name (Judges 6:32). *Bədān* (11, WLC)
  is not named elsewhere in the WLC files searched (Genesis–2 Samuel; lemma 917), and no judge called Bedan appears
  in Judges. Some ancient versions are commonly reported to read *Barak* here, and others have proposed Abdon
  (Judges 12:13) or a reading of *ben-Dan*, *the son of Dan*, for Samson; all of this is recalled, not checked, and
  no version was consulted. See [[Bedan]]. Samuel names himself last, as the latest of the deliverers. Some
  manuscripts and versions are said to read *Samson* in place of *Samuel* (recalled, not checked); the WLC has
  *Šəmûʾēl*.
- **You lived in safety.** *Wattēšəḇû beṭaḥ* (11, WLC), the condition Deuteronomy promised, that Israel would
  *dwell in safety*, given *rest from all your enemies around you* (Deuteronomy 12:10, stored WEB; WLC
  *mikkol-ʾōyəḇêḵem missāḇîḇ … wîšaḇtem beṭaḥ*), the same words as *your enemies on every side* here. The judges brought it each time, and the request for a king
  came after.
- **When Yahweh your God was your king.** *WaYHWH ʾĕlōhêḵem malkəḵem* (12, WLC). Samuel puts into his own words
  what Yahweh told him privately: *they have rejected me as the king over them* (8:7). See
  [[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]].
- **The king whom you have chosen, and whom you have asked for.** *Hammeleḵ ʾăšer bəḥartem ʾăšer šəʾeltem* (13,
  WLC). *Šāʾal*, *ask*, is the root of Saul's name, and it returns in 17 and 19, *to ask for a king*. In 10:24
  Samuel had said *him whom Yahweh has chosen*; here the people have *chosen*. Then, in the same verse, *Yahweh has
  set a king over you*. Both are said together, and the text does not resolve them. See
  [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].
- **Then both you and also the king … are followers of Yahweh your God.** *Wihyîṯem gam-ʾattem wəḡam-hammeleḵ …
  ʾaḥar YHWH ʾĕlōhêḵem* (14, WLC), literally *you will be, both you and the king …, after Yahweh your God*. The
  verse can be read as a complete condition (*if you fear … then you will be after Yahweh*), as the WEB reads it,
  or as a protasis whose consequence is left unspoken; the second reading is commonly discussed (recalled, not
  checked). The WEB's *rebel against the commandment of Yahweh* is *ûmərîṯem ʾeṯ-pî YHWH* (15, WLC), literally
  *rebel against the mouth of Yahweh*, the phrase of Israel at Kadesh (Deuteronomy 1:26, 43; WLC). See
  [[Transgressing the Mouth of Yahweh - Numbers 14 and 1 Samuel 15]], which reaches the king at 15:24.
- **As it was against your fathers.** *Wəhāyəṯâ yaḏ-YHWH bāḵem ûḇaʾăḇōṯêḵem* (15, WLC), literally *against you
  and against your fathers*; the WEB's *as it was* interprets. The Septuagint is commonly reported to read *and
  against your king* (recalled, not checked), which would match verse 25. The WLC has *fathers*.
- **Wheat harvest … thunder and rain.** *Qəṣîr-ḥiṭṭîm … qōlôṯ ûmāṭār* (17, WLC). Wheat harvest is early summer
  (the *first fruits of wheat harvest* belong to the feast of weeks, Exodus 34:22), when rain was not expected; that is the commonly given reason the sign is
  a sign (the climate claim is recalled, not checked; the text says only *Isn't it wheat harvest today?*). The ark
  came home to Beth Shemesh at wheat harvest (6:13). *Yahweh sent thunder and rain*, *wayyittēn YHWH qōlōṯ
  ûmāṭār* (18, WLC), uses the verb and plural noun of the hail in Egypt, *Yahweh sent thunder, hail*, *YHWH nāṯan
  qōlōṯ ûḇārāḏ* (Exodus 9:23, WLC). In the WLC of Genesis–2 Samuel, the plural *qōlōṯ*, *thunders*, occurs only in
  Exodus 9, at Sinai (Exodus 19:16; 20:18), and here. See
  [[Thunders in Harvest - Exodus 9 and 1 Samuel 12]].
- **Feared Yahweh and Samuel.** *Wayyîrāʾ ḵol-hāʿām məʾōḏ ʾeṯ-YHWH wəʾeṯ-šəmûʾēl* (18, WLC). After the sea,
  *the people feared Yahweh; and they believed in Yahweh and in his servant Moses* (Exodus 14:31, stored WEB). The
  pairing of Yahweh with his prophet is the same. *Yārēʾ*, *fear*, runs through the chapter: *if you will fear*
  (14), *greatly feared* (18), *Don't be afraid* (20), *Only fear* (24). Samuel tells them not to fear and to
  fear in the same speech. See [[The Fear of God]].
- **Pray for your servants … that we not die.** *Hiṯpallēl bəʿaḏ-ʿăḇāḏeḵā* (19, WLC). The people ask the prophet
  to pray, as Pharaoh asked Moses in the hail, *Pray to Yahweh* (Exodus 9:28, stored WEB), though with a different
  verb (*ʿāṯar*, WLC). See [[Prayer]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].
- **Vain things which can't profit or deliver, for they are vain.** *Hattōhû … lōʾ-yôʿîlû wəlōʾ yaṣṣîlû kî-ṯōhû
  hēmmâ* (21, WLC). *Tōhû* is the word of *The earth was formless and empty* (Genesis 1:2) and of *the waste
  howling wilderness* (Deuteronomy 32:10) (stored WEB); in the WLC of Genesis–2 Samuel those are its only other
  occurrences (lemma 8414). *Yāʿal*, *profit*, occurs in those files only here (lemma 3276). *Can't deliver*,
  *lōʾ yaṣṣîlû*, uses *nāṣal*, the verb of *deliver us* in 10 and *delivered you* in 11: what Yahweh did by the
  judges, *tōhû* cannot do. The verse does not say what the vain things are; idols are the usual reading. See
  [[Vanity]] and [[Idolatry]].
- **Yahweh will not forsake his people for his great name's sake.** *Lōʾ-yiṭṭōš YHWH ʾeṯ-ʿammô baʿăḇûr šəmô
  haggāḏôl* (22, WLC). *Nāṭaš* is Gideon's word, *now Yahweh has cast us off* (Judges 6:13, stored WEB; WLC
  *nəṭāšānû*), and Deuteronomy's for Israel, *he abandoned God who made him* (32:15, WLC). Here Yahweh will not do
  to Israel what Israel did to him. *His great name* is Joshua's plea after Ai, *What will you do for your great
  name?* (Joshua 7:9, stored WEB; WLC *ləšimḵā haggāḏôl*). See [[The Names of God]].
- **It has pleased Yahweh to make you a people for himself.** *Hôʾîl YHWH* (22, WLC). *Hôʾîl* elsewhere means
  *undertake, be willing* (Genesis 18:27; Deuteronomy 1:5; Judges 19:6); in 2 Samuel 7:29 David asks Yahweh to
  *be pleased* to bless his house (lemma 2974). See [[Call and Election]].
- **Far be it from me that I should sin against Yahweh in ceasing to pray for you.** *Ḥālîlâ llî mēḥăṭōʾ
  laYHWH mēḥăḏōl ləhiṯpallēl baʿaḏḵem* (23, WLC). *Ḥālîlâ* is the people's word at Shechem, *Far be it from us that
  we should forsake Yahweh* (Joshua 24:16, stored WEB; WLC). Not praying is called a sin. *Ḥāḏal*, *cease*, is the
  verb of the thunders that *ceased* when Moses prayed (Exodus 9:29, 33–34, WLC). Psalm 99:6 sets *Samuel among
  those who call on his name*, and Jeremiah 15:1 names *Moses and Samuel* as those who might *stand before me*
  (WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-18; not stored). See
  [[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].
- **I will instruct you in the good and the right way.** *Wəhôrêṯî ʾeṯḵem bəḏereḵ haṭṭôḇâ wəhayšārâ* (23, WLC).
  *Hôrâ*, *teach*, is the verb from which *tôrâ* is commonly derived (recalled; the lexical link is standard but not
  checked here). Samuel is retiring from judging, not from teaching. See [[Law and Obedience]].
- **Serve him in truth with all your heart.** *Waʿăḇaḏtem ʾōṯô beʾĕmeṯ bəḵol-ləḇaḇḵem* (24, WLC). Joshua: *fear
  Yahweh, and serve him in sincerity and in truth* (Joshua 24:14, stored WEB; WLC *yərʾû … wəʿiḇḏû ʾōṯô bəṯāmîm
  ûḇeʾĕmeṯ*). Both have *fear* and *serve* and *truth*.
- **You will be consumed, both you and your king.** *Gam-ʾattem gam-malkəḵem tissāp̄û* (25, WLC). *Sāp̄â* in the
  Niphal is the word of the warnings at Sodom, *lest you be consumed* (Genesis 19:15, 17), and at Korah's tents
  (Numbers 16:26) (stored WEB; WLC). The speech's last word puts the king under the same sentence as the people.
  The book ends with Saul and his sons dead on Gilboa (31:1–6, stored WEB; not yet ingested); whether verse 25 is
  meant to anticipate that, the text does not say.

**How it connects.** The pericope closes the section that began in [[Israel Asks for a King]] and ran through
[[Saul Comes to the Seer]], [[Samuel Anoints Saul]], [[Saul Chosen by Lot at Mizpah]], and
[[Saul Rescues Jabesh Gilead]]. Its form is the farewell of a leader: Joshua's two speeches in
[[Joshua's Farewell to the Leaders]] and [[The Covenant at Shechem]] share its old age, its history, its witnesses,
its *far be it*, and its *fear Yahweh and serve him in truth*. Its history is the cycle of
[[Another Generation Arose]], and its confession is that of [[Israel's Repentance Under Ammon]]. Its sign goes back
to the plague of hail and Sinai, and its prayer to Moses' intercession. It also does not end Samuel's role: he
resigns as judge and keeps praying and teaching, and the next chapter brings him back to Gilgal to rebuke the king
(13:13, stored WEB; not yet ingested).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 12:1–25 as planned; 13:1 begins the account of Saul's reign. The speech's
location is left as the text leaves it, with Gilgal noted as implied. [[Bedan]] gets a page, because the name is a
standing puzzle that a reader will look for and the existing connection on Deborah and Samuel already raised it.
The king of Moab is linked to [[Eglon (person)]] only in the People list, as the referent Judges 3 supplies; the
speech does not name him. Two new connections:
[[Witnesses Against Yourselves - Joshua's Farewell and Samuel's]] and
[[Thunders in Harvest - Exodus 9 and 1 Samuel 12]]. Five existing connections that quoted or cited this chapter
before it was ingested are updated to link here.

**Unresolved.** Where the speech is given. How 12:12 relates to chapters 8–11. Who Bedan is. Whether verse 14 is a
complete condition. Whether verse 15 should read *fathers* or *king*. Whether *my sons are with you* defends them.
What the *vain things* of verse 21 are.
