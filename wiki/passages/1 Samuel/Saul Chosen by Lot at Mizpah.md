# Saul Chosen by Lot at Mizpah

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 10:17–27

## Summary

> **17** Samuel called the people together to Yahweh to Mizpah;
> **18** and he said to the children of Israel, "Yahweh, the God of Israel, says 'I brought Israel up out of
> Egypt, and I delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians, and out of the hand of all the kingdoms that
> oppressed you.'
> **19** But you have today rejected your God, who himself saves you out of all your calamities and your
> distresses; and you have said to him, 'No! Set a king over us.' Now therefore present yourselves before Yahweh
> by your tribes, and by your thousands."
> **20** So Samuel brought all the tribes of Israel near, and the tribe of Benjamin was chosen.
> **21** He brought the tribe of Benjamin near by their families; and the family of the Matrites was chosen. Then
> Saul the son of Kish was chosen; but when they looked for him, he could not be found.
> **22** Therefore they asked of Yahweh further, "Is there yet a man to come here?" Yahweh answered, "Behold, he
> has hidden himself among the baggage."
> **23** They ran and got him there. When he stood among the people, he was higher than any of the people from his
> shoulders and upward.
> **24** Samuel said to all the people, "Do you see him whom Yahweh has chosen, that there is no one like him among
> all the people?" All the people shouted, and said, "Long live the king!"
> **25** Then Samuel told the people the regulations of the kingdom, and wrote it in a book, and laid it up before
> Yahweh. Samuel sent all the people away, every man to his house.
> **26** Saul also went to his house to Gibeah; and the army went with him, whose hearts God had touched.
> **27** But certain worthless fellows said, "How could this man save us?" They despised him, and brought him no
> present. But he held his peace.

[[Samuel]] calls the people together *to Yahweh to Mizpah* (17), at [[Mizpeh]], where he had judged Israel and
prayed down the thunder (7:5–11). He opens with an oracle in the form of a history: Yahweh brought Israel up out
of [[Egypt]] and delivered them from the Egyptians and from *all the kingdoms that oppressed you* (18). *But you
have today rejected your God, who himself saves you*, and said *Set a king over us*. Then, with no pause, he
orders them to stand before Yahweh by tribes and by thousands (19).

The choosing goes from large to small. [[Benjamin]] is *chosen* from the tribes, then the [[Matrites]] from
Benjamin's families, then *Saul the son of Kish* (20–21). But Saul is not there. They ask Yahweh again, *Is there
yet a man to come here?*, and Yahweh answers, *Behold, he has hidden himself among the baggage* (22). They run
and bring him, and when he stands among the people he is taller than all of them from the shoulders up (23).
Samuel says, *Do you see him whom Yahweh has chosen, that there is no one like him among all the people?*, and
the people shout, *Long live the king!* (24).

Samuel then tells the people *the regulations of the kingdom*, writes them in a book, and lays the book up *before
Yahweh*, and sends everyone home (25). [[Saul]] goes home to [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], and with him go *the army …
whose hearts God had touched* (26). But [[Worthless Fellows (of Saul's time)]] say *How could this man save us?*;
they despise him and bring him no present, *But he held his peace* (27).

**What is going on.** The previous pericope made Saul *prince* in private, with no witness but Samuel. This one
makes him king in public, before all the tribes. The two scenes do not mention each other. Nobody at Mizpah is
told about the oil, and Saul says nothing about it. The private anointing and the public lot are two ways of
saying that Yahweh chose this man, and the narrative lets each stand by itself.

The assembly begins with a charge. Samuel's speech (18–19) is shaped like the judgment speeches of Judges: a
prophet recalls the exodus and the deliverance from oppressors (Judges 6:8–9; 10:11–12) and then says what Israel
did instead. Here the charge is *you have today rejected your God*, which repeats Yahweh's words to Samuel in
8:7, *they have rejected me*. Yet Samuel goes straight from the charge to the lot. The king is given in the same
breath as the rebuke, as at 8:22. The text does not say whether the lot is Yahweh's consent, Yahweh's judgment,
or both.

The lot is the procedure of Achan's discovery in Joshua 7, where the same verb moves from tribe to family to
household to man and ends with a guilty man who had hidden what he took. Here it ends with a man who has hidden
himself. The choice is Yahweh's (24), and it falls on someone who cannot be found. The narrative does not say why
he hid. Saul had kept *the matter of the kingdom* from his uncle (10:16); he called himself the least of the
smallest tribe (9:21); later Samuel will say *you were little in your own sight* (15:17, stored WEB; not yet
ingested). Modesty, fear, and reluctance are all possible readings, and the text supplies none.

The pericope ends with Israel divided over the king it asked for. Some go with him *whose hearts God had touched*.
Others ask *How could this man save us?* The people had wanted a king to *go out before us, and fight our
battles* (8:20), and the doubters say that this man cannot. The next pericope answers them: Nahash comes up
against Jabesh Gilead, the Jabesh elders send for someone *to save us* (11:3), and Saul does (stored WEB; not yet
ingested). After that victory the people ask for the doubters to be put to death (11:12).

**What is notable.**

- **Called together.** *Wayyaṣʿēq šəmûʾēl ʾeṯ-hāʿām ʾel-YHWH hammiṣpâ* (17, WLC). The verb is *ṣāʿaq*, *cry
  out*, in the causative: Samuel *made the people cry out*, that is, summoned them. In the WLC files searched
  (Genesis–2 Samuel and Proverbs), this is its only causative form. Its passive, *were called together*, gathers
  Israel to war in Judges (7:23–24; 10:17; 12:1) and again at 13:4. Its plain sense is the cry of the oppressed:
  Israel *cried* to Yahweh in Egypt (Exodus 14:10) and under Sisera (Judges 4:3) (lemma 6817). The cry that
  Yahweh heard (9:16) and the summons to choose a king use the same verb.
- **Mizpah again.** *Hammiṣpâ*, with the article, as at 7:5–6 and in Judges 20–21 (WLC). The tribes gathered
  here to punish Benjamin for Gibeah (Judges 20:1–3), and swore not to give their daughters to Benjamin (21:1).
  Now, at the same place, a man of Benjamin from Gibeah is chosen king. The wiki notes the irony and does not
  claim the narrator intends it. See [[Mizpeh]].
- **I brought Israel up out of Egypt.** *ʾĀnōḵî heʿĕlêṯî ʾeṯ-yiśrāʾēl mimmiṣrāyim wāʾaṣṣîl ʾeṯḵem miyyaḏ
  miṣrayim ûmiyyaḏ kol-hammamlāḵôṯ hallōḥăṣîm ʾeṯḵem* (18, WLC). The unnamed prophet of Judges 6:9 says
  *wāʾaṣṣîl ʾeṯḵem miyyaḏ miṣrayim ûmiyyaḏ kol-lōḥăṣêḵem* (WLC), *I delivered you out of the hand of the
  Egyptians, and out of the hand of all who oppressed you* (stored WEB). The two sentences agree word for word
  except that Samuel says *the kingdoms*, *hammamlāḵôṯ*, where the prophet said *all who oppressed you*. The
  oppressors are now called kingdoms, at the moment Israel asks for a king. *Lāḥaṣ*, *oppress*, is the verb of
  Exodus 3:9 and of the cycles of Judges (2:18; 4:3; 10:12) (WLC, lemma 3905).
- **You have today rejected your God.** *Wəʾattem hayyôm məʾastem ʾeṯ-ʾĕlōhêḵem* (19, WLC). *Māʾas* was Yahweh's
  word at 8:7, *they have rejected me*. Samuel now says it to the people's faces. See
  [[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]].
- **Who himself saves you.** *ʾĂšer-hûʾ môšîaʿ lāḵem* (19, WLC). God is the one who saves (*yāšaʿ*). At 9:16
  Yahweh said the new prince *will save my people*, and at 10:27 the doubters ask *How could this man save us?*,
  *mah-yōšiʿēnû zeh* (WLC). The same verb appears three times: of God, of the promised king, and in the doubters'
  question. 11:3 brings it back, *if there is no one to save us* (*môšîaʿ*, WLC).
- **"No! Set a king over us."** The WLC reads *wattōʾmərû lô kî-meleḵ tāśîm ʿālênû* (19), *you said to him,
  "But a king you shall set over us"*. *Lô* is *to him*. The WEB's *No!* matches the reading *lōʾ*, *no*,
  which some manuscripts and versions are reported to have here, and which is the wording at 8:19 and 12:12.
  That report is recalled and not checked. The verb *śîm*, *set*, is the verb of Deuteronomy 17:14–15 and of the
  elders' request at 8:5. See [[Like All the Nations - Deuteronomy 17.14 and 1 Samuel 8.5, 20]].
- **Was chosen.** *Wayyillāḵēḏ*, three times (20–21, WLC), the passive of *lāḵaḏ*, *capture, take*. The WEB
  translates it *was chosen*; in Joshua 7:16–18 and 1 Samuel 14:41–42 the WEB gives the same form as *was
  selected*. In the WLC files searched, this passive is used of a lot or a divine selection only in those three
  passages (lemma 3920). The text does not name the means, and the wiki does not assume lots, the Urim and
  Thummim, or something else. See [[Taken by Lot - Achan, Saul, and Jonathan]] and
  [[The Lot Is Cast into the Lap - Proverbs 16.33 and the Casting of Lots]].
- **By families; the Matrites.** At 10:21 the WLC has a *kethiv* and a *qere*, a written form and a form to be
  read: *ləmišpaḥtô*, *by his family*, and *ləmišpəḥōṯāyw*, *by its families*. The WEB follows the second.
  *Hammaṭrî* occurs only here in the WLC files searched (lemma 4309), and no Matri appears in Benjamin's
  genealogies in the books stored so far (Genesis 46:21; Numbers 26:38–41). Saul is said to come from Kish's
  house (9:1), but the text skips the household step that Joshua 7:14, 18 includes. See [[Matrites]].
- **He could not be found.** *Wayḇaqšuhû wəlōʾ nimṣāʾ* (21, WLC). Saul set out to *seek* the donkeys (*ləḇaqqēš*,
  10:14, WLC). The donkeys *have been found*, *nimṣāʾû* (9:20; 10:2, 16, WLC). Now the people seek Saul, and he
  is not found. The searcher has become the one searched for.
- **They asked of Yahweh further.** *Wayyišʾălû-ʿôḏ baYHWH* (22, WLC). *Šāʾal*, the root of Saul's name, with
  *bə-*, *ask of* or *inquire of* Yahweh, the form used for seeking an oracle. The people asked Samuel for a king
  (8:10); now they ask Yahweh where the king is. See [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].
- **He has hidden himself among the baggage.** *Hinnēh-hûʾ neḥbāʾ ʾel-hakkēlîm* (22, WLC). *Kēlîm* is a general
  word for gear, vessels, or equipment. *Neḥbāʾ*, *he has hidden himself*, is the same form used of Jotham, the
  youngest son, who *was left; for he hid himself* when Abimelech made himself king (Judges 9:5, WLC; stored WEB).
  The first would-be king in Judges killed his brothers to rule; the first king in Samuel hides from the crown.
  The wiki notes the shared word and does not claim an allusion. Adam *hid* from Yahweh in the garden with the
  same verb (Genesis 3:8, 10, lemma 2244).
- **Higher than any of the people.** *Wayyiḡbah mikkol-hāʿām miššiḵmô wāmāʿəlâ* (23, WLC), echoing the narrator's
  introduction, *miššiḵmô wāmaʿlâ gāḇōah mikkol-hāʿām* (9:2, WLC). Samuel's question, *Do you see …?*, asks the
  people to look, and what they can see is his height. When the next king is chosen, Yahweh will tell Samuel
  *Don't look on his face, or on the height of his stature … man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh
  looks at the heart* (16:7, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **Him whom Yahweh has chosen.** *Hărəʾîṯem ʾăšer bāḥar-bô YHWH* (24, WLC). Deuteronomy's king is the one
  *ʾăšer yiḇḥar YHWH … bô* (17:15, WLC), *whom Yahweh your God chooses* (stored WEB): same verb, same construction.
  At 8:18 the people were the subject: *your king whom you will have chosen for yourselves* (*ʾăšer
  bəḥartem lāḵem*, WLC). At 12:13 Samuel will put both side by side: *the king whom you have chosen … Yahweh has
  set a king over you* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **There is no one like him.** *Kî ʾên kāmōhû bəḵol-hāʿām* (24, WLC). In the stored books, *there is none like*
  is mostly said of Yahweh (for example Exodus 8:10; Deuteronomy 33:26, stored WEB). Here it is said of the king.
  The wiki does not read it as a claim to divinity. It notes only that the language of incomparability, used of
  Yahweh, is applied to the king Israel chose instead of him.
- **Long live the king!** *Wayyāriʿû kol-hāʿām wayyōʾmərû yəḥî hammeleḵ* (24, WLC). This is the first
  acclamation of a king of Israel. *Rûaʿ*, *shout*, is the war cry of Jericho (Joshua 6:5, 20) and of Gideon's
  men (Judges 7:21). It is also the shout that greeted the ark at Ebenezer just before the defeat (4:5) (lemma
  7321). *Yəḥî*, the jussive *let him live*, appears in the files searched only here, in Moses' blessing *Let
  Reuben live* (Deuteronomy 33:6), and in Hushai's *Long live the king* to Absalom (2 Samuel 16:16, WLC; not
  stored). 1 Kings uses the same acclamation for Solomon (cited from memory; not stored and not checked).
- **The regulations of the kingdom, written in a book.** *Mišpaṭ hammelūḵâ* (25, WLC). At 8:9, 11 Samuel told
  them *mišpaṭ hammeleḵ*, *the way of the king*, a list of what a king will take. Now it is *the mišpāṭ of the
  kingdom*, and it is written down. The text does not give its contents. Deuteronomy's king must *write himself a
  copy of this law in a book* (*wəḵāṯaḇ lô … ʿal-sēp̄er*, 17:18, WLC; stored WEB), and Moses had the book of the
  law put *by the side of the ark* (31:26, stored WEB). Here Samuel writes and *laid it up before Yahweh*
  (*wayyannaḥ*). Whether this book is a version of Deuteronomy 17:14–20, a statement of the king's rights like
  8:11–17, or something else, the text does not say. The wiki does not decide. *Məlūḵâ*, *kingship*, appears here
  for the second time in the WLC files searched, after *the matter of the kingdom* (10:16).
- **Whose hearts God had touched.** *Heḥāyil ʾăšer-nāḡaʿ ʾĕlōhîm bəlibbām* (26, WLC). *Ḥayil* can mean an army,
  strength, or men of worth. The WEB has *the army*, and some translations have *valiant men*; this is a
  translation choice, not a textual variant. *Nāḡaʿ*, *touch*, is the verb the Philistine priests used for
  Yahweh's hand, *his hand that struck us* (6:9, WLC). Here God touches hearts, and the narrator does not say how.
  At 10:9 God gave Saul *another heart*.
- **Worthless fellows.** *Ûḇənê ḇəliyyaʿal* (27, WLC), *sons of bəliyyaʿal*. The same phrase names the men of
  Gibeah who surrounded the house in Judges 19:22 and whom the tribes demanded from Benjamin at Mizpah (20:13),
  as well as the sons of Eli (1 Samuel 2:12). Deuteronomy 13:13 (WLC 13:14) uses it for those who lead a town
  to other gods. Saul goes home to Gibeah, and some who reject him are called by the word once used of Gibeah's
  men. See [[Sons of Belial - Worthless Men from Deuteronomy to Samuel]].
- **They despised him.** *Wayyiḇzuhû* (27, WLC). *Bāzâ* is the word of the oracle against Eli's house: *those who
  despise me will be cursed* (2:30, *ûḇōzay*, WLC). It returns when Goliath *disdained* David (17:42, stored WEB;
  WLC) (lemma 959).
- **No present.** *Wəlōʾ-hēḇîʾû lô minḥâ* (27, WLC). *Minḥâ* is the ordinary word for a gift brought to a superior,
  and it is also the grain offering. In 1 Samuel it was used for the *offering of Yahweh* that Eli's sons
  despised (2:17, 29). Bringing a present acknowledges the king's rank, and withholding it refuses that. The
  text gives no further detail.
- **But he held his peace.** *Wayhî kəmaḥărîš* (27, WLC), *and he was as one keeping silent*. Saul was silent
  about the kingdom at 10:16, and he is silent now about the insult. At 11:13, after the victory, he refuses to
  let anyone be put to death (stored WEB; not yet ingested). Two things are commonly reported about this
  half-verse and were not checked here. First, the Septuagint is said to read *after about a month* instead, a
  difference of vowels and one letter (*kəmēḥōḏeš*). Second, a Dead Sea Scrolls manuscript of Samuel
  (4QSamᵃ) is said to have a longer paragraph between 10:27 and 11:1 about Nahash's earlier oppression of Gad
  and Reuben. Neither text was consulted. The WEB follows the Masoretic text.

**How it connects.** The public lot completes the private anointing of [[Samuel Anoints Saul]] and answers the
request of [[Israel Asks for a King]] in that chapter's own words: *rejected*, *set a king*, *chosen*, and *the
regulations* (*mišpāṭ*). The procedure goes back to Achan
([[Taken by Lot - Achan, Saul, and Jonathan]]), and the same procedure will later point to Saul's own son at
14:42. The language of choosing and of the written book goes back to the law of the king
([[Like All the Nations - Deuteronomy 17.14 and 1 Samuel 8.5, 20]]). The worthless fellows call back Gibeah
([[Sons of Belial - Worthless Men from Deuteronomy to Samuel]]), and the summons to Mizpah calls back the war
against Benjamin held at the same place. The next pericope, at Jabesh Gilead, tests whether *this man* can save.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 10:17–27 as planned. 11:1 opens a new episode at Jabesh Gilead, and the
WEB follows the Masoretic text in leaving no bridge between them. The Matrites get a collective page, following
the wiki's other family pages (for example [[Japhletites]]). The doubters get a page qualified by period,
[[Worthless Fellows (of Saul's time)]], because they come back at 11:12. *The army … whose hearts God had touched*
gets no page. Two new connections were made. [[Taken by Lot - Achan, Saul, and Jonathan]] collects the three
passages where the passive *lāḵaḏ* marks someone as chosen. [[Sons of Belial - Worthless Men from Deuteronomy to Samuel]]
traces *bəliyyaʿal* through the stored books. The existing lot thread page now points to the new page and is
not repeated in it.

**Unresolved.** How the private anointing (10:1) and the public lot (10:20–24) relate, since neither mentions the
other. Why Saul hid. What was in *the regulations of the kingdom* (10:25), and how they relate to 8:11–17 and to
Deuteronomy 17:14–20. Whether *Long live the king* answers Samuel's rebuke or ignores it. Whether *ḥayil* is an
army or men of worth. How *wayhî kəmaḥărîš* should be read beside the reported Greek and Qumran readings. None of
these is settled here.
