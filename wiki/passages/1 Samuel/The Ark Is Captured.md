# The Ark Is Captured

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 4:1b–22

## Summary

> **4:1b** Now Israel went out against the Philistines to battle, and encamped beside Ebenezer; and the
> Philistines encamped in Aphek.
> **2** The Philistines put themselves in array against Israel. When they joined battle, Israel was
> defeated by the Philistines, who killed about four thousand men of the army in the field.
> **3** When the people had come into the camp, the elders of Israel said, "Why has Yahweh defeated us
> today before the Philistines? Let us get the ark of Yahweh's covenant out of Shiloh and bring it to us,
> that it may come among us, and save us out of the hand of our enemies."
> **4** So the people sent to Shiloh; and they brought from there the ark of the covenant of Yahweh of
> Armies, who sits above the cherubim: and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were there with the
> ark of the covenant of God.
> **5** When the ark of Yahweh's covenant came into the camp, all Israel shouted with a great shout, so
> that the earth rang again.
> **6** When the Philistines heard the noise of the shout, they said, "What does the noise of this great
> shout in the camp of the Hebrews mean?" They understood that Yahweh's ark had come into the camp.
> **7** The Philistines were afraid, for they said, "God has come into the camp." They said, "Woe to us!
> For there has not been such a thing before.
> **8** Woe to us! Who shall deliver us out of the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods that
> struck the Egyptians with all kinds of plagues in the wilderness.
> **9** Be strong, and behave like men, O you Philistines, that you not be servants to the Hebrews, as
> they have been to you. Strengthen yourselves like men, and fight!"
> **10** The Philistines fought, and Israel was defeated, and each man fled to his tent. There was a very
> great slaughter; for thirty thousand footmen of Israel fell.
> **11** God's ark was taken; and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain.
> **12** A man of Benjamin ran out of the army, and came to Shiloh the same day, with his clothes torn,
> and with dirt on his head.
> **13** When he came, behold, Eli was sitting on his seat by the road watching; for his heart trembled
> for God's ark. When the man came into the city and told about it, all the city cried out.
> **14** When Eli heard the noise of the crying, he said, "What does the noise of this tumult mean?" The
> man hurried, and came and told Eli.
> **15** Now Eli was ninety-eight years old. His eyes were set, so that he could not see.
> **16** The man said to Eli, "I am he who came out of the army, and I fled today out of the army." He
> said, "How did the matter go, my son?"
> **17** He who brought the news answered, "Israel has fled before the Philistines, and there has been
> also a great slaughter among the people. Your two sons also, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead, and God's
> ark has been captured."
> **18** When he made mention of God's ark, Eli fell from off his seat backward by the side of the gate;
> and his neck broke, and he died; for he was an old man, and heavy. He had judged Israel forty years.
> **19** His daughter-in-law, Phinehas' wife, was with child, near to be delivered. When she heard the
> news that God's ark was taken, and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, she bowed herself
> and gave birth; for her pains came on her.
> **20** About the time of her death the women who stood by her said to her, "Don't be afraid; for you
> have given birth to a son." But she didn't answer, neither did she regard it.
> **21** She named the child Ichabod, saying, "The glory has departed from Israel"; because God's ark was
> taken, and because of her father-in-law and her husband.
> **22** She said, "The glory has departed from Israel; for God's ark has been taken."

Israel goes out against the Philistines and camps *beside Ebenezer*; the Philistines camp at [[Aphek]]. The
first battle costs Israel *about four thousand men* (4:1b–2). The elders ask *Why has Yahweh defeated us
today?*, and answer their own question with a plan: bring *the ark of Yahweh's covenant out of Shiloh … that
it may come among us, and save us* (3). The ark comes from [[Shiloh]], *the ark of the covenant of Yahweh of
Armies, who sits above the cherubim*, and with it *the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas* (4). Israel
shouts *with a great shout, so that the earth rang again* (5). The Philistines hear it, learn that the ark
has come, and are afraid: *God has come into the camp … Who shall deliver us out of the hand of these mighty
gods? These are the gods that struck the Egyptians with all kinds of plagues in the wilderness* (6–8). They
rally one another, *Be strong, and behave like men … that you not be servants to the Hebrews, as they have
been to you* (9), and fight. Israel is routed, *thirty thousand footmen* fall, *God's ark was taken; and the
two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain* (10–11).

*A man of Benjamin* runs from the battle to Shiloh the same day, *with his clothes torn, and with dirt on
his head* (12). [[Eli]] is sitting on his seat by the road, watching, *for his heart trembled for God's ark*.
The city cries out; Eli, ninety-eight and blind, asks *What does the noise of this tumult mean?* (13–15). The
runner tells him in four steps: Israel has fled, there has been a great slaughter, *Your two sons also,
Hophni and Phinehas, are dead, and God's ark has been captured* (16–17). *When he made mention of God's ark,
Eli fell from off his seat backward by the side of the gate; and his neck broke, and he died; for he was an
old man, and heavy. He had judged Israel forty years* (18). Phinehas' wife, near her time, hears the news and
goes into labor. As she dies the women with her say *Don't be afraid; for you have given birth to a son*,
and she neither answers nor heeds it (19–20). She names the child Ichabod: *The glory has departed from
Israel; for God's ark has been taken* (21–22).

**What is going on.** This is the day the sign falls. The man of God had told Eli *in one day they will both
die* (2:34), and Yahweh had told Samuel *I will perform against Eli all that I have spoken concerning his
house* (3:12). Both come true here in the order of the runner's news, and the chapter adds what neither
oracle had named: the ark itself goes into Philistine hands. The narrator never says why Israel lost. The
elders ask (3) and do not wait for an answer; the reader has had it since 2:25 and 3:13–14. Read with what
precedes it, the defeat is the judgment on Eli's house, carried out on all Israel, and the ark that was
fetched to *save us* (3) is taken because the house that kept it was condemned. That reading is the
sequence's, not a sentence in the chapter.

The pericope is built as two scenes, the battlefield (1b–11) and Shiloh (12–22), and both turn on hearing a
noise. The Philistines hear Israel's shout and ask what it means (6); Eli hears the city's cry and asks the
same question in the same form, *meh qôl* … *what is the noise of* (6, 14, WLC). In each the answer is the
ark. [[Samuel]], whose word *came to all Israel* in the verse before (4:1a), does not appear at all until
7:3 (stored WEB; not yet ingested); no one consults Yahweh or his prophet before the ark is sent for.

**What is notable.**

- **The stone of help.** *Hāʾeḇen hāʿēzer* (4:1, WLC), *the stone of the help*: the WEB's *Ebenezer*. The
  place is named here by anticipation. At 7:12 Samuel sets up a stone *and called its name Ebenezer, saying,
  "Yahweh helped us until now"* (stored WEB; not yet ingested), and the WLC spells all three occurrences
  (4:1; 5:1; 7:12) with the same lemma. The first battle at the *stone of help* is the battle where no help
  comes. Whether 4:1 and 7:12 name the same spot, 7:12 sets its stone *between Mizpah and Shen*, is debated;
  see [[Ebenezer]].
- **Why has Yahweh defeated us?** *Lāmmâ nəḡāp̄ānû YHWH* (3, WLC). *Nāḡap̄*, *strike, defeat*, is the verb
  of the covenant curses, *you will be struck before your enemies* (Leviticus 26:17; Deuteronomy 28:25;
  stored WEB). Here the elders put Yahweh as its subject, which is correct, and then treat the ark as the
  remedy. See [[Struck Before Your Enemies - Leviticus 26, Numbers 14, Deuteronomy 28, and 1 Kings 8]].
- **That it may come among us.** *Wəyāḇōʾ bəqirbēnû wəyōšiʿēnû* (3, WLC): the subject of *come* and *save*
  is grammatically the ark, or Yahweh through it; the Hebrew does not decide. Numbers 14:44 had Israel
  going up *nevertheless* without the ark and being beaten down to Hormah; here they bring the ark and are
  beaten worse. See [[Is Yahweh Among Us - Exodus 17, Numbers 14, 1 Samuel 4, and Micah 3]].
- **Yahweh of Armies, who sits above the cherubim.** *YHWH ṣəḇāʾôṯ yōšēḇ hakkərubîm* (4, WLC). The full
  title is given at the moment the ark is carried into a battle it will lose. The cherubim of the mercy seat
  (Exodus 25:18–22) become a throne in this title; see [[Cherubim]]. The next clause sets Hophni and
  Phinehas *there with the ark*, the condemned priests beside the enthroned God.
- **A great shout.** *Tərûʿâ ḡəḏôlâ* (5, WLC), the phrase of Jericho, where *the people shouted with a great
  shout, and the wall fell down flat* (Joshua 6:5, 20; WLC; stored WEB). The ark was at Jericho too. The
  same shout before the same ark brings no wall down here. *The earth rang again*, *wattēhōm hāʾāreṣ*: the
  Niphal of *hûm* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel only here and at Ruth 1:19, where *all
  the city was moved* at Naomi's return (lemma search). See
  [[Horns, a Shout, and a Fallen Enemy - Jericho and the Camp of Midian]].
- **The Hebrews.** *The camp of the Hebrews* (6); *servants to the Hebrews, as they have been to you* (9).
  *ʿIḇrîm* is what foreigners call Israel, here and through the Philistine chapters; see
  [[Abram the Hebrew - The First Use of the Name]]. Verse 9 also tells the reader that Israel had been
  serving the Philistines, the situation of Judges 13:1, *Yahweh delivered them into the hand of the
  Philistines forty years* (stored WEB).
- **God, or gods.** The Philistines say *ʾĕlōhîm bāʾ*, *God has come*, with a singular verb (7), then *these
  mighty gods*, *hāʾĕlōhîm hāʾaddîrîm hāʾēlleh*, with plural adjective and demonstrative (8, WLC). They
  speak as polytheists, and the narrator lets them. They also know the exodus, and garble it: the gods
  *that struck the Egyptians with all kinds of plagues in the wilderness* (8). The plagues fell in Egypt;
  *in the wilderness*, *bammiḏbār* (WLC), is either a Philistine confusion or a compressed reference to the
  whole deliverance, and commentators take it both ways. Rahab's Canaanites had also heard (Joshua 2:9–11);
  the Philistines fear, and fight anyway. See [[Egypt]] and [[The Nations]].
- **Behave like men.** *Hiṯḥazzəqû wihyû laʾănāšîm* (9, WLC), repeated *wihyîṯem laʾănāšîm*, framing their
  fear. The enemies speak courage to each other; no one in Israel's camp speaks at all after the elders.
- **Each man fled to his tent.** *Wayyānusû ʾîš ləʾōhālāw* (10, WLC): the army dissolves. *Thirty thousand*,
  where the first battle cost four thousand (2). The ark that was to make the difference made the defeat
  larger.
- **God's ark was taken.** *Waʾărôn ʾĕlōhîm nilqāḥ* (11, WLC). From verse 11 to the end it is only *God's
  ark*, *ʾărôn (hā)ʾĕlōhîm*, seven times (11, 13, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22); *the ark of Yahweh's covenant* is the
  name in 3–5, the elders' plan and its arrival (WLC). The shift is observable; whether it means
  anything is a reader's question. The capture verb, *lāqaḥ* in the Niphal, recurs in 11, 17, 19, 21, and
  22: the elders had said *let us get*, *niqḥâ*, the ark (3), with the same verb.
- **The man of Benjamin.** *His clothes torn, and with dirt on his head*, *ûmaddāw qərūʿîm waʾăḏāmâ ʿal-
  rōʾšô* (12, WLC). The same description, nearly word for word, introduces the Amalekite who brings David
  the news of Saul's death: *with his clothes torn, and earth on his head* (2 Samuel 1:2; WEB checked at
  bible-api.com; WLC). The Philistines will carry the news of Saul's death *to the house of their idols*
  (31:9, stored WEB). The first battle report of 1 Samuel and the report of Saul's death that opens 2
  Samuel come by runners described alike. Joshua and the elders had *put dust on their heads* before the ark after Ai
  (Joshua 7:6). See [[Man of Benjamin (of Eli's time)]] and [[Mourning and Lament]].
- **The one who brought the news.** *Hamməḇaśśēr* (17, WLC), the Piel participle of *bāśar*, *bring news*,
  in later books the word of good news. In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel the verb occurs only
  here and at 31:9 (lemma search): the first news carried in the stored text is of Israel's defeat.
- **Sitting on his seat by the road.** *ʿal-hakkissēʾ* (13, WLC), the *seat* or *throne* of 1:9 and of Hannah's
  *throne of glory*, *kissēʾ ḵāḇôḏ* (2:8, WLC). The consonantal text (ketiv) of 13 reads *yk*; the
  Masoretic reading (qere) is *yaḏ*, *beside*, which the WEB follows (WLC, both recorded). Eli is placed
  where he was when the book opened, now watching the road. *His heart trembled for God's ark*, *ḥārēḏ*:
  his fear is for the ark before his sons, and the narrative keeps that order.
- **What is the noise?** Eli's question, *meh qôl hehāmôn hazzeh* (14, WLC), repeats the Philistines' *meh
  qôl hattərûʿâ* (6). His eyes *were set*, *qāmâ* (15), so he can only hear: the dimming of 3:2 is now
  complete.
- **The news in rising order.** *Israel has fled … a great slaughter … Your two sons … are dead, and God's ark
  has been captured* (17). The runner's report climbs from the army to the house to the ark. *Great
  slaughter* is *maggēpâ ḡəḏôlâ* (WLC), the word of the plagues that Aaron and [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]]
  stopped (Numbers 16:48–50, Hebrew 17:13–15; 25:8–9; stored WEB; lemma search). The Phinehas of Numbers
  stayed a *maggēpâ*; this Phinehas dies in one. The pairing is a reader's. See
  [[Two Priests Stop the Plague - Aaron and Phinehas]].
- **When he made mention of God's ark.** Eli survives the news of his sons; it is *God's ark* that kills
  him (18). He falls *backward*, *ʾăḥōrannîṯ*, *by the side of the gate*, *bəʿaḏ yaḏ haššaʿar*, and his
  neck breaks, *mapraqtô*: *maprəqeṯ*, *neck*, occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel only
  here (lemma search).
- **Old, and heavy.** *Kî-zāqēn hāʾîš wəḵāḇēḏ* (18, WLC). *Zāqēn* answers *there will not be an old man in
  your house* (2:31): Eli is the old man, and his house ends its old age with him. *Kāḇēḏ*, *heavy*, is
  the root of *you honor*, *wattəḵabbēḏ*, *your sons above me* (2:29) and of *those who honor me I will
  honor* (2:30, WLC), and of *kāḇôḏ*, *glory*, in Ichabod's name three verses on (21). The man who made his
  sons *heavy* dies *heavy*; the *glory* departs. The words share a root (morphhb lemmas 3513, 3515, 3519);
  the wordplay is widely noted, and the text does not comment on it. See [[The Glory of God]].
- **He had judged Israel forty years.** *Wəhûʾ šāp̄aṭ ʾeṯ-yiśrāʾēl ʾarbāʿîm šānâ* (18, WLC), the formula of
  the judges (e.g. Judges 12:7; 15:20; 16:31; stored WEB). It is the first time Eli is called a judge. The
  Septuagint is commonly reported to read *twenty years* here (recalled, not checked).
- **Don't be afraid; for you have given birth to a son.** *ʾAl-tîrʾî kî ḇēn yālāḏt* (20, WLC). Rachel's
  midwife said *ʾal-tîrʾî kî-ḡam-zeh lāḵ bēn*, *Don't be afraid, for now you will have another son*, as Rachel
  died naming her son Benoni (Genesis 35:17–18; WLC; stored WEB). Both women die in childbirth and name the
  child for the grief, not the birth. Rachel's son was renamed by his father; Ichabod's father is dead.
  Phinehas' wife *didn't answer, neither did she regard it*, *wəlōʾ šāṯâ libbāh*, *did not set her heart*
  (20, WLC). See [[Do Not Be Afraid, You Have a Son - Rachel and Phinehas' Wife]].
- **Ichabod.** *ʾÎ-ḵāḇôḏ* (21, WLC; spelled as one word at 14:3). The meaning of the first element is
  debated: *no glory* or *where is the glory?* Morphhb tags it as a particle; the Job page discusses the rare
  *ʾî* of Job 22:30, *ʾî-nāqî* (see [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]]). The text supplies the explanation itself:
  *The glory has departed from Israel* (21). Ichabod reappears once, as the uncle of Ahijah, *the son of
  Ahitub, Ichabod's brother* (14:3, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Ichabod]] and
  [[Names and Naming]].
- **The glory has gone into exile.** *Gālâ ḵāḇôḏ miyyiśrāʾēl* (21, 22, WLC). *Gālâ* in the Qal is the verb
  of *the captivity of the land* (Judges 18:30, stored WEB; WLC), and the same lemma, in the Niphal, was
  *Yahweh revealed himself to Samuel in Shiloh* (3:21) and *Did I reveal myself to the house of your
  father?* (2:27) (WLC). Two verses after Yahweh is *revealed* at Shiloh, the glory *departs* from it. The
  verb is the same lemma in different stems; whether the narrator intended the echo is not recoverable,
  and the lexical data are reported. Ezekiel later sees *Yahweh's glory* go out *from over the threshold of
  the house* (Ezekiel 10:18, WEB checked at bible-api.com), with a different verb, *yāṣāʾ* (WLC). See
  [[The Glory of God]].
- **Said twice.** She gives the reason twice, the second time without her husband and father-in-law: *The
  glory has departed from Israel; for God's ark has been taken* (22). The narrator's own reason (21) lists
  all three; her last words are about the ark alone.

**How it connects.** The chapter fulfils the sign of
[[A Man of God Against the House of Eli]] (2:34) and begins the sentence of [[Yahweh Calls Samuel]] (3:12),
whose word *both the ears of everyone who hears it will tingle* (3:11) is this news. It continues
[[The Sins of Eli's Sons]], where Yahweh *intended to kill them* (2:25), and it turns Hannah's reversals in
[[The Song of Hannah]] on the priest who heard her: the one who sat on the *seat* falls from it. The ark
will travel through the Philistine cities and humble Dagon (5:1–12; see
[[The Ark Among the Philistines]] and
[[Dagon Fallen - Samson's Pillars and the Ark in Ashdod]]), and come home without Shiloh (6:1–7:1; see [[The Ark Returns to Israel]]). Shiloh
itself drops out of the narrative as a sanctuary; Psalm 78 and Jeremiah 7 read this day as the time Yahweh
*abandoned the tent of Shiloh*. See [[Shiloh Forsaken - 1 Samuel 4, Psalm 78.60-64, and Jeremiah 7.12-14]].
The Philistine war that Samson *began* (Judges 13:5) is now Israel's open defeat; see
[[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 4:1b–22 as planned; the battle and the news at Shiloh are one day and
one unit. *Ebenezer* gets its own place page now, though 7:12 is where it is explained, because 4:1 and 5:1
already use the name. Phinehas' wife, Ichabod, and the man of Benjamin get person pages; the women standing
by her (20) do not, being a single line with no action but speech. The wife's page is named
*Phinehas' Wife* in the WEB's own form, on the model of *Lot's Wife* and *Manoah's Wife*; the earlier
Phinehas, son of Eleazar, has no wife in the text, so the name is not ambiguous.

**Unresolved.** Whether the subject of *come* and *save* in 3 is the ark or Yahweh; whether *in the
wilderness* (8) is a Philistine error; the meaning of *ʾî-* in *Ichabod*; whether the *kāḇēḏ* / *kāḇôḏ*
and *gālâ* echoes (18, 21; 2:27; 3:21) are intended; and whether the Ebenezer of 4:1 is the site Samuel
names at 7:12. Each is reported above, not settled.
