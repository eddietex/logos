# Jonathan Speaks for David

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 19:1–7

## Summary

> **19:1** Saul spoke to Jonathan his son, and to all his servants, that they should kill David. But Jonathan, Saul’s
> son, greatly delighted in David.
> **2** Jonathan told David, saying, “Saul my father seeks to kill you. Now therefore, please take care of yourself in
> the morning, and live in a secret place, and hide yourself.
> **3** I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will talk with my father about you;
> and if I see anything, I will tell you.”
> **4** Jonathan spoke good of David to Saul his father, and said to him, “Don’t let the king sin against his servant,
> against David; because he has not sinned against you, and because his works have been very good toward you;
> **5** for he put his life in his hand, and struck the Philistine, and Yahweh worked a great victory for all Israel.
> You saw it, and rejoiced. Why then will you sin against innocent blood, to kill David without a cause?”
> **6** Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan: and Saul swore, “As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death.”
> **7** Jonathan called David, and Jonathan showed him all those things. Then Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was
> in his presence, as before.

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 19.)

What [[Saul]] had kept in his thoughts through chapter 18, *let the hand of the Philistines be on him* (18:17, 21, 25),
he now says aloud: he *spoke to Jonathan his son, and to all his servants, that they should kill David* (19:1). The
same verse sets the son against the order. *But Jonathan, Saul's son, greatly delighted in David.*

[[Jonathan (son of Saul)]] warns [[David]] first: *Saul my father seeks to kill you*, so in the morning he is to stay
*in a secret place, and hide yourself* (2). Jonathan will go out with his father *in the field where you are*, speak
about him, and report what he sees (3). Then he speaks *good of David* to Saul (4). The plea has three parts. Its
charge is *Don't let the king sin against his servant*. Its grounds are that David *has not sinned against you*, that
*his works have been very good toward you*, and that *he put his life in his hand, and struck the Philistine, and
Yahweh worked a great victory for all Israel* (4–5). Its appeal is to what Saul himself saw: *You saw it, and
rejoiced*. It ends on a question: *Why then will you sin against innocent blood, to kill David without a cause?* (5).

*Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan*, and swore, *As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death* (6). Jonathan
calls David, tells him *all those things*, and brings him to Saul, *and he was in his presence, as before* (7).

**Where it happens.** The pericope names no town. Saul's home is [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] (10:26; 15:34, stored WEB), and
David's house, where Saul's messengers will watch him that night, is in the same unnamed town (19:11, stored WEB; not
yet ingested), but 19:1–7 places nothing there. The one place is *the field where you are*, *baśśāḏeh ʾăšer ʾattâ
šām* (3, WLC), unnamed. The wiki gives the pericope no place page.

**What is going on.** This is the first time in the book that anyone speaks to Saul for David, and it works. The
pericope is built on the verb *spoke*, *dibber*. Saul *spoke … that they should kill David* (1); Jonathan *will speak*
about David to his father (3); Jonathan *spoke good of David* (4). Saul's order and Jonathan's answer use one verb. The
one who reports is also Jonathan. He *told*, *wayyaggēḏ*, David the danger (2), promises *I will tell you* (3), and
*told him* the outcome (7), the *hiphil* of *nāḡaḏ* three times (WLC). He stands between the two men and carries words
both ways.

The plea is a legal argument. It names the act Saul proposes as *sin*, *ḥāṭāʾ*, three times: *Don't let the king sin
against his servant*; *he has not sinned against you*; *why then will you sin against innocent blood* (4–5, WLC). It
turns the king into the possible offender and David into the innocent party. It cites the evidence of Elah, and it
names the witness: *You saw it, and rejoiced* (5). Then it uses the vocabulary of Deuteronomy's homicide law, *innocent
blood*, *dām nāqî* (5, WLC), the blood that the cities of refuge exist to keep from being shed *in the middle of your
land* (Deuteronomy 19:10, stored WEB). Jonathan does not appeal to love or to the covenant of 18:3. He appeals to the
law and to the record.

And Saul listens. *Wayyišmaʿ šāʾûl bǝqôl yǝhônāṯān* (6, WLC). The idiom is the book's word for obedience. Samuel
opened the Amalek command with *listen to the voice of the words of Yahweh* (15:1); Saul claimed *I have obeyed the
voice of Yahweh* (15:20) and then confessed *I feared the people, and obeyed their voice* (15:24) (stored WEB; WLC).
Eli's sons *didn't listen to the voice of their father* (2:25). Here a father listens to the voice of his son. Saul
seals it with an oath, the second of his three by *As Yahweh lives*. The first, at Michmash, swore Jonathan's death and
was overruled by the people (14:39, 45). This one swears David's life, and it lasts three verses: at 19:10 the spear
flies again, and at 19:11 Saul sends men to kill David *in the morning* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
[[As Yahweh Lives - The Three Oaths of Saul]].

The reconciliation is complete in form. Jonathan *brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence, as before*,
*kǝʾeṯmôl šilšôm* (7, WLC), literally *as yesterday and the day before*. It is the phrase Jacob used when he saw that
Laban's face *was not toward him as before* (Genesis 31:2, 5, stored WEB; the WLC spells *tǝmôl* there), and fled. Here
the face is restored. The reader has been told at 18:29 that *Saul was David's enemy continually*, and the next
verse, 19:8, brings *war again*.

**What is notable.**

- **Spoken aloud.** *Wayǝḏabbēr šāʾûl … lǝhāmîṯ ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ* (1, WLC). In chapter 18 the narrator opened Saul's mind
  three times (18:17, 21, 25); the servants carried a pleasant message *secretly* (18:22). Now the order goes *to
  Jonathan his son, and to all his servants*. The plan to let the Philistines do it is over. See
  [[David Marries Michal]].
- **Greatly delighted.** *Ḥāp̄ēṣ bǝḏāwiḏ mǝʾōḏ* (1, WLC). *Ḥāp̄ēṣ bǝ*, *delight in* (morphhb lemma 2654 a), is the
  verb Saul's servants were told to use falsely, *the king has delight in you* (18:22). The delight Saul pretended,
  his son has. In the Hebrew files checked the verb is used of a person's delight in another person at Genesis 34:19
  (Shechem in Dinah), Deuteronomy 21:14 (a captive wife), 18:22, here, and 2 Samuel 20:11 (*He who favors Joab*), and
  of Yahweh at Numbers 14:8 and 2 Samuel 15:26; 22:20 (WLC; 2 Samuel paraphrased). See [[Love]].
- **Two spellings of one name.** In 19:1 the WLC spells *yônāṯān* first and *wîhônāṯān* second; 2–7 have the long
  form throughout (morphhb tags both 19:1 forms 3129). The wiki records it, as [[Jonathan Loves David]] did, and draws
  no conclusion.
- **Hide yourself.** *Wǝyāšaḇtā ḇassēṯer wǝnaḥbēʾṯā* (2, WLC). *Neḥbāʾ*, the *niphal* of *ḥāḇāʾ* (2244), is the verb
  of Adam and Eve among the trees (Genesis 3:8, 10) and of Saul himself *among the baggage* (10:22, stored WEB). The king
  who once hid from the kingship now makes his rival hide. *Sēṯer*, *secret place* (5643 a), is the word of the
  curses on what is done *in secret* (Deuteronomy 27:15, 24) and of Ehud's *secret message* (Judges 3:19) (WLC).
- **In the morning.** *Babbōqer* (2, WLC). The danger has a time. In 19:11 the same word comes back: Saul's messengers
  are to kill David *in the morning* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The field.** Jonathan will stand with his father *in the field where you are* (3). The field returns in chapter
  20, where David asks *let me go, that I may hide myself in the field* (20:5), and Jonathan's arrows fall there (20:35,
  stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Spoke good.** *Wayǝḏabbēr yǝhônāṯān bǝḏāwiḏ ṭôḇ* (4, WLC). The same verb and object that carried Saul's order
  (1) now carry its answer. The same pairing, *dibber ṭôḇ*, *speak good*, is Moses' promise to Hobab, *Yahweh has
  spoken good concerning Israel* (Numbers 10:29), and Joshua's *not one thing has failed of all the good things which
  Yahweh your God spoke* (Joshua 23:14) (WLC; stored WEB).
- **Don't let the king sin.** *ʾAl-yeḥĕṭāʾ hammeleḵ bǝʿaḇdô bǝḏāwiḏ* (4, WLC). The negative command *ʾal* with *ḥāṭāʾ
  bǝ*, *don't sin against* a person, occurs in the files checked only here and in Reuben's memory of the pit, *Don't
  sin against the child*, *ʾal-teḥeṭʾû ḇayyeleḏ* (Genesis 42:22). Reuben had also said *Let's not take his life* and
  *Shed no blood* (Genesis 37:21–22). Both men speak for a younger man the household wants dead. Reuben was not
  listened to; Jonathan is. The wiki records the shared phrase as a parallel; nothing in 1 Samuel points to Genesis.
  See [[Mediation and Advocacy]] and [[Sin]].
- **His servant.** Jonathan calls David *his servant*, *ʿaḇdô* (4), the word of the order *to all his servants* (1).
  The pericope treats David as one of the men Saul told to kill him.
- **His works.** *Maʿăśāyw ṭôḇ-lǝḵā mǝʾōḏ* (4, WLC), *his works are very good to you*. The word *very*, *mǝʾōḏ*, is
  the last word of verse 1 and of verse 4: Jonathan *greatly* delighted, and David's works are *very* good.
- **His life in his hand.** *Wayyāśem ʾeṯ-napšô ḇǝḵappô* (5, WLC), *he set his soul in his palm*. In the files checked
  the idiom occurs only here, in Jephthah's defense to Ephraim, *I put my life in my hand* (Judges 12:3), and in the
  medium's words to Saul at Endor, *I have put my life in my hand* (28:21, stored WEB; not yet ingested); Job uses it
  at 13:14 (stored WEB; Hebrew not checked). Jephthah and the medium say it of themselves. Here it is said of another
  man, to the king who had sent him out. See [[My Life in My Hand - Jephthah, David, and the Medium of Endor]].
- **The Philistine.** *Wayyaḵ ʾeṯ-happǝlištî* (5, WLC), the singular, as 17:26–57 named [[Goliath]]. Jonathan does not
  use the name. Nor did David at Elah.
- **A great victory.** *Wayyaʿaś YHWH tǝšûʿâ ḡǝḏôlâ lǝḵol-yiśrāʾēl* (5, WLC). *Tǝšûʿâ* (8668) occurs in the files
  checked at Judges 15:18 (Samson's *great deliverance*), 1 Samuel 11:9, 13 (Jabesh, where Saul said *today Yahweh
  has rescued Israel*), here, and in 2 Samuel 19:3 and 23:10, 12. The phrase *Yahweh worked a great victory*, *wayyaʿaś
  YHWH tǝšûʿâ gǝḏôlâ*, is the one 2 Samuel 23:10, 12 uses of David's mighty men (WLC; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB
  stored). Jonathan's own Michmash was *this great salvation*, *hayǝšûʿâ haggǝḏôlâ* (14:45), a different noun (WLC). The
  victory Jonathan credits to Yahweh is one Saul himself had once credited to Yahweh. See [[Redemption]],
  [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]], and
  [[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].
- **You saw it, and rejoiced.** *Rāʾîṯā wattiśmāḥ* (5, WLC). Not *you heard* but *you saw*: Saul is made the witness.
  *Śāmaḥ* has Saul as subject in 1 Samuel only here and at Gilgal after Jabesh, *Saul and all the men of Israel
  rejoiced greatly* (11:15, stored WEB; WLC). At 18:6–8 the women came out *with joy*, and Saul was *very angry*. See
  [[Joy and Enjoyment]].
- **Innocent blood.** *Teḥĕṭāʾ bǝḏām nāqî* (5, WLC). *Dām nāqî* occurs in the files checked at Deuteronomy 19:10, 13
  (the cities of refuge); 21:8–9 (the unsolved killing and the heifer); 27:25 (*Cursed is he who takes a bribe to kill
  an innocent person*, *nep̄eš dam nāqî*); and here (WLC; stored WEB). Joshua 2:19 and 2 Samuel 3:28 put the two words
  side by side in other constructions. Here is the term's first use outside Deuteronomy's law in the files checked,
  and the first time anyone speaks it to a king. See [[Blood]] and
  [[Washing the Hands of Innocent Blood - Deuteronomy 21, Psalms 26 and 73, and Matthew 27]].
- **Without a cause.** *Ḥinnām* (5, WLC; 2600), *for nothing*. In the files checked it is the wage-free service of
  Jacob (Genesis 29:15), the slave who goes out *free* (Exodus 21:2, 11), the fish of Egypt eaten *without cost*
  (Numbers 11:5), here, Abigail's *that you have shed blood without cause* (25:31, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and
  David's *offerings … which cost me nothing* (2 Samuel 24:24, WLC; paraphrased). Twice it qualifies a killing: here,
  said to Saul about David, and at 25:31, said to David about Nabal.
- **Saul listened.** *Wayyišmaʿ šāʾûl bǝqôl yǝhônāṯān* (6, WLC). *Šāmaʿ bǝqôl* with Saul as subject occurs in the
  files checked at 15:20, 24 and here; at 28:21–23 the medium *has listened to your voice*, asks him to *listen also to
  the voice of your servant*, and *he listened to their voice* (stored WEB; WLC). See [[Law and Obedience]].
- **As Yahweh lives.** *Wayyiššāḇaʿ šāʾûl ḥay-YHWH ʾim-yûmāṯ* (6, WLC), *as Yahweh lives, if he is put to death*, the
  negative oath with the condition suppressed. Saul is the subject of the *niphal* of *šāḇaʿ*, *swear*, in the files
  checked only here and at 28:10, where he swears to the medium *As Yahweh lives, no punishment will happen to you*
  (WLC). His *as Yahweh lives* at 14:39 swore the opposite, a death. See [[Oaths and Vows]] and
  [[As Yahweh Lives - The Three Oaths of Saul]].
- **Put to death.** *Yûmāṯ*, the *hophal* of *mûṯ* (6, WLC), is the verb of the law's *shall surely be put to death*
  (Exodus 21:12, stored WEB). At Jabesh, Saul said *No man shall be put to death today* (11:13), the same form with
  *lōʾ*. At 20:32 Jonathan will ask *Why should he be put to death? What has he done?*, and Saul will answer with the
  spear (20:33, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **As before.** *Kǝʾeṯmôl šilšôm* (7, WLC; morphhb lemmas 865 and 8032). In 1 Samuel it is the Philistines' *there has
  not been such a thing before* (4:7), those who *knew him before* (10:11), the Hebrews who were with the Philistines
  *before* (14:21), here, and David's *as usual* at Nob (21:5 WEB; 21:6 WLC, spelled *kiṯmôl*). In the files checked, with the spelling *tǝmôl* (8543), it is also Laban's face (Genesis
  31:2, 5) and Deuteronomy's manslayer who *didn't hate him in time past* (19:4, 6), the chapter that also gives *innocent
  blood* (19:10) (stored WEB; WLC). The wiki records the cluster in Deuteronomy 19 and does not claim the pericope draws
  on it.

**How it connects.** The pericope opens the run of chapters in which David is kept from Saul four times, three of them
by Saul's own children: Jonathan's warning here (19:1–7), Michal's window (19:11–17), Samuel and the prophets at Naioth
(19:18–24), and Jonathan's arrows (20:1–42) (stored WEB; not yet ingested). Jonathan's plea succeeds once; his second,
*What has he done?* (20:32), draws the spear at Jonathan himself. His love for David (18:1, 3) is the ground the
narrator gives for both. Ahimelech will make the same defense at Nob, *Who among all your servants is so faithful as
David, who is the king's son-in-law …?* (22:14, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and die for it.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 19:1–7 as planned: 19:1 opens on Saul's spoken order, and 19:7 closes on David
*in his presence, as before*; 19:8 opens with *There was war again*. No new person page: Saul's *servants* (1) are not
individuated, and the pericope adds no named figure. No place page, because no place is named. Two new connections,
[[My Life in My Hand - Jephthah, David, and the Medium of Endor]], for an idiom whose three occurrences in the files
checked are now verified, and [[As Yahweh Lives - The Three Oaths of Saul]], for the oath that frames Saul's reign
from Michmash to Endor. The Reuben parallel (Genesis 42:22) is recorded on this page and in [[Mediation and Advocacy]]
rather than given a page of its own: one shared phrase, and no sign in 1 Samuel that it is meant.

**Recorded, not resolved.** Whether Saul's oath was sincere when sworn; the text says only that he listened and swore,
and three verses later throws the spear. Whether *kǝʾeṯmôl šilšôm* (7) is meant to echo Genesis 31; the phrase is
common enough in Samuel that the wiki does not claim it. Why Jonathan's plea omits the covenant of 18:3.
