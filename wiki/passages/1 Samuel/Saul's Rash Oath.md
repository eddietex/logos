# Saul's Rash Oath

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 14:24–46

## Summary

> **24** The men of Israel were distressed that day; for Saul had adjured the people, saying, "Cursed is the man who
> eats any food until it is evening, and I am avenged of my enemies." So none of the people tasted food.
> **25** All the people came into the forest; and there was honey on the ground.
> **26** When the people had come to the forest, behold, honey was dripping, but no one put his hand to his mouth; for
> the people feared the oath.
> **27** But Jonathan didn't hear when his father commanded the people with the oath. Therefore he put out the end of
> the rod that was in his hand, and dipped it in the honeycomb, and put his hand to his mouth; and his eyes
> brightened.
> **28** Then one of the people answered, and said, "Your father directly commanded the people with an oath, saying,
> 'Cursed is the man who eats food today.'" The people were faint.
> **29** Then Jonathan said, "My father has troubled the land. Please look how my eyes have brightened, because I
> tasted a little of this honey.
> **30** How much more, if perhaps the people had eaten freely today of the plunder of their enemies which they
> found? For now has there been no great slaughter among the Philistines."
> **31** They struck the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aijalon. The people were very faint;
> **32** and the people pounced on the plunder, and took sheep, cattle, and calves, and killed them on the ground; and
> the people ate them with the blood.
> **33** Then they told Saul, saying, "Behold, the people are sinning against Yahweh, in that they eat meat with the
> blood." He said, "You have dealt treacherously. Roll a large stone to me today!"
> **34** Saul said, "Disperse yourselves among the people, and tell them, 'Every man bring me here his ox, and every
> man his sheep, and kill them here, and eat; and don't sin against Yahweh in eating meat with the blood.'" All the
> people brought every man his ox with him that night, and killed them there.
> **35** Saul built an altar to Yahweh. This was the first altar that he built to Yahweh.
> **36** Saul said, "Let us go down after the Philistines by night, and take plunder among them until the morning
> light, and let us not leave a man of them." They said, "Do whatever seems good to you." Then the priest said, "Let
> us draw near here to God."
> **37** Saul asked counsel of God, "Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you deliver them into the hand of
> Israel?" But he didn't answer him that day.
> **38** Saul said, "Draw near here, all you chiefs of the people; and know and see in which this sin has been today.
> **39** For, as Yahweh lives, who saves Israel, though it is in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die." But there was
> not a man among all the people who answered him.
> **40** Then he said to all Israel, "You be on one side, and I and Jonathan my son will be on the other side." The
> people said to Saul, "Do what seems good to you."
> **41** Therefore Saul said to Yahweh, the God of Israel, "Show the right." Jonathan and Saul were chosen, but the
> people escaped.
> **42** Saul said, "Cast lots between me and Jonathan my son." Jonathan was selected.
> **43** Then Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what you have done!" Jonathan told him, and said, "I certainly did taste
> a little honey with the end of the rod that was in my hand; and behold, I must die."
> **44** Saul said, "God do so and more also; for you shall surely die, Jonathan."
> **45** The people said to Saul, "Shall Jonathan die, who has worked this great salvation in Israel? Far from it! As
> Yahweh lives, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground; for he has worked with God today!" So the
> people rescued Jonathan, that he didn't die.
> **46** Then Saul went up from following the Philistines; and the Philistines went to their own place.

The day of the rout is also the day of an oath. The narrator gives it after the fact: [[Saul]] *had adjured the
people*, *Cursed is the man who eats any food until it is evening, and I am avenged of my enemies*, and the men of
Israel are *distressed* by it (24). They come into a forest where honey lies on the ground and drips, and no one
touches it, *for the people feared the oath* (25–26). [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]], who was over the pass when the oath
was sworn, *didn't hear*. He dips the end of his rod in the honeycomb, and *his eyes brightened* (27). One of the
people tells him of the oath, and adds that the people are faint (28). Jonathan's answer is a verdict on his father:
*My father has troubled the land*. If the people had eaten of the plunder, the slaughter of [[The Philistines]] would
have been greater (29–30).

They strike the Philistines *from [[Michmash]] to [[Aijalon]]*, and the people are *very faint* (31). At evening they
*pounced on the plunder*, slaughtered sheep and cattle on the ground, and *ate them with the blood* (32). Saul is told
that the people are *sinning against Yahweh*; he calls it treachery and has a great stone rolled to him, and sends
word that every man is to bring his ox and his sheep and slaughter it there (33–34). *Saul built an altar to Yahweh.
This was the first altar that he built to Yahweh* (35).

Saul proposes a night pursuit, *let us not leave a man of them*, and the people say *Do whatever seems good to you*.
The priest, unnamed here and presumably [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]] of 14:3, says *Let us draw near here to God*
(36). Saul asks, *Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you deliver them into the hand of Israel?*, and *he
didn't answer him that day* (37). Saul concludes that a sin lies somewhere. He calls the chiefs and swears a second
oath: *as Yahweh lives, who saves Israel, though it is in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die*. No one answers him
(38–39).

He divides Israel into two sides, the people on one and himself and Jonathan on the other; the people say again *Do
what seems good to you* (40). Saul says *Show the right*: Saul and Jonathan are *taken*, and the people go free (41).
*Cast lots between me and Jonathan my son*: Jonathan is taken (42). Jonathan tells what he did, *I certainly did
taste a little honey … and behold, I must die*, and Saul swears a third time: *God do so and more also; for you shall
surely die, Jonathan* (43–44). The people overrule him. *Shall Jonathan die, who has worked this great salvation in
Israel? Far from it!* They swear in turn, *As Yahweh lives, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground;
for he has worked with God today*, and they *rescued Jonathan* (45). Saul goes up from the pursuit, and the
Philistines go *to their own place* (46).

**Where it happens.** In the field of the pursuit, westward from the pass. The rout runs *from Michmash to Aijalon*
(31), off the Benjamin plateau toward the lowland; Aijalon is the town of Dan's list and the Levitical cities, by
the valley where the moon stood still (Joshua 10:12; 19:42; 21:24, stored WEB). The forest of the honey (25–26) is
unnamed. The altar (35), the night council (36), and the lot (40–42) happen at the evening camp, also unnamed.

**What is going on.** The pericope turns the victory of 14:1–23 into a near-execution. It is told in three oaths by
Saul and one by the people, and each oath is answered by something Saul did not foresee.

The first oath (24) is sworn before the reader hears it. The narrator says *Saul had adjured the people*, so the
reader learns it at the same moment as the cost: the men of Israel *were distressed*. The verb is *niggaś* (WLC). In
the WLC files searched (Genesis–2 Samuel) the niphal occurs only here and at 13:6, where the people *were distressed*
before hiding in caves; its qal participle is the noun of Pharaoh's *taskmasters* (Exodus 3:7; 5:6–14; morphhb lemma
5065). The oath lays on Israel the condition the Philistines had laid on it. Its purpose is stated as Saul's own:
*until I am avenged of my enemies*, *wᵊniqqamtî mēʾōyᵊḇay* (WLC). Samson had used the same form, *niqqamtî*, of his
private revenge on the Philistines (Judges 15:7, WLC; see also 16:28). The people keep the oath out of fear (26). The
oath forbids exactly what a tired army needs.

Jonathan breaks it without knowing it, and his eyes *brightened* (27, 29). His verdict, *ʿāḵar ʾāḇî ʾeṯ-hāʾāreṣ*,
*My father has troubled the land* (29, WLC), uses the verb Joshua spoke over Achan, *Why have you troubled us? Yahweh
will trouble you today* (Joshua 7:25), and Jephthah spoke to his daughter, *you are one of those who trouble me; for
I have opened my mouth to Yahweh* (Judges 11:35) (stored WEB; WLC). In Joshua 7 the troubler was the man the lot
found. Here the man the lot will find names his father as the troubler before the lot is cast. See
[[The Valley of Achor - Trouble Turned to a Door of Hope]].

The second consequence is the blood (32–34). Starved all day, the people fall on the plunder at evening and eat *ʿal
haddām*, *with the blood* (32–33, WLC). The phrase is Leviticus 19:26, *You shall not eat any meat with the blood
still in it*, *lōʾ ṯōʾḵᵊlû ʿal-haddām* (stored WEB; WLC), the only other place in the files searched where *eat* is
joined to *ʿal haddām*. Behind it stand Genesis 9:4, Leviticus 17:10–14, and Deuteronomy 12:16, 23 (stored WEB).
The oath that kept Israel from honey drove it to blood. Saul responds correctly and at once: a great stone,
slaughter at one place, *don't sin against Yahweh* (34). Then the narrator adds that this was his first altar (35).

The third consequence is silence. When Saul asks God, *he didn't answer him that day*, *wᵊlōʾ ʿānāhû bayyôm hahûʾ*
(37, WLC). Samuel had warned that when the people cried out because of their king, *Yahweh will not answer you in
that day*, *wᵊlōʾ-yaʿăneh YHWH ʾeṯḵem bayyôm hahûʾ* (8:18, stored WEB; WLC). At Mizpah, *Samuel cried to Yahweh for
Israel; and Yahweh answered him* (7:9). Saul reads the silence as evidence of hidden sin, as Joshua read the defeat
at Ai (Joshua 7:10–13), and the narrator does not say he is wrong to. The text does not say what the silence meant.
It reports the inquiry, the silence, and the lot. When Saul swears that the guilty man will die *though it is in
Jonathan my son*, *there was not a man among all the people who answered him* (39). The same verb, *ʿānâ*, is used
of God's silence (37) and the people's (39) (WLC, lemma 6030 b). Either the people know, or they will not speak
against Jonathan.

The lot takes Jonathan (41–42), with the verb that took Achan and Saul himself, *wayyillāḵēḏ* (WLC). See
[[Taken by Lot - Achan, Saul, and Jonathan]]. Jonathan does not hide anything and does not plead. He says *I
certainly did taste a little honey … behold, I must die*, *hinnî ʾāmûṯ* (43, WLC). Saul swears again (44). Then the
people speak for the first time in the pericope with anything other than *Do what seems good to you* (36, 40).
They give the reason as a matter of record: Jonathan *has worked this great salvation in Israel*, *ʿāśâ
hayšûʿâ haggᵊḏôlâ* (45, WLC). Moses at the sea had said *see the salvation of Yahweh, which he will work for you
today*, *yᵊšûʿaṯ YHWH ʾăšer-yaʿăśeh lāḵem hayyôm* (Exodus 14:13, stored WEB; WLC). The people claim that Jonathan *has
worked with God today*. They answer Saul's *as Yahweh lives* with their own (39, 45), and they *rescued* him,
*wayyip̄dû* (45, WLC), the verb of redeeming the firstborn and of Yahweh's redeeming Israel from Egypt (Exodus 13:13,
15; Deuteronomy 7:8, stored WEB; morphhb lemma 6299). The text does not say whether anything was paid; it says only
that he *didn't die*.

The pericope ends with the pursuit abandoned. *Saul went up from following the Philistines; and the Philistines
went to their own place* (46). The night raid of verse 36 never happens. Jonathan had said *now has there been no
great slaughter among the Philistines* (30), and the narrator's last sentence confirms it.

**What is notable.**

- **The oath before the story.** *Wayyōʾel šāʾûl* (24, WLC), *Saul adjured*, a form morphhb assigns to *ʾālâ*,
  *swear, curse* (lemma 422), whose only other verbal use in the files searched is Micah's mother's curse over the
  stolen silver (Judges 17:2). The oath is a curse formula, *ʾārûr hāʾîš*, *Cursed is the man*, the same opening as
  Joshua's oath over Jericho (Joshua 6:26), which set its price on a builder's firstborn and youngest son (stored
  WEB; WLC). The
  other oath in the books stored so far sworn with *ʾārûr* over Benjamin is Israel's at Mizpah, *Cursed is he who
  gives a wife to Benjamin* (Judges 21:18, stored WEB; WLC). Saul is a Benjamite.
- **Food.** *Leḥem* (24, 28, WLC) is the general word, *bread* or *food*. The verb *ṭāʿam*, *taste* (24, 29, 43, WLC),
  occurs in Genesis–2 Samuel only in this pericope and in 2 Samuel 3:35; 19:35 (Hebrew 19:36) (WLC; morphhb lemma
  2938).
- **The forest and the honey.** *Wᵊḵol-hāʾāreṣ bāʾû ḇayyāʿar* (25, WLC), literally *all the land came into the
  forest*; the WEB has *All the people*. The honeycomb of verse 27 is *yaʿraṯ haddᵊḇāš* (WLC), from the same root as
  *forest*. In the WLC files searched (Genesis–2 Samuel) the word occurs only here (morphhb lemma 3295). The
  pun is the wiki's observation, not the narrator's comment.
- **His eyes brightened.** The Masoretic text writes the verb two ways in verse 27: the consonants read
  *wattirʾenâ*, *his eyes saw*, and the vowels read *wattāʾōrᵊnâ*, *brightened* (WLC ketiv and qere). Verse 29 has
  *ʾōrû*, *brightened*, without a variant. The WEB follows the reading *brightened*.
- **The people were faint.** *Wayyāʿap̄ hāʿām* (28, 31, WLC). Verse 28 puts it at the end of the informant's speech.
  Read as his words, it reports the oath's cost; read as the narrator's, it confirms it. The WEB punctuates it as the
  narrator's.
- **Pounced on the plunder.** *Wayyaʿaṭ hāʿām ʾel-haššālāl* (32, qere; WLC). The verb *ʿîṭ* occurs in Genesis–2
  Samuel only here, at 15:19, where Samuel asks Saul *Why … did you take the plunder*, literally *pounce on the
  plunder*, *wattaʿaṭ ʾel-haššālāl*, and at 25:14, of Nabal *railing at* David's men (WLC; WEB stored, not yet
  ingested). The next time the phrase is heard, it is Saul who is accused of it.
- **On the ground.** *Wayyišḥăṭû ʾārṣâ* (32, WLC), *slaughtered them to the ground*. Deuteronomy allows slaughter
  for food away from the altar so long as the blood is poured *on the earth like water* (12:16, stored WEB). The
  people's fault is named as eating *with the blood*, not as slaughtering away from an altar.
- **You have dealt treacherously.** *Bᵊḡaḏtem* (33, WLC). In the files searched (Genesis–2 Samuel) the verb occurs
  before this only at Exodus 21:8, of a master who *dealt deceitfully* with a bought woman, and Judges 9:23, the men
  of Shechem against Abimelech (morphhb lemma 898).
- **Roll a large stone.** *Gōllû ʾēlay hayyôm ʾeḇen gᵊḏôlâ* (33, WLC). The imperative *gōllû* is Joshua's at the
  cave of Makkedah, *Roll large stones* (Joshua 10:18), and the verb is the one of the stone rolled from the well's
  mouth (Genesis 29:3, 8, 10) (stored WEB; WLC).
- **The first altar.** *ʾŌṯô hēḥēl liḇnôṯ mizbēaḥ layhwh* (35, WLC), literally *it he began to build, an altar to
  Yahweh*. The WEB reads it as *the first altar that he built*. Other readings take it as *it was the first that he
  began*, or *he began to build it* without finishing (both commonly reported, not checked). Whether the notice
  praises Saul, or marks how late the altar came, the text does not say. The *large stone* of verse 33 may be the
  same stone; the text does not say that either.
- **Do whatever seems good to you.** *Kol-haṭṭôḇ bᵊʿêneyḵā ʿăśēh* (36, 40, WLC). The people say it twice, to the
  night raid and to the lot. They say something else only in verse 45.
- **The priest draws near.** *Niqrᵊḇâ hălōm ʾel-hāʾĕlōhîm* (36, WLC). It is the priest, not the king, who stops to
  ask. In 14:19 Saul had cut the priest's inquiry off.
- **Saul asked.** *Wayyišʾal šāʾûl bēʾlōhîm* (37, WLC). The verb *šāʾal* and the name *šāʾûl* share their letters,
  the wordplay the wiki noted at Samuel's birth (1:20, 28). The king the people asked for asks, and is not answered.
- **The chiefs.** *Pinnôṯ hāʿām* (38, WLC), literally *the corners of the people*. In the files searched the word is
  used of people only here and at Judges 20:2, *the chiefs of all the people*, at the assembly before the war on
  Benjamin; elsewhere it is the corners of the altar (Exodus 27:2; 38:2) (stored WEB; morphhb lemma 6438).
- **As Yahweh lives, who saves Israel.** *Ḥay-YHWH hammôšîaʿ ʾeṯ-yiśrāʾēl* (39, WLC). Saul swears by Yahweh as the
  saviour of Israel on the day the narrator has said *So Yahweh saved Israel* (14:23). He uses that title to condemn
  the man through whom the saving came.
- **Show the right.** *Hāḇâ ṯāmîm* (41, WLC). The Masoretic verse is short. A longer Greek text that names Urim and
  Thummim is commonly reported (recalled, not checked; no Septuagint consulted). See
  [[Taken by Lot - Achan, Saul, and Jonathan]] and
  [[Urim and Thummim - The Breastplate of Judgment and Later Inquiry]].
- **I and Jonathan my son.** Saul puts himself on the side that will be tested (40). The first lot takes both (41).
  He is not cleared until the second.
- **Behold, I must die.** *Hinnî ʾāmûṯ* (43, WLC). It can be read as acceptance or as protest; the WEB's *must*
  leans to acceptance. Jephthah's daughter had said *do to me according to that which has proceeded out of your
  mouth* (Judges 11:36, stored WEB). See
  [[A Father's Oath and His Child - Jephthah's Daughter and Jonathan]].
- **God do so and more also.** *Kōh yaʿăśeh ʾĕlōhîm wᵊḵōh yôsip̄* (44, WLC). Saul's self-curse has no object; the
  usual *to me* is missing. See [[God Do So to Me - The Self-Curse Oath in Ruth and 1 Samuel]].
- **Not one hair.** *ʾIm-yippōl miśśaʿăraṯ rōʾšô ʾarṣâ* (45, WLC). The same oath, *as Yahweh lives* with *not one
  hair … to the ground*, is David's to the woman of Tekoa over her son (2 Samuel 14:11, WEB checked at bible-api.com;
  WLC). That is the only other place in the files searched where the hair and the falling go together.
- **With God.** *Kî-ʿim-ʾĕlōhîm ʿāśâ hayyôm hazzeh* (45, WLC). The people's theology agrees with the narrator's
  (14:23) and with Jonathan's own *It may be that Yahweh will work for us* (14:6, *yaʿăśeh*, WLC). The verb *ʿāśâ*,
  *work*, joins all three.
- **Ransomed.** *Wayyip̄dû hāʿām ʾeṯ-yônāṯān* (45, WLC). In Genesis–2 Samuel *pāḏâ* is used of redeeming firstborn,
  persons, and Israel itself; this is its only use there with *the people* as subject (morphhb lemma 6299). See
  [[Redemption]].
- **To their own place.** *Lᵊmᵊqômām* (46, WLC). The phrase was used of the ark sent *to its own place* from the
  Philistine cities (5:11; 6:2) (stored WEB; WLC). The Philistines go home undefeated in full.

**How it connects.** The pericope reverses [[Jonathan's Attack at Michmash]]. There Jonathan acted on a sign, and the
narrator closed *So Yahweh saved Israel that day* (14:23). Here Saul acts on an oath, and the day ends with the
pursuit abandoned. In the earlier pericope Saul cut the inquiry off (14:19); here he inquires, and gets no answer.
It answers [[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]] too: there he offered without waiting, and was told *You have done
foolishly. You have not kept the commandment* (13:13). Here he is scrupulous about the commandment on blood and
builds an altar, and the commandment he cannot keep is his own. Read with [[Jephthah's Vow]], it is the second father
in the books stored so far whose oath binds him against his child. Jephthah's daughter dies by her father's word;
Jonathan is redeemed by the people's. See [[A Father's Oath and His Child - Jephthah's Daughter and Jonathan]] and
[[Whoever You Find Your Gods With - Two Rash Oaths Over a Search]]. Read with [[The Sin of Achan]], the lot that
found the troubler of Israel finds the man who called his father its troubler. Jonathan will next be seen loving
David (18:1–4, stored WEB; not yet ingested). Saul's next commission, against Amalek (15:1–3, stored WEB; not yet
ingested), will turn on *pounce on the plunder* and on *the voice of the people*.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 14:24–46 as planned. Verse 46 ends it: the pursuit is abandoned and the
Philistines go home, and 14:47 opens a summary of Saul's reign. No new person or place page. The informant of 14:28
is one of *the people* and gets no page. The priest of 14:36 is linked to [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]], with
*presumably*, since the verse does not name him and 14:3, 18 have just placed Ahijah with Saul. The forest, the honey,
and the great stone get no pages. The rout's end is linked to [[Aijalon]] (the town of Dan's list), not to
[[The Valley of Aijalon]] or [[Aijalon (Zebulun)]]; the verse names a place, *ʾayyālōnâ*, *to Aijalon* (WLC), not
