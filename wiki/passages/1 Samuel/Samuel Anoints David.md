# Samuel Anoints David

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 16:1–13

## Summary

> **1** Yahweh said to Samuel, "How long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him from being king over
> Israel? Fill your horn with oil, and go. I will send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite; for I have provided a king for
> myself among his sons."
> **2** Samuel said, "How can I go? If Saul hears it, he will kill me." Yahweh said, "Take a heifer with you, and say,
> I have come to sacrifice to Yahweh.
> **3** Call Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will show you what you shall do. You shall anoint to me him whom I name to
> you."
> …
> **6** When they had come, he looked at Eliab, and said, "Surely Yahweh's anointed is before him."
> **7** But Yahweh said to Samuel, "Don't look on his face, or on the height of his stature, because I have rejected
> him; for I don't see as man sees. For man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh looks at the heart."
> …
> **11** Samuel said to Jesse, "Are all your children here?" He said, "There remains yet the youngest. Behold, he is
> keeping the sheep." Samuel said to Jesse, "Send and get him, for we will not sit down until he comes here."
> **12** He sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, with a handsome face and good appearance. Yahweh said,
> "Arise! Anoint him, for this is he."
> **13** Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the middle of his brothers. Then Yahweh's Spirit came
> mightily on David from that day forward. So Samuel rose up and went to Ramah.

(Excerpts; the full text is in `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 16.)

The chapter opens on the last verse of the one before. [[Samuel]] is still mourning [[Saul]], and Yahweh asks *How
long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him from being king over Israel?* He is to fill his horn with oil
and go to [[Jesse]] *the Bethlehemite*, *for I have provided a king for myself among his sons* (1). Samuel answers
with the danger: *If Saul hears it, he will kill me.* Yahweh gives him a reason to be in the town. He is to take a
heifer, say *I have come to sacrifice to Yahweh*, and call Jesse to the sacrifice; *I will show you what you shall
do. You shall anoint to me him whom I name to you* (2–3).

At [[Bethlehem]] the elders come out *trembling*: *Do you come peaceably?* *Peaceably; I have come to sacrifice to
Yahweh.* Samuel tells them to sanctify themselves, and he himself sanctifies Jesse and his sons and calls them to the
sacrifice (4–5). When they arrive he looks at [[Eliab (son of Jesse)]] and thinks *Surely Yahweh's anointed is before
him* (6). Yahweh corrects him: *Don't look on his face, or on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him;
for I don't see as man sees. For man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh looks at the heart* (7). Jesse brings
[[Abinadab (son of Jesse)]], then [[Shammah (son of Jesse)]], and Samuel says of each *Yahweh has not chosen this one,
either* (8–9). Seven sons pass, and *Yahweh has not chosen these* (10).

*Are all your children here?* There is one more, *the youngest*, and he is *keeping the sheep*. Samuel will not sit
down until he comes (11). He is brought: *ruddy, with a handsome face and good appearance*. *Arise! Anoint him, for
this is he* (12). Samuel takes *the horn of oil* and anoints him *in the middle of his brothers*, and *Yahweh's Spirit
came mightily on David from that day forward*. Only then, in the last clause, is the young man named. Samuel gets up
and goes home to [[Ramah]] (13).

**Where it happens.** The whole scene is at Bethlehem, Jesse's town, which Ruth left as the town of Elimelech's family
and ended as the town of Obed, *the father of Jesse, the father of David* (Ruth 4:17). The Hebrew calls Jesse *bêṯ
hallaḥmî*, *the Bethlehemite* (1, WLC), the form used again at 16:18 and 17:58 (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
Samuel's journey starts from Ramah, where 15:34 left him, and ends there (13). Bethlehem is in Judah, Saul's Gibeah
in Benjamin, a short distance apart, and Samuel's fear in verse 2 assumes Saul would hear.

**What is going on.** This is the third anointing of a king in 1 Samuel, and the second by Samuel's hand. The first
two were Saul's: in private, with *the vial of oil* (10:1), and in effect again at Gilgal, where *they made Saul king
before Yahweh* (11:15). The sentence of 13:14 said Yahweh *has sought for himself a man after his own heart*; the
sentence of 15:28 gave the kingdom *to a neighbor of yours who is better than you*. Neither named him. This chapter
finds the man and names him, in the last clause.

It does not make him king. Saul remains king to the end of the book, and the next pericope brings David into Saul's
service as a harpist (16:14–23, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The anointing is secret in the same way Saul's was:
done by Samuel on Yahweh's word, with a few witnesses, and followed by the Spirit. The difference is that Saul's
secret was kept by Saul (10:16), while this one is witnessed by David's family, and nothing in the narrative says that
anyone outside it knows. Eliab's anger at David at the battle line (17:28, stored WEB; not yet ingested) does not
mention it.

The chapter is built on *seeing*. Yahweh's first word about David is *rāʾîṯî bəḇānāw lî meleḵ* (1, WLC), *I have seen
among his sons a king for myself*; the WEB's *provided* is the sense the verb has at Moriah, *God will provide himself
the lamb* (Genesis 22:8), and *Yahweh Will Provide*, *YHWH yirʾeh* (Genesis 22:14) (WLC; stored WEB). Samuel *looked
at*, *wayyarʾ*, Eliab (6). Yahweh then uses the verb three times in one line: *for not as man sees*, *for man sees
according to the eyes*, *and Yahweh sees according to the heart* (7, WLC *yirʾeh … yirʾeh laʿênayim … yirʾeh
lallēḇāḇ*). David is introduced through his appearance: *yəp̄ēh ʿênayim wəṭôḇ rōʾî* (12, WLC), literally *beautiful
of eyes, and good of appearance*. *Rōʾî* is a noun from the same root (morphhb lemma 7210), and in the Hebrew files
checked, Genesis through 2 Samuel and Ruth, it occurs only here and in Hagar's name for God, *ʾĒl Rŏʾî*, *You are a
God who sees* (Genesis 16:13, WLC; stored WEB). See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

**What is notable.**

- **How long will you mourn.** *ʿAḏ-māṯay ʾattâ miṯʾabbēl* (1, WLC), the same verb as *Samuel mourned for Saul* in
  15:35 (morphhb lemma 56). Yahweh does not tell Samuel he was wrong to mourn. He gives him a job. See
  [[Mourning and Lament]].
- **Rejected, again.** *Məʾastîw*, *I have rejected him*, of Saul (1), and *məʾastîhû* of Eliab (7) (WLC, lemma
  3988), the verb of 15:23, 26. Eliab is *rejected* in the same word as the king, although he had never been chosen.
  The likeness suggests that he is turned down for the reason Saul was first chosen. See
  [[Call and Election]].
- **The height of his stature.** *Gəḇōah qômāṯô* (7, WLC). Saul was *taller than any of the people* *from his shoulders
  and upward*, *gāḇōah* (9:2, WLC), and stood *higher than any of the people* at Mizpah (10:23). Samuel had pointed it
  out: *Do you see him whom Yahweh has chosen, that there is no one like him among all the people?* (10:24). Samuel
  looks at Eliab the same way. *Qômâ* is used of a person in Genesis–2 Samuel only here and at 28:20, where Saul
  *fell … his full length*, *məlōʾ qômāṯô*, at Endor (WLC; stored WEB, not yet ingested). In the WLC files checked,
  every other occurrence measures an object: Noah's ark, or the tabernacle, its court, and its furniture
  (morphhb lemma 6967). See [[A Man After His Own Heart - 1 Samuel 13.14, 16.7, and Acts 13.22]].
- **The heart.** *Lallēḇāḇ* (7, WLC) is the same noun as *kilḇāḇô*, *after his own heart* (13:14). Whether verse 7
  explains 13:14 the text does not say. The heart Yahweh *sees* in David is not described here. The narrator describes
  only his looks (12), and does not say whether that is ironic. Eliab will claim to see David's heart, *I know your
  pride, and the naughtiness of your heart* (17:28, WLC *rōaʿ ləḇāḇeḵā*; stored WEB, not yet ingested).
- **And yet he is handsome.** Yahweh rejects the eldest's *appearance*, *marʾēhû* (7), and the chosen son is *ruddy,
  with a handsome face and good appearance* (12). The point of verse 7 is not that David is plain. It is that the
  choice was not made by what Samuel could see. In Genesis the same kind of note is made about Joseph, *well-built and
  handsome*, *yəp̄ēh-ṯōʾar wîp̄ēh marʾeh* (Genesis 39:6, WLC).
- **Ruddy.** *ʾAḏmônî* (12, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked (Genesis through 2 Samuel, and Ruth), the word occurs
  only here, at 17:42, where Goliath despises David for being *a youth, and ruddy*, and at Esau's birth: *The first
  came out red all over*, *ʾaḏmônî* (Genesis 25:25, WLC; morphhb lemma 132). Whether it means hair or complexion is
  not settled. See [[Ruddy - Esau and David]].
- **The youngest.** *Haqqāṭān* (11, WLC), the *small one*. 17:14 repeats it: *David was the youngest*. It is the
  adjective Samuel used of Saul, *little in your own sight*, *qāṭōn* (15:17, WLC). Abel, Jacob, Joseph and Ephraim are
  younger sons preferred in Genesis, and Gideon was *the least in my father's house* (Judges 6:15). Here Jesse does not
  even bring the youngest to the sacrifice. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]],
  [[The Elder Will Serve the Younger - Genesis 25.23 in Malachi and Romans]], and
  [[The Least in My Father's House - Judges 6.15 and 1 Samuel 9.21]].
- **Keeping the sheep.** *Rōʿeh baṣṣōʾn* (11, WLC), the phrase used of Joseph at seventeen, *feeding the flock with his
  brothers*, *rōʿeh … baṣṣōʾn* (Genesis 37:2, WLC), and close to Abel, *a keeper of sheep*, *rōʿēh ṣōʾn* (Genesis 4:2,
  WLC). David will say *Your servant was keeping his father's sheep* to Saul (17:34, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
  Yahweh will say *I took you from the sheep pen, from following the sheep, to be prince over my people* (2 Samuel
  7:8, WEB checked at bible-api.com), and the tribes will remember *You will be shepherd of my people Israel* (2
  Samuel 5:2, WEB checked). See [[Taken from Following the Sheep - 1 Samuel 16.11, 2 Samuel 7.8, and Psalm 78.70-72]].
- **Seven sons, and one more.** *Jesse made seven of his sons to pass before Samuel* (10), so David is the eighth;
  17:12 agrees, *he had eight sons*. 1 Chronicles 2:13–15 lists seven, *Eliab* to *David the seventh*, with *Shimea*
  for Shammah (WEB checked at bible-api.com). The two counts are recorded, not reconciled. See [[Jesse]].
- **Yahweh has not chosen.** *Lōʾ-bāḥar YHWH* (8, 9, 10, WLC), three times. The law of the king said Israel could set
  over itself only *the king whom Yahweh your God chooses … one from among your brothers*, *miqqereḇ ʾaḥeḵā*
  (Deuteronomy 17:15, WLC; stored WEB). David is anointed *in the middle of his brothers*, *bəqereḇ ʾeḥāw* (13, WLC).
  The noun is used in its literal sense, but the words of the law are there. See
  [[Like All the Nations - Deuteronomy 17.14 and 1 Samuel 8.5, 20]].
- **Surely Yahweh's anointed.** *ʾAḵ neḡeḏ YHWH məšîḥô* (6, WLC). Samuel uses the title before the oil, and of the
  wrong man. *Məšîaḥ*, *anointed*, has been used in this book of the coming king in Hannah's song (2:10), of the
  faithful priest's king (2:35), and of Saul (12:3, 5). Its next uses are David's for Saul, *Yahweh's anointed*, whom
  he will not touch (24:6, 10; 26:9–23, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].
- **The horn, not the vial.** *Malleʾ qarnəḵā šemen* (1) and *qeren haššemen* (13), *horn*, not the *pak*, *vial*, of
  Saul's anointing (10:1) (WLC). *Qeren* is the noun of Hannah's *My horn is exalted* (2:1) and of her last line, *exalt
  the horn of his anointed*, *qeren məšîḥô* (2:10, WLC). In Genesis through 2 Samuel the horn is a vessel of oil only
  in 16:1 and 16:13 (morphhb lemma 7161). The wiki notes the echo and does not build a reading on the difference
  between the two vessels.
- **We will not sit down.** *Lōʾ nāsōḇ* (11, WLC), from *sāḇaḇ*, *turn* or *go around* (lemma 5437). The WEB's *sit
  down* follows the usual reading, that they will not take their places around the sacrificial meal (recalled, not
  checked); the Hebrew does not mention sitting. The meal to which Saul was invited (9:22–24) is the closest parallel in
  the book.
- **The Spirit from that day.** *Wattiṣlaḥ rûaḥ-YHWH ʾel-dāwiḏ mēhayyôm hahûʾ wāmāʿlâ* (13, WLC). The verb is the one
  used of Samson and of Saul (10:6, 10; 11:6). Only for David is *from that day forward* added, and the next verse says
  *Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul* (16:14, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The preposition is *ʾel*, *to*, not the
  *ʿal*, *on*, of the other rushes (WLC). See [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]] and
  [[The Spirit of God]].
- **The name last.** *Dāwiḏ* (13) is the first time the narrative of 1 Samuel names him. In this chapter he has been
  *a king for myself among his sons* (1), *the youngest* (11), *he* (12), and *this is he* (12).
- **A pretext from Yahweh.** The sacrifice is real: the heifer is brought, the family sanctified, and a meal
  implied (2–5, 11). It also covers the purpose of the visit, at Yahweh's suggestion, in answer to Samuel's *he will
  kill me* (2). The text does not comment. Readers have differed on whether this is a deception, a concealment, or
  merely a partial statement (recalled, not checked); the wiki records that the word Samuel is given to say is true,
  and that it is not the whole truth. See [[Deception]] and
  [[The Midwives and Rahab - Deceiving the King to Save Life]].
- **Trembling elders.** *Wayyeḥerḏû* (4, WLC), the verb of the people at Gilgal *trembling* after Saul (13:7) and of
  the *trembling of God* at Michmash (14:15) (lemma 2729). The elders' *Do you come peaceably?* is not explained. The
  last time Samuel arrived to sacrifice, Agag died (15:33); the text does not connect the two.
- **Sanctify yourselves.** *Hiṯqaddəšû* (5, WLC), the command Joshua gave before the Jordan (Joshua 3:5) and before
  Achan was taken (Joshua 7:13) (stored WEB). Samuel then sanctifies Jesse's family himself (5). See [[Holiness]].
- **Samuel goes home.** The last act of the scene is Samuel's: *So Samuel rose up and went to Ramah* (13). He
  anoints, and he leaves. In 1 Samuel he next appears with David at Ramah, when David flees to him (19:18, stored
  WEB; not yet ingested).

**How it connects.** Chapters 13–15 were Saul's three failures; chapter 16 begins the long overlap of two anointed
men. The next pericope, *David Plays the Harp for Saul* (16:14–23, planned, not yet ingested), sends the Spirit away
from the first and brings the second into his house. A servant will say *I have seen a son of Jesse the
Bethlehemite*, *rāʾîṯî bēn ləyišay bêṯ hallaḥmî* (16:18, WLC; stored WEB), the same words Yahweh used in verse 1.
From here to the end of 2 Samuel, David is the main figure of the narrative.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 16:1–13 as planned: 16:13 ends with Samuel going home, and 16:14 turns to Saul.
Three new person pages, one for each named son: [[Eliab (son of Jesse)]], [[Abinadab (son of Jesse)]], and
[[Shammah (son of Jesse)]]. All three are qualified, since [[Eliab (father of Dathan and Abiram)]] and
[[Abinadab (of Kiriath Jearim)]] already exist. Shammah is qualified too, because 1 Samuel 31:2 names an Abinadab son of
Saul and other men named Shammah are ahead. No page for *the elders of the city* (4) or for the four sons not named.
Two new connections: [[Ruddy - Esau and David]], since *ʾaḏmônî* occurs only three times in the files checked, and
[[Taken from Following the Sheep - 1 Samuel 16.11, 2 Samuel 7.8, and Psalm 78.70-72]], for the shepherd-king thread.
The link between Deuteronomy 17:15 and 16:13 is added to the existing
[[Like All the Nations - Deuteronomy 17.14 and 1 Samuel 8.5, 20]] rather than given its own page.

**Unresolved.** Seven or eight sons (16:10; 17:12; 1 Chronicles 2:13–15), and Shammah or Shimea. Whether the
sacrifice of 16:2 counts as a deception. Why the elders tremble (4). What *lōʾ nāsōḇ* exactly means (11). Whether 16:7
explains *kilḇāḇô* in 13:14. Whether *ʾaḏmônî* refers to hair or skin. Who outside Jesse's household knew of the
anointing.
