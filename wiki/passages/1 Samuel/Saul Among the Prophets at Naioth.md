# Saul Among the Prophets at Naioth

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 19:18–24

## Summary

> **19:18** Now David fled and escaped, and came to Samuel at Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him. He
> and Samuel went and lived in Naioth.
> **19** Saul was told, saying, "Behold, David is at Naioth in Ramah."
> **20** Saul sent messengers to seize David: and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel
> standing as head over them, God's Spirit came on Saul's messengers, and they also prophesied.
> **21** When Saul was told, he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. Saul sent messengers again the third
> time, and they also prophesied.
> **22** Then he also went to Ramah, and came to the great well that is in Secu: and he asked, "Where are Samuel and
> David?" One said, "Behold, they are at Naioth in Ramah."
> **23** He went there to Naioth in Ramah. Then God's Spirit came on him also, and he went on, and prophesied, until
> he came to Naioth in Ramah.
> **24** He also stripped off his clothes, and he also prophesied before Samuel, and lay down naked all that day and
> all that night. Therefore they say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 19.)

[[David]] goes from the window of his own house to the prophet who anointed him. *Now David fled and escaped, and came
to Samuel at Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him* (18). He and [[Samuel]] go and stay *in Naioth* (18),
which the pericope places four times *in Ramah*. It is the first time the two have been together since the anointing
at Bethlehem (16:13).

[[Saul]] is told where David is (19) and does what he did the night before: he sends. The first messengers are sent
*to seize David*. They see *the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing as head over them*, and *God's
Spirit came on Saul's messengers, and they also prophesied* (20). He sends others, and they prophesy; he sends a third
time, and they prophesy (21).

Then Saul goes himself. At *the great well that is in Secu* he asks *Where are Samuel and David?* and is told *at
Naioth in Ramah* (22). On the road there *God's Spirit came on him also*, and he goes on prophesying until he arrives
(23). He strips off his clothes, prophesies *before Samuel*, and lies *naked all that day and all that night*. The
narrator closes with a proverb: *Therefore they say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"* (24).

**Where it happens.** *Ramah* is Samuel's town, his house, altar, and later his grave (7:17; 25:1, stored WEB). The
wiki files it at [[Ramah]]. *Naioth*, *nāyôṯ* (WLC), is named only in this pericope and at 20:1, *David fled from
Naioth in Ramah*. In the Hebrew files checked it occurs in five verses (19:18, 19, 22, 23; 20:1). Each time the
consonantal text and the vocalization differ (written *nwyt*, read *nāyôṯ*; *nwwt* at 20:1). The phrase *Naioth in
Ramah* suggests a place inside or beside the town, not a second town. It is often taken to mean *dwellings*, perhaps of
the prophets (recalled, not checked); the text does not explain it. See [[Naioth]]. *Secu*, *śeḵû*, occurs only at 19:22
in those files, with no detail beyond its *great well*. See [[Secu]].

**What is going on.** This is the third of the four times in 19:1–20:42 that David is kept from Saul, and the only one
that no one in Saul's house arranges. Jonathan used words (19:1–7) and Michal a window and a bed (19:11–17). Here
there is no ruse and no plea, and David does nothing at all after he arrives. What stops Saul's men is *God's Spirit*,
coming on each group as it arrives, and then on Saul himself. A text that has just said *an evil spirit of Yahweh*,
*rûaḥ YHWH rāʿâ*, was on Saul (19:9, WLC) now has *rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm*, *the Spirit of God*, come on him (19:23), and the
narrator does not say how the two are related.

The pericope is built from the word *also*, *gam*. It comes eight times in five verses: the messengers *also
prophesied*, three times (20, 21 twice); Saul *also* went (22); the Spirit came on him *also* (23); he *also* stripped,
and he *also* prophesied (24); and the proverb asks *Is Saul also …?* (24) (WLC). The repetition makes Saul the last
of a series. Each group sent to fetch David becomes, for a time, one of the prophets, and so does the king who sent
them.

The sending has its own pattern. *Wayyišlaḥ šāʾûl*, *Saul sent*, opened 19:11, 14, and 15; here it comes three more
times (20, 21 twice), so that Saul sends six times in eleven verses. The third time is *wayyōsep̄ šāʾûl wayyišlaḥ*,
*Saul added and sent* (21, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked, *yāsap̄* with *šālaḥ* is the idiom of Noah sending the
dove again (Genesis 8:10), of Jephthah sending messengers to Ammon again (Judges 11:14), and of Balak: *Balak again sent
princes, more, and more honorable than they* (Numbers 22:15, *wayyōsep̄ ʿôḏ bālāq šǝlōaḥ*). Balak sent messengers to
have Israel cursed, and the man he sent for blessed Israel *these three times* (Numbers 24:10). Saul sends three times
to have David seized, and all three groups prophesy. Then *the Spirit of God came on him*, *wattǝhî ʿālāyw … rûaḥ
ʾĕlōhîm* (23). In those files the clause with *ʾĕlōhîm* occurs only here, at 19:20, and of Balaam (Numbers 24:2), who
calls himself one *falling down, and having his eyes open* (24:4, 16). Saul *fell*, *wayyippōl*, naked (24, WLC). The
wiki records the parallel and claims no dependence. See
[[Falling Down Under the Spirit - Balaam and Saul at Naioth]] and
[[The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah]].

The proverb has been told once already. At the hill of God, after Samuel anointed him, *the Spirit of God came mightily
on him, and he prophesied among them*, and people who knew him asked *Is Saul also among the prophets?* (10:10–12).
*Therefore it became a proverb* (10:12); here, *Therefore they say* (19:24). The book gives the saying two origins. In
chapter 10 the Spirit came as a sign that confirmed his anointing. In chapter 19 it stops him from what he came to do,
and leaves him lying before the prophet who had anointed him. See
[[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]].

**What is notable.**

- **Fled and escaped.** *Bāraḥ wayyimmālēṭ* (18, WLC). This is the fifth *mālaṭ* in 19:10–18 and the second
  *bāraḥ* of David, after 19:12 (19:10 has *nās*, from *nûs*). The same pair returns at 22:20 for Abiathar,
  *escaped, and fled after David* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Michal Helps David Escape]].
- **Told him all.** *Wayyaggēḏ lô ʾēṯ kol-ʾăšer ʿāśâ-lô šāʾûl* (18, WLC). The *hiphil* of *nāḡaḏ*, *tell*, is the verb
  of Jonathan's warnings (19:2–7) and Michal's (19:11). Then it turns to Saul's side: *Saul was told* (19, the
  *hophal*) and *when Saul was told* (21, *wayyaggîḏû*, *they told*). Both David and Saul are kept informed, and the
  pericope does not name who tells Saul. David's report to Samuel is not given in his own words. He has no direct
  speech in 19:8–24; his next words are to Jonathan, *What have I done?* (20:1, [[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]]).
- **Samuel and David.** *Wayyēleḵ hûʾ ûšǝmûʾēl wayyēšǝḇû* (18, WLC), *he and Samuel went and lived*. The prophet who
  anointed Saul as *prince* (10:1) and was sent to anoint David (16:1–13) now shelters David from Saul. Samuel speaks
  no word in the pericope. Saul's question in 22 joins the two names, *Samuel and David*.
- **To seize David.** *Lāqaḥaṯ ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ* (20, WLC), the infinitive used at 19:14, when the messengers came *to take
  David* and Michal said *He is sick*. The errand is the same; at Naioth there is nothing in the bed to find, and the
  messengers do not reach David at all.
- **They saw.** *Wayyarʾ* (20, WLC) is singular in the Hebrew, though *messengers* is plural; WEB reads *they saw*.
  The wiki records the form and does not emend it.
- **The company of the prophets.** *Lahăqaṯ hannǝḇîʾîm* (20, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked *lahăqâ* (3862) occurs
  only here; at 10:5, 10 the group was a *ḥeḇel*, *band*. Some read *lahăqâ* as a variant of *qǝhillâ*, *assembly*
  (recalled, not checked). The text does not say whether this is the band of chapter 10. See
  [[Band of Prophets (of Samuel's time)]].
- **Prophesying.** The company are *nibbǝʾîm*, the *niphal* participle; the messengers and Saul *prophesied*,
  *wayyiṯnabbǝʾû* and *wayyiṯnabbēʾ*, the *hitpael* (20, 21, 23, 24, WLC). In the files checked, *nāḇāʾ* occurs only in
  Numbers 11:25–27 and 1 Samuel, and the *niphal* only at 10:11 (Saul *prophesied with the prophets*) and here, of the
  company. Every other form is *hitpael*, including Saul's prophesying with the evil spirit at 18:10. The distribution
  is recorded; what difference the stems make, the text does not say. No words of anyone's prophesying are given.
- **Samuel standing as head over them.** *Ûšǝmûʾēl ʿōmēḏ niṣṣāḇ ʿălêhem* (20, WLC), two participles of standing,
  *standing, stationed over them*. The *niphal* participle of *nāṣaḇ* with *ʿal* is used in the files checked of
  Yahweh standing above the ladder at Bethel (Genesis 28:13), of Balak *standing by his burnt offering* while Balaam
  speaks (Numbers 23:6, 17), and of Doeg the Edomite, *who stood by the servants of Saul* (22:9, stored WEB; not yet
  ingested), where the Hebrew is *niṣṣāḇ ʿal-ʿaḇḏê-šāʾûl*, *set over*. Here it is the only description of what Samuel
  does: he stands at the head of the prophets.
- **Again the third time.** *Wayyōsep̄ šāʾûl wayyišlaḥ malʾāḵîm šǝlišîm* (21, WLC). See above, and Numbers 22:15. The
  verb *yāsap̄* has a history with these two men. At 15:35, *Samuel came no more to see Saul*, *wǝlōʾ-yāsap̄ šǝmûʾēl
  lirʾôṯ ʾeṯ-šāʾûl*, *Samuel did not again see Saul* (WLC). Samuel does not go again; Saul sends again, and then comes.
- **The great well.** *Bôr haggāḏôl ʾăšer baśśeḵû* (22, WLC). WEB's *well* renders *bôr*, in the files checked a
  cistern or pit: Joseph's pit (Genesis 37:20–29), *the dungeon* (40:15; 41:14; Exodus 12:29), and the pits the
  Israelites hid in (13:6). A well is usually *bǝʾēr*. *Secu* occurs only here, and the place is otherwise unknown.
  Some ancient versions read the verse differently (recalled, not checked); the wiki follows the stored WEB and the WLC.
  See [[Secu]].
- **Where are Samuel and David?** *ʾÊp̄ōh šǝmûʾēl wǝḏāwiḏ* (22, WLC). In the files checked *ʾêp̄ōh*, *where?*, is asked
  by Joseph looking for his brothers (Genesis 37:16), by Gideon of the men killed at Tabor (Judges 8:18, where WEB has
  *what kind*), by Naomi, *Where have you gleaned today?* (Ruth 2:19), here, and by David, asking where Saul's grandson
  is so that he can show him kindness (2 Samuel 9:4, WLC; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB stored). Saul asks where David
  is so that he can seize him; David will ask the same word about Saul's house. The answer here comes from no one
  named: *One said*, *wayyōʾmer*.
- **He went on, and prophesied.** *Wayyēleḵ hālôḵ wayyiṯnabbēʾ ʿaḏ-bōʾô* (23, WLC), *he went, going and prophesying,
  until he came*. In 1 Samuel the infinitive absolute *hālôḵ* with a second verb describes the cows lowing as they
  went to Beth Shemesh (6:12) and the tumult in the Philistine camp *going on and increasing* (14:19). Saul is
  overtaken on the road, before he reaches the place; the messengers were overtaken when they saw the prophets.
- **Stripped off his clothes.** *Wayyip̄šaṭ gam-hûʾ bǝḡāḏāyw* (24, WLC). *He also* may mean that the messengers had
  stripped too, or only that Saul did as well as prophesying; the text does not say. In the files checked, *pāšaṭ* of
  garments is used of Joseph's coat (Genesis 37:23), of the priest changing his clothes (Leviticus 6:4 in the Hebrew,
  6:11 in English; 16:23), of Aaron's garments taken off and put on Eleazar (Numbers 20:26, 28), of Jonathan stripping
  off his robe and giving it to David (18:4), and of the Philistines stripping Saul's body (31:8–9, stored WEB; not yet
  ingested). The garments of 18:4 passed to David; here nothing passes to anyone. The wiki records the sequence of
  stripped garments in 1 Samuel and does not read 19:24 as a sign of the kingdom's loss, which the text does not say.
  See [[Aaron's Garments Pass to Eleazar]] and [[Jonathan Loves David]].
- **Before Samuel.** *Lip̄nê šǝmûʾēl* (24, WLC). This is the last time Saul and the living Samuel are in a scene
  together; the next notice of Samuel is his death (25:1, stored WEB), and Saul's last meeting with him is at Endor
  (28:11–19, stored WEB; not yet ingested). On 15:35, see the Notes.
- **Naked.** *ʿĀrōm* (24, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked, the adjective *ʿārōm* (6174) occurs only here and at
  Genesis 2:25, *the man and his wife were both naked, and they were not ashamed*; Genesis 3:7, 10–11 use the related
  *ʿêrōm* (5903). The text passes no judgment on Saul's nakedness and gives no one's reaction to it. See
  [[Nakedness and Covering]] and [[Naked and Ashamed - Genesis 3 and Genesis 9]].
- **He fell.** *Wayyippōl ʿārōm* (24, WLC), literally *he fell naked*; WEB *lay down naked*. Balaam describes himself
  as *falling down, and having his eyes open* (Numbers 24:4, 16, *nōp̄ēl*). At Endor Saul will fall again, *his full
  length on the earth*, *because of Samuel's words* (28:20, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **All that day and all that night.** *Kol-hayyôm hahûʾ wǝḵol-hallaylâ* (24, WLC). In the files checked the phrase
  occurs only here, of the east wind that brought the locusts (Exodus 10:13), and of the people gathering quail (Numbers
  11:32), the chapter in which the elders prophesied. At 28:20 Saul *had eaten no bread all day long or all night
  long*, *kol-hayyôm wǝḵol-hallaylâ*, and falls before Samuel's words. See
  [[All That Day and All That Night - Saul Fallen Before Samuel]].
- **Therefore they say.** *ʿAl-kēn yōʾmǝrû hăḡam šāʾûl bannǝḇîʾim* (24, WLC). In 10:12 it *became a proverb*,
  *māšāl*; here it is something people *say*, in the imperfect. The speakers are not named.
- **Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear.** No one fights, and no one is harmed. The messengers come to seize
  David, and the Spirit stops them; in this pericope Saul has no spear. David told Goliath that *Yahweh doesn't save
  with sword and spear* (17:47). See
  [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].

**How it connects.** Saul's children protected David with an argument and with a ruse. Here God protects him, and
the means is the gift that confirmed Saul's own anointing at the hill of God. The Spirit *departed from Saul* at
16:14, came on David *from that day forward* (16:13), and here comes on Saul again. The text does not reconcile these
statements, and the wiki does not either: at 19:9 it is *an evil spirit*, at 19:23 *the Spirit of God*, and in both
Saul is the one it comes on. Later prophets are protected in a similar way, Elijah from the three captains of fifty
sent to take him (2 Kings 1:9–16) and Elisha from the Aramean force sent to seize him at Dothan (2 Kings 6:13–23)
(recalled, not checked; no Kings text is stored). David leaves while Saul lies there. The next verse is *David fled
from Naioth in Ramah* (20:1), and the next pericope sends him to Jonathan.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 19:18–24 as planned. 19:18 opens with the flight to Ramah and 19:24 closes on
the proverb; 20:1 opens with David leaving Naioth for Jonathan. Two new place pages: [[Naioth]], named five times in
the files checked, and [[Secu]], named once; both are named in the text, and the wiki's practice is to give named
places a page. The *great well* is kept on the Secu page, not given a page of its own. No new person page: the
messengers are three unindividuated groups, as at 19:11–16; the *company of the prophets* goes on the existing
[[Band of Prophets (of Samuel's time)]] page, which already anticipated it, without deciding whether it is the same
group. Two new connections. [[Falling Down Under the Spirit - Balaam and Saul at Naioth]] records the parallel with
Balaam: a sender who sends again, a mission turned to prophecy three times, the same clause for the Spirit, and a
falling. [[All That Day and All That Night - Saul Fallen Before Samuel]] records the two scenes in which Saul falls
before Samuel. The proverb's two origins were already a connection page, so they get a new section there.

**Recorded, not resolved.** 15:35 says *Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death*, and here Saul is
*before Samuel* all day and night. The Hebrew of 15:35 has Samuel as the subject, *Samuel did not again see Saul*
(WLC), and here it is Saul who comes; the text records no word or look between them. Whether the two verses conflict
depends on how *see* is read, and the wiki does not decide. Also recorded and not resolved: how *the Spirit of God*
comes on Saul after *Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul* (16:14); whether the *company* is the *band* of 10:5;
whether *he also stripped* implies the messengers stripped; the meaning of *Naioth* and the site of *Secu*; whether the
two origins of the proverb are two traditions or one story echoing the other (see the connection page).
