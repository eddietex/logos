# Saul's Wars and Household

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 14:47–52

## Summary

> **47** Now when Saul had taken the kingdom over Israel, he fought against all his enemies on every side, against
> Moab, and against the children of Ammon, and against Edom, and against the kings of Zobah, and against the
> Philistines. Wherever he turned himself, he defeated them.
> **48** He did valiantly, and struck the Amalekites, and delivered Israel out of the hands of those who plundered
> them.
> **49** Now the sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchishua; and the names of his two daughters were these:
> the name of the firstborn Merab, and the name of the younger Michal.
> **50** The name of Saul's wife was Ahinoam the daughter of Ahimaaz. The name of the captain of his army was Abner
> the son of Ner, Saul's uncle.
> **51** Kish was the father of Saul; and Ner the father of Abner was the son of Abiel.
> **52** There was severe war against the Philistines all the days of Saul; and when Saul saw any mighty man, or any
> valiant man, he took him into his service.

The story stops, and the narrator sums up a reign. Once [[Saul]] *had taken the kingdom over Israel*, he fought
his enemies *on every side*: Moab ([[Moab (place)]]), the children of [[Ammon]], [[Edom]], *the kings of*
[[Zobah]], and [[The Philistines]], and *wherever he turned himself, he defeated them* (47). He *did valiantly*,
struck the Amalekites ([[Amalek]]), and *delivered Israel out of the hands of those who plundered them* (48).

Then the household. His sons are [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]], [[Ishvi (son of Saul)]], and [[Malchishua]]; his
daughters [[Merab]], the firstborn, and [[Michal]], the younger (49). His wife is [[Ahinoam (wife of Saul)]],
daughter of [[Ahimaaz (father of Ahinoam)]]. The commander of his army is [[Abner]] son of [[Ner]], *Saul's
uncle* (50). [[Kish (father of Saul)]] was Saul's father, and Ner, Abner's father, was the son of
[[Abiel (father of Kish)]] (51). The last verse returns to war: *severe war against the Philistines all the days
of Saul*, and every strong or valiant man Saul saw, he took into his service (52).

**Where it happens.** Nowhere in particular. The list of enemies is a compass: Moab and Ammon east of the
Jordan, Edom to the south-east, Zobah to the north, the Philistines to the west, and Amalek in the south (the
compass reading of Zobah depends on its usual placement in Syria, which the passage does not give; see [[Zobah]]).
Saul's home at [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] is not named.

**What is going on.** This is a *summary notice*, the kind of paragraph that closes an account of a reign. It
stands between the battle of Michmash and the rejection over Amalek (chapter 15), and it reads as though Saul's
story were over. In one sense it is: chapter 15 removes the kingdom from him, and chapter 16 anoints David. From
here on Saul is a king in decline in a story about someone else. The narrator gives his reign its due before
that begins. The record is a strong one: wars on every side, victory wherever he turned, Israel delivered from its
plunderers.

The summary has the shape of a judge's success more than of a dynasty's founding. *Those who plundered them*,
*šōsēhû* (48, WLC), is the word of the programme of Judges: *he delivered them into the hands of raiders who
plundered them … Yahweh raised up judges, who saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them* (Judges
2:14, 16, stored WEB). In the WLC of Genesis–2 Samuel the participle occurs only in those two verses, here, and
at 1 Samuel 23:1 (morphhb lemma 8154). Judges 2:14 also has *their enemies all around*, *ʾôyəḇêhem missāḇîḇ*;
Saul fights *sāḇîḇ bəḵol-ʾōyəḇāyw*, *on every side against all his enemies* (47, WLC). The king the people asked
for *like all the nations* (8:5) is described, at the height of his reign, in the vocabulary of the judges he
replaced.

The household list prepares the story ahead. Every name in verses 49–51 except Ishvi, Ahimaaz, and Abiel acts
in the chapters that follow (stored WEB; not yet ingested). Jonathan loves David (18:1–4; [[Jonathan Loves David]]). Merab is promised to
David and given to another man (18:17–19). Michal loves David and marries him (18:20–27). Abner seats himself
beside Saul at the new moon (20:25) and guards him in the camp David enters (26:5–15). Malchishua dies with
Jonathan and Abinadab at Gilboa (31:2). The last verse, *when Saul saw any mighty man … he took him*, is the
narrator's hinge into chapter 16: a servant describes a son of Jesse as *a mighty man of valor*, *gibbôr ḥayil*,
and Saul takes him into his service, *he became his armor bearer* (16:18, 21, stored WEB; WLC). The household and
the court the narrator has just catalogued are the ones David enters.

**What is notable.**

- **Had taken the kingdom.** *Lāḵaḏ hamməlûḵâ* (47, WLC). *Lāḵaḏ* is the verb of the lot: *Saul the son of Kish
  was chosen*, *wayyillāḵēḏ* (10:21), and *Saul and Jonathan were taken* (14:41, 42) (WLC). In 1 Samuel it occurs
  six times; the five others are passive (niphal) and all describe the lot falling (10:20, 21 twice; 14:41, 42).
  Here alone Saul is its subject (qal) (morphhb lemma 3920, `1Sam`). The man *taken* by the lot is now the one who
  *takes*. Whether the verb implies seizure or only accession is not settled by its form; the WEB has *had taken*.
- **Wherever he turned himself, he defeated them.** *Bəḵōl ʾăšer-yip̄neh yaršîaʿ* (47, WLC). *Yaršîaʿ* is the
  hiphil of *rāšaʿ*, in the two other occurrences in Genesis–2 Samuel *condemn* or *declare guilty* (Exodus 22:9
  WEB, 22:8 WLC; Deuteronomy 25:1) (morphhb lemma 7561). Its sense here, *he defeated them* or *he punished them*,
  is the WEB's reading of an unusual use. That the Septuagint has *he was saved* here instead, implying a different
  Hebrew verb, is commonly reported (recalled, not checked; no Septuagint was consulted). The David summary uses
  the same frame with a different subject: *Yahweh gave victory to David wherever he went*, *wayyōšaʿ YHWH
  ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ bəḵōl ʾăšer hālāḵ* (2 Samuel 8:6, 14, WEB at bible-api.com; WLC). Of Saul the text says he prevailed;
  of David, that Yahweh saved him. See [[Saul's Wars and David's Wars - 1 Samuel 14.47-52 and 2 Samuel 8]].
- **Turned.** *Yip̄neh* (47, WLC) is the verb used three times of the Philistine raiders a chapter earlier, *one
  company turned to the way that leads to Ophrah* (13:17–18, stored WEB; WLC, morphhb lemma 6437). There the raiders
  turned where they liked; here Saul does.
- **The list of enemies.** Moab, Ammon, Edom, the kings of Zobah, the Philistines, then Amalek (47–48). Five of the
  six reappear in the list of nations David subdues and plunders, *of Syria, of Moab, of the children of Ammon, of
  the Philistines, of Amalek, and of the plunder of Hadadezer … king of Zobah* (2 Samuel 8:12, WEB at
  bible-api.com). Edom is there too (8:14). Of Saul's six wars, the narrative has told one, against Ammon at
  Jabesh Gilead (11:1–11), and the Philistine battle at Michmash (13–14); the Amalekite war comes next (15). Moab,
  Edom, and Zobah are known only from this verse.
- **The kings of Zobah.** *Malḵê ṣôḇâ* (47, WLC), plural. Zobah's first appearance in Scripture; in Genesis–2
  Samuel it recurs only in 2 Samuel (8:3, 5, 12; 10:6, 8; 23:36, WLC). See [[Zobah]].
- **He did valiantly.** *Wayyaʿaś ḥāyil* (48, WLC). *Ḥayil* recurs at 14:52, *ben-ḥayil*, *valiant man*, and Saul's
  father was a *gibbôr ḥāyil* (9:1); so is the son of Jesse (16:18) (WLC). The army that followed Saul home from
  Mizpah was *haḥayil*, *the army … whose hearts God had touched* (10:26, stored WEB; WLC).
- **Struck the Amalekites.** *Wayyak ʾeṯ-ʿămālēq* (48, WLC). Placed before chapter 15, the verse tells in a clause
  the war that chapter 15 will tell at length, with the same verb (*Saul struck the Amalekites*, 15:7, stored
  WEB). The summary credits the war as a deliverance and says nothing of Agag or the spoil. Whether it summarises
  chapter 15 in advance or records a separate campaign, the text does not say.
- **Ishvi.** *Yišwî* (49, WLC). The list of sons differs from the list of the dead at Gilboa (*Jonathan, Abinadab,
  and Malchishua*, 31:2) and from Chronicles (*Jonathan, Malchishua, Abinadab, and Eshbaal*, 1 Chronicles 8:33;
  9:39, WEB at bible-api.com), and none of the three names Ishbosheth, *the son of Saul* whom Abner makes king
  (2 Samuel 2:8, WEB at bible-api.com). Whether Ishvi is another name for one of them, the text does not say. See
  [[Ishvi (son of Saul)]].
- **Two daughters, firstborn and younger.** *Habbəḵîrâ* and *haqqəṭannâ* (49, WLC), the pair used of Leah and
  Rachel (Genesis 29:26, *the younger before the firstborn*, stored WEB). Here too the elder is promised and the
  younger loves (18:17–20).
- **Abiner.** The commander's name is spelled *ʾăḇînēr* at 14:50 and *ʾaḇnēr* at 14:51 and everywhere else (WLC;
  morphhb gives both lemma 74). The WEB writes *Abner* both times.
- **Saul's uncle.** *Dôḏ šāʾûl* (50, WLC) stands after *Abner the son of Ner*, and can be read of either man.
  Verse 51 makes Ner and Kish both sons of Abiel (with 9:1), so that Ner is Saul's uncle and Abner his cousin;
  1 Chronicles 8:33; 9:39 instead make Ner Kish's father (WEB at bible-api.com). The tension is recorded on
  [[Kish (father of Saul)]] and [[Ner]], and in [[Saul's Uncle]], whom 10:14–16 does not name.
- **He took him into his service.** *Wayyaʾasp̄ēhû ʾēlāyw* (52, WLC), literally *he gathered him to himself*.
  Samuel had warned that the king *will take your sons, and appoint them as his servants* (8:11, stored WEB), with
  a different verb, *yiqqāḥ* (WLC). The warning is now practice.

**How it connects.** The pericope closes the first movement of Saul's reign: anointed (9:26–10:16), chosen
(10:17–27), proved at Jabesh (11), warned (12), sentenced at Gilgal (13:1–15), victorious at Michmash (14:1–23),
and caught in his own oath (14:24–46). The summary records the military success that the people asked for, *go
out before us, and fight our battles* (8:20), and it records it while Samuel's sentence of 13:13–14 stands
unrevoked. The next pericope, *Saul Rejected over Amalek* (15:1–35, planned, not yet ingested), turns on the war verse
48 has already credited to him.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 14:47–52 as planned: 14:46 ends the pursuit, and 15:1 begins a new
commission. Eight new person pages, because every name in 49–51 either acts later in 1 Samuel or anchors Saul's
genealogy: [[Ishvi (son of Saul)]] (qualified against the Ishvi of Asher, Genesis 46:17; Numbers 26:44),
[[Malchishua]], [[Merab]], [[Michal]], [[Ahinoam (wife of Saul)]] (qualified against Ahinoam of Jezreel, David's
wife, 25:43), [[Ahimaaz (father of Ahinoam)]] (qualified against the later Ahimaaz son of Zadok, recalled),
[[Abner]], [[Ner]], and [[Abiel (father of Kish)]] (qualified against Abiel of David's mighty men, recalled). One
new place, [[Zobah]]. One new connection,
[[Saul's Wars and David's Wars - 1 Samuel 14.47-52 and 2 Samuel 8]]. The Moab link goes to [[Moab (place)]]
because the verse names the nation, not the man.

**Unresolved.** Whether Ner or Abner is *Saul's uncle* at 14:50, and how 14:51 and 9:1 relate to 1 Chronicles
8:33; 9:39. Whether Ishvi is another name of Abinadab, Ishbosheth/Eshbaal, or a fourth son. What *yaršîaʿ* means
here, and the Greek reading. Whether 14:48 anticipates chapter 15 or records an earlier campaign. Where Zobah lay.
