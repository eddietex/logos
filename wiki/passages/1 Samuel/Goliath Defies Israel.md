# Goliath Defies Israel

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 17:1–11

## Summary

> **1** Now the Philistines gathered together their armies to battle; and they were gathered together at Socoh, which
> belongs to Judah, and encamped between Socoh and Azekah, in Ephesdammim.
> **2** Saul and the men of Israel were gathered together, and encamped in the valley of Elah, and set the battle in
> array against the Philistines.
> **3** The Philistines stood on the mountain on the one side, and Israel stood on the mountain on the other side: and
> there was a valley between them.
> **4** A champion out of the camp of the Philistines named Goliath, of Gath, whose height was six cubits and a span
> went out.
> **5** He had a helmet of brass on his head, and he wore a coat of mail; and the weight of the coat was five thousand
> shekels of brass.
> **6** He had brass shin armor on his legs, and a brass javelin between his shoulders.
> **7** The staff of his spear was like a weaver's beam; and his spear's head weighed six hundred shekels of iron. His
> shield bearer went before him.
> **8** He stood and cried to the armies of Israel, and said to them, "Why have you come out to set your battle in
> array? Am I not a Philistine, and you servants to Saul? Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me.
> **9** If he is able to fight with me and kill me, then will we be your servants; but if I prevail against him and
> kill him, then you will be our servants and serve us."
> **10** The Philistine said, "I defy the armies of Israel today! Give me a man, that we may fight together!"
> **11** When Saul and all Israel heard those words of the Philistine, they were dismayed, and greatly afraid.

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 17.)

[[The Philistines]] gather their armies *at Socoh, which belongs to Judah*, and camp *between Socoh and Azekah, in
Ephesdammim* (1). [[Saul]] and *the men of Israel* gather and camp *in the valley of Elah*, and set the battle in array
(2). The two armies stand on two mountains, *and there was a valley between them* (3). Out of the Philistine camp comes
*a champion … named Goliath, of Gath*, six cubits and a span tall (4). The narrator describes his armor piece by piece:
a bronze helmet, a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels of bronze, bronze greaves, a bronze javelin between his
shoulders, a spear with a shaft *like a weaver's beam* and a head of six hundred shekels of iron, and a shield bearer
going before him (5–7).

Then he speaks. *Why have you come out to set your battle in array? Am I not a Philistine, and you servants to Saul?
Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me* (8). He proposes that one fight decide the war, and that the
loser's people serve the winner's (9). *I defy the armies of Israel today! Give me a man, that we may fight together!*
(10). The pericope ends on the answer: *When Saul and all Israel heard those words of the Philistine, they were
dismayed, and greatly afraid* (11).

**Where it happens.** The Philistines are inside Judah, in the lowland. [[Socoh (lowland)]] and [[Azekah]] stand next to
each other in Judah's first lowland list, *Jarmuth, Adullam, Socoh, Azekah* (Joshua 15:35), and 17:1 names Socoh
*which belongs to Judah*; that is why this page links the lowland Socoh and not [[Socoh (hill country)]] (15:48).
[[Ephes Dammim]] is named only here in the Hebrew files checked (*ʾep̄es dammîm*, 1, WLC). The [[Valley of Elah]] is
the battlefield, and 21:9 will remember it as the place *whom you killed in the valley of Elah* (stored WEB; not yet
ingested). [[Gath]] is Goliath's city, one of the three where the Anakim remnant stayed (Joshua 11:22).

**What is going on.** The chapter opens with a standoff, not a battle. Both armies are *gathered*, *ʾāsap̄* (1, 2,
WLC), both *set the battle in array* (2, 8), and neither moves. A valley lies between them. Into the gap steps one man,
and the narrator stops to measure him. The description runs four verses and gives his height, the metal of each piece,
and the weight of two of them. No one in
Israel answers, and in this pericope no one prays, inquires, or names Yahweh. Neither *Yahweh* nor *God* occurs in
17:1–11 (WLC). The first to name him is David, at 17:26, and he answers Goliath's phrase *the armies of Israel* with
*the armies of the living God* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).

Saul is the man the reader expects to go down. The people asked for a king who would *go out before us, and fight our
battles* (8:20). Saul was taller than all Israel *from his shoulders and upward* (9:2; 10:23). Here a taller man *went
out* (4), calls for *a man*, and Saul hears and is afraid with everyone else (11).

**What is notable.**

- **The champion, the man of the between.** *ʾÎš habbēnayim* (4, WLC), WEB *champion*. *Bēnayim* is a dual of the
  preposition *bên*, *between*, and in the Hebrew files checked (Genesis through 2 Samuel, and Ruth) the word occurs
  only here and at 17:23 (morphhb lemma 1143). The verse before has the valley *between them*, *bênêhem* (3, WLC;
  lemma 996). The usual explanation is *the man who fights between the armies* (recalled, not checked). The wiki notes
  that the champion is named from the space he stands in.
- **Height.** *Gāḇhô šēš ʾammôṯ wāzāreṯ* (4, WLC), *his height six cubits and a span*. The root *g-b-h* has run through
  the book: Saul *gāḇōah*, *taller*, than all the people (9:2), *wayyiḡbah*, *he was higher* (10:23), and Eliab's
  *the height of his stature*, *gəḇōah qômāṯô*, which Yahweh told Samuel not to look at (16:7) (WLC; stored WEB). Each
  verse uses a different form, and morphhb gives them three lemma numbers (1363, 1364, 1361), but the consonants are
  the same. The Septuagint and a Qumran manuscript are reported to read *four cubits and a span* (recalled, not
  checked; no Septuagint or Qumran text was consulted). The wiki records the Masoretic height and the report of the
  other reading, and does not convert cubits to feet. See [[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]].
- **Bronze, four times.** *Nəḥōšeṯ* names the helmet, the weight of the coat, the greaves, and the javelin (5–6, WLC).
  The spearhead alone is iron, *barzel* (7). Israel, at 13:19–22, had no smith, and *neither sword nor spear was found
  in the hand of any of the people … but Saul and Jonathan his son had them* (stored WEB). See [[No Smith in Israel]].
- **Scales.** *Širyôn qaśqaśśîm* (5, WLC), a coat of mail, literally *of scales*. *Qaśqeśeṯ* (lemma 7193) occurs in
  the Hebrew files checked only here and in the food laws, the *fins and scales* of clean fish (Leviticus 11:9–12;
  Deuteronomy 14:9–10, stored WEB). The wiki records the word, not a meaning for the echo. *Širyôn* occurs in those
  files only here and at 17:38, where Saul puts a coat of mail on David (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The javelin.** *Kîḏôn* (6, WLC; lemma 3591). In the Hebrew files checked it occurs four times: Joshua's javelin
  stretched out toward Ai (Joshua 8:18, 26), Goliath's here, and in David's answer, *You come to me with a sword, with a
  spear, and with a javelin* (17:45) (stored WEB). Joshua's was held out *until he had utterly destroyed all the
  inhabitants of Ai* (Joshua 8:26). The wiki notes the two javelins; the text does not compare them.
- **A weaver's beam.** *Kimnôr ʾōrəḡîm* (7, WLC). The phrase recurs in the Hebrew files checked only at 2 Samuel 21:19,
  of the spear of the Goliath Elhanan kills (lemma 4500). See the contradiction note below, and
  [[Goliath and Elhanan - 1 Samuel 17 and 2 Samuel 21.19]].
- **Arrow or shaft.** The consonantal text of verse 7 reads *wḥṣ*, *and the arrow*; the margin reads *wəʿēṣ*, *and the
  wood*, the shaft (WLC ketiv and qere). The WEB follows the reading, *the staff of his spear*. 2 Samuel 21:19 has
  *wəʿēṣ* with no variant (WLC).
- **His shield bearer.** *Nōśēʾ haṣṣinnâ* (7, WLC). The man goes before him again at 17:41 (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested). *Ṣinnâ*, the large shield, occurs in the Hebrew files checked only in these two verses. The previous
  pericope ended with David as Saul's *nōśēʾ ḵēlîm*, armor bearer (16:21). See [[Jonathan's Armor Bearer]].
- **Why have you come out?** *Lāmmâ ṯēṣəʾû* (8, WLC). Goliath *went out*, *wayyēṣēʾ* (4); Israel asked for a king who
  would *go out before us*, *wəyāṣāʾ ləp̄ānênû* (8:20, WLC). In this pericope only the Philistine goes out.
- **The armies of Israel.** *Maʿarḵôṯ yiśrāʾēl* (8, 10, WLC; lemma 4634), the ranks drawn up for battle. In the
  Hebrew files checked the word is concentrated in 1 Samuel 4 and 17, and chapter 17 has it in ten verses. David takes up
  Goliath's phrase three times: *the armies of the living God* (17:26, 36) and *the God of the armies of Israel* (17:45)
  (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Servants.** *ʿĂḇāḏîm* three times in two verses: *you servants to Saul*; *we will be your servants*; *you will be
  our servants and serve us* (8–9, WLC). The duel is for which people serves which. Samuel had warned that the king
  would make Israel *his servants* (8:17), and under the Philistines Israel had already gone down to them to sharpen
  its tools (13:20) (stored WEB). See [[Slavery and Servitude]].
- **Choose a man.** *Bərû-lāḵem ʾîš* (8, WLC). The verb is rare; in the Hebrew files checked morphhb gives it the lemma
  (1262) it also gives to *eat* in 2 Samuel 3:35; 12:17; 13:5–10. Whether these are one verb or two is a lexical
  question this page does not settle. *A man* comes twice, *ʾîš* (8, 10), and Goliath himself is *ʾîš habbēnayim* (4).
- **I defy.** *Ḥēraptî* (10, WLC), from *ḥārap̄*, *taunt, reproach* (lemma 2778). In the Hebrew files checked it occurs
  nine times, five of them in this chapter (17:10, 25, 26, 36, 45); the others are Zebulun who *jeopardized their
  lives* (Judges 5:18), the men of Succoth who *taunted* Gideon (Judges 8:15), and two later Philistine defiances
  (2 Samuel 21:21; 23:9, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored). David's *takes away the reproach from Israel*
  (17:26) uses the related noun *ḥerpâ*. See [[The Reproach of Israel - Joshua 5.9, 1 Samuel 11.2, and 17.26]].
- **Today.** *Hayyôm hazzeh* (10, WLC). 17:16 has him come out *morning and evening … forty days* (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested).
- **Dismayed, and greatly afraid.** *Wayyēḥattû wayyirʾû məʾōḏ* (11, WLC). *Ḥāṯaṯ*, *be dismayed, shattered* (lemma
  2865), occurs in the Hebrew files checked six times. Five are commands not to be: *Don't be afraid, neither be
  dismayed* (Deuteronomy 1:21), *Don't be afraid. Don't be discouraged* (31:8), *Don't be dismayed* (Joshua 1:9), *Don't
  be afraid, and don't be dismayed* (8:1), *Don't be afraid, nor be dismayed* (10:25) (stored WEB). Four of those pair it
  with *yārēʾ*, *fear*, as 17:11 does. The sixth is this verse, the first time Israel is said to do what it was told
  not to. See [[Afraid and Dismayed - Deuteronomy 1.21, Joshua 1.9, and 1 Samuel 17.11]].
- **Those words.** *Wayyišmaʿ šāʾûl wəḵol-yiśrāʾēl ʾeṯ-diḇrê happəlištî hāʾēlleh* (11, WLC). At Jabesh Gilead, *God's
  Spirit came mightily on Saul when he heard those words*, *ʾeṯ-hadəḇārîm hāʾēlleh*, *and his anger burned hot* (11:6,
  stored WEB; WLC). The same king hears *those words* again, and this time he is afraid. In 16:14 the Spirit had
  departed from him. The narrator does not connect the two verses; the wiki notes them side by side.

**How it connects.** The pericope sets up the rest of chapter 17. 17:12–40 brings David from the sheep with bread for
his brothers, to hear the same words (17:23); 17:41–54 is the fight (stored WEB; not yet ingested). Goliath's sword will
be kept at Nob *wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod* and given to David (21:9; 22:10, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
Gath, Goliath's city, will be David's refuge twice, under Achish (21:10; 27:2, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The
scene also closes the long line of the giants of Canaan: the Anakim of the spies' report, cut off by Joshua except in
Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod (Numbers 13:33; Joshua 11:22). 1 Samuel 17 does not call Goliath one of the Anakim; the link
is the city.

**Contradiction flagged.** 1 Samuel 17 has David kill Goliath of Gath (17:4, 50–51). 2 Samuel 21:19 reads, in the WLC,
*Elhanan the son of Jaare-Oregim the Bethlehemite struck Goliath the Gittite, and the shaft of his spear was like a
weaver's beam*; the Hebrew has no word for *brother*. The WEB reads *killed Goliath the Gittite's brother*, supplying
the word from 1 Chronicles 20:5, *Elhanan the son of Jair killed Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite* (WEB checked
at bible-api.com; not stored). Readers have explained this by a scribal error in 2 Samuel, by two men named Goliath, by
Elhanan as another name for David, or by a tradition that later passed to David (all recalled, not checked). The wiki
records both accounts, and the WEB's harmonizing word, and does not reconcile them. The same note is on [[Goliath]] and
the connection page.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 17:1–11 as planned: verse 12 turns to Jesse and David with a fresh introduction.
New pages: [[Goliath]], [[Ephes Dammim]], and [[Valley of Elah]]; the Socoh of 17:1 is the lowland Socoh of Joshua 15:35,
for the reason given above. No page for Goliath's shield bearer (7), who returns at 17:41 but is never named; the
wiki can add one if he becomes more than a line. Two new connections:
[[Afraid and Dismayed - Deuteronomy 1.21, Joshua 1.9, and 1 Samuel 17.11]], because *ḥāṯaṯ* occurs only six times in
the files checked and 17:11 is the only one that is not a command, and
[[Goliath and Elhanan - 1 Samuel 17 and 2 Samuel 21.19]], for the contradiction. The *g-b-h* root, the scale armor,
and the two javelins are noted, not argued.

**Unresolved.** The height (six cubits, or four in the reported Greek and Qumran reading). The relation of 1 Samuel 17
to 2 Samuel 21:19. What *ʾîš habbēnayim* meant as a term. Whether *bārâ*, *choose*, is the same verb as *eat*.
