**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 20:1–42

(Excerpts; the full text is in `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 20.)

[[David]] leaves [[Naioth]] in [[Ramah]] while [[Saul]] lies among the prophets and goes straight to
[[Jonathan (son of Saul)]]: *What have I done? What is my iniquity? What is my sin before your father, that he seeks my
life?* (1). Jonathan refuses to believe it: *Far from it; you will not die*, because Saul hides nothing from him,
*either great or small* (2). David swears in reply that Saul knows Jonathan favors him and is hiding it *lest he be
grieved*, *but truly as Yahweh lives, and as your soul lives, there is but a step between me and death* (3). Jonathan
agrees to do whatever David asks (4).

David proposes a test at the next day's new-moon feast, where he is expected to dine with the king (5). He will hide in
the field until the third evening; if Saul misses him, Jonathan will say David earnestly asked leave to run to
[[Bethlehem]] for *the yearly sacrifice there for all the family* (5–6). If Saul says *It is well*, there is peace; if
he is angry, *evil is determined by him* (7). David appeals to *a covenant of Yahweh* between them, and adds: *if there
is iniquity in me, kill me yourself* (8). Jonathan rejects the thought and promises to tell him if evil is determined
(9). Asked how he will get word out if Saul answers roughly, Jonathan takes David out into the field (10–11).

There Jonathan swears a self-curse oath — *Yahweh do so to Jonathan, and more also* — to sound out his father and send
David away in peace if Saul means him evil, and speaks a blessing over him: *May Yahweh be with you, as he has been
with my father* (12–13). Then he names his own terms for the future: David must show him *the loving kindness of Yahweh*
while he lives, and *not cut off your kindness from my house forever; no, not when Yahweh has cut off every one of the
enemies of David from the surface of the earth* (14–15). *So Jonathan made a covenant with David's house*, and causes
David to swear again, *for he loved him as he loved his own soul* (16–17). He arranges a signal for the third day at
[[The Stone Ezel]]: he will shoot three arrows and send a boy after them; *the arrows are on this side of you* means
peace, *the arrows are beyond you* means *go your way; for Yahweh has sent you away* (18–22). *Yahweh is between you
and me forever* (23).

David hides in the field. At the new-moon meal Saul takes his usual seat *by the wall*, Jonathan stands up (or sits
opposite, as some versions read), [[Abner]] sits at Saul's side, and David's place is empty (24–25). Saul says nothing
on the first day, assuming ritual impurity: *Something has happened to him. He is not clean. Surely he is not clean*
(26). On the second day David's seat is still empty, and Saul asks Jonathan, *Why doesn't the son of Jesse come to eat,
either yesterday, or today?* (27). Jonathan delivers the Bethlehem excuse and expands it: *My brother has commanded me
to be there … please let me go away and see my brothers* (28–29).

Saul's anger explodes. He curses Jonathan — *You son of a perverse rebellious woman, don't I know that you have chosen
the son of Jesse to your own shame, and to the shame of your mother's nakedness?* — and names the dynasty at stake: *as
long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, you will not be established, nor will your kingdom* (30–31). He orders
Jonathan to send and fetch him, *for he shall surely die!* (31). Jonathan asks, *Why should he be put to death? What
has he done?* (32). Saul throws his spear at his own son to strike him, and Jonathan knows David's death is settled (33).
He rises from the table in fierce anger and eats nothing on the second day, *grieved for David, because his father had
treated him shamefully* (34).

In the morning Jonathan goes out to the field with a little boy, shoots an arrow beyond him, and calls after him,
*Isn't the arrow beyond you?* and *Go fast! Hurry! Don't delay!* (35–38). The boy gathers the arrows, knowing nothing:
*Only Jonathan and David knew the matter* (39). Jonathan sends the boy back to the city with his weapons (40). As soon
as the boy is gone, David rises *out of the south*, falls on his face, and bows three times; they kiss and weep
together, *and David wept the most* (41). Jonathan sends him off: *Go in peace, because we have both sworn in Yahweh's
name, saying, 'Yahweh is between me and you, and between my offspring and your offspring, forever.'* David departs, and
Jonathan goes back into the city (42).

**Where it happens.** The chapter opens with David's flight from [[Naioth]] in [[Ramah]] (1), the fifth and last mention
of Naioth in the stored books. The rest of the scene moves between an unnamed city — Saul's table (24–34), where the
boy takes Jonathan's weapons and Jonathan returns (40, 42), presumably Saul's seat at [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] (10:26;
15:34), though ch. 20 never names it — and *the field* outside it (5, 11, 24, 35), where David hides by
[[The Stone Ezel]] (19). [[Bethlehem]] is named twice as the invented destination of David's leave (6, 28).

**What is going on.** This is the fourth and last escape in the sequence of 19:1–20:42, and it closes the section of 1
Samuel in which David lives inside Saul's house (chapters 16–20). Jonathan's plea worked briefly (19:1–7); Michal's
window saved David for one night (19:8–17); the Spirit at Naioth stopped Saul without human help (19:18–24). Here the
rupture becomes final. Up to this chapter David has kept returning to Saul's presence or to his own house; from 20:42
onward he is a fugitive at Nob, Gath, Adullam, and the wilderness until Saul dies.

The turning point is Jonathan's complete reversal of standing. At the beginning of the chapter Jonathan still believes
his father will not kill David without telling him (2). By the middle of the chapter — even before the feast — Jonathan
speaks as a man who already knows David will reign and Saul's enemies will be cut off (13–15), and asks David to spare
his own life and his house. At the table Saul makes the dynastic conflict explicit: *you will not be established, nor
will your kingdom* (31). And when Jonathan asks the exact question he had argued successfully in 19:4–5 — *Why should he
be put to death? What has he done?* (32) — Saul answers with the spear (33). Jonathan has chosen David over his own
throne, and Saul treats his son as he treated David.

**What is notable.**

- **Jonathan's disbelief after 19:1.** In 19:1 Saul *spoke to Jonathan his son, and to all his servants, that they
  should kill David*, and yet here Jonathan answers David, *Far from it; you will not die. Behold, my father does
  nothing either great or small, but that he discloses it to me* (2). Saul had sworn *As Yahweh lives, he shall not be
  put to death* (19:6), and Jonathan was not narrated as present for the third spear, the messengers at David's house,
  or Naioth (19:8–24). Whether Jonathan assumes the oath of 19:6 still holds or two strands of tradition meet at the
  chapter seam is not explained by the text; the wiki records the tension and does not resolve it.
- **As Yahweh lives, and as your soul lives.** David's oath in verse 3 joins the divine oath (*as Yahweh lives*) to the
  royal or personal oath (*as your soul lives*). In the stored books this doubled form appears here for the first time;
  Abigail uses the exact same pair to David at 25:26 (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[By the Life of Pharaoh - Oaths Sworn by a King]] and [[Oaths and Vows]].
- **A step between me and death.** *There is but a step between me and death* (3) is David's sharpest statement of his
  danger so far. The contrast with Jonathan's *you will not die* (2) and Saul's later *he shall surely die!* (31) frames
  the whole chapter around life and death.
- **The new moon and ritual cleanness.** The *new moon* (5, 18, 24, 27, 34) appears in Numbers 10:10 and 28:11–15 as a
  day of trumpets and sanctuary burnt offerings. Here, for the first time in the stored narrative, it is a royal banquet
  lasting at least two days, with assigned seats (25) and a purity requirement: Saul excuses David's absence on day one
  by thinking, *Something has happened to him. He is not clean. Surely he is not clean* (26). Under Leviticus 15:16 and
  Deuteronomy 23:10–11, a nocturnal emission left a man unclean until evening, when bathing restored cleanness; so a
  one-day absence could be explained by ritual impurity, while a second empty day (27) could not. See
  [[Clean and Unclean]] and [[Sabbath and New Moon - Numbers 28, Ezekiel 46, Isaiah 66, and Colossians 2]].
- **The fabricated family sacrifice.** David invents a *yearly sacrifice* for all his family at [[Bethlehem]] (6), and
  Jonathan embellishes the story at the table (*My brother has commanded me to be there … please let me go away and see
  my brothers*, 28–29). As with Samuel's heifer at Bethlehem (16:2–5) and Michal's teraphim (19:13–17), the narrator
  passes no moral verdict on the deception. See [[Deception]].
- **Covenant of Yahweh, and kindness forever.** In 18:3 Jonathan and David made a covenant with no terms and no mention
  of God. Here David calls it *a covenant of Yahweh* (8), and Jonathan expands it in three steps: a self-curse oath in
  Yahweh's name (*Yahweh do so to Jonathan, and more also*, 13), a request for *the loving kindness of Yahweh* while he
  lives and *kindness* never cut off from his house *forever* (14–15), and a formal *covenant with David's house* (16)
  sealed by a second oath (17) and a perpetual witness formula — *Yahweh is between me and you, and between my offspring
  and your offspring, forever* (23, 42). The covenant governs David's later sparing of and provision for Jonathan's son
  Mephibosheth (2 Samuel 9:1–13; 21:7, recalled, not checked), and Saul himself will ask David for the same promise not
  to cut off his offspring at En Gedi (24:21–22, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[Kindness to My House Forever - Jonathan's Covenant and Mephibosheth]],
  [[God Do So to Me - The Self-Curse Oath in Ruth and 1 Samuel]], and
  [[A Witness Between Us - Galeed, the Jordan Altar, and the Shechem Stone]].
- **May Yahweh be with you, as he has been with my father.** Jonathan's blessing in verse 13 transfers the succession
  formula of Israel's leadership — spoken by the eastern tribes to Joshua, *Only may Yahweh your God be with you, as he
  was with Moses* (Joshua 1:17, stored WEB) — from his own father to David. The narrator has already stated that *Yahweh
  was with him, and had departed from Saul* (18:12); here Saul's heir speaks that transfer aloud. See
  [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].
- **The seat by the wall, and the son of Jesse.** Saul sits *on the seat by the wall* (25), the wall against which he
  had tried to pin David (18:11; 19:10), with [[Abner]] beside him. Throughout his outburst Saul never speaks David's
  name, calling him *the son of Jesse* three times (27, 30, 31) — a distancing habit that continues at Gibeah (22:7, 8,
  13, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Jesse]].
- **The insult to Jonathan's mother.** *You son of a perverse rebellious woman, don't I know that you have chosen the
  son of Jesse to your own shame, and to the shame of your mother's nakedness?* (30). Saul's only wife named in 1 Samuel
  is [[Ahinoam (wife of Saul)]] (14:50), but 20:30 does not name Jonathan's mother. The insult uses *nakedness* as
  extreme family dishonor (compare [[Nakedness and Covering]]), and Jonathan leaves the table *grieved for David,
  because his father had treated him shamefully* (34).
- **The fourth spear.** *Saul cast his spear at him to strike him* (33) is Saul's fourth spear throw in three chapters
  — twice at David in 18:11, a third time at David in 19:10, and now at his own son. Where Jonathan's first advocacy
  drew Saul's oath *he shall not be put to death* (19:6), his second — *Why should he be put to death? What has he
  done?* (32) — draws the spear at Jonathan himself. See
  [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].
- **Peace as test and farewell.** *Peace* frames the whole episode: *your servant shall have peace* (7), *that you may
  go in peace* (13), *there is peace to you and no danger* (21), and Jonathan's last word to David here, *Go in peace*
  (42). Peace is absent from Saul's house, yet remains intact between the two men through their sworn covenant. See
  [[Strife and Peacemaking]].
- **The arrows and the parting.** The prearranged signal uses three arrows (20) and an unwitting boy (*the boy didn't
  know anything. Only Jonathan and David knew the matter*, 39) so Jonathan can warn David without openly meeting him
  (21–22). In execution, Jonathan shoots *an arrow* (36–37, singular; *the arrows*, 38, plural), sends the boy back to
  the city with his weapons (40), and then meets David face to face anyway (41–42). David rises *out of the south* (41),
  falls on his face, and bows three times before the prince; they kiss and weep together, *and David wept the most* (41).
  Compare Jacob bowing seven times before Esau, followed by kissing and weeping (Genesis 33:3–4, stored WEB). Across
  18:1–20:17 the narrator stated love only from Jonathan's side; at the parting, it is David whose grief exceeds
  Jonathan's.

**How it connects.** Chapter 20 closes the arc of David at Saul's court (16–20) and launches his fugitive years (21–27).
Jonathan and David will meet only once more in the entire book, in the woods at Horesh, where Jonathan *strengthened
his hand in God*, says plainly *you will be king over Israel, and I will be next to you*, and renews their covenant a
third time *before Yahweh* (23:16–18, stored WEB; not yet ingested). After Jonathan falls on Gilboa (31:2), the oath
sworn in this field — *not cut off your kindness from my house forever* — brings Jonathan's lame son Mephibosheth to
David's own table (2 Samuel 9:1–13, recalled, not checked), answering the empty seat at Saul's table here.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 20:1–42 as planned: 20:1 begins with David fleeing Naioth to Jonathan, 20:42
closes with their parting (*He arose and departed; and Jonathan went into the city*), and 21:1 opens David's arrival
at Nob. One new place page: [[The Stone Ezel]] (20:19), following the wiki's practice for named landmarks (compare
[[The Stone of Bohan]]). No new person page for Jonathan's *little boy* (20:21–22, 35–40): he is unnamed, speaks no
words, and the narrator's point about him is that he *didn't know anything* (20:39); unlike [[Jonathan's Armor Bearer]]
(who speaks and fights at Michmash), he functions purely as the unwitting carrier of the signal and weapons, so he is
recorded here rather than given a person page. One new connection:
[[Kindness to My House Forever - Jonathan's Covenant and Mephibosheth]], tracking the sworn kindness to Jonathan's
house from 18:3 and 20:14–17, 42 through 23:16–18, Saul's parallel plea at 24:21–22, and its keeping for Mephibosheth
in 2 Samuel 9 and 21. The other parallels already have strong home pages in the wiki — self-curse oath, doubled life
oath, presence-blessing, Yahweh as witness between two parties, new moon, friendship, soul-love, and Saul's spear — so
each receives a new section on its existing connection page.

**Recorded, not resolved.**
- **19:1 vs. 20:2.** In 19:1 Saul explicitly told Jonathan to kill David; in 20:2 Jonathan says *Far from it; you will
  not die*, claiming his father hides nothing from him. Whether Jonathan trusts Saul's oath of 19:6 or two narrative
  strands stand side by side is left open.
- **The arrow signal vs. the face-to-face parting.** The arrow code of 20:20–22 is designed so David can leave safely
  without meeting Jonathan; yet once the boy leaves, David emerges and they meet, weep, and speak openly (20:41–42).
  The text narrates both without explaining why the open meeting is suddenly safe.
- **One arrow or three.** Jonathan plans to shoot *three arrows* (20:20), shoots *an arrow* (20:36–37), and the boy
  gathers *the arrows* (20:38); both singular and plural are recorded as written in WEB.
- ***Out of the south* and *the stone Ezel*.** Verse 41 has David arise *out of the south* (WEB); the Septuagint is
  commonly reported to read a mound or stone here (matching verse 19's *the stone Ezel*, recalled, not checked; no
  Septuagint or Hebrew text was consulted this pass).
- **Jonathan's mother.** 20:30 insults Jonathan's mother without naming her; [[Ahinoam (wife of Saul)]] is Saul's only
  named wife in 1 Samuel (14:50), but the text does not explicitly identify her as Jonathan's mother.
- **The unnamed city.** 20:40, 42 call Saul's seat *the city*; [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] is presumed from 10:26 and 15:34,
  not named here.
