# The Ark Among the Philistines

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 5:1–12

## Summary

> **5:1** Now the Philistines had taken God's ark, and they brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.
> **2** The Philistines took God's ark, and brought it into the house of Dagon, and set it by Dagon.
> **3** When the people of Ashdod arose early on the next day, behold, Dagon had fallen on his face to the
> ground before Yahweh's ark. They took Dagon, and set him in his place again.
> **4** When they arose early on the following morning, behold, Dagon had fallen on his face to the ground
> before Yahweh's ark; and the head of Dagon and both the palms of his hands were cut off on the
> threshold. Only Dagon's torso was intact.
> **5** Therefore neither the priests of Dagon, nor any who come into Dagon's house, step on the threshold
> of Dagon in Ashdod, to this day.
> **6** But Yahweh's hand was heavy on the people of Ashdod, and he destroyed them, and struck them with
> tumors, even Ashdod and its borders.
> **7** When the men of Ashdod saw that it was so, they said, "The ark of the God of Israel shall not stay
> with us; for his hand is severe on us, and on Dagon our god."
> **8** They sent therefore and gathered together all the lords of the Philistines, and said, "What shall
> we do with the ark of the God of Israel?" They answered, "Let the ark of the God of Israel be carried
> over to Gath." They carried the ark of the God of Israel there.
> **9** It was so, that after they had carried it there, Yahweh's hand was against the city with a very
> great confusion; and he struck the men of the city, both small and great so that tumors broke out on
> them.
> **10** So they sent God's ark to Ekron. As God's ark came to Ekron, the Ekronites cried out, saying,
> "They have brought the ark of the God of Israel here to us, to kill us and our people."
> **11** They sent therefore and gathered together all the lords of the Philistines, and they said, "Send
> the ark of the God of Israel away, and let it go again to its own place, that it not kill us and our
> people." For there was a deadly confusion throughout all the city. The hand of God was very heavy there.
> **12** The men who didn't die were struck with the tumors; and the cry of the city went up to heaven.

The Philistines bring the captured ark *from Ebenezer to Ashdod* and set it in the house of [[Dagon]], *by
Dagon* (5:1–2). The next morning the people of [[Ashdod]] find Dagon *fallen on his face to the ground before
Yahweh's ark*, and set him up again. The morning after, he has fallen again, and his head and *both the palms
of his hands* lie *cut off on the threshold* (3–4). From that day the priests of Dagon and all who enter his
house do not step on the threshold (5). Then *Yahweh's hand was heavy on the people of Ashdod*: he destroys
them and strikes them with tumors (6). The men of Ashdod decide the ark cannot stay, *for his hand is severe
on us, and on Dagon our god* (7). The lords of the Philistines, gathered, send it on to [[Gath]] (8), where
Yahweh's hand brings *a very great confusion* and tumors break out on *small and great* (9). It is sent to
[[Ekron]], and the Ekronites cry out before it arrives: *They have brought the ark of the God of Israel here
to us, to kill us and our people* (10). The lords are gathered again, and this time the demand is to send
the ark *to its own place* (11). *The men who didn't die were struck with the tumors; and the cry of the city
went up to heaven* (12).

**What is going on.** Chapter 4 ended with *The glory has departed from Israel*. Chapter 5 shows where it
went, and what it does there. No Israelite appears in the chapter, and no priest carries the ark; the
captive goes where its captors take it, and in every city it goes, the captors suffer. The narrator gives
the agent plainly from verse 6 on, *Yahweh's hand*, but leaves the fall of Dagon (3–4) unexplained, told
only as what the people of Ashdod saw when they *arose early*. The chapter is a contest between gods held
in the losing god's own house, and its last scene reverses the first: the Philistines who had carried the
ark in as a trophy end by begging to send it away.

Read with chapter 4, the defeat at Ebenezer was not Yahweh's defeat. Israel lost the ark because of the house
of Eli ([[The Ark Is Captured]]); the Philistines discover that holding it is worse than losing it. The
chapter does not say this as a moral, and Israel learns nothing from it in the text: the lesson is drawn by
the Philistines' own priests at 6:6, where they cite Egypt and Pharaoh (stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**What is notable.**

- **By Dagon.** *Wayyaṣṣîḡû ʾōṯô ʾēṣel dāḡôn* (2, WLC), *set it beside Dagon*. The ark is placed as a
  trophy, or as a lesser god beside the greater; the text does not say which. *Dāḡôn* (lemma 1712) occurs
  eleven times in 5:2–7 and, in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings and Psalms searched here, otherwise
  only at Judges 16:23 (lemma search). See [[Dagon Fallen - Samson's Pillars and the Ark in Ashdod]].
- **Fallen on his face before Yahweh's ark.** *Nōp̄ēl ləp̄ānāyw ʾarṣâ lip̄nê ʾărôn YHWH* (3, 4, WLC), word for
  word on both mornings. *Fallen on his face to the ground* is the posture of worship and submission
  elsewhere, as when Joshua *fell to the earth on his face before Yahweh's ark* after Ai (Joshua 7:6, stored WEB).
  The narrator switches names: it is *God's ark* when the Philistines take it (1–2) and *Yahweh's ark* when
  Dagon lies before it (3, 4). The Philistines never use the name Yahweh in the chapter; they say *the God
  of Israel* (7, 8, 10, 11).
- **They set him in his place again.** *Wayyāšiḇû ʾōṯô limqōmô* (3, WLC). The god must be put back by his
  worshippers. Eight verses later the lords ask that the ark *go again to its own place*, *wəyāšōḇ
  limqōmô* (11, WLC): the same verb and the same noun. Dagon is returned to his place by hands; the ark
  is asked to return to its place, as if of itself.
- **Head and palms cut off.** *Wərōʾš dāḡôn ûštê kappôṯ yāḏāyw kərutôṯ* (4, WLC): the parts that see,
  speak, and act. *Kārat*, *cut off*, is the verb of making a covenant and of cutting off from the people.
  The WEB's *Only Dagon's torso was intact* renders *raq dāḡôn nišʾar ʿālāyw*, literally *only Dagon was
  left on him* (WLC). The Hebrew is compressed, and the WEB supplies *torso*. Readers who derive Dagon from
  *dāḡ*, *fish*, take *dāḡôn* here as *the fish part*; that reading depends on a derivation the wiki
  reports and does not build on (see [[Dagon]]).
- **The threshold.** *Hammip̄tān* (4, 5, WLC). The etiology *to this day* (5) explains a custom at Ashdod by
  this morning. *Mip̄tān* occurs in the WLC files searched here only in 5:4–5 and Zephaniah 1:9, *I will
  punish all those who leap over the threshold* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; lemma search). Whether
  Zephaniah alludes to the Dagon custom is debated, and the wiki does not decide it. The full WLC was not
  searched; Ezekiel also uses the word (recalled, not checked).
- **Yahweh's hand was heavy.** *Wattiḵbaḏ yaḏ-YHWH* (6), and again *kāḇḏâ məʾōḏ yaḏ hāʾĕlōhîm* (11, WLC).
  *Kāḇēḏ*, *be heavy*, is the root of *heavy* in Eli's death (4:18) and of *glory* in Ichabod's name
  (4:21–22). The glory, *kāḇôḏ*, that departed from Israel arrives in Philistia as a hand that is *heavy*,
  *kāḇēḏ*. The Philistine priests will tell the lords to *give glory*, *kāḇôḏ*, to the God of Israel, and
  not to *harden*, *təḵabbəḏû*, their hearts as Pharaoh did (6:5–6, WLC; stored WEB). The root is shared
  (morphhb lemmas 3513, 3519); whether the narrator meant the chain is not recoverable, and the data are
  reported. See [[Heavy Hand and Departed Glory - Kabed in 1 Samuel 4-6]].
- **The hand, and Dagon's hands.** Dagon's *hands*, *yāḏāyw*, lie cut off on the threshold (4); then
  *Yahweh's hand*, *yaḏ-YHWH*, is heavy on Ashdod (6). *Yād* runs through the chapter: 4, 6, 7, 9, 11 (WLC).
  The god with no hands is answered by the God whose hand is everywhere.
- **Tumors.** *ʿŏp̄ālîm* is written in the consonantal text (ketiv) in 6, 9, and 12; the Masoretic reading
  (qere) is *ṭəḥōrîm*, which the WEB translates *tumors* (WLC, both recorded). The same ketiv/qere pair
  occurs at Deuteronomy 28:27, *Yahweh will strike you with the boils of Egypt, with the tumors* (stored
  WEB), and in the WLC files searched here *ṭəḥōrîm* occurs only there and in 1 Samuel 5–6 (lemma search).
  The covenant curse threatened against Israel falls on its enemy. What the *tumors* were medically is not
  stated; the mice of 6:4–5 have led many readers to suggest bubonic plague, and the Septuagint is commonly
  reported to add mice in chapter 5 (recalled, not checked). See
  [[Tumors and Confusion - The Covenant Curses on Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron]].
- **A very great confusion.** *Məhûmâ gəḏôlâ məʾōḏ* (9), *məhûmaṯ māweṯ*, *a confusion of death* (11, WLC).
  The noun occurs in the WLC files searched here at Deuteronomy 7:23, where Yahweh *will confuse them with a
  great confusion*, at Deuteronomy 28:20 among the curses, here, and at 1 Samuel 14:20 (lemma search).
  Deuteronomy 7:23 promised it against the nations of the land; 28:20 threatened it against Israel. See
  [[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].
- **Ashdod, Gath, Ekron.** Three of the five Philistine lordships of Joshua 13:3 (stored WEB). The ark moves
  inland from Ashdod to Gath and then north to Ekron, nearer Israel's border; the route is reported from
  the order of the text, and no map is drawn here. Gaza and Ashkelon are not visited, but 6:17 lists all
  five cities in the trespass offering (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The lords.** *Sarnê p̄əlištîm* (8, 11, WLC), the word used only of Philistine rulers. The lords who
  gathered to sacrifice to Dagon after Samson's capture (Judges 16:23) gather here to decide what to do
  with Israel's God. At Ashdod the decision is to *carry it around*, *yissōḇ*, to Gath (8, WLC); at Ekron it
  is to *send* it home (11).
- **Both small and great.** *Miqqāṭōn wəʿaḏ-gāḏôl* (9, WLC): the merism of the whole population. At Ekron
  the people speak in the singular, *to kill me and my people*, *lahămîṯēnî wəʾeṯ-ʿammî* (10, WLC); the
  WEB renders it *us*. The city speaks as one person.
- **The cry of the city went up to heaven.** *Wattaʿal šawʿaṯ hāʿîr haššāmāyim* (12, WLC). The words are
  those of Exodus 2:23, *their cry came up to God*, *wattaʿal šawʿāṯām ʾel-hāʾĕlōhîm*, where Israel cried
  under Egypt (WLC; stored WEB). In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel the noun *šawʿâ* occurs only
  in those two verses (lemma search). Israel's cry in Egypt was heard; the Philistine cry is recorded, and
  the next chapter shows the Philistines acting to be rid of the hand rather than appealing to it. See
  [[Their Cry Went Up - Exodus 2.23 and 1 Samuel 5.12]].
- **Egypt again.** The Philistines had said the ark's gods *struck the Egyptians* (4:8). Now the ark
  strikes them, *wayyaḵ*, *he struck* (6, 9; *hukkû* 12, WLC), with the verb of the plagues, and the next
  chapter makes the comparison explicit (6:6, stored WEB). Yahweh had said *Against all the gods of Egypt I
  will execute judgments* (Exodus 12:12, stored WEB); here the judgment falls on one god of Philistia in
  his house. See [[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

**How it connects.** The chapter follows [[The Ark Is Captured]] and answers its last line: the glory that
*departed* from Israel is shown still to be Yahweh's, even in exile. It is the second time Dagon's house sees
the God of Israel's enemies brought low, after [[The Death of Samson]]; see
[[Dagon Fallen - Samson's Pillars and the Ark in Ashdod]]. Psalm 78 sets the two chapters side by side: after
Yahweh *delivered his strength into captivity, his glory into the adversary's hand* (78:61), *he struck his adversaries backward* (78:66, WEB
checked at bible-api.com); whether 78:66 refers to the tumors is a common reading, not a statement of the
psalm (see [[Shiloh Forsaken - 1 Samuel 4, Psalm 78.60-64, and Jeremiah 7.12-14]]). The return of the ark is
the next pericope (6:1–7:1, not yet ingested). Later, 1 Chronicles 10:10 says the Philistines *fastened
[Saul's] head in the house of Dagon* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored): the head of Dagon fell
before the ark, and the head of Israel's first king will hang in Dagon's house.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 5:1–12 as planned; chapter 6 opens with a time note (*seven months*)
and a new council, so the chapter break is the natural boundary. No new person pages: the priests of Dagon
(5) and the lords (8, 11) are corporate and sit under [[The Philistines]]; the Ekronites and men of Ashdod
likewise. No new place pages: Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron already exist.

**Unresolved.** Whether the ark was set *by Dagon* as trophy or as a subordinate god; what *only Dagon was
left on him* (4) means, and the *fish* derivation it is often read with; whether Zephaniah 1:9 alludes to
the Ashdod threshold custom; the medical identity of the tumors; whether the *kāḇēḏ* chain from 4:18 through
6:6 is intended; and whether Psalm 78:66 refers to this chapter. Each is reported, not settled.
