# David Plays the Harp for Saul

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 16:14–23

## Summary

> **14** Now Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from Yahweh troubled him.
> **15** Saul's servants said to him, "See now, an evil spirit from God troubles you.
> **16** Let our lord now command your servants who are in front of you to seek out a man who is a skillful player
> on the harp. Then when the evil spirit from God is on you, he will play with his hand, and you will be well."
> **17** Saul said to his servants, "Provide me now a man who can play well, and bring him to me."
> **18** Then one of the young men answered, and said, "Behold, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who is
> skillful in playing, a mighty man of valor, a man of war, prudent in speech, and a handsome person; and Yahweh is
> with him."
> **19** Therefore Saul sent messengers to Jesse, and said, "Send me David your son, who is with the sheep."
> **20** Jesse took a donkey loaded with bread, and a bottle of wine, and a young goat, and sent them by David his son
> to Saul.
> **21** David came to Saul, and stood before him. He loved him greatly; and he became his armor bearer.
> **22** Saul sent to Jesse, saying, "Please let David stand before me; for he has found favor in my sight."
> **23** When the spirit from God was on Saul, David took the harp, and played with his hand; so Saul was refreshed,
> and was well, and the evil spirit departed from him.

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 16.)

The verse after David's anointing turns to [[Saul]]: *Now Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from
Yahweh troubled him* (14). His servants name the trouble themselves, *an evil spirit from God troubles you*, and
propose a remedy: a man *who is a skillful player on the harp*, who will play *when the evil spirit from God is on
you*, *and you will be well* (15–16). Saul agrees: *Provide me now a man who can play well* (17). One of the young men
already knows one. *I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite*, and he lists six things about him: *skillful in
playing, a mighty man of valor, a man of war, prudent in speech, and a handsome person; and Yahweh is with him* (18).

Saul sends to [[Jesse]]: *Send me David your son, who is with the sheep* (19). Jesse sends David with a donkey loaded
with bread, a skin of wine, and a young goat (20). David comes and *stood before him*, and *He loved him greatly; and
he became his armor bearer* (21). Saul asks Jesse to let David stay, *for he has found favor in my sight* (22). The
pericope ends on the remedy working: *When the spirit from God was on Saul, David took the harp, and played with his
hand; so Saul was refreshed, and was well, and the evil spirit departed from him* (23).

**Where it happens.** The text does not name the place. Saul's court is presumably at Gibeah, his town (10:26; 15:34),
but this pericope does not say so. David comes from [[Bethlehem]], where 16:1–13 left him, and is sent for *with the
sheep* (19). Jesse is called *the Bethlehemite*, *bêṯ hallaḥmî* (18, WLC), as in 16:1.

**What is going on.** The pericope is framed by one verb. *Sārâ*, *departed* (morphhb lemma 5493), opens it, *Yahweh's
Spirit departed from Saul* (14), and closes it, *the evil spirit departed from him* (23) (WLC). The same verb returns
at the turning points of Saul's story: *Yahweh was with him, and had departed from Saul* (18:12), and at Endor, Saul's
*God has departed from me* and Samuel's reply, *Yahweh has departed from you* (28:15–16) (WLC; stored WEB; 18:12 in [[Saul's Jealousy of David]], 28:15–16 not yet
ingested). What leaves at the start of the pericope is Yahweh's Spirit, which does not come back. What leaves at the
end is the evil spirit, and it leaves only for a while.

The two halves of chapter 16 face each other. In 16:13 *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on David from that day
forward*; in 16:14 *Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul*. The narrator does not explain the connection between the two
verses. He puts them next to each other.

Then the man anointed in secret is brought into the house of the king he will replace. No one in the scene, as far as
the text says, knows of the anointing. The servant recommends David for his playing, and Saul keeps him because he
loves him. The reader knows what Saul does not.

**What is notable.**

- **An evil spirit from Yahweh.** *Rûaḥ-rāʿâ mēʾēṯ YHWH* (14, WLC). The narrator says it comes *from Yahweh*. The
  servants say *from God* (15, 16), and the last verse says *the spirit from God*, *rûaḥ-ʾĕlōhîm* (23), without
  *evil*, before naming it *the evil spirit*, *hārûaḥ hārāʿâ*, as it leaves (WLC). The phrase recurs at 18:10 and 19:9,
  and both times Saul has a spear in his hand while David plays. In the stored books the closest precedent is
  Judges 9:23, *God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem*. The text does not say what the
  spirit is: madness, depression, or a being sent from Yahweh's court. Whether *rāʿâ* means *morally evil* or
  *harmful, calamitous* here is debated (recalled, not checked). The wiki records the phrase as written. See
  [[An Evil Spirit from God - Abimelech, Saul, and Ahab's Prophets]] and
  [[Shall We Receive Good and Not Evil - Weal and Woe from One Hand]].
- **Troubled.** *Ûḇiʿăṯattû* (14) and *məḇaʿitteḵā* (15), from *bāʿaṯ* (lemma 1204), *terrify*, *overwhelm*. In the
  Hebrew files checked (Genesis through 2 Samuel, and Ruth) it occurs only in these two verses and in David's song,
  *The floods of ungodliness made me afraid* (2 Samuel 22:5, WEB checked at bible-api.com;
  not stored). The WEB's *troubled* is mild for it.
- **Departed and departed.** *Sārâ mēʿim šāʾûl* (14) and *sārâ mēʿālāyw* (23) (WLC). The first is the Spirit of
  Yahweh, the second the evil spirit. See [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].
- **The harp.** *Kinnôr* (16, 23, WLC; lemma 3658). In the Hebrew files checked it occurs six times: Jubal, *the
  father of all who handle the harp and pipe* (Genesis 4:21); Laban's *with tambourine and with harp* (Genesis 31:27);
  the band of prophets Saul met, with *a lute, a tambourine, a pipe, and a harp before them* (10:5); these two verses;
  and the procession of the ark, *with harps* (2 Samuel 6:5, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored). The verb *to
  play*, *nāḡan* (lemma 5059), occurs in those files only in this pericope (16, 17, 18, 23) and at 18:10 and 19:9.
  The instrument is a lyre, not a modern harp (recalled, not checked). See
  [[The Harp of David - Jubal, the Prophets, and the Sweet Psalmist]].
- **Played with his hand.** *Wəniggēn bəyāḏô* (16, 23, WLC). In 18:10 and 19:9 the phrase is repeated, *David played
  with his hand*, and Saul has *his spear in his hand*. *Bəyāḏô* is in both.
- **You will be well; refreshed.** *Wəṭôḇ lāḵ* (16) and *wərāwaḥ ləšāʾûl wəṭôḇ lô* (23) (WLC). *Rāwaḥ* (lemma 7304),
  *be refreshed*, *have room to breathe*, occurs in the Hebrew files checked only here. It shares its consonants with
  *rûaḥ*, *spirit, breath* (both ר־ו־ח), and morphhb gives them different lemma numbers (7304, 7307). Whether the
  narrator meant the sound to be heard is not stated; the wiki notes it. See [[Healing]].
- **I have seen.** *Rāʾîṯî bēn ləyišay bêṯ hallaḥmî* (18, WLC), the words Yahweh used in 16:1, *rāʾîṯî bəḇānāw*. Saul
  says *Provide me*, literally *See for me*, *rəʾû-nāʾ lî* (17). The verb of chapter 16 continues. See
  [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **Six qualities.** *Yōḏēaʿ naggēn*, *knowing how to play*; *gibbôr ḥayil*, *mighty man of valor*; *ʾîš milḥāmâ*,
  *a man of war*; *nəḇôn dāḇār*, *understanding of word*; *ʾîš tōʾar*, *a man of form*; *and Yahweh is with him*,
  *waYHWH ʿimmô* (18, WLC). Saul's father Kish was *a mighty man of valor* (9:1, *gibbôr ḥāyil*). *Nāḇôn* is the word
  of Joseph, *a discreet and wise man* (Genesis 41:33, 39); *tōʾar* is used of Joseph, *well-built*, *yəp̄ēh-ṯōʾar*
  (Genesis 39:6), and of Rachel (Genesis 29:17) (WLC; stored WEB). *Yahweh was with Joseph* (Genesis 39:2) and *Yahweh
  was with* Samuel (3:19). See [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].
- **A man of war, already.** The servant calls David *a man of war* before he has fought. In the next chapter Saul
  says *you are but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth* (17:33, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and the
  same phrase, *ʾîš milḥāmâ*, is used there of Goliath (17:33, WLC). See
  the contradiction note below.
- **With the sheep.** *ʾĂšer baṣṣōʾn* (19, WLC). Saul's message identifies David the way his father did in 16:11.
  17:15 says *David went back and forth from Saul to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem* (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested). See [[Taken from Following the Sheep - 1 Samuel 16.11, 2 Samuel 7.8, and Psalm 78.70-72]].
- **Bread, wine, and a young goat.** *Ḥămôr leḥem wənōʾḏ yayin ûḡəḏî ʿizzîm ʾeḥāḏ* (20, WLC). At Saul's own anointing
  Samuel told him he would meet three men, *one carrying three young goats, and another carrying three loaves of
  bread, and another carrying a bottle of wine* (10:3), who would give him two loaves (10:4). The items are the same
  three. Before that, Saul worried *there is not a present to bring to the man of God* (9:7). The text does not draw
  the connection; the wiki notes it.
- **Stood before him.** *Wayyaʿămōḏ ləp̄ānāw* (21), and Saul's *yaʿămāḏ-nāʾ dāwiḏ ləp̄ānay* (22) (WLC). *To stand
  before* is the language of service at court. The servants in verse 16 are those *in front of you*, *ləp̄ānêḵā*.
- **He loved him greatly.** *Wayyeʾĕhāḇēhû məʾōḏ* (21, WLC). The subject is not named, but the natural reading is
  that Saul loved David. It is the first time in 1 Samuel that anyone is said to love David. Jonathan (18:1, 3; [[Jonathan Loves David]]), *all Israel and
  Judah*, Michal, and Saul's servants follow (18:16, [[Saul's Jealousy of David]]; 18:20, 22, stored WEB, not yet ingested). See [[Love]].
- **His armor bearer.** *Nōśēʾ ḵēlîm* (21, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked the phrase is used of Abimelech's young
  man (Judges 9:54), of Jonathan's (14:1, 6, 7, 12), of David here, of Saul's at Gilboa (31:4–6), and of Joab's ten young men
  (2 Samuel 18:15). Abimelech and Saul, the two men troubled by *an evil spirit from God* in those books, both die by
  asking their armor bearer to kill them; Abimelech's does, and Saul's refuses (Judges 9:54; 1 Samuel 31:4, stored
  WEB). The text does not say whether the armor bearer of 31:4 is David; by then David is with the Philistines
  (29:1–11, not yet ingested). See [[Jonathan's Armor Bearer]].
- **Found favor in my sight.** *Kî-māṣāʾ ḥēn bəʿênāy* (22, WLC), the idiom of Noah (Genesis 6:8) and of Ruth before
  Boaz (Ruth 2:10) (stored WEB). See [[Favor and Grace]].
- **The spirit is *on* Saul, and *to* Saul.** Verse 16 has *ʿālêḵā*, *on you*; verse 23 *ʾel-šāʾûl*, *to Saul*; 18:10
  and 19:9 also have *ʾel* (WLC). 16:13 has *ʾel* for the Spirit of Yahweh coming to David. The variation is recorded;
  the wiki does not build an argument on it.
- **David says nothing.** David's first quoted words in 1 Samuel are at 17:26. In this pericope he is sent, comes, stands,
  is loved, carries armor, and plays. The servant, Saul, and Jesse act; David only obeys.

**How it connects.** The pericope opens the story of Saul and David that runs to the end of the book. Saul's love
(21) turns to fear: *Saul was afraid of David, because Yahweh was with him, and had departed from Saul* (18:12, stored
WEB; [[Saul's Jealousy of David]]). The music that calms him in 16:23 is the setting of the spear thrown at David in 18:10–11 and
19:9–10. The servant's *Yahweh is with him* (18) becomes the narrator's (18:14). David is later remembered as *the
sweet psalmist of Israel* (2 Samuel 23:1, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored), and his playing here is the first
time the narrative shows him making music.

**Contradiction flagged.** In this pericope Saul knows David by name, sends to Jesse for him, loves him, makes him his
armor bearer, and asks Jesse's leave to keep him (19–22). In 17:55–58, after Goliath, Saul asks Abner *whose son is
this youth?* and David answers *I am the son of your servant Jesse the Bethlehemite* (stored WEB; see [[Jonathan Loves David]]).
17:15 has David going *back and forth from Saul* to the sheep. Readers have explained this by a court musician who was
not well known, by Saul's question being about the family rather than the man, or by two sources placed side by side;
the Septuagint is reported to lack parts of chapters 17–18 (all recalled, not checked; no Septuagint was consulted).
The wiki records both accounts and does not reconcile them. The same note is on [[David]] and [[Saul]], and the two
scenes are set side by side on [[Whose Son Is This Youth - 1 Samuel 16.18-22 and 17.55-58]].

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 16:14–23 as planned: 16:14 turns from David to Saul, and 17:1 opens the
Philistine war. No new person page for the young man who recommends David (18) or for Saul's servants (15–17); the
servant is unnamed and does not reappear by any marker in the text. Two new connections:
[[An Evil Spirit from God - Abimelech, Saul, and Ahab's Prophets]], since the phrase is rare and the two men it
troubles in the stored books end alike, and
[[The Harp of David - Jubal, the Prophets, and the Sweet Psalmist]], since *kinnôr* occurs only six times in the
files checked. The *sārâ* frame went into the existing
[[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]] rather than a page of its own. The *rāwaḥ*/*rûaḥ* sound is
noted, not argued.

**Unresolved.** What the evil spirit is, and what *rāʿâ* means of it. How Saul can know David here and ask whose son
he is in 17:55–58. Whether Saul's court is at Gibeah in this scene. Whether the subject of *he loved him* (21) is Saul
(the natural reading, taken here). Whether the gift of verse 20 is meant to recall 10:3.
