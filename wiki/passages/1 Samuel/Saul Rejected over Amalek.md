# Saul Rejected over Amalek

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 15:1–35

## Summary

> **1** Samuel said to Saul, "Yahweh sent me to anoint you to be king over his people, over Israel. Now therefore
> listen to the voice of Yahweh's words.
> **2** Yahweh of Armies says, 'I remember what Amalek did to Israel, how he set himself against him on the way,
> when he came up out of Egypt.
> **3** Now go and strike Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have, and don't spare them; but kill both man and
> woman, infant and nursing baby, ox and sheep, camel and donkey.'"
> …
> **9** But Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, of the cattle, and of the fat calves, and the
> lambs, and all that was good, and were not willing to utterly destroy them; but everything that was vile and
> refuse, that they destroyed utterly.
> **10** Then Yahweh's word came to Samuel, saying,
> **11** "It grieves me that I have set up Saul to be king; for he has turned back from following me, and has not
> performed my commandments." Samuel was angry; and he cried to Yahweh all night.
> …
> **22** Samuel said, "Has Yahweh as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying Yahweh's voice?
> Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams.
> **23** For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as idolatry and teraphim. Because you have
> rejected Yahweh's word, he has also rejected you from being king."
> …
> **28** Samuel said to him, "Yahweh has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has given it to a neighbor of
> yours who is better than you.
> **29** Also the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent; for he is not a man, that he should repent."
> …
> **35** Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death; for Samuel mourned for Saul: and Yahweh grieved
> that he had made Saul king over Israel.

(Excerpts; the full text is in `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 15.)

[[Samuel]] begins with his own commission: *Yahweh sent me to anoint you*, so *listen to the voice of Yahweh's
words* (1). The word is about [[Amalek]]: *I remember what Amalek did to Israel … when he came up out of Egypt*
(2). [[Saul]] is to *strike Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have*, sparing nothing, people or animals (3).
He musters the people at [[Telaim]], *two hundred thousand footmen, and ten thousand men of Judah* (4), comes to *the
city of Amalek*, and sets an ambush in the valley (5). First he warns the [[Kenites]] to leave, *for you showed
kindness to all the children of Israel, when they came up out of Egypt*, and they go (6). He strikes the Amalekites
*from [[Havilah]] as you go to [[Shur]], that is before [[Egypt]]* (7), takes [[Agag]] their king alive, and
destroys *all the people with the edge of the sword* (8). But *Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best* of the
flocks and herds, *and were not willing to utterly destroy them*; what was *vile and refuse* they destroyed (9).

Yahweh's word comes to Samuel: *It grieves me that I have set up Saul to be king; for he has turned back from
following me, and has not performed my commandments*. Samuel is angry, and cries to Yahweh all night (10–11). In
the morning he is told that Saul went to Carmel ([[Carmel (Judah)]], presumably), *set up a monument for himself*,
and went down to [[Gilgal]] (12). Saul greets him: *You are blessed by Yahweh! I have performed the commandment of
Yahweh.* Samuel answers with a question: *Then what does this bleating of the sheep in my ears, and the lowing of
the cattle which I hear mean?* (13–14). Saul says *the people* spared the best *to sacrifice to Yahweh your God*;
*we have utterly destroyed the rest* (15).

Samuel tells him what Yahweh said in the night. *Though you were little in your own sight, weren't you made the head
of the tribes of Israel?* He was sent to destroy *the sinners the Amalekites*: *Why then didn't you obey Yahweh's
voice, but took the plunder …?* (16–19). Saul insists, *But I have obeyed Yahweh's voice*; he brought Agag and
destroyed the Amalekites, and *the people* took *the chief of the devoted things, to sacrifice to Yahweh your God in
Gilgal* (20–21). Samuel answers in verse: *to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams*;
rebellion is like *witchcraft*, stubbornness like *idolatry and teraphim*; *Because you have rejected Yahweh's word,
he has also rejected you from being king* (22–23).

Saul confesses: *I have sinned … because I feared the people, and obeyed their voice*, and asks Samuel to pardon his
sin and return with him to worship (24–25). Samuel refuses and repeats the rejection (26). As he turns to go, Saul
grabs *the skirt of his robe, and it tore*. Samuel: *Yahweh has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has
given it to a neighbor of yours who is better than you*, and *the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent; for he
is not a man, that he should repent* (27–29). Saul confesses a second time and asks, *please honor me now before the
elders of my people*; Samuel goes back with him, and Saul worships (30–31).

Then Samuel has Agag brought. Agag comes *cheerfully*: *Surely the bitterness of death is past.* Samuel: *As your
sword has made women childless, so your mother will be childless among women!*, and he *cut Agag in pieces before
Yahweh in Gilgal* (32–33). Samuel goes to [[Ramah]], Saul to his house at *Gibeah of Saul*
([[Gibeah (Benjamin)]]) (34). *Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death; for Samuel mourned for
Saul: and Yahweh grieved that he had made Saul king over Israel* (35).

**Where it happens.** The muster is at Telaim (4), a place named only here; the battle runs across the southern
desert *from Havilah as you go to Shur, that is before Egypt* (7), the line Genesis 25:18 drew for Ishmael's sons.
Saul passes through Carmel (12), most likely the Judahite town by Maon (25:2; Joshua 15:55), though the text does not
say which Carmel. The confrontation is at Gilgal (12, 21, 33), where Saul was made king (11:15) and sentenced the first
time (13:8–15). The chapter ends with the two men going to their own towns, Ramah and Gibeah, a few miles apart, and
never meeting again while Saul lives (34–35); the next time Samuel speaks to Saul, at Endor, he is dead (28:14–19,
stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**What is going on.** This is the second rejection, and the final one. At Gilgal in chapter 13 Saul lost his dynasty:
*your kingdom will not continue* (13:14). Here he loses the kingship itself: *he has also rejected you from being
king* (23), *Yahweh has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today* (28). The next chapter sends Samuel to Bethlehem with
*How long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him from being king over Israel?* (16:1, stored WEB; not yet
ingested). From here to chapter 31, Saul reigns without the kingdom.

The command has a history. Amalek attacked Israel *on the way* out of Egypt, and at Rephidim Yahweh swore *war with
Amalek from generation to generation* and told Moses *I will utterly blot out the memory of Amalek* (Exodus 17:14–16).
Deuteronomy turned this into a charge to Israel: *Remember what Amalek did to you by the way as you came out of Egypt
… when Yahweh your God has given you rest … you shall blot out the memory of Amalek* (Deuteronomy 25:17–19). Samuel's
oracle repeats Deuteronomy's *what Amalek did … on the way … when he came up out of Egypt* (2) and gives the king the
war that Deuteronomy deferred until Israel had rest. Saul has just been credited with rest on every side (14:47–48). See
[[Amalek - From a Concubine's Son to Israel's First Enemy]] and [[Blot Out - The Flood, Amalek, and God's Book]].

The verb of the command is *ḥāram*, *devote to destruction* (3, 8, 9, 15, 18, 20, WLC). Everything devoted belongs to
Yahweh and cannot be kept or redeemed (Leviticus 27:28–29); at Jericho, Achan's theft of *the devoted thing* brought
death (Joshua 7). The fault here is in verse 9: Saul and the people *spared*, *wayyaḥmōl*, the king and the best of the
flocks, *and were not willing*, *wəlōʾ ʾāḇû*, to devote them (WLC). What they destroyed was what was not worth
keeping. Saul's defence turns on a distinction the law does not seem to allow: the animals are not plunder kept but
*the chief of the devoted things* (*rēʾšîṯ haḥērem*, 21, WLC) brought back *to sacrifice to Yahweh your God*. If the
devoted things already belong to Yahweh, then sacrificing them is not obviously wrong. Samuel's answer does not
address the category. He answers on obedience: *to obey is better than sacrifice*. See
[[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]] and
[[Achan and Ananias - Keeping Back What Belonged to God]].

The chapter is built on *listening* to a *voice*. It opens with *listen to the voice of Yahweh's words* (*šəmaʿ
ləqôl*, 1); Samuel hears *the voice of the sheep* (*qôl haṣṣōʾn*, 14); he asks *why didn't you obey Yahweh's voice?*
(19); Saul claims *I have obeyed Yahweh's voice* (20); Samuel answers that *to obey* (*šəmōaʿ*) is better than
sacrifice (22); and Saul at last confesses that he *feared the people, and obeyed their voice* (*wāʾešmaʿ bəqôlām*,
24) (all WLC, lemmas 8085 and 6963). The same pair began the monarchy: *Listen to the voice of the people* (8:7, 9,
22), Yahweh's word to Samuel. Samuel's farewell set the condition, *If you will … listen to his voice … But if you will
not listen to Yahweh's voice …* (12:14–15). The king was given to a people who would not listen to Yahweh, and is
rejected because he listened to them.

**What is notable.**

- **Two verbs for one sin.** Yahweh says Saul *has not performed my commandments*, *dəḇāray lōʾ hēqîm* (11); Saul's
  first words are *I have performed the commandment of Yahweh*, *hăqîmōṯî ʾeṯ-dəḇar YHWH* (13) (WLC). He uses the
  exact verb of the charge, hiphil *qûm*, to deny it. Samuel answers with the noise of the animals.
- **I remember.** *Pāqaḏtî* (2, WLC), not *zāḵar*, the verb of Exodus 17:14 and Deuteronomy 25:17 (WLC). *Pāqaḏ* is
  the verb of Yahweh *visiting* Hannah (2:21), and it is the verb two verses later for Saul *counting* the people at
  Telaim, *wayyip̄qəḏēm* (4) (WLC, morphhb lemma 6485). WEB *I remember* is a fair rendering; the Hebrew carries a
  sense of attending to and settling an account that the English does not.
- **It grieves me.** *Niḥamtî* (11, WLC), *I regret* or *I am sorry*, is letter for letter the word of the flood: *for
  I am sorry that I have made them*, *niḥamtî* (Genesis 6:7, WLC). Both times Yahweh regrets having made someone,
  *made man* there, *made Saul king* here (35). In Genesis–2 Samuel the form *niḥamtî* occurs only in those two
  verses (morphhb lemma 5162).
- **Will not repent.** Between verse 11 and verse 35, Samuel says *the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent; for
  he is not a man, that he should repent* (29), with the same verb, niphal *nāḥam* (WLC), that the narrator uses in 11
  and 35 of Yahweh. The sentence takes most of its words from Balaam's: *God is not a man, that he should lie, nor a
  son of man, that he should repent* (Numbers 23:19, stored WEB), where the verb is the hitpael *wəyiṯneḥām* (WLC).
  Balaam's line goes on *Has he spoken, and will he not make it good?*, *yəqîmennâ*, hiphil *qûm* (Numbers 23:19,
  WLC), the verb of Saul's *I have performed*. The chapter says Yahweh *regretted* making Saul king, and says
  Yahweh does not *regret*, and does not reconcile them. The most common reading takes 29 as about the sentence
  just spoken: the decision to tear the kingdom will not be revoked. That reading is the usual one, not the text's
  own explanation. See [[God Does Not Repent - Balaam and the Golden Calf]] and [[Divine Grief and Relenting]].
- **The Strength of Israel.** *Nēṣaḥ yiśrāʾēl* (29, WLC), a title of God found nowhere else in Genesis–2 Samuel. The
  noun *nēṣaḥ* occurs in that range only here and at 2 Samuel 2:26, where Abner asks *Shall the sword devour
  forever?*, *lāneṣaḥ* (morphhb lemma 5331; WEB checked at bible-api.com). Whether the title means *strength*,
  *glory*, *endurance*, or *eternity* is disputed (recalled, not checked); the WEB has *Strength*.
- **A monument for himself.** *Maṣṣîḇ lô yāḏ* (12, WLC), literally *setting up a hand for himself*. Absalom, who
  had no son *to keep my name in memory*, set up a pillar that *is called Absalom's monument*, *yaḏ ʾaḇšālôm*
  (2 Samuel 18:18, WLC; WEB checked at bible-api.com). The king sent to blot out Amalek's memory sets up his own.
- **The bleating of the sheep.** *Qôl haṣṣōʾn* (14, WLC), the voice of the sheep. Saul had not obeyed Yahweh's
  *voice*; the animals' voice gives him away.
- **Spared.** *Ḥāmal* (3, 9, 15, WLC): *don't spare them* (3), *Saul and the people spared* (9), *the people spared*
  (15). The verb is used of Pharaoh's daughter who *had compassion* on the infant Moses (Exodus 2:6, WLC; stored WEB).
  Here it is the sparing of a king and the best livestock, while the people were destroyed. See
  [[Compassion and Mercy]].
- **The people.** Saul's account moves the act to *the people* each time: *the people spared* (15), *the people took
  of the plunder* (21), *I feared the people, and obeyed their voice* (24). The narrator's verse 9 says *Saul and the
  people*. The king the people asked for *that our king may judge us, and go out before us* (8:20) is led by them.
- **The sinners the Amalekites.** *Haḥaṭṭāʾîm* (18, WLC), in Samuel's retelling of the command, an addition to the
  wording of verse 3. It gives the reason that verse 2 gave as memory.
- **Rebellion and stubbornness.** *Ḥaṭṭaʾṯ qesem meri wəʾāwen ûṯərāp̄îm hap̄ṣar* (23, WLC), a compressed line whose
  syntax the WEB smooths. *Qesem*, divination, is one of the arts Deuteronomy forbids (Deuteronomy 18:10, WLC); in
  Genesis–2 Samuel the noun occurs only there, at Balaam's *rewards of divination* and *no divination* (Numbers 22:7;
  23:23), and here (morphhb lemma 7081). The teraphim were Laban's household gods, which Rachel stole (Genesis
  31:19), and Micah's (Judges 17:5); they recur in Saul's own house, in the bed Michal disguises (19:13, stored WEB; not
  yet ingested). Saul, who *sent away those who had familiar spirits*, will consult one at Endor (28:3–25, stored WEB;
  not yet ingested). See
  [[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]] and
  [[Diviners, Mediums, and the Dead - Deuteronomy 18.10-11 and the Torah's Forbidden Arts]].
- **Rejected for rejecting.** *Yaʿan māʾastā ʾeṯ-dəḇar YHWH wayyimʾāsəḵā* (23, WLC): the verb is the one Yahweh
  used of the people, *they have rejected me as the king over them* (8:7), and Samuel at Mizpah, *you have today
  rejected your God* (10:19). It returns in the next chapter for Saul (16:1) and for Eliab, *I have rejected him*
  (16:7) (WLC, morphhb lemma 3988).
- **I have sinned.** *Ḥāṭāʾṯî* (24, 30, WLC), twice. The first time Saul asks for pardon and Samuel's return so he
  can worship (25); the second time he asks to be honoured *before the elders of my people, and before Israel* (30).
  Samuel refuses the first and grants the second. The first is Pharaoh's: *I have sinned against Yahweh your God,
  and against you. Now therefore please forgive my sin*, *ḥāṭāʾṯî … wəʿattâ śāʾ nāʾ ḥaṭṭāʾṯî* (Exodus 10:16–17,
  stored WEB; WLC); Saul's is *ḥāṭāʾṯî … wəʿattâ śāʾ nāʾ ʾeṯ-ḥaṭṭāʾṯî* (24–25, WLC). In Genesis–2 Samuel the
  request *śāʾ nāʾ* with *ḥaṭṭāʾṯ*, *please forgive my sin*, occurs only in those two verses (morphhb lemmas 5375,
  4994, 2403). Both kings confess under pressure and are not reprieved. The confession is joined to the words of Numbers 14, *I have
  transgressed the commandment of Yahweh*, *ʿāḇartî ʾeṯ-pî YHWH* (24, WLC), Israel's phrase at Kadesh after the
  spies, also against Amalek. See [[Transgressing the Mouth of Yahweh - Numbers 14 and 1 Samuel 15]].
- **The torn robe.** *Wayyaḥăzēq biḵnap̄ məʿîlô wayyiqqāraʿ* (27, WLC). Whose hand grabbed and whose robe tore is
  not grammatically certain; the WEB, with verse 28, makes it Saul grabbing Samuel's robe. The *məʿîl* is the garment
  Hannah made for the boy Samuel each year, *a little robe* (2:19, WLC). Samuel turns the accident into the sign:
  *Yahweh has torn*, *qāraʿ*, *the kingdom … and has given it to a neighbor of yours who is better than you* (28). At
  En Gedi David will cut off *the skirt of Saul's robe*, *kənap̄ hamməʿîl* (24:4 WEB, 24:5 WLC), and Saul will
  answer *I know that you will surely be king* (24:20, stored WEB; not yet ingested). At Endor Samuel will say the
  sentence a third time with David's name in it (28:17, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[The Torn Robe - 1 Samuel 15.27-28, 24.4-5, 28.17, and 1 Kings 11.29-31]].
- **A neighbor … better than you.** *Ləreʿăḵā haṭṭôḇ mimmekkā* (28, WLC). Saul had been introduced as the man with
  no one in Israel *better*, *ṭôḇ*, than he (9:2, WLC). The successor is unnamed here as in 13:14.
- **Cheerfully.** *Maʿădannōṯ* (32, WLC). The word's sense is uncertain: *cheerfully*, *delicately*, and *in fetters*
  have all been proposed (recalled, not checked). Morphhb files it with *maʿădannîm*, the *royal dainties* of Genesis
  49:20 (lemma 4574), its only other occurrence in Genesis–2 Samuel. Agag's words, *Surely the bitterness of death is
  past*, can be read as relief or as resignation.
- **Childless.** *Šikkəlâ … tiškal* (33, WLC), the verb of Jacob's *you have bereaved me of my children* (Genesis
  42:36) and Rebekah's *Why should I be bereaved of you both in one day?* (Genesis 27:45) (WLC, lemma 7921). Samuel
  states Agag's death as retribution in kind, for what his sword had done.
- **Cut in pieces.** *Wayšassēp̄* (33, WLC), a verb that occurs only here in Genesis–2 Samuel (morphhb lemma 8158),
  and whose exact sense is not certain. *Before Yahweh in Gilgal*: the prophet does what the king did not, at the
  place where Saul meant to sacrifice.
- **Samuel mourned.** *Kî-hiṯʾabbēl šəmûʾēl ʾel-šāʾûl* (35, WLC), the verb of the mourning at Beth Shemesh (6:19) and
  of Yahweh's question in 16:1, *How long will you mourn for Saul?* (WLC, lemma 56). Samuel cried to Yahweh all night
  at the start (11) and mourns at the end. The chapter's last clause gives Yahweh's grief, *nāḥam*, the same word as
  verse 11.
- **The Kenites.** Saul spares a people for *ḥeseḏ*, *kindness*, done *when they came up out of Egypt* (6, WLC),
  the same occasion as Amalek's attack (2). Israel remembers both, one for good and one for ill. See
  [[Hobab and the Kenites - Numbers 10, Judges 1 and 4, and 1 Samuel 15]].
- **Agag.** Balaam had said of Israel *His king shall be higher than Agag* (Numbers 24:7, stored WEB), and then
  *Amalek was the first of the nations, but his latter end shall come to destruction* (24:20). Whether the Agag of the
  oracle is this man, an earlier king of the same name, or a royal name, the text does not say. Esther's Haman is
  *the Agagite* (Esther 3:1, WEB checked at bible-api.com); the link to this Agag is a common reading, not stated
  there. See [[Agag]].
- **Not finished.** *All the people* of Amalek were destroyed (8), and yet Amalekites raid Ziklag and David strikes
  them in chapter 30 (30:1, 17–18, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and an Amalekite tells David he killed Saul (2 Samuel
  1:8–10, WEB checked at bible-api.com). Whether verse 8 means the people in the battle, or overstates, the text
  does not reconcile. Samuel at Endor names this chapter as the cause of Saul's end: *Because you didn't obey Yahweh's
  voice, and didn't execute his fierce wrath on Amalek* (28:18, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The numbers.** *Two hundred thousand … and ten thousand men of Judah* (4), against the six hundred of 13:15 and
  14:2. Like the other large numbers of these books, the size is recorded, not explained.

**The moral problem.** Verse 3 commands the killing of *infant and nursing baby*. The chapter does not ask whether
the command is just; its question is whether Saul obeyed it. The wiki records the command and Saul's partial
disobedience, does not defend or explain away the command, and does not treat Saul's sparing of Agag and the
livestock as mercy, since the text does not present it as mercy: the people were killed and the king and the best
animals kept. Readers have answered the problem in many ways; none is adopted here. See
[[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]] and [[War]].

**How it connects.** The chapter closes the account of Saul's reign as king in the full sense. Chapters 13–15 are
three tests and three failures: a sacrifice offered without waiting (13), an oath that nearly killed his son (14),
and a war left half done for the sake of a sacrifice (15). Both rejections take place at Gilgal, and both turn on
*the commandment of Yahweh* (13:13; 15:24). The next pericope, *Samuel Anoints David* (16:1–13, planned, not yet
ingested), begins with Samuel still mourning, and Yahweh telling him to go to Jesse.

## People
- [[Saul]] — commanded to devote Amalek; spares Agag and the best; rejected from being king (15:1–35)
- [[Samuel]] — brings the commission, cries all night, confronts Saul at Gilgal, kills Agag, and mourns (15:1–35)
- [[Amalek]] — struck *from Havilah as you go to Shur*; their king spared, then killed (15:2–9, 32–33)
- [[Agag]] — king of the Amalekites, taken alive and cut in pieces at Gilgal (15:8–9, 20, 32–33)
- [[Kenites]] — warned to leave the Amalekites, for their kindness to Israel (15:6)
- [[David]] — not named; *a neighbor of yours who is better than you* (15:28)
- [[God]] — *it grieves me*; *the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent* (15:11, 29, 35)

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 15:1–35 as planned: 14:52 closes the summary of the reign, and 16:1 opens a new
commission. One new place page, [[Telaim]], named only at 15:4, with the commonly proposed identification with Telem
(Joshua 15:24) recorded as a proposal. No page for *the city of Amalek* (5), which the text does not name, or for
*the elders of my people* (30). The Carmel of verse 12 is linked to [[Carmel (Judah)]] with *presumably*: the text
names no region, and the Judahite Carmel lies on a southern route. [[Agag]] stays one page for the narrated king of
Amalek and the Agag of Numbers 24:7, with the identity left open on the page; it is not qualified as a royal title,
since the text does not say it is one. One new connection,
[[The Torn Robe - 1 Samuel 15.27-28, 24.4-5, 28.17, and 1 Kings 11.29-31]].

**Unresolved.** How 15:29 relates to 15:11 and 35. Whether Saul's distinction between plunder and *the chief of the
devoted things* (21) had any standing. Who grabbed whose robe (27). The sense of *maʿădannōṯ* (32), *wayšassēp̄* (33),
and the title *nēṣaḥ yiśrāʾēl* (29). Whether *all the people* (8) is reconcilable with the Amalekites of chapter 30 and
2 Samuel 1. Whether the Agag of Numbers 24:7 is this man. The size of the muster (4).
