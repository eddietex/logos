# David at Nob

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 21:1–9

## Summary

> **21:1** Then David came to Nob to Ahimelech the priest. Ahimelech came to meet David trembling, and said to him,
> “Why are you alone, and no man with you?”
> **2** David said to Ahimelech the priest, “The king has commanded me to do something, and has said to me, ‘Let no one
> know anything about the business about which I send you, and what I have commanded you. I have sent the young men to a
> certain place.’
> **3** Now therefore what is under your hand? Please give me five loaves of bread in my hand, or whatever is available.”
> **4** The priest answered David, and said, “I have no common bread, but there is holy bread; if only the young men have
> kept themselves from women.”
> **5** David answered the priest, and said to him, “Truly, women have been kept from us as usual these three days. When
> I came out, the vessels of the young men were holy, though it was only a common journey. How much more then today shall
> their vessels be holy?”
> **6** So the priest gave him holy bread; for there was no bread there but the show bread that was taken from before
> Yahweh, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken away.
> **7** Now a certain man of the servants of Saul was there that day, detained before Yahweh; and his name was Doeg the
> Edomite, the best of the herdsmen who belonged to Saul.
> **8** David said to Ahimelech, “Isn’t there here under your hand spear or sword? For I have neither brought my sword nor
> my weapons with me, because the king’s business required haste.”
> **9** The priest said, “Behold, the sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you killed in the valley of Elah, is here
> wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod. If you would like to take that, take it; for there is no other except that here.”
> David said, “There is none like that. Give it to me.”

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 21.)

Fresh from his parting with [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]], [[David]] comes to [[Nob]], to [[Ahimelech (son of Ahitub)]] the
priest (1). Ahimelech comes to meet him *trembling*, and asks the obvious question: *Why are you alone, and no man with
you?* (1). David answers with an invented secret errand from [[Saul]]: *The king has commanded me to do something*, and
ordered that no one know anything about it; he says he has stationed *the young men* at *a certain place* (2). Then he
asks for food: *what is under your hand? Please give me five loaves of bread in my hand, or whatever is available* (3).

Ahimelech has *no common bread*, only *holy bread*, and names one condition: *if only the young men have kept themselves
from women* (4). David assures him that *women have been kept from us as usual these three days*, and that the young men's
*vessels* were holy even on *a common journey* — *How much more then today shall their vessels be holy?* (5). The priest
gives him the *holy bread*, because the only bread there is *the show bread that was taken from before Yahweh, to put hot
bread in the day when it was taken away* (6).

Between the bread and the sword, the narrator pauses for one ominous verse: *a certain man of the servants of Saul was
there that day, detained before Yahweh; and his name was Doeg the Edomite, the best of the herdsmen who belonged to Saul*
(7). [[Doeg]] says nothing and does nothing here; he simply sees.

David then asks for a weapon: *Isn't there here under your hand spear or sword?* — excusing his unarmed state on the
ground that *the king's business required haste* (8). Ahimelech answers that *the sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom
you killed in the valley of Elah*, is *wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod*, and offers it: *for there is no other except
that here* (9). David replies, *There is none like that. Give it to me* (9).

**Where it happens.** At [[Nob]] (1), named here for the first time in the stored books. The narrator does not locate it
geographically or explain how the sanctuary of the show bread and the ephod came to stand there after [[Shiloh]], while
the ark remains at [[Kiriath Jearim]] (7:1–2). The [[Valley of Elah]] is recalled by name as the place where David killed
[[Goliath]] (9), and [[Edom]] appears through Doeg's gentilic, *the Edomite* (7).

**What is going on.** This pericope opens the sixth section of 1 Samuel — David as a fugitive (chapters 21–27). In
[[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]], David finally broke with Saul's court; here he arrives at Yahweh's sanctuary alone,
hungry, unarmed, and lying. Every earlier deception in David's story either came from someone else (Samuel's heifer at
16:2–5, Michal's teraphim at 19:13–17) or was arranged jointly with Jonathan to test Saul (20:5–6). Here David deceives an
innocent priest on his own initiative, invoking *the king's business* to get bread and a sword — and the lie will cost
Ahimelech and eighty-five priests their lives in the next chapter (22:9–19, stored WEB; not yet ingested).

Yet the narrator passes no verdict on David's lie here, and neither does Ahimelech suspect it. Instead, the scene hinges
on two transfers from the sanctuary into the fugitive's hands: **bread from before Yahweh** and **a sword from behind the
ephod**. Both are extraordinary. The bread belongs by law to Aaron and his sons *in a holy place* (Leviticus 24:9), and
the sword is the trophy of the very victory in which David declared *Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear* (17:47).
Standing silently between those two gifts is [[Doeg]] — Saul's Edomite herdsman, *detained before Yahweh* — turning a
brief stop for provisions into the trap that closes on Nob in chapter 22.

**What is notable.**

- **Ahimelech comes trembling.** *Ahimelech came to meet David trembling, and said to him, "Why are you alone, and no man
  with you?"* (1). The same reaction met Samuel when he arrived unexpectedly at Bethlehem: *The elders of the city came to
  meet him trembling, and said, "Do you come peaceably?"* (16:4). A famous commander and royal son-in-law arriving alone
  at a priestly town signals trouble. Unlike Samuel at Bethlehem, David does not answer with peace; he answers with a
  fabricated royal commission.
- **Alone — or with young men?** Ahimelech asks *Why are you alone, and no man with you?* (1), and the narrator never
  shows anyone else with David at Nob. David himself introduces *the young men* (2, 4, 5) as part of his cover story
  (*I have sent the young men to a certain place*), and Ahimelech adopts David's phrasing in setting his condition (4).
  Later, when Jesus cites this episode in the Gospels (Matthew 12:3–4; Mark 2:25–26, as recorded on
  [[The Bread of the Presence - David's Hunger and Matthew 12]]), he speaks of *David … and those who were with him*.
  Whether David actually had companions waiting nearby or invented them to explain why he asked for *five loaves* (3) is
  not settled by 1 Samuel 21; the wiki records the tension and does not resolve it.
- **Holy bread and common bread.** Ahimelech distinguishes *common bread* from *holy bread* (4), and the narrator
  identifies the gift as *the show bread that was taken from before Yahweh, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken
  away* (6). Under [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] (Exodus 25:30) and [[The Lamps and the Bread Before Yahweh]]
  (Leviticus 24:5–9), twelve cakes are set in two rows before Yahweh and renewed *every Sabbath day*; Leviticus 24:9
  assigns the removed loaves strictly to *Aaron and his sons* to eat *in a holy place: for it is most holy*, and
  [[Eating the Holy Things]] (Leviticus 22:10–16) bars any outsider from eating a holy thing. Here the priest gives the
  just-replaced loaves to a non-priest — and implies by *in the day when it was taken away* that David has arrived on the
  Sabbath, the day of renewal. The narrator neither condemns Ahimelech nor explains the exception; Jesus later appeals to
  this precedent in his Sabbath grain-field dispute (Matthew 12:3–8; Mark 2:25–26). See
  [[The Bread of the Presence - David's Hunger and Matthew 12]] and
  [[Who May Eat the Holy Portions - Leviticus 6 and 7]].
- **Kept from women for three days.** Ahimelech's single condition is sexual abstinence: *if only the young men have kept
  themselves from women* (4). David answers that *women have been kept from us as usual these three days*, and argues from
  lesser to greater: if *the vessels of the young men were holy* even on a common journey, *How much more then today shall
  their vessels be holy?* (5). The three-day abstinence recalls the consecration of Israel before Sinai (*Be ready by the
  third day. Don't have sexual relations with a woman*, Exodus 19:15), and the purity of men on a campaign recalls the
  holy-camp rule of Deuteronomy 23:9–14 and the marital impurity until evening of Leviticus 15:18 (and later Uriah's
  abstinence in 2 Samuel 11:11, recalled, not checked). What *vessels* means — their gear and packs, or a euphemism for
  their bodies — is left open by the text. See
  [[Kept from Women - Consecration at Sinai, the Holy Camp, and Nob]].
- **Detained before Yahweh — Doeg the Edomite.** Verse 7 interrupts the dialogue without joining it: *a certain man of the
  servants of Saul was there that day, detained before Yahweh; and his name was Doeg the Edomite, the best of the herdsmen
  who belonged to Saul*. Why Doeg is *detained before Yahweh* (whether by a vow, ritual impurity, an offering, or
  sanctuary business) is never explained. That an Edomite stands *before Yahweh* at Israel's sanctuary sits beside
  Deuteronomy 23:7–8 (*You shall not abhor an Edomite; for he is your brother*, admissible to Yahweh's assembly in the
  third generation) and Saul's war with Edom (14:47). In chapter 22 Doeg reports what he saw — adding that Ahimelech
  *inquired of Yahweh for him* (22:10), an act 21:1–9 never narrates — and when Saul's own guards refuse to touch the
  priests, Doeg kills eighty-five men wearing a linen ephod and destroys Nob (22:9–19, stored WEB; not yet ingested). At
  22:22 David will confess to Abiathar, *I knew on that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell
  Saul. I am responsible for the death of all the persons of your father's house.*
- **Spear or sword, and the sword of Goliath.** David asks, *Isn't there here under your hand spear or sword?* (8) —
  repeating the exact weapon pair of Israel's disarmament (13:19, 22) and his own speech at Elah (*Yahweh doesn't save
  with sword and spear*, 17:47). The man who had *no sword in the hand of David* (17:50), who refused Saul's sword
  (17:39), and who received Jonathan's sword (18:4) is now unarmed and desperate. The only weapon in Nob is *the sword of
  Goliath the Philistine, whom you killed in the valley of Elah*, kept *wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod* (9). In 17:54
  David brought Goliath's head to Jerusalem and put his armor in his tent; 1 Samuel never explains how Goliath's sword came
  to be deposited behind the ephod at Nob. Taking it, David says, *There is none like that. Give it to me* (9) — the
  weapon of the giant he defeated without a sword now becomes the fugitive's sidearm, carried straight into Goliath's own
  city of Gath in the next verse (21:10). See
  [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]] and
  [[The Head and the Armor - Goliath at Elah and Saul at Gilboa]].
- **Mark 2:26 and Abiathar.** 1 Samuel 21:1, 2, 8 name the priest at Nob as *Ahimelech*, and 22:20 names *Abiathar* as
  *one of the sons of Ahimelech, the son of Ahitub*. Mark 2:26 has Jesus place this event *at the time of Abiathar the
  high priest* (recorded on [[The Bread of the Presence - David's Hunger and Matthew 12]]). Matthew 12:4 and Luke 6:4
  omit the priest's name. The wiki records the discrepancy and does not resolve it.
- **Ahimelech and Ahijah.** Chapter 22 identifies Ahimelech as *the son of Ahitub* (22:9, 11, 20), the same patronymic
  given to [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]], the priest wearing an ephod with Saul at Gibeah (14:3). Whether Ahijah and
  Ahimelech are brothers or one man under two names is not stated in 1 Samuel; the wiki keeps them as separate pages.

**How it connects.** 1 Samuel 21:1–9 looks backward to the Torah's sanctuary laws (Exodus 19:15; 25:30; Leviticus 15:18;
22:10–16; 24:5–9; Deuteronomy 23:9–14) and to Elah (17:45–54), and forward to two very different destinations: immediately
to the slaughter of Nob in chapter 22 (which advances the sentence against Eli's house from 2:27–36 and leaves Abiathar as
the sole surviving priest fleeing to David with the ephod, 22:20; 23:6), and centuries later to Jesus's defense of his
hungry disciples on the Sabbath (Matthew 12:3–8; Mark 2:25–26). In the next verse (21:10), David leaves Nob and flees
with Goliath's sword to Achish king of Gath.

## People
- [[David]] — arrives at Nob alone, fabricates a secret royal errand, obtains five loaves of the replaced show bread by
  declaring ritual abstinence, and takes Goliath's sword (21:1–9)
- [[Ahimelech (son of Ahitub)]] — the priest at Nob; meets David trembling, gives him the holy show bread on condition of
  sexual purity, and gives him Goliath's sword from behind the ephod (21:1–9)
- [[Doeg]] — *Doeg the Edomite*, *a certain man of the servants of Saul*, *the best of the herdsmen who belonged to Saul*,
  present and *detained before Yahweh* that day (21:7)
- [[Saul]] — not present; invoked by David as *the king* whose secret, urgent business explains his lone arrival and lack
  of weapons (21:2, 8), and named as Doeg's master (21:7)
- [[Goliath]] — not present; recalled as *Goliath the Philistine, whom you killed in the valley of Elah*, whose sword is
  kept behind the ephod and given to David (21:9)

## Places
- [[Nob]] — the sanctuary town where Ahimelech ministers, the show bread is renewed, the ephod stands, Doeg is detained,
  and Goliath's sword is kept (21:1–9)
- [[Valley of Elah]] — recalled by Ahimelech as the place where David killed Goliath (21:9)
- [[Edom]] — present in Doeg's gentilic, *the Edomite* (21:7)

## Themes
- [[Priesthood]] — Ahimelech exercises priestly judgment over holy bread and guards the sanctuary and ephod at Nob (21:1–9)
- [[Holiness]] — *common bread* vs. *holy bread*; *vessels* kept holy; the show bread from *before Yahweh* (21:4–6)
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — keeping from women for three days as the condition for eating holy bread (21:4–5)
- [[Deception]] — David's invented secret royal errand and absent *young men* to obtain provisions and a weapon (21:2, 8)
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — the sanctuary at Nob with the table of show bread, the ephod, and standing *before Yahweh*
  after Shiloh and apart from the ark at Kiriath Jearim (21:6–7, 9)
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — the show bread taken from before Yahweh and replaced with hot bread on the appointed day
  (21:6)
- [[Hospitality]] — the trembling priest feeding an unarmed fugitive with the only bread in the sanctuary (21:3–6)
- [[War]] — David asking for *spear or sword* and receiving Goliath's sword; the ritual purity of warriors on a journey
  (21:4–5, 8–9)
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Leviticus 24:9's priestly restriction bent for David's need, without narrated condemnation
  (21:4–6)
- [[Sabbath]] — *to put hot bread in the day when it was taken away*, the Sabbath renewal day of Leviticus 24:8, later
  central to the Gospel Sabbath disputes (21:6)

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 21:1–9 as planned: 21:1 opens David's arrival at Nob, 21:9 closes the exchange with
Ahimelech over Goliath's sword, and 21:10 opens David's flight to Achish of Gath. Three new entity pages:
[[Ahimelech (son of Ahitub)]] (qualified from the start because `Ahimelech the Hittite` appears at 1 Samuel 26:6 and
`Ahimelech son of Abiathar` in 2 Samuel 8:17 / 1 Chronicles 24:3, matching [[Ahijah (son of Ahitub)]]), [[Doeg]] (bare
name, unique in Scripture), and [[Nob]] (bare name, no collision with [[Nobah (town)]]). *The young men* (21:2, 4, 5) get
no person page: they never appear in the scene, are unnamed, and may be David's invention. One new connection:
[[Kept from Women - Consecration at Sinai, the Holy Camp, and Nob]], linking the three-day sexual abstinence and vessel
holiness of 21:4–5 to Exodus 19:15, Leviticus 15:18, Deuteronomy 23:9–14, and 2 Samuel 11:11. The bread of the Presence,
sword-and-spear, Goliath's trophy, holy-portions, and house-of-Eli threads already had home pages and were updated there.

**Recorded, not resolved.**
- **Alone vs. with companions.** Ahimelech sees David *alone, and no man with you* (21:1); David claims he sent *the young
  men* to a certain place (21:2, 5); the Gospels later speak of David *and those who were with him* (Matthew 12:3–4; Mark
  2:25–26). Whether companions waited nearby or David invented them to justify asking for five loaves is not decided.
- **Ahimelech vs. Abiathar (Mark 2:26).** 1 Samuel 21:1–9 names *Ahimelech*, and 22:20 names *Abiathar* as his son; Mark
  2:26 locates the event *at the time of Abiathar the high priest*. Recorded, not harmonized.
- **Ahimelech vs. Ahijah (14:3).** Both are called *son of Ahitub* (14:3; 22:9, 11, 20) and minister with an ephod;
  whether they are brothers or one man under two names is left open.
- **Did Ahimelech inquire of God for David?** Doeg tells Saul that Ahimelech *inquired of Yahweh for him* (22:10), and
  Ahimelech's reply *Have I today begun to inquire of God for him?* (22:15) does not deny the practice; yet 21:1–9 records
  only bread and a sword, never an inquiry. Recorded as a gap between chapters 21 and 22.
- **Why Doeg was *detained before Yahweh* (21:7).** The text gives no reason (vow, impurity, offering, or sanctuary duty).
- **How Goliath's sword reached Nob (21:9).** 17:54 records Goliath's head going to Jerusalem and his armor to David's
  tent, without mentioning the sword's deposit behind the ephod at Nob.
- **What *vessels* means (21:5).** Either the young men's gear/packs or a euphemism for their bodies; the WEB leaves
  *vessels* ambiguous.
- **How the sanctuary moved from Shiloh to Nob.** 1 Samuel narrates the ark's departure from Shiloh (4:3–11) and stay at
  Kiriath Jearim (7:1–2), but never explains the establishment of the sanctuary and show bread table at Nob.
