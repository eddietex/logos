# Jonathan Loves David

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 17:55–18:5

## Summary

> **17:55** When Saul saw David go out against the Philistine, he said to Abner, the captain of the army, “Abner,
> whose son is this youth?” Abner said, “As your soul lives, O king, I can’t tell.”
> **56** The king said, “Inquire whose son the young man is!”
> **57** As David returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, Abner took him and brought him before Saul with the
> head of the Philistine in his hand.
> **58** Saul said to him, “Whose son are you, you young man?” David answered, “I am the son of your servant Jesse the
> Bethlehemite.”
> **18:1** When he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan
> loved him as his own soul.
> **2** Saul took him that day, and would let him go no more home to his father’s house.
> **3** Then Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as his own soul.
> **4** Jonathan stripped himself of the robe that was on him, and gave it to David, and his clothing, even including
> his sword, his bow, and his sash.
> **5** David went out wherever Saul sent him, and behaved himself wisely; and Saul set him over the men of war. It was
> good in the sight of all the people, and also in the sight of Saul’s servants.

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapters 17–18.)

The scene goes back in time. As [[David]] went out against the Philistine, [[Saul]] asked [[Abner]], *the captain of
the army*, *whose son is this youth?*, and Abner swore he did not know (17:55). The king told him to find out (56).
When David came back from the killing, Abner brought him to Saul *with the head of the Philistine in his hand* (57).
Saul asked him directly, and David answered with his father's name: *I am the son of your servant Jesse the
Bethlehemite* (58).

Then the chapter break, and the speech is over. *The soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan
loved him as his own soul* (18:1). Saul *took him that day* and would not let him go home (2).
[[Jonathan (son of Saul)]] and David *made a covenant*, the reason given again, *because he loved him as his own soul* (3).
Jonathan strips off his robe and gives it to David, and with it his clothing, his sword, his bow, and his sash (4).
David goes out wherever Saul sends him, *behaved himself wisely*, is set over the men of war, and it is *good in the
sight of all the people, and also in the sight of Saul's servants* (5).

**Where it happens.** At the battle line in the [[Valley of Elah]], before Saul, after the fight of 17:41–54. Verse 57
has David still carrying the head, though 17:54 has already taken it to [[Jerusalem]]; the order is the narrator's,
and the text does not reconcile the two. *Jesse the Bethlehemite* names [[Bethlehem]] (58).

**What is going on.** Saul asks the question three times, twice to Abner (55, 56) and once to David (58), and each time
it is *whose son*, *ben-mî*, not *who* (WLC). The book has given him a reason to ask about the father: the reward for
killing Goliath was *his daughter*, and to *make his father's house free in Israel* (17:25, stored WEB). The answer is
a father's name and a town, and it is the last thing David says in the pericope. Everything after it is done to him or
for him: Jonathan loves him, Saul takes him, Jonathan makes a covenant with him and clothes him, Saul sends him and sets
him over the army.

The tension with 16:14–23 is plain. There Saul's servant had named David as *a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite* (16:18),
Saul sent to Jesse for *David your son* (16:19), *loved him greatly*, made him his armor bearer, and sent to Jesse
again, *Please let David stand before me* (16:21–22, stored WEB). Here neither Saul nor his army commander knows whose
son he is. The text does not explain it. Readings offered include that Saul asks about the family he must now free
(17:25) rather than the man; that the court harpist was not known by sight to the commander; and that two accounts of
David's arrival at court have been put side by side; the Septuagint (Codex Vaticanus) is reported to lack 17:55–18:5
along with 17:12–31 (all recalled, not checked; no Septuagint text was consulted). The wiki records both accounts and
does not reconcile them. See [[Whose Son Is This Youth - 1 Samuel 16.18-22 and 17.55-58]].

Then Jonathan. He has not been named since 14:49, and he is not said to have been present at Elah. The verse begins
*when he had finished speaking to Saul* and moves at once to Jonathan's *nepeš*. What Jonathan does next is in the
language of covenant and of clothing. He does what his father did not get to do: Saul dressed David in *his clothing*,
*maddāyw*, with helmet, mail, and sword, and David took them off, *I can't go with these* (17:38–39); Jonathan gives
David *his clothing*, *maddāyw*, again, with his sword (18:4, WLC; stored WEB), and nothing says David refuses. In
13:22, *Saul and Jonathan his son* were the only men in Israel's army with sword and spear; one of those two swords is
now David's.

**What is notable.**

- **Whose son.** *Ben-mî-zeh hannaʿar* (55), *ben-mî-zeh hāʿālem* (56), *ben-mî ʾattâ hannaʿar* (58) (WLC). Saul's
  word changes once: in 56 it is *ʿelem* (lemma 5958), *young man*, which in the Hebrew files checked occurs only here
  and at 20:22, the boy Jonathan sends after the arrows (*If I say this to the boy*, stored WEB). The book has asked
  *whose son* about a man before: of Saul among the prophets, *Who is their father?* (10:12, stored WEB). That link is
  the wiki's; the text draws none.
- **As your soul lives.** *Ḥê-nap̄šəḵā hammeleḵ ʾim-yāḏāʿtî* (55, WLC): literally *if I know*, the oath form that
  swears a negative. Hannah used the same oath to Eli, *as your soul lives, my lord* (1:26, stored WEB). See
  [[By the Life of Pharaoh - Oaths Sworn by a King]] and [[Oaths and Vows]].
- **The head in his hand.** *Wərōʾš happəlištî bəyāḏô* (57, WLC). Verse 54 has already brought the head to Jerusalem;
  here it is still in David's hand before Saul. See [[David Kills Goliath]] and
  [[The Head and the Armor - Goliath at Elah and Saul at Gilboa]].
- **Jesse the Bethlehemite.** *Yišay bêṯ hallaḥmî* (58, WLC), the phrase of 16:1 (Yahweh to Samuel) and 16:18 (Saul's
  servant) (stored WEB). David calls his father *your servant*, *ʿaḇdəḵā*. See [[Jesse]].
- **Knit.** *Wənep̄eš yəhônāṯān niqšərâ bənep̄eš dāwiḏ* (18:1, WLC; *qāšar*, lemma 7194). In the Hebrew files checked,
  *qāšar* with *nepeš* on both sides of it occurs only here and at Genesis 44:30, Judah of Jacob and Benjamin: *his
  life is bound up in the boy's life* (stored WEB). The same verb is Saul's charge four chapters on: *all of you have
  conspired against me … when my son makes a treaty with the son of Jesse* (22:8, stored WEB; *qəšartem*, WLC; not yet
  ingested). Jonathan's soul *knit* to David's becomes, in Saul's mouth, a conspiracy. See
  [[His Life Is Bound Up in the Boy's Life - Nephesh Tied to Nephesh]].
- **As his own soul.** *Kənap̄šô*, twice (18:1, 3, WLC). In the Hebrew files checked, *nepeš* with the prefix *kə-*
  occurs otherwise only at Deuteronomy 23:24 WEB (23:25 WLC), *at your own pleasure*, and Deuteronomy 13:6 WEB (13:7
  WLC), *your friend, who is as your own soul*, the closest companion, there the one who might entice to other gods
  (stored WEB). 20:17 returns to it: *he loved him as he loved his own soul* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]] and [[Love]].
- **Loved him.** The WLC's consonantal text (*ketiv*) reads *wyʾhbw* and the vocalization (*qere*) reads
  *wayyeʾĕhāḇēhû*, *and he loved him* (18:1). Jonathan is the second person in 1 Samuel said to love David, after Saul
  (16:21); *all Israel and Judah* and Michal follow (18:16, [[Saul's Jealousy of David]]; 18:20, stored WEB, not yet ingested). In the files checked,
  *ʾāhaḇ* and *nepeš* in one verse is otherwise Isaac's savory food (Genesis 27:4), Shechem and Dinah (34:3), Ruth's
  daughter-in-law *who loves you* (Ruth 4:15), and the commands to love Yahweh *with all your soul* (Deuteronomy 6:5;
  10:12; 11:13; 13:3 WEB; 30:6; Joshua 22:5; 23:11) (WLC; stored WEB).
- **Saul took him.** *Wayyiqqāḥēhû šāʾûl* (18:2, WLC; *lāqaḥ*, lemma 3947). It is the verb of Samuel's warning, *he
  will take your sons* (8:11), and Abner has just *taken* him to Saul (17:57) (stored WEB). The summary of Saul's reign
  had said *when Saul saw any mighty man … he took him into his service* (14:52), with a different verb, *ʾāsap̄*
  (WLC). In 16:22 Saul asked Jesse's leave; here he does not.
- **His father's house.** *Wəlōʾ nəṯānô lāšûḇ bêṯ ʾāḇîw* (18:2, WLC). 17:15 had David going *back and forth from
  Saul to feed his father's sheep* (stored WEB). That ends here.
- **A covenant.** *Wayyiḵrōṯ yəhônāṯān wədāwiḏ bərîṯ* (18:3, WLC). It is the first *bərîṯ* in 1 Samuel since the men of
  Jabesh asked one of Nahash (11:1); before that it was *the ark of the covenant* (4:3–5). The next two are this one's sequels,
  *a covenant of Yahweh* (20:8) and *They both made a covenant before Yahweh* (23:18) (stored WEB; WLC). The text gives
  no terms here; only the reason, love. Saul's *makes a treaty* (22:8) has *kāraṯ* without *bərîṯ* (WLC). See
  [[Covenant]].
- **Stripped himself.** *Wayyiṯpaššēṭ* (18:4, WLC; *pāšaṭ*, lemma 6584, in the reflexive *hitpael*). In the Hebrew
  files checked, this is the only *hitpael* of the verb. The *qal* is Saul's at Ramah, *He also stripped off his
  clothes* (19:24), and the Philistines' at Gilboa, who came *to strip the slain* and *stripped off his armor* (31:8–9)
  (stored WEB; WLC; not yet ingested). Saul strips himself before Samuel; the Philistines strip Saul. Jonathan strips
  himself, and gives what he takes off to David.
- **The robe.** *Hamməʿîl ʾăšer ʿālāyw* (18:4, WLC; lemma 4598). *Məʿîl* is the high priest's robe of the ephod in
  Exodus and Leviticus, Samuel's *little robe* from his mother (2:19), the robe Saul tore (15:27), the robe of Saul's
  that David cuts at En Gedi (24:4–5, 11 WEB), the robe the medium sees on Samuel (28:14), and in 2 Samuel the robe of
  the king's virgin daughters (13:18, WLC; paraphrased) (stored WEB). Jonathan is the only one in the list who gives
  his away. See [[The Torn Robe - 1 Samuel 15.27-28, 24.4-5, 28.17, and 1 Kings 11.29-31]].
- **His clothing.** *Maddāyw* (18:4; lemma 4055). In 1 Samuel the word is the clothing torn by the runner from
  Ebenezer (4:12), Saul's clothing put on David (17:38–39), and Jonathan's (18:4) (WLC). In Judges 3:16 Ehud wears his sword
  *under his clothing* (WLC; stored WEB). David came to the fight in a shepherd's dress after taking
  off Saul's; he leaves it in the king's son's.
- **Sword, bow, and sash.** *Ḥarbô … qaštô … ḥăḡōrô* (18:4, WLC). *Ḥăḡôr* (lemma 2290) occurs in the files checked
  only here, at Genesis 3:7, the fig-leaf *coverings* (stored WEB), and twice in 2 Samuel (18:11; 20:8, WLC;
  paraphrased), a belt offered by Joab and a belt worn by him. *Qešeṯ*, *bow*, in 1 Samuel is otherwise Hannah's *The
  bows of the mighty men are broken* (2:4) and the archers who overtake Saul at Gilboa (31:3); 2 Samuel 1:18, 22 names
  *the bow of Jonathan* in David's lament (stored WEB for 1 Samuel; WLC for 2 Samuel, paraphrased). See
  [[The Bow in the Cloud]] and
  [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].
- **Behaved himself wisely.** *Yaśkîl* (18:5, WLC; *śāḵal*, lemma 7919). In the files checked the verb occurs in
  1 Samuel only in this chapter (18:5, 14, 15, 30), and before it at Genesis 3:6, *desired to make one wise*; Genesis
  48:14, Jacob *guiding his hands knowingly*; Deuteronomy 29:9 WEB, *that you may prosper*, and 32:29, *Oh that they
  were wise*; and Joshua 1:7–8, *that you may have good success* (stored WEB; WLC). The WEB renders it *wisely* here and *prosper* or *success* elsewhere; the Hebrew holds both. See
  [[Wisdom]].
- **Good in the sight of all.** *Wayyîṭaḇ bəʿênê kol-hāʿām wəḡam bəʿênê ʿaḇdê šāʾûl* (18:5, WLC). The people and Saul's
  servants approve; Saul's own eyes are not mentioned. The next verse turns to them: the women sing, and Saul *watched
  David* from that day (18:6–9, [[Saul's Jealousy of David]]). See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **Jonathan's name.** From 18:1 the WLC spells him *yəhônāṯān* (lemma 3083), the long form it used before only at
  14:6, 8; through chapters 18–20 it uses the long form everywhere except 19:1 (morphhb lemmas 3083 and 3129). The wiki
  records the spelling and draws no conclusion from it.

**How it connects.** The pericope finishes the Goliath story and opens the house of Saul to David. The question of
16:14–23 and 17:12–40, how David comes to stand before Saul, is answered twice, and both answers stand in the text.
Jonathan's love is the first of a series: Saul's servants, *all Israel and Judah*, and Michal (18:16, 20, 22), and
Saul's turning against him begins in the next verse (18:6–9, [[Saul's Jealousy of David]]). The covenant is renewed at
20:8–17, 42 and 23:18; the robe and the sword point toward the kingdom passing from Jonathan's house to David's, which
Jonathan himself will say at Horesh, *you will be king over Israel, and I will be next to you* (23:17, stored WEB; not
yet ingested). The text of 18:4 does not say what the gift means; that reading is the wiki's, from 23:17.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 17:55–18:5 as planned: 17:55 goes back to the moment David went out, and 18:5
closes with David sent out, set over the army, and approved; 18:6 opens the women's song and Saul's jealousy, the next
unit. No new person or place pages. The one new connection,
[[Whose Son Is This Youth - 1 Samuel 16.18-22 and 17.55-58]], holds the doublet that two earlier passage pages had
recorded as *not yet ingested*. The reading of the robe and the sword as a sign of succession is the wiki's, from 23:17,
and is marked as such.

**Unresolved.** Why Saul and Abner do not know David after 16:14–23. Why the head is in David's hand (57) after 17:54.
Whether *whose son* asks about the man or about the father whose house 17:25 promised to free. Whether Jonathan was at
Elah. What the covenant of 18:3 committed either man to; 18:3 gives no terms.
