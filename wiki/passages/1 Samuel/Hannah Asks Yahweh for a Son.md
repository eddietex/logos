# Hannah Asks Yahweh for a Son

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 1:1–28

## Summary

> **1** Now there was a certain man of Ramathaim Zophim, of the hill country of Ephraim, and his name was
> Elkanah, the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, an Ephraimite.
> **2** He had two wives. The name of one was Hannah, and the name of other Peninnah. Peninnah had children,
> but Hannah had no children.
> **3** This man went up out of his city from year to year to worship and to sacrifice to Yahweh of Armies in
> Shiloh. The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, priests to Yahweh, were there.
> **4** When the day came that Elkanah sacrificed, he gave to Peninnah his wife, and to all her sons and her
> daughters, portions;
> **5** but to Hannah he gave a double portion, for he loved Hannah, but Yahweh had shut up her womb.
> **6** Her rival provoked her severely, to irritate her, because Yahweh had shut up her womb.
> **7** As he did so year by year, when she went up to Yahweh's house. Her rival provoked her; therefore she
> wept, and didn't eat.
> **8** Elkanah her husband said to her, "Hannah, why do you weep? Why don't you eat? Why is your heart
> grieved? Am I not better to you than ten sons?"
> **9** So Hannah rose up after they had finished eating and drinking in Shiloh. Now Eli the priest was
> sitting on his seat by the doorpost of Yahweh's temple.
> **10** She was in bitterness of soul, and prayed to Yahweh, weeping bitterly.
> **11** She vowed a vow, and said, "Yahweh of Armies, if you will indeed look at the affliction of your
> servant, and remember me, and not forget your servant, but will give to your servant a boy, then I will
> give him to Yahweh all the days of his life, and no razor shall come on his head."
> **12** As she continued praying before Yahweh, Eli saw her mouth.
> **13** Now Hannah spoke in her heart. Only her lips moved, but her voice was not heard. Therefore Eli
> thought she was drunk.
> **14** Eli said to her, "How long will you be drunk? Get rid of your wine!"
> **15** Hannah answered, "No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit. I have not been drinking wine or
> strong drink, but I poured out my soul before Yahweh.
> **16** Don't consider your servant a wicked woman; for I have been speaking out of the abundance of my
> complaint and my provocation."
> **17** Then Eli answered, "Go in peace; and may the God of Israel grant your petition that you have asked
> of him."
> **18** She said, "Let your servant find favor in your sight." So the woman went her way, and ate; and her
> facial expression wasn't sad any more.
> **19** They rose up in the morning early, and worshiped before Yahweh, and returned, and came to their
> house to Ramah. Then Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and Yahweh remembered her.
> **20** When the time had come, Hannah conceived, and bore a son; and she named him Samuel, saying,
> "Because I have asked him of Yahweh."
> **21** The man Elkanah, and all his house, went up to offer to Yahweh the yearly sacrifice, and his vow.
> **22** But Hannah didn't go up; for she said to her husband, "Not until the child is weaned; then I will
> bring him, that he may appear before Yahweh, and stay there forever."
> **23** Elkanah her husband said to her, "Do what seems good to you. Wait until you have weaned him; only
> may Yahweh establish his word." So the woman waited and nursed her son, until she weaned him.
> **24** When she had weaned him, she took him up with her, with three bulls, and one ephah of meal, and a
> bottle of wine, and brought him to Yahweh's house in Shiloh. The child was young.
> **25** They killed the bull, and brought the child to Eli.
> **26** She said, "Oh, my lord, as your soul lives, my lord, I am the woman who stood by you here, praying to
> Yahweh.
> **27** I prayed for this child; and Yahweh has given me my petition which I asked of him.
> **28** Therefore I have also given him to Yahweh. As long as he lives he is given to Yahweh." He worshiped
> Yahweh there.

[[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]], a man of [[Ramah]] (*Ramathaim Zophim*) in
[[The Hill Country of Ephraim]], has two wives. [[Peninnah]] has children; [[Hannah]] has none (1–2).
Every year he goes up to [[Shiloh]] to sacrifice, where [[Eli]]'s sons [[Hophni]] and
[[Phinehas (son of Eli)]] are priests (3). At the meal he gives Hannah a special portion, because he loves
her, but Yahweh has shut her womb, and her rival torments her for it until she weeps and will not eat
(4–7). Elkanah asks why she grieves: *Am I not better to you than ten sons?* (8). After the meal Hannah
prays at the temple, weeping, and vows: if Yahweh will look on her affliction and give her a son, she will
give him to Yahweh for life, and no razor will touch his head (9–11). Eli, watching her lips move without
sound, takes her for drunk. She answers that she has been pouring out her soul; he sends her away in peace
with a blessing, and she eats (12–18). At home in Ramah, *Yahweh remembered her*; she bears a son and
names him [[Samuel]], *Because I have asked him of Yahweh* (19–20). She stays home from the next pilgrimage
until the child is weaned, with Elkanah's consent (21–23), then brings him to Shiloh with an offering and
gives him to Eli: *As long as he lives he is given to Yahweh* (24–28).

**What is going on.** The book that will bring Israel its kings begins with a private grief, told at
length, in a family on pilgrimage. The structure is the barren-wife pattern of Genesis: a loved wife who
cannot conceive, a fertile rival, a husband who does not fix it, and a child who comes when God
*remembers*. Two things are new. Hannah asks, and the asking is the center of the story: no angel announces
this child (contrast Judges 13:3), and no husband prays for her (contrast Genesis 25:21). And the child is
given away. The son for whom she prayed is handed over at Shiloh before the chapter ends. The rest of 1
Samuel 1–3 sets this child beside Eli's sons, who serve at the same altar.

**What is notable.**

- **A certain man … of the hill country of Ephraim.** *Wayhî ʾîš ʾeḥāḏ min-hārāmāṯayim ṣôp̄îm mēhar
  ʾep̄rāyim* (1, WLC). The opening is the opening of the last two stories of Judges: *There was a man of the
  hill country of Ephraim* (Micah, Judges 17:1) and *a certain Levite living on the farther side of the
  hill country of Ephraim* (Judges 19:1). Those two stories ended in a stolen shrine and a civil war; this
  one ends in a child given to Yahweh. The same region, *the hill country of Ephraim*, frames all three.
  The comparison is a reading; the text does not draw it.
- **An Ephraimite, or a Levite.** The WEB's *an Ephraimite* renders *ʾep̄rāṯî* (1, WLC), the word that
  meant *Ephrathite* of Bethlehem in Ruth 1:2 (see [[Famine and Death in Moab]]). Here it most naturally
  marks the region. 1 Chronicles 6:33–38 traces Samuel through *Elkanah, the son of Jeroham, the son of
  Eliel, the son of Toah, the son of Zuph* to *Korah, the son of Izhar, the son of Kohath, the son of Levi*
  (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested), so Chronicles makes the family Levites. The two
  claims are flagged on [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]], not resolved there. 1 Samuel itself never calls
  Elkanah or Samuel a Levite.
- **Yahweh of Armies.** *YHWH ṣəḇāʾôṯ* (3, 11, WLC). In the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth the title never
  occurs; its first occurrence in the books stored so far is here, at 1:3, and Hannah is the first person
  to address God by it (11). The WEB renders it *Yahweh of Armies*. What *armies* means here, Israel's
  hosts or heavenly ones, the text does not say. See [[The Names of God]].
- **Her rival.** *Ṣārāṯāh* (6, WLC), *her rival*, from the root *ṣrr*, *to bind, to be hostile*. Leviticus
  18:18 forbids taking a woman's sister as a second wife *to be a rival*, *liṣrōr*, the verb of the same
  root. In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel the noun with this sense occurs only here. Peninnah's
  weapon is *kaʿas*, *provocation, vexation*: the noun and verb appear three times in verses 6–7, and
  Hannah uses the noun of herself in 16, *my complaint and my provocation*. See [[Envy]].
- **Yahweh had shut up her womb.** *Sāḡar … raḥmāh* (5, 6, WLC), said twice, once in the narrator's voice
  and once as the reason for the rival's taunting. The phrase occurs in the WLC files searched (Genesis
  through Ruth, and 1 Samuel) only in these two verses. Genesis gives the cause to God in the same way
  (Genesis 20:18; 29:31; 30:2), and Rachel's *Give me children, or else I will die* drew Jacob's angry *Am I
  in God's place?* (Genesis 30:1–2). Elkanah's question in 1:8 is gentler and answers nothing. See
  [[The Barren Matriarchs]] and [[Barrenness]].
- **A double portion.** The WEB's *double portion* (5) renders *mānâ ʾaḥaṯ ʾappāyim* (WLC), literally *one
  portion of faces* or *of nostrils*. The phrase is obscure, and translations divide between a larger
  portion and a single one. The sense rests on the following clause, *for he loved Hannah*, which the
  Hebrew states plainly. The range of renderings is recalled, not checked against a lexicon.
- **Am I not better to you than ten sons?** *Hălôʾ ʾānōḵî ṭôḇ lāḵ mēʿăśārâ bānîm* (8, WLC). The women of
  Bethlehem use the same construction of Ruth, *better to you than seven sons* (Ruth 4:15). There the
  comparison consoles; here it is a question Hannah does not answer. See
  [[Better Than Seven Sons - Ruth 4.15 and 1 Samuel 1.8]].
- **Eli on his seat.** *Wəʿēlî hakkōhēn yōšēḇ ʿal-hakkissēʾ ʿal-məzûzaṯ hêḵal YHWH* (9, WLC). *Kissēʾ* is
  the ordinary word for *throne*. Hannah's song uses it for *the throne of glory* (2:8), and Eli will fall
  from it backward and die when he hears the ark is taken (4:18). Those two verses are cited from the WLC
  and are not yet ingested. *Hêḵal*, *temple, palace*, occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel
  only here and at 3:3. A building with a doorpost at Shiloh, called *temple* and *Yahweh's house* (7, 24),
  is more than the tent of Joshua 18:1. The text does not explain the change. See
  [[The Dwelling of God]].
- **Bitterness of soul.** *Mārat nāp̄eš* (10, WLC). In the WLC files searched the phrase occurs three times:
  of the *angry fellows* of Dan who might kill Micah (Judges 18:25), of Hannah here, and of the
  *discontented* who gather to David in the cave of Adullam (1 Samuel 22:2). Naomi used the same root of
  herself, *Call me Mara* (Ruth 1:20).
- **The vow.** *Wattiddōr neḏer* (11, WLC), the phrase of Jacob at Bethel (Genesis 28:20) and Jephthah
  (Judges 11:30). Hannah asks God to *look* at her *affliction*, *rāʾōh tirʾeh bəʿŏnî* (WLC). In the WLC files
  searched, God *seeing affliction* (*rāʾâ* with *ʿŏnî*) recurs at Leah's naming of Reuben (Genesis 29:32),
  Jacob's words to Laban (31:42), the bush (Exodus 3:7), the people's belief (4:31), and the creed of
  Deuteronomy 26:7. See
  [[Yahweh Has Looked on My Affliction - Leah, Israel in Egypt, and Hannah]]. She asks him to *remember*
  and *not forget*: *zəḵartanî wəlōʾ-tiškaḥ* (WLC). In the files searched, *zāḵar* and *šāḵaḥ* stand
  together in one verse only at Genesis 40:23, the cupbearer who *didn't remember Joseph, but forgot him*,
  at Deuteronomy 9:7, and here. See
  [[Remember Me - Genesis 40.14 and the Prayers of the Condemned]].
- **A boy, a seed of men.** The WEB's *a boy* (11) renders *zeraʿ ʾănāšîm* (WLC), *offspring of men*. The
  phrase is the vow's whole request, and it is plainer than a child: an heir, a man.
- **No razor.** *Ûmôrâ lōʾ-yaʿăleh ʿal-rōʾšô* (11, WLC). *Môrâ*, *razor*, occurs in the WLC files searched
  only here and of Samson (Judges 13:5; 16:17). Numbers 6:5 uses another word, *taʿar*. The Masoretic
  text of 1:11 does not call Samuel a *nāzîr*, a Nazirite, and does not mention wine. See
  [[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]].
- **Prayer without voice.** *Wəḥannâ hîʾ məḏabberet ʿal-libbāh* (13, WLC): *she spoke upon her heart*, her
  lips moving and her voice unheard. Abraham's servant had prayed *speaking in my heart* at the well
  (Genesis 24:45), but no one saw him. Here the priest at the sanctuary sees, and reads a woman praying
  silently as a drunk woman at a feast. His mistake is
  plausible: the family has *finished eating and drinking* (9). It is still a priest failing to recognize
  prayer, and the next chapter shows him failing to restrain his sons. See [[Prayer]].
- **I poured out my soul.** *Wāʾešpōḵ ʾeṯ-nap̄šî lip̄nê YHWH* (15, WLC). *Šāp̄aḵ* is the verb of poured
  blood and poured drink. Hannah denies *wine or strong drink* and says what she poured instead. In the WLC
  files searched, *pour* with *soul* as object occurs only here. She calls herself *qəšaṯ-rûaḥ*, *hard of
  spirit* (WEB *sorrowful spirit*); that pairing too is unique in the files searched.
- **Not a daughter of Belial.** *ʾAl-tittēn ʾeṯ-ʾămāṯəḵā lip̄nê baṯ-bəlîyāʿal* (16, WLC); the WEB has
  *Don't consider your servant a wicked woman*. *Bəlîyaʿal* is the word of the *wicked fellows* of Gibeah
  (Judges 19:22; 20:13) and of those who entice to other gods (Deuteronomy 13:13 WEB; 13:14 WLC). Two
  verses into the next chapter, *the sons of Eli were wicked men*, *bənê ḇəlîyāʿal* (2:12, WLC). The woman
  the priest suspects is not one; his own sons are.
- **Asked.** The chapter's key verb is *šāʾal*, *ask*. Eli prays that God grant *your petition that you have
  asked of him*, *šēlāṯēḵ ʾăšer šāʾalt* (17). Hannah names the boy *because I have asked him of Yahweh*,
  *šəʾiltîw* (20). She says *Yahweh has given me my petition which I asked of him* (27), and then *I have
  also given him to Yahweh*, *hišʾiltihû*, the causative, *I have caused him to be asked*, that is, *lent*
  or *granted* him; *he is given*, *hûʾ šāʾûl*, *to Yahweh* (28, all WLC). The name *Šəmûʾēl* does not
  derive from *šāʾal* by any obvious etymology; the explanation in 1:20 is a sound-play. The participle
  *šāʾûl*, written שאול, has exactly the consonants of the name of Saul, *šāʾûl* (9:2, WLC). The book
  will soon be about the king Israel *asks for* (8:10; 12:13, not yet ingested). See
  [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]] and [[Names and Naming]].
- **Yahweh remembered her.** *Wayyizkərehā YHWH* (19, WLC), the verb of *God remembered Noah* and *God
  remembered Rachel* (Genesis 8:1; 30:22). Elkanah *knew* her, as Adam *knew* Eve (Genesis 4:1), and the
  conception is credited to both, in that order. See [[God Remembered - Noah, Abraham, Rachel, and Israel]]
  and [[Remembrance]].
- **Elkanah's vow.** Elkanah goes up to offer *the yearly sacrifice, and his vow*, *wəʾeṯ-niḏrô* (21, WLC).
  The text has not said that he vowed anything. Either he made the vow his own, or he had made one of his
  own that the narrator did not record. His words in 23, *only may Yahweh establish his word*, take
  responsibility for the vow, in the terms Numbers 30 gives a husband. See
  [[May Yahweh Establish His Word - Numbers 30 and 1 Samuel 1]].
- **Appear before Yahweh, forever.** Hannah keeps the child home *until the child is weaned*, then he will
  *appear before Yahweh, and stay there forever* (22). *Wənirʾâ ʾeṯ-pənê YHWH* (WLC) is the phrasing of
  the pilgrimage law, *appear before Yahweh your God … They shall not appear before Yahweh empty*
  (Deuteronomy 16:16, WLC *yērāʾeh ʾeṯ-pənê YHWH*). What the law asks of every male three times a year,
  Hannah gives *forever*, *ʿaḏ-ʿôlām*. She does not come *empty* either: she brings bulls, flour, and wine
  (24). The weaning, *gāmal*, recalls Isaac's (Genesis 21:8), the only other weaning in the WLC files
  searched; Abraham's feast marked that one, and this one is marked by the gift of the child.
- **Three bulls, and the bull.** Verse 24 says *three bulls*; verse 25 says *They killed the bull*. The
  Masoretic text has the plural in 24 (*bəp̄ārîm šəlōšâ*, WLC) and the singular, with the article, in 25.
  The Greek and a Qumran manuscript are reported to read *a three-year-old bull* in 24; this is recalled
  and was not checked here. The wiki reports the variant without choosing.
- **The child was a child.** *Wəhannaʿar nāʿar* (24, WLC), which the WEB renders *The child was young*. The
  Hebrew repeats the noun. The narrator does not give his age.
- **I am the woman who stood by you here.** Hannah speaks to Eli first (26), reminding him of the scene he
  misread. She does not mention his mistake. She gives the boy back to the man who blessed her asking.
- **He worshiped Yahweh there.** The chapter's last clause has a masculine singular subject (*wayyištaḥû*,
  28, WLC). Who bows, Elkanah, Eli, or the boy, is not said. The WEB keeps the ambiguity with *He*.

**How it connects.** The pericope stands where several threads in the wiki meet. Behind it are the barren
wives of Genesis and Samson's mother (Judges 13); the pilgrimage *from year to year* to Shiloh of Judges
21:19 (see [[From Year to Year - Jephthah's Daughter, the Feast at Shiloh, and Elkanah]]); the laws of a
wife's vow (Numbers 30) and of vowed persons (Leviticus 27; see
[[A Person Vowed to Yahweh - Leviticus 27.1-8, Judges 11, and 1 Samuel 1]]); and Jephthah's vow, where a
father gave a child to Yahweh and the child died. Hannah's child is given and lives. Ahead of it are
Hannah's song (2:1–10), Eli's sons (2:12–36), and Yahweh's call to the boy (3). Luke's infancy narrative
also has a presentation of a firstborn son at the sanctuary, *to present him to the Lord* (Luke 2:22, WEB
checked at bible-api.com); whether Luke models it on this chapter is a common reading, reported here and
not argued.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope is the whole of chapter 1. Hannah's song (2:1–10) is a separate unit in
the book's plan, though it continues the scene at Shiloh. Elkanah's ancestors Jeroham, Elihu, Tohu, and
Zuph get no pages yet; they are named once here and, with different spellings, in 1 Chronicles 6:34–35.
Samuel's Ramah is filed on the existing [[Ramah]] page, with its identification flagged there; *Ramathaim
Zophim* is recorded as an alternative name, not as a separate place.

**Ephraimite or Levite.** 1 Samuel 1:1 calls Elkanah *ʾep̄rāṯî*; 1 Chronicles 6:33–38 lists him among the
Kohathites. The wiki keeps both claims, with their sources, on [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]].
