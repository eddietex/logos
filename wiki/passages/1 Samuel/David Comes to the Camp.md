# David Comes to the Camp

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 17:12–40

## Summary

> **12** Now David was the son of that Ephrathite of Bethlehem Judah, whose name was Jesse; and he had eight sons.
> The man was an elderly old man in the days of Saul.
> **13** The three oldest sons of Jesse had gone after Saul to the battle: and the names of his three sons who went
> to the battle were Eliab the firstborn, and next to him Abinadab, and the third Shammah.
> **14** David was the youngest; and the three oldest followed Saul.
> **15** Now David went back and forth from Saul to feed his father’s sheep at Bethlehem.
> **16** The Philistine came near morning and evening, and presented himself forty days.
> **17** Jesse said to David his son, “Now take for your brothers an ephah of this parched grain, and these ten

> **18** and bring these ten cheeses to the captain of their thousand, and see how your brothers are doing, and

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 17.)

The narrator steps away from the valley and introduces [[David]] as if for the first time: *the son of that Ephrathite
of Bethlehem Judah, whose name was Jesse; and he had eight sons* (12). [[Jesse]] is old. His three eldest,
[[Eliab (son of Jesse)]], [[Abinadab (son of Jesse)]], and [[Shammah (son of Jesse)]], have followed [[Saul]] to the
war; David, *the youngest*, goes *back and forth from Saul to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem* (13–15). Meanwhile
[[Goliath]] comes out *morning and evening*, forty days (16).

Jesse sends David with parched grain and ten loaves for his brothers, ten cheeses for *the captain of their
thousand*, and a charge to *see how your brothers are doing* (17–18). David rises early, leaves the sheep with a
keeper, and reaches the camp as the army goes out shouting to the battle line (20–21). He leaves his baggage, runs to
the line, and greets his brothers. While he is talking with them, the champion comes up and says *the same words;
and David heard them* (22–23). Israel flees from the man (24). The soldiers tell each other what the king has
promised the man who kills him: great riches, his daughter, and freedom for his father's house (25).

David asks, and his first words in the book are a question: *What shall be done to the man who kills this
Philistine, and takes away the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy
the armies of the living God?* (26). Eliab hears and is angry: *With whom have you left those few sheep in the
wilderness? I know your pride, and the naughtiness of your heart* (28). David answers *What have I now done?* and
asks someone else (29–30). His words reach Saul, who sends for him (31).

Before the king, David volunteers: *Let no man's heart fail because of him. Your servant will go and fight with this
Philistine* (32). Saul refuses: *you are but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth* (33). David answers with the
sheep. A lion or a bear took a lamb; he went after it, struck it, rescued the lamb from its mouth, and when it rose
against him caught it *by his beard* and killed it (34–35). The Philistine *shall be as one of them, since he has
defied the armies of the living God* (36). *Yahweh who delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of
the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine* (37). Saul says *Go! Yahweh will be with you* (37).
He dresses David in his own clothing, a bronze helmet, and a coat of mail; David straps on the sword, tries to walk,
and takes it all off, *for I have not tested them* (38–39). He takes his staff, chooses *five smooth stones out of the
brook*, puts them in his shepherd's bag, and with his sling in his hand *came near to the Philistine* (40).

**Where it happens.** From [[Bethlehem]] to the [[Valley of Elah]] (15, 19). The camp has *the place of the wagons*,
*hammaʿgālâ* (20, WLC), a word the Hebrew files checked use only here and of Saul's camp in 26:5, 7 (lemma 4570). Only
Eliab puts the flock *in the wilderness* (28).

**What is going on.** Two stories meet in this chapter, and the seam shows. 16:14–23 has already brought David to
court as Saul's harpist and armor bearer, and Saul asked Jesse to let him *stand before me* (16:22). Here David is
introduced again, as the youngest of eight, still with the sheep (12–14), and Saul treats him as a stranger with no
war in him (33). Verse 15, *David went back and forth from Saul*, reads as the narrator's own bridge between the two.
The sharper conflict is at 17:55–58, where Saul asks whose son he is (stored WEB; [[Jonathan Loves David]]); the contradiction
is flagged on [[David Plays the Harp for Saul]], on [[David]], and on
[[Whose Son Is This Youth - 1 Samuel 16.18-22 and 17.55-58]], and is not reconciled here. Septuagint Codex
Vaticanus is reported to lack 17:12–31 and 17:55–18:5 (recalled, not checked; no Septuagint text was consulted).

Inside the story, the pericope turns on who names God. The previous eleven verses did not (17:1–11, WLC). The soldiers
here talk about Goliath and the reward (25, 27), Eliab about sheep and pride (28), Saul about age and experience (33).
David is the first to name God, twice as *ʾĕlōhîm ḥayyîm*, *the living God* (26, 36, WLC), and then Yahweh, who
*delivered me* (37). Only after that does Saul name Yahweh: *Go! Yahweh will be with you* (37). It echoes what
one of Saul's young men said of David at 16:18, *and Yahweh is with him*.

**What is notable.**

- **Sent to see his brothers.** Jesse's errand has the shape of Jacob's at Genesis 37:13–14. A father sends his younger
  son from home to the brothers who are away with the flock or the army, to see their *šālôm* and bring back word
  (Genesis 37:14, *see whether it is well with your brothers … and bring me word again*; 1 Samuel 17:18, *see how your
  brothers are doing*; WLC *šəlôm* / *ləšālôm*). The eldest brother answers the younger with hostility in both: at
  Dothan the brothers plot (Genesis 37:18–20), here Eliab accuses (28). The plotters at Dothan planned to say *An evil
  animal has devoured him* (Genesis 37:20); David tells Saul he has rescued a lamb from the lion and the bear. The text
  makes no comparison; see [[Sent to See His Brothers - Joseph at Shechem and David at Elah]].
- **Their pledge.** WEB *bring back news* (18) renders *wəʾeṯ-ʿărubbāṯām tiqqāḥ*, literally *and their ʿărubbâ you
  shall take* (WLC). Morphhb gives the noun a lemma (6161) found only here in the files checked. It is usually glossed
  *pledge* or *token*, some sign that the brothers are well (recalled, not checked). The wiki gives the Hebrew and does
  not settle the meaning.
- **Left, three times.** *Nāṭaš*, *leave, forsake* (lemma 5203). David *left the sheep with a keeper* (20) and *left
  his baggage in the hand of the keeper of the baggage* (22). Eliab asks *with whom have you left those few sheep?*
  (28). Eliab's question has an answer the reader has already been told, twice (WLC).
- **The battle line.** *Maʿărāḵâ*, the word Goliath used for *the armies of Israel* (17:8, 10), comes six more times
  here (20, 21 twice, 22, 26, 36), and a seventh in the qere of 23, where the ketiv reads *mmʿrwt* (WLC).
  David changes its owner: Goliath said *the armies of Israel*; David says *the armies of the living God*,
  *maʿarḵôṯ ʾĕlōhîm ḥayyîm* (26, 36). See [[To Defy the Living God - 1 Samuel 17 and 2 Kings 19]].
- **The living God.** In the Hebrew files checked, *ʾĕlōhîm ḥayyîm* as a title occurs at Deuteronomy 5:26, *the voice
  of the living God speaking out of the middle of the fire*, and at 1 Samuel 17:26, 36; Joshua 3:10 has *ʾēl ḥay*, *the
  living God is among you* (stored WEB; WLC). In 2 Kings 19:4, 16, Hezekiah says that Sennacherib and his Rabshakeh
  were sent *to defy the living God* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored; the Hebrew of 2 Kings was not checked).
- **Defy, and reproach.** *Ḥārap̄* is now used three times by Israel and David (25, 26, 36), after Goliath's own
  *ḥēraptî* (10). David puts beside it the noun *ḥerpâ*, *reproach* (26). See
  [[The Reproach of Israel - Joshua 5.9, 1 Samuel 11.2, and 17.26]].
- **Uncircumcised.** *Hehārēl* (26, 36, WLC; lemma 6189). In 1 Samuel the word has been Jonathan's for the Philistine
  garrison, *these uncircumcised* (14:6), and in Judges Samson's parents' and then Samson's (Judges 14:3; 15:18) (stored
  WEB). David's word puts the Philistine outside the covenant sign, which is the ground of the comparison to the lion
  and bear. See [[Circumcision]] and [[Uncircumcised Lips, Ears, and Heart]].
- **The king's reward.** Riches (*ʿōšer*), the king's daughter, and *make his father's house free* (25). The adjective
  *ḥop̄šî* (lemma 2670) occurs in the files checked seven other times, all in the laws of the Hebrew servant who *goes
  out free* (Exodus 21:2, 5, 26–27; Deuteronomy 15:12–13, 18) (stored WEB; WLC). What freedom from the crown meant is
  not said; release from taxes or service is the usual suggestion (recalled, not checked). Saul offers Merab at 18:17
  and gives Michal at 18:27; neither verse refers back to 17:25 ([[David Marries Michal]]). See [[Merab]] and
  [[The Hebrew Servant Goes Free - Exodus 21, Deuteronomy 15, and Jeremiah 34]].
- **Pride, and the heart.** Eliab says *I know your pride, and the naughtiness of your heart*, *zəḏōnəḵā wəʾēṯ rōaʿ
  ləḇāḇeḵā* (28, WLC). *Zāḏôn* (lemma 2087) occurs in the files checked only here and in Deuteronomy's laws of the man
  who *does presumptuously* (17:12) and the prophet who speaks *presumptuously* (18:22) (stored WEB). *Ləḇāḇ* is the
  word of 16:7, *Yahweh looks at the heart* (WLC). Eliab, whose stature Yahweh told Samuel not to look at, now claims to
  see David's heart. The narrator does not comment. See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].
- **Is there not a cause?** *Hălôʾ dāḇār hûʾ* (29, WLC), literally *Was it not a word?* The WEB's *cause* is one
  reading; *it was only a word* is another (recalled, not checked). Either way, *dāḇār* runs through the scene:
  Goliath speaks *these words* (23), the people answer *according to this word* (27, 30), and David's *words* are
  reported to Saul (31).
- **A man of war.** Saul says *you are but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth*, *ʾîš milḥāmâ* (33, WLC). At
  16:18 Saul's servant had called David *a man of war*, the same phrase (WLC). In the files checked the phrase first
  names Yahweh: *Yahweh is a man of war* (Exodus 15:3, stored WEB). Hushai will use it of David at 2 Samuel 17:8, with a
  bear: *like a bear robbed of her cubs … Your father is a man of war* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored).
- **The lion and the bear.** *Hāʾărî* and *haddōḇ* (34–37, WLC). *Dōḇ* (lemma 1677) occurs in the files checked only in
  17:34–37 and Hushai's simile at 2 Samuel 17:8. *ʾĂrî* (lemma 738) is Judah's image in Genesis 49:9 and Samson's
  lion in Judges 14:5–9, which Samson tore *with nothing in his hand* when *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him*
  (Judges 14:6, stored WEB). David's account names no Spirit; it names Yahweh, *who delivered me*, *hiṣṣilanî*, and the
  same verb, *nāṣal*, is his own act, *rescued it out of his mouth* (35, 37, WLC). See
  [[The Shepherd's Liability - Genesis 31.39 and the Law of Exodus 22]], and Amos 3:12, *As the shepherd rescues out of
  the mouth of the lion two legs* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored).
- **By his beard.** *Bizqānô* (35, WLC; lemma 2206). A lion's *beard* is odd; the word may mean the jaw or mane here
  (recalled, not checked). The same word comes back when David lets his saliva run *down on his beard* at Gath (21:13
  WEB; 21:14 WLC) (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Tested.** *Kî lōʾ nissâ … kî lōʾ nissîṯî* (39, WLC). *Nissâ* (lemma 5254) occurs in the files checked nineteen
  times. Most often its subject is God testing people (Genesis 22:1; Exodus 16:4; 20:20; Deuteronomy 8:2), people
  testing God (Exodus 17:2, 7; Numbers 14:22; Deuteronomy 6:16), or Gideon testing with the fleece (Judges 6:39); twice
  it means *venture* or *try* (Deuteronomy 4:34; 28:56) (stored WEB). Here it is a young man who has not tried his
  armor. See [[Testing]].
- **Saul's armor.** *Qôḇaʿ nəḥōšeṯ*, a bronze helmet, and a *širyôn*, coat of mail (38, WLC). Goliath's helmet and mail
  were the same two things, bronze helmet and coat of mail (17:5); his helmet is spelled *kôḇaʿ* there and *qôḇaʿ* here,
  and morphhb gives them two lemmas (3553, 6959). Saul dresses David as the Philistine is dressed. His *mad*, *clothing*
  (38–39, lemma 4055), is the word for the garment Jonathan gives David at 18:4 (WLC; stored WEB; [[Jonathan Loves David]]).
- **The staff and the stones.** *Maqlô*, his staff (40; lemma 4731), the word for Jacob's rods in Genesis 30:37–41 and
  for the staff with which Jacob crossed the Jordan (Genesis 32:10 WEB; 32:11 WLC), and the one Goliath will mock,
  *sticks* (17:43) (stored WEB). *Ḥallūqê ʾăḇānîm*, smooth stones (lemma 2512), and *yalqûṭ*, pouch (lemma 3219), each
  occur only here in the files checked. *Wayyiḇḥar*, *he chose* (40), is the verb of 16:8–10, *Yahweh has not chosen
  these* (WLC); Goliath's *choose a man* (17:8) used a different and rare verb (see [[Goliath Defies Israel]]).
- **The sling.** *Qalʿô* (40, WLC). The noun and its verb in the files checked: the seven hundred left-handed
  Benjamites who *could sling a stone at a hair* (Judges 20:16), David's sling at 17:40, 49–50, and Abigail's blessing
  that God will *sling out* the lives of David's enemies *as from the hollow of a sling* (25:29) (stored WEB; WLC; the
  noun's lemma, 7050, is shared with the tabernacle's *hangings*, which morphhb separates only by a sub-letter).

**How it connects.** This is the first scene in which David acts and speaks. The faith he speaks in verse 37 is the
answer to the fear of 17:11, and it is a shepherd's faith: what he knows of Yahweh he knows from the flock. The book
has already called him the youngest, *keeping the sheep* (16:11), and *with the sheep* (16:19); the next pericope is the
fight (17:41–54). The reward of verse 25 opens the story of Saul's daughters (18:17–27), and Eliab's anger opens a line
of brothers who mistake the youngest, which Genesis began with Joseph. See [[Faith]], [[Brotherhood]], and [[Envy]].

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 17:12–40 as planned: it opens with David's reintroduction and closes as he
*came near to the Philistine* (40); 17:41 begins the fight with the Philistine coming near in turn. No new person or
place page. The keeper of the sheep (20), the keeper of the baggage (22), and *the captain of their thousand* (18) are
unnamed and get no page. [[Merab]] is linked for 17:25 though not named, because she is the first daughter Saul
offers David (18:17, stored WEB); 18:17 does not cite the promise, so the link is the wiki's. Two new connections: [[Sent to See His Brothers - Joseph at Shechem and David at Elah]], because
the two errands share their shape and their word, *šālôm* (Genesis 37:14; 1 Samuel 17:18, 22), and
[[To Defy the Living God - 1 Samuel 17 and 2 Kings 19]], because the WEB's *defy the living God* recurs there and
nowhere else the wiki has checked. The seam between 16:14–23 and 17:12–31 is described, not resolved; the main
contradiction note stays on [[David Plays the Harp for Saul]].

**Unresolved.** Whether 17:12–31 comes from a second account of David's arrival (and the reported Septuagint shorter
text). What *ʿărubbâ* (18) meant. What *ḥop̄šî* meant for the father's house (25). Whether *hălôʾ dāḇār hûʾ* (29) is
*cause* or *word*. Whether *zāqān* (35) is a lion's jaw or mane.
