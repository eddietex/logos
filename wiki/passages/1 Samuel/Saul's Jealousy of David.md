# Saul's Jealousy of David

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 18:6–16

## Summary

> **18:6** As they came, when David returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, the women came out of all the cities
> of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet king Saul, with tambourines, with joy, and with instruments of music.
> **7** The women sang to one another as they played, and said, “Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten
> thousands.”
> **8** Saul was very angry, and this saying displeased him. He said, “They have ascribed to David ten thousands, and to
> me they have ascribed only thousands. What can he have more but the kingdom?”
> **9** Saul watched David from that day and forward.
> **10** On the next day, an evil spirit from God came mightily on Saul, and he prophesied in the middle of the house.
> David played with his hand, as he did day by day. Saul had his spear in his hand;
> **11** and Saul threw the spear, for he said, “I will pin David even to the wall!” David escaped from his presence
> twice.
> **12** Saul was afraid of David, because Yahweh was with him, and had departed from Saul.
> **13** Therefore Saul removed him from his presence, and made him his captain over a thousand; and he went out and came
> in before the people.
> **14** David behaved himself wisely in all his ways; and Yahweh was with him.
> **15** When Saul saw that he behaved himself very wisely, he stood in awe of him.
> **16** But all Israel and Judah loved David; for he went out and came in before them.

(The full text; from `raw/bible/1 Samuel.md`, chapter 18.)

The army comes home from the killing of the Philistine, and the women of *all the cities of Israel* come out to meet
*king Saul* with singing, dancing, tambourines, joy, and instruments (18:6). They answer one another as they play:
*Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands* (7). [[Saul]] is *very angry*; the saying is evil in his
eyes; and he works out where it ends: *What can he have more but the kingdom?* (8). From that day he *watched David*
(9).

The next day *an evil spirit from God came mightily on Saul*, and he *prophesied in the middle of the house*. [[David]]
plays, *as he did day by day*, and Saul has his spear in his hand (10). He throws it, saying *I will pin David even to
the wall*, and David gets away from him twice (11). Then the narrator gives the reason for everything that follows:
*Saul was afraid of David, because Yahweh was with him, and had departed from Saul* (12). Saul sends him from his
presence and makes him a *captain over a thousand*, and David *went out and came in before the people* (13). He
*behaved himself wisely in all his ways; and Yahweh was with him* (14). Saul sees it and *stood in awe of him* (15).
*But all Israel and Judah loved David; for he went out and came in before them* (16).

**Where it happens.** On the road home from the [[Valley of Elah]], through *all the cities of Israel* (6), and then
in *the house*, Saul's, where David plays his harp (10). The text names no town. Saul's home is [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] (10:26;
15:34, stored WEB), but 18:10 says only *the house*, and the wiki does not place the spear there.

**What is going on.** Four verses turn a victory into a grudge. The song gives Saul thousands and David ten thousands,
and Saul hears the second number. He has just made David his commander (18:5); now he does the arithmetic of a king
and finds the throne at the end of it, *What can he have more but the kingdom?* It is the reader, not Saul, who has
been told at 15:28 and 16:1–13 that the kingdom has already been given to *a neighbor of yours who is better than you*
(15:28, stored WEB). Saul guesses, from a song, what Samuel said to his face.

Then the pericope sets the two men's fortunes side by side twice over, and the hinge is verse 12. Yahweh *was with*
David and *had departed from* Saul. In 16:13–14 the Spirit came on David *from that day forward*, and the Spirit of
Yahweh *departed from Saul*; here the same exchange is said with Yahweh as subject. Saul's response is to *remove*
David *from his presence*, and the Hebrew uses one verb for both movements: Yahweh *sār*, *departed*, from Saul, and
Saul *wayəsirēhû*, *removed him*, from himself (12–13, *sûr*, lemma 5493, WLC). Yahweh turns from Saul; Saul turns
David away. Sending him to command a thousand puts him in front of the people, where they come to love him (13, 16).

Saul's fear grows in three steps. He is *angry* (8); he is *afraid of David* (12, *yārēʾ*); he *stood in awe of him*
(15, *gûr*). Love for David grows alongside it, and in the same chapter: Jonathan's (18:1, 3), now *all Israel and
Judah* (16), and next Michal's and the servants' (18:20, 22, [[David Marries Michal]]). The narrator never says
that Saul stopped loving him (16:21); he says only what Saul feared and what he did.

**What is notable.**

- **The women came out.** *Wattēṣeʾnâ hannāšîm … lāšîr wəhamməḥōlôṯ … bəṯuppîm bəśimḥâ ûḇəšālišîm* (6, WLC; the
  consonantal text has *lšwr*, the reading tradition *lāšîr*, *to sing*). *Məḥōlâ*, *dance* (lemma 4246), occurs in
  the files checked at Exodus 15:20; 32:19; Judges 11:34; 21:21; and 1 Samuel 18:6; 21:11 WEB (21:12 WLC); 29:5: Miriam
  at the sea, the calf, Jephthah's daughter, the daughters of Shiloh, and three times this song. *Šālišîm*, *instruments
  of music* (morphhb lemma 7991 b), occurs in those files only here. See
  [[With Tambourines and with Dances - Miriam, Jephthah's Daughter, and the Daughters of Shiloh]].
- **To meet king Saul.** *Liqraʾṯ šāʾûl hammeleḵ* (6, WLC). The women go out to meet the king, as Jephthah's daughter
  went out *to meet him* (Judges 11:34, stored WEB). The song they sing names David, too.
- **They sang to one another.** *Wattaʿănênâ hannāšîm hamməśaḥăqôṯ* (7, WLC): *answered*, *ʿānâ* in the sense morphhb
  tags as a separate root (lemma 6030 c), *sing*. In the files checked that root occurs at Exodus 15:21, *Miriam
  answered them, "Sing to Yahweh"*; Exodus 32:18, *the noise of those who sing*, at the calf; Numbers 21:17, the song of
  the well, *Spring up, well! Sing to it*; and at 1 Samuel 18:7; 21:11 WEB (21:12 WLC); 29:5 (stored WEB). It is Miriam's
  verb for the women's song. *Məśaḥăqôṯ*, *as they played* (*śāḥaq*, lemma 7832), is the verb of Samson made to
  *entertain* the Philistines (Judges 16:25, stored WEB) and, in 2 Samuel 6:5, 21, of David celebrating before the ark
  (WLC; paraphrased; no 2 Samuel WEB checked this run).
- **Thousands and ten thousands.** *Hikkâ šāʾûl baʾălāp̄āyw wəḏāwiḏ bəriḇəḇōṯāyw* (7, WLC; the consonantal text spells
  *bʾlpw* without the *yod*). The pair *ʾelep̄ / rəḇāḇâ* in the files checked occurs at Genesis 24:60, Rebekah's
  blessing, *the mother of thousands of ten thousands*; Numbers 10:36; Deuteronomy 32:30, *How could one chase a
  thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight*; Deuteronomy 33:17, *the ten thousands of Ephraim … the thousands of
  Manasseh*; Judges 20:10; and 1 Samuel 18:7, 8; 21:11 WEB (21:12 WLC); 29:5 (stored WEB). Read as poetry, the second
  number may simply answer the first, as in Deuteronomy 33:17, where the larger number goes to the younger, Ephraim;
  Saul does not read it so. Whether the women meant a slight is not said. The couplet is quoted twice more, both times
  by Philistines (21:11; 29:5, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[Saul Has Slain His Thousands - 1 Samuel 18.7, 21.11, and 29.5]].
- **Slain, slaughter, strike.** *Mēhakkôṯ*, *from the slaughter* (6); *hikkâ*, *has slain* (7); and Saul's *ʾakkeh*,
  *I will pin* (11, WLC): all *nāḵâ* (lemma 5221) in the *hiphil*. The WEB's *pin* is literally *I will strike David
  and the wall*, *ʾakkeh ḇəḏāwiḏ ûḇaqqîr* (WLC). The song said what David had struck; Saul answers by trying to strike
  David.
- **Displeased him.** *Wayyēraʿ bəʿênāyw* (8, WLC), literally *it was evil in his eyes*. The verse before, the
  approval of 18:5 was *good in the sight of all the people*, *wayyîṭaḇ bəʿênê* (WLC). Good in their eyes, evil in his.
  See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].
- **What more but the kingdom.** *Wəʿôḏ lô ʾaḵ hamməlûḵâ* (8, WLC). *Məlûḵâ* (lemma 4410) in 1 Samuel before this is
  *the matter of the kingdom* Saul did not tell his uncle (10:16), *the regulations of the kingdom* (10:25), *renew the
  kingdom* at Gilgal (11:14), and *when Saul had taken the kingdom* (14:47) (stored WEB; WLC). Every one of them was Saul's
  kingdom; here for the first time he imagines it as David's.
- **Watched.** *Wayəhî šāʾûl ʿôyēn ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ* (9, WLC, the reading tradition, *qere*). The participle *ʿôyēn*, morphhb
  lemma 5770, occurs in the files checked only here; it is commonly glossed *eyed*, *looked askance at* (recalled,
  not checked against a lexicon). The written consonants (*ketiv*) are *ʿwn*, which morphhb tags as *ʿāwōn*,
  *iniquity* (lemma 5771); the wiki records the tagging and draws nothing from it.
- **Came mightily.** *Wattiṣlaḥ rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm rāʿâ ʾel-šāʾûl* (10, WLC). *Ṣālaḥ* in this sense (morphhb lemma 6743 a)
  is the verb of the Spirit rushing on Samson (Judges 14:6, 19; 15:14), on Saul at the hill of God and at Jabesh
  (10:6, 10; 11:6), and on David at his anointing (16:13) (stored WEB; WLC). In the files checked this is the only
  time its subject is an *evil* spirit. The rush that made Saul a prophet and a deliverer now comes on him from the
  other side. See [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]] and
  [[An Evil Spirit from God - Abimelech, Saul, and Ahab's Prophets]].
- **He prophesied.** *Wayyiṯnabbēʾ bəṯôḵ-habbayiṯ* (10, WLC), the reflexive *hitpael* of *nāḇāʾ*, the stem of Saul's
  prophesying among the band of prophets (10:5–13) and at Naioth (19:20–24), and of the elders in Numbers 11:25–27.
  The WEB says *prophesied*; what Saul did is not described. Many readers take it as raving rather than prophecy
  (recalled, not checked). See [[Is Saul Also Among the Prophets - 1 Samuel 10.11-12 and 19.24]].
- **As he did day by day.** *Mənaggēn bəyāḏô kəyôm bəyôm* (10, WLC). It is the music of 16:16, 23, which made the evil
  spirit depart; this time it does not. See [[The Harp of David - Jubal, the Prophets, and the Sweet Psalmist]].
- **Threw the spear.** *Wayyāṭel* (11, WLC; *ṭûl*, lemma 2904, in the *hiphil*). In the files checked this verb occurs
  only here and at 20:33, where Saul *cast his spear* at Jonathan (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The spear,
  *ḥănîṯ*, was one of the two in Israel's army at 13:22, Saul's and Jonathan's, and Goliath's shaft was *like a weaver's
  beam* (17:7) (stored WEB). David's *Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear* (17:47) is a verse back. See
  [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].
- **Twice.** *Wayyissōḇ dāwiḏ mippānāyw paʿămāyim* (11, WLC). Two throws or two escapes, the text does not say
  which. The scene comes again at 19:9–10 almost word for word, *An evil spirit from Yahweh was on Saul, as he sat in
  his house with his spear in his hand; and David was playing with his hand*, and the third throw uses Saul's own
  *David and the wall* (19:10, WLC). See [[Michal Helps David Escape]].
- **Afraid of David.** *Wayyirāʾ šāʾûl millip̄nê dāwiḏ* (12, WLC). At Elah, Saul and all Israel were *dismayed, and
  greatly afraid* of Goliath (17:11, stored WEB); now he fears the man who killed him.
- **Yahweh was with him.** *Kî-hāyâ YHWH ʿimmô ûmēʿim šāʾûl sār* (12, WLC). Said of Samuel at 3:19, by Saul's servant
  of David at 16:18, *Yahweh is with him*, and by Saul himself at 17:37, *Yahweh will be with you* (stored WEB). Here the
  narrator says it, and again at 14. *Sār*, *had departed*, is the verb of 16:14, *Yahweh's Spirit departed from Saul*,
  and of Saul at Endor, *God has departed from me* (28:15, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].
- **Captain over a thousand.** *Śar ʾālep̄* (13, WLC). Jesse sent cheeses to *the captain of their thousand* (17:18,
  *śar-hāʾālep̄*), and Samuel's warning was that the king *will appoint them to him for captains of thousands* (8:12)
  (stored WEB; WLC). David is given one of the thousands the women had just credited to Saul.
- **Went out and came in.** *Wayyēṣēʾ wayyāḇōʾ lip̄nê hāʿām* (13), and again *kî-hûʾ yôṣēʾ wāḇāʾ lip̄nêhem* (16, WLC).
  It is the phrase of Moses' prayer for a successor, *who may go out before them, and who may come in before them*,
  *that the congregation of Yahweh may not be as sheep which have no shepherd* (Numbers 27:17); of Moses' *I can no more
  go out and come in* (Deuteronomy 31:2); and of Caleb's strength *for war, to go out and to come in* (Joshua 14:11)
  (stored WEB). Saul sends David away, and the sending gives him the shepherd's place before the people. See
  [[Sheep Without a Shepherd - Numbers 27.17, 1 Kings 22.17, and the Gospels]].
- **Very wisely.** *Maśkîl* (14, 15, WLC; *śāḵal*, lemma 7919), the verb of 18:5 and 18:30, four times in this chapter
  and nowhere else in 1 Samuel in the files checked. The WEB renders it *prosper* or *success* elsewhere (Joshua 1:7–8,
  stored WEB). See [[Wisdom]].
- **Stood in awe.** *Wayyāḡor mippānāyw* (15, WLC). *Gûr* in this sense (morphhb lemma 1481 c) occurs in the files
  checked only at Numbers 22:3, *Moab was very afraid of the people*; Deuteronomy 1:17 and 18:22, *you shall not be
  afraid*; Deuteronomy 32:27, *I feared the provocation of the enemy*; and here (stored WEB). Moab dreaded Israel
  because it was many; Saul dreads David because Yahweh is with him.
- **All Israel and Judah.** *Wəḵol-yiśrāʾēl wîhûḏâ ʾōhēḇ ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ* (16, WLC). The book has counted Israel and Judah
  apart before, at Bezek (11:8) and at Elah, *the men of Israel and of Judah* (17:52) (stored WEB). The love is the
  people's; they love him *for he went out and came in before them*. See [[Love]].

**How it connects.** The pericope opens the long middle of 1 Samuel, Saul against David. Chapter 18 is built on the
contrast the narrator states at 12 and 14, and closes on it again at 28–29: *Saul saw and knew that Yahweh was with
David … Saul was even more afraid of David* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The song follows David into Philistine
mouths at Gath (21:11) and at Aphek (29:5); the spear is thrown again at David (19:10) and at Jonathan (20:33). The
evil spirit and the harp go back to 16:14–23, and the *Yahweh was with him* to 16:18. Envy is the wiki's word for
Saul here; the text says *very angry*, *displeased*, *watched*, *afraid*, and *in awe*, and never uses *qinʾâ*.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 18:6–16 as planned: 18:6 starts a new scene, the homecoming, and 18:16 closes the
unit with *all Israel and Judah loved David*, matching the summary of 18:5; 18:17 opens the marriage offers. One new
person page, [[Singing Women (of Saul's time)]], because the women's couplet is quoted twice more (21:11; 29:5) and
the group needs a page to carry it. No new place page: the text names no town. One new connection,
[[Saul Has Slain His Thousands - 1 Samuel 18.7, 21.11, and 29.5]].

**Unresolved.** Whether the couplet meant to slight Saul or is the ordinary parallelism of a song, as in Deuteronomy
33:17. Whether *twice* (11) counts throws or escapes. What Saul's *prophesying* (10) looked like. Whether Saul's
removing David to a command (13) was meant as an honor, a demotion from the court, or a way to put him in danger, as
18:17, 21, 25 say outright of the marriage offers ([[David Marries Michal]]); 18:13 gives no motive beyond
the fear of 12.
