# The Song of Hannah

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 2:1–11

## Summary

> **1** Hannah prayed, and said: "My heart exults in Yahweh! My horn is exalted in Yahweh. My mouth is
> enlarged over my enemies, because I rejoice in your salvation.
> **2** There is no one as holy as Yahweh, For there is no one besides you, nor is there any rock like our
> God.
> **3** "Don't keep talking so exceedingly proudly. Don't let arrogance come out of your mouth, For Yahweh
> is a God of knowledge. By him actions are weighed.
> **4** "The bows of the mighty men are broken. Those who stumbled are armed with strength.
> **5** Those who were full have hired themselves out for bread. Those who were hungry are satisfied. Yes,
> the barren has borne seven. She who has many children languishes.
> **6** "Yahweh kills, and makes alive. He brings down to Sheol, and brings up.
> **7** Yahweh makes poor, and makes rich. He brings low, he also lifts up.
> **8** He raises up the poor out of the dust. He lifts up the needy from the dunghill, To make them sit with
> princes, and inherit the throne of glory. For the pillars of the earth are Yahweh's. He has set the world
> on them.
> **9** He will keep the feet of his holy ones, but the wicked shall be put to silence in darkness; for no
> man shall prevail by strength.
> **10** Those who strive with Yahweh shall be broken to pieces. He will thunder against them in the sky.
> "Yahweh will judge the ends of the earth. He will give strength to his king, and exalt the horn of his
> anointed."
> **11** Elkanah went to Ramah to his house. The child served Yahweh before Eli the priest.

[[Hannah]], having just given [[Samuel]] to Yahweh at Shiloh, prays, and the prayer is a song. She exults
in Yahweh, who has lifted her *horn* and opened her mouth against her enemies (1). No one is holy like him,
and there is no rock like him (2). The proud should stop talking, because Yahweh knows and weighs what is
done (3). Then come the reversals, in pairs: the strong are broken and the stumbling are armed; the full
hire themselves out and the hungry are fed; the barren bears seven and the mother of many withers (4–5).
Yahweh is the one who does it: he kills and makes alive, brings down to Sheol and up, makes poor and rich,
lowers and lifts; he raises the poor from the dust to sit with princes, for the earth stands on his pillars
(6–8). He guards his faithful ones and silences the wicked, because no one prevails by strength (9). His
opponents will be shattered, he will thunder from heaven and judge the ends of the earth, and he will give
strength to *his king* and lift *the horn of his anointed* (10). [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]] goes home
to [[Ramah]], and the boy stays to serve Yahweh before [[Eli]] (11).

**What is going on.** The chapter before told a private grief; this one sets it to music and widens it to
the whole world. Hannah never names Peninnah, Samuel, or her own womb. Her one line that matches her
story, *the barren has borne seven* (5), stands in a list of reversals about soldiers, the hungry, the
poor, and the dead, and the song ends not on a child but on a king. No king exists in Israel yet. Judges
closed on *In those days there was no king in Israel* (Judges 21:25), and the people will not ask for
one until chapter 8. The song, sung by a woman at Shiloh, names *his king* and *his anointed* before
either exists. Whatever its origin, the book places it here, at the hinge from judges to kings, as the
lens for what follows: the proud brought low, the lowly raised, and a king whose strength is given.

**What is notable.**

- **She prayed.** *Wattiṯpallēl ḥannâ wattōʾmar* (1, WLC), the same verb as her silent prayer in 1:10, 12.
  This time her words are given. The prayer that asked in silence is answered aloud, and it contains no
  request.
- **My horn.** *Rāmâ qarnî ba-YHWH* (1, WLC), *my horn is exalted in Yahweh*. The horn is an image of a
  wild ox's strength and bearing; Moses blesses Joseph with *the horns of the wild ox* (Deuteronomy 33:17).
  The song opens with Hannah's horn lifted (1) and closes with *the horn of his anointed* lifted (10), the
  same noun *qeren* and the same verb *rûm* (WLC). In the WLC files searched (Genesis through Ruth, and
  1 Samuel), *qeren* elsewhere means an animal's horn, the ram's horn trumpet (Joshua 6:5), or the
  altar's horns; the one other figurative use is Joseph's *horns of the wild ox* (Deuteronomy 33:17). A
  horn *exalted*, with *rûm*, appears in those files only in these two verses. It next appears as *the horn of oil* with which Samuel anoints David (16:1, 13, stored
  WEB; not yet ingested). The link between the song's *horn of his anointed* and that anointing horn is a
  reader's observation, not the text's.
- **My heart exults.** *ʿĀlaṣ libbî* (1, WLC). The verb *ʿālaṣ* occurs in the WLC files searched (Genesis
  through Ruth, 1 Samuel, Job, Psalms, Proverbs) only here and in Psalms and Proverbs (Psalm 5:11 WEB; 9:2;
  25:2; 68:3 WEB; Proverbs 11:10; 28:12). Hannah's vocabulary is the vocabulary of the Psalter more than of
  the narrative around her.
- **Your salvation.** *Kî śāmaḥtî bîšûʿāṯeḵā* (1, WLC). *Yəšûʿâ* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis
  through Ruth only at Genesis 49:18, Exodus 14:13 and 15:2, and Deuteronomy 32:15: in Jacob's blessing,
  at the sea, in the Song of the Sea (*Yah … has become my salvation*), and in the Song of Moses. Hannah's
  is the next. David's song at the far end of Samuel closes on the plural, *great deliverance*, *yəšûʿôṯ*
  (2 Samuel 22:51, WLC).
- **No rock like our God.** *Wəʾên ṣûr kēʾlōhênû* (2, WLC). *Ṣûr* as a name for God is the signature of
  the Song of Moses: *The Rock, his work is perfect* and *their rock is not as our Rock* (Deuteronomy 32:4,
  31), eight occurrences of *ṣûr* in Deuteronomy 32 in the WLC. See
  [[No Rock Like Our God - The Song of Moses and the Song of Hannah]]. *There is no one as holy as Yahweh*
  recalls *Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods? … glorious in holiness* (Exodus 15:11).
- **A God of knowledge.** *ʾĒl dēʿôṯ YHWH* (3, WLC). The plural *dēʿôṯ* occurs in the WLC files searched
  only here and at Job 36:4, where Elihu says *one who is perfect in knowledge is with you*. As a divine
  title, *ʾēl dēʿôṯ* occurs in the files searched only in this verse. See [[The Names of God]].
- **By him actions are weighed, or are not.** The Hebrew of 3 has a written text (*ketiv*) *lōʾ*, *not*,
  and a reading tradition (*qere*) *lô*, *by him* (WLC, which prints both). The WEB follows the *qere*. The
  *ketiv* could be read *and actions are not weighed* (that is, his acts are beyond measuring) or as a
  question. Two more *ketiv/qere* pairs fall in 9 and 10 (*his holy one* / *his holy ones*; *his opponent*
  / *his opponents*), each singular in the written text and plural in the reading (WLC). The WEB follows
  the plural each time.
- **The barren has borne seven.** *ʿAḏ-ʿăqārâ yālᵊḏâ šiḇʿâ* (5, WLC). Hannah will in fact bear Samuel and
  then *three sons and two daughters* (2:21, stored WEB; not yet ingested): six, not seven. The number is
  the number of a full house, as in the women's *better to you than seven sons* (Ruth 4:15). See
  [[Better Than Seven Sons - Ruth 4.15 and 1 Samuel 1.8]]. *She who has many children languishes*,
  *ʾumlālâ* (WLC), is the line most easily read as Peninnah, though the song does not name her.
- **Yahweh kills, and makes alive.** *YHWH mēmîṯ ûməḥayyeh* (6, WLC), the participles of the two verbs in
  Deuteronomy 32:39, *I kill and I make alive* (*ʾămîṯ waʾăḥayyeh*). *He brings down to Sheol, and brings
  up*: in the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth, *šəʾôl* occurs at Genesis 37:35; 42:38; 44:29, 31;
  Numbers 16:30, 33; and Deuteronomy 32:22, always as a place one goes down to. Here for the first time in
  those books someone is brought *up* (*wayyaʿal*, WLC). Whether that means resurrection or rescue from
  near death the verse does not say, and readings divide; the wiki reports both. See
  [[Shall We Receive Good and Not Evil - Weal and Woe from One Hand]] and [[Life and Death]].
- **From the dust, from the dunghill.** Verse 8's first line, *mēqîm mēʿāp̄ār dāl mēʾašpōṯ yārîm
  ʾeḇyôn ləhôšîḇ ʿim-nəḏîḇîm* (WLC), is Psalm 113:7–8 almost word for word (*məqîmî mēʿāp̄ār dāl
  mēʾašpōṯ yārîm ʾeḇyôn ləhôšîḇî ʿim-nəḏîḇîm*, WLC). *ʾAšpōṯ*, *dunghill, ash heap*, occurs in the WLC
  files searched only in these two verses. The psalm's next verse is *He settles the barren woman in her
  home, as a joyful mother of children* (Psalm 113:9, WEB checked at bible-api.com). See
  [[From the Dust and the Ash Heap - 1 Samuel 2.8 and Psalm 113.7-9]].
- **The throne of glory.** *Wəḵissēʾ ḵāḇôḏ yanḥilēm* (8, WLC). *Kissēʾ* is the word for Eli's *seat* at
  the doorpost (1:9), and the seat he will fall from when the ark is lost (4:18). The poor are made to
  inherit a throne; the priest on his seat will fall from it. The pairing is a reader's observation.
- **The pillars of the earth.** *Məṣuqê ʾereṣ* (8, WLC). The reversals rest on creation: the one who set
  the world on its foundations can reorder those who live on it. See [[Creation]].
- **His holy ones.** *Ḥăsîḏāyw* (9, WLC *qere*), *his faithful ones*, from *ḥeseḏ*. In the WLC files of
  Genesis through Ruth the noun *ḥāsîḏ* occurs only at Deuteronomy 33:8, of Levi, *your godly one*.
- **Not by strength.** *Kî-lōʾ ḇəḵōaḥ yiḡbar-ʾîš* (9, WLC). The line that judges the bows of the mighty
  (4) and anticipates the boy with a sling who will say *Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear*
  (17:47, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **He will thunder against them in the sky.** *Bašāmayim yarʿēm* (10, WLC). At Mizpah, *Yahweh thundered
  with a great thunder on that day on the Philistines* (7:10), the same verb; see
  [[He Will Thunder Against Them - Hannah's Song and Mizpah]].
- **His king, his anointed.** *Wəyitten-ʿōz ləmalkô wəyārēm qeren məšîḥô* (10, WLC). *Māšîaḥ*, *anointed*,
  occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Ruth only of *the anointed priest* (Leviticus 4:3, 5, 16;
  6:22 WEB, 6:15 WLC). This is its first use of a king. In 1 Samuel it returns of the priest's master (*my
  anointed*, 2:35), of Saul (12:3, 5; 24:6, 10; 26:9, 11, 16, 23 WEB numbering), and of the one Samuel
  thinks is chosen in Bethlehem (16:6). David's song at the end of 2 Samuel closes on the same pair:
  *He gives great deliverance to his king, and shows loving kindness to his anointed, to David and to his
  offspring* (2 Samuel 22:51, WEB checked at bible-api.com; WLC *malkô … limšîḥô*). See
  [[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].
- **Before any king.** Whether Hannah's song was composed for this moment or is an older psalm placed here
  is a standard question, and the mention of a king in a time without kings is the usual reason it is
  asked. The wiki reports the question and does not settle it. Read in place, the line is a prophecy in a
  prayer, and the book goes on to test it twice: in Saul, who is anointed and falls, and in David.
- **The child served.** *Wəhannaʿar hāyâ məšārēṯ ʾeṯ-YHWH ʾeṯ-pənê ʿēlî hakkōhēn* (11, WLC). *Šērēṯ*,
  *serve, minister*, is the verb of priestly service at the tabernacle (for example Exodus 28:43; Numbers
  3:6; Deuteronomy 10:8). The boy given *as long as he lives* (1:28) begins at once, *before Eli*. Elkanah
  goes home *to Ramah*; Hannah is not named in 11, and is assumed to go with him.

**How it connects.** The song looks back to the Song of the Sea (*salvation*, *who is like you … in
holiness*, Exodus 15:2, 11) and above all to the Song of Moses (*Rock*, *I kill and I make alive*, *Sheol*,
Deuteronomy 32). It shares a line with Psalm 113. It looks forward to David's song at the end of 2 Samuel,
which opens with *Yahweh is my rock* and *the horn of my salvation* (22:2–3) and ends with *his king* and
*his anointed* (22:51); the two songs are commonly read as framing the books of Samuel, a reading the wiki
reports. And it looks forward to Mary's song in Luke 1:46–55, where *he has put down princes from their
thrones, and has exalted the lowly; he has filled the hungry with good things; he has sent the rich away
empty* (Luke 1:52–53, WEB checked at bible-api.com) restates Hannah's reversals. See
[[The Song of Hannah and the Song of Mary - 1 Samuel 2.1-10 and Luke 1.46-55]]. In the wiki's own
sequence, Hannah's song stands beside the women who sing in the books stored so far: Miriam at the sea
(Exodus 15:20–21) and Deborah (Judges 5). The next pericope, [[1 Samuel]] 2:12–26, turns from the song to
Eli's sons, *wicked men* who *despised the offering of Yahweh* (2:12, 17): the proud the song warned.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope keeps verse 11 with the song, as planned: it closes the Shiloh scene of
1:24–2:11 and sets the boy in place before Eli's sons are introduced in 2:12. No boundary was redrawn.
*Shiloh* is listed as the setting, though 2:1–11 does not name it. *Wealth and Possessions* is linked for
2:7 only. Four connection pages were created rather than one, because the song's ties to Deuteronomy 32,
Psalm 113, 2 Samuel 22, and Luke 1 are distinct and each is verifiable on its own.

**Unresolved.** Whether *brings up* from Sheol (6) means resurrection or rescue; whether the song was
composed for its setting; and how to read the *ketiv* of 3. Each is reported above, not settled.
