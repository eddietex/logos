# Saul Comes to the Seer

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 9:1–25

## Summary

> **9:1** Now there was a man of Benjamin, whose name was Kish, the son of Abiel, the son of Zeror, the son of
> Becorath, the son of Aphiah, the son of a Benjamite, a mighty man of valor.
> **2** He had a son, whose name was Saul, an impressive young man; and there was not among the children of
> Israel a better person than he. From his shoulders and upward he was taller than any of the people.
> **3** The donkeys of Kish, Saul's father, were lost. Kish said to Saul his son, "Take now one of the servants
> with you, and arise, go look for the donkeys."
> **4** He passed through the hill country of Ephraim, and passed through the land of Shalishah, but they didn't
> find them. Then they passed through the land of Shaalim, and there they weren't there. Then he passed through
> the land of the Benjamites, but they didn't find them.
> **5** When they had come to the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant who was with him, "Come, and let us
> return, lest my father stop caring about the donkeys, and be anxious for us."
> **6** The servant said to him, "Behold now, there is in this city a man of God, and he is a man who is held in
> honor. All that he says surely happens. Now let us go there. Perhaps he can tell us which way to go."
> **7** Then Saul said to his servant, "But, behold, if we go, what should we bring the man? For the bread is
> spent in our sacks, and there is not a present to bring to the man of God. What do we have?"
> **8** The servant answered Saul again, and said, "Behold, I have in my hand the fourth part of a shekel of
> silver. I will give that to the man of God, to tell us our way."
> **9** (In earlier times in Israel, when a man went to inquire of God, he said, "Come, and let us go to the
> seer"; for he who is now called a prophet was before called a seer.)
> **10** Then Saul said to his servant, "Well said. Come, let us go." So they went to the city where the man of
> God was.
> **11** As they went up the ascent to the city, they found young maidens going out to draw water, and said to
> them, "Is the seer here?"
> **12** They answered them, and said, "He is. Behold, he is before you. Hurry now, for he has come today into
> the city; for the people have a sacrifice today in the high place.
> **13** As soon as you have come into the city, you will immediately find him, before he goes up to the high
> place to eat; for the people will not eat until he come, because he blesses the sacrifice. Afterwards those
> who are invited eat. Now therefore go up; for at this time you will find him."
> **14** They went up to the city. As they came within the city, behold, Samuel came out toward them, to go up to
> the high place.
> **15** Now Yahweh had revealed to Samuel a day before Saul came, saying,
> **16** "Tomorrow about this time I will send you a man out of the land of Benjamin, and you shall anoint him to
> be prince over my people Israel. He will save my people out of the hand of the Philistines; for I have looked
> upon my people, because their cry has come to me."
> **17** When Samuel saw Saul, Yahweh said to him, "Behold, the man of whom I spoke to you! He will have
> authority over my people."
> **18** Then Saul approached Samuel in the gateway, and said, "Please tell me where the seer's house is."
> **19** Samuel answered Saul, and said, "I am the seer. Go up before me to the high place, for you are to eat
> with me today. In the morning I will let you go, and will tell you all that is in your heart.
> **20** As for your donkeys who were lost three days ago, don't set your mind on them; for they have been found.
> For whom is all that is desirable in Israel? Is it not for you, and for all your father's house?"
> **21** Saul answered, "Am I not a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel? And my family the least
> of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? Why then do you speak to me like this?"
> **22** Samuel took Saul and his servant, and brought them into the guest room, and made them sit in the best
> place among those who were invited, who were about thirty persons.
> **23** Samuel said to the cook, "Bring the portion which I gave you, of which I said to you, 'Set it aside.'"
> **24** The cook took up the thigh, and that which was on it, and set it before Saul. Samuel said, "Behold, that
> which has been reserved! Set it before yourself and eat; because for the appointed time has it been kept for
> you, for I said, 'I have invited the people.'" So Saul ate with Samuel that day.
> **25** When they had come down from the high place into the city, he talked with Saul on the housetop.

A man of Benjamin, [[Kish (father of Saul)]], *a mighty man of valor*, is given a five-generation pedigree, and
then a son: [[Saul]], *an impressive young man*, better than anyone in Israel and taller than all the people
from the shoulders up (9:1–2). Kish's donkeys are lost, and he sends Saul with one of the servants to look for
them (3). They cross [[The Hill Country of Ephraim]], [[The Land of Shalishah]], [[The Land of Shaalim]], and
the land of the Benjamites, finding nothing (4). In [[The Land of Zuph]] Saul wants to turn back before his
father starts worrying about them instead of the donkeys (5). The servant, [[Saul's Servant (of the lost donkeys)]],
knows of *a man of God* in the city, *held in honor*, whose every word comes true; perhaps he can tell them their
way (6). Saul worries that they have no present to bring, their bread gone; the servant has a quarter shekel of
silver (7–8). The narrator stops to explain a word: the one now called a *prophet* used to be called a *seer*
(9). Going up toward the city they meet young women coming out to draw water, who tell them the seer has just
arrived, because the people have a sacrifice at the high place today, and nobody eats until he blesses it; hurry
and you will find him (11–13). As they enter, [[Samuel]] is coming out toward them on his way up (14).

The narrative then steps back a day. Yahweh had *uncovered Samuel's ear*: tomorrow at this time he will send *a
man out of the land of Benjamin*, whom Samuel is to anoint *prince over my people Israel*, to save them from the
hand of [[The Philistines]], *for I have looked upon my people, because their cry has come to me* (15–16). When
Samuel sees Saul, Yahweh says *Behold, the man* (17). Saul, at the gate, asks the seer himself where the seer's
house is. *I am the seer*, Samuel answers, and invites him to the meal, promises to tell him in the morning *all
that is in your heart*, tells him the donkeys lost three days ago have been found, and asks, *For whom is all that
is desirable in Israel? Is it not for you, and for all your father's house?* (18–20). Saul protests that he is a
Benjamite, of the smallest tribe and the least family in it (21). Samuel seats him and his servant at the head of
about thirty guests, and has the cook bring the portion he had told him to set aside: the thigh, *reserved* and
*kept for you* for the appointed time. Saul eats with Samuel, and back in the city they talk on the housetop
(22–25).

**What is going on.** The king the people asked for in chapter 8 arrives in chapter 9 without knowing it.
Everything in the story is small and domestic — lost animals, a worried father, an empty bread bag, a coin, girls
at a well, a dinner — and everything is being arranged by someone else. Saul does not seek Samuel; his servant
suggests it. He does not know the way; the young women give it. He does not know who Samuel is when he meets him.
The reader is told, in the one verse that breaks the frame (15–16), that Yahweh had set the meeting a day before,
and the rest of the chapter plays out the gap between what Samuel knows and what Saul knows. Saul is looking for
donkeys; he is found for a kingdom.

Yahweh's word to Samuel reframes chapter 8. There the people asked for a king *whom you will have chosen for
yourselves* (8:18). Here Yahweh sends the man, names his office, and gives the reason in the language of the
Exodus: *I have looked upon my people, because their cry has come to me* (16). The king is still the one the
people asked for, but he is given as the answer to a cry, not only as the granting of a demand. The chapter does
not say how the two framings fit, and the wiki records both.

The pericope stops before the anointing. Samuel has spoken of *all that is desirable in Israel*, and seated Saul
in the place of honor with a reserved portion, but the word *king* has not been said to Saul, and he has not
answered except to call himself the least.

**What is notable.**

- **Now there was a man of Benjamin.** *Wayhî ʾîš mibbinyāmîn ûšəmô qîš* (1, WLC qere). The WLC's written text
  (ketiv) divides the tribal name as *mbn ymyn*, *from the son of the right hand*; the qere reads it as one word.
  The opening has the shape of 1:1, *wayhî ʾîš ʾeḥāḏ … ûšəmô ʾelqānâ*, and of Judges 13:2, the introduction of
  Manoah (WLC): the book's second story opens as its first did, on a man, his place, and his name.
- **A mighty man of valor.** *Gibbôr ḥāyil* (1, WLC), of Kish, not of Saul. The phrase is said of Gideon (Judges
  6:12, *gibbôr heḥāyil*), of Jephthah (Judges 11:1), and of Boaz, whom the WEB renders *a mighty man of wealth*
  (Ruth 2:1) (WLC; stored WEB). Whether it means valor, standing, or wealth here the text does not settle.
- **Five generations.** Abiel, Zeror, Becorath, Aphiah, and *a Benjamite* (1). Elkanah was given four (1:1).
  **Recorded, not resolved:** 1 Samuel 14:51 reads *Kish was the father of Saul; and Ner the father of Abner was
  the son of Abiel* (stored WEB; not yet ingested), consistent with Kish son of Abiel; but 1 Chronicles 8:33 and
  9:39 read *Ner became the father of Kish. Kish became the father of Saul* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; not
  yet ingested). The two genealogies give Kish different fathers. Harmonizations proposed (two men named Kish;
  *Ner* as a more distant ancestor; a textual slip) are recalled, not verified, and the wiki does not choose.
- **An impressive young man.** *Bāḥûr wāṭôḇ* (2, WLC), literally *a young man, and good*. *Ṭôḇ*, *good*, is said
  twice in the verse: there was no one in Israel *better*, *ṭôḇ mimmennû*. The height is repeated when he is
  presented, *higher than any of the people from his shoulders and upward* (10:23, stored WEB; not yet ingested),
  and answered when the next king is chosen: *Don't look on his face, or on the height of his stature, because I
  have rejected him … man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh looks at the heart* (16:7, stored WEB; not
  yet ingested).
- **Saul.** *Šāʾûl* (2, WLC), the passive participle of *šāʾal*, *ask*: the people *asked* for a king (8:10). The
  name's first appearance is unglossed; the narrator makes no pun on it. See
  [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]].
- **The donkeys … were lost.** *Wattōʾḇaḏnâ hāʾăṯōnôṯ* (3, WLC). *ʾĂṯōnôṯ* are she-donkeys, livestock of value.
  Samuel's *the king … will take … your donkeys* is a chapter old (8:16, a different noun, *ḥămôrêḵem*, WLC). The
  donkeys frame the episode: *lost* (3, 20), *found* (20), and again at Rachel's tomb (10:2, stored WEB; not yet
  ingested).
- **The route.** The hill country of Ephraim, Shalishah, Shaalim, the land of *Yəmînî* (Benjamin), and Zuph
  (4–5). *Šālišâ* and *Šaʿălîm* occur only here in the WLC files searched (lemmas 8031, 8171); *Ṣûp̄* elsewhere
  only as Elkanah's ancestor at 1:1, beside *Ramathaim Zophim*. The route runs through or near Samuel's country,
  and the search ends at its edge. None of the three districts can be located with confidence; see the place
  pages.
- **Lest my father … be anxious for us.** *Pen-yeḥdal ʾāḇî min-hāʾăṯōnôṯ wəḏāʾaḡ lānû* (5, WLC). Saul's first
  words are of his father's worry. The two men at Rachel's tomb will repeat them almost word for word: *your
  father has stopped caring about the donkeys, and is anxious for you* (10:2, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **A man of God … held in honor.** *ʾÎš ʾĕlōhîm … wəhāʾîš niḵbāḏ* (6, WLC). *Niḵbāḏ* is from *kāḇēḏ*, the root
  of the *glory* that departed from Israel (4:21–22) and of Yahweh's *heavy* hand on Ashdod (5:6). *Man of God* is
  how the oracle against Eli's house was introduced (2:27); see [[Man of God (of Eli's time)]]. *All that he says
  surely happens*, *bôʾ yāḇôʾ* (6), restates 3:19, where Yahweh *let none of his words fall to the ground*.
- **A present.** *Təšûrâ* (7, WLC), a noun found only here in the WLC files searched (lemma 8670), and its meaning
  is uncertain (WEB *a present*). The quarter shekel of silver is the servant's; the narrative does not say
  whether the seer took it, and it never comes up again.
- **Prophet and seer.** *Lannāḇîʾ hayyôm yiqqārēʾ ləp̄ānîm hārōʾeh* (9, WLC): *he who is now called a prophet was
  before called a seer*. The note is a narrator's gloss, written from a later *now*. In the WLC files searched
  (Genesis through 2 Samuel, Ruth) the noun *rōʾeh* occurs only in this chapter, five times (9, 11, 18, 19). The
  verse sits awkwardly where it is, explaining a word the servant has not yet used (he says *man of God*; the
  maidens are asked for *the seer* at 11); the wiki notes the placement and does not explain it. See
  [[Prophecy and Vision]].
- **To inquire of God.** *Lidrôš ʾĕlōhîm* (9, WLC). Rebekah *went to inquire of Yahweh* about the twins (Genesis
  25:22, stored WEB). In 9:6–8 what is asked for is a way, and a lost animal: the first consultation of a prophet
  in 1 Samuel is about livestock.
- **Young maidens going out to draw water.** *Nəʿārôṯ yōṣəʾôṯ lišʾōḇ māyim* (11, WLC) — the words of Genesis 24:13,
  *the daughters of the men of the city are coming out to draw water*, *yōṣəʾōṯ lišʾōḇ māyim* (WLC; stored WEB).
  A young man from far away, outside a city, meets young women at water: the setting of
  [[The Betrothal at the Well]]. Here they give directions and hurry him on, and the scene never becomes a meeting
  with a bride. What is waiting for Saul in the city is not a wife but a kingdom.
- **The high place.** *Bāmâ* (12, 13, 14, 19, 25, WLC). Before this chapter, in the WLC files searched, the noun
  occurs only in poetry or in commands to destroy the high places of Canaan (Leviticus 26:30; Numbers 21:28; 33:52;
  Deuteronomy 32:13; 33:29). Here a high place is where the people sacrifice and Samuel blesses the sacrifice, with
  no comment from the narrator. The ark is in Kiriath Jearim (7:1–2) and Shiloh has dropped from the story.
- **He blesses the sacrifice.** *Hûʾ yəḇārēḵ hazzeḇaḥ* (13, WLC). The people do not eat until he comes; then *those
  who are invited*, *haqqəruʾîm*, eat. Samuel presides at the meal as at Mizpah he offered the lamb (7:9). Whether
  Samuel is acting as priest the text does not say. See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
- **Yahweh had uncovered Samuel's ear.** *Wayhwh gālâ ʾeṯ-ʾōzen šəmûʾēl* (15, WLC). The idiom is used by Boaz, *I
  thought to disclose it to you*, literally *uncover your ear* (Ruth 4:4, WLC), and by Jonathan (20:2, WLC; not yet
  ingested): to tell someone privately. Samuel, whose call was a voice in his ear at night (3:11), hears the king
  announced the same way.
- **Tomorrow about this time.** *Kāʿēṯ māḥār* (16, WLC), the phrase of the seventh plague, *tomorrow about this
  time I will cause it to rain a very grievous hail* (Exodus 9:18, WLC; stored WEB). There it announced a judgment;
  here, a deliverer.
- **You shall anoint him.** *Ûməšaḥtô* (16, WLC). Hannah sang of Yahweh's *anointed*, *məšîḥô* (2:10), and the man
  of God promised a priest who would walk *before my anointed* (2:35). The verb had been used of a king only in
  Jotham's fable, where *the trees set out to anoint a king over themselves* (Judges 9:8, 15, WLC; stored WEB);
  this is the first time Yahweh commands the anointing of a ruler. The anointing itself is in the next pericope
  (10:1, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[His King and His Anointed - Hannah's Song and David's]].
- **Prince, not king.** *Lənāḡîḏ ʿal-ʿammî yiśrāʾēl* (16, WLC). Yahweh does not say *meleḵ*, *king*, the word chapter 8
  kept repeating. *Nāḡîḏ* occurs first here in the WLC files searched, and after it at 10:1;
  13:14; 25:30; 2 Samuel 5:2; 6:21; 7:8 — of Saul twice, then of the man after Yahweh's own heart, then of David
  (lemma 5057). Its exact sense (designated leader, *one placed in front*) is debated; the gloss is general
  knowledge, not checked against a lexicon. See [[Kingship and Empire]].
- **He will save my people out of the hand of the Philistines.** *Wəhôšîaʿ … mîyaḏ pəlištîm* (16, WLC). The verb is
  the judges' verb (Judges 2:16, 18). The Philistines were *subdued* under Samuel, and *Yahweh's hand was against
  the Philistines all the days of Samuel* (7:13); this verse assumes that their hand is still on Israel. The wiki
  noted the tension at 7:13 and notes it again here; the text does not reconcile them. See [[The Philistines]].
- **I have looked upon my people, because their cry has come to me.** *Kî rāʾîṯî ʾeṯ-ʿammî kî bāʾâ ṣaʿăqāṯô ʾēlāy*
  (16, WLC). The words of the burning bush: *I have surely seen the affliction of my people* (Exodus 3:7), and *the
  cry of the children of Israel has come to me*, *ṣaʿăqaṯ bənê-yiśrāʾēl bāʾâ ʾēlāy* (Exodus 3:9, WLC; stored WEB).
  Yahweh sends Saul as he sent Moses. Chapter 8 promised that a cry against the king would go unanswered (8:18);
  here the king is himself the answer to a cry. See
  [[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]].
- **Behold, the man.** *Hinnēh hāʾîš* (17, WLC). The verb that follows, *yaʿṣōr bəʿammî*, *he will restrain*
  (WEB *have authority over*), is *ʿāṣar*, which elsewhere in the files searched means *shut up* or *hold back*: the
  wombs of Abimelech's house (Genesis 20:18), Sarai's barrenness (Genesis 16:2), the plague stopped (Numbers 17:13,
  15; 25:8) (lemma 6113, WLC). Its use for ruling is unusual, and translations vary; the wiki does not decide what
  nuance is meant.
- **I am the seer.** *ʾĀnōḵî hārōʾeh* (19, WLC). Saul asks the seer where the seer lives. The comedy is gentle, and
  it is the chapter's method: the one who sees knows; the one who is seen does not.
- **All that is in your heart.** *Wəḵōl ʾăšer bilḇāḇəḵā ʾaggîḏ lāḵ* (19, WLC). Saul's heart is not described in this
  chapter. The narrator will say at 10:9 that *God gave him another heart* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **All that is desirable in Israel.** *Ûləmî kol-ḥemdaṯ yiśrāʾēl* (20, WLC). The noun *ḥemdâ* occurs nowhere else in
  the WLC files searched (lemma 2532; Genesis 27:15 is a related form, *the good clothes*). Samuel says it before
  he says *king*. It answers the donkeys: Saul is told not to set his heart on what was lost, because more than that
  is meant for him.
- **The smallest of the tribes; the least of the families.** *Miqqəṭannê šiḇṭê yiśrāʾēl ûmišpaḥtî haṣṣəʿirâ* (21,
  WLC). Benjamin is *small* because Judges 20–21 nearly ended it; Israel wept at Bethel that *there should be one
  tribe lacking in Israel* (Judges 21:3). *Ṣāʿîr*, *least, youngest*, is Gideon's word: *my family is the poorest in
  Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house*, *haṣṣāʿîr* (Judges 6:15, WLC; stored WEB). See
  [[The Least in My Father's House - Judges 6.15 and 1 Samuel 9.21]]. Saul will be found hiding among the baggage
  when chosen by lot (10:22, stored WEB; not yet ingested); Samuel will remember the humility against him: *Though
  you were little in your own sight* (15:17, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The guest room … the best place.** *Liškâ*, a chamber (22), occurs only here in the WLC files searched (lemma
  3957). *Bərōʾš haqqəruʾîm*, *at the head of those invited*. A man who calls himself the least is seated first,
  as Benjamin, *the youngest according to his youth*, was seated at Joseph's table and given a portion *five times
  as much* (Genesis 43:33–34, stored WEB; the noun there is *maśʾēṯ*, not this chapter's *mānâ*, WLC).
- **The portion.** *Hammānâ* (23, WLC). The noun names Elkanah's portions at the sacrifice in Shiloh, and the *double
  portion* he gave to Hannah (1:4–5, WLC); it is also the priest's *portion* of the ordination ram and of the peace
  offering (Exodus 29:26; Leviticus 7:33; 8:29, WLC). In the WLC files searched it occurs only in those places and
  here. Hannah's son now gives *the portion* to the king.
- **The thigh.** *Haššôq wəheʿāleyhā* (24, WLC), *the thigh and what was on it*. The law gives *the right thigh*,
  *šôq hayyāmîn*, of the peace offering to the priest (Leviticus 7:32–33, WLC; stored WEB). Here the verse does not
  say *right*, the sacrifice is not named as a peace offering, and the thigh goes to a layman. Whether this is a
  priestly portion given away, an ordinary choice cut of a feast, or something between, the text does not say. See
  [[The Breast and the Right Thigh - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 7]].
- **Kept for you for the appointed time.** *Kî lammôʿēḏ šāmûr-lāḵ lēʾmōr hāʿām qārāʾṯî* (24, WLC). The last clause,
  *saying, "I have invited the people"*, is difficult in Hebrew, and translations differ in how they construe it;
  the WEB's rendering is followed here without further claim. *Môʿēḏ*, *appointed time*, is the word of the feasts
  and of the Tent of Meeting.
- **On the housetop.** *Wayḏabbēr ʿim-šāʾûl ʿal-haggāḡ* (25, WLC). What they discuss is not reported. At 9:26 the
  ketiv *hgg* is read as *haggāḡâ*, *to the housetop* (qere). Ancient versions are commonly reported to read 9:25–26
  differently (a bed spread on the roof); that is recalled, not checked, and no Greek text was consulted.

**How it connects.** The chapter answers [[Israel Asks for a King]] by showing the king's arrival from Yahweh's side
and from Saul's side at once. Yahweh's word to Samuel uses the language of the Exodus call
([[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]]), and Saul's protest uses the language of
Gideon's ([[The Least in My Father's House - Judges 6.15 and 1 Samuel 9.21]]), so the first king is introduced in
the words of a deliverer. The meeting at the water borrows the opening of
[[The Betrothal at the Well]] and declines to complete it. The portion and the thigh look back to Hannah's double
portion at Shiloh (1:5) and to the priest's share in Leviticus
([[The Breast and the Right Thigh - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 7]]). Benjamin, the tribe nearly destroyed in
[[The War Against Benjamin]] and rebuilt in [[Wives for Benjamin]], supplies the king, and Saul's home will be
Gibeah (10:26, stored WEB; not yet ingested), the town of [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]]. The next pericope
completes what this one prepares: the anointing at the edge of the city, and the signs.

## People
- [[Saul]] — son of Kish; sent after the donkeys; meets Samuel, is seated first and given the portion (9:2–25)
- [[Kish (father of Saul)]] — the Benjamite whose donkeys are lost (9:1, 3)
- [[Saul's Servant (of the lost donkeys)]] — knows of the man of God and carries the silver (9:3–10, 22)
- [[Samuel]] — the seer; told the day before, he meets Saul, hosts him, and talks with him on the housetop
  (9:6–25)
- [[The Philistines]] — the people Saul is to save Israel from (9:16)
- [[Benjamin]] — Saul's tribe, *the smallest of the tribes of Israel* (9:1, 16, 21)

## Places
- [[The Hill Country of Ephraim]] — the first district of the search (9:4)
- [[The Land of Shalishah]] — the second (9:4)
- [[The Land of Shaalim]] — the third (9:4)
- [[The Land of Zuph]] — where Saul proposes to turn back, and the seer's city lies (9:5–6)
- [[Ramah]] — Samuel's home (7:17; 8:4), often identified with the unnamed city of this chapter; the text does
  not name it (9:6–25)

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 9:1–25 as planned; 9:26 begins the next morning and leads straight into the
anointing, so it stays with 10:1. New person pages for [[Saul]], [[Kish (father of Saul)]] (qualified because
another Kish, an ancestor of Mordecai, appears at Esther 2:5, not yet stored), and
[[Saul's Servant (of the lost donkeys)]] (unnamed, qualified by his errand, since Saul's servants recur from 16:15).
Abiel, Zeror, Becorath, and Aphiah get no pages, as Elkanah's ancestors did not at 1:1; the young women at the water
and the cook get none. The three districts of 9:4–5 get place pages titled *The Land of …*, following
[[The Land of the Philistines]]; *The Land of Zuph* is so titled partly so that it never collides with a future page
for Zuph, Elkanah's ancestor. The seer's city is unnamed; it is linked to [[Ramah]] only as a common identification.
*The land of the Benjamites* is linked to [[Benjamin]], as the tribe's other territory references are. New connection
[[The Least in My Father's House - Judges 6.15 and 1 Samuel 9.21]].

**Unresolved.** Who Kish's father was (9:1; 14:51; 1 Chronicles 8:33; 9:39). How Yahweh's sending of Saul (9:16)
relates to the people's choosing (8:18). How 9:16's Philistine oppression fits 7:13. Whether the thigh is a priestly
portion. The sense of *nāḡîḏ*, *təšûrâ*, and *ʿāṣar* here. The syntax of 9:24's last clause. Each is reported, not
settled.
