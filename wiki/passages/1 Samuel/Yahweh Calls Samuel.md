# Yahweh Calls Samuel

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 3:1–4:1a

## Summary

> **1** The child Samuel ministered to Yahweh before Eli. Yahweh's word was precious in those days. There
> were not many visions, then.
> **2** At that time, when Eli was laid down in his place (now his eyes had begun to grow dim, so that he
> could not see),
> **3** and God's lamp hadn't yet gone out, and Samuel had laid down in Yahweh's temple, where God's ark
> was;
> **4** Yahweh called Samuel; and he said, "Here I am."
> **5** He ran to Eli, and said, "Here I am; for you called me." He said, "I didn't call. Lie down again."
> He went and lay down.
> **6** Yahweh called yet again, "Samuel!" Samuel arose and went to Eli, and said, "Here I am; for you
> called me." He answered, "I didn't call, my son. Lie down again."
> **7** Now Samuel didn't yet know Yahweh, neither was Yahweh's word yet revealed to him.
> **8** Yahweh called Samuel again the third time. He arose and went to Eli, and said, "Here I am; for you
> called me." Eli perceived that Yahweh had called the child.
> **9** Therefore Eli said to Samuel, "Go, lie down. It shall be, if he calls you, that you shall say,
> 'Speak, Yahweh; for your servant hears.'" So Samuel went and lay down in his place.
> **10** Yahweh came, and stood, and called as at other times, "Samuel! Samuel!" Then Samuel said, "Speak;
> for your servant hears."
> **11** Yahweh said to Samuel, "Behold, I will do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of everyone who
> hears it will tingle.
> **12** In that day I will perform against Eli all that I have spoken concerning his house, from the
> beginning even to the end.
> **13** For I have told him that I will judge his house forever, for the iniquity which he knew, because
> his sons brought a curse on themselves, and he didn't restrain them.
> **14** Therefore I have sworn to the house of Eli, that the iniquity of Eli's house shall not be removed
> with sacrifice or offering forever."
> **15** Samuel lay until the morning, and opened the doors of Yahweh's house. Samuel feared to show Eli
> the vision.
> **16** Then Eli called Samuel, and said, "Samuel, my son!" He said, "Here I am."
> **17** He said, "What is the thing that he has spoken to you? Please don't hide it from me. God do so to
> you, and more also, if you hide anything from me of all the things that he spoke to you."
> **18** Samuel told him every bit, and hid nothing from him. He said, "It is Yahweh. Let him do what seems
> good to him."
> **19** Samuel grew, and Yahweh was with him, and let none of his words fall to the ground.
> **20** All Israel from Dan even to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of Yahweh.
> **21** Yahweh appeared again in Shiloh; for Yahweh revealed himself to Samuel in Shiloh by Yahweh's word.
>
> **4:1** The word of Samuel came to all Israel. …

[[Samuel]], still *the child*, ministers before [[Eli]] at a time when *Yahweh's word was precious* and
visions were few (3:1). Eli, his eyes failing, is lying in his place; Samuel is lying *in Yahweh's
temple, where God's ark was*, before *God's lamp* has gone out (2–3). Yahweh calls the boy, and he runs to
Eli: *Here I am; for you called me* (4–5). It happens three times, because *Samuel didn't yet know
Yahweh, neither was Yahweh's word yet revealed to him* (7). The third time Eli understands, and tells him
to answer *Speak, Yahweh; for your servant hears* (8–9). *Yahweh came, and stood*, and called *Samuel!
Samuel!*; the boy answers (10). The word is the one Eli has already heard: *I will do a thing in Israel,
at which both the ears of everyone who hears it will tingle*; Yahweh will carry out all he has spoken
against Eli's house, *because his sons brought a curse on themselves, and he didn't restrain them*, and
the iniquity *shall not be removed with sacrifice or offering forever* (11–14). In the morning Samuel opens
the doors of Yahweh's house, afraid to tell Eli. Eli calls him *my son*, puts him under oath to hide
nothing, and hears it all: *It is Yahweh. Let him do what seems good to him* (15–18). Samuel grows,
Yahweh is with him and lets *none of his words fall to the ground*, and *all Israel from Dan even to
Beersheba* knows him as *a prophet of Yahweh*. Yahweh keeps appearing at [[Shiloh]], and *the word of
Samuel came to all Israel* (19–4:1a).

**What is going on.** This is the call of the first prophet in the books stored so far whose whole career
the narrative follows, and it is staged as a transfer. The old priest lies *in his place* with his eyes
going dark; the boy lies by the ark with the lamp still lit (2–3). By the end the word that was *precious*
(1) has a regular channel again, at Shiloh, through Samuel (21), and Eli's house has heard its sentence for
the second time, now from the boy who serves in it. The previous pericope's oracle came from a stranger,
[[Man of God (of Eli's time)]]; this one comes to the child Eli raised, and Eli has to ask for it. The
sentence is the same one (12: *all that I have spoken concerning his house*), and 13–14 add two things:
the charge that Eli *didn't restrain* his sons, and the oath that no sacrifice will cover the house's
iniquity. The next pericope begins to carry it out at Aphek (4:11, 18; stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**What is notable.**

- **Precious, and not many visions.** *Dəḇar-YHWH hāyâ yāqār*, *Yahweh's word was precious*, *rare*;
  *ʾên ḥāzôn nip̄rāṣ* (3:1, WLC), *no vision broke out*, or *spread abroad*: the WEB's *There were not
  many visions*. In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings, *ḥāzôn*, *vision*, and the Niphal of
  *pāraṣ* each occur only here (lemma search). The verse sets the scene by absence: the word is scarce,
  and the house that should carry it has its sentence. See [[Prophecy and Vision]].
- **Dim eyes, and a lamp not yet out.** Eli's eyes *had begun to grow dim, so that he could not see*
  (2); *God's lamp hadn't yet gone out* (3). *Kēhôṯ*, *dim* (2, WLC), is the adjective of the root used of
  [[Isaac]] (*his eyes were dim, so that he could not see*, Genesis 27:1) and, negated, of [[Moses]] (*His
  eye was not dim*, Deuteronomy 34:7) (stored WEB; WLC). *Nēr ʾĕlōhîm ṭerem yiḵbeh* (3, WLC) places the
  scene at night, before morning: the lamp was to be tended *from evening to morning before Yahweh*
  (Exodus 27:21; Leviticus 24:3, stored WEB). Many readers also hear a figure in it, the light of Israel
  not yet out; the text does not say so. See
  [[From Evening to Morning - The Lamp in Exodus 27 and Leviticus 24]] and [[Light and Darkness]].
- **Where Samuel slept.** *In Yahweh's temple, where God's ark was* (3). *Hêḵal YHWH* (WLC) is the word of
  1:9; the clause places the ark there, the ark that 4:3–4 will bring out of Shiloh (stored WEB; not yet
  ingested). Whether the boy slept in the room with the ark or in the precinct, the text does not specify,
  and readers differ; a Levite lying beside the ark would sit oddly with Numbers 4:15–20, which the text
  does not raise.
- **Here I am, five times.** *Hinnēnî* (3:4, 5, 6, 8, 16, WLC). Samuel says it to Yahweh once without
  knowing who called (4), to Eli three times on the same mistake (5, 6, 8), and to Eli once more in the
  morning when Eli calls *Samuel, my son* (16). The boy's readiness is the same toward both voices; what
  he lacks is knowledge (7).
- **Didn't yet know Yahweh.** *Ṭerem yāḏaʿ ʾeṯ-YHWH* (3:7, WLC). The phrase with the negative is Pharaoh's
  (*I don't know Yahweh*, Exodus 5:2), the generation after Joshua's (Judges 2:10), and Eli's sons' (*they
  didn't know Yahweh*, 2:12) (lemma search; stored WEB). Samuel's *not yet*, *ṭerem*, is the difference: it
  is the same word as *God's lamp hadn't yet*, *ṭerem*, *gone out* (3). The verse explains the narrative,
  not a fault. See [[They Didn't Know Yahweh - Pharaoh, the Generation After Joshua, and Eli's Sons]].
- **Revealed.** *Yiggāleh*, *revealed* (7), and *niḡlâ YHWH*, *Yahweh revealed himself* (21), the Niphal of
  *gālâ* (WLC). It is the verb of the man of God's *Did I reveal myself to the house of your father* (2:27)
  and of Jacob's *El Beth El … there God was revealed to him* (Genesis 35:7) (WLC; stored WEB). The revealing
  that once marked Eli's ancestral house now marks Samuel.
- **Eli perceived.** *Wayyāḇen ʿēlî* (8, WLC). Eli is slow at the start of the book, taking Hannah's prayer
  for drunkenness (1:13–14), and on the third call he understands. The old priest who cannot see is the
  one who teaches the boy how to hear. His instruction includes the divine name, *Speak, Yahweh* (9); when
  the moment comes Samuel says only *Speak; for your servant hears* (10), without *Yahweh* (WLC agrees
  with the WEB). The text does not explain the omission.
- **Yahweh came, and stood.** *Wayyāḇōʾ YHWH wayyiṯyaṣṣaḇ* (10, WLC). The Hithpael of *yāṣaḇ* with Yahweh as
  subject occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings elsewhere only at Exodus 34:5, *Yahweh
  descended in the cloud, and stood with him there*; the nearest other is the angel of Yahweh who *placed
  himself* in Balaam's way (Numbers 22:22) (stored WEB; lemma search). The call is not only a
  voice; the text gives it presence, and does not describe what Samuel saw. 15 calls it *the vision*,
  *hammarʾâ* (WLC).
- **As at other times.** *Kəp̄aʿam bəp̄aʿam* (10, WLC). The idiom occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1
  Kings at Numbers 24:1 (Balaam, *as at the other times*), Judges 16:20 (Samson, *I will go out as at other
  times* — *But he didn't know that Yahweh had departed from him*), Judges 20:30–31, here, and 1 Samuel 20:25
  (lemma search; stored WEB). At Samson's it marks the moment the presence had gone without his knowing; here
  the presence comes to one who does not yet know it.
- **Samuel! Samuel!** The doubled vocative of Abraham (Genesis 22:11), Jacob (46:2), and Moses (Exodus 3:4).
  In those three the man answers *Here I am*. **Samuel does not**: at 10 he answers *Speak; for your
  servant hears*, having already said *hinnēnî* to the single call at 4 (WLC). The wiki's connection page
  had said all four Old Testament cases are answered with *hinnēnî*; that is corrected there. See
  [[Jacob, Jacob - The Doubled Name and Hinneni]] and [[Call and Election]].
- **Your servant hears.** *Šōmēaʿ ʿaḇdeḵā* (9, 10, WLC). *Šāmaʿ* is the root Hannah's son's name is most
  often heard in (*Šəmûʾēl*), though the text's own explanation was *asked* (1:20); the connection is a
  common reader's observation, not the text's. Eli's sons *didn't listen*, *lōʾ yišməʿû* (2:25, WLC), to
  their father's voice. See [[Names and Naming]].
- **Both ears will tingle.** *Kol-šōmʿô təṣillênâ šəttê ʾozənāw* (11, WLC). The verb *ṣālal* occurs in the WLC
  files of Genesis through 1 Kings only here. The same image recurs, with Yahweh bringing *evil* on
  Jerusalem, in 2 Kings 21:12 (*whoever hears of it, both his ears will tingle*) and Jeremiah 19:3 (WEB and
  WLC checked; morphhb tags 1 Samuel 3:11 Hiphil and the other two Qal). Yahweh's judgment on the sanctuary
  at Shiloh is the template for his word against Jerusalem. See
  [[Both His Ears Will Tingle - 1 Samuel 3.11, 2 Kings 21.12, and Jeremiah 19.3]].
- **From the beginning even to the end.** *Hāḥēl wəḵallēh* (12, WLC), two infinitive absolutes, *beginning
  and finishing*. *Hāḥēl* is from *ḥālal*, the root of Eli's eyes that *had begun*, *hēḥēllû*, to grow dim
  (2, WLC; morphhb lemma 2490 c for both). The verse claims completeness: nothing of 2:27–36 will be dropped.
- **He didn't restrain them.** *Wəlōʾ kihâ bām* (13, WLC). Morphhb lemmatizes *kihâ* as a homonym (3543 b,
  Piel, *rebuke, restrain*) distinct from *kāhâ*, *grow dim* (3543 a; and the adjective *kēhôṯ*, 3544, of
  verse 2). The consonants are the same: the father whose eyes *grew dim* is the father who did not
  *restrain*. Whether the narrator meant the echo is not recoverable; the lexical data are reported. In the
  WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings the Piel occurs only here. Eli did rebuke them (2:23–25); this verse
  says it was not enough. See [[Eli]].
- **Brought a curse on themselves.** *Məqalləlîm lāhem bānāw* (13, WLC), literally *his sons were cursing
  for themselves*, or *making themselves contemptible*. A rabbinic list of *tiqqûnê sōp̄ərîm*, scribal
  corrections, is commonly said to include this verse, with an original *ʾĕlōhîm* (*cursing God*) softened
  to *lāhem*, and the Septuagint is commonly reported to read *speaking evil of God* (both recalled from
  standard commentary, not checked). *Qālal* is the verb of *those who despise me will be cursed*,
  *yēqallû* (2:30, WLC): those who make God light are made light. See [[Curse]].
- **Not removed with sacrifice or offering.** *ʾIm-yiṯkappēr … bəzeḇaḥ ûḇəminḥâ ʿaḏ-ʿôlām* (14, WLC), *I have
  sworn … if it will be atoned*, the oath form whose unstated clause is the curse. The Hithpael of *kāp̄ar*
  occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Kings only here. The house that abused the sacrifices (2:13–17,
  29) is told the sacrifices cannot help it. Numbers 15:30–31 had already left the high-handed sinner with
  no offering, *his iniquity shall be on him* (stored WEB). See [[Atonement]] and
  [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
- **The doors of Yahweh's house.** *Daltôṯ bêṯ-YHWH* (15, WLC). A sanctuary with doors, opened each morning
  by the boy, is a building, not only the Tent of Meeting (2:22). The text does not reconcile them. See
  [[The Dwelling of God]].
- **God do so to you, and more also.** *Kōh yaʿăśeh-ləḵā ʾĕlōhîm wəḵōh yôsîp̄* (17, WLC). The self-curse oath
  formula occurs first in the stored text at Ruth 1:17, *May Yahweh do so to me, and more also*; here a
  father puts it on a son, and 1–2 Samuel and 1 Kings use it often after (14:44; 20:13; 25:22; 2 Samuel 3:9,
  35; 19:13, Hebrew 19:14; 1 Kings 2:23; lemma search). The oath Eli lays on Samuel mirrors the oath Yahweh swore in 14.
  See [[Oaths and Vows]].
- **It is Yahweh.** *YHWH hûʾ haṭṭôḇ bəʿênāw yaʿăśeh* (18, WLC). Eli neither argues nor intercedes. Readers
  divide: resignation and submission, or the passivity that failed to restrain his sons now turned on
  himself. The text gives only the words. See [[Eli]].
- **None of his words fall to the ground.** *Wəlōʾ-hippîl mikkol-dəḇārāw ʾārṣâ* (19, WLC), with Yahweh as
  subject of the Hiphil: Yahweh let none of *his*, Samuel's, words fall. Joshua 21:45 and 23:14 said the same
  of Yahweh's own promises with the Qal, *Nothing failed* (*lōʾ-nāp̄al*, WLC; stored WEB), and Jehu says
  *nothing will fall to the earth of Yahweh's word* against Ahab's house (2 Kings 10:10, WEB and WLC
  checked). The prophet's word is held to the standard of Yahweh's. Deuteronomy 18:21–22 made fulfilment the
  test of a prophet (stored WEB).
- **Established to be a prophet.** *Neʾĕmān šəmûʾēl lənāḇîʾ laYHWH* (20, WLC). *Neʾĕmān* is the word of the
  *faithful priest* and *sure house* of 2:35. This is the first *nāḇîʾ* in 1 Samuel (lemma search); 9:9
  notes that *he who is now called a prophet was before called a seer* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[A Sure House - The Faithful Priest and the House of David]].
- **From Dan even to Beersheba.** The formula for the whole land (20). In the WLC files of Genesis through 1
  Kings its first use is Judges 20:1, where Israel assembles to make war on Benjamin; this is the second
  (lemma search). The land that gathered to fight itself now gathers to recognize a prophet. See
  [[Dan (city)]] and [[Beersheba]].
- **The word of Samuel.** *Wayhî dəḇar-šəmûʾēl ləḵol-yiśrāʾēl* (4:1a, WLC). The phrase that would usually
  read *the word of Yahweh came* is here *the word of Samuel*. The chapter that opened with *Yahweh's word*
  scarce (3:1) closes with Samuel's word reaching all Israel, and 3:21 has made the two the same: Yahweh
  revealed himself *by Yahweh's word*. Peter later names *all the prophets from Samuel and those who
  followed after* (Acts 3:24, WEB checked at bible-api.com).

**How it connects.** The pericope confirms [[A Man of God Against the House of Eli]] and hands its
fulfilment to the next pericope, *The Ark Is Captured* (4:1b–22, not yet ingested), where both sons die and Eli falls from his
seat. It answers [[The Sins of Eli's Sons]], whose alternating scenes of the sons and the boy it resolves:
the sons do not listen, and the boy says *your servant hears*. It continues the doubled-name call of
Abraham, Jacob, and Moses, and the burning bush's *Here I am*, with a difference noted above. The prophet
it establishes will judge Israel at Mizpah (7), anoint Saul (10), and anoint David (16). The *tingle* of
Shiloh returns in the words against Jerusalem in Kings and Jeremiah, and the shrine whose lamp is still
burning here will itself become an example of judgment (Jeremiah 7:12, cited by reference only).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 3:1–4:1a as planned. 4:1a, *The word of Samuel came to all Israel*,
closes the call rather than opening the battle, as the plan already assumed; the WEB and the WLC both print
it as the first clause of 4:1, so the next pericope starts at 4:1b. The next passage title, *The Ark
Is Captured*, is named in plain text, not linked, until its page exists. Hophni and Phinehas are listed
though unnamed, because 3:13 is about them. No new person or place pages: everyone in the passage already
has one.

**Contradiction flagged.** [[Jacob, Jacob - The Doubled Name and Hinneni]] said that all four Old Testament
doubled-name calls are answered with *hinnēnî* and that Samuel's reply *is Jacob's*. The WLC and WEB of 3:10
have Samuel answer the doubled call with *Speak; for your servant hears*; his *hinnēnî* is at 3:4, to the
single call. The connection page is corrected and says so.

**Unresolved.** Whether Samuel slept beside the ark or in the precinct (3); the text and sense of
*məqalləlîm lāhem* (13), and the reported scribal correction and Septuagint reading (recalled, not checked);
whether the *kihâ* / *kēhôṯ* echo (2, 13) is intended; and whether Eli's *It is Yahweh* (18) is submission
or resignation. Each is reported above, not settled.
