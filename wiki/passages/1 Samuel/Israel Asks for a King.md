# Israel Asks for a King

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 8:1–22

## Summary

> **8:1** When Samuel was old, he made his sons judges over Israel.
> **2** Now the name of his firstborn was Joel; and the name of his second, Abijah. They were judges in
> Beersheba.
> **3** His sons didn't walk in his ways, but turned away after dishonest gain, took bribes, and perverted
> justice.
> **4** Then all the elders of Israel gathered themselves together and came to Samuel to Ramah.
> **5** They said to him, "Behold, you are old, and your sons don't walk in your ways. Now make us a king to
> judge us like all the nations."
> **6** But the thing displeased Samuel, when they said, "Give us a king to judge us." Samuel prayed to
> Yahweh.
> **7** Yahweh said to Samuel, "Listen to the voice of the people in all that they tell you; for they have
> not rejected you, but they have rejected me as the king over them.
> **8** According to all the works which they have done since the day that I brought them up out of Egypt
> even to this day, in that they have forsaken me, and served other gods, so they also do to you.
> **9** Now therefore listen to their voice. However you shall protest solemnly to them, and shall show them
> the way of the king who will reign over them."
> **10** Samuel told all Yahweh's words to the people who asked him for a king.
> **11** He said, "This will be the way of the king who shall reign over you: he will take your sons, and
> appoint them as his servants, for his chariots, and to be his horsemen; and they will run before his
> chariots.
> **12** He will appoint them to him for captains of thousands, and captains of fifties; and he will assign
> some to plow his ground, and to reap his harvest, and to make his instruments of war, and the
> instruments of his chariots.
> **13** He will take your daughters to be perfumers, to be cooks, and to be bakers.
> **14** He will take your fields, your vineyards, and your olive groves, even their best, and give them to
> his servants.
> **15** He will take one tenth of your seed, and of your vineyards, and give it to his officers, and to his
> servants.
> **16** He will take your male servants, your female servants, your best young men, and your donkeys, and
> assign them to his own work.
> **17** He will take one tenth of your flocks; and you will be his servants.
> **18** You will cry out in that day because of your king whom you will have chosen for yourselves; and
> Yahweh will not answer you in that day."
> **19** But the people refused to listen to the voice of Samuel; and they said, "No; but we will have a
> king over us,
> **20** that we also may be like all the nations, and that our king may judge us, and go out before us,
> and fight our battles."
> **21** Samuel heard all the words of the people, and he rehearsed them in the ears of Yahweh.
> **22** Yahweh said to Samuel, "Listen to their voice, and make them a king." Samuel said to the men of
> Israel, "Everyone go to your own city."

[[Samuel]] grows old and makes his sons judges over Israel: the firstborn [[Joel (son of Samuel)]] and the
second [[Abijah (son of Samuel)]], judging at [[Beersheba]] (8:1–2). They do not walk in his ways; they
turn aside after dishonest gain, take bribes, and pervert justice (3). All the elders of Israel come to
Samuel at [[Ramah]] with a request and its reason: *you are old, and your sons don't walk in your ways.
Now make us a king to judge us like all the nations* (4–5). It displeases Samuel, and he prays (6).
Yahweh tells him to listen to the people, *for they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me as
the king over them*, as they have done ever since [[Egypt]], forsaking him and serving other gods; Samuel
is to listen, but to *protest solemnly* and show them *the way of the king* (7–9). Samuel tells all
Yahweh's words to *the people who asked him for a king* (10) and describes what the king will do. He will
take their sons for his chariots, horsemen, officers, ploughing, harvest, and weapons; their daughters as
perfumers, cooks, and bakers; their best fields, vineyards, and olive groves for his servants; a tenth of
their seed, vineyards, and flocks; their servants, best young men, and donkeys for his work; *and you will
be his servants* (11–17). *You will cry out in that day because of your king whom you will have chosen for
yourselves; and Yahweh will not answer you in that day* (18). The people refuse: *No; but we will have a
king over us, that we also may be like all the nations, and that our king may judge us, and go out before
us, and fight our battles* (19–20). Samuel repeats their words to Yahweh, and Yahweh says a third time,
*Listen to their voice, and make them a king*. Samuel sends everyone home (21–22).

**What is going on.** The chapter that closed on Samuel judging Israel *all the days of his life* (7:15)
is followed by his age and his sons. The request has two reasons, and the elders give both: Samuel is old,
and his sons are corrupt. Neither is a reason for a king rather than a better judge, and the elders say
what they want instead: a king *like all the nations*. Yahweh's answer separates the request from its
object. It is granted three times (7, 9, 22), and at the same time named for what it is: not a rejection
of Samuel but of Yahweh as king, the latest act in a history of forsaking him that began at the Exodus.
Samuel's warning, which Yahweh commands, is a list of what a king takes, built on one verb, and the people
answer it by repeating the request with more reasons. The chapter ends with the decision made and nothing
done: no king is named, and everyone goes home. The king arrives in the next chapter, from Benjamin, looking
for donkeys.

The narrative does not pretend the request has no grounds. Samuel's sons are judges *in Beersheba*, at the
far south of the land, and they do what the Torah forbids a judge to do. Samuel's displeasure is reported
when the elders say *give us a king to judge us*, not when they mention his sons (6), and the wiki does not
decide whether the narrator means him to be read as wounded, as right, or as both. Yahweh's *they have not
rejected you* (7) answers the personal hurt before it is spoken.

**What is notable.**

- **When Samuel was old.** *Kaʾăšer zāqēn šəmûʾēl* (1, WLC). The elders repeat the verb to his face,
  *hinnēh ʾattâ zāqantā* (5, WLC). Eli was *very old*, *zāqēn məʾōḏ*, when he heard what his sons were doing
  (2:22, WLC), and Samuel will open his farewell, *I am old and gray-headed. Behold, my sons are with you*
  (12:2, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The book introduces its two judges' old age beside their sons. See
  [[Not in His Ways - The Sons of Eli and the Sons of Samuel]].
- **He made his sons judges.** *Wayyāśem ʾeṯ-bānāyw šōp̄ṭîm ləyiśrāʾēl* (1, WLC). No judge in the book of
  Judges appoints his sons to judge, and none is said to have been appointed by his father. The nearest is
  Gideon's household, where Israel offered rule to *you, and your son, and your son's son* and was refused
  (Judges 8:22–23, stored WEB). Samuel does what Gideon refused, under the title of judge rather than king.
- **Joel and Abijah.** *Yôʾēl* and *ʾăḇiyyâ* (2, WLC), names commonly glossed *Yahweh is God* and *my father
  is Yahweh* (conventional glosses, not verified here). Both carry the divine name. In the WLC files searched
  (Genesis through 2 Kings, Ruth, Psalms) Joel's name occurs only here; an Abijah son of Jeroboam appears at
  1 Kings 14:1, so this one is filed as [[Abijah (son of Samuel)]]. 1 Chronicles 6:28 names the two in the
  same order, and 6:33 makes Heman the singer *the son of Joel, the son of Samuel* (WEB checked at
  bible-api.com; not yet ingested).
- **In Beersheba.** *Biḇʾēr šāḇaʿ* (2, WLC). Samuel's circuit of 7:16 ran Bethel, Gilgal, Mizpah; his sons
  sit far to the south, at the town that bounds *all Israel from Dan even to Beersheba* (3:20). The text does
  not say why there, or whether they judged anywhere else.
- **They didn't walk in his ways.** *Wəlōʾ-hāləḵû bānāyw biḏrāḵāyw* (3, WLC). The ketiv *bdrkw*, *in his
  way*, stands beside the qere *biḏrāḵāyw*, *in his ways* (WLC). The elders repeat it in the second person,
  *lōʾ hāləḵû biḏrāḵeyḵā* (5).
- **Dishonest gain, bribes, perverted justice.** *Wayyiṭṭû ʾaḥărê habbāṣaʿ wayyiqḥû-šōḥaḏ wayyaṭṭû mišpāṭ*
  (3, WLC). Three clauses, framed by the same verb, *nāṭâ*: they *turned aside* after gain, and they *turned
  aside* justice. The last two clauses are the two prohibitions of Deuteronomy 16:19, *lōʾ-ṯaṭṭeh mišpāṭ … wəlōʾ-ṯiqqaḥ
  šōḥaḏ*, *You shall not pervert justice … You shall not take a bribe* (WLC; stored WEB), given to the judges
  *in all your gates*. *Beṣaʿ*, *unjust gain*, is what Jethro's judges were to hate, *śōnʾê ḇāṣaʿ* (Exodus
  18:21, WLC). See [[A Bribe Blinds - Proverbs 17.23, Exodus 23.8, and Deuteronomy 16.19]].
- **All the elders of Israel.** *Kōl ziqnê yiśrāʾēl* (4, WLC). *The elders of Israel* sent for the ark after
  the defeat at Ebenezer (4:3). Then they reached for the ark; now they reach for a king.
- **A king to judge us.** *Śîmâ-lānû meleḵ ləšop̄ṭēnû kəḵol-haggôyim* (5, WLC). The imperative of *śîm* with
  *meleḵ* and *kəḵol-haggôyim* is the phrasing of Deuteronomy 17:14, *ʾāśîmâ ʿālay meleḵ kəḵol-haggôyim*
  (WLC). The elders ask for a king to do a judge's work, and the work their own judges had failed at. See
  [[Like All the Nations - Deuteronomy 17.14 and 1 Samuel 8.5, 20]].
- **The thing displeased Samuel.** *Wayyēraʿ haddāḇār bəʿênê šəmûʾēl* (6, WLC), literally *the thing was evil
  in Samuel's eyes*. It is the idiom of the judges cycle with Yahweh as the one who sees, *the children of
  Israel did that which was evil in Yahweh's sight* (Judges 2:11, stored WEB). Here the eyes are Samuel's.
  The narrator ties the displeasure to *Give us a king*, where the elders' words become *tənâ-lānû*, *give
  us*, rather than *śîmâ*, *set* (WLC). The whole clause, *wayyēraʿ haddāḇār … bəʿênê*, is the one used when
  Sarah asked for Hagar's son to be sent away: *the thing was very grievous in Abraham's sight* (Genesis
  21:11, WLC; stored WEB). See the next note.
- **Samuel prayed.** *Wayyiṯpallēl šəmûʾēl ʾel-YHWH* (6, WLC), the verb of his intercession at Mizpah (7:5).
  This time he prays about the people, not for them.
- **Listen to the voice of the people.** *Šəmaʿ bəqôl hāʿām* (7, WLC). Yahweh says it three times (7, 9, 22).
  The people *refused to listen to the voice of Samuel*, *wayməʾănû … lišmōaʿ bəqôl šəmûʾēl* (19, WLC). The
  chapter turns on who listens to whom. Genesis 21:11–12 has the same sequence: a request is *grievous in
  Abraham's sight*, and God answers *Don't let it be grievous in your sight … listen to her voice*, *ʾal-yēraʿ
  bəʿêneyḵā … šəmaʿ bəqōlāh* (WLC; stored WEB). In both, the one displeased is told by God to grant what
  displeases him. The parallel is of wording and pattern; the wiki does not claim the narrator intends it.
- **They have rejected me.** *Kî lōʾ ʾōṯəḵā māʾāsû kî-ʾōṯî māʾăsû mimməlōḵ ʿălêhem* (7, WLC), literally
  *they have rejected me from reigning over them*. The verb *mālaḵ*, *reign*, is not the *māšal*, *rule*, of
  Gideon's refusal, *Yahweh shall rule over you* (Judges 8:23, WLC): an agreement of idea, not of wording
  (see [[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]]). *Māʾas* with Yahweh as object is Moses'
  charge in the wilderness, *you have rejected Yahweh who is among you*, *məʾastem ʾeṯ-YHWH* (Numbers 11:20,
  WLC), and Samuel will say it again at Mizpah: *you have today rejected your God* (10:19, stored WEB; not yet
  ingested).
- **Since the day that I brought them up out of Egypt.** *Mîyyôm haʿălōṯî ʾōṯām mimmiṣrayim* (8, WLC). The
  charge has the shape of the indictments at Bochim and in Gideon's day; see
  [[I Brought You Up from Egypt - The Indictments at Bochim and in Gideon's Day]]. *Forsaken me, and served other
  gods* puts the request in a line with the idolatry Samuel had just had Israel put away (7:3–4).
- **Protest solemnly.** *Hāʿēḏ tāʿîḏ bāhem* (9, WLC), the doubled verb of witness. Samuel is to warn, not to
  refuse.
- **The way of the king.** *Mišpaṭ hammeleḵ* (9, 11, WLC). *Mišpāṭ* is the word Samuel's sons perverted (3).
  In 1 Samuel it also names *the custom of the priests with the people*, *mišpaṭ hakkōhănîm* (2:13, WLC), the
  practice by which Eli's sons took meat with a fork; and later *the regulations of the kingdom*, *mišpaṭ
  hammǝlūḵâ*, which Samuel writes in a book and lays up before Yahweh (10:25, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
  Read together, the chapter sets justice perverted, a priestly *custom* of taking, and a royal *way* of
  taking under one word. Whether 8:11–18 describes a right of the king or a practice he will claim is the
  question the word leaves open: *mišpāṭ* can mean either (lexical range, not verified here).
- **He will take.** *Yiqqāḥ* (11, 13, 14, 16, WLC), four times, and *yaʿśōr*, *he will tithe* (15, 17).
  The sons *took*, *wayyiqḥû*, bribes (3). In his farewell Samuel asks *Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey
  have I taken? … Of whose hand have I taken a ransom …?*, with *lāqaḥ* three times (12:3, stored WEB; WLC;
  not yet ingested). The donkeys appear in both lists (8:16; 12:3).
- **His chariots and his horsemen.** *Bəmerkaḇtô ûḇəp̄ārāšāyw* (11, WLC). The law of the king forbids him to
  *multiply horses* (Deuteronomy 17:16, stored WEB). Samuel's first item is the thing Deuteronomy withholds.
- **Captains of thousands, and captains of fifties.** *Śārê ʾălāp̄îm wəśārê ḥămiššîm* (12, WLC). The same
  ranks Jethro proposed for Moses' judges, *rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and
  rulers of tens*, *śārê ʾălāp̄îm … śārê ḥămiššîm* (Exodus 18:21, WLC), chosen for *hating unjust gain*. In
  1 Samuel 8 the offices serve the king's chariots.
- **Perfumers, cooks, and bakers.** *Raqqāḥôṯ* and *ṭabbāḥôṯ* (13) occur only here in the WLC files searched
  (lemmas 7548, 2879). The daughters go to the palace kitchen and its ointments.
- **The best.** *Haṭṭôḇîm* (14, 16, WLC), the best fields and vineyards and the best young men. The king's
  claim is on the first quality, not a share.
- **One tenth.** *Yaʿśōr* (15, 17, WLC). In the WLC files searched the verb *ʿāśar*, *take or give a tenth*,
  occurs only at Genesis 28:22 (Jacob's vow at Bethel), Deuteronomy 14:22 and 26:12 (Israel's tithe), and these
  two verses. Elsewhere in those files Israel gives a tenth to Yahweh; here the king takes it. See
  [[Tithing]] and [[A Fifth to Pharaoh - Joseph's Tax and the Rights of the King]].
- **His officers.** *Sārîsāyw* (15, WLC), the noun used of Potiphar, *an officer of Pharaoh's* (Genesis 37:36,
  stored WEB). It is often translated *eunuch* elsewhere; whether that sense is meant here is not stated.
- **You will be his servants.** *Wəʾattem tihyû-lô laʿăḇāḏîm* (17, WLC). The Egyptians said it of themselves
  to Joseph, *we will be Pharaoh's servants*, *wəhāyînû ʿăḇāḏîm ləp̄arʿōh* (Genesis 47:25, WLC). The people
  brought up out of the house of servants are told they will become servants again, to a king of their own.
  See [[Slavery and Servitude]].
- **Cry out, and no answer.** *Ûzəʿaqtem … wəlōʾ-yaʿăneh YHWH ʾeṯḵem* (18, WLC). At Mizpah, a chapter ago,
  *Samuel cried*, *wayyizʿaq*, *to Yahweh for Israel; and Yahweh answered him*, *wayyaʿănēhû* (7:9, WLC). The
  same two verbs, the second negated. The cry of the judges cycle, which Yahweh always answered with a
  deliverer (Judges 3:9, 15, stored WEB), is promised here to go unanswered when the oppressor is the king
  Israel chose.
- **Whom you will have chosen.** *ʾăšer bəḥartem lāḵem* (18, WLC). The law of the king says *whom Yahweh your God
  chooses*, *ʾăšer yiḇḥar YHWH ʾĕlōheyḵā bô* (Deuteronomy 17:15, WLC). The verb is the same and the subject is
  reversed. When the king is presented, Samuel will say both: *see the king whom you have chosen, and whom you
  have asked for. Behold, Yahweh has set a king over you* (12:13, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The people who asked him for a king.** *Hāʿām haššōʾălîm mēʾittô meleḵ* (10, WLC). *Šāʾal*, *ask*, the verb
  of Hannah's *I have asked him of Yahweh* (1:20), and the consonants of Saul's name; see
  [[Asked of Yahweh - Samuel's Name and Saul's]]. Later the people confess *we have added to all our sins this
  evil, to ask for a king* (12:19, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **No; but we will have a king.** *Lōʾ kî ʾim-meleḵ yihyeh ʿālênû* (19, WLC). Samuel will quote it back in
  12:12 with Nahash of Ammon as the occasion, *No, but a king shall reign over us; when Yahweh your God was your
  king* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The two accounts give different occasions for the same request; the
  text does not reconcile them, and the wiki will take this up at 12:12.
- **Go out before us, and fight our battles.** *Wəyāṣāʾ ləp̄ānênû wənilḥam ʾeṯ-milḥămōṯēnû* (20, WLC).
  Moses asked for a successor *who may go out before them, and who may come in before them*, *ʾăšer yēṣēʾ
  lip̄nêhem* (Numbers 27:17, WLC), so that Israel would not be *as sheep which have no shepherd*; see
  [[Sheep Without a Shepherd - Numbers 27.17, 1 Kings 22.17, and the Gospels]]. And the fighting had been
  Yahweh's: *Yahweh your God who goes before you, he will fight for you*, *hû yillāḥēm lāḵem* (Deuteronomy 1:30,
  WLC). At Mizpah, the chapter before, Yahweh fought with thunder (7:10). See [[War]].
- **He rehearsed them in the ears of Yahweh.** *Wayḏabbərēm bəʾozənê YHWH* (21, WLC). Samuel carries the
  people's words to Yahweh as he carried Yahweh's words to the people (10).
- **Everyone go to your own city.** *Ləḵû ʾîš ləʿîrô* (22, WLC). The assent is given and Samuel adjourns
  without acting on it. The chapter has no king at its end.

**How it connects.** Judges ended on *In those days there was no king in Israel* (Judges 21:25), and this is
the request that answers it. Deuteronomy 17:14–20 had already supplied the people's words, with the condition
that the king be the one Yahweh chooses ([[The Law for the King]];
[[Like All the Nations - Deuteronomy 17.14 and 1 Samuel 8.5, 20]]). Gideon had refused a crown with *Yahweh
shall rule over you*, and Jotham's fable had given kingship to the bramble
([[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]]). Samuel's list of what a king takes reads like
an abridgement of Joseph's measures in Egypt
([[A Fifth to Pharaoh - Joseph's Tax and the Rights of the King]]), and Solomon's levies and Ahab's seizure of
Naboth's vineyard are often read as its fulfilment (1 Kings 4:7; 9:22; 12:4; 21:1–16; not yet ingested, not
quoted). The chapter also closes the house of Samuel as it closed the house of Eli: two old judges, each with
sons who abuse the office, and each succession broken
([[Not in His Ways - The Sons of Eli and the Sons of Samuel]]). Within 1 Samuel the request reaches back to
Hannah's song, which sang *his king* before there was one (2:10), and forward to Saul, the *asked* king, who
appears in the next chapter (9:1–2, stored WEB; not yet ingested).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 8:1–22 as planned: 8:1 opens on Samuel's age, and 9:1 introduces Kish
and Saul in a new scene. New person pages for Joel and Abijah, Samuel's sons; Abijah is qualified because an
Abijah son of Jeroboam appears at 1 Kings 14:1, and Joel because Joel is also a book (the patronymic already
disambiguates it, so no `(person)` is needed). *All the elders of Israel* are not given a page, as in 4:3,
consistent with earlier ingests; the wiki has a page only for the [[Seventy Elders (of Moses' time)]]. New
connection [[Not in His Ways - The Sons of Eli and the Sons of Samuel]].

**Unresolved.** Whether Samuel's displeasure is personal, principled, or both; whether *mišpaṭ hammeleḵ* is a
right or a practice; how the occasion of 8:5 relates to 12:12's Nahash; and how 8:7's *rejected me* relates to
