# No Smith in Israel

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 13:16–23

## Summary

> **16** Saul, and Jonathan his son, and the people who were present with them, stayed in Geba of Benjamin; but
> the Philistines encamped in Michmash.
> **17** The raiders came out of the camp of the Philistines in three companies: one company turned to the way
> that leads to Ophrah, to the land of Shual;
> **18** another company turned the way to Beth Horon; and another company turned the way of the border that looks
> down on the valley of Zeboim toward the wilderness.
> **19** Now there was no blacksmith found throughout all the land of Israel; for the Philistines said, "Lest the
> Hebrews make themselves swords or spears";
> **20** but all the Israelites went down to the Philistines, each man to sharpen his own plowshare, mattock, ax,
> and sickle.
> **21** The price was one payim each to sharpen mattocks, plowshares, pitchforks, axes, and goads.
> **22** So it came to pass in the day of battle, that neither sword nor spear was found in the hand of any of the
> people who were with Saul and Jonathan; but Saul and Jonathan his son had them.
> **23** The garrison of the Philistines went out to the pass of Michmash.

The armies face each other across the pass. [[Saul]], [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]], and the six hundred men left
after Gilgal hold [[Geba]] of Benjamin; [[The Philistines]] are camped at [[Michmash]] (16). From the camp the
*raiders* go out in three companies: one on the road to [[Ophrah (Benjamin)]] and [[The Land of Shual]], one on the
road to [[Beth Horon]], and one on the road of the border overlooking [[The Valley of Zeboim]] toward the
wilderness (17–18). Nobody stops them.

Then the narrator pauses the story to explain the state of Israel's arms. No smith is to be found anywhere in the
land, because the Philistines have said *Lest the Hebrews make themselves swords or spears* (19). Every Israelite
goes down to the Philistines to have his plowshare, mattock, ax, and sickle sharpened (20), and the charge is
given: one *payim* each for mattocks, plowshares, pitchforks, axes, and the setting of goads (21). So on the day of
battle no sword or spear is found in the hand of any of the people with Saul and Jonathan; only Saul and Jonathan
have them (22). The last verse moves the scene again: the Philistine garrison goes out to the pass of Michmash
(23), where Jonathan will cross in the next chapter.

**Where it happens.** On the Benjamin plateau north of Jerusalem, with Israel at Geba and the Philistines at
Michmash on the far side of the pass (16, 23). The three raiding routes are named by destination. Beth Horon lies
on the western descent, already the road of Joshua's pursuit from Gibeon (Joshua 10:10–11, stored WEB). The
*wilderness* toward which the third company turns is most naturally the Judean wilderness falling to the Jordan
valley east of the plateau, and Ophrah is usually placed north of Michmash; neither placement is stated by the
text (the usual identifications, recalled, not checked). On that reading the three companies go north, west, and
east, every direction except back toward Israel's camp to the south.

**What is going on.** The pericope is a pause between two scenes. The previous one ended with Samuel gone, the
dynasty forfeited, and six hundred men counted (13:15). The next begins with Jonathan and his armour-bearer
crossing to the garrison (14:1, stored WEB; not yet ingested). Between them, the narrator does two things. He
shows the Philistines free to plunder: *hammašḥîṯ*, *the destroyer* (17, WLC), goes out in three companies and
meets no resistance. And he shows why: Israel has no smith, and therefore no weapons.

The Philistine policy is given in the Philistines' own words, *Lest the Hebrews make themselves swords or spears*
(19). The text presents it as a deliberate rule of the occupier, not as a technological accident. The cost of it is
told in farming tools, a list of implements and a price, in the detail of someone who has paid it. Then the
consequence: *in the day of battle* only the king and his son are armed (22). Israel goes to war against an army
*as the sand which is on the seashore* (13:5) with six hundred men and two swords.

The narrator does not say what metal is at issue. The word is *ḥārāš*, a craftsman or worker in any material, not
a word for iron. The Philistines' iron and Israel's lack of it is a common historical reading of the verse, set
beside the *chariots of iron* of Judges 1:19 and 4:3 and Goliath's spear-head of *six hundred shekels of iron*
(17:7) (stored WEB). The text of 13:19–22 names no metal. The archaeological claim that the Philistines held an iron
monopoly is commonly made and also commonly disputed (recalled, not checked). The wiki records the text: no
craftsman, sharpening done by the Philistines, and swords and spears forbidden.

The emptiness of Israel's hands prepares the battle that follows. In chapter 14 the victory begins with two men
and one armour-bearer's weapons, and *the earth quaked* (14:15, stored WEB). In chapter 17 a boy with a sling
says *Yahweh doesn't save with sword and spear* (17:47), and *there was no sword in the hand of David* (17:50)
(stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].

**What is notable.**

- **Found.** *Māṣāʾ* recurs five times in eight verses (WLC): *the people who were present* (*hannimṣəʾîm*, 15;
  *hannimṣāʾ*, 16), *no blacksmith found* (*lōʾ yimmāṣēʾ*, 19), *neither sword nor spear was found* (*lōʾ nimṣāʾ*,
  22), and *Saul and Jonathan his son had them* (*wattimmāṣēʾ*, literally *it was found with Saul*, 22). Saul's
  story began with a search, and its first good news was *they have been found* (*nimṣāʾû*, 9:20; also 10:16,
  WLC). Here the verb counts what is left: six hundred men, no smith, two swords.
- **The raiders.** *Hammašḥîṯ* (17, WLC), a participle of *šāḥaṯ*, *destroy*, used as a noun with the article. In
  the WLC of Genesis–2 Samuel the morphhb tagging reads it as a noun only at Exodus 12:23, *the destroyer*, and
  here and at 14:15 (*the raiders also trembled*, stored WEB); 2 Samuel 24:16 has the article with the participle
  for the destroying angel (WLC). The word in the Passover is Yahweh's agent; here it is the Philistine strike
  force. See [[The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night]].
- **Three companies.** *Šəlōšâ rāʾšîm* (17, WLC), literally *three heads*. The same tactical phrase is used of
  Gideon's three hundred (Judges 7:16), of Abimelech at Shechem (Judges 9:43), and of Saul's own army at Jabesh
  Gilead (1 Samuel 11:11) (stored WEB; WLC *rāʾšîm* each time). There Saul divided his force and *those who remained
  were scattered*. Now the enemy divides, and it is Israel's people who have scattered (13:8).
- **The land of Shual.** *ʾEreṣ šûʿāl* (17, WLC), *fox land* or *jackal land* (the gloss is the usual one, not
  checked against a lexicon). The name occurs only here in Genesis–2 Samuel (morphhb lemma 7777); *Hazar Shual*,
  *fox village*, in Judah's and Simeon's lists (Joshua 15:28; 19:3, stored WEB) is a different place. See
  [[The Land of Shual]].
- **The valley of Zeboim.** *Gêʾ haṣṣəḇōʿîm* (18, WLC), commonly glossed *valley of hyenas* (recalled, not checked).
  This is not [[Zeboiim]], *ṣəḇōyim*, the city overthrown with Sodom (Genesis 10:19; 14:2, 8; Deuteronomy 29:23,
  WLC); the two names differ in a consonant (*ʿ* against *y*) and morphhb gives them different lemmas (6650 and
  6636). See [[The Valley of Zeboim]].
- **No blacksmith.** *Ḥārāš* (19, WLC). The WEB's *blacksmith* specifies more than the Hebrew does. In Genesis–2
  Samuel *ḥārāš* is the *engraver* of the priest's stones and the tabernacle (Exodus 28:11; 35:35; 38:23), the
  *craftsman* of the idol in the curses of Ebal (Deuteronomy 27:15), and Hiram's carpenters and masons, *ḥārāšê ʿēṣ
  wəḥārāšê ʾeḇen* (2 Samuel 5:11, WLC; not yet stored). The occupiers forbid the trade, not one metal.
- **The Philistines said.** The written text (*kethiv*) is *ʾmr*, singular, and the read text (*qere*) *ʾāmrû*,
  plural (WLC). Either way the policy is quoted as Philistine speech, and it calls Israel *the Hebrews*, the
  Philistines' word for them throughout Samuel (4:6, 9; 14:11; 29:3, stored WEB). See [[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]]
  on *Let the Hebrews hear!* (13:3).
- **Swords or spears.** *Ḥereḇ ʾô ḥănîṯ* (19, WLC). The pair returns in 13:22, in David's *Yahweh doesn't save with
  sword and spear*, *lōʾ bəḥereḇ ûḇaḥănîṯ* (17:47), and in David's request to Ahimelech, *Isn't there here under your
  hand spear or sword?* (21:8 WEB; 21:9 WLC, *ḥănîṯ ʾô ḥāreḇ*) (stored WEB; not yet ingested). Deborah's song had
  asked *Was there a shield or spear seen among forty thousand in Israel?* (Judges 5:8, stored WEB), with a
  different word for spear, *rōmaḥ* (WLC).
- **To sharpen.** *Lilṭôš* (20, WLC). The verb *lāṭaš* occurs in Genesis–2 Samuel only here and at Genesis 4:22,
  where [[Tubal Cain]] is *the forger* (*lōṭēš*) *of every cutting instrument of brass and iron* (stored WEB; WLC).
  The first sharpener in Scripture was Cain's descendant; Israel's sharpeners are Philistines.
- **Plowshare, mattock, ax, and sickle.** *Maḥărašṯô wəʾeṯ-ʾēṯô wəʾeṯ-qardummô wəʾēṯ maḥărēšāṯô* (20, WLC). The
  first and last words are nearly the same (morphhb lemmas 4282 and 4281), and the WEB renders the second *sickle*.
  That the Septuagint reads a different fourth tool is commonly reported (recalled, not checked). Of the four, only
  *qardōm*, *ax*, occurs elsewhere in Genesis–2 Samuel: Abimelech's axe at Mount Zalmon (Judges 9:48, WLC).
- **One payim.** *Happəṣîrâ pîm* (21, WLC). *Pəṣîrâ*, the WEB's *price*, occurs only here in the WLC files
  consulted, and so do *qillešôn*, *pitchfork*, and *dārəḇān*, *goad* (morphhb lemmas 6477, 7053, 1861). *Pîm* is
  read by the WEB as a weight. That small stone weights inscribed *pym*, about two-thirds of a shekel, have been
  found in excavations, and that older translations, before those finds, rendered the verse differently, are
  commonly reported (recalled, not checked). morphhb tags the word under lemma 6310, *peh*, *mouth*; the wiki does
  not decide between the lexical analyses. The verse states the price precisely, and it is not small.
- **Goads.** *Ûləhaṣṣîḇ haddārəḇān* (21, WLC), *and to set the goad*, the fitting of the point. Shamgar *struck six
  hundred men of the Philistines with an ox goad* (Judges 3:31, stored WEB), with a different word, *malmad*
  (WLC). In Ecclesiastes *the words of the wise are like goads*, *kaddārəḇōnôṯ* (12:11, stored WEB; WLC), a form
  close to this one (not checked against a lexicon). The goad that once killed six hundred Philistines is now
  sharpened by them for a fee.
- **In the day of battle.** *Bəyôm milḥemeṯ* (22, WLC). The battle is the one of chapter 14, not yet begun. The
  verse is the narrator speaking ahead.
- **But Saul and Jonathan his son had them.** The two named men are armed. Jonathan's sword passes to David with
  his robe (18:4, stored WEB; not yet ingested); David later goes to Nob unarmed and is given Goliath's sword,
  *there is no other except that here* (21:9, stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The garrison … the pass.** *Maṣṣaḇ pəlištîm* (23, WLC), from the same root as *nəṣîḇ*, the garrison Jonathan
  struck at Geba (13:3; the roots are matched by form, and morphhb gives the nouns different lemmas, 4673 and
  5333). *Maṣṣāḇ* is elsewhere in Genesis–2 Samuel the place where the priests' feet *stood firm* in the Jordan
  (Joshua 4:3, 9), and the word for the Philistine post through 1 Samuel 14 (14:1, 4, 6, 11, 15) and at Bethlehem
  (2 Samuel 23:14) (WLC). *Maʿăḇar*, *pass*, occurs elsewhere in those files only at *the ford of the Jabbok*
  (Genesis 32:22 WEB; 32:23 WLC), and it is the noun of *ʿāḇar*, *cross over*, the verb the Hebrew puns on
  with *Hebrews* in 13:7.

**How it connects.** The pericope closes chapter 13 and sets up [[Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal]]'s unfought battle.
Saul's army has shrunk from three thousand to six hundred; here it turns out to be unarmed as well. The scene of
helplessness under the Philistines continues the oppression the book opened with at Aphek, where the ark was taken
(4:1–11), and the commission given at Saul's anointing, *he will save my people out of the hand of the
Philistines* (9:16) (stored WEB). Judges had already recorded a time when *there was not a shield or spear seen*
(5:8), and a judge who saved Israel with an ox goad (3:31). The next pericope, Jonathan's attack at Michmash, is
fought with exactly the weapons this one counts.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 13:16–23 as planned. Verse 23 is kept here rather than with chapter 14: it
places the garrison at the pass, and 14:1 begins a new movement with Jonathan. Two new place pages:
[[The Land of Shual]] (named so it cannot collide with [[Hazar Shual]] or a later person Shual) and
[[The Valley of Zeboim]] (kept distinct from [[Zeboiim]]). *The wilderness* of 13:18 is not linked, because the
text does not name which wilderness. *The Hebrews* get no page, as in the previous pericope. One new connection:
[[Yahweh Doesn't Save with Sword and Spear - 1 Samuel 13.22, 17.47, and Judges 5.8]].

**Unresolved.** What *pîm* is lexically, and the exact sense of *pəṣîrâ*. What the fourth tool of verse 20 is.
Whether the policy of verse 19 concerns iron in particular. The location of Ophrah, the land of Shual, and the
valley of Zeboim. Whether *Geba* in verse 16 is the same post as *Gibeah* in 13:2, 15 and 14:2, the confusion
already recorded on [[Geba]].
