# Samuel Judges Israel at Mizpah

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 7:2–17

## Summary

> **7:2** From the day that the ark stayed in Kiriath Jearim, the time was long; for it was twenty years;
> and all the house of Israel lamented after Yahweh.
> **3** Samuel spoke to all the house of Israel, saying, "If you are returning to Yahweh with all your
> heart, then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth from among you, and direct your hearts to Yahweh,
> and serve him only; and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines."
> **4** Then the children of Israel removed the Baals and the Ashtaroth, and served Yahweh only.
> **5** Samuel said, "Gather all Israel to Mizpah, and I will pray to Yahweh for you."
> **6** They gathered together to Mizpah, and drew water, and poured it out before Yahweh, and fasted on that
> day, and said there, "We have sinned against Yahweh." Samuel judged the children of Israel in Mizpah.
> **7** When the Philistines heard that the children of Israel were gathered together at Mizpah, the lords
> of the Philistines went up against Israel. When the children of Israel heard it, they were afraid of the
> Philistines.
> **8** The children of Israel said to Samuel, "Don't stop crying to Yahweh our God for us, that he will
> save us out of the hand of the Philistines."
> **9** Samuel took a suckling lamb, and offered it for a whole burnt offering to Yahweh. Samuel cried to
> Yahweh for Israel; and Yahweh answered him.
> **10** As Samuel was offering up the burnt offering, the Philistines came near to battle against Israel;
> but Yahweh thundered with a great thunder on that day on the Philistines, and confused them; and they
> were struck down before Israel.
> **11** The men of Israel went out of Mizpah, and pursued the Philistines, and struck them, until they
> came under Beth Kar.
> **12** Then Samuel took a stone, and set it between Mizpah and Shen, and called its name Ebenezer, saying,
> "Yahweh helped us until now."
> **13** So the Philistines were subdued, and they stopped coming within the border of Israel. Yahweh's
> hand was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel.
> **14** The cities which the Philistines had taken from Israel were restored to Israel, from Ekron even to
> Gath; and Israel recovered its border out of the hand of the Philistines. There was peace between Israel
> and the Amorites.
> **15** Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life.
> **16** He went from year to year in a circuit to Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpah; and he judged Israel in all
> those places.
> **17** His return was to Ramah, for his house was there; and he judged Israel there; and he built an altar
> to Yahweh there.

The ark stays at [[Kiriath Jearim]] *twenty years*, and *all the house of Israel lamented after Yahweh* (7:2).
[[Samuel]], silent since 4:1a, speaks to all Israel: if they are returning to Yahweh *with all your heart*,
they must put away *the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth* and *serve him only*, and he will deliver them from
[[The Philistines]] (3). They remove *the Baals and the Ashtaroth* (4). Samuel gathers all Israel to
[[Mizpeh]] to pray for them; they draw water and pour it out before Yahweh, fast, and confess *We have sinned
against Yahweh*, and *Samuel judged the children of Israel in Mizpah* (5–6). The Philistine lords hear of the
gathering and come up; Israel is afraid and begs Samuel not to stop *crying to Yahweh* (7–8). Samuel offers
a suckling lamb as a whole burnt offering and cries to Yahweh, *and Yahweh answered him* (9). While the
offering is still going up, the Philistines close in, and *Yahweh thundered with a great thunder* and
confused them; they are struck down, and Israel pursues them from Mizpah to below [[Beth Kar]] (10–11).
Samuel sets up a stone between Mizpah and [[Shen]] and names it [[Ebenezer]], *Yahweh helped us until now*
(12). The Philistines are *subdued* and stop entering Israel's border; *Yahweh's hand was against the
Philistines all the days of Samuel* (13). Towns from [[Ekron]] to [[Gath]] are restored, and there is peace
with [[The Amorites]] (14). A closing notice: Samuel judges Israel all his life, on a yearly circuit of
[[Bethel]], [[Gilgal]], and Mizpah, returning to his house at [[Ramah]], where he builds an altar (15–17).

**What is going on.** This is the first time since the call that Samuel acts, and the chapter shows him in
every office the book gives him at once: prophet (he speaks a conditional word, 3), intercessor (*I will pray
to Yahweh for you*, 5; he *cried to Yahweh for Israel*, 9), priest (he offers the burnt offering, 9–10), and
judge (*Samuel judged*, 6, 15–17). It is told as a judges cycle and completes one. Israel has served *the
Baals and the Ashtaroth* (Judges 2:13; 10:6), lives under the Philistines, cries out, confesses, puts away the
gods, and is delivered; the enemy is *subdued*; and the deliverer *judged Israel all the days of his life*.
What is missing is a sword in Samuel's hand. The battle is a prayer and a sacrifice, the victory is thunder,
and Israel's army only pursues. Set against chapter 4 the reversal is exact: there Israel fought, lost, and
sent for the ark as a weapon (4:3); here Israel repents, Samuel prays, the ark stays where it is, and Israel
wins. The stone that names the victory, *Ebenezer*, has the name of the camp of the defeat (4:1).

The pericope also ends a period. Its last verses read like the closing notice of a judge's career, and 8:1
opens on Samuel *old*. The next chapter's demand for a king is made against this background: the deliverance
the people will ask a king to bring (8:20) has just been given without one.

**What is notable.**

- **Twenty years.** *Wayyirbû hayyāmîm wayyihyû ʿeśrîm šānâ* (2, WLC), *the days were many, and they were
  twenty years*. The ark stays at Kiriath Jearim until David moves it (2 Samuel 6:2–3), which the book's own
  chronology places well after twenty years; the *twenty years* is therefore usually read as the interval
  before the assembly at Mizpah, not the ark's whole stay. That is a reading; the verse does not say which.
- **Lamented after Yahweh.** *Wayyinnāhû kol-bêṯ yiśrāʾēl ʾaḥărê YHWH* (2, WLC). The verb *nāhâ* (lemma 5091)
  occurs only here in the WLC files searched (Genesis through 2 Kings, Ruth, Psalms). The WEB's *lamented* is
  the usual sense of the root; the phrase *after Yahweh* is unusual and is read as *longing after*, *mourning
  for*, or *turning to follow*. The wiki gives the WEB and does not decide.
- **Returning with all your heart.** *ʾIm-bəḵol-ləḇaḇḵem ʾattem šāḇîm ʾel-YHWH* (3, WLC), the language of
  Deuteronomy's promise of return, *if you turn to Yahweh your God with all your heart*, *kî ṯāšûḇ ʾel-YHWH
  ʾĕlōheyḵā bəḵol-ləḇāḇəḵā* (Deuteronomy 30:10, stored WEB; WLC). Samuel's *if* tests the lament: it must
  become an act.
- **Put away the foreign gods.** *Hāsîrû ʾeṯ-ʾĕlōhê hannēḵār mittôḵəḵem* (3, WLC), the order Jacob gave at
  Shechem, *hāsirû ʾeṯ-ʾĕlōhê hannēḵār ʾăšer bəṯōḵəḵem* (Genesis 35:2, WLC), down to the noun *tāweḵ*, *midst*.
  Joshua repeated it (Joshua 24:23), and Israel under Ammon carried it out (Judges 10:16). See
  [[Put Away the Foreign Gods - Genesis 35.2-4 and Joshua 24.14-23]].
- **The Baals and the Ashtaroth.** *Habbəʿālîm wəʾeṯ-hāʿaštārōṯ* (4, WLC). In the WLC files searched the plural
  *ʿaštārôṯ* names the goddesses at Judges 2:13; 10:6; 1 Samuel 7:3, 4; 12:10; 31:10, and is otherwise the
  town *Ashtaroth* of Og (Deuteronomy 1:4 and Joshua) (lemma 6252, which covers both). Samuel's summary in
  12:10 puts the same pair in the people's own confession (stored WEB; not yet ingested). See
  [[Baal (deity)]] and [[Ashtoreth (deity)]].
- **Serve him only.** *Wəʿiḇḏuhû ləḇaddô* (3) and *wayyaʿaḇḏû ʾeṯ-YHWH ləḇaddô* (4, WLC). The order and its
  keeping are given in the same words, one verse apart.
- **I will pray for you.** *Wəʾeṯpallēl baʿaḏḵem ʾel-YHWH* (5, WLC). Eli had asked *who will intercede for
  him?* with the same verb, *pālal* (2:25); Samuel is the answer to the question in this pericope, and calls it
  sin to stop (12:23, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].
- **Drew water, and poured it out before Yahweh.** *Wayyišʾăḇû-mayim wayyišpəḵû lip̄nê YHWH* (6, WLC). No law
  in the stored books prescribes such a rite, and the text does not explain it. Readings include a sign of
  repentance (the heart *poured out*), a libation, or self-denial alongside the fast; none is stated.
  Hannah used the same verb and phrase of her prayer at Shiloh: *I poured out my soul before Yahweh*,
  *wāʾešpōḵ ʾeṯ-nap̄šî lip̄nê YHWH* (1:15, WLC). David, later, will not drink water drawn at Bethlehem and pours
  it out *to Yahweh* (2 Samuel 23:16, WEB checked at bible-api.com), with *šāʾaḇ*, *draw*, as here, but a
  different verb for *pour* (*nāsaḵ*, WLC).
- **Fasted.** *Wayyāṣûmû* (6, WLC). In the WLC files searched the verb *ṣûm* first occurs at Judges 20:26, the
  fast at Bethel before the last battle with Benjamin; this is its second occurrence. See
  [[The Place of Weepers - Allon Bacuth, Bochim, and Bethel]].
- **We have sinned.** *Ḥāṭāʾnû laYHWH* (6, WLC), the confession of Judges 10:10, 15 (*ḥāṭāʾnû lāḵ*, WLC), and
  of the people Samuel quotes in 12:10.
- **Samuel judged.** *Wayyišpōṭ šəmûʾēl* (6, WLC). The first time *šāp̄aṭ* is used of Samuel. The last judge
  named before him was Eli, who *judged Israel forty years* (4:18).
- **Don't stop crying.** *ʾAl-taḥărēš mimmennû mizzəʿōq* (8, WLC), literally *don't be silent from us from
  crying out*. *Zāʿaq* is the cry of the judges cycle (Judges 3:9, 15; 6:6; 10:10, stored WEB); here the people
  ask Samuel to cry *for* them, and *Samuel cried to Yahweh for Israel* (9).
- **A suckling lamb, a whole burnt offering.** *Ṭəlēh ḥālāḇ ʾeḥāḏ … ʿôlâ kālîl* (9, WLC). *Ṭāleh*, *lamb*
  (lemma 2924), occurs only here in the WLC files searched. *Kālîl*, *whole*, names the offering Levi puts on
  Yahweh's altar in Moses' blessing (Deuteronomy 33:10, stored WEB). The ketiv *wyʿlh* stands beside the qere
  *wayyaʿălēhû* (WLC). Samuel offers in person. 1 Samuel 1:1 traces his father's line in Ephraim; 1 Chronicles
  6:27–28 names Elkanah and *the sons of Samuel* in a list the wiki has elsewhere read as Levitical (WEB checked
  at bible-api.com; the Chronicles context not examined in this pass). See
  [[Elkanah (father of Samuel)]] for that question; the narrator here says nothing about it.
- **Yahweh answered him.** *Wayyaʿănēhû YHWH* (9, WLC). The answer is the thunder of the next verse.
- **Thunder.** *Wayyarʿēm YHWH bəqôl-gāḏôl … ʿal-pəlištîm* (10, WLC). Hannah sang *He will thunder against
  them in the sky*, *ʿālāw baššāmayim yarʿēm* (2:10, WLC). In the WLC files searched the verb *rāʿam* occurs in
  1 Samuel only at 1:6, 2:10, and here. See [[He Will Thunder Against Them - Hannah's Song and Mizpah]].
- **And confused them.** *Wayhummēm* (10, WLC), *hāmam*, the verb of the Sea and of Gibeon and Kishon (Exodus
  14:24; Joshua 10:10; Judges 4:15). See [[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]].
- **Struck down before Israel.** *Wayyinnāḡəp̄û lip̄nê yiśrāʾēl* (10, WLC), the verb and phrase of 4:2,
  *wayyinnāḡep̄ yiśrāʾēl lip̄nê pəlištîm*, *Israel was struck before the Philistines*, and the verb of 4:10
  (WLC). The defeat is returned in the same words.
- **Beth Kar and Shen.** Both names occur only here in the WLC files searched (lemmas 1033, 8129). Neither is
  located; see [[Beth Kar]] and [[Shen]].
- **Ebenezer.** *Wayyiqrāʾ ʾeṯ-šəmāh ʾeḇen hāʿāzer wayyōʾmar ʿaḏ-hēnnâ ʿăzārānû YHWH* (12, WLC). The name is
  explained here for the first time, though the book used it at 4:1 and 5:1 for the camp of the defeat. Whether
  the two are one place, the narrator naming the earlier site by its later name, is not settled; see
  [[Ebenezer]]. *Until now*, *ʿaḏ-hēnnâ*, can be read of time or of place (*thus far*). The stone joins the
  memorial stones of Jacob, Joshua, and the Jordan crossing; see
  [[The Pillar Jacob Raised and the Pillars the Law Forbids]].
- **Subdued.** *Wayyikkānəʿû happəlištîm* (13, WLC), the verb that closes the deliverances of Ehud, Deborah,
  Gideon, and Jephthah (Judges 3:30; 4:23; 8:28; 11:33) and was never used of the Philistines in Judges. See
  [[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].
- **All the days of Samuel.** *Wattəhî yaḏ-YHWH bappəlištîm kōl yəmê šəmûʾēl* (13, WLC), the formula of the
  judges: *Yahweh was with the judge, and saved them out of the hand of their enemies all the days of the
  judge*, *kōl yəmê haššôp̄ēṭ* (Judges 2:18, WLC). The *hand* that was *heavy* on Ashdod (5:6) is now *against*
  the Philistines in Israel's own land. Deuteronomy 2:15 has both phrases together, against Israel's own fighting
  men: *Yahweh's hand was against them, to destroy them*, *yaḏ-YHWH hāyəṯâ bām ləhummām* (WLC).
- **The tension with chapters 9–13 — flagged.** Verse 13 says the Philistines *stopped coming within the border
  of Israel … all the days of Samuel*. Samuel is alive through chapter 25, and before then there is a
  *garrison of the Philistines* at the hill of God (10:5), Yahweh sends Saul to *save my people out of the hand
  of the Philistines* (9:16), Jonathan strikes a Philistine garrison at Geba, and the Philistines muster at
  Michmash (13:3–5) (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The summary and the later chapters are not reconciled in the
  text. Readings take 13 as a summary of Samuel's own judgeship before the monarchy, take *the border of Israel*
  as the recovered lowland towns of 14, or treat the summary as idealizing. The wiki records the tension and
  will revisit it at 9:16 and 13:3–5.
- **From Ekron even to Gath.** *Mēʿeqrôn wəʿaḏ-gaṯ* (14, WLC), the last two cities the ark passed through
  (5:8–10). Read as a border line, it is the edge of the Philistine plain, not the cities themselves; the text
  does not say whether Ekron and Gath were taken.
- **Peace with the Amorites.** *Wayhî šālôm bên yiśrāʾēl ûḇên hāʾĕmōrî* (14, WLC). *The Amorite* here is
  usually read as the remaining non-Israelite population of the land, like the Amorites who held Aijalon and
  Shaalbim against Dan (Judges 1:34–36), rather than a distinct kingdom. Peace with them is reported, not explained.
- **A circuit.** *Wəhālaḵ middê šānâ bəšānâ wəsāḇaḇ* (16, WLC), *year by year, and went around*. *Šānâ
  bəšānâ* and *middê* stand together, in another order, in the verse on Elkanah's household going up to
  Shiloh: *šānâ ḇəšānâ middê ʿălōṯāh* (1:7, WLC). The three towns all
  have earlier stories: Bethel, where the ark stood in the war with Benjamin (Judges 20:26–27); Gilgal, Israel's
  first camp across the Jordan (Joshua 4:19); Mizpah, the assembly against Benjamin (Judges 20:1). Shiloh is
  not on the list. See [[Between Ramah and Bethel - Deborah and Samuel as Judges]].
- **His return was to Ramah.** *Ûṯəšuḇāṯô hārāmāṯâ* (17, WLC). *Təšûḇâ* elsewhere in the files searched
  is *the return of the year* (2 Samuel 11:1; 1 Kings 20:22, 26), the season of war. Samuel's house is in the
  town of his birth (1:1, 19), and he will be buried there (25:1, stored WEB).
- **An altar at Ramah.** *Wayyiḇen-šām mizbēaḥ laYHWH* (17, WLC). The ark is at Kiriath Jearim and Shiloh is
  not mentioned; Samuel builds his own altar. The narrator neither commends nor questions it, as with the
  sacrifices of Beth Shemesh (6:14–15) and Deuteronomy 12:13–14's restriction to *the place which Yahweh
  chooses* (stored WEB).

**How it connects.** The pericope closes the ark narrative's frame. It began with Samuel's word coming to
all Israel (4:1a) and Israel losing at Ebenezer without him; it ends with Samuel praying and Israel winning
at a new Ebenezer, the ark still at Kiriath Jearim. It closes the Philistine thread Judges left open: Samson
was to *begin to save* (Judges 13:5) and *judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines* (15:20);
under Samuel they are *subdued* (see
[[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]]). It fulfils one line of
[[The Song of Hannah]], the thunder of 2:10 (see [[He Will Thunder Against Them - Hannah's Song and Mizpah]]),
and gives Eli's question about intercession (2:25) its first answer. And it presents Samuel as the last of the
judges in the shape Judges 2:16–19 set out, judging, like Deborah, between Ramah and Bethel (see
[[Between Ramah and Bethel - Deborah and Samuel as Judges]]). The next chapter opens with his sons as judges
and the elders at Ramah asking for a king (8:1–5, stored WEB; not yet ingested).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 7:2–17 as planned: 7:2 opens with the *twenty years* and a new subject,
and 8:1 opens a new scene, *When Samuel was old*. New place pages for Beth Kar and Shen, which occur only
here; both get pages because each marks a point in the battle's geography. Mizpah is filed on [[Mizpeh]],
Benjamin's town, where the wiki already filed the Mizpah of Judges 20. The text does not name the tribe; the
identification follows the circuit with Bethel and Gilgal. Samuel's Ramah stays on [[Ramah]], with the
question already flagged there. The lords of the Philistines stay under [[The Philistines]]. The Amorites of
7:14 are recorded on [[The Amorites]].

**Unresolved.** What the *twenty years* measures; what *lamented after Yahweh* means; the meaning of the water
poured out; whether Samuel's sacrifice implies Levitical descent; whether Ebenezer here is the site of 4:1; and
how 7:13 relates to the Philistine wars of chapters 9–14. Each is reported, not settled.
