# Saul's Sacrifice at Gilgal

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 13:1–15

## Summary

> **1** Saul was thirty years old when he became king, and he reigned over Israel forty-two years.
> **2** Saul chose for himself three thousand men of Israel, of which two thousand were with Saul in Michmash and
> in the Mount of Bethel, and one thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin. He sent the rest of the people
> to their own tents.
> **3** Jonathan struck the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it. Saul
> blew the trumpet throughout all the land, saying, "Let the Hebrews hear!"
> **4** All Israel heard that Saul had struck the garrison of the Philistines, and also that Israel was considered
> an abomination to the Philistines. The people were gathered together after Saul to Gilgal.
> **5** The Philistines assembled themselves together to fight with Israel, thirty thousand chariots, and six
> thousand horsemen, and people as the sand which is on the seashore in multitude. They came up and encamped in
> Michmash, eastward of Beth Aven.
> **6** When the men of Israel saw that they were in trouble (for the people were distressed), then the people hid
> themselves in caves, in thickets, in rocks, in tombs, and in pits.
> **7** Now some of the Hebrews had gone over the Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead; but as for Saul, he was yet
> in Gilgal, and all the people followed him trembling.
> **8** He stayed seven days, according to the time set by Samuel; but Samuel didn't come to Gilgal, and the people
> were scattering from him.
> **9** Saul said, "Bring the burnt offering to me here, and the peace offerings." He offered the burnt offering.
> **10** It came to pass that as soon as he had finished offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came; and Saul
> went out to meet him, that he might greet him.
> **11** Samuel said, "What have you done?" Saul said, "Because I saw that the people were scattered from me, and
> that you didn't come within the days appointed, and that the Philistines assembled themselves together at
> Michmash;
> **12** therefore I said, 'Now the Philistines will come down on me to Gilgal, and I haven't entreated the favor of
> Yahweh.' I forced myself therefore, and offered the burnt offering."
> **13** Samuel said to Saul, "You have done foolishly. You have not kept the commandment of Yahweh your God, which
> he commanded you; for now Yahweh would have established your kingdom on Israel forever.
> **14** But now your kingdom will not continue. Yahweh has sought for himself a man after his own heart, and Yahweh
> has appointed him to be prince over his people, because you have not kept that which Yahweh commanded you."
> **15** Samuel arose, and went from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin. Saul counted the people who were present with
> him, about six hundred men.

The account of [[Saul]]'s reign opens with a regnal formula (1) and a standing army: three thousand chosen men, two
thousand with Saul at [[Michmash]] and the hill country of [[Bethel]], one thousand with
[[Jonathan (son of Saul)]], named here for the first time, at [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]]; the rest are sent home (2).
Jonathan strikes the Philistine *garrison* at [[Geba]]. Saul has the trumpet blown through the land, *Let the
Hebrews hear!*, and all Israel hears that *Saul* has struck the garrison and that Israel now *stinks* to the
Philistines. The people are called out after Saul to [[Gilgal]] (3–4).

[[The Philistines]] muster thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and foot soldiers *as the sand which is
on the seashore*, and camp at Michmash, *eastward of* [[Beth Aven]] (5), in the position Saul had held. Israel's
men see they are *in trouble*. They hide in caves, thickets, rocks, tombs, and pits, and some cross [[The Jordan]]
to the land of [[Gad]] and [[Gilead]]. Saul is still at Gilgal, and the people with him *followed him trembling*
(6–7).

He waits *seven days, according to the time set by* [[Samuel]]. Samuel does not come, and the people are scattering
(8). Saul calls for the burnt offering and the peace offerings and offers the burnt offering (9). As soon as he has
finished, Samuel arrives, and Saul goes out *to greet him*, literally *to bless him* (10). Samuel asks *What have
you done?* Saul gives three reasons: the people were scattering, Samuel had not come *within the days appointed*,
and the Philistines were gathering at Michmash. He feared they would come down on him at Gilgal before he had
*entreated the favor of Yahweh*, so *I forced myself, and offered the burnt offering* (11–12). Samuel answers *You
have done foolishly*. Saul has not kept *the commandment of Yahweh your God*, and if he had, Yahweh would have
established his kingdom over Israel *forever*. *But now your kingdom will not continue.* Yahweh has sought *a man
after his own heart* and appointed him *prince over his people* (13–14). Samuel leaves Gilgal for Gibeah of
Benjamin. Saul counts the people with him, about six hundred men (15).

**Where it happens.** At Gilgal, in the Jordan valley, with the Philistines on the ridge at Michmash and Israel's
garrison at Gibeah and Geba across the pass. The text sets the Philistine camp *eastward of Beth Aven* (5). Which
Gilgal is meant is not stated; the wiki files it with the Gilgal of Joshua 4 and 1 Samuel 10:8 and 11:14–15, as
the earlier pericopes did.

**What is going on.** The order Samuel gave at the anointing comes due here. *Go down ahead of me to Gilgal; and
behold, I will come down to you, to offer burnt offerings, and to sacrifice sacrifices of peace offerings. Wait
seven days, until I come to you, and show you what you are to do* (10:8). The verb of *wait* there, *tôḥēl*, is the
verb of *He stayed* here, *wayyôḥel* in the read text (10:8; 13:8, WLC). The narrator times the scene exactly to
that instruction: seven days, *the time set by Samuel*, the burnt offering and the peace offerings, and Samuel
arriving *as soon as he had finished*. Saul has the order nearly right. He waits the seven days, and on the
seventh he does what Samuel had said Samuel would do.

The text does not say what the wrong was. Samuel's charge is that Saul *has not kept the commandment … which he
commanded you*, twice (13, 14), and the commandment in view is most naturally 10:8, *until I come to you*. Two
readings are common. On one, the fault is impatience: Saul acted before the prophet came, and so took the war out
of the hands of the word that was to *show you what you are to do*. On the other, the fault is that a king, not a
priest, offered the sacrifice. The second reading meets a difficulty in the stored text: Samuel, an Ephraimite by
1:1, offered the suckling lamb at Mizpah and built an altar at Ramah (7:9, 17), and the narrative does not fault
him for it; David and Solomon are later said to offer sacrifices (2 Samuel 6:17–18, not yet stored; 1 Kings
3:4, not yet stored; both recalled, not checked). The first reading is closer to what Samuel actually says. The
wiki records both and the text's own words, which name the commandment and not the altar. See
[[Priesthood]].

The sentence is out of proportion to the offence on any reading, and the passage does not soften that. What
Samuel weighs is not the sacrifice but the kingdom: *now Yahweh would have established your kingdom … forever.
But now your kingdom will not continue*. *Mamlaḵtəḵā*, *your kingdom* (13–14, WLC), is a dynasty, and the word
*forever*, *ʿaḏ-ʿôlām*, is the word Nathan will use for David's house, *Your house and your kingdom will be made
sure forever before you* (2 Samuel 7:16, WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-18; not stored; WLC *ûmamlaḵtəḵā
ʿaḏ-ʿôlām*). Saul himself goes on reigning; the next two chapters make him a war leader at Michmash and against
Amalek. What is lost here is the house. The full rejection of Saul *from being king* comes in chapter 15 (15:23,
26, stored WEB; not yet ingested), and the book will tell it a second time.

The battle the passage sets up is not fought in it. Samuel leaves, Saul counts six hundred men, and the
Philistine raiders go out from Michmash (13:16–18, the next pericope). Jonathan fights the battle in chapter 14.

**What is notable.**

- **Saul was thirty years old … and he reigned … forty-two years.** The WLC reads *ben-šānâ šāʾûl bəmolḵô
  ûštê šānîm mālaḵ ʿal-yiśrāʾēl* (1), literally *Saul was a son of a year in his reigning, and two years he reigned
  over Israel*. The words are the regnal formula used of Ish-bosheth and David, *ben-ʾarbāʿîm šānâ … bəmolḵô* and
  *ben-šəlōšîm šānâ dāwiḏ bəmolḵô* (2 Samuel 2:10; 5:4, WLC), with the numbers wanting: no tens before *a year*,
  and *two years* for the reign. The WEB's *thirty* and *forty-two* are supplied, not in the Hebrew. That the
  Septuagint's best witnesses omit the verse and that *thirty* comes from later Greek manuscripts is commonly
  reported (recalled, not checked; no Septuagint consulted). Paul's speech at Pisidian Antioch gives Saul *forty
  years* (Acts 13:21, WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-18; not stored). The wiki records the Hebrew as it
  stands and does not choose numbers.
- **Jonathan.** *Yônāṯān*, *Yahweh has given* (the gloss is the usual one, not checked against a lexicon). He is
  introduced without a patronymic; 13:16 calls him *Jonathan his son*. He strikes the garrison, and verse 4 credits
  the blow to Saul. The text does not comment on the change. See [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]].
- **The garrison of the Philistines.** *Nəṣîḇ pəlištîm* (3, WLC), the word of *the garrison of the Philistines*
  at the hill of God where Saul was to prophesy (10:5, *nəṣiḇê*, WLC). Lemma 5333 occurs in the WLC of
  Genesis–2 Samuel only at Genesis 19:26, Lot's wife's *pillar of salt*, at 1 Samuel 10:5 and 13:3–4, and for
  David's garrisons in Aram and Edom (2 Samuel 8:6, 14). Whether *nəṣîḇ* is a garrison, an officer, or a pillar
  marking Philistine control is debated (recalled, not checked).
- **Let the Hebrews hear!** *Yišməʿû hāʿiḇrîm* (3, WLC). *Hebrews* in Samuel is mostly the Philistines' word for
  Israel (4:6, 9; 13:19; 14:11; 29:3, stored WEB), and Saul uses it here in his own proclamation. In verse 7 it is
  a pun: *wəʿiḇrîm ʿāḇərû ʾeṯ-hayyardēn*, *Hebrews crossed the Jordan* (WLC), the noun and the verb *ʿāḇar* side by
  side. 14:21 will distinguish *the Hebrews who were with the Philistines* from *the Israelites who were with Saul*.
  Whether the word names Israelites or a wider class, the text here does not settle.
- **Israel was considered an abomination to the Philistines.** *Niḇʾaš yiśrāʾēl bappəlištîm* (4, WLC),
  literally *Israel stank among the Philistines*. *Bāʾaš* is Jacob's word to Simeon and Levi, *to make me odious
  to the inhabitants of the land* (Genesis 34:30), and the foremen's to Moses and Aaron, *you have made us a stench
  … in the eyes of Pharaoh* (Exodus 5:21) (stored WEB; WLC *ləhaḇʾîšēnî*, *hiḇʾaštem*). In both, the stench is
  provoked by an act of violence or defiance and brings worse oppression. The Niphal form here recurs in these files
  for the Ammonites and for Absalom (2 Samuel 10:6; 16:21, WLC; not yet stored).
- **As the sand which is on the seashore.** *Kaḥôl ʾăšer ʿal-śəp̄aṯ-hayyām lārōḇ* (5, WLC), the phrase of the
  northern kings at the waters of Merom (Joshua 11:4) and of Midian's camels (Judges 7:12) (stored WEB). Both
  enemies were defeated with Israel outnumbered, by Joshua and by Gideon's three hundred. The Hebrew number of
  chariots is *šəlōšîm ʾelep̄*, *thirty thousand* (WLC); some Greek and Syriac witnesses are commonly reported to read
  three thousand (recalled, not checked).
- **Caves, thickets, rocks, tombs, and pits.** Five hiding places (6). Under Midian Israel *made themselves the dens
  which are in the mountains, and the caves, and the strongholds* (Judges 6:2, stored WEB), with the same noun for
  *caves*, *məʿārôṯ* (WLC). The Philistines will mock *the Hebrews … coming out of the holes where they had hidden
  themselves* (14:11, stored WEB). Saul himself had *hidden himself among the baggage* (10:22), with the same verb,
  *ḥāḇāʾ* (WLC).
- **Followed him trembling.** *Ḥārəḏû ʾaḥărāyw* (7, WLC). After Jabesh Gilead the people came out *as one man*
  under *the dread of Yahweh* (11:7). Here the people are trembling, and they are not coming out but scattering
  away from him.
- **He stayed seven days, according to the time set by Samuel.** The written text (*kethiv*) is *wyyḥl*, read as a
  Niphal, and the read text (*qere*) is *wayyôḥel*, a Hiphil, the binyan of *tôḥēl*, *wait*, at 10:8 (WLC prints
  both). Both are forms of *yāḥal*. The clause *lammôʿēḏ ʾăšer šəmûʾēl* lacks a verb, literally *to the appointed
  time which Samuel*; the WEB supplies *set*. *Môʿēḏ*, *appointed time*, returns in Saul's defence, *within the
  days appointed*, *ləmôʿēḏ hayyāmîm* (11, WLC).
- **Bring the burnt offering to me here.** *Haggišû ʾēlay hāʿōlâ wəhaššəlāmîm* (9, WLC). Both offerings of 10:8
  are named; only the burnt offering is offered before Samuel arrives. The text does not say whether a priest was
  present. Ahijah, *wearing an ephod*, is with Saul in 14:3 (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **To greet him.** *Ləḇārăḵô* (10, WLC), literally *to bless him*, the ordinary verb of greeting; the king goes
  out to bless the prophet.
- **What have you done?** *Meh ʿāśîṯā* (11, WLC), the words Yahweh says to Cain (Genesis 4:10) and Laban to Jacob
  (Genesis 31:26) (stored WEB; WLC *meh ʿāśîṯā* in both); to the woman in the garden it is *What is this you have
  done?* (Genesis 3:13, *mah-zōʾṯ ʿāśîṯ*, WLC). Each time the question is asked by someone who already knows the
  answer.
- **I haven't entreated the favor of Yahweh.** *Ûp̄ənê YHWH lōʾ ḥillîṯî* (12, WLC), literally *I have not soothed
  the face of Yahweh*. Saul treats the burnt offering as the way to secure Yahweh's favour before battle. Samuel had
  said the waiting would end with *show you what you are to do* (10:8); Saul's speech asks for favour and does not
  mention the word.
- **I forced myself.** *Wāʾeṯʾappaq* (12, WLC), the Hithpael of *ʾāp̄aq*, *restrain oneself*. In the WLC of
  Genesis–2 Samuel it occurs only three times: Joseph *controlled himself* (Genesis 43:31), *Joseph couldn't control
  himself* (45:1), and here (lemma 662). Joseph held himself back; Saul uses the same verb for forcing himself to
  act. See [[Joseph Reveals Himself]].
- **You have done foolishly.** *Niskāltā* (13, WLC). *Sāḵal* occurs five times in the WLC of Genesis–2 Samuel:
  Laban to Jacob, *Now have you done foolishly* (Genesis 31:28, *hiskaltā*); here; Saul's own confession to
  David, *Behold, I have played the fool* (1 Samuel 26:21, *hiskaltî*, stored WEB; not yet ingested); David's
  prayer about Ahithophel (2 Samuel 15:31); and David after the census, *I have done very foolishly*, *niskaltî
  məʾōḏ* (2 Samuel 24:10, WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-18; not stored). Only Saul here and David there
  have the Niphal. See [[You Have Done Foolishly - Saul at Gilgal and David at the Census]].
- **The commandment of Yahweh your God, which he commanded you.** *Miṣwaṯ YHWH … ʾăšer ṣiwwāḵ* (13, WLC), and again
  *ʾēṯ ʾăšer-ṣiwwəḵā YHWH* (14). The instruction of 10:8 was spoken by Samuel; Samuel calls it Yahweh's. Verse 14
  uses the same verb, *ṣiwwâ*, for Yahweh's appointing the new man: *wayṣawwēhû YHWH lənāḡîḏ*, *Yahweh has
  commanded him as prince* (WLC). Saul did not keep what Yahweh *commanded*; the other man is *commanded* prince.
- **Yahweh would have established your kingdom … forever.** *Hēḵîn YHWH ʾeṯ-mamlaḵtəḵā ʾel-yiśrāʾēl ʿaḏ-ʿôlām*
  (13, WLC). *Kûn*, *establish*, with *forever*, is the vocabulary of the promise to David (2 Samuel 7:16, WLC
  *nāḵôn ʿaḏ-ʿôlām*; WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored). The verse is a conditional the text does not
  explain further: Saul's dynasty could have stood.
- **Your kingdom will not continue.** *Mamlaḵtəḵā lōʾ-ṯāqûm* (14, WLC), literally *will not stand*. The verb is
  *qûm*, and in verse 15 Samuel *arose*, *wayyāqom* (WLC), and leaves.
- **A man after his own heart.** *ʾÎš kilḇāḇô* (14, WLC), literally *a man according to his heart*. The phrase is
  read two ways: of a man whose heart matches Yahweh's, or of a man of Yahweh's choosing, *according to his
  wish*. The second reading has scholarly defenders (recalled, not checked). Whose heart *his* refers to is
  Yahweh's on either reading. Yahweh will tell Samuel at Bethlehem *Yahweh looks at the heart*, *YHWH yirʾeh
  lallēḇāḇ* (16:7, stored WEB; WLC), and Paul quotes the phrase of David: *I have found David the son of Jesse, a man
  after my heart, who will do all my will* (Acts 13:22, WEB checked at bible-api.com, 2026-11-18; not stored). The
  man is not named in this chapter. See [[A Man After His Own Heart - 1 Samuel 13.14, 16.7, and Acts 13.22]].
- **Yahweh has sought … and Yahweh has appointed him.** *Biqqēš … wayṣawwēhû* (14, WLC), both perfect or
  past-narrative forms. Samuel speaks of the replacement as already sought and already appointed. 15:28 will say
  the kingdom is given *to a neighbor of yours who is better than you*, and 16:1–13 narrates the anointing (stored
  WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Prince over his people.** *Lənāḡîḏ ʿal-ʿammô* (14, WLC), Saul's own title at his anointing (9:16; 10:1), now
  given to someone else. Abigail and the elders of Israel later use it of David (25:30, stored WEB; 2 Samuel 5:2,
  WLC *lənāḡîḏ*).
- **About six hundred men.** *Kəšēš mēʾôṯ ʾîš* (15, WLC). Three thousand were chosen in verse 2. Six hundred is
  the Danites' number (Judges 18:11), the Benjamites left at the rock of Rimmon (Judges 20:47), Saul's again at
  14:2, and David's band (23:13; 27:2) (stored WEB). Gideon fought with three hundred.
- **Samuel went from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin.** *Wayyaʿal min-haggilgāl giḇʿaṯ binyāmin* (15, WLC). The
  Septuagint is commonly reported to have a longer verse here, in which Saul and the people also go up from Gilgal
  to Gibeah (recalled, not checked). In the WLC, Samuel goes up, and Saul is next found at Geba of Benjamin (16).

**How it connects.** The scene completes the instruction of [[Samuel Anoints Saul]] (10:8) and returns to the
Gilgal of [[Saul Rescues Jabesh Gilead]], where the kingdom had been *renewed* with peace offerings. It follows
directly on [[Samuel's Farewell Address]], which made king and people answerable to the same commandment and ended
*you will be consumed, both you and your king* (12:25). Here the commandment is broken and the dynasty forfeited.
The Philistines, whose rescue was the king's commission (9:16), are back in force. The *man after his own heart*
looks ahead to David's anointing (16:1–13), and the rejection is told a second time over Amalek (15), where Samuel
will say *to obey is better than sacrifice* (15:22, stored WEB; not yet ingested). The two scenes, Gilgal and
Amalek, end Saul's dynasty and then his reign.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 13:1–15 as planned; 13:16 shifts to the Philistine raiders and the lack of
smiths, the next box. Verse 1 is kept with the pericope as the opening of Saul's reign. [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]]
gets a page, qualified because [[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]] already exists. [[Michmash]] gets a page. *The Mount
of Bethel* is filed under [[Bethel]], and *the land of Gad* under [[Gad]], the existing page for the son of Jacob
and his tribe. *The Hebrews* get no page. [[David]] is linked as the referent of 13:14, though the chapter does not
name him. Two new connections: [[You Have Done Foolishly - Saul at Gilgal and David at the Census]] and
[[A Man After His Own Heart - 1 Samuel 13.14, 16.7, and Acts 13.22]].

**Unresolved.** The numbers of verse 1. What exactly Saul's offence was: impatience before the prophet, a king
offering sacrifice, or both. Whether Samuel arrived within the seventh day. What *nəṣîḇ* is. Whom *the Hebrews*
names in verses 3 and 7. Whether *a man after his own heart* describes character or choice. Why the WLC's verse 15
moves Samuel to Gibeah and leaves Saul's own movement unstated.
