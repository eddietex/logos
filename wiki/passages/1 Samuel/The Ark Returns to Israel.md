# The Ark Returns to Israel

**Book:** [[1 Samuel]]
**Reference:** 1 Samuel 6:1–7:1

## Summary

> **6:1** Yahweh's ark was in the country of the Philistines seven months.
> **2** The Philistines called for the priests and the diviners, saying, "What shall we do with Yahweh's ark?
> Show us how we should send it to its place."
> **3** They said, "If you send away the ark of the God of Israel, don't send it empty; but by all means
> return a trespass offering to him. Then you will be healed, and it will be known to you why his hand is
> not removed from you."
> **4** Then they said, "What should the trespass offering be which we shall return to him?" They said,
> "Five golden tumors, and five golden mice, for the number of the lords of the Philistines; for one plague
> was on you all, and on your lords.
> **5** Therefore you shall make images of your tumors, and images of your mice that mar the land; and you
> shall give glory to the God of Israel. Perhaps he will release his hand from you, from your gods, and from
> your land.
> **6** Why then do you harden your hearts, as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? When he had
> worked wonderfully among them, didn't they let the people go, and they departed?
> **7** "Now therefore take and prepare yourselves a new cart, and two milk cows, on which there has come no
> yoke; and tie the cows to the cart, and bring their calves home from them;
> **8** and take Yahweh's ark, and lay it on the cart. Put the jewels of gold, which you return him for a
> trespass offering, in a coffer by its side; and send it away, that it may go.
> **9** Behold; if it goes up by the way of its own border to Beth Shemesh, then he has done us this great
> evil; but if not, then we shall know that it is not his hand that struck us. It was a chance that happened
> to us."
> **10** The men did so, and took two milk cows, and tied them to the cart, and shut up their calves at home.
> **11** They put Yahweh's ark on the cart, and the coffer with the golden mice and the images of their
> tumors.
> **12** The cows took the straight way by the way to Beth Shemesh. They went along the highway, lowing as
> they went, and didn't turn away to the right hand or to the left; and the lords of the Philistines went
> after them to the border of Beth Shemesh.
> **13** The people of Beth Shemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley; and they lifted up their
> eyes, and saw the ark, and rejoiced to see it.
> **14** The cart came into the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh, and stood there, where there was a great
> stone. Then they split the wood of the cart, and offered up the cows for a burnt offering to Yahweh.
> **15** The Levites took down Yahweh's ark, and the coffer that was with it, in which the jewels of gold
> were, and put them on the great stone; and the men of Beth Shemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrificed
> sacrifices the same day to Yahweh.
> **16** When the five lords of the Philistines had seen it, they returned to Ekron the same day.
> **17** These are the golden tumors which the Philistines returned for a trespass offering to Yahweh: for
> Ashdod one, for Gaza one, for Ashkelon one, for Gath one, for Ekron one;
> **18** and the golden mice, according to the number of all the cities of the Philistines belonging to the
> five lords, both of fortified cities and of country villages, even to the great stone, on which they set
> down Yahweh's ark. That stone remains to this day in the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh.
> **19** He struck of the men of Beth Shemesh, because they had looked into Yahweh's ark, he struck fifty
> thousand seventy of the men. Then the people mourned, because Yahweh had struck the people with a great
> slaughter.
> **20** The men of Beth Shemesh said, "Who is able to stand before Yahweh, this holy God? To whom shall he
> go up from us?"
> **21** They sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kiriath Jearim, saying, "The Philistines have brought
> back Yahweh's ark. Come down, and bring it up to yourselves."
> **7:1** The men of Kiriath Jearim came, and took Yahweh's ark, and brought it into Abinadab's house on the
> hill, and consecrated Eleazar his son to keep Yahweh's ark.

After *seven months* [[The Philistines]] call their priests and diviners: *What shall we do
with Yahweh's ark?* (6:1–2). The answer is not to send it *empty* but with *a trespass offering*: five golden
tumors and five golden mice, one for each lord, so that they may *give glory to the God of Israel* and he may
lift his hand (3–5). The priests warn them not to *harden your hearts, as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened
their hearts* (6). They devise a test: a new cart, two milk cows never yoked, their calves shut up at home.
If the cows go up to [[Beth Shemesh]], it was Yahweh; if not, *It was a chance that happened to us* (7–9). The
cows go straight up the highway, *lowing as they went*, turning neither right nor left, and the lords follow
to the border (10–12). The people of Beth Shemesh, reaping wheat, look up and *rejoiced to see it* (13). The
cart stops by a great stone in the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh; the cart is split for wood and the cows
offered as a burnt offering; the Levites set the ark and the coffer on the stone (14–15). The lords watch and
go home to [[Ekron]] (16). The narrator lists the gold: a tumor each for [[Ashdod]], [[Gaza]], [[Ashkelon]],
[[Gath]], and Ekron, and mice for all their towns, and adds that the stone is still there (17–18). Then Yahweh
strikes the men of Beth Shemesh *because they had looked into Yahweh's ark*, and the people mourn (19). *Who
is able to stand before Yahweh, this holy God?* they ask, and send to [[Kiriath Jearim]] to fetch it (20–21).
The men of Kiriath Jearim bring it to the house of [[Abinadab (of Kiriath Jearim)]] *on the hill*, and
consecrate his son [[Eleazar (son of Abinadab)]] to keep it (7:1).

**What is going on.** The chapter answers the question the Philistine lords asked twice in chapter 5: *What
shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?* (5:8; 6:2). In chapter 5 they moved it from city to city; now
they ask their own religious experts, and the experts get the answer right. They know the ark has a *place*,
that its God must be paid and honored, and that Egypt's resistance ended badly. Then they build a test in
which nature itself must contradict them: cows still nursing, never broken to the yoke, should go home to
their calves. Instead they go straight to Israel's border. The Philistines who had called Israel's God
*these mighty gods* (4:8) now act on what they have learned, and send him home.

Israel, by contrast, does not come off well. Beth Shemesh welcomes the ark with joy and sacrifice, and its
Levites handle it correctly; then the town is struck *because they had looked into Yahweh's ark*, and its
people ask the same question the Philistines asked in 5:7–10: who can keep this God? They send it on, as
Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron did. The chapter ends with the ark neither at Shiloh nor with the priests of Eli's
line, but in a private house in a town of the old Gibeonite league (Joshua 9:17), kept by a man consecrated
for the purpose. [[Samuel]] does not appear in the pericope.

**What is notable.**

- **Seven months.** The only time note in the ark narrative before 7:2 (*twenty years*, stored WEB; not yet
  ingested). It measures how long the Philistines held out before asking.
- **Priests and diviners.** *Lakkōhănîm wəlaqqōsəmîm* (2, WLC). The root *q-s-m*, *divine*, names Balaam's
  craft (*the rewards of divination*, Numbers 22:7; *no … divination with Israel*, 23:23) and is forbidden
  in Israel (Deuteronomy 18:10, 14) (stored WEB; the root was not lemma-searched). The pagan experts are given the right answer, as Balaam was.
- **Don't send it empty.** *ʾAl-təšalləḥû ʾōṯô rêqām* (3, WLC). *Rêqām*, *empty*, is the word of Exodus 3:21,
  *when you go, you shall not go empty-handed*, and of the laws on the released slave and the pilgrim feasts
  (Exodus 23:15; 34:20; Deuteronomy 15:13; 16:16). In the WLC files of Genesis through 2 Kings, Ruth, Psalms,
  and Ecclesiastes the word occurs at those verses, here, and at Genesis 31:42, Ruth 1:21; 3:17, 2 Samuel
  1:22, and Psalms 7:5; 25:3 (lemma search). Israel left Egypt with gold; the ark leaves Philistia with gold. See
  [[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]].
- **A trespass offering.** *ʾĀšām* (3, 4, 8, 17, WLC), the word for the *trespass offering* (guilt offering)
  of Leviticus 5:14–19, which is due for a wrong done *in the holy things of Yahweh* and pays back the loss
  with a fifth added (5:15–16). The Philistines' *ʾāšām* is not the Levitical rite: there is no ram and no
  priest. But the word matches the offense, a wrong against a holy thing, and pairs restitution with
  appeasement, as the law does. See [[Restitution to the Neighbor and for Holy Things]].
- **Tumors and mice.** *Five golden tumors, and five golden mice* (4). The tumors keep the ketiv/qere pair
  *ʿŏp̄ālîm* / *ṭəḥōrîm* of chapter 5 (4, 5, WLC; 11 and 17 have only *ṭəḥōrîm*). The mice, *ʿaḵbārîm*
  (lemma 5909), have not been mentioned before; the priests speak of *your mice that mar the land*,
  *hammašḥîṯim ʾeṯ-hāʾāreṣ* (5, WLC). In the WLC files searched, the noun occurs only here and at Leviticus
  11:29, where the WEB translates it *rat* among the unclean creeping things (stored WEB). Whether the mice
  were a separate plague on the land, or are linked to the tumors as a single disease, the text does not
  say; *one plague was on you all* (4) is read both ways. The Septuagint is commonly reported to mention mice
  already in chapter 5 (recalled, not checked). See
  [[Tumors and Confusion - The Covenant Curses on Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron]].
- **Give glory, and lighten the hand.** *Ûnəṯattem lēʾlōhê yiśrāʾēl kāḇôḏ*, *give glory to the God of
  Israel*; *ʾûlay yāqēl ʾeṯ-yāḏô*, *perhaps he will lighten his hand* (5, WLC). The WEB's *release* renders
  *qālal*, *be light* (lemma 7043), the antonym of *kāḇēḏ*, *be heavy*. Chapter 5 said *Yahweh's hand was
  heavy* (5:6, 11); the priests hope that giving *weight* to Yahweh will make his hand *light*. See
  [[Heavy Hand and Departed Glory - Kabed in 1 Samuel 4-6]].
- **As the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts.** *Təḵabbəḏû … kibbəḏû* (6, WLC), the verb *kāḇēḏ*
  that Exodus uses of Pharaoh (Exodus 8:15, 32; 9:34; 10:1). The second half of the verse, *When he had
  worked wonderfully among them*, renders *hiṯʿallēl bāhem* (WLC), the verb of Exodus 10:2, *what things I
  have done to Egypt*, *hiṯʿallaltî* (stored WEB; WLC). In the WLC files searched the stem occurs
  also at Numbers 22:29 (Balaam's donkey), Judges 19:25 (the concubine), 1 Samuel 31:4 (Saul's fear of the
  Philistines), and Psalm 141:4, mostly with the sense *deal harshly with, make sport of* (lemma search). The Philistine
  priests know Exodus better than they did in 4:8, where the plagues were *in the wilderness*. See
  [[Hardening of the Heart]] and [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]].
- **Never yoked.** *Pārôṯ ʿālôṯ ʾăšer lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿălêhem ʿōl* (7, WLC), *milk cows, on which there has come no
  yoke*. The clause *ʾăšer lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿāleyhā ʿōl* describes the red heifer of Numbers 19:2 (WLC; WEB *which was
  never yoked*). Deuteronomy 21:3 has a similar condition with a different verb (*which has not drawn in the
  yoke*, *lōʾ-māšəḵâ bəʿōl*, WLC). The Philistine priests choose animals fit for sacrifice, and the men of Beth
  Shemesh sacrifice them. See [[Never Yoked - The Red Heifer and the Philistine Cows]].
- **A chance.** *Miqreh hûʾ hāyâ lānû* (9, WLC). *Miqreh*, *chance, what happens*, occurs in the WLC files of
  Genesis through 2 Kings, Ruth, Psalms, and Ecclesiastes only here, at 1 Samuel 20:26, at Ruth 2:3 (*she
  happened to come*), and five times in Ecclesiastes (2:14, 15; 3:19; 9:2, 3; lemma search). The test is
  built to tell Yahweh's *hand* from *chance*; in Ruth, a *chance* turns out to be the way Yahweh's kindness
  comes. See [[It Was a Chance - Miqreh in 1 Samuel 6, Ruth 2, and Ecclesiastes]].
- **Not his hand that touched us.** *Lōʾ yāḏô nāḡəʿâ bānû* (9, WLC): literally *his hand did not touch us*;
  the WEB has *struck*. *Nāgaʿ* is also the root of *plague* (*neḡaʿ*) elsewhere, though here the priests'
  word for plague is *maggēp̄â* (4, WLC).
- **The straight way.** *Wayyiššarnâ happārôṯ baddereḵ* (12, WLC), *the cows went straight*; *they didn't
  turn away to the right hand or to the left*, *wəlōʾ-sārû yāmîn ûśəmōʾl* (WLC). The phrase is the one Joshua
  is given about the law, *Don't turn from it to the right hand or to the left* (Joshua 1:7, *ʾal-tāsûr …
  yāmîn ûśəmōʾl*, WLC; stored WEB). Unyoked cows keep the line Israel was told to keep. *Lowing*, *gāʿô*
  (lemma 1600), occurs only here in the WLC files searched: the cows cry for their calves and go on anyway.
- **Wheat harvest.** Late spring, if the harvest calendar of the Torah is assumed; the text gives no month.
  The ark arrives at harvest, and *they rejoiced to see it*, *wayyiśməḥû lirʾôṯ* (13, WLC). Six verses later
  they are struck because they *looked*, *rāʾû* (19, WLC): the same verb, *see*.
- **Joshua of Beth Shemesh.** *Yəhôšuaʿ bêṯ-haššimšî* (14, 18, WLC), a local landowner, named only here. His
  field and its stone become a landmark *to this day* (18).
- **The great stone, or Abel the great.** At 6:14–15 the stone is *ʾeḇen gəḏôlâ* (WLC). At 6:18 the WLC reads
  *wəʿaḏ ʾāḇēl haggəḏôlâ* (lemma 59, *ʾāḇēl*, *meadow*, as in Abel Meholah and the other Abels), which reads
  awkwardly with a feminine adjective. The WEB translates *the great stone*, following *ʾeḇen* as in 6:14–15;
  that reading is commonly said to be supported by the Septuagint and Targum (recalled, not checked). The
  wiki reports the WLC reading and the WEB's rendering, and does not decide between them.
- **The Levites.** *Wəhalwiyyim hôrîḏû ʾeṯ-ʾărôn YHWH* (15, WLC). [[Beth Shemesh]] is one of the Levitical
  cities given to Aaron's descendants (Joshua 21:16), so Levites at hand are not surprising. Numbers 4:15,
  19–20 assigns the carrying to the Kohathites and forbids them to *see* the holy things; the Levites at Beth
  Shemesh handle the ark, and the men of the town then *look into* it. See
  [[Lest They Die - The Ark Carried and the Ark Looked Into]].
- **Sacrifice at Beth Shemesh.** They split the cart for fuel and offer the cows (14), then offer more burnt
  offerings and sacrifices (15). The place is not a central sanctuary. Deuteronomy 12:13–14 restricts burnt
  offerings to *the place which Yahweh chooses* (stored WEB); the narrator neither commends nor condemns the
  Beth Shemesh offerings, and Shiloh, the earlier place, has gone unmentioned since 4:12.
- **Fifty thousand seventy.** The WLC reads *šiḇʿîm ʾîš ḥămiššîm ʾelep̄ ʾîš*, *seventy men, fifty thousand
  men* (19, WLC), without a conjunction, and the WEB renders it *fifty thousand seventy*. The number is
  very large for a village, and the syntax is unusual; many readers take *fifty thousand* as a gloss and
  read *seventy men*, a reading commonly attributed to some Hebrew manuscripts and to Josephus (recalled,
  not checked). The wiki gives the text and records the problem.
- **Looked into Yahweh's ark.** *Kî rāʾû baʾărôn YHWH* (19, WLC). *Rāʾâ bə-* can mean *look at* with
  attention, *gaze on*, or *look into*; the WEB's *into* is an interpretation of the preposition, and the
  Septuagint is commonly reported to read a different opening clause here (recalled, not checked). Either
  way, the offense lies in seeing, which Numbers 4:20 forbids even to the Kohathites, *lest they die*.
- **Who is able to stand?** *Mî yûḵal laʿămōḏ lip̄nê YHWH hāʾĕlōhîm haqqāḏôš hazzeh* (20, WLC): *this holy
  God*. The question echoes Hannah's *There is no one as holy as Yahweh* (2:2), and answers it the hard way.
  The second half, *To whom shall he go up from us?*, repeats the Philistine move: send the ark on.
- **Come down, bring it up.** *Rəḏû haʿălû ʾōṯô ʾălêḵem* (21, WLC): from the lowland town up to the hill
  country. *Go up*, *ʿālâ*, is the verb of the cows going *up* to Beth Shemesh (9, 12) and of the burnt
  offering, *ʿōlâ*, *going up* (14, 15).
- **On the hill, and consecrated.** *ʾEl-bêṯ ʾăḇînāḏāḇ baggiḇʿâ* (7:1, WLC), *in Abinadab's house on the
  hill*; 2 Samuel 6:3 repeats the location exactly, *baggiḇʿâ*, when David takes the ark out *on a new cart*
  driven by Uzzah and Ahio, *the sons of Abinadab* (WEB checked at bible-api.com; WLC). Eleazar is not
  called a priest or Levite; they *consecrated*, *qiddəšû* (7:1, WLC), him *to keep*, *lišmōr*, the ark. The
  verb *šāmar* with *mišmereṯ*, *keeping*, describes the Levites' charge of the sanctuary in Numbers 3 (by
  reference, not searched). Whether Abinadab's household was Levitical the text does not say.
- **Kiriath Jearim.** One of the four Gibeonite cities (Joshua 9:17), a city of Judah on Benjamin's border
  (Joshua 15:9, 60; 18:14). The ark rests among people who had once deceived Joshua into a treaty, and the
  narrator draws nothing from it. Psalm 132:6, *We found it in the field of Jaar* (WEB checked at
  bible-api.com), is commonly read as a reference to the ark at Kiriath Jearim, *yəʿārîm* (*forests*) beside
  *yāʿar* (*forest*, WLC); that identification is a reading of the psalm, not its statement.

**How it connects.** This is the third panel of the ark narrative after [[The Ark Is Captured]] and
[[The Ark Among the Philistines]]. The Philistines' *What shall we do?* (5:8; 6:2) gets its answer, and the
*heavy* hand of chapter 5 is answered by the priests' *give glory* and *lighten his hand* (6:5; see
[[Heavy Hand and Departed Glory - Kabed in 1 Samuel 4-6]]). The Exodus frame, which the Philistines raised
garbled at 4:8, is now cited correctly (6:6): they let the ark *go*, as Egypt let Israel *go*, *and they
departed*, and they send it with gold, as Egypt did (Exodus 12:35–36; see
[[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]]). The death at Beth Shemesh fulfils, in narrative,
the warning of Numbers 4:20 ([[Lest They Die - The Ark Carried and the Ark Looked Into]]). The ark will stay
at Kiriath Jearim *twenty years* (7:2, next pericope) and is next moved by David, on another *new cart*, at
the cost of Uzzah's life (2 Samuel 6:3–7, not yet ingested).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope runs 6:1–7:1 as planned: 7:1 completes the ark's journey, and 7:2 opens a
twenty-year time note and a new subject, Samuel and Israel at Mizpah. New person pages for Abinadab and
Eleazar, both qualified because the names recur (Abinadab son of Jesse at 16:8 and son of Saul at 31:2;
Eleazar son of Aaron already has a page). No page for Joshua of Beth Shemesh: he is named only as the owner
of a field and a landmark, and is recorded on [[Beth Shemesh]]. The men of Beth Shemesh are recorded there
too. The priests and diviners and the five lords are corporate and sit under [[The Philistines]]. The
Levites are recorded on [[Levi]].

**Unresolved.** The number at 6:19 (*seventy men, fifty thousand men*); whether *looked into* or *looked at*
is meant; the *great stone* or *Abel the great* at 6:18; whether the mice were a separate plague; whether
Abinadab's house was Levitical; and whether Psalm 132:6 refers to Kiriath Jearim. Each is reported, not
settled.
