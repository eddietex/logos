# Now They Mock Me

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 30:1–31

## Summary

*"But now those who are younger than I have me in derision"* (30:1). Chapter 29 was the "before";
this is the "after", and it is built as its negative. **Three times the chapter turns on
*wəʿattâ*, *"But now"* (30:1, 9, 16)**, and each turn goes lower: from the men who mock him (1–15),
to God who has done it (16–23), to the mourner who is left (24–31). Chapter 29 had no complaint and no
addressee; chapter 30 is nothing but complaint, and in the middle of it Job speaks to God directly for
the last time before the whirlwind.

**1. The mockers (30:1–15).** The first *"But now"* brings back the young men of 29:8, who once hid
when they saw him. Now they *"have me in derision"* (30:1) — *śāḥăqû ʿālay*, the verb of 29:24, *"I
smiled on them"* (*ʾeśḥaq*). **The smile he gave is the laugh he gets.** Then Job does something no
reader of chapter 29 expects: he describes the mockers' families with contempt. Their fathers he
*"would have disdained to put with my sheep dogs"* (30:1); they are *"men in whom ripe age has
perished"* (30:2) — *kelaḥ*, the rare word for the full age Eliphaz promised him at 5:26. And for six
verses (30:3–8) he paints them as a people expelled from society: gaunt with famine, gnawing the dry
ground *"in the gloom of waste and desolation"* (30:3), plucking salt herbs, eating the roots of the
broom (30:4), *"driven out from among men"* and shouted after *"as after a thief"* (30:5), living in
gullies and holes in the rocks (30:6). *"Among the bushes they bray"* (30:7) — *nāhaq*, a verb used
elsewhere in the Bible only of the wild donkey at 6:5. *"They are children of fools, yes, children of
wicked men"* (30:8) — literally *bənê bəlî-šēm*, **sons of no name**: the namelessness Bildad promised
the wicked man (18:17), given to the people who now mock the man Bildad meant.

The second *"But now"* (30:9) is what they do: *"Now I have become their song. Yes, I am a byword to
them"* — *nəgînātām*, their taunt-song, the same noun as Lamentations 3:14, *"I have become a derision
to all my people, and their song all day long"*. *"They abhor me, they stand aloof from me, and don't
hesitate to spit in my face"* (30:10): the princes who laid a hand on their mouth (29:9) have become
the rabble who spit (*rōq*, the *"shame and spitting"* of Isaiah 50:6). The reason is given in one
line: *"For he has untied his cord, and afflicted me; and they have thrown off restraint before me"*
(30:11). **God loosened something, and the restraint went with it.** Then the mockery becomes a siege:
*"On my right hand rise the rabble … They cast up against me their ways of destruction"* (30:12) —
*wayyāsōllû ʿālay*, the siege ramp God's troops built at 19:12, now built by men; *"As through a wide
breach they come"* (30:14), the *pereṣ* of 16:14, *"He breaks me with breach on breach"*. And at the
end, *"Terrors have turned on me. They chase my honor as the wind. My welfare has passed away as a
cloud"* (30:15) — *ballāhôt*, **Bildad's terrors**, which *"chase him at his heels"* (18:11) and wait
for the wicked man as *"the king of terrors"* (18:14).

**2. God (30:16–23).** The third *"But now"*: *"Now my soul is poured out within me. Days of
affliction have taken hold on me"* (30:16). Night pain in the bones (30:17); a garment twisted on him
like a collar (30:18). Then the subject changes to God, and the chapter's centre:

> *"He has cast me into the mire. I have become like dust and ashes."* (30:19)

***ʿĀpār wāʾēper*** — **the pair occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible**: Abraham's *"I am dust and
ashes"* (Genesis 18:27), this verse, and Job's last words, *"I abhor myself, and repent in dust and
ashes"* (42:6). Job sat among the ashes at 2:8 and called the friends' maxims *"proverbs of ashes"* at
13:12; here, for the first time, he says he has *become* the material. See
[[Sitting in Dust and Ashes]].

And then, for four verses, he turns and speaks to God — **the last time he addresses God in the second
person until 40:4**, since chapter 31 speaks of him only in the third:

> *"I cry to you, and you do not answer me. I stand up, and you gaze at me. You have turned to be
> cruel to me. With the might of your hand you persecute me."* (30:20–21)

*ʾĂšawwaʿ*, *I cry for help* — the verb of 29:12, *"I delivered the poor who cried"* (*məšawwēaʿ*).
**Job answered every cry that came to him; his own is not answered.** It is the complaint of Psalm 22:2,
*"My God, I cry in the daytime, but you don't answer"*, and of Job's own 19:7, *"I cry for help, but
there is no justice."* *"You have turned to be cruel to me"* (30:21) — *ʾakzār*, the word God himself
will use of anyone fierce enough to rouse Leviathan (41:10). *"You lift me up to the wind, and drive me
with it. You dissolve me in the storm"* (30:22). And the end of it: *"For I know that you will bring me
to death, To the house appointed for all living"* (30:23) — *bêt môʿēd*, a meeting house, the grave as
the one appointment every living thing keeps. **Job has wanted a meeting with God for twenty chapters
(9:32–35; 13:3; 23:3); the only appointment he now expects is that one.** See [[Sheol]].

**3. The mourner (30:24–31).** The last section circles back to chapter 29 and forward to chapter 31.
*"Didn't I weep for him who was in trouble? Wasn't my soul grieved for the needy?"* (30:25) — the
*ʾebyôn* he was a father to (29:16). *"When I looked for good, then evil came; When I waited for light,
there came darkness"* (30:26): the *good* and *evil* he told his wife to receive alike from God's hand
(2:10), and the light of 29:3, *"by his light I walked through darkness"*, gone out. *"I go mourning
without the sun. I stand up in the assembly, and cry for help"* (30:28) — *qōdēr hillaktî*, the
psalmist's *"I go mourning all day long"* (Psalm 38:6), and *"I stand up in the assembly"* in place of
29:7's seat at the gate: **the judge who heard cries at the gate is now the one crying in it.** *"I am a
brother to jackals, and a companion to ostriches"* (30:29) — the animals the prophets put in a ruined
city. See [[A Brother to Jackals - Job 30.29 and the Creatures of the Ruins]]. *"My skin grows black
and peels from me. My bones are burned with heat"* (30:30) — the disease of 2:7 in Job's own words. And
the close:

> *"Therefore my harp has turned to mourning, and my pipe into the voice of those who weep."* (30:31)

*Kinnōr* and *ʿûgāb*, the instruments of [[Jubal]] (Genesis 4:21), which Job gave to the prosperous
wicked at 21:12 — *"They sing to the tambourine and harp, and rejoice at the sound of the pipe."* **The
man who lived *"as one who comforts the mourners"* (29:25) ends the chapter as the mourner, with no
comforter named.** See [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]].

**What the chapter does.** Placed beside chapter 29 it is a point-by-point reversal:

| Chapter 29 — "the months of old" | Chapter 30 — "but now" |
|---|---|
| *"The young men saw me and hid themselves"* (29:8) | *"those who are younger than I have me in derision"* (30:1) |
| *"I smiled on them"* — *ʾeśḥaq* (29:24) | *"have me in derision"* — *śāḥăqû* (30:1) |
| *"The princes … laid their hand on their mouth"* (29:9) | *"don't hesitate to spit in my face"* (30:10) |
| *"My glory is fresh in me"* (29:20) | *"They chase my honor as the wind"* (30:15) |
| *"I put on righteousness, and it clothed me"* (29:14) | *"By great force is my garment disfigured"* (30:18) |
| *"I delivered the poor who cried"* — *məšawwēaʿ* (29:12) | *"I cry to you, and you do not answer me"* — *ʾăšawwaʿ* (30:20) |
| *"by his light I walked through darkness"* (29:3) | *"When I waited for light, there came darkness"* (30:26) |
| *"when I prepared my seat in the street"* (29:7) | *"I stand up in the assembly, and cry for help"* (30:28) |
| *"as one who comforts the mourners"* (29:25) | *"my harp has turned to mourning"* (30:31) |

**Chapter 29 is entered into evidence as the life; chapter 30 as the injury.** Chapter 31 will supply
the third thing a defence needs — the oath that the injury was not deserved.

## People

- [[Job (person)]] — the speaker, continuing the speech headed at 29:1. **The present tense of his
  defence**: mocked by the sons of men he would not have hired (30:1–8), a song and a byword (30:9),
  spat on (30:10), besieged (30:12–14), poured out (30:16), *"like dust and ashes"* (30:19), unanswered
  (30:20), and a mourner whose harp has turned to weeping (30:31).
- [[God]] — the one who *"has untied his cord"* (30:11) and *"has cast me into the mire"* (30:19);
  addressed directly in 30:20–23 for the last time before 40:4, as one who does not answer, who
  *"gaze[s]"* (30:20), who has *"turned to be cruel"* (30:21), and who will bring Job *"to the house
  appointed for all living"* (30:23).
- **The mockers** — unnamed; *"those who are younger than I"* (30:1), the sons of an outcast people
  *"driven out from among men"* (30:5) and *"flogged out of the land"* (30:8). Not given a page: they
  are a class, not characters, and appear only here.
- [[Bildad the Shuhite]] — not named, and his vocabulary turned on Job: the *terrors* that chase
  (30:15; 18:11, 14) and the namelessness of 30:8 (*bəlî-šēm*; 18:17).

## Places

- [[Sheol]] — not named, but meant: *"the house appointed for all living"* (30:23), *bêt môʿēd
  ləkol-ḥāy*.

## Themes

- [[Suffering]] — the whole chapter: social (30:1–15), divine (30:16–23), bodily (30:17, 30), and
  inward (30:16, 27).
- [[Mourning and Lament]] — *"I go mourning without the sun"* (30:28) and *"my harp has turned to
  mourning"* (30:31); the comforter of 29:25 become the mourner. See [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]].
- [[Remembrance]] — *"But now"* three times (30:1, 9, 16): the present measured against the memory of
  chapter 29, line for line.
- [[Prayer]] — *"I cry to you, and you do not answer me"* (30:20); the last direct address to God
  until 40:4, and an unanswered cry from the man who answered every cry (29:12).
- [[Light and Darkness]] — *"When I waited for light, there came darkness"* (30:26), the lamp of 29:3
  gone; *"I go mourning without the sun"* (30:28).
- [[Life and Death]] — *"the house appointed for all living"* (30:23); *"I have become like dust and
  ashes"* (30:19).
- [[Language and Speech]] — the taunt-song and the byword (30:9), the shout after a thief (30:5), the
  braying in the bushes (30:7): speech as degradation, against the gate's ordered silence in 29.
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — *"Didn't I weep for him who was in trouble?"* (30:25), and its absence
  everywhere else in the chapter; and **the tension of 30:1–8**, where the man who was a father to the
  needy describes an outcast people with contempt (see Notes).
- [[Hope]] — *"When I looked for good, then evil came"* (30:26); *qiwwâ*, the hope of waiting, met by
  its opposite.
- [[Retribution]] — every mark of the wicked man's fate in the friends' speeches — terrors, a lost
  name, darkness, a dwelling among wild creatures — is now on Job, and the chapter says so without
  admitting the inference.

## Notes

- **The tension of 30:1–8.** At 24:4–8 Job described the dispossessed poor of the wilderness — *"as wild
  donkeys in the desert"*, gleaning, naked in the cold, embracing the rock for shelter — with pity, and as
  evidence against God's governance. At 29:16 he was *"a father to the needy"*. Here he describes a
  wilderness people in nearly the same terms with open contempt, down to their fathers' unfitness to
  run with his dogs. **Two readings are offered, and the wiki records both.** (a) The groups differ: 24:4
  is the oppressed poor *"turned out of the way"* by others, while 30:5 and 30:8 describe a people
  expelled for cause — shouted after *"as after a thief"*, *"flogged out of the land"* — so that Job's
  contempt is for an outlaw class, not the poor as such. (b) The groups are the same and the contempt is
  real: a man stripped of rank reaches for the one rank left to him, and the chapter lets the reader see
  the old hierarchy in him without endorsing it. **This is a tension in Job's speech, not a contradiction
  between wiki pages**; [[Compassion and Mercy]] notes it.
- **"He has untied his cord" (30:11).** The consonantal text reads *yitrô*, *his* cord, and the margin
  (*qere*) *yitrî*, *my* cord. The WEB follows the written text: God has loosened his own bowstring, or
  his tent cord, or the restraint on the rabble. On the margin's reading Job's cord is untied — the tent
  cord of 4:21, whose plucking-up is death — or his bowstring slackened, the *"bow … renewed in my hand"*
  of 29:20 undone. Either way the second half of the verse follows: when the cord is loosed, the mockers
  *"have thrown off restraint"* (*resen*, a bridle).
- **"You gaze at me" (30:20).** *Wattitbōnen bî*. Many interpreters carry the first half's negative over
  — *"I stand up, and you do not look at me"* — which matches the complaint; read as it stands, the line
  is harsher: God looks and does nothing. The WEB keeps the received text, and so does the wiki.
- **"Without the sun" (30:28).** *Bəlōʾ ḥammâ*. *Ḥammâ* is the sun's heat; some commentators propose
  reading *neḥāmâ*, *comfort* — *"I go about mourning without comfort"* — which would fit 29:25 and the
  chapter's close. It is a proposal without manuscript support; the wiki reads the WEB's text and notes
  the conjecture.
- **The chapter's hard places.** 30:13, 18, 22 and 24 are among the most obscure lines in the book, and
  translations differ sharply (the WEB's *"However doesn't one stretch out a hand in his fall?"* at
  30:24 is one guess among several). The wiki's reading rests on the clearer lines around them and
  claims nothing that depends on those four.
- **"Dust and ashes" is the hinge, and it was missing from the wiki's thread page.**
  [[Sitting in Dust and Ashes]] had said that 2:8 and 42:6 are the only places in the book where ashes
  appear, which its own entry for 13:12 already contradicted, and it did not list 30:19 at all. Both are
  corrected there in this pass. [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]] already listed 30:19.
- **No contradiction between wiki pages found** beyond that correction. The chapter's existing forward
  references — 30:2 at [[A Shock of Grain in Its Season - Eliphaz's Promise and the Epilogue]], 30:10 at
  [[They Didn't Recognize Him - Marred Beyond Knowing]], 30:30 at
  [[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]], and 30:31 at [[Jubal]], [[Why Do the Wicked Live]]
  and [[Mourning and Lament]] — are borne out by the text.
