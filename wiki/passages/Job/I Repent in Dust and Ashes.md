# I Repent in Dust and Ashes

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 42:1–6

## Summary

*"Then Job answered Yahweh"* (42:1). These are Job's last words in the book: five verses, spoken to God
and to no one else, after the second speech out of the whirlwind. The first speech ended with Job's hand
on his mouth: *"I have spoken once … Yes, twice, but I will proceed no further"* (40:5). God did not
accept silence as an answer and spoke again (40:6). This is the answer he gets the second time.

It is short, and it is built almost entirely out of quotation. Job quotes God twice (42:3a, 42:4), uses
the vocabulary of the whole dialogue, and says only three things in his own voice: what he now knows
(42:2), what he did (42:3b), and what has changed (42:5–6).

**1. What he knows (42:2).** *"I know that you can do all things, and that no purpose of yours can be
restrained."* The first claim answers the second speech. God asked whether Job had *"an arm like God"*
(40:9), offered him the robe and the work of bringing the proud low, and then showed him two creatures
no human hand can touch. Job does not take the robe. He says the arm is God's.

The second clause has an exact echo outside the book. *No purpose of yours can be restrained* is 
*lōʾ-yibbāṣēr mimməkā məzimmâ*; at Babel God said of humanity, *"Now nothing will be withheld from them,
which they intend to do"* (Genesis 11:6) — *lōʾ-yibbāṣēr mēhem kōl ʾăšer yāzəmû*. **The same verb and
the same root for *intend*.** At Babel it was a warning about what human beings could do if united, and
God scattered them. In Job a human being says it about God, freely, as a confession. See
[[No Purpose of Yours Can Be Restrained - Job 42.2 and Genesis 11.6]] and [[The Tower of Babel]].

The Hebrew of *I know* has a textual wrinkle: the written text (*kətîb*) reads *yādaʿtā*, *you know*, and
the reading tradition (*qərê*) reads *yādaʿtî*, *I know*. The WEB follows the *qərê*, as nearly all
translations do (see Notes).

**2. What he did (42:3).** *"You asked, 'Who is this who hides counsel without knowledge?' therefore I
have uttered that which I did not understand, things too wonderful for me, which I didn't know."*

The quoted question is God's opening line at 38:2, and it is not quite word for word. At 38:2 the
speaker *darkens* (*maḥšîḵ*) counsel *by words* (*bəmillîn*) without knowledge; here he *hides* (*maʿlîm*)
counsel without knowledge, and *by words* is gone. Whether the change is Job's own or just the freedom of
quotation, *hides* is the weaker charge and *darkens* the one with chapter 3 in it (*"Let darkness and
the shadow of death claim it"*, 3:5). See [[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]].

Job's answer is that the charge is true. *I have uttered that which I did not understand* — and the
things he did not understand were *niplāʾôt mimmennî*, **too wonderful for me**. That is the root *p-l-ʾ*
of Genesis 18:14, *"Is anything too hard for Yahweh?"*, which the wiki traces at
[[Is Anything Too Hard for Yahweh]]. Job's confession is about range, not guilt: he spoke beyond what he
knew. **He does not confess any of the sins the friends named, and he does not withdraw the oath of
chapter 31.** God has not asked him to.

**3. God quoted again (42:4).** *"You said, 'Listen, now, and I will speak; I will question you, and you
will answer me.'"* The second half is the summons of 38:3 and 40:7, which in Hebrew it repeats exactly.
The first half — *listen, now, and I will speak* — is in neither of God's speeches. Its nearest wording
in the book is Elihu's (*"listen to me. Hold your peace, and I will speak"*, 33:31) and Job's own
procedure at 13:22: *"Then call, and I will answer; or let me speak, and you answer me."* The words *You
asked* and *You said* in 42:3–4 are the WEB's; the Hebrew has no introduction, and the quotations simply
stand in Job's mouth. Read that way, **Job is putting the whole hearing back together in a sentence** —
his own demand to speak and be answered, and God's reversal of it — before he says what the hearing did.

**4. What has changed (42:5).**

> *"I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you."* (42:5)

This is the turn of the book, and it is a turn from hearing to seeing. Job has spent the dialogue
hearing about God — from Eliphaz's night-vision, in which *"my ear received a whisper"* and *"a form was
before my eyes"* but *"I couldn't discern its appearance"* (4:12, 16); from the friends' tradition; from
his own *"How small a whisper do we hear of him!"* (26:14). In 28:22 even Destruction and Death have only
*"heard a rumor"* of wisdom *"with our ears"*. And he has said again and again that he cannot see God:
*"he goes by me, and I don't see him"* (9:11); *"He works to the north, but I can't see him"* (23:9).

Against all of that stood one hope, at 19:26–27: *"in my flesh shall I see God, whom I, even I, shall see
on my side. My eyes shall see, and not as a stranger."* **42:5 is that hope met, in its own vocabulary — the
eye (*ʿênî*, against 19:27's *ʿênay*) and the verb *rāʾâ*, to see (*rāʾātəkā*, against *rāʾû*).** Not after death and not by a redeemer standing on the
dust; now, out of a storm, in the body he is sitting in. See
[[Now My Eye Sees You - Hearing and Seeing in Job]] and [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]].

What Job *saw* is not described. The narrative reports a voice out of a whirlwind and nothing else —
no form, no figure, no glory. Either the storm itself is what he saw, or *seeing* here means what it
often means in Hebrew: knowing directly, rather than by report. The book does not say, and the wiki does
not choose. What it does say is that **the change is not in what Job knows about his case.** God told him
nothing about the wager, the accuser, or the reason for his suffering. The change is that he has been
addressed.

**5. Therefore (42:6).**

> *"Therefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes."* (42:6)

*Therefore* (*ʿal-kēn*) ties the verse to the seeing, not to any argument. And the verse is the most
debated line in the book, because each of its parts can be read more than one way.

- ***I abhor myself*** is one Hebrew word, *ʾemʾas*, and it has no object; *myself* is supplied. It is
  the verb of Job's despair in the dialogue — *"I loathe (māʾastî) my life"* (7:16), *"I despise
  (ʾemʾas) my life"* (9:21), in the same form as here. It can mean *I reject* (what? his words? his case?),
  *I despise*, or — taking a second root — *I melt away*, *I sink down*.
- ***Repent*** is *niḥamtî*, from *nḥm*. The verb means to change one's mind about something, to regret
  it, or to be comforted about it. It is the verb of God's regret in Genesis 6:6 (*"Yahweh was sorry that
  he had made man"*) and of his relenting at Exodus 32:14 and Jonah 3:10 — see
  [[Divine Grief and Relenting]]. And it is **the friends' verb**: they came *"to comfort him"*,
  *ûlənaḥămô* (2:11); Job called them *"miserable comforters"*, *mənaḥămê ʿāmāl* (16:2); he asked *"how can
  you comfort me with nonsense?"* (21:34); and his family *"comforted him"* at 42:11. **The comfort the
  friends could not give is in the verb of Job's last line.** See
  [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]].
- ***In dust and ashes*** is *ʿal-ʿāpār wāʾēper*, and *ʿal* can mean *on*, *in*, or *concerning*. *Dust and
  ashes* is Abraham's pair (Genesis 18:27) and Job's own at 30:19, *"I have become like dust and ashes"*. On
  one reading Job repents sitting on the ash-heap where he has been since 2:8. On another he is comforted,
  or changes his mind, *concerning* dust and ashes — that is, concerning the human condition, what Abraham
  called himself when he dared to speak to the Lord.

So the verse can be read, roughly, two ways:

1. ***"I despise myself, and repent in dust and ashes."*** The traditional reading, and the WEB's: an act
   of abasement before God, in the posture of a penitent.
2. ***"I retract, and am comforted concerning dust and ashes."*** Job withdraws his case (or his words),
   and accepts his place as a creature — dust and ashes — and finds comfort in it.

**The wiki records both and does not choose.** What either reading must fit is the next verse but one:
*"you have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job has"* (42:7). Whatever Job takes
back here, God does not count it as a sin that he said it. And whatever he repents of, it is not his
integrity — he swore at 27:5 that he would not put it away, and he does not. The friends wanted a
confession of guilt. **What Job gives is a confession of ignorance (42:3) and a change of posture before
a God he has now seen (42:5–6).** See [[Sitting in Dust and Ashes]] and [[Integrity]].

**What the reply answers.** It answers God, and it also answers the wager. The accuser's question was
*"Does Job fear God for nothing?"* (1:9), and his prediction was that Job would *"renounce you to your
face"* (1:11; 2:5). At 42:5 Job does not curse God to his face; he sees him. And **he says these words
before anything is given back.** The restoration begins at 42:10. When Job speaks his last line he is
still diseased, still childless, still poor, and still on the ash-heap. He has been given no reason and no
compensation — only an answer. If he fears God here, he fears him for nothing, and the accuser's
question is settled without the accuser ever hearing it. See [[Testing]] and [[Satan]].

And there is a pattern behind the pair *dust and ashes*. Abraham said it and then interceded for Sodom
(Genesis 18:27–32). Job says it and is then told to pray for the friends who wronged him, and God accepts
his prayer (42:8–10). **Both men use the phrase at the moment they speak to God with no standing to do
so, and both then become intercessors.** See [[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].

## Key verses

> **1** Then Job answered Yahweh,

> **2** “I know that you can do all things, and that no purpose of yours can be restrained.

> **3** You asked, ‘Who is this who hides counsel without knowledge?’ therefore I have uttered that which I did not understand, things too wonderful for me, which I didn’t know.

> **4** You said, ‘Listen, now, and I will speak; I will question you, and you will answer me.’

> **5** I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you.

> **6** Therefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes.”

## People

- [[Job (person)]] — speaking for the last time in the book, to God; confesses ignorance, not sin, and
  says *"now my eye sees you"* (42:5) while still on the ash-heap and before any restoration.
- [[God]] — *Yahweh*, addressed and quoted twice (42:3a from 38:2; 42:4b from 38:3 and 40:7); confessed as
  the one who *"can do all things"* (42:2). He does not speak in this pericope.
- [[Elihu the Buzite]] — not named; his *"Hold your peace, and I will speak"* (33:31) is the closest wording
  in the book to the first half of 42:4.
- [[Satan]] — not named; his question *"for nothing?"* (1:9) and his prediction *"to your face"* (1:11; 2:5)
  are answered here without his knowing it.
- [[Abraham]] — not named; *dust and ashes* (42:6) is his phrase at Genesis 18:27.

## Places

- None named. The scene is still the ash-heap of 2:8, in [[Uz]], which the passage does not mention.

## Themes

- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — *"I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you"*
  (42:5): the dialogue's *I don't see him* (9:11; 23:9) ended, and 19:26–27's hope met.
- [[Wisdom]] — *"things too wonderful for me, which I didn't know"* (42:3); the limit of human knowledge
  accepted.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Job's last words; *"I have uttered that which I did not understand"* (42:3);
  God's words quoted back.
- [[Divine Grief and Relenting]] — *niḥamtî* (42:6), the verb of God's *was sorry* (Genesis 6:6) and *relented*
  (Exodus 32:14; Jonah 3:10), here said by a man.
- [[Mourning and Lament]] — *dust and ashes* (42:6); the ash-heap of 2:8, and the comfort (*nḥm*) the friends
  came to give.
- [[Suffering]] — not explained and not mentioned; the answer to it is a meeting, not a reason.
- [[Integrity]] — not given up; what Job retracts is not the innocence of 27:5.
- [[The Fear of God]] — feared *for nothing* (1:9), before anything is restored.
- [[Testing]] — the wager of chapters 1–2 resolved in Job's own words, with no sign that he knows of it.
- [[Providence]] — *"no purpose of yours can be restrained"* (42:2).
- [[Righteousness]] — the man who confesses here is the one God says spoke rightly (42:7).

## Notes

- **Boundary.** The plan's 42:1–6 is kept. 42:1 opens Job's reply; 42:7 (*"It was so, that after Yahweh
  had spoken these words to Job"*) returns to prose and opens the epilogue.
- **42:2, *kətîb* and *qərê*.** The consonantal text reads *yādaʿtā*, *you know*; the Masoretic reading
  tradition reads *yādaʿtî*, *I know*. The *qərê* is almost universally followed and is the WEB's. The
  *kətîb* would make the line *"You know that you can do all things"* — possible, but strange as the opening
  of Job's reply. Recorded, not adopted.
- **42:3a and 38:2.** *Maʿlîm*, *hides*, against *maḥšîḵ*, *darkens*, and no *bəmillîn*. The wiki notes the
  difference and does not build on it; quotation in Hebrew poetry is often loose.
- **42:4.** Some commentators take 42:4 as Job's own words, not a quotation — Job asking God to listen while
  *he* speaks, as at 13:22 — and some regard 42:3a and 42:4 as a later scribe's insertion of God's lines. The
  WEB, by supplying *You asked* and *You said*, takes both as quotations. Recorded.
- **42:6.** The readings of *ʾemʾas*, *niḥamtî*, and *ʿal* are set out in the summary; the wiki does not
  choose between them. The Septuagint's rendering differs from the Hebrew here, but it has not been checked,
  so it is not described or quoted.
- **What Job saw (42:5).** Nothing in chapters 38–41 describes a vision; there is a voice and a storm. The
  wiki notes that *seeing* may be literal (the theophany) or may mean direct knowledge, and does not
  decide.
- **On contradictions.** No wiki page is contradicted by this pericope. One nuance is added to
  [[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]], which called the Hebrew of 38:2 and 42:3
  *the same at its core*: it is — *bəlî-dāʿat* in both — but the verb differs (*maḥšîḵ* / *maʿlîm*), and the
  page now says so.
