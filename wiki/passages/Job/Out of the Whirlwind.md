# Out of the Whirlwind

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 38:1–40:5

## Summary

*"Then Yahweh answered Job out of the whirlwind"* (38:1). It is the first time the narrator has used the
name **Yahweh** since 2:7, when the accuser *"went out from the presence of Yahweh"*; in thirty-five
chapters of poetry only Job has spoken it, once (12:9). The heading puts the book back in the prologue's
voice, and then the prologue's God speaks — for the first time to Job. He has been talked about by five
men since chapter 3. He has been summoned (*"Let the Almighty answer me!"*, 31:35), and the last speaker
said no one could reach him (37:23). Now he answers, and **he answers Job** — not the friends, not
[[Elihu the Buzite]], whose four speeches are passed over as if they had not happened.

What he says is not what anyone in the book expected. There is no word about the wager, the accuser, the
dead children, Job's sores, the friends' doctrine, or Job's oath. There is no charge of sin and no
acquittal. There are questions — more than fifty of them in two chapters — about the founding of the
earth, the sea, the dawn, the deep, death, light and darkness, snow, rain, the stars, and then about
animals: the lioness, the raven, the mountain goat, the wild donkey, the wild ox, the ostrich, the
war-horse, the hawk and the eagle. Then a single challenge (40:2), and Job's first answer, which is to
stop answering (40:4–5).

**1. "Who is this?" (38:1–3).** The *whirlwind* is *səʿārâ*, the storm-wind — **the word of Job's
complaint at 9:17**, *"he breaks me with a storm"*. The storm Job said God attacked him with is now the
place God speaks from. Then the first sentence:

> *"Who is this who darkens counsel by words without knowledge? Brace yourself like a man, for I will
> question you, then you answer me!"* (38:2–3)

*Darkens* is *maḥšîk*, the root of the darkness Job called down on his birthday in chapter 3 — *"Let that
day be darkness"* (3:4). *Without knowledge* (*bəlî-daʿat*) is the phrase Elihu used twice of Job (34:35;
35:16), and God now uses it too; but where Elihu said Job's words lacked knowledge to prove him wicked, God
says it to open an examination. See [[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]] and
[[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]].

*Brace yourself like a man* is literally *gird your loins like a geber* — a man, a warrior, the word Job
used of himself at 3:3 (*"There is a boy conceived"*, *geber*) and at 3:23. And the procedure is the one
Job proposed: *"Then call, and I will answer; or let me speak, and you answer me"* (13:22). **God accepts
Job's terms and takes the first of the two options** — he will call, and Job will answer. The trial Job
said could not be held (9:32–33) is being held, and Job is not the questioner.

**2. The foundation and the sea (38:4–11).** *"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?
Declare, if you have understanding"* (38:4). The earth is a building: measured, with a line stretched on it,
its bases sunk, its cornerstone laid (38:5–6) — *"when the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of
God shouted for joy"* (38:7). **The sons of God of 1:6 and 2:1 return**, not as an assembly where the
accuser makes a wager but as a choir at the world's dedication. The heavenly court appears once in the
poetry, and it is singing. See [[The Sons of God]] and [[The Divine Council]].

*Where were you?* had been Eliphaz's taunt — *"Are you the first man who was born? Or were you brought out
before the hills?"* (15:7) — and God will press it home with the one sarcastic line in the speech, at 38:21.
The question has the same point as Proverbs 8, where Wisdom was present *"when he marked out the
foundations of the earth"* (8:29): someone was there; it was not Job. See
[[The First Man and Wisdom Before the Hills]].

Then the sea:

> *"Or who shut up the sea with doors, when it broke out of the womb, when I made clouds its garment, and
> wrapped it in thick darkness, marked out for it my bound, set bars and doors, and said, 'Here you may
> come, but no further. Here your proud waves shall be stayed?'"* (38:8–11)

**This is chapter 3's womb and chapter 3's doors.** Job cursed the night *"because it didn't shut up the
doors of my mother's womb"* (3:10). Here a womb opens, something is born, and God shuts the doors — on the
sea. The sea, which Job feared he was being treated as (*"Am I a sea, or a sea monster, that you put a guard
over me?"*, 7:12), is a newborn: clothed in cloud, swaddled in darkness, and given a limit. The guard Job
resented is a parent's bound, and the sea's *proud waves* are stopped there and not destroyed. Job had said
God *"described a boundary on the surface of the waters"* (26:10); God says what the boundary is for. See
[[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]] and [[Let That Day Be Darkness - Job 3 as Counter-Creation]].

**3. The dawn and the wicked (38:12–15).** *"Have you commanded the morning in your days, and caused the dawn
to know its place; that it might take hold of the ends of the earth, and shake the wicked out of it?"*
(38:12–13). The dawn takes the earth by its edges like a cloth and shakes, and the wicked fall out. *"It is
changed as clay under the seal"* (38:14) — the light gives the land its relief, as a seal gives clay its
shape. *"From the wicked, their light is withheld. The high arm is broken"* (38:15).

**This is the one place in the speech where the wicked appear**, and it answers Job's chapter 24 — *"These
are of those who rebel against the light … For the morning is to all of them like thick darkness"*
(24:13, 17). Job said the wicked did their work in the dark and went unpunished. God says the morning is a
daily judgment on them: the dawn shakes them out, and their *light* — which is darkness (24:17) — is taken
from them. It is not the verdict Job asked for in chapter 21 and it is not the friends' retribution; it is
the world's ordinary daybreak described as justice, every day, without a trial. See [[Retribution]] and
[[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]. *Eyelids of the morning* (3:9) were what Job wished his
birth-night never to see; God asks whether Job has ever commanded a morning.

**4. The deep, death, light and darkness (38:16–21).** *"Have you entered into the springs of the sea? Or
have you walked in the recesses of the deep? Have the gates of death been revealed to you? Or have you seen
the gates of the shadow of death?"* (38:16–17). The *recesses* are *ḥēqer*, the *unsearchable* of 5:9, 9:10
and 36:26 — what everyone in the book has said cannot be searched, God asks whether Job has walked in. The
*shadow of death* is *ṣalmāwet*, the word Job called down on his day (3:5) and the land he expected to go to
(10:21–22). **Job has spent his speeches wishing himself dead; God asks whether he has seen the gate.** See
[[Sheol]] and [[Higher Than Heaven, Deeper Than Sheol - The Measure of the Unsearchable]].

> *"What is the way to the dwelling of light? As for darkness, where is its place, that you should take it
> to its bound, that you should discern the paths to its house? Surely you know, for you were born then, and
> the number of your days is great!"* (38:19–21)

Light and darkness each have a *house* and a *bound*. Job's chapter 3 treated them as weapons to aim at a
day; God treats them as residents with addresses, both kept. And then the sarcasm, which lands exactly where
Job's first speech began: **his birth**. *"Let the day perish in which I was born"* (3:3); *"Surely you know,
for you were born then"*. See [[Light and Darkness]].

**5. The storehouses of weather (38:22–30).** *"Have you entered the treasuries of the snow, or have you seen
the treasures of the hail, which I have reserved against the time of trouble, against the day of battle and
war?"* (38:22–23). Hail as a weapon kept for war is the hail of Joshua 10:11, where *"Yahweh hurled down great
stones from the sky"* and more died *"from the hailstones than those whom the children of Israel killed with
the sword"*. Then lightning and east wind (38:24), and the channel cut for the flood and *"the path for the
thunderstorm"* (38:25) — the *path for the thunderstorm* of 28:26, word for word in Hebrew, where God *"made a
decree for the rain, and a way for the lightning of the thunder"* when he saw wisdom. These are also
Elihu's subjects — the snow of 37:6, the rain of 36:27, the lightning of 36:32 — and God's grammar is the one
Elihu borrowed at 37:15–18. See [[From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1]].

And the line that most changes the book's angle of vision:

> *"To cause it to rain on a land where no man is; on the wilderness, in which there is no man; to satisfy
> the waste and desolate ground, to cause the tender grass to grow?"* (38:26–27)

**Rain on land where no one lives.** Every speaker in the book, Job included, has assumed that the world is
organized around human beings — that rain is a reward, drought a punishment, and weather a message (so
Elihu, 37:13). God describes rain that is no one's reward and no one's lesson, falling where no human will
ever see it, for the grass. *Waste and desolate* is *šōʾâ ûmšōʾâ*, the phrase of 30:3, where it described the
wasteland of the outcasts who mock Job. The land of Job's humiliation is the land God waters. See
[[A Brother to Jackals - Job 30.29 and the Creatures of the Ruins]] and [[Providence]].

*"Does the rain have a father? Or who fathers the drops of dew? Out of whose womb came the ice?"* (38:28–29).
The weather is given a father and a mother and has neither; the birth imagery of 38:8 returns, and Job, who
cursed the womb he came out of, is asked about the womb of the frost.

**6. The stars and the clouds (38:31–38).** *"Can you bind the cluster of the Pleiades, or loosen the cords
of Orion? Can you lead the constellations out in their season? Or can you guide the Bear with her cubs? Do
you know the laws of the heavens?"* (38:31–33). **Job named these three at 9:9** as things God makes; God
names them as things he governs, by *laws* (*ḥuqqôt*) Job does not know. See
[[The Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades]].

*"Can you lift up your voice to the clouds, That abundance of waters may cover you?"* (38:34) — *abundance of
waters* is *šipʿat-mayim*, the *"floods of waters"* that Eliphaz said would cover Job at 22:11 (the WEB renders
the same Hebrew two ways); here it is rain Job cannot summon.
*"Can you send out lightnings, that they may go? Do they report to you, 'Here we are?'"* (38:35) — the
lightning answers God with *hinnēnû*, the servant's *here I am*. Then the one question about the human mind:
*"Who has put wisdom in the inward parts? Or who has given understanding to the mind?"* (38:36). The two nouns
are rare and disputed (see Notes). *"Who can count the clouds by wisdom?"* (38:37). See [[Wisdom]].

**7. The lioness and the raven (38:39–41).** At the end of the chapter the speech turns from the world's
structure to the creatures in it, and starts with feeding.

> *"Can you hunt the prey for the lioness, or satisfy the appetite of the young lions, when they crouch in
> their dens, and lie in wait in the thicket? Who provides for the raven his prey, when his young ones cry to
> God, and wander for lack of food?"* (38:39–41)

Job said God hunted him *"like a lion"* (10:16). God says he hunts *for* the lion. And the raven's young
*cry to God* — *yəšawwəʿû*, the cry for help of Job's own complaint (*"I cry for help, but there is no
justice"*, 19:7; and *"I cry to you, and you do not answer me"*, 30:20). **The first creatures God names
after the stars are crying to him, and he feeds them.** The speech does not apply it to Job, and does not
need to. Psalm 147:9 says it as praise — *"He provides food for the livestock, and for the young ravens when
they call"* — and Jesus as an argument: *"Consider the ravens … God feeds them. How much more valuable are you
than birds!"* (Luke 12:24). See [[Who Provides for the Raven - Job 38.41, Psalm 147.9, and Luke 12.24]].

**8. The wild things (39:1–18).** Chapter 39 is a gallery of animals, and almost every one of them is
outside human use.

- **The mountain goat and the doe** (39:1–4): *"Do you know the time when the mountain goats give birth?"*
  God watches births no one sees. *"Their young ones become strong … They go out, and don't return again"*
  (39:4) — a birth that ends in leaving, said without lament.
- **The wild donkey** (39:5–8): *"Who has set the wild donkey free? … Whose home I have made the wilderness,
  and the salt land his dwelling place? He scorns the tumult of the city, neither does he hear the shouting
  of the driver."* God claims the animal's freedom as his own gift — the *pereʾ* of Ishmael's oracle
  (Genesis 16:12) and of Zophar's insult (11:12). See [[A Wild Donkey of a Man - Genesis 16.12 and Job 11.12]].
- **The wild ox** (39:9–12): *"Will the wild ox be content to serve you? … Will you trust him, because his
  strength is great?"* The strongest animal in the list is the one that will not work for Job. (The *rêm*
  is the KJV's *unicorn*; see Notes.)
- **The ostrich** (39:13–18): the comic portrait. She lays her eggs in the dust and forgets them, *"deals
  harshly with her young ones, as if they were not hers … because God has deprived her of wisdom"* (39:16–17)
  — and yet *"When she lifts up herself on high, she scorns the horse and his rider"* (39:18). **God made a
  creature without wisdom and is amused by it.** Job had called himself *"a companion to ostriches"* (30:29),
  a creature of the ruins; God describes the ostrich with something like affection. See
  [[A Brother to Jackals - Job 30.29 and the Creatures of the Ruins]].

Three of these animals laugh. The donkey *scorns* (*yiśḥaq*) the city (39:7), the ostrich *scorns*
(*tiśḥaq*) the horse (39:18), and the horse *mocks* (*yiśḥaq*) at fear (39:22). It is the verb of Job's *"But
now those who are younger than I have me in derision"* (*śāḥăqû*, 30:1). The derision that humiliated Job is,
in God's animals, freedom.

**9. The war-horse (39:19–25).** The longest portrait, and the only domesticated animal — but not a tame one.
*"Have you given the horse might? Have you clothed his neck with a quivering mane? … He mocks at fear, and is
not dismayed, neither does he turn back from the sword … As often as the trumpet sounds he snorts, 'Aha!' He
smells the battle afar off, the thunder of the captains, and the shouting"* (39:19–25). The horse belongs to
the army, but its courage does not; God asks whether Job gave it. See [[War]].

**10. The hawk and the eagle (39:26–30).** *"Is it by your wisdom that the hawk soars, and stretches her wings
toward the south? Is it at your command that the eagle mounts up, and makes his nest on high?"* (39:26–27).
*Your wisdom* and *your command* are the two things the whole speech has been asking about. The eagle sees
from the cliff — *"His eyes see it afar off"* (39:29) — and the speech ends, startlingly, on carrion:

> *"His young ones also suck up blood. Where the slain are, there he is."* (39:30)

The first speech began with morning stars singing and ends with eaglets drinking blood. **God includes death
in the order he is proud of**: the lioness's prey, the raven's prey, the eagle's slain. The line became a
proverb, and Jesus used it: *"For wherever the carcass is, that is where the vultures gather together"*
(Matthew 24:28; compare Luke 17:37). See [[Where the Slain Are - Job 39.30 and Matthew 24.28]] and
[[Life and Death]].

**11. The challenge (40:1–2).** *"Moreover Yahweh answered Job"* — a new heading, though Job has not spoken.
God has stopped and is waiting.

> *"Shall he who argues contend with the Almighty? He who argues with God, let him answer it."* (40:2)

*Contend* is *rîb*, the lawsuit — the case Job brought. *He who argues with God* is *mōkîaḥ ʾĕlôah*, and
**mōkîaḥ is the umpire of 9:33**: *"There is no umpire (mōkîaḥ) between us."* Job wanted an arbiter between
himself and God; God calls Job the arbiter — the one who has set himself to correct God — and asks him to
answer. *The Almighty* is *Šadday*, the name Job used to summon him (*"Let the Almighty answer me!"*,
31:35). The Almighty has answered; now it is Job's turn. See [[The Witness in Heaven]],
[[Who Will Contend with Me - Job 13, Isaiah 50, and Romans 8]], and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

**12. "I lay my hand on my mouth" (40:3–5).** *"Then Job answered Yahweh"* — the first time in the book that
Job is said to answer Yahweh, and the first time since the prologue that he speaks to God and is heard.

> *"Behold, I am of small account. What shall I answer you? I lay my hand on my mouth. I have spoken once,
> and I will not answer; Yes, twice, but I will proceed no further."* (40:4–5)

*I am of small account* is *qallōtî* — I am light, slight, of no weight. The root is *qll*, which in the
intensive is *curse*: it is the verb of 3:1, where Job *"cursed (wayqallēl) the day of his birth"*. The
link is the wiki's observation and the stems differ, but the irony holds in the vocabulary Job has. The man
who opened by making his day *light* in the sense of cursed now says he is light himself. And the man who
swore he would approach God *"as a prince"* (31:37) has nothing to say.

*What shall I answer you?* is the question of his oath — *"When he visits, what shall I answer him?"*
(31:14) — now answered by not answering. *I lay my hand on my mouth* is the gesture he told the friends to
make (21:5) and the princes once made before him (29:9). See
[[Lay Your Hand on Your Mouth - Job 21.5, 29.9, and 40.4]].

*Once … twice* (*ʾaḥat … ûštayim*) is Elihu's formula: *"For God speaks once, yes twice"* (33:14). **Job uses
of his own speech the numbers Elihu used of God's.** And it is a refusal to go on, not a confession: Job
does not say he was wrong, does not withdraw his oath, and does not repent. He says he will stop. See
[[Once, Yes Twice - Job 33.14, 40.5, and Psalm 62.11]].

**How to read it.** It is the most quoted non-answer in literature, and the complaint is easy to state: Job
asked why, and God asked him where he was. But four things in the text push against reading it as power
crushing a question.

1. **God answers Job, in person, at length, and Job alone.** Elihu said no one could reach him (37:23) and
   that to ask for a hearing was to ask to be *swallowed up* (37:20). Job asks, and is not swallowed up;
   he is examined. The answer is first of all that there is an answer.
2. **The speech is built out of Job's words.** The whirlwind is the storm of 9:17; *darkens* is chapter 3's
   darkness; the womb and doors are 3:10; the shadow of death is 3:5; the birth is 3:3; the stars are 9:9;
   the lion is 10:16; the wasteland is 30:3; the ostrich is 30:29; the arbiter is 9:33. God has been
   listening. See [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]].
3. **It removes the human centre without removing the human.** The rain falls where no man is; the wild
   animals are free of every human purpose; the ostrich is foolish and the eagle feeds on the dead, and God
   is pleased with all of it. That is a refutation of the friends' world, in which everything that happens
   is a message to a man about his sins. It is not a refutation of Job's integrity, which is never
   mentioned. See [[Creation]] and [[Providence]].
4. **It charges Job with ignorance, not wickedness.** *"Words without knowledge"* (38:2) is the whole
   charge so far. The heavier one — *"Will you condemn me, that you may be justified?"* (40:8) — belongs to
   the second speech. God does not accuse Job of anything that caused his suffering; the speech has no
   *because*.

**And it is not enough.** Job's first answer is silence, and God speaks again (40:6). Whatever Job has
understood at 40:4–5, it is not yet what he says at 42:5, *"now my eye sees you"*. The first speech produces
a man who will stop talking; the second will produce one who has seen — *Behemoth and Leviathan*, the next
pericope, not yet ingested.

## People

- [[God]] — named **Yahweh** by the narrator for the first time since 2:7 (38:1; 40:1, 3); speaks out of the
  storm, examines Job on creation and the creatures, and calls himself *Šadday* (40:2), the title of Job's
  summons (31:35). He names no sin, no reason, and no wager.
- [[Job (person)]] — addressed throughout, never by name; examined, not accused of wickedness; answers once,
  briefly, and refuses to go on (40:4–5).
- [[The Sons of God]] — at the founding of the earth, *"all the sons of God shouted for joy"* (38:7), with the
  morning stars: their third and last appearance in the book, and their only one in the poetry.
- [[Elihu the Buzite]] — not mentioned. He spoke last, about this storm; God speaks out of it to Job and
  passes over him entirely.
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — not named; his *"Are you the first man who was born?"* (15:7) is the question
  God asks at 38:4 and 38:21, and his *šipʿat-mayim*, the *floods of waters* of 22:11, is God's *abundance of waters* at 38:34.
- [[Zophar the Naamathite]] — not named; his wild donkey (11:12) is God's free creature at 39:5–8.
- **The animals** — the lioness, raven, mountain goat, doe, wild donkey, wild ox, ostrich, horse, hawk, and
  eagle — are creatures, not persons, and get no pages.

## Places

- [[Sheol]] — not by name; *"the gates of death … the gates of the shadow of death"* (38:17), the underworld
  under another of its names, and the place Job has wished himself into since chapter 3.
- The *springs of the sea* and *recesses of the deep* (38:16), the *treasuries of the snow* (38:22), and the
  *dwelling of light* (38:19) are cosmic regions, not places with pages. The *south* (39:26) is a direction.

## Themes

- [[Creation]] — the earth as a building with foundations and a cornerstone (38:4–7); the sea born and
  bounded (38:8–11); light and darkness in their houses (38:19–20); the stars under law (38:31–33).
- [[Providence]] — rain *"on a land where no man is"* (38:26); food for the lioness and the raven (38:39–41);
  births that only God watches (39:1–3).
- [[Wisdom]] — *"Who has put wisdom in the inward parts?"* (38:36); the ostrich deprived of it (39:17); *"Is
  it by your wisdom that the hawk soars?"* (39:26).
- [[Light and Darkness]] — *"Who is this who darkens counsel?"* (38:2); the dawn (38:12–15); the dwelling of
  light and the place of darkness (38:19–20).
- [[Suffering]] — never mentioned; the speech answers the sufferer without naming the suffering.
- [[Retribution]] — the dawn that *"shake[s] the wicked out"* of the earth and withholds their light
  (38:13–15); and otherwise a world in which weather is not a verdict (38:26–27).
- [[Language and Speech]] — *"words without knowledge"* (38:2); *"I will question you, then you answer me"*
  (38:3); *"I have spoken once … I will proceed no further"* (40:5).
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — the hearing Job asked for, granted on his own terms (13:22); and *mōkîaḥ*, the
  umpire of 9:33, applied to Job (40:2).
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — *"Here your proud waves shall be stayed"* (38:11); *"The high arm is
  broken"* (38:15); and *"I am of small account"* (40:4) against *"as a prince would I go near to him"*
  (31:37).
- [[Judgment]] — the morning as a daily judgment on the wicked (38:13–15); *"Shall he who argues contend with
  the Almighty?"* (40:2).
- [[Life and Death]] — the gates of death (38:17); the hunt and the prey (38:39–41); *"Where the slain are,
  there he is"* (39:30).
- [[The Names of God]] — *Yahweh* in the narration again (38:1; 40:1, 3); *Šadday* and *ʾĔlôah* in God's own
  mouth (40:2).
- [[War]] — the hail reserved *"against the day of battle and war"* (38:23); the war-horse (39:19–25).
- [[Famine and Plenty]] — prey for the lion and the raven's young *"for lack of food"* (38:39–41); grass on
  the desolate ground (38:27).

## Notes

- **Boundary.** The plan's 38:1–40:5 is kept: one divine speech (38:2–39:30), a one-verse coda that invites a
  reply (40:1–2), and Job's reply (40:3–5). 40:6 begins again with *"Then Yahweh answered Job out of the
  whirlwind"*, the heading of 38:1 repeated, and opens the second speech. Some commentators move 40:1–2 to
  the end of chapter 39 and treat it as the speech's conclusion, which is how it is read here; the verse
  numbering does not affect the division.
- **The number of questions.** "More than fifty" is a count of interrogative clauses in the WEB of 38:2–40:2,
  and the exact figure depends on how compound questions are divided; it is offered as an order of
  magnitude.
- **38:36.** *Ṭuḥôt* (*inward parts*) occurs elsewhere only at Psalm 51:6, and *śekwî* (*mind*) only here.
  Because the verse sits between clouds and rain, many modern readers take the two as birds that forecast
  weather — the ibis, which announced the Nile flood in Egypt, and the rooster, which announces the dawn —
  so that the verse asks who gave the birds their weather-wisdom. The WEB's *inward parts … mind* is the
  traditional reading. Recorded, not adopted.
- **39:9.** *Rêm* is the wild ox, the aurochs, now extinct; the KJV, following the Septuagint's
  *monokerōs*, made it the *unicorn*. Nothing in the text supports a single horn.
- **39:13.** *Rənānîm*, the bird of *ringing cries*, is identified as the ostrich from its description; the
  description (eggs left in the sand, apparent neglect of the young) reflects how the ostrich was observed,
  not modern ornithology, and the speech's point is the creature's strangeness, not a field note. It is
  **not** the word of Job's *"companion to ostriches"* (30:29), which is *bənôt yaʿănâ*; the link between the
  two passages drawn above is to the creature as the WEB and the tradition identify it, not to a shared
  Hebrew word.
- **40:4, *qallōtî*.** The verb is in the simple stem, *I am light*; the *curse* of 3:1 is the intensive
  stem of the same root. The connection is lexical, not grammatical, and is offered as a resonance.
- **40:2, *mōkîaḥ*.** The participle of *ykḥ*, the verb of Job's *"I desire to reason with God"* (13:3) and
  *"There the upright might reason with him"* (23:7), of the *umpire* of 9:33, and of Elihu's *"no one who
  convinced Job"* (32:12). God uses Job's own legal word for the role Job has taken.
- **What the speech omits** is as notable as what it includes: no word of Israel, covenant, law, temple, or
  sacrifice; almost no human beings (the wicked of 38:13–15, the riders and captains of 39:18–25, the
  *"slain"* of 39:30); and nothing of the prologue. The reader knows why Job suffered; God does not tell
  Job, and the book does not make him.
- **On contradictions.** No wiki page is contradicted by the text here. One connection page is corrected:
  [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]] said, before this pericope was ingested, that the first questions
  track chapter 3 *in chapter 3's order*. Checked against the text, the correspondences hold but run in
  **reverse** (3:10 → 3:9 → 3:5 → 3:4 → 3:3), and the opening questions (38:4–7) have no counterpart in
  chapter 3. The page now records both the original claim and the correction.
