# Elihu on God's Greatness

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 36:1–37:24

## Summary

The fourth heading is not *"Elihu answered"* but *"Elihu also continued"* (36:1) — there is still nothing
to answer. Job has been silent since 31:40, and [[Elihu the Buzite]] has now spoken through three
invitations to reply (33:5, 32; 34:33) without receiving one. This is his last speech, and it is the one
in which he stops arguing about Job and starts describing God. It falls into two halves, and the hinge
is a single verse: *"Behold, God is great, and we don't know him"* (36:26). Before it, the doctrine of
**suffering as instruction**, applied to Job directly; after it, a hymn to God in the weather — rain,
cloud, thunder, lightning, snow, ice, wind — that grows louder as it goes, until Elihu says his heart is
trembling (37:1). It ends in the north, in *"golden splendor"* and *"awesome majesty"* (37:22). **The
next verse is *"Then Yahweh answered Job out of the whirlwind"*** (38:1).

**1. "One who is perfect in knowledge is with you" (36:1–4).** *"Bear with me a little, and I will show
you; for I still have something to say on God's behalf"* (36:2). *On God's behalf* — *lēʾĕlôah*, for
God — is the brief Job accused the friends of holding: *"Will you speak unrighteously for God?"* (13:7).
Elihu takes it up without embarrassment, and promises to *"ascribe righteousness to my Maker"* (36:3) —
*my Maker* again, as at 32:22 and 35:10. Then the claim that has made readers wince for centuries:
*"For truly my words are not false. One who is perfect in knowledge is with you"* (36:4). *Təmîm
dēʿôt*, **perfect in knowledge** — and at 37:16 the same phrase, *təmîm dēʿîm*, is a title of God:
*"the wondrous works of him who is perfect in knowledge"*. **Elihu calls himself what he will call
God**, a speech apart. *Tāmîm* is the root of *tām*, the *blameless* of 1:1, Job's own adjective. Some
readers take 36:4 as sheer presumption, of a piece with the wineskins of 32:19; others as Elihu's claim
that his knowledge is *sound* — *"my words are not false"* is the parallel line — and fetched *"from afar"*
(36:3), not his own. See [[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]].

**2. The bound and the opened ear (36:5–15).** *"Behold, God is mighty, and doesn't despise anyone"*
(36:5) — *lōʾ yimʾās*, the verb of Bildad's *"God will not cast away a blameless man"* (8:20) and of Job's
*"that you should despise the work of your hands"* (10:3). Then the moral order in its plainest terms:
*"He doesn't preserve the life of the wicked, but gives to the afflicted their right"* (36:6). *Doesn't
preserve the life* — *lōʾ-yəḥayyeh* — is the direct contradiction of Job's chapter 21, *"Why do the wicked
live?"* (21:7), in the same verb. The righteous he seats *"with kings on the throne"* (36:7), Hannah's
picture of the poor raised *"to make them sit with princes"* (1 Samuel 2:8). See [[Retribution]] and
[[Kingship and Empire]].

And the righteous who suffer? The paragraph that follows is Elihu's doctrine of [[Suffering]], restated
from 33:14–30 in its most compact form:

> *"If they are bound in fetters, and are taken in the cords of afflictions, then he shows them their
> work, and their transgressions, that they have behaved themselves proudly. He also opens their ears
> to instruction, and commands that they return from iniquity. If they listen and serve him, they shall
> spend their days in prosperity, and their years in pleasures. But if they don't listen, they shall
> perish by the sword; they shall die without knowledge."* (36:8–12)

Fetters and cords are a prisoner's; the affliction is a captivity with a purpose. *He opens their ears*
is 33:16's *"he opens the ears of men"*, and *instruction* is *mûsār*, the *chastening* of Eliphaz's
*"do not despise the chastening of the Almighty"* (5:17). The fault named is not any crime but pride —
*"that they have behaved themselves proudly"* (*yitgabbārû*, 36:9). And the two outcomes are those of
Deuteronomy: listen, and prosper; refuse, and *"die without knowledge"* — *bəlî-daʿat*, **the phrase
of 35:16**, *"he multiplies words without knowledge"*. Elihu does not draw the line from one to the
other. He does not need to. See [[Do Not Despise the Chastening - Job 5.17, Proverbs 3.11-12, and Hebrews 12]].

The godless, by contrast, *"don't cry for help when he binds them"* (36:13) — the cry of 35:9, not
uttered at all — and *"die in youth"*, their life ending *"among the unclean"* (36:14), a word the WEB
softens (see Notes). Then the line that sums up Elihu's whole contribution to the book:

> *"He delivers the afflicted by their affliction, and opens their ear in oppression."* (36:15)

*Yəḥallēṣ ʿānî bəʿonyô* — the afflicted one *through* his affliction. The friends said suffering was the
sentence; Elihu says it is the *means*. **It is the one idea in his four speeches that the friends never
had**, and he ends his argumentative section by saying it in a single line. See [[Testing]].

**3. "You have chosen this rather than affliction" (36:16–21).** Now the doctrine is turned on Job, and
the text becomes difficult (see Notes). *"Yes, he would have allured you out of distress, into a wide
place, where there is no restriction. That which is set on your table would be full of fatness"*
(36:16). The *wide place* is the Psalms' picture of rescue, *"He brought me out also into a large place"*
(Psalm 18:19); the *table full of fatness* is the life of 29:6, *"my steps were washed with butter"*.
**God was luring Job out**, Elihu says, and Job would not come: *"But you are full of the judgment of the
wicked. Judgment and justice take hold of you"* (36:17). Job has been so consumed with the case — with
*dîn*, the lawsuit Elihu conceded was before God at 35:14 — that the case has hold of him.

Two warnings follow. *"Don't let riches entice you to wrath, neither let the great size of a bribe turn
you aside"* (36:18). The *bribe* is *kōper*, **the word of 33:24**, *"I have found a ransom"*. On the WEB's
reading Elihu warns Job against being bought; on another, widely held, *kōper* keeps its sense from 33:24
and the *great ransom* is Job's suffering itself — do not let the size of the price you are paying turn
you away. See [[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]]. *"Would your wealth sustain
you in distress?"* (36:19) — the wealth Job no longer has.

And then: *"Don't desire the night, when people are cut off in their place"* (36:20). **This is Job's
first speech remembered.** In chapter 3 he wished the night of his conception to be seized by darkness
(3:3–6) and spoke for those who *"long for death, but it doesn't come"* (3:21). Elihu tells him not to want it.
*"Take heed, don't regard iniquity; for you have chosen this rather than affliction"* (36:21): Job has
preferred protest — the *iniquity* of charging God — to accepting the affliction as instruction. That is
the charge in its final form. It is no longer that Job sinned before his suffering (34:37), but that he
has refused its lesson. See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] and [[Light and Darkness]].

**4. "Who is a teacher like him?" (36:22–25).** *"Behold, God is exalted in his power. Who is a teacher
like him? Who has prescribed his way for him? Or who can say, 'You have committed unrighteousness?'"*
(36:22–23). The questions begin here, and they are the bridge from the lesson to the hymn. *Teacher* is
*môreh*, from the verb Job used when he asked, *"Teach me, and I will hold my peace"* (6:24) — and which
Elihu put in the mouth of a penitent at 34:32, *"Teach me that which I don't see"*. **The teacher Job
asked for is God, and the lesson is the world.** *"Remember that you magnify his work, whereof men have
sung"* (36:24): the work is to be sung, not litigated. *"All men have looked on it. Man sees it afar
off"* (36:25). See [[Wisdom]] and [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

**5. The hinge (36:26).** *"Behold, God is great, and we don't know him. The number of his years is
unsearchable."* *Lōʾ-ḥēqer* — **the *ḥēqer* Eliphaz used at 5:9 and Job repeated at 9:10**, *"great things
past finding out"*, and the word God will use at 38:16 for the *recesses* of the deep. Job had asked
whether God's years were *"as man's years"* (10:5); Elihu answers that they cannot be counted. The verse
is the confession every speaker in the book has made in some form, and the only one in which the speaker
includes himself: *we* don't know him. See
[[Higher Than Heaven, Deeper Than Sheol - The Measure of the Unsearchable]].

**6. The rain and the storm (36:27–33).** *"For he draws up the drops of water, which distill in rain
from his vapor, which the skies pour down and which drop on man abundantly"* (36:27–28). *His vapor* is
*ʾēd* — a word found in only one other verse of the Bible, Genesis 2:6, *"a mist went up from the earth,
and watered the whole surface of the ground"*. The first water of the garden is the first thing Elihu's
hymn describes. Eliphaz praised the God *"who gives rain on the earth"* (5:10), and Job the God who
*"binds up the waters in his thick clouds"* (26:8); Elihu describes the cycle — water drawn up, distilled,
poured down.

Then the storm assembles: *"can any understand the spreading of the clouds, and the thunderings of his
pavilion?"* (36:29). The *pavilion*, *sukkâ*, is the booth of cloud of Psalm 18:11 — *"He made darkness
his hiding place, his pavilion around him, darkness of waters, thick clouds of the skies"*. *"He spreads
his light around him. He covers the bottom of the sea"* (36:30). And the storm has two purposes: *"For by
these he judges the people. He gives food in abundance"* (36:31). The same rain that floods is the rain
that feeds. See [[Judgment]] and [[Famine and Plenty]].

*"He covers his hands with the lightning, and commands it to strike the mark"* (36:32). *Strike the mark*
is *bəmapgîaʿ*, from *pāgaʿ*, to strike or meet — and **Job used the noun of himself**: *"Why have you set
me as a mark for you?"* (*mipgāʿ*, 7:20). The word is rare and the link is the wiki's observation, but it
is exact: in chapter 7 Job was God's target, and in chapter 36 the lightning is aimed. *"Its noise tells
about him, and the livestock also concerning the storm that comes up"* (36:33) — even the cattle sense it
coming.

**7. "At this my heart trembles" (37:1–5).** Then Elihu breaks off his own description:

> *"Yes, at this my heart trembles, and is moved out of its place. Hear, oh, hear the noise of his voice,
> the sound that goes out of his mouth. He sends it out under the whole sky, and his lightning to the ends
> of the earth. After it a voice roars. He thunders with the voice of his majesty. He doesn't hold back
> anything when his voice is heard. God thunders marvelously with his voice. He does great things, which
> we can't comprehend."* (37:1–5)

**The thunder is God's voice** — *qôl*, five times in four verses — and *the voice of his majesty* (*qôl
gəʾônô*) is the language of Psalm 29, where *"Yahweh's voice is full of majesty"* (29:4) and *"The God of
glory thunders"* (29:3). Job had said that God's thunder was beyond hearing: *"How small a whisper do we
hear of him! But the thunder of his power who can understand?"* (26:14). Elihu says, *hear it*. And God
will say it of himself: *"Can you thunder with a voice like him?"* (40:9). See
[[The Voice of His Majesty - Job 37.2-5 and Psalm 29]].

Many readers take 37:1 as the narrative's own signal: that the storm is not only Elihu's subject but is
rising as he speaks, and that the *whirlwind* (*səʿārâ*) of 38:1 is the storm he has been describing.
The text does not say so; it only places the two side by side. See
[[From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1]].

**8. Snow, ice, and the sealed hand (37:6–13).** *"For he says to the snow, 'Fall on the earth;' likewise
to the shower of rain, and to the showers of his mighty rain. He seals up the hand of every man, that all
men whom he has made may know it. Then the animals take cover, and remain in their dens"* (37:6–8). God
speaks to the weather and it obeys — the creation by word of Genesis 1, applied to every winter. *He
seals up the hand* (*yaḥtôm*, the verb of 9:7, where God *"seals up the stars"*, and of 33:16, where he
*"seals their instruction"*) is the stopping of work: in the hard rains no one can labour in the field,
and men and animals alike take shelter. **The weather is a lesson in creaturehood**, *"that all men whom
he has made may know it"*. *"Out of its room comes the storm"* (37:9) — *ḥeder*, the *chamber*, the word of
9:9's *"rooms of the south"*. *"By the breath of God, ice is given"* (37:10): the breath that keeps all
flesh alive (34:14–15) is also the breath that freezes the waters.

And the clouds go where he sends them, *"that they may do whatever he commands them on the surface of the
habitable world, whether it is for correction, or for his land, or for loving kindness, that he causes it
to come"* (37:12–13). *Correction* is *šēbeṭ*, **the rod** — the word of Job's *"Let him take his rod away
from me"* (9:34). *Loving kindness* is *ḥesed*. The same rain can be either, and the hymn does not say how
to tell them apart. That is the nearest Elihu comes to Job's position: the event does not interpret
itself. See [[Compassion and Mercy]] and [[Creation]].

**9. "Listen to this, Job" (37:14–20).** The hymn turns into an examination, and the form is new.

> *"Listen to this, Job. Stand still, and consider the wondrous works of God. Do you know how God controls
> them, and causes the lightning of his cloud to shine? Do you know the workings of the clouds, the
> wondrous works of him who is perfect in knowledge? You whose clothing is warm, when the earth is still
> by reason of the south wind? Can you, with him, spread out the sky, which is strong as a cast metal
> mirror?"* (37:14–18)

***"Do you know …? Can you …?"*** — **this is the grammar of the whirlwind.** God will ask *"Do you know
the laws of the heavens?"* (38:33), *"Can you send out lightnings, that they may go?"* (38:35), *"Can you
lift up your voice to the clouds?"* (38:34). Elihu asks four such questions; God will ask dozens. And
the last is about the sky. *Spread out* is *rāqaʿ*, the verb behind the *expanse*, *rāqîaʿ*, of Genesis
1:6 — the firmament beaten out like metal — and *"strong as a cast metal mirror"* (*mûṣāq*, cast) makes the
image explicit: the sky is a sheet of bronze. Job himself had said God *"alone stretches out the
heavens"* (9:8). *"You whose clothing is warm"* (37:17) is the most human line in the speech: Job, who
can feel the south wind's heat and do nothing about it, is asked whether he made the sky that brings it.

Then the conclusion Elihu draws, which is the opposite of the one God will draw:

> *"Teach us what we shall tell him, for we can't make our case by reason of darkness. Will it be told
> him that I would speak? Or should a man wish that he were swallowed up?"* (37:19–20)

*Make our case* is *naʿărōk*, the verb of Job's *"I have set my cause in order"* (13:18) and *"I would set
my cause in order before him"* (23:4). **Elihu says the case cannot be set out**, because we are in the
dark — and that to ask for a hearing with God is to ask to be swallowed up. It is ironic, and probably
meant to be: *Teach us what we shall tell him* is addressed to the one man in the book who has been
telling God things for thirty chapters. And within a verse and a half God will ask for exactly what Elihu
says no one should want: *"I will question you, then you answer me!"* (38:3). See
[[Mediation and Advocacy]] and [[Language and Speech]].

**10. Out of the north (37:21–24).** The storm clears.

> *"Now men don't see the light which is bright in the skies, but the wind passes, and clears them. Out
> of the north comes golden splendor. With God is awesome majesty. We can't reach the Almighty. He is
> exalted in power. In justice and great righteousness, he will not oppress. Therefore men revere him. He
> doesn't regard any who are wise of heart."* (37:21–24)

*Golden splendor* is *zāhāb*, simply *gold* — the light after the storm, out of the north, *ṣāpôn*, which
in the poetry of Israel's neighbours was the mountain of the gods (see Notes). *Awesome majesty* is
*nôrāʾ hôd*, and God will offer Job the same word: *"Array yourself with honor and majesty"* (*hôd*, 40:10).

*We can't reach the Almighty* is *lōʾ məṣāʾnūhû* — literally, **we have not found him**. It is the verb of
Job's great wish, *"Oh that I knew where I might find him!"* (*ʾemṣāʾēhû*, 23:3), and of Zophar's *"Can you
find out God?"* (11:7, where the WEB has *fathom*). Elihu's last word on Job's search is that it cannot
succeed. **Then God finds Job** — at 38:1, uninvited, unsummoned, and in the next verse.

*In justice and great righteousness, he will not oppress* (*lōʾ yəʿanneh*, 37:23): the verb is *ʿānâ*, to
afflict, the root of the *afflicted* (*ʿānî*) of 36:6, 15. God does not afflict — in a speech whose central
claim is that God teaches through affliction. The WEB's *oppress* takes it as *does not afflict unjustly*,
and that is the sense; but the word is the same.

And the last sentence: *"Therefore men revere him (yərēʾûhû). He doesn't regard any who are wise of
heart."* *Revere* is *fear* — the fear of 1:1 and of 28:28, *"the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom"*. And
*wise of heart* (*ḥakmê-lēb*) is **the phrase Job used of God**, *"God who is wise in heart, and mighty in
strength"* (9:4). Elihu ends where the hymn of chapter 28 ended — fear is wisdom; self-made wisdom is
nothing to God — and the sentence is aimed, at least in part, at Job. *Doesn't regard* is *lōʾ-yirʾeh*, **does not
see** — a play on *yərēʾûhû*, *revere*, in the line before: men fear him, and he does not see the
self-wise. That God *"doesn't regard"* the wise is the last thing Elihu says. **God's next act is to regard Job**, at length, by name. See
[[The Fear of God]] and [[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]].

**How to read him.** These two chapters are the best of Elihu, and the most balanced. The first half
says the one thing he contributed to the book — that God *"delivers the afflicted by their
affliction"* (36:15), suffering as instruction rather than sentence — and then, as in every speech of
his, applies it to Job as a charge: *"you have chosen this rather than affliction"* (36:21). The second
half is a hymn that the whirlwind will recognize. Elihu's rain, lightning, snow, ice and sky are God's
subjects in chapter 38, and his *"Do you know …?"* is God's grammar. Readers who see Elihu as God's herald
have their strongest evidence here.

**The difference is in the conclusion.** Elihu describes the storm to prove that no one can speak with
God (37:19–20) and no one can find him (37:23). God comes out of the storm to speak with Job and to be
found. Elihu says God *"doesn't regard any who are wise of heart"*; God regards Job. **The herald has the
right sky and the wrong verdict** — the pattern of [[Elihu on God's Justice]], where he had the right
charge and the wrong conclusion. And then he is never mentioned again: not in God's speeches, which
begin *"Who is this who darkens counsel …?"* about Job, not in the epilogue, not anywhere. See
[[From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1]].

## People

- [[Elihu the Buzite]] — the speaker (36:1), *"perfect in knowledge"* by his own account (36:4); his last
  speech, after which he is never mentioned again.
- [[Job (person)]] — addressed throughout and by name (37:14); told he was being allured out of distress
  (36:16), that he has *"chosen this rather than affliction"* (36:21), and asked whether he can spread out
  the sky (37:18). He does not answer.
- [[God]] — mighty and despising no one (36:5), the teacher (36:22), great and unknown (36:26), the rain-maker
  and thunderer whose voice is the storm (36:27–37:5), *"perfect in knowledge"* (37:16), unreachable (37:23),
  and — in the next verse after the speech — the one who answers Job out of the whirlwind (38:1).
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — not named; his *ʾên ḥēqer* (5:9), his rain (5:10), and his *mûsār* (5:17) all
  return here.
- [[Bildad the Shuhite]] — not named; his *"God will not cast away (yimʾas) a blameless man"* (8:20) behind
  36:5.
- [[Zophar the Naamathite]] — not named; his *"Can you find out God?"* (11:7) behind 37:23.
- **"Kings on the throne"** (36:7), **"the godless in heart"** (36:13), **"all men whom he has made"**
  (37:7) — types, not people. No pages.

## Places

None in the narrative. The **north** (*ṣāpôn*, 37:9, 22) is a direction here, not a place; see Notes on its
associations. The **sea** (36:30) is the sea as such. No pages.

## Themes

- [[Suffering]] — *"He delivers the afflicted by their affliction"* (36:15): suffering as instruction, in
  its most compact form.
- [[Testing]] — the fetters and cords that show men their work (36:8–9); *"you have chosen this rather than
  affliction"* (36:21).
- [[Retribution]] — *"He doesn't preserve the life of the wicked, but gives to the afflicted their right"*
  (36:6); prosperity for the listening, the sword for the deaf (36:11–12).
- [[Creation]] — the water cycle from the *ʾēd* of Genesis 2:6 (36:27); God speaking to the snow (37:6); the
  sky spread out like cast metal (37:18).
- [[Wisdom]] — *"Who is a teacher like him?"* (36:22); *"He doesn't regard any who are wise of heart"*
  (37:24).
- [[The Fear of God]] — *"Therefore men revere him"* (37:24), Elihu's last sentence.
- [[Language and Speech]] — the thunder as God's voice (37:2–5); *"we can't make our case by reason of
  darkness"* (37:19); *"Will it be told him that I would speak?"* (37:20).
- [[Light and Darkness]] — *"Don't desire the night"* (36:20); the light spread around him (36:30);
  darkness that prevents the case (37:19); the bright light after the wind (37:21).
- [[Judgment]] — *"by these he judges the people"* (36:31); *"In justice and great righteousness, he will
  not oppress"* (37:23).
- [[Righteousness]] — *"I will … ascribe righteousness to my Maker"* (36:3); *"great righteousness"* (37:23).
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — the righteous seated *"with kings on the throne"* (36:7).
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — *"that they have behaved themselves proudly"* (36:9); God *"exalted in his
  power"* (36:22; 37:23).
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — *"Don't let riches entice you"*; *"Would your wealth sustain you in
  distress?"* (36:18–19).
- [[Famine and Plenty]] — *"He gives food in abundance"* (36:31); the table *"full of fatness"* (36:16).
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — rain sent *"for loving kindness"* (*ḥesed*, 37:13).
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — *"He doesn't withdraw his eyes from the righteous"* (36:7); *"All men have
  looked on it"* (36:25).
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — the hearing Elihu says no one should want (37:19–20).
- [[Life and Death]] — *"they shall die without knowledge"* (36:12); *"They die in youth"* (36:14).

## Notes

- **The plan's boundary and title are kept.** Elihu's fourth speech is headed at 36:1 and runs to 37:24;
  the turn at 36:26 from argument to hymn is within one speech, not a new one, and the hymn's address to
  Job by name (37:14) ties the halves together.
- **36:4 and 37:16, "perfect in knowledge".** *Təmîm dēʿôt* (36:4) and *təmîm dēʿîm* (37:16) differ in the
  form of the noun, but are the same phrase in sense. That Elihu uses of himself the title he gives God is
  the wiki's observation and is widely made; whether it is presumption or a claim that his knowledge is
  *sound* (from God, *"from afar"*) is left open.
- **36:14, "among the unclean".** Hebrew *baqqədēšîm*, the *qədēšîm* — men associated with the shrines,
  usually understood as cult prostitutes (as at Deuteronomy 23:17). The WEB's *unclean* is a gentle
  rendering. The sense of the line is premature and shameful death.
- **36:16–21 is among the hardest passages in the book.** Translations differ widely, and some
  commentators regard the Hebrew as damaged. The WEB is followed. The readings this page relies on — the
  *wide place* (36:16), *kōper* (36:18), the *night* (36:20), and *"you have chosen this rather than
  affliction"* (36:21) — are each recorded as the WEB's, with the main alternatives where they matter.
- **36:18, *kōper*: bribe or ransom.** The WEB's *bribe* treats the line as a warning against being bought;
  another common reading keeps the sense of 33:24 and takes the *great ransom* to be Job's suffering, the
  price that must not turn him aside. Both are recorded; the page does not choose.
- **36:32, "to strike the mark".** *Bəmapgîaʿ* is variously rendered *at the mark*, *against the
  assailant*, or *as one who intercedes* (the Hiphil of *pāgaʿ* means *to intercede* at Isaiah 53:12 and
  Jeremiah 15:11). The link to *mipgāʿ* at 7:20 is the wiki's observation from the shared root.
- **37:1 and the approaching storm.** That Elihu's storm is the whirlwind of 38:1 approaching is an old and
  common reading, not something the text states. The wiki records it and the alternative — that God
  answers out of a storm Elihu merely described — on the connection page.
- **37:22, "out of the north".** *Ṣāpôn* is also the name of Mount Zaphon, the mountain of the gods in
  Ugaritic poetry, and Psalm 48:2 and Isaiah 14:13 use *the north* with that resonance. Whether Elihu's
  *golden splendor out of the north* carries it, or is simply the bright northern sky after a storm, is
  disputed. Recorded, not adopted.
- **37:23, "he will not oppress".** *Lōʾ yəʿanneh* could also be read, with a small change of vowels, as
  *he does not answer* (*ʿānâ* I, to answer, against *ʿānâ* II, to afflict). The WEB follows the received
  vowels. The answering reading would make Elihu's last claim that God does not answer — immediately
  before he does.
- **37:24, "wise of heart".** *Ḥakmê-lēb* can mean the truly wise or the wise *in their own estimation*; most
  translations take the second, and the WEB's *"He doesn't regard any who are wise of heart"* allows both.
  The link to 9:4, where Job uses the phrase of God, is the wiki's observation from the shared phrase.
- **Psalm 29.** The verbal overlap — *qôl*, *thunders*, *majesty* — is close but not exact (*gəʾôn* at Job
  37:4, *hādār* at Psalm 29:4); the shared subject is the thunder as God's voice. See the connection page.
- **No contradiction between wiki pages found.** The forward references already in the wiki — 36:8–15 at
  [[Eliphaz's First Speech]] and the chastening connection; 36:18 at [[Elihu Speaks in Anger]] and the
  ransom connection; 37:2–5 at [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]; 37:18's *mūṣāq* at
  [[Zophar's First Speech]] — are borne out by the text. **One tension is recorded rather than resolved**:
  36:6 (*"He doesn't preserve the life of the wicked"*) directly contradicts Job's claim at 21:7, and the
  wiki's own reading of [[Why Do the Wicked Live]]; this is a disagreement between speakers in the book,
  not between wiki pages, and both claims stand with their sources.
