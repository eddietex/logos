# Behemoth and Leviathan

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 40:6–41:34

## Summary

*"Then Yahweh answered Job out of the whirlwind"* (40:6) — the heading of 38:1, word for word. Job has
just said he will speak no further (40:5), and God does not accept that as an ending. The first speech
produced a man with his hand on his mouth. The second is meant to produce something else, and when Job
speaks again (42:1–6) it has: *"now my eye sees you"*.

The second speech is shorter than the first and built differently. The first asked more than fifty
questions across the whole of creation. The second asks one question about justice (40:8), sets one
challenge (40:9–14), and then describes two creatures at length: **Behemoth** in ten verses (40:15–24)
and **Leviathan** in thirty-four (41:1–34). It ends on Leviathan, and with that the speeches end too.

**1. The same summons (40:6–7).** *"Now brace yourself like a man. I will question you, and you will
answer me"* (40:7) repeats 38:3 almost exactly — *geber*, the warrior-man, and Job's own procedure from
13:22 with the roles reversed. Job's silence at 40:4–5 has not ended the hearing.

**2. The charge (40:8).** The first speech's charge was ignorance: *"words without knowledge"* (38:2).
The second asks a harder question:

> *"Will you even annul my judgment? Will you condemn me, that you may be justified?"* (40:8)

*Annul* is *tāpēr*, to break or make void; *my judgment* is *mišpāṭî*, the word Job has used of the case
he could not get heard (*"God has taken away my right"*, *mišpāṭî*, 27:2). *Condemn* is *taršîʿēnî*, to
declare guilty — **the very form of Job's own plea at 10:2**, *"Do not condemn me"* (*ʾal-taršîʿēnî*), and
the verb of 9:20, *"my own mouth shall condemn me"*. And *that you may be justified* is *tiṣdāq*, the
verb of Job's oath, *"I hold fast to my righteousness"* (27:6), and of Elihu's anger, that Job *"justified
himself rather than God"* (32:2).

**This is the only point in either speech where God names what was actually wrong in Job's argument.**
It is not a sin that caused his suffering; the speeches still name none. It is the move the lawsuit made:
to establish Job's innocence, Job had to make God the guilty party. God does not say that Job is guilty.
He asks whether Job's innocence needs God's guilt. Elihu had put nearly the same question at 34:17 —
*"Will you condemn him who is righteous and mighty?"* — and God now asks it himself. See
[[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]] and [[Righteousness]].

**3. The challenge: be God for a day (40:9–14).** Then an invitation that reads as irony and is also an
argument:

> *"Or do you have an arm like God? Can you thunder with a voice like him? Now deck yourself with
> excellency and dignity. Array yourself with honor and majesty. Pour out the fury of your anger. Look at
> everyone who is proud, and bring him low. Look at everyone who is proud, and humble him. Crush the
> wicked in their place. Hide them in the dust together. Bind their faces in the hidden place. Then I will
> also admit to you that your own right hand can save you."* (40:9–14)

*Thunder with a voice like him* is the thunder Elihu called *"the voice of his majesty"* (37:4); see
[[The Voice of His Majesty - Job 37.2-5 and Psalm 29]] and
[[From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1]]. *Honor and majesty* is *hôd wəhādār*,
the royal pair of the Psalms. **The robe is the one Job remembered wearing** — *"I put on righteousness,
and it clothed me. My justice was like a robe and a diadem"* (29:14). God offers him a larger one: the
robe of the judge of all the earth. See [[I Put On Righteousness - Job 29.14 and the Garments of Justice]].

And the work that goes with it is the work Job accused God of neglecting. In chapter 21 and again in
chapter 24 Job said the wicked prosper and nobody brings them down. God answers: *you* do it. Find every
proud man, bring him low, crush the wicked in their place, and bury them. The repetition —
*"Look at everyone who is proud"* twice in two verses (40:11–12) — is the speech dwelling on the size of
the task. If Job can do that, *"your own right hand can save you"* (40:14) — he would not need God's
vindication at all.

**God does not claim here that he does all of it himself, now, on demand.** He does not say the wicked
are always crushed. He asks Job to try governing a world with the proud in it, and then shows him two
creatures that are, in effect, pride with a body.

**4. Behemoth (40:15–24).** *"See now, behemoth, which I made as well as you. He eats grass as an ox"*
(40:15). *Bəhēmôt* is the plural of *bəhēmâ*, the ordinary word for a beast or for livestock — the
*livestock* of Genesis 1:24–26, made on the sixth day with the man and placed under his dominion. The
plural makes it *the beast of beasts*. And *which I made as well as you* is *ʾăšer-ʿāśîtî ʿimmāk*,
literally *which I made with you*: **Behemoth is Job's fellow creature, made on his day.**

He is described entirely in terms of strength at rest. Strength in his loins and his belly (40:16), a tail
like a cedar (40:17), bones like bronze tubes and iron bars (40:18). He eats grass; the mountains bring him
food *"where all the animals of the field play"* (40:20); he lies under the lotus and in the reeds of the
marsh, shaded by the willows of the brook (40:21–22). *"Behold, if a river overflows, he doesn't tremble.
He is confident, though the Jordan swells even to his mouth"* (40:23). The flood that would drown a man is
a bath to him.

At the centre: *"He is the chief of the ways of God. He who made him gives him his sword"* (40:19).
*Chief of the ways of God* is *rēʾšît darkê-ʾēl* — the *first* of God's ways, his masterpiece or his
first-made — and it is the phrase Wisdom uses of herself in Proverbs 8:22: *"Yahweh possessed me in the
beginning of his work"*, *rēʾšît darkô*. What Wisdom claims in Proverbs, a grass-eating beast is given in
Job. See [[The First of the Ways of God - Job 40.19 and Proverbs 8.22]]. The second half of the verse is
obscure (see Notes).

The section ends with a question: *"Shall any take him when he is on the watch, or pierce through his nose
with a snare?"* (40:24) — and with that the speech turns from a creature Job cannot trap to one he cannot
even approach.

**5. Leviathan: can you catch him? (41:1–11).** *"Can you draw out Leviathan with a fish hook, or press
down his tongue with a cord?"* (41:1). This is the creature Job called on in his first speech — *"those
who are ready to rouse up leviathan"* (3:8) — and the only other place in the book he is named. Job wanted
him woken. God asks whether Job could land him.

Then a run of questions that turns Leviathan into each of the things human beings make of animals:

- **a fish** — hook, cord, rope through the nose, a hook through the jaw (41:1–2);
- **a suppliant** — *"Will he make many petitions to you, or will he speak soft words to you?"* (41:3);
- **a slave** — *"Will he make a covenant with you, that you should take him for a servant forever?"*
  (41:4). *A servant forever* is *ʿebed ʿôlām*, the term of the slave who chooses to stay with his master
  for life, whose ear is pierced at the door (Exodus 21:6; Deuteronomy 15:17). Leviathan will sign no
  such covenant. See [[Covenant]] and [[Slavery and Servitude]];
- **a pet** — *"Will you play with him as with a bird? Or will you bind him for your girls?"* (41:5);
- **a commodity** — *"Will traders barter for him? Will they part him among the merchants?"* (41:6);
- **game** — *"Can you fill his skin with barbed irons, or his head with fish spears?"* (41:7).

The answer to all of them is no. The dominion of Genesis 1:26 — *"over the fish of the sea"* — stops at
this fish.

Then the advice: *"Lay your hand on him. Remember the battle, and do so no more"* (41:8). Job laid his
hand on his own mouth at 40:4. If he lays it on Leviathan, he will not need to be told twice.

> *"Behold, the hope of him is in vain. Won't one be cast down even at the sight of him? None is so fierce
> that he dare stir him up. Who then is he who can stand before me? Who has first given to me, that I
> should repay him? Everything under the heavens is mine."* (41:9–11)

**Here the speech states its argument openly, the only time it does.** *None is so fierce that he dare stir
him up* — *lōʾ-ʾakzār kî yəʿûrennû*. *Stir up* is *ʿûr*, **the verb of 3:8**, where Job called for those
*ready to rouse* (*ʿōrēr*) Leviathan. And *fierce* is *ʾakzār*, the word of Job's charge at 30:21, *"You
have turned to be cruel (ʾakzār) to me"*. Nobody is *ʾakzār* enough to wake Leviathan — so who can stand
before the one who made him? **Job wanted Leviathan roused against his birthday, and was asking for a
power he could not stand in front of; and the God he accused of cruelty is the only one who can.**

Then 41:11, in the form Paul quotes at Romans 11:35: *"Who has first given to me, that I should repay
him? Everything under the heavens is mine."* **God owes no one.** That sentence takes apart the friends'
whole system — in which righteousness is an account God must settle — and it also takes apart the
lawsuit, in which Job's integrity was a debt God had failed to pay. It is the line Eliphaz came close to
and drew the wrong conclusion from (22:2–3), and Elihu too (35:7). See
[[Who Has First Given to Me - Job 41.11 in Romans 11.35]] and
[[Can a Man Be Profitable to God - Job 22.2-3, Job 35.6-8, and Luke 17.10]].

**6. Leviathan described (41:12–34).** *"I will not keep silence concerning his limbs, nor his mighty
strength, nor his goodly frame"* (41:12). The rest of the speech is a portrait, and God says he will not
stop himself making it.

- **The armour** (41:13–17): no one can strip his outer garment or come inside his jaws; *"Around his teeth
  is terror"* (41:14). *"Strong scales are his pride"* (41:15) — *gaʾăwâ*, the root of *"everyone who is
  proud"* (*gēʾeh*) at 40:11–12. The scales sit so close *"that no air can come between them"* (41:16).
- **The fire** (41:18–21): *"His sneezing flashes out light. His eyes are like the eyelids of the
  morning. Out of his mouth go burning torches. Sparks of fire leap out. Out of his nostrils a smoke goes,
  as of a boiling pot over a fire of reeds. His breath kindles coals. A flame goes out of his mouth."*
  **The eyelids of the morning** are the words of 3:9, the dawn Job wished his birth-night never to see —
  the phrase occurs in the book only there and here. And **smoke from the nostrils, fire from the mouth,
  coals kindled** is the language of the theophany in Psalm 18:8, where it is said of God. See
  [[Smoke from His Nostrils - Leviathan and the Theophany of Psalm 18]] and [[Light and Darkness]].
- **The terror** (41:22–25): *"Terror dances before him"* (41:22); his heart is *"as firm as a stone, yes,
  firm as the lower millstone"* (41:24); *"When he raises himself up, the mighty are afraid"* (41:25) — the
  *mighty* are *ʾēlîm*, a word that can also mean *gods* (see Notes).
- **The weapons** (41:26–29): sword, spear, dart, shaft, iron, bronze, arrow, sling-stone, club, javelin —
  *"He counts iron as straw"* (41:27), *"He laughs at the rushing of the javelin"* (41:29). It is the whole
  armoury of a human army, and none of it works. See [[War]].
- **The wake** (41:30–32): *"He makes the deep to boil like a pot … He makes a path shine after him. One
  would think the deep had white hair"* (41:31–32). *The deep* in 41:32 is *təhôm*, the deep of Genesis 1:2
  over which darkness lay. The chaos-water of the first verse of the Bible is, behind Leviathan, a bright
  wake that looks like an old man's hair.

And the last two verses of the speech:

> *"On earth there is not his equal, that is made without fear. He sees everything that is high. He is
> king over all the sons of pride."* (41:33–34)

*Made without fear* — he is the one creature that was never given terror of anything. And *king over all
the sons of pride*, *bənê-šāḥaṣ*: the *"proud animals"* of 28:8, the phrase's only other occurrence, which
had never trodden the path to wisdom. See [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]].

**The speech closes the circle it opened at 40:11–12.** Job was told to find *everyone who is proud* and
bring him low. He is then shown the king of the proud, a creature whose scales are *his pride*, whom no
human hand can hook, and whom God made, describes with admiration, and does not bring low. **God governs
pride; he does not abolish it.** The proud, in God's world, have a king, and the king is God's creature.
See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] and [[Kingship and Empire]].

**How to read it.** The speech has been read in three main ways, and the wiki records all three without
choosing among them except where the text decides.

1. **As natural history.** Behemoth is the hippopotamus (grass, lotus, reeds, the river) and Leviathan the
   Nile crocodile (scales, teeth, the impenetrable hide, the hunt with hooks and harpoons). On this reading
   God shows Job two real animals, the largest and the most dangerous a reader in the ancient Near East
   could picture, and the point is the same as in chapter 39: creatures God made that are wholly outside
   human control. The fire-breathing of 41:18–21 is then poetic exaggeration — the crocodile's spray and
   red eyes at sunrise.
2. **As myth.** Leviathan is the chaos-serpent of the combat tradition: the many-headed Leviathan Psalm
   74:14 says God broke, *"leviathan, the fleeing serpent, and leviathan the twisted serpent"* of Isaiah
   27:1, and behind both the Ugaritic *Lôtan*, the *fleeing* and *twisting* serpent of the Baal texts. A
   creature who breathes fire, whose wake turns *təhôm* white, and before whom *the gods* tremble is not
   a crocodile. Behemoth is then his land-counterpart; later Jewish tradition paired them as monsters of
   land and sea held for the end of days (1 Enoch 60; 4 Ezra 6; 2 Baruch 29 — cited by reference only).
3. **As both at once,** which is the reading the text itself most supports. The animals of 40:15–24 and
   41:1–7 are drawn from the river, the reeds, and the fishing trade; the creature of 41:18–34 breathes fire
   and churns the deep. **The speech moves from the zoo to the myth without marking the seam.** Its point
   survives either way: whatever Leviathan is, God made him (40:15, 19), owns him (41:11), and speaks of him
   with pleasure (41:12), and Job cannot touch him.

What the text does settle is the thing [[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]] traced
from 7:12: **Leviathan is not God's enemy here.** There is no combat in Job 41, no broken heads and no
pierced dragon. God is not fighting the chaos-monster; he is showing him off. Job feared that he was being
treated like the sea monster (7:12), and called for Leviathan to be woken (3:8). God's last word is that
Leviathan is his, and he is not afraid of him.

**What the speech still does not say.** No word, again, of the wager, the accuser, the children, the
sores, or the friends. Job's question — *why me?* — is never answered. What has changed since 40:5 is the
question God is answering. The first speech answered chapter 3 (see
[[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]]); the second answers the lawsuit — 40:8's *"Will you condemn me, that
you may be justified?"* — and it answers it not with a verdict but with an offer of the bench and a
portrait of what the bench would mean. Job's reply, the next pericope, is *"I know that you can do all
things"* (42:2), and it is a reply to this.

## People

- [[God]] — *Yahweh* in the heading (40:6), speaking out of the whirlwind a second time; asks the question
  of justice (40:8), offers Job his own robe and office (40:10–14), and describes two creatures he made,
  ending with *"Everything under the heavens is mine"* (41:11). Names no sin and gives no reason.
- [[Job (person)]] — addressed throughout, never by name; asked whether his innocence requires God's guilt
  (40:8). He does not answer within the pericope; his reply is 42:1–6.
- [[Elihu the Buzite]] — not mentioned; his 34:17, *"Will you condemn him who is righteous and mighty?"*,
  is the question God asks at 40:8, and his *"voice of his majesty"* (37:4) the thunder of 40:9.
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — not named; his *"Is it any pleasure to the Almighty, that you are righteous?"*
  (22:3) is answered from the other side at 41:11.
- **Behemoth and Leviathan** are creatures, not persons, and get no pages; they are treated here and on the
  connection pages below. (Leviathan is the subject of a page-level thread at
  [[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]].)

## Places

- [[The Jordan]] — *"He is confident, though the Jordan swells even to his mouth"* (40:23); its only
  appearance in the book, and possibly used for any rushing river (see Notes).
- The lotus, reeds and marsh (40:21–22), *the deep* (41:31–32, *təhôm*) and *the sea* (41:31) are
  habitats and cosmic regions, not places with pages.

## Themes

- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — *"Look at everyone who is proud, and bring him low"* (40:11–12); *"Strong
  scales are his pride"* (41:15); *"king over all the sons of pride"* (41:34).
- [[Righteousness]] — *"Will you condemn me, that you may be justified?"* (40:8): Job's righteousness bought
  with God's guilt.
- [[Judgment]] — *"Will you even annul my judgment?"* (40:8); the work of judging the proud offered to Job
  (40:11–13).
- [[Creation]] — Behemoth *"which I made as well as you"* (40:15), *"the chief of the ways of God"* (40:19);
  the *təhôm* of Genesis 1:2 behind Leviathan (41:32).
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — the royal robe of *hôd wəhādār* (40:10); Leviathan *"king over all the sons of
  pride"* (41:34).
- [[Covenant]] — *"Will he make a covenant with you?"* (41:4): the one covenant the book proposes, and it is
  refused by an animal.
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — *ʿebed ʿôlām*, *"a servant forever"* (41:4), the slave-for-life formula of
  Exodus 21:6 and Deuteronomy 15:17.
- [[Light and Darkness]] — *"His sneezing flashes out light. His eyes are like the eyelids of the morning"*
  (41:18; 3:9); *"He makes a path shine after him"* (41:32).
- [[War]] — sword, spear, dart, arrow, sling-stone, club and javelin all useless (41:26–29); *"Remember the
  battle"* (41:8).
- [[The Fear of God]] — *"Who then is he who can stand before me?"* (41:10); Leviathan *"made without fear"*
  (41:33).
- [[Retribution]] — the wicked not crushed on demand; the work of crushing them offered to Job (40:11–14).
- [[Providence]] — Behemoth fed by the mountains (40:20) and sheltered by the lotus (40:22); *"Everything
  under the heavens is mine"* (41:11).
- [[Suffering]] — still unmentioned; the second speech answers the lawsuit, not the pain.

## Notes

- **Boundary.** The plan's 40:6–41:34 is kept: the heading of 40:6 repeats 38:1 and opens a new speech,
  and *"Then Job answered Yahweh"* (42:1) closes it.
- **Verse numbering.** The WEB follows the English chapter division. In the Hebrew Bible 41:1–8 are 40:25–32,
  and 41:9–34 are 41:1–26; so Job 41:11, quoted at Romans 11:35, is Hebrew 41:3. Leviathan's portrait is 34
  verses in either system.
- **40:19b.** *"He who made him gives him his sword"* — Hebrew *hāʿōśô yaggēš ḥarbô*, literally *his maker
  brings near his sword*. The WEB reads it as the maker arming his creature (the hippopotamus's tusks, on
  the natural-history reading). Many translations and commentators take it the other way: *only his maker
  can approach him with a sword*. Both are grammatically possible; the wiki records both.
- **40:23, the Jordan.** No hippopotamus lived in the Jordan in the historical period as far as the wiki can
  state, and *yardēn* here may stand for any river in flood. Recorded, not resolved.
- **41:15, *gaʾăwâ*.** *"Strong scales are his pride"* follows the Hebrew text. A widely adopted
  emendation reads *gēwōh*, *his back*, which suits the description better and removes the pun on 40:11–12.
  The pun is noted above as the Hebrew stands.
- **41:25, *ʾēlîm*.** The WEB's *the mighty* could also be *the gods*: *when he raises himself up, the gods
  are afraid*. That reading suits the mythic interpretation and is taken by some translations. Recorded, not
  adopted.
- **The Lôtan parallel.** The Ugaritic Baal cycle describes *ltn* as *the fleeing serpent* and *the twisting
  serpent* — the two epithets of Isaiah 27:1. The connection between Leviathan and Lôtan is standard in
  the scholarship; the wiki has not quoted the Ugaritic text and does not reproduce it here.
- **Later tradition.** 1 Enoch 60:7–9, 4 Ezra 6:49–52 and 2 Baruch 29:4 pair Behemoth (land) and
  Leviathan (sea) as primordial monsters kept for the end; none of these is Scripture in the Protestant
  canon and none is quoted here. Christian tradition from the early centuries often read Leviathan as Satan,
  helped by Revelation 12:9; Job 41 never makes the connection, and the accuser of chapters 1–2 is not
  mentioned in either speech. See [[Satan]].
- **On contradictions and corrections.** No wiki page is contradicted by the text of this pericope in a way
  that changes its argument. Two connection pages are corrected:
  [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]] said Leviathan's description runs *forty-one verses*; it runs
  thirty-four (41:1–34). Its other claims about chapter 41 hold, and one correspondence it missed (41:10's
  *ʿûr* against 3:8) is added. [[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]] said the whirlwind
  makes *Leviathan a pet*; 41:5 asks whether *Job* could make him one, and expects *no*. Both pages now keep
  the original wording and the correction side by side.
