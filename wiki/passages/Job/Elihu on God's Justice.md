# Elihu on God's Justice

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 34:1–35:16

## Summary

Elihu ended his first speech with an invitation — *"If you have anything to say, answer me. Speak, for
I desire to justify you"* (33:32) — and Job said nothing. *"Moreover Elihu answered"* (34:1): the
heading of an answer, given to a silence. This page takes the next two of
[[Elihu the Buzite]]'s four speeches together, because they are one argument in two movements.
Chapter 34 defends God's justice against Job, and is spoken mostly *about* Job, to an audience of
*"wise men"* (34:2, 10, 34). Chapter 35, headed again *"Moreover Elihu answered"* (35:1), turns to
Job directly and takes up the question underneath the first — what a man's righteousness is worth
to God at all. Both end with the same verdict in almost the same words: *"Job speaks without
knowledge"* (34:35), *"he multiplies words without knowledge"* (35:16). **Three chapters later God
opens his reply to Job with that charge** (38:2), and that is the fact that makes this the hardest of
Elihu's speeches to weigh.

**1. To the wise men: the ear tries words (34:1–4).** *"Hear my words, you wise men. Give ear to me,
you who have knowledge. For the ear tries words, as the palate tastes food"* (34:2–3). The proverb
is Job's — *"Doesn't the ear try words, even as the palate tastes its food?"* (12:11) — taken from the
speech in which Job claimed the friends' wisdom for himself and quoted back in the same nearly exact
form. Who the *wise men* are is not said. They may be the three friends, whom Elihu has just
condemned for having *"found no answer"* (32:3) and would hardly call wise without irony; or a wider
circle of hearers, the kind of assembly at the gate that [[The Months of Old]] described falling
silent when Job came in. *"Let us choose for us that which is right"* (34:4) — *mišpāṭ*, a verdict
— makes them a jury, and Job the case before it.

**2. The charge, in Job's words and not quite in Job's words (34:5–9).** Elihu quotes again, as he
did at 33:9–11, and the quotations are a mixture.

| Elihu's quotation | What Job said |
|---|---|
| *"I am righteous"* (34:5) | *"I know that I am righteous"* (13:18); *"I hold fast to my righteousness"* (27:6) |
| *"God has taken away my right"* (34:5) | *"As God lives, who has taken away my right"* (27:2) — *hēsîr mišpāṭî*, word for word, from Job's oath |
| *"Notwithstanding my right I am considered a liar"* (34:6) | no single verse; the sense of 9:20 and 27:4–6, that to confess would be to lie |
| *"My wound is incurable, though I am without disobedience"* (34:6) | *"For the arrows of the Almighty are within me"* (6:4) — *ḥiṣṣî*, literally **my arrow**, which the WEB renders *wound* |
| *"It profits a man nothing that he should delight himself with God"* (34:9) | **nowhere** — this is the wicked's question that Job quoted and disowned at 21:15–16 |

The last line is the one that matters. *Lōʾ yiskān-geber*, *it profits a man nothing*: the verb is
*sākan*, Eliphaz's at 22:2 — *"Can a man be profitable to God?"* — and the sentence is the wicked
men's of 21:15, *"What profit should we have, if we pray to him?"*, which Job reported and then put
from him: *"The counsel of the wicked is far from me"* (21:16). The nearest Job came to saying it in
his own voice was despair, not doctrine — *"I shall be condemned. Why then do I labor in vain?"*
(9:29), *"he destroys the blameless and the wicked"* (9:22). **Elihu takes the words Job put in the
mouths of the wicked and puts them in Job's**, and on that ground places him among them: *"What man
is like Job, who drinks scorn like water, who goes in company with the workers of iniquity, and
walks with wicked men?"* (34:7–8). *Drinks … like water* is Eliphaz's image for fallen man, *"a man
who drinks iniquity like water"* (15:16), now with *scorn* (*laʿag*) for *iniquity*; *the workers of
iniquity* (*pōʿălê ʾāwen*) are the people Job swore disaster on in his oath (31:3). The man who
listened best in [[Elihu Speaks in Anger]] has begun, in his second speech, to listen the way the
friends did. See
[[Can a Man Be Profitable to God - Job 22.2-3, Job 35.6-8, and Luke 17.10]].

**3. Far be it from God (34:10–15).** Then the thesis, stated three ways in three verses:

> *"Far be it from God, that he should do wickedness, from the Almighty, that he should commit
> iniquity. For the work of a man he will render to him, and cause every man to find according to
> his ways. Yes surely, God will not do wickedly, neither will the Almighty pervert justice."*
> (34:10–12)

*Ḥālilâ lāʾēl* — *far be it from God*. The word has two histories in this wiki. It is Abraham's,
twice, at Sodom: *"Be it far from you to do things like that … May that be far from you. Shouldn't
the Judge of all the earth do right?"* (Genesis 18:25). And it is Job's own, at the head of his oath:
*"Far be it from me that I should justify you"* (27:5). Abraham used it to hold God to the standard;
Job used it to refuse to buy peace by lying; Elihu uses it to close the question. And 34:12 is
**Bildad's first sentence restated** — *"Does God pervert justice? Or does the Almighty pervert
righteousness?"* (8:3) — with the same verb, *ʿāwat*, the verb Job turned around at 19:6, *"God has
subverted me"*. 34:11 is retribution in its plainest form, the form of Psalm 62:12 (*"you reward every
man according to his work"*), Proverbs 24:12 (*"Shall he not render to every man according to his
work?"*), and Romans 2:6, which quotes the formula. See
[[Shall Not the Judge of All the Earth Do Right]] and [[Retribution]].

The ground of the thesis is new, and it is the best thing in the chapter. *"Who put him in charge of
the earth? or who has appointed him over the whole world?"* (34:13). God's justice is not
delegated; he answers to no one above him, and so he has no motive to be unjust — no master to
please, no office to keep. And his rule is the only thing between the world and its end:

> *"If he set his heart on himself, if he gathered to himself his spirit and his breath, all flesh
> would perish together, and man would turn again to dust."* (34:14–15)

This is Genesis 2:7 run backwards — the *nišmâ*, the **breath** God breathed into the dust — to the
sentence of Genesis 3:19, *"to dust you shall return"*. Psalm 104:29 says it of every creature:
*"you take away their breath: they die, and return to the dust"*. Elihu has already said it of
himself, *"the breath of the Almighty gives me life"* (33:4), and Job said it before him: *"in whose
hand is the life of every living thing, and the breath of all mankind"* (12:10). **The argument is
that a God who keeps the world breathing cannot be indifferent to it**, and so cannot be its enemy.
It is a real argument, and it is the one the whirlwind will make at length. See [[Life and Death]]
and [[Creation]].

**4. The king who calls kings vile (34:16–30).** The address turns singular — *"If now you have
understanding, hear this"* (34:16) — and the question is put to Job:

> *"Shall even one who hates justice govern? Will you condemn him who is righteous and mighty? — who
> says to a king, 'Vile!' or to nobles, 'Wicked!'? Who doesn't respect the persons of princes, nor
> respects the rich more than the poor; for they all are the work of his hands."* (34:17–19)

*Taršîaʿ*, *will you condemn* — declare guilty, the opposite of *ṣādaq*. **God asks Job the same
question at 40:8**: *"Will you even annul my judgment? Will you condemn me, that you may be
justified?"* — *taršîʿēnî*, the same verb, in the second divine speech. Elihu has anticipated God's
question exactly. Whether he has also anticipated God's answer is the subject of the epilogue.

The portrait of God as the impartial ruler is drawn from the court. He *"doesn't respect the persons
of princes"* — *lōʾ nāśāʾ pənê śārîm*, the idiom of lifting a face, which is Deuteronomy 10:17's
(*"who doesn't respect persons, nor takes reward"*) and Peter's at Acts 10:34 (*"God doesn't show
favoritism"*). It is also the charge Job laid against the friends: *"Will you show partiality to
him? Will you contend for God?"* (13:8). Job accused the friends of lifting God's face; Elihu says God
lifts no one's. The powerful die *"in a moment … even at midnight"* (34:20), the hour of Exodus
12:29, and *"without a hand"* — by no human agency. And nothing is hidden from him:

> *"For his eyes are on the ways of a man. He sees all his goings. There is no darkness, nor thick
> gloom, where the workers of iniquity may hide themselves."* (34:21–22)

*Ṣalmāwet*, the *thick gloom* — the WEB's *shadow of death* at 3:5, which Job asked to reclaim his
birthday. This answers two speeches at
once. Eliphaz had put in Job's mouth the creed of the fool — *"What does God know? Can he judge through
the thick darkness?"* (22:13) — and Job had described the murderer and the adulterer who work by
night, *"saying, 'No eye shall see me'"* (24:15), and go unpunished. Elihu's answer is Psalm 139:12's:
*"even the darkness doesn't hide from you"*. See [[Seeing and Being Seen]] and
[[Light and Darkness]].

Then a sentence aimed at the heart of Job's case. *"For he doesn't need to consider a man further,
that he should go before God in judgment"* (34:23). Job has asked for a hearing for twenty chapters
— *"I desire to reason with God"* (13:3), *"Oh that I knew where I might find him! That I might come
even to his seat!"* (23:3), and the signed demand of 31:35. **Elihu's reply is that God needs no
trial**, because he already sees; he *"breaks in pieces mighty men in ways past finding out"*
(*lōʾ-ḥēqer*, 34:24), and strikes them *"in the open sight of others"* (34:26). Job's whole legal
project — the umpire, the witness, the summons — is ruled unnecessary, by the same argument that
makes God just. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

And the reason God strikes the mighty is the poor: *"so that they caused the cry of the poor to come
to him. He heard the cry of the afflicted"* (34:28). *Ṣaʿăqâ*, the *cry*, is the word of Sodom's
outcry at Genesis 18:20 and of Israel's in Egypt — *"I … have heard their cry"* (Exodus 3:7). Job had
said the opposite: *"the soul of the wounded cries out, yet God doesn't regard the folly"* (24:12).
**Here Elihu says God hears the cry. In the next chapter he will explain why God does not** (35:9–13).
The section ends on God's freedom — *"When he gives quietness, who then can condemn? When he hides
his face, who then can see him?"* (34:29) — and *hides his face* is Job's complaint of 13:24 conceded
and called sovereign. See [[Kingship and Empire]].

**5. The confession not made, and the trial wished on him (34:31–37).** The text of 34:31–33 is among
the most difficult in the book, and the WEB's sense is one of several. It imagines a man saying to
God, *"I am guilty, but I will not offend any more. Teach me that which I don't see. If I have done
iniquity, I will do it no more"* (34:31–32) — the confession Elihu's sickbed sequence ended in
(33:27), offered to Job as a form of words. It also echoes Job: *"Teach me, and I will hold my peace.
Cause me to understand wherein I have erred"* (6:24), said to the friends in the first cycle. *"For
you must choose, and not I"* (34:33). Then Elihu calls his jury back, and gives their verdict for them:

> *"Men of understanding will tell me, yes, every wise man who hears me: 'Job speaks without
> knowledge. His words are without wisdom.' I wish that Job were tried to the end, because of his
> answering like wicked men. For he adds rebellion to his sin. He claps his hands among us, and
> multiplies his words against God."* (34:34–37)

*Tried* is *bāḥan*, the assayer's verb — Job's own at 23:10, *"When he has tried me, I shall come out
like gold"*. Job welcomed the test as the thing that would clear him; Elihu wishes it on him *"to the
end"* as the thing that would finish him. *Claps his hands* is *śāpaq*, the gesture Job assigned to
the crowd that hisses the wicked man out of his place (27:23); Elihu gives it to Job. And *"adds
rebellion to his sin"* (*pešaʿ*) is the sentence that ends the chapter's case: **Elihu now holds that
Job sinned before his suffering, and has rebelled since.** That is a harder verdict than his first
speech gave, and the same verdict as Eliphaz's third (22:5). See [[Testing]] and [[Sin]].

**6. "My righteousness is more than God's" (35:1–8).** The third speech is short, and it is to Job.
*"Do you think this to be your right, or do you say, 'My righteousness is more than God's,' that you
ask, 'What advantage will it be to you? What profit shall I have, more than if I had sinned?'"*
(35:2–3). *Ṣidqî mēʾēl* can be read *more than God's*, as the WEB has it, or *before God* — *I am in the
right against God* — which is the charge of 32:2 again, that Job *"justified himself rather than God"*
(*mēʾĕlōhîm*), with the same preposition. And the profit question is put in Job's mouth a second time,
again with *sākan* (35:3), and again without a verse to support it.

The answer is the sky. *"Look to the heavens, and see. See the skies, which are higher than you"*
(35:5) — Eliphaz's gesture, *"See the height of the stars, how high they are!"* (22:12), and a small
anticipation of the whirlwind's. And then:

> *"If you have sinned, what effect do you have against him? If your transgressions are multiplied,
> what do you do to him? If you are righteous, what do you give him? Or what does he receive from
> your hand? Your wickedness may hurt a man as you are, and your righteousness may profit a son of
> man."* (35:6–8)

**Two earlier speeches are joined here.** 35:6 is Job's own question at 7:20, *"If I have sinned, what
do I do to you, you watcher of men?"*, returned to him: Job asked why God pursues a sin that cannot
touch him, and Elihu answers that it cannot touch him — and leaves the pursuit unexplained. 35:7 is
Eliphaz's 22:2–3, *"Is it any pleasure to the Almighty, that you are righteous?"*, nearly word for word.
The conclusion of 35:8 is humane and true: sin and righteousness are felt by other people. It is also,
as an answer to Job, an argument that God has no stake in him — which is the one thing the prologue
has shown to be false (1:8; 2:3). God will say the part of this that is right at 41:11: *"Who has first
given to me, that I should repay him?"* — he owes Job nothing. He does not say he does not care.

**7. The cry that is not answered (35:9–16).** Then Elihu turns to the cry he said, at 34:28, God
hears.

> *"By reason of the multitude of oppressions they cry out. They cry for help by reason of the arm of
> the mighty. But no one says, 'Where is God my Maker, who gives songs in the night, who teaches us
> more than the animals of the earth, and makes us wiser than the birds of the sky?' There they cry,
> but no one gives answer, because of the pride of evil men. Surely God will not hear an empty cry,
> neither will the Almighty regard it."* (35:9–13)

*They cry for help* is *yəšawwəʿû*, the verb of Job's *"I cry for help, but there is no justice"*
(19:7). Elihu's distinction is between the cry of pain and the prayer — between crying *out* and
crying *to God*. The oppressed scream at the arm of the mighty; none of them asks *"Where is God my
Maker?"*, and so none is answered. It is a real distinction, and the Psalms know it: *"In the night his
song shall be with me: a prayer to the God of my life"* (Psalm 42:8); *"I remember my song in the
night"* (Psalm 77:6). *Who gives songs in the night* is one of the most beautiful sentences in the
book.

**It is also a sentence Job cannot be accused of.** Job has done nothing for thirty chapters but say
*Where is God?* — *"Oh that I knew where I might find him!"* (23:3) — and his cries are addressed:
*"I cry to you, and you do not answer me"* (30:20). His night is real — *"In the night season my bones
are pierced in me"* (30:17) — and so is the song he lost: *"my harp has turned to mourning"* (30:31).
And 35:11 takes Job's own appeal and reverses it. Job told the friends, *"But ask the animals, now, and
they shall teach you; the birds of the sky, and they shall tell you"* (12:7). Elihu's God *"teaches us
more than the animals of the earth, and makes us wiser than the birds of the sky"*, the same two
classes in the same order. Job sent the friends to the animals to learn what God does; Elihu says God
makes men wiser than the animals, so that they need not ask them.

So God does not answer, and the reason is *"the pride of evil men"* (35:12) and the *emptiness*
(*šāwʾ*) of the cry (35:13) — the word of the third commandment's vain use of the name. *"How much less
when you say you don't see him"* (35:14): Job's *"he goes by me, and I don't see him"* (9:11) and his
search in four directions (23:8–9). And then, unexpectedly, a concession: *"The cause is before him,
and you wait for him!"* *Dîn*, the case — **Elihu admits that Job's lawsuit is on God's docket**, and
tells him to wait. It is the most hopeful clause in either speech, and the next verse withdraws it:
because God *"has not visited in his anger"*, Job has mistaken patience for neglect (35:15). The WEB's
*arrogance* there translates *paš*, a word found nowhere else in the Bible; the sense is a guess.

> *"Therefore Job opens his mouth with empty talk, and he multiplies words without knowledge."*
> (35:16)

*Hebel*, *empty talk* — the breath and vapour of Ecclesiastes, and the word Job used of the friends:
*"why then have you become altogether vain?"* (27:12), *"how can you comfort me with nonsense?"*
(21:34). Elihu gives it back. *Opens his mouth* is the phrase of 3:1, where Job's speaking began. See
[[Language and Speech]], [[Prayer]], and [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].

**How to read him.** These two speeches are where Elihu stops being new. His first speech brought
two things the friends had never said — suffering as warning, and a mediator who finds a ransom — and
these bring one: that God's sustaining of every breath is the ground of his justice (34:13–15). The
rest is the friends' case, put better: Bildad's thesis (34:12), Eliphaz's image (34:7) and his
question of profit (35:7), Zophar's certainty that God sees (11:11, at 34:21). And he has started to
misquote, putting the wicked's sentence of 21:15 into Job's mouth twice, and concluding that Job
*"adds rebellion to his sin"*.

**And yet God's first words agree with his last.** *"Job speaks without knowledge"* (34:35), *"he
multiplies words without knowledge"* (35:16) — and then *"Who is this who darkens counsel by words
without knowledge?"* (38:2), which Job himself repeats in his surrender (42:3). God also asks Elihu's
question of 34:17 at 40:8. The book will not let either half of this go. At 38:2 God says of Job's
words what Elihu said; at 42:7 God says of Job's words that they were *"the thing that is right"*, as
the friends' were not. **Elihu has the charge right and the conclusion wrong**: Job did speak beyond
his knowledge, and was not therefore wicked. See
[[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]].

## People

- [[Elihu the Buzite]] — the speaker of both speeches (34:1; 35:1); to *"wise men"* about Job in
  chapter 34, to Job in chapter 35.
- [[Job (person)]] — quoted (34:5–6, 9; 35:2–3, 14), judged *"without knowledge"* (34:35; 35:16), and
  wished *"tried to the end"* (34:36). He does not answer.
- [[God]] — *"the Almighty"*, who does no wickedness (34:10–12), answers to no one (34:13), holds every
  breath (34:14–15), respects no prince (34:19), sees in every darkness (34:21–22), hears the cry of
  the poor (34:28), is *"God my Maker, who gives songs in the night"* (35:10), and does not hear an
  empty cry (35:13).
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — not named; his 15:16 at 34:7, his 22:2–3 at 35:6–8, his 22:12 at 35:5,
  and the charge of 22:5 restated at 34:37.
- [[Bildad the Shuhite]] — not named; his 8:3 restated at 34:12.
- **The "wise men"** (34:2, 10, 34) — Elihu's audience and jury in chapter 34, never identified; possibly
  the three friends, possibly a wider assembly. *"Your companions"* (35:4) is equally unclear: the
  friends, or the *"company"* of the wicked Elihu has just put Job in (34:8). No pages.

## Places

None named. The *thick gloom* of 34:22 (*ṣalmāwet*) is the *shadow of death* of 3:5 and of the land of
the dead at 10:21–22, but [[Sheol]] is not in view here.

## Themes

- [[Retribution]] — *"For the work of a man he will render to him"* (34:11), its plainest statement in
  the book.
- [[Righteousness]] — *"I am righteous"* (34:5); *"Will you condemn him who is righteous and
  mighty?"* (34:17); *"My righteousness is more than God's"* (35:2); *"If you are righteous, what do you
  give him?"* (35:7).
- [[Judgment]] — God's justice defended (34:10–12); no trial needed (34:23); the mighty struck at
  midnight and in open sight (34:20, 26).
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — God as the ruler appointed by no one (34:13), who calls kings vile and
  respects no prince (34:17–19), *"that the godless man may not reign"* (34:30).
- [[Life and Death]] — the spirit and breath gathered back, and all flesh to dust (34:14–15).
- [[Creation]] — *"they all are the work of his hands"* (34:19); *"God my Maker"* (35:10).
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — *"his eyes are on the ways of a man"* (34:21); *"you say you don't see
  him"* (35:14).
- [[Light and Darkness]] — *"There is no darkness, nor thick gloom"* where the wicked can hide
  (34:22); *"songs in the night"* (35:10).
- [[Prayer]] — the cry of the poor heard (34:28); the cry of the oppressed unanswered because no one
  asks *"Where is God my Maker?"* (35:9–13).
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — *"because of the pride of evil men"* (35:12).
- [[Language and Speech]] — *"the ear tries words"* (34:3); *"without knowledge"* (34:35; 35:16);
  *"multiplies his words against God"* (34:37); *"empty talk"* (35:16).
- [[Wisdom]] — the wise men as jury (34:2, 34); *"His words are without wisdom"* (34:35); God who
  *"makes us wiser than the birds of the sky"* (35:11).
- [[Testing]] — *"I wish that Job were tried to the end"* (34:36), *bāḥan*, the verb of 23:10.
- [[Sin]] — *"he adds rebellion to his sin"* (34:37); *"If you have sinned, what effect do you have
  against him?"* (35:6).
- [[Suffering]] — *"My wound is incurable"* (34:6), Job's arrow; the multitude of oppressions (35:9).
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — the hearing Job asked for, declared unnecessary (34:23); *"The cause is
  before him"* (35:14).

## Notes

- **Two speeches or one?** The heading at 35:1 repeats 34:1, and the book counts four Elihu speeches
  (32:6; 34:1; 35:1; 36:1). The plan joins the second and third on one page because chapter 35 answers
  the question chapter 34 raised (34:9) and both close on *"without knowledge"*. The book page's
  boundary is unchanged.
- **The addressee shifts.** Chapter 34 addresses the *"wise men"* in the plural (34:2, 10, 34) and speaks
  of Job in the third person, but 34:16–33 is in the singular *you*, and most readers take it as turning
  to Job in the middle. Chapter 35 is to Job throughout. The wiki reads it this way and notes that the
  singular at 34:16 could be addressed to each of the hearers instead.
- **34:6, "my wound".** Hebrew *ḥiṣṣî*, *my arrow*; the WEB's *wound* is the usual sense in context (the
  arrow-wound). The link to 6:4 is the wiki's observation from the shared word; it is widely made but
  not certain to be intended.
- **34:29–33 is textually difficult.** Translations differ widely, especially at 34:30–33; some
  commentators regard the Hebrew as damaged. The WEB is followed, and nothing on this page rests on its
  details beyond the imagined confession of 34:31–32.
- **34:36, "I wish".** Hebrew *ʾābî*, a rare word; the Vulgate took it as *my father* (*Pater mi*),
  making the line a prayer — *My father, let Job be tried*. The WEB's *I wish* follows the more common
  modern understanding. Recorded, not adopted.
- **35:2, "more than God's".** *Ṣidqî mēʾēl* may be comparative (*more than God's*) or mean *my right
  before God* / *from God*. The first is a harder charge than anything Job said; the second is close to
  32:2 and to Job's claim at 27:6. The WEB's reading is kept, and the ambiguity flagged.
- **35:15, "arrogance".** *Paš* occurs only here; *arrogance*, *folly*, and (by emendation, *pešaʿ*)
  *transgression* have all been proposed. Nothing is built on it.
- **Elihu's misquotation is a finding, not a correction.** The WEB has Elihu attribute to Job a sentence
  (34:9; 35:3) that Job attributed to the wicked (21:15) and disowned (21:16). The wiki treats this as
  part of Elihu's characterization, which is how most commentators take it; it is not a claim that the
  text is in error. A softer reading is that Elihu is summarizing the implication of 9:22 and 9:29–31
  rather than quoting.
- **Agreement with God at 38:2 and 40:8.** Recorded on both sides: God's first words echo Elihu's last
  (34:35; 35:16), and God's 40:8 asks Elihu's 34:17, so readers who see Elihu as the book's preparer for
  the whirlwind have textual grounds. Readers who see him set aside have 42:7, which commends Job's speech
  without qualification and says nothing of Elihu's. The wiki does not choose.
- **No contradiction between wiki pages found.** The forward references already in the wiki — 34:3 at
  [[Though He Slay Me]] and [[Language and Speech]]; 34:7 at [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]]; 34:12 at
  [[Bildad's First Speech]]; 34:10–19 at [[Shall Not the Judge of All the Earth Do Right]]; 35:6 at
  [[The Arrows of the Almighty]]; 35:6–8 at [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] and
  [[Can a Man Be Profitable to God - Job 22.2-3, Job 35.6-8, and Luke 17.10]] — are borne out by the
  text.
