# I Will Not Put Away My Integrity

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 26:1–27:23

## Summary

The last speech of the dialogue. [[Bildad the Shuhite]] has just answered Job's dare (24:25) with six
verses about God's dominion and a man who is a worm, and the cycle's next speaker,
[[Zophar the Naamathite]], never comes. Job speaks twice in a row instead — the text marks the second time with a
heading found nowhere else in the dialogue, *"Job again took up his parable"* (27:1) — and when he
finishes, the three friends have said their last word in the book. The speech has four movements, and
each of them raises a question the wiki has to answer rather than assume.

**1. Sarcasm aimed at one man (26:1–4).** *"How have you helped him who is without power! How have you
saved the arm that has no strength! How have you counseled him who has no wisdom, and plentifully
declared sound knowledge!"* The *you* is singular: this is addressed to Bildad, and it is the
answer his speech deserved — a man who asked for counter-evidence received a hymn and a definition
of himself as vermin, and Job thanks him for the help. The fourth verse is the sharp one: *"To whom
have you uttered words? Whose spirit came out of you?"* The word is *nišmâ*, breath. Bildad's
breath was borrowed. Half his speech was Eliphaz's (*"born of a woman … clean"*, 15:14; the descent
from the heavens, 4:17–19 and 15:15–16), and Eliphaz claimed to have had it from a spirit in the
night (4:15–16). Job's question asks whose wind Bildad is passing on, and the answer the reader
knows is: someone else's, at second hand.

**2. A hymn to God's power below and above (26:5–14).** Job then does what Bildad tried to do and does
it better. Bildad's hymn (25:2–3) stayed in the heights; Job's begins at the bottom of the world.
*"The departed spirits tremble, those beneath the waters"* — *rəpāʾîm*, the shades — and *"Sheol is
naked before God, and Abaddon has no covering"* (26:5–6). From there it rises: the north stretched
*"over empty space"*, the earth hung *"on nothing"* (26:7), water bound in clouds that do not
burst (26:8), the throne veiled (26:9), a circle drawn on the waters at the boundary of light and
darkness (26:10), the pillars of heaven trembling at his rebuke (26:11), the sea stirred and Rahab
struck through (26:12), the heavens made fair by his Spirit and the fleeing serpent pierced (26:13).
Then the verse that turns the whole hymn against the man it outbids:

> *"Behold, these are but the outskirts of his ways. How small a whisper do we hear of him! But the
> thunder of his power who can understand?"* (26:14)

**Bildad drew from God's greatness the conclusion that man is a worm. Job draws from it the
conclusion that nobody — Bildad included — knows very much about God.** Everything in the hymn is
*the outskirts* (*qəṣôt*, the edges, the fringe), and all of it together is a *whisper*
(*šēmeṣ dābār*, a faint trace of a word). The friends have spent twenty chapters reasoning from God's
character to Job's guilt as if they had heard the thunder. Job's hymn ends by saying that no one has.
It is the speech's bridge to chapter 28, which asks *"Where shall wisdom be found?"* and answers
that only God knows the way to it, and to the whirlwind, where the thunder finally speaks
(37:2–5; 40:9).

**3. The oath (27:1–12).** *"As God lives, who has taken away my right, the Almighty, who has made my
soul bitter … surely my lips shall not speak unrighteousness, neither shall my tongue utter deceit.
Far be it from me that I should justify you. Until I die I will not put away my integrity from me. I
hold fast to my righteousness, and will not let it go. My heart shall not reproach me so long as I
live"* (27:2–6). This is the book's hinge, and the most compressed statement of Job's position
anywhere in the dialogue. Four things in it are load-bearing:

- **He swears by the God he accuses.** *Ḥay-ʾēl*, *as God lives*, is the strongest oath formula in
  Hebrew, and its object is described in the same breath as the one *"who has taken away my right"*
  (*hēsîr mišpāṭî*). Job appeals to God as the guarantor of truth against God as the one who has
  denied him justice. It is the same split he has been living since 16:19 — the witness in heaven
  against the God who tears him — made into a single sentence. No one swears by a God he has given
  up on.
- **The verb answers itself.** God *hēsîr*, removed, Job's right (27:2); Job *lōʾ-ʾāsîr*, will not
  remove, his integrity (27:5). The same verb, *sûr*, three verses apart. **God has taken away what
  was owed to Job; Job will not take away what is his to keep.** That is the whole of his ethic in
  the dialogue, and the reason he cannot accept the friends' offer: the restoration they promise
  costs a confession, and the confession would be *deceit* (27:4).
- **It is the prologue's word, and the prologue's verb.** *Tummâ*, integrity, has been in two mouths
  before this: God's, *"He still maintains his integrity"* (2:3), and his wife's, *"Do you still
  maintain your integrity?"* (2:9). Both used the verb *ḥāzaq*, to hold fast. Job now says
  *tummātî*, *my integrity*, and *heḥĕzaqtî*, *I hold fast* — to his righteousness. **He answers his
  wife's question, twenty-five chapters late, in her own words, and he confirms God's verdict
  without knowing it was given.** See [[Renounce God and Die - The Euphemism and the Accuser's Prediction]].
- **The breath of God in his nostrils** (27:3) — *rûaḥ ʾĕlôah bəʾappî* — is Genesis 2:7's picture,
  where God *"breathed into his nostrils the breath of life"*. Job's oath runs *for the length of my
  life*, and his life is God's breath. One verse after asking Bildad *whose breath* came out of him
  (26:4), Job swears on the breath God put in him.

The words *"Far be it from me that I should justify you"* turn the friends' vocabulary back on them.
*ʾAṣdîq ʾetkem* — *declare you in the right*, the forensic verb Bildad had just used of man and God
(25:4) — and the *you* is plural. Job will not concede the case to the three of them. Then he does
what the friends have been doing all along, and wishes the fate of the wicked on someone: *"Let my
enemy be as the wicked"* (27:7). The questions that follow (27:8–10) — *"Will God hear his cry when
trouble comes on him? Will he delight himself in the Almighty?"* — are the difference between the
godless and Job, and they are the difference Job has been demonstrating for twenty-four chapters: he
has cried out to God continually, and the friends never once have. *"I will teach you about the hand
of God"* (27:11): the man the friends have been instructing claims the role of teacher.

**4. The portion of a wicked man (27:13–23).** And then, in Job's mouth, the friends' doctrine: *"This
is the portion of a wicked man with God, the heritage of oppressors, which they receive from the
Almighty."* His children multiplied for the sword (27:14), his widows without lament (27:15), his
silver worn by the just (27:16–17), his house a moth's (27:18), lying down rich and waking to
nothing (27:19), terrors overtaking him *"like waters"*, a storm stealing him away in the night, the
east wind sweeping him out of his place, and men clapping their hands and hissing (27:20–23).

**The first line is Zophar's last line.** *"This is the portion of a wicked man from God, the
heritage appointed to him by God"* closed Zophar's second speech (20:29); 27:13 repeats it with two
words changed. And the passage says, nearly clause for clause, what Job denied in chapter 21:

| Chapter 21 (Job) | Chapter 27 (Job, as received) |
|---|---|
| *"Their child is established with them in their sight"* (21:8) | *"If his children are multiplied, it is for the sword"* (27:14) |
| *"How often is it that they are as stubble before the wind, as chaff that the storm carries away?"* (21:18) | *"A storm steals him away in the night"* (27:20) |
| *"They spend their days in prosperity. In an instant they go down to Sheol"* (21:13) | *"He lies down rich, but he shall not do so again"* (27:19) |
| *"Where is the house of the prince?"* — the friends' question, quoted (21:28) | *"He builds his house as the moth"* (27:18) |

The second row is exact in the Hebrew: 21:18 is *gənābattû sûpâ*, *the storm steals it away*, and
27:20 is *laylâ gənābattû sûpâ*, *by night the storm steals him away*. **In chapter 21 Job asked how
often the storm takes the wicked and implied the answer was rarely. In chapter 27, as the text
stands, he says that it does.** The wiki flags this as a contradiction within Job's own speeches
rather than smoothing it; the readings are set out in the Notes. Whoever is speaking, the doctrine
being recited is the one the epilogue will rule out as a diagnosis of Job (42:7) — and it is the
last time anyone in the dialogue recites it.

## People

- [[Job (person)]] — speaking twice in succession, where Zophar should have spoken between. Mockery
  of Bildad (26:2–4), a hymn that outbids Bildad's (26:5–14), the oath on which his whole case rests
  (27:2–6), and a portrait of the wicked man's ruin that contradicts his own chapter 21 (27:13–23).
  **The last thing he says to the friends is that he will not say they are right.**
- [[Bildad the Shuhite]] — the singular *you* of 26:2–4. His help, counsel and *"sound knowledge"*
  are thanked with sarcasm, and his breath is called borrowed (26:4). On the reconstruction
  recorded below, 26:5–14 is the rest of his speech.
- [[Zophar the Naamathite]] — absent, and quoted. 27:13 is his 20:29 with two words changed, which
  is the strongest single piece of evidence for the reconstruction that gives him 27:13–23 as a lost
  third speech.
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — present by implication in 26:4: the *"spirit"* behind Bildad's
  borrowed words is, at one remove, the spirit of Eliphaz's night vision (4:15–16), whose message
  (4:17–19) Bildad had just repeated.
- [[God]] — sworn by and accused in one breath (27:2). The God of the hymn is the master of Sheol,
  the waters, the clouds, the pillars of heaven, the sea and the serpent, and he is known only at his
  edges: *"How small a whisper do we hear of him!"* (26:14).

## Places

- [[Sheol]] — *"naked before God"* (26:6), with **Abaddon** in parallel, *"no covering"*. The depth
  of the world is the first thing in the hymn, and the point is exposure: nothing below is hidden
  from God. The *rəpāʾîm*, the shades, tremble beneath the waters (26:5). See
  [[Abaddon Has No Covering - Job 26.6, Proverbs 15.11, and Revelation 9.11]].

## Themes

- [[Integrity]] — *tummâ* at 27:5, the prologue's word (2:3, 9) in Job's own mouth for the first
  time, with the prologue's verb *ḥāzaq* (27:6). The theme's centre.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — *ḥay-ʾēl*, *as God lives* (27:2), an oath sworn by God against God; the
  first of Job's two great oaths, the second being chapter 31.
- [[Righteousness]] — *"Far be it from me that I should justify (ʾaṣdîq) you"* (27:5) and *"I hold
  fast to my righteousness (ṣədāqâ)"* (27:6); the forensic and the personal sense of the word in two
  consecutive verses.
- [[Judgment]] — *mišpāṭî*, *my right*, *my case* (27:2), taken away by God; the lawsuit language of
  chapters 9, 13 and 23 in its bitterest form.
- [[Creation]] — 26:7–13, the north over *tōhû* (the *formless* of Genesis 1:2), the earth hung on
  nothing, the circle on the waters, the sea and Rahab, the heavens made fair by God's Spirit; and
  the breath of God in Job's nostrils (27:3, Genesis 2:7).
- [[Wisdom]] — *"How have you counseled him who has no wisdom"* (26:3), and *"these are but the
  outskirts of his ways"* (26:14): the limits of knowledge about God, set out one chapter before the
  hymn to wisdom.
- [[Light and Darkness]] — *"to the confines of light and darkness"* (26:10): the boundary God drew,
  and the night in which the storm steals the wicked away (27:20).
- [[Retribution]] — 27:13–23, the friends' portrait of the wicked man's end in Job's mouth, in
  direct contradiction of 21:7–18. **Flagged, not resolved**: see the Notes.
- [[Life and Death]] — the shades trembling (26:5), *"the length of my life is still in me"* (27:3),
  *"Until I die"* (27:5), the wicked man's children *"buried in death"* (27:15).
- [[Hope]] — *"what is the hope of the godless, when he is cut off?"* (27:8): hope defined by whether
  God hears the cry (27:9), which is the test Job has passed and the friends have never sat.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — 27:16–17, silver heaped *"as the dust"* and clothing *"as the clay"*,
  worn by the just and divided by the innocent; the inheritance motif of Proverbs 13:22.

## Notes

- **Whose is 26:5–14?** The question [[Bildad's Third Speech]] left for this page. **The case for
  Bildad:** the sarcasm of 26:2–4 is addressed to a singular *you* and stops abruptly; 26:5–14
  continues the subject of 25:2–3 (God's sovereignty in the heights, his light, his host) and would
  bring Bildad's speech up to the length of his others; and the hymn contains no complaint and no
  first person. **The case for Job:** the text gives 26:1 to Job and no heading intervenes; Job has
  already composed two hymns of exactly this kind (9:5–13, with Rahab's helpers at 9:13, and
  12:13–25), so the form is his as much as anyone's; and 26:14 draws a conclusion the friends never
  draw — not *"how much less man"* but *"how small a whisper do we hear"*, the limit of knowledge
  rather than the lowliness of man. **The wiki reads it as Job's**, in keeping with the received
  arrangement, and reads it as an act of one-upmanship with a point: Job takes Bildad's subject, does
  it at greater depth and height, and ends it in a confession of ignorance that Bildad's speech, and
  the friends' whole case, cannot survive.
- **Whose is 27:13–23, and does it contradict chapter 21?** The contradiction is real on the surface
  and in the Hebrew (the table in the Summary). Four readings, recorded without choosing:
  **(a) A lost speech of Zophar.** The third cycle was damaged; 27:13 is Zophar's own formula from
  20:29, and the section's doctrine is his. On this view a heading such as *"Then Zophar the
  Naamathite answered"* has fallen out before 27:13, and Zophar's third speech survives in part.
  **(b) Job's imprecation on his accuser.** 27:7 has just said *"Let my enemy be as the wicked"*, and
  the portrait that follows is what Job wishes on the man who would falsely condemn him — the friends'
  doctrine turned on the friends, as Job warned them at 13:7–11 that God would rebuke their
  partiality. **(c) Job's genuine concession.** Job never denied that God punishes the wicked; he
  denied that it happens reliably or visibly (21:17, *"how often?"*) and that suffering proves guilt.
  He may grant the friends' end-point while refusing their inference — and 27:11–12 frames the section
  as *Job's* teaching about *"the hand of God"* to men who *"have seen it"* and drawn vain conclusions.
  **(d) Citation.** Job is reciting the friends' teaching back to them, with 27:12's *"why then have
  you become altogether vain?"* as his comment on it. **The wiki keeps the received arrangement**
  and treats the conflict with 21:7–18 as a flagged contradiction between two of Job's speeches,
  recorded also on [[Retribution]], [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]] and [[Job (person)]]. What none of
  the four readings changes is the oath of 27:2–6, which is unambiguously Job's and is the speech's
  centre.
- ***"Job again took up his parable"*** **(27:1; 29:1).** *Wayyōsep ʾiyyôb śəʾēt məšālô*. Every other
  speech in the dialogue is introduced with *"Then X answered"*; these two are not. *Māšāl* is a
  proverb, a parable, a set speech — the word for Balaam's oracles (Numbers 23:7) — and the formula
  implies that Job resumes after a pause in which no one has answered. On the received text that pause
  is Zophar's silence, and the heading records it.
- **"He hangs the earth on nothing" (26:7).** *Tōleh ʾereṣ ʿal-bəlî-mâ* — literally *on
  without-anything*. The verse has often been cited as a remarkable anticipation of modern cosmology;
  the wiki notes the citation and reads the verse as poetry about God's power, whose point is that the
  earth has no visible support but God. The parallel clause stretches the *north* (*ṣāpôn*, also the
  name of the mountain of the gods in Canaanite myth, cf. Isaiah 14:13) over *tōhû*, the word of
  Genesis 1:2's *"formless and empty"*. **Job's cosmos rests on the void of the first verse of
  Genesis, held there by God alone.**
- ***"He stirs up the sea"*** **(26:12).** *Rāgaʿ hayyām* — the verb can mean *stir up* or *still*,
  and translations divide; the WEB takes *stirs up*. Either way the sea is in God's control, and the
  parallel clause strikes Rahab through. The verb *rāgaʿ* is the root of *regaʿ*, *a moment*, which
  Zophar (20:5) and Job (21:13) used of the wicked man's sudden end.
- **"The spirit of God is in my nostrils" (27:3).** *Rûaḥ* here, *nišmâ* in the first line of the
  verse; Genesis 2:7 has *nišmat ḥayyîm*, the breath of life, *bəʾappāyw*, *in his nostrils*. Elihu
  will use the same pairing of himself (33:4), not yet ingested.
- **No contradiction between wiki pages found** other than the flagged one within Job's speeches.
