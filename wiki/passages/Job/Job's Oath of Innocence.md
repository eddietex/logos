# Job's Oath of Innocence

**Book:** [[Job]]
**Reference:** Job 31:1–40

## Summary

*"I made a covenant with my eyes, how then should I look lustfully at a young woman?"* (31:1).
Chapter 29 entered the life into evidence and chapter 30 the injury; chapter 31 supplies the third
thing a defence needs, **the sworn statement that the injury was not deserved.** It is the last and
longest of Job's speeches, and its form is the Hebrew oath of clearance: a string of *ʾim* clauses —
*"If I have …"* — each of which either names its own penalty (*"then let me sow, and let another
eat"*, 31:8) or leaves the penalty unsaid, which is how a Hebrew oath normally works: *"if I have done
it"* with the curse suppressed means *"I have not."* Job lists the sins a man of his wealth and
standing could commit, one area of life at a time, and denies each under a curse on himself.

**What he swears to, in order:**

| Verses | The sin denied | The curse invoked |
|---|---|---|
| 31:1–4 | the lustful look — *"a covenant with my eyes"* | *"calamity to the unrighteous"* (31:3), stated as principle |
| 31:5–8 | falsehood and deceit; *"if my heart walked after my eyes"* (31:7) | *"let me sow, and let another eat"* (31:8) |
| 31:9–12 | adultery — *"laid wait at my neighbor's door"* (31:9) | *"let my wife grind for another"* (31:10) |
| 31:13–15 | despising the lawsuit of his slaves | none — a question: *"what shall I answer him?"* (31:14) |
| 31:16–23 | withholding from the poor, the widow, the fatherless, the naked | *"let my shoulder fall from the shoulder blade"* (31:22) |
| 31:24–25 | trust in gold | none |
| 31:26–28 | worship of the sun and moon | none — *"I should have denied the God who is above"* (31:28) |
| 31:29–30 | gloating over an enemy, cursing him | none |
| 31:31–32 | refusing food or lodging to a stranger | none |
| 31:33–34 | hiding sin *"like Adam"*, for fear of the crowd | none — broken off by the cry of 31:35 |
| 31:38–40 | wronging the land and its owners | *"let briers grow instead of wheat"* (31:40) |

**1. The eye and the heart (31:1–12).** The oath opens not with a deed but with a look. *Bərît
kāratî ləʿênay*, *"I cut a covenant with my eyes"* (31:1) — the idiom of covenant-making, used of
Yahweh and Abram (Genesis 15:18) and of Abraham and Abimelech (21:27, 32), made here with a part of his
own body. The sin is the one Genesis 3:6 begins with, the fruit *"a
delight to the eyes"*, and the one Jesus will define at Matthew 5:28; Job's *"if my heart walked after
my eyes"* (31:7) is the order of Numbers 15:39's *"your own heart and your own eyes"*. See
[[A Covenant with My Eyes - Job 31.1 and Matthew 5.28]]. Between the look and the deed sits the
oath's one explicit request: ***"let me be weighed in an even balance, that God may know my
integrity"*** (31:6) — *bəmōʾzənê-ṣedeq*, *scales of righteousness*, and ***tummātî***, the word of
2:3, 2:9 and 27:5. **Job asks God to do what the friends did not: weigh the evidence.** Adultery follows
(31:9–12), with the most terrible of the self-curses — *"let my wife grind for another, and let
others sleep with her"* (31:10), the futility curse of Deuteronomy 28:30, *"You will betroth a wife,
and another man shall lie with her"*, called down by a man on himself. Grinding is slave work, and
the line carries a sexual sense as well; [[Job's Wife]] is named in it, the only time after 19:17 that
she is mentioned. Adultery is *"a heinous crime"* (*zimmâ*), *"an iniquity to be punished by the
judges"* (31:11), and *"a fire that consumes to destruction"* — *ʿad-ʾăbaddôn*, to Abaddon (31:12).
See [[Abaddon Has No Covering - Job 26.6, Proverbs 15.11, and Revelation 9.11]].

**2. The servant, the poor, and the shared womb (31:13–23).** The centre of the oath is the household
and the town. *"If I have despised the cause of my male servant or of my female servant, when they
contended with me"* (31:13) — *bərībām ʿimmādî*, **their lawsuit against me**: Job claims that his own
slaves could take him to law and be heard. The reason is the most radical sentence in the chapter:

> *"Didn't he who made me in the womb make him? Didn't one fashion us in the womb?"* (31:15)

Master and slave have one maker and one manner of making. It is the thought of Proverbs 14:31 and
22:2, *"Yahweh is the maker of them all"*, and of Malachi 2:10, *"Hasn't one God created us?"*, and it
turns 10:8–12's *"Your hands have framed me"* outward: the God who shaped Job in the womb shaped his
slave the same way. See [[You Have Fashioned Me as Clay - Job 10.8-12, Psalm 139, and the Potter]].
And the question before it is the one Job will be asked: *"When he visits, what shall I answer
him?"* (31:14) — *māh ʾăšîbennû*, which he will say again to God's face at 40:4, *"What shall I answer
you?"* (*māh ʾăšîbekā*).

Then the poor (31:16–23), and here the oath is **Eliphaz's charge sheet answered clause by clause**.
Eliphaz said Job had *"withheld bread from the hungry"* (22:7); Job swears *"If I have withheld the poor
from their desire"* (31:16). Eliphaz said *"You have sent widows away empty"* (22:9); Job, *"or have
caused the eyes of the widow to fail"* (31:16). Eliphaz said Job *"stripped the naked of their
clothing"* (22:6); Job, *"if I have seen any perish for want of clothing … if he hasn't been warmed
with my sheep's fleece"* (31:19–20). And Eliphaz said *"the arms of the fatherless have been broken"*
(22:9); Job, *"if I have lifted up my hand against the fatherless … then let my shoulder fall from the
shoulder blade, and my arm be broken from the bone"* (31:21–22). **Eliphaz said the orphans' arms were
broken; Job offers his own.** *"Because I saw my help in the gate"* (31:21) — the court of 29:7, where a
great man could win any case. See
[[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]].

**3. God above (31:24–28).** Two sins against God himself, both secret. **Gold** — *"If I have made gold
my hope, and have said to the fine gold, 'You are my confidence'"* (31:24) — answering Eliphaz's
*"Lay your treasure in the dust, the gold of Ophir among the stones of the brooks"* (22:24), which
assumed Job's wealth was his god. *Ketem*, *fine gold*, is the word wisdom outprices at 28:16 and 19.
See [[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]]. And **the heavens** — *"if I have seen the sun when it
shined, or the moon moving in splendor, and my heart has been secretly enticed, and my hand threw a kiss
from my mouth"* (31:26–27): the worship Deuteronomy 4:19 forbids, *"lest you lift up your eyes to the
sky, and when you see the sun and the moon … you are drawn away and worship them"*, performed by the
gesture of 1 Kings 19:18, *"every mouth which has not kissed him"*. *"For I should have denied the God
who is above"* (31:28). **This is the one sin on the list that would make the accuser right.** The
wager of 1:11 and 2:5 was that Job *"will renounce you to your face"*; Job swears that he has not
renounced God even in the heart. See [[Idolatry]].

**4. The enemy, the stranger, and the hidden sin (31:29–34).** *"If I have rejoiced at the destruction
of him who hated me … (yes, I have not allowed my mouth to sin by asking his life with a curse)"*
(31:29–30) — *lōʾ-nātattî laḥăṭōʾ ḥikkî*, the claim of 2:10, *"In all this Job didn't sin with his
lips"*, extended from God to his enemies. It is the ethic of Proverbs 24:17, *"Don't rejoice when your
enemy falls"*, and of Exodus 23:4–5, and it reaches toward Matthew 5:44. Then hospitality: *"the
foreigner has not camped in the street, but I have opened my doors to the traveler"* (31:32) —
[[Lot]]'s doorway at Sodom, where the visitors said *"we will stay in the street all night"* and he
*"urged them greatly"* (Genesis 19:2–3). And then the last and deepest denial:

> *"if like Adam I have covered my transgressions, by hiding my iniquity in my heart, because I feared
> the great multitude, and the contempt of families terrified me, so that I kept silence, and didn't go
> out of the door—"* (31:33–34)

***Kəʾādām*** — *like Adam*, or *as men do*; the Hebrew allows both and translations divide. On the
WEB's reading Job sets himself against the first man, who *"hid himself"* among the trees and blamed
the woman (Genesis 3:8–12). **Every other clause has denied a sin; this one denies the cover-up.** Job
has hidden nothing, and the proof is the speech itself — thirty chapters in public, in front of the
men whose contempt he is said to fear. See
[[If Like Adam I Have Covered - Job 31.33 and Genesis 3]].

**5. The signature (31:35–37).** The sentence breaks off. The oath's *if* never reaches its *then*;
instead:

> *"oh that I had one to hear me! Behold, here is my signature! Let the Almighty answer me! Let the
> accuser write my indictment!"* (31:35)

*Mî yittēn-lî*, *who will give me* — the wish of 6:8, 14:13, 19:23 and 23:3, for the last time.
***Hen-tāwî***, *"here is my taw"*: *taw* is the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet, written in the
old script as a cross-mark, and it is the *mark* set on the foreheads of the faithful at Ezekiel 9:4.
**Job signs his deposition.** *"Let the accuser write my indictment"* — *ʾîš rîbî*, *the man of my
lawsuit*, the opposing party at law. It is not *haśśāṭān*; the reader knows who the accuser of the
prologue was, and Job does not, and the word is a different one (see [[Satan]]). *"Surely I would
carry it on my shoulder; and I would bind it to me as a crown"* (31:36): the indictment, when it
comes, will be worn as a decoration, because it will be empty. *"I would declare to him the number of
my steps. as a prince would I go near to him"* (31:37). God was the one counting Job's steps — *"But now
you count my steps"* (14:16), *"Doesn't he see my ways, and count all my steps?"* (31:4) — and Job
offers to read out the count himself, and to walk in *"as a prince"* (*nāgîd*), **the posture of the
man who sat at the gate in chapter 29, not of the man in ashes in chapter 2.**

**6. The land, and the end (31:38–40).** *"If my land cries out against me, and its furrows weep
together; if I have eaten its fruits without money, or have caused its owners to lose their life, let
briers grow instead of wheat, and stinkweed instead of barley"* (31:38–40). The ground as witness —
the ground that received Abel's blood and whose *"voice … cries to me"* (Genesis 4:10), and that was
cursed to bear *"thorns and thistles"* (Genesis 3:18). See [[Do Not Cover My Blood]]. And then the
narrator, or an editor: ***"The words of Job are ended."*** *Tammû dibrê ʾiyyôb* — *tammû*, **from the
*t-m-m* root of *tām* and *tummâ***: the words of the man of integrity are *complete*. Psalm 72:20 closes
a collection the same way, *"This ends the prayers by David, the son of Jesse"*, with a different verb.
Job speaks again, but only twice and briefly, and only in reply (40:3–5; 42:1–6).

**What the oath does.** It is the end of the dialogue, and a legal act rather than an argument. Every
earlier speech of Job's asked for a trial; this one **files the case.** It puts God in a position the
book has not yet seen: a man has sworn, under curses that God alone can execute, that he is innocent of
everything a man could be charged with, and either the curses fall or the oath stands. The friends do
not answer it — they have stopped (32:1) — and the speaker who does, Elihu, is not one Job asked for.
**God answers, at 38:1, and does not mention a single item on the list.** The epilogue's verdict (42:7)
is the only comment the book ever makes on whether the oath was true.

## People

- [[Job (person)]] — the speaker, closing the speech headed at 29:1 and the whole of his part in the
  dialogue. **He swears his innocence under curses**, area by area (31:1–34, 38–40), signs the
  deposition and demands the charge in writing (31:35–37). *"The words of Job are ended"* (31:40).
- [[God]] — spoken of throughout, addressed nowhere (the last second-person address was 30:20–23): the
  one who *"count[s] all my steps"* (31:4), who will weigh Job *"in an even balance"* (31:6), who
  *"rises up"* and *"visits"* (31:14), *"he who made me in the womb"* and the slave too (31:15), *"the
  God who is above"* (31:28), and *"the Almighty"*, summoned to answer (31:35).
- [[Job's Wife]] — named in the self-curse of 31:10, *"let my wife grind for another"*: the last time
  she is mentioned in the book.
- [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] — not named, and answered: the charges of 22:6–9 and 22:24 are denied one by
  one at 31:16–25.
- [[Adam]] — *"if like Adam I have covered my transgressions"* (31:33), on the WEB's reading of
  *kəʾādām*.
- [[Satan]] — **not present.** The *"accuser"* of 31:35 in the WEB is *ʾîš rîbî*, Job's opponent at law,
  not *haśśāṭān*; but the sin Job denies at 31:27–28 is the one the accuser predicted at 1:11 and 2:5.
- **The servants, the poor, the widow, the fatherless, the enemy, the stranger** — classes, not
  characters; no pages.

## Places

- [[Sheol]] — Abaddon, *"a fire that consumes to destruction"* (31:12), *ʿad-ʾăbaddôn*.

## Themes

- [[Oaths and Vows]] — the whole chapter: the conditional self-curse, with the apodosis sometimes
  stated (31:8, 10, 22, 40) and more often suppressed. **The second and fuller of Job's oaths** (the
  first, 27:2–6).
- [[Integrity]] — *"that God may know my integrity"* (31:6), *tummātî*; and *tammû*, *"are ended"*,
  from the same root, in the chapter's last words (31:40).
- [[Righteousness]] — the book's working definition, given as a list of deeds and refusals.
- [[Sexual Sin]] — the look (31:1), the heart that walks after the eyes (31:7), adultery at a neighbour's
  door (31:9–12).
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — the servants' lawsuit heard (31:13), on the ground of a common maker
  (31:15).
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — the poor, the widow, the fatherless, the naked (31:16–23).
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — gold as hope and confidence (31:24–25).
- [[Idolatry]] — the sun, the moon, and the kiss (31:26–28).
- [[Vengeance]] — no gloating over an enemy, no curse on his life (31:29–30).
- [[Hospitality]] — no stranger in the street (31:31–32).
- [[Guilt and Conscience]] — *"if like Adam I have covered my transgressions"* (31:33–34).
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — *"oh that I had one to hear me!"* (31:35); the case filed, the written
  indictment demanded.
- [[Curse]] — the self-imprecations, and the cursed ground of 31:40.
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — the covenant with the eyes (31:1), *"Doesn't he see my ways"* (31:4), the
  sun and moon *seen* (31:26).
- [[Retribution]] — 31:2–3 states the doctrine as Job still holds it, *"Is it not calamity to the
  unrighteous?"*, and the oath stakes his life on it.

## Notes

- **The form.** A Hebrew oath of clearance is a conditional sentence whose consequence is a curse on the
  speaker; very often the consequence is left unspoken, and the bare *"if I have …"* means *"I have
  not."* Chapter 31 uses both kinds: four curses are spelled out (31:8, 10, 22, 40), and the rest are
  suppressed. Scholars commonly compare the Egyptian "negative confession" of the Book of the Dead,
  in which the dead man declares before the judges a list of sins he has not committed. The comparison
  is with a genre, not a source; the wiki records it as context.
- **How many clauses.** Counts of the *if*-clauses vary (commonly fourteen or sixteen) depending on
  how the subordinate *ʾim* clauses inside a single denial are grouped; the table above groups them by
  subject, and the wiki makes no claim that depends on a number.
- **31:38–40 after the signature.** The land oath comes after the climax of 31:35–37, and many
  commentators propose that it originally stood earlier, so that the speech ended on *"as a prince
  would I go near to him."* There is no manuscript support for moving it. The wiki reads the received
  order and notes that, read as it stands, the speech ends where the book began — on the ground, with
  thorns, the curse of Genesis 3:18 — and with *tammû*.
- **"Like Adam" (31:33).** *Kəʾādām* can be read *"as humankind does"* (so several modern versions) or
  *"like Adam"* (so the WEB, and the King James); the two readings are not exclusive. The wiki follows
  the WEB and records the alternative. The same ambiguity attends Hosea 6:7, *"But they, like Adam, have
  broken the covenant"*, as [[The Fall]] already notes.
- **"The accuser" (31:35).** The WEB's word could suggest that Job is naming the figure of the prologue.
  He is not: the Hebrew is *ʾîš rîbî*, *the man of my case*, an adversary in a lawsuit, and the book
  never lets Job learn of *haśśāṭān*. **No contradiction with [[Satan]]**, whose page says he is present
  in no verse after 2:7; a note is added there so the WEB's English is not mistaken for a counter-example.
- **31:18 is hard.** *"from my youth he grew up with me as with a father, her I have guided from my
  mother's womb"* — the orphan raised in Job's house, and the widow guided from his own birth, which is
  hyperbole on any reading. The wiki argues nothing from the line's details.
- **A misquotation corrected.** [[Integrity]] quoted 31:6 as *"let him weigh me in an even balance"*,
  which is not the WEB's wording; it now reads *"let me be weighed in an even balance"*, from
  `raw/bible/Job.md`.
