# Balaam's Last Oracles

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 23:27–24:25

## Summary

Balak takes Balaam to Peor and again supplies seven altars, bulls, and rams. Balaam now
stops seeking enchantments, turns toward the wilderness, and sees Israel camped by tribes.
The Spirit of God comes on him. His third blessing praises Israel’s tents as watered gardens,
anticipates an exalted kingdom, recalls the exodus, and ends with the lion and the blessing
and curse formula (23:27–24:9).

Balak angrily dismisses him, saying Yahweh has withheld the honor he intended to give.
Balaam repeats that wealth cannot authorize him to exceed Yahweh’s word, then announces
what Israel will do to Moab in the future (24:10–14).

The fourth main oracle sees a distant star and scepter from Israel, striking Moab and
taking Edom and Seir. Shorter sayings pronounce Amalek’s end, the Kenite’s captivity by
Asshur, and affliction from ships of Kittim against Asshur and Eber, followed by further
destruction. Balaam and Balak part company (24:15–25).

## Notes

### Gift, sight, and moral character

The seer who failed to see the angel now claims open eyes and a divine vision.
The narrator attributes the Spirit to him, while his own introductions describe his hearing
and seeing (24:2–4, 15–16). This does not erase the earlier rebuke of his way in Numbers 22:32.
Nor does ceasing to seek enchantments establish a complete conversion narrative. Balak counts
three blessings (24:10); the subsequent future oracle and shorter sayings follow his dismissal.

### Future rule and uncertain names

The star and scepter function here as images of rule, with Moab and Edom as its targets.
The text names no individual ruler. A messianic interpretation may develop from the royal image,
but an identification with a particular person is not established by this unit alone.
Agag is not automatically identified with the king in 1 Samuel 15; Sheth is not silently
equated with Seth. Eber’s scope, the precise reach of Kittim, and the final pronoun in
24:24 remain uncertain here. Kain is handled within the Kenite saying, not as a biography of Cain.
The unnamed city of 24:19 receives no invented geographical identity.

The promise that Edom becomes a possession (24:18) differs from the actual withdrawal in
Numbers 20:21: one is prediction and the other narrated history. Neither should overwrite
the other. The watered-garden imagery is poetic blessing, not a literal survey of the camp.

### Sources and editorial scope

The planned boundary is retained. Named oracle figures and places receive pages even when
their identification remains open. Jacob, Amalek, and Eber are collective references here;
Beor is a named father. Kenites is a collective person page, and Seir is covered by the existing
Edom page. All quotations are copied from stored WEB Numbers; comparisons use stored texts
in paraphrase. No new exact Hebrew or Greek assertion or externally unverified quotation
is introduced. Later fulfillment is left open rather than inferred from a recalled text.
