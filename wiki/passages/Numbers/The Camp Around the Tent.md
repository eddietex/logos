# The Camp Around the Tent

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 2:1–34

## Summary

The census said how many. This chapter says where. Yahweh speaks to Moses and Aaron and gives every
man a place: by his own standard, under the banners of his fathers' house, facing the Tent of
Meeting from a distance on all sides (1–2).

Twelve tribes form four camps of three. Judah with Issachar and Zebulun takes the east, toward the
sunrise, and sets out first (3–9). Reuben with Simeon and Gad takes the south and sets out second
(10–16). Then the Tent moves, with the Levites in the middle of the camps (17). Ephraim with
Manasseh and Benjamin takes the west and sets out third (18–24). Dan with Asher and Naphtali takes
the north and sets out last (25–31).

Each tribe's prince is named again and each figure from chapter 1 is repeated without change. The
sum is the same 603,550, and the Levites are again outside it (32–33). The chapter ends as the last
one did, with Israel doing all that Yahweh commanded (34).

Nothing happens in this chapter except arrangement, and that is its content. Twelve tribes who were
a crowd at the sea now have a side, a neighbor, a leader, and a turn. The same order holds at rest
and on the road: *As they encamp, so shall they set out* (17). At the center of all of it is the
place where Yahweh speaks.

## Notes

**Boundary.** Chapter 2 is one speech with a narrative close, and 3:1 starts a new heading. Taking
the chapter whole kept the plan as drawn.

**The camp.**

The tribe figures match chapter 1 exactly. The four subtotals add up to the stated total, and each
subtotal is the sum of its three tribes. The largest camp leads and the second-largest, Dan's, closes
the column, but the text gives no reason for either. The march goes east, south, west, north, around the Tent.

**The groups follow the mothers, loosely.** The east camp is three sons of Leah: Judah, Issachar, and
Zebulun. The south camp is Leah's first two sons, Reuben and Simeon, with Gad,
the son of her servant Zilpah, taking the place Levi has left. The west camp is all Rachel: Joseph's
two sons and Benjamin. The north camp is the servants' sons: Bilhah's Dan and Naphtali, and Zilpah's
Asher. No verse says this is the principle. It is visible only by reading Genesis 29–30 beside the
chapter. The census had moved Gad to third, behind Simeon, and this is where Gad stands. See [[The Twelve Sons and the Lists That Reorder Them]].

**Judah in front.** The chapter never explains why Judah takes the east and goes first while Reuben,
called *Israel's firstborn* at 1:20, goes second. Genesis 49:3–4 and 8–10 is the obvious background,
and Judah's numbers would serve too, but Numbers cites neither. The east is also the side of the
entrance, for the court's east side has the gate (Exodus 27:13–16), and at 3:38 Moses and the priests
camp there too. Judah therefore stands directly in front of the door. That is a reading of the
layout, and the text does not draw it.

**Standard and banner.** Two words stand in verse 2. *Degel* (WEB *standard*) occurs fourteen times
in the Hebrew Bible by the Strong's tags of the WLC: thirteen in Numbers (1:52, eight times in this
chapter, four in chapter 10) and once at Song of Solomon 2:4. *ʾŌtōt*, WEB's *banners*, is the plural
of the ordinary word for *sign*, used elsewhere in Numbers of Yahweh's signs (WLC 14:11, 22) and of
memorial signs (WLC 17:3, 25; WEB 16:38, 17:10). Whatever a *degel* looked like, the text does not
say. There is a later Jewish tradition assigning emblems to the four standards, and a later Christian
one that sees a cross in the shape of the camp. Neither is in the chapter, and neither was checked
against a source here. See [[By His Own Standard - Degel in Numbers and the Song of Solomon]].

**At a distance.** *Minneged sābîb*, *at a distance … around* (WLC, 2), gives no measure. It places
Israel facing the Tent and held back from it, which is the arrangement 1:53 explained: *that there may
be no wrath on the congregation*.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **In the middle, or divided?** Verse 17 has the Tent set out *with the camp of the Levites in the
   middle of the camps*, and *as they encamp, so shall they set out*. At 10:17–21 the actual march has
   Gershon and Merari going after Judah's camp with the tabernacle, and Kohath after Reuben's with
   the sanctuary. Those are not the same order, though 10:21 gives a practical reason. See
   [[As They Encamp, So They Set Out - Numbers 2.17 and 10.14-28]].
2. **The center or far outside?** Exodus 33:7 says Moses pitched the tent *outside the camp, far away
   from the camp*, and everyone who sought Yahweh went out to it. Here the Tent of Meeting is in the
   middle. The two descriptions stand at different moments of the story and may be different tents.
   Neither text says. See [[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]].
3. **Reuel or Deuel.** Verse 14 has *ben-rəʿûʾēl* (WLC), and 1:14, 7:42, and 10:20 have Deuel. WEB
   follows each. The tension is already recorded on [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]; this is the verse that creates it. This
   Reuel is not Moses' father-in-law of Exodus 2:18 or Esau's son.
4. **The figures.** Verse 32 repeats 603,550, and the wiki still adopts no answer to the size of the
   army. Four camps of that size also raise a practical question, which the text does not address.
   See [[Six Hundred Three Thousand Five Hundred Fifty - Exodus 38.26 and Numbers 1.46]].

**Ahead.** Balaam will look down on this arrangement from a height (24:2), and Ezekiel and Revelation
take up three tribes to a side. See [[Three Gates to a Side - Numbers 2, Ezekiel 48, and Revelation 21]].
