# The Unclean Sent Out and Wrongs Repaid

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 5:1–10

## Summary

The camp has been counted and arranged around the Tent, and now it is cleared. Yahweh gives Moses two
short laws, and both repair something inside the camp.

The first is about uncleanness (1–4). Three kinds of people are to be sent outside: *every leper*,
*everyone who has an issue*, and *whoever is unclean by the dead* (2). Men and women are treated alike
(3). The reason is given in one clause: they must not defile the camp, *in the midst of which I dwell*
(3). The arrangement of chapter 2 had a theological point, and this law states it: the camp is where
Yahweh lives. Israel does what it is told at once (4).

The second is about wrongs against other people (5–10). A man or woman commits a sin of the kind people
commit, and it is counted as a trespass against Yahweh (6). The guilty person must confess, repay in
full, add a fifth, and give it to the one wronged (7). If that person has no kinsman to receive it, the
payment goes to Yahweh and so to the priest, along with the ram of atonement (8). Two general rules
about what belongs to the priest close the unit (9–10).

The two laws are not linked by the text, but they sit side by side. One deals with defilement that is
no one's fault, and the other with a fault that has a victim. Either can harm the people's life with
God, and each has a remedy that does not wait for the march to begin. That reading is the wiki's.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Numbers.md`. Complete unit:

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **2** “Command the children of Israel that they put out of the camp every leper, and everyone who has an issue, and whoever is unclean by the dead.

> **3** Both you shall put male and female outside of the camp; that they not defile their camp, in the midst of which I dwell.”

> **4** The children of Israel did so, and put them outside of the camp; as Yahweh spoke to Moses, so did the children of Israel.

> **5** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **6** “Speak to the children of Israel: ‘When a man or woman commits any sin that men commit, so as to trespass against Yahweh, and that soul is guilty;

> **7** then he shall confess his sin which he has done, and he shall make restitution for his guilt in full, and add to it the fifth part of it, and give it to him in respect of whom he has been guilty.

> **8** But if the man has no kinsman to whom restitution may be made for the guilt, the restitution for guilt which is made to Yahweh shall be the priest’s; besides the ram of the atonement, by which atonement shall be made for him.

> **9** Every heave offering of all the holy things of the children of Israel, which they present to the priest, shall be his.

> **10** Every man’s holy things shall be his: whatever any man gives the priest, it shall be his.’”

## Notes

**Boundary.** The plan's break holds. Verse 11 begins a new speech on a new subject, the suspected
wife. The word that connects them is noted below.

**The list is wider than Leviticus.** Leviticus 13:46 puts only the leper outside the camp. Leviticus
15 regulates discharges without mentioning the camp at all. Here all three are sent out. The difference
is stated on [[Who Is Put Out of the Camp - Numbers 5 and the Purity Laws]] and is not resolved.

**The same three.** Leviticus 22:4 keeps a priest from the holy things if he is a leper, has an issue,
or touches what is unclean by the dead. By Strong's tags (WLC), those three terms occur together in the
Pentateuch only there and here. Leviticus 22 applies the three to the priest's table, and Numbers 5
applies them to the whole camp. The comparison is the wiki's.

**Unclean by the dead.** The Hebrew is *ṭāmēʾ lānāpeš* (2, WLC), literally *unclean for a soul*. *Nepeš*
is the word WEB elsewhere renders *soul*, and here it stands for a dead person, as it does at Numbers
9:6–7, 10. WEB's rendering gives the sense.

**Their camps.** *Maḥănêhem* (3, WLC) is plural in the Hebrew, while WEB has *their camp*. The plural may
look back to the four camps of chapter 2, but the verse does not say so. *Hammaḥăneh* in verses 2 and 4
is singular.

**Sent out.** The verb in all three verses is *šillaḥ* (2–4, WLC), the Piel of *šālaḥ*, the same form as
Genesis 3:23, *Yahweh God sent him out from the garden*. The verb is common, so the echo is slight, and
the link is the wiki's. The reason is also different: Genesis gives a fault, and here no fault is named.

**A dwelling with a condition.** *Šōkēn bətôkām* (3, WLC) uses the verb of Exodus 25:8 and 29:45, the
promise that Yahweh will dwell among Israel. Numbers 35:34 joins the same verb to the same fear of
defilement, applied to the land: *You shall not defile the land which you inhabit, in the middle of
which I dwell*. See [[The Dwelling of God]].

**Sin of a man.** *Ḥaṭṭōʾt hāʾādām* (6, WLC) could mean a sin that people commit, as WEB takes it, or
a sin done against a person. WEB's reading stands here. Verse 7's recipient shows that a person has
been wronged in either case.

**Trespass.** WEB's *trespass* renders *limʿōl maʿal* (6, WLC). The same pair of words is used for the
holy things (Leviticus 5:15) and for fraud (Leviticus 6:2; 5:21 in the Hebrew numbering). It returns in
verses 12 and 27 of this chapter for the wife who is unfaithful, which is the verbal link between the
two units. A wrong against a neighbor is treated as a breach of faith with Yahweh, as at Leviticus 6:2.

**Guilt as a payment.** In verses 7–8 the noun *ʾāšām* names the thing paid back, and verse 8 calls it
*the ʾāšām that is returned*. WEB renders this *restitution for his guilt*. In Leviticus the same noun
usually names the ram, as at 5:15 and 6:6. The two are kept apart here because verse 8 places the ram
*besides* the payment.

**The kinsman.** The *gōʾēl* (8) is the relative of Leviticus 25:25 who buys back a brother's land. Here
he is the person entitled to receive a payment in the injured party's place. The verse says only that
the man has no kinsman; that he has died is an inference, though a natural one, since otherwise he could
receive it himself.

**The ram of atonement.** *ʾÊl hakkippurîm* (8, WLC) occurs only here by Strong's tags. *Kippurîm* is
the plural noun of the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 23:27) and of the ordination offering (Exodus 29:36).

**Whose are the holy things?** *Every man's holy things shall be his* (10) can be read two ways. The
*his* may be the giver, whose holy things remain his until he gives them, or the priest, as verse 9
says. The second half, *whatever any man gives the priest, it shall be his*, is clear either way. The
wiki leaves the first half open.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Who leaves the camp?** Leviticus 13:46 sends the leper out, and Leviticus 15 does not send anyone
   out. Taken at its widest, *everyone who has an issue* would include a woman's monthly period, which
   Leviticus 15:19–24 handles by contact and waiting. The text does not say whether it does. See
   [[Who Is Put Out of the Camp - Numbers 5 and the Purity Laws]].
2. **Dwelling among uncleanness.** Here the unclean go out so that the camp Yahweh lives in is not
   defiled (3). Leviticus 16:16 has the Tent of Meeting dwelling *with them in the middle of their
   uncleanness*. The two are not reconciled. Numbers 5 removes persons, and Leviticus 16 deals with
   what remains.
3. **Confession.** Numbers 5:7 requires it, and Leviticus 6:1–7, on the same kind of wrong, does not
   mention it. See [[Confess, Repay, and Add a Fifth - Leviticus 6 and Numbers 5]].
4. **The rates.** Exodus 22 sets multiples for theft, and this law sets a fifth. The earlier page records
   the gap; see [[Restitution to the Neighbor and for Holy Things]].
5. **Numbers 9.** Men *unclean because of the dead body of a man* come before Moses and Aaron at 9:6.
   The text does not say where they were living.
