# Balaam's First Two Oracles

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 22:36–23:26

## Summary

Balak receives Balaam at the Arnon border and challenges his delay. Balaam insists that only
God’s supplied word can be spoken. They go to Kiriath Huzoth, where Balak sacrifices and sends
portions; the next morning he takes Balaam to the high places of Baal (22:36–41).

After seven altars and paired bulls and rams, God meets Balaam and gives the first oracle.
Balaam cannot curse whom God has not cursed. Israel is distinct, as uncountable as dust, and
Balaam wishes to share the righteous people’s end. Balak protests the blessing (23:1–12).

A second viewpoint at Zophim on Pisgah and another seven altars produce a stronger assurance:
God’s word stands, the blessing cannot be reversed, Yahweh is with Israel, and the exodus people
rise like a lion. Balak asks for silence instead. Balaam again insists that he must obey the
given word (23:13–26).

## Notes

### Blessing beside judgment

The WEB of 23:21 denies seen iniquity or perverseness in Israel, whereas Numbers 14 narrates
rebellion, pardon, and a wilderness death sentence in [[The People Refuse the Land]]. The
contradiction in a flat claim of national sinlessness must be explicit. A contextual reading
understands the oracle as protection against Balak’s indictment, but the verse does not spell
out a full reconciliation. Likewise 23:19’s denial of repentance remains beside Exodus 32:14’s
affirmation of it; the connection page treats both rather than suppressing one.

### Sight, sacrifice, and control

Both the first and second stations involve a partial view (22:41; 23:13). Changing the location
does not change the answer. Sevenfold preparations do not give the king control of the word,
and the text does not say that Balaam accepted payment. The shout of a king in 23:21 accompanies
Yahweh’s presence; it need not imply an otherwise unmentioned human king of Israel.

### Editorial scope and sources

The planned boundary is retained: the move to Peor at 23:27 belongs to the next unit.
Unnamed princes remain a collective group. Jacob/Israel refers to the nation; Zippor is named
only as Balak’s father. Named stations receive place pages without speculative archaeological
identifications; Aram’s broad regional designation is kept distinct from Aram Naharaim.
