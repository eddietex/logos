# The Offerings of the Twelve Princes

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 7:1–89

## Summary

The chapter goes back in time. It opens *on the day that Moses had finished setting up the tabernacle*
(1), which is a month before the census that opens the book (Exodus 40:17; Numbers 1:1). The twelve
tribal heads who have led the census and the camp now bring gifts, and they do it in two rounds.

The first gift is transport: six covered wagons and twelve oxen, one wagon for every two princes and one
ox for each (3). Nobody has commanded it. Yahweh then tells Moses to accept it for the service of the
Tent (5). Moses gives two wagons and four oxen to Gershon and four wagons and eight oxen to Merari. He
gives none to Kohath, *because the service of the sanctuary belonged to them; they carried it on their
shoulders* (9).

The second gift is for the dedication of the altar, and Yahweh sets the pace: *each prince on his day*
(11). Over twelve days, twelve men bring the same thing: a silver platter, a silver bowl, a gold ladle of
incense, and twenty-one animals. The text writes it out in full every time, six verses for each man and
72 verses in all (12–83). The only things that change are the day, the name, and the tribe. Verses
84–88 add up the total.

The last verse does not seem to belong to the list. Moses goes into the Tent to speak with Yahweh and
hears the voice *from above the mercy seat … from between the two cherubim* (89). Exodus 25:22 promised
exactly this, and this is the first time the Bible narrates it.

## Key verses

> **1** On the day that Moses had finished setting up the tabernacle, and had anointed it and sanctified it, with all its furniture, and the altar with all its vessels, and had anointed and sanctified them;

> **3** and they brought their offering before Yahweh, six covered wagons, and twelve oxen; a wagon for every two of the princes, and for each one an ox: and they presented them before the tabernacle.

> **5** “Accept these from them, that they may be used in doing the service of the Tent of Meeting; and you shall give them to the Levites, to every man according to his service.”

> **9** But to the sons of Kohath he gave none, because the service of the sanctuary belonged to them; they carried it on their shoulders.

> **11** Yahweh said to Moses, “They shall offer their offering, each prince on his day, for the dedication of the altar.”

> **12** He who offered his offering the first day was Nahshon the son of Amminadab, of the tribe of Judah,

> **13** and his offering was: one silver platter, the weight of which was one hundred thirty shekels, one silver bowl of seventy shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary; both of them full of fine flour mixed with oil for a meal offering;

> **14** one golden ladle of ten shekels, full of incense;

> **15** one young bull, one ram, one male lamb a year old, for a burnt offering;

> **16** one male goat for a sin offering;

> **17** and for the sacrifice of peace offerings, two head of cattle, five rams, five male goats, and five male lambs a year old. This was the offering of Nahshon the son of Amminadab.

> **84** This was the dedication of the altar, on the day when it was anointed, by the princes of Israel: twelve silver platters, twelve silver bowls, twelve golden ladles;

> **85** each silver platter weighing one hundred thirty shekels, and each bowl seventy; all the silver of the vessels two thousand four hundred shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary;

> **86** the twelve golden ladles, full of incense, weighing ten shekels apiece, after the shekel of the sanctuary; all the gold of the ladles weighed one hundred twenty shekels;

> **87** all the cattle for the burnt offering twelve bulls, the rams twelve, the male lambs a year old twelve, and their meal offering; and the male goats for a sin offering twelve;

> **88** and all the cattle for the sacrifice of peace offerings twenty-four bulls, the rams sixty, the male goats sixty, the male lambs a year old sixty. This was the dedication of the altar, after it was anointed.

> **89** When Moses went into the Tent of Meeting to speak with Yahweh, he heard his voice speaking to him from above the mercy seat that was on the ark of the Testimony, from between the two cherubim: and he spoke to him.

The order is the camp's, side by side around the Tent, and not the birth order or the census order of
1:5–15. So Reuben, the firstborn, gives fourth, and Judah gives first. The tribe list in the chapter
assumes the arrangement of [[The Camp Around the Tent]], although by the book's dates that arrangement
comes later. See the fourth tension below.

**The sums check.** Each man gives three burnt animals, one goat for sin, and seventeen for peace, 21
head. Twelve men give 252 head, which matches the eight figures of verses 87–88 (12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 24
+ 60 + 60 + 60). Twelve platters at 130 and twelve bowls at 70 give 2,400 shekels of silver (85), and
twelve ladles at 10 give 120 of gold (86). The peace offerings are by far the largest part, and the
meat of a peace offering is eaten (Leviticus 7:15). The chapter does not mention a meal, so any
feasting over the twelve days is an inference.

**Why write it out twelve times?** Verse 19 could have said *the same*. The text never does. Twelve
equal entries also mean that no tribe's gift is shorter than Judah's or summarized under it. That
reading is the wiki's, and the text gives no reason. Yahweh's only stated rule is *each prince on his
day* (11), which at least shows that the separate days were meant.

**Judah's man has no title.** Eleven entries call the man *prince*, either *prince of Issachar* (18) or
*prince of the children of* a tribe (24 and following). Nahshon alone is only *of the tribe of Judah*
(12, WEB; *ləmaṭṭēh yəhûdâ*, WLC). He is already counted among the *princes* at 2:3 and 7:2, so this is
not a denial of rank. His entry also has a different form, *He who offered*, *hammaqrîb*. It may be
only that the first entry in a list is set up differently from the rest. The text gives no reason, and
the wiki rests nothing on it.

**An uncommanded gift.** No word from Yahweh comes before the wagons. The princes bring them, and
Yahweh's *Accept these* (5) comes afterward. Exodus 35 is different, because there Moses asks for the
gifts first. The altar gifts come with a command about timing (11), though not about content. The
chapter does not say who chose the amounts, or how twelve men came to bring identical gifts.

**Wagons by load.** Merari has the frames, bars, pillars, and sockets (4:31–32), and Gershon the
curtains and coverings (4:25–26). Merari gets twice the wagons, and the text does not say the load
is heavier, though frames and sockets suggest it. Verse 5 and verses 7–8 use the same reason: *according to his service*, *according to their service*. All six wagons and twelve oxen
are used up, two oxen to a wagon. *Under the direction of Ithamar* is attached in verse 8 to Merari
alone, while 4:28 and 4:33 put both houses under him.

**No wagon for Kohath.** Verse 9 is a sentence of law inside a report of a gift: *baktēp yiśśāʾû*, *on
the shoulder they shall carry* (WLC). WEB's past tense *they carried it* is one way of taking the
Hebrew imperfect, and a future or customary sense is also open. The holiest load gets the least help,
and the text does not say why a cart is unfit. The poles of Exodus 25:13–15
and the order of 4:15 are the context. David's new cart is the later failure of this verse, and
Chronicles says so (1 Chronicles 15:13–15). See [[Lest They Die - The Ark Carried and the Ark Looked Into]].

**The word for wagon.** *ʿEglōt ṣāb* (3, WLC). *ʿĂgālâ* elsewhere is the Egyptian wagons sent for Jacob
(Genesis 45:19), the Philistines' new cart for the ark (1 Samuel 6:7), and David's (2 Samuel 6:3).
*Ṣāb* is rare: the tags give the same noun only at Isaiah 66:20, WEB's *litters*, a different word from
the *ṣāb* of Leviticus 11:29. WEB's *covered* for Numbers 7:3 is accepted here, and no lexicon was
checked for it.

**Dedication.** *Ḥănukkat hammizbēaḥ* (10, 11, 84, 88, WLC). The noun comes only three other places in
the Hebrew Bible: 2 Chronicles 7:9, Nehemiah 12:27, and the heading of Psalm 30. This chapter is its
first occurrence and holds four of the seven uses by verse. See
[[The Dedication of the Altar - Numbers 7, 2 Chronicles 7, and Ezra 6]].

**The vessels.** *Qəʿārâ*, WEB's *platter*, is a table vessel at Exodus 25:29 and Numbers 4:7. Outside
this chapter the only other tagged use is Exodus 37:16. *Mizrāq*, the *bowl*, is a vessel of the bronze
altar at Exodus 27:3. *Kap*, the *ladle*, shares its Strong's number with the word for the palm of the
hand, and it is one of the table's vessels at Exodus 25:29. WEB says *spoon* at verse 80 and *ladle*
everywhere else, but the Hebrew is the same at verses 14 and 80. English readers should not take verse 80
as a change in the gift.

**One day that lasts twelve.** Verse 1 says *on the day*, verse 10 *in the day that it was anointed*, and
verse 84 sums up twelve days of gifts as given *on the day when it was anointed*. So the chapter's own
sum shows that *bəyôm* here cannot be limited to a single daylight. *When* is the fair sense of it. This
matters for how far verse 1 can be pressed as a date, and it does not settle how the twelve days relate
to Leviticus 8–9.

**No priest at the altar.** The chapter is about the altar and lists burnt, sin, and peace offerings,
but nobody is said to burn them.
Aaron is named once, as Ithamar's father. The text may simply assume priests. Leviticus 1–7 has already
given them their role, but the silence is notable next to Leviticus 8–9, where each act is narrated.

**Verse 89.** The Hebrew is more spare than WEB's. *Ləḏabbēr ʾittô* is *to speak with him*; WEB supplies
*Yahweh*, and the last time the chapter named him was verse 11. *Hăqqôl*, *the voice*, has the article
where WEB has *his voice*. *Middabbēr*, *speaking*, is tagged in the WLC as a hitpael participle, and this
search found the verb tagged hitpael at only two other places, Ezekiel 2:2 and 43:6. In both, someone
hears a voice addressing them. No grammar was consulted, and the wiki does not press a sense from the
stem. The final clause, *and he spoke to him*, has no stated content. The chapter ends with a speech
it does not report. The next chapter opens *Yahweh spoke to Moses* (8:1), and whether 8:1 should be taken
as what was said is open.

**Only here.** *Kappōret*, *mercy seat*, is tagged in 22 verses. Twenty of them are in Exodus and
Leviticus and one in 1 Chronicles 28:11. Numbers 7:89 is the other, and it is the only one that shows Moses
receiving speech from it. See [[The Voice from Between the Cherubim - Exodus 25.22 and Numbers 7.89]].

**Why here?** The chapter does not link the gifts to the voice. A reading in which the altar is
furnished outside, and the voice then begins inside, is natural, but it is an inference. *When Moses went
in* could as well be a general statement of what happened whenever he did.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **The order of the book and the order of events.** Chapter 7 is earlier than chapter 1 by the dates
   it gives, and it uses things chapters 1–4 set up. See the fourth tension and
   [[On the Day Moses Finished - Exodus 40, Leviticus 8-9, and Numbers 1, 7, and 9]].
2. **Twelve days and the ordination.** If the gifts began when verse 1 says, they ran alongside the seven
   days of Leviticus 8:33, when Aaron and his sons could not leave the door, and the seven days of altar
   atonement (Exodus 29:37), and past the eighth day when fire came (Leviticus 9:24). The text does not
   say how these fit. No date is given for the first of the twelve days.
3. **A Sabbath in the twelve.** Twelve days in a row must include a Sabbath, and verses 12–83 count them
   by ordinals with no break. The text does not comment, and the laws on Sabbath offerings come later in
   the book (Numbers 28:9–10, not yet ingested).
4. **Who was counted, and when?** Verse 2 calls the princes *they who were over those who were counted*.
   If that is the census of 1:1–46, it had not happened yet by the book's dates. The earlier count at
   Exodus 38:25–26 would ease that, but the men are named only at Numbers 1:5–15. Likewise the wagons are
   given *according to their service* (7–8), which chapter 4 assigns. Either the description looks ahead
   from the time of writing, or the dates are not meant to be pressed. The text does not say which.
5. **Moses barred and Moses admitted.** At Exodus 40:35 Moses *wasn't able to enter into the Tent of
   Meeting*. Here he goes in. Leviticus 1:1 has Yahweh calling him from the Tent, with no statement that
   the bar was lifted.
