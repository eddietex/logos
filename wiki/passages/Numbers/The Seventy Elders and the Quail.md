# The Seventy Elders and the Quail

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 11:4–35

## Summary

Two stories are woven together here, and each one answers a complaint. The people want meat. Moses wants help. Yahweh
gives both, and the two gifts end in opposite ways.

The first complaint starts with *the mixed multitude*, whose craving spreads to the children of Israel (4). They weep,
remember Egypt's fish and vegetables, and say there is nothing to look at but manna (5–6). The narrator stops to describe
the manna, and the description is kind to it (7–9). Moses hears the weeping at every tent door. Yahweh's anger burns, and
the thing is evil in Moses' eyes (10).

Moses then makes the bitterest speech of his life (11–15). He asks why Yahweh has treated him badly, and whether he
conceived this people, that he should carry them like a nursing child. He cannot bear them alone, and if this is how it
will be, he asks Yahweh to kill him.

Yahweh answers both complaints (16–23). Moses is to gather seventy elders to the Tent, and Yahweh will take of the Spirit
on Moses and put it on them, so that they share the burden. The people are to sanctify themselves, and they will have
meat for a whole month, until it comes out at their nostrils, because they have rejected Yahweh who is among them. Moses
doubts the arithmetic, and Yahweh asks whether his hand has grown short.

The Spirit comes first (24–30). Yahweh comes down in the cloud, the Spirit rests on the seventy, and they prophesy once.
Two men left in the camp, Eldad and Medad, prophesy too. A young man runs to tell Moses, and Joshua says to stop them.
Moses answers, *I wish that all Yahweh's people were prophets*.

The meat comes last (31–35). A wind from Yahweh drives quail in from the sea and drops them around the camp. The people
gather for two days and a night, and the one who gathers least has ten homers. While the meat is still between their
teeth, Yahweh strikes them with a very great plague. The place is called Kibroth Hattaavah, *the graves of craving*, and
the people move on to Hazeroth.

The same word, *rûaḥ*, runs through both stories. It is the Spirit that rests on the elders (17, 25, 26, 29), and it is the
wind that brings the quail (31). One verse separates them, and the outcomes could not be further apart.

## Notes

**The mixed multitude.** *Hāʾsapsup* (4, WLC) occurs only here, by the tags. It is not the *ʿēreb* of Exodus 12:38, though
WEB gives both the same English. The word is often linked to *ʾāsap*, *gather*, which fits the sound of the chapter but is
recalled and not checked in a lexicon. Who these people are the verse does not say, beyond *in the midst of* Israel.
Rahlfs has *ὁ ἐπίμικτος*. The children of Israel are not excused: *gam*, *also*, puts them beside the others.

**Craving, doubled.** *Hitʾawwû taʾăwâ* (4, WLC) is verb and noun from one root, *they craved a craving*. WEB's *lusted
exceedingly* catches the force, though the Hebrew has no separate word for *exceedingly*. The noun is the *delight to the
eyes* of Genesis 3:6, and the verb is Deuteronomy 5:21's *Neither shall you desire*, where Exodus 20:17 has only *ḥāmad*
(WLC). The chapter opens and closes on this root (4, 34). Neither word means sin by itself, as Deuteronomy 12:20 and
14:26 use the verb for appetite that is allowed (tags; those verses not read closely here). The wrong here is shown in
what the craving says about Yahweh (20).

**Wept again.** *Wayyāšubû wayyibkû* (4, WLC) says they *wept again*, but no earlier weeping is reported on the road, and
Taberah's cry was a different verb. Rahlfs reads *καθίσαντες ἔκλαιον*, *sitting down they wept*. Weeping is the chapter's
word for the complaint: *bākâ* comes at 4, 10, 13, 18, and 20. No one has died.

**Free fish.** *Ḥinnām* (5, WLC) means *for nothing*, and it is a slave's memory turned upside down, since Israel in Egypt
was the labor that went unpaid. See [[Bricks Without Straw]]. Exodus 16:3 remembered meat pots and bread. This memory is
fish and five items, and four of the Hebrew plant words are found only here by the tags: cucumbers, melons, onions, and
garlic. *Ḥāṣîr*, WEB's *leeks*, is usually grass elsewhere (tags). Rahlfs has *τὰ πράσα*. The list is the most specific
food memory in the Torah's wilderness stories, and the wiki notes that the detail is what makes it persuasive.

**Dried up.** *Napšēnû yəbēšâ* (6, WLC) is literally *our throat* or *our life is dry*, which WEB gives as *we have lost our
appetite*. Rahlfs has *ἡ ψυχὴ ἡμῶν κατάξηρος*. The sense of *nepeš* as throat is recalled, not checked. Psalm 106:15 turns
the word around: he *sent leanness into their soul*, *bənapšām*.

**The narrator's answer.** Verses 7–9 interrupt the complaint and defend the food. *Gad*, *coriander*, occurs only here and
at Exodus 16:31, and *bədōlaḥ*, *bdellium*, only here and at Genesis 2:12, among the good things of the land around Eden
(tags). The people say there is nothing to look at, and the narrator says what it looks like. *ʿÊn*, *eye*, is the word
for *appearance* in verse 7, and the people's eyes are in verse 6. Exodus 16:31's taste is *wafers with honey*, and verse 8's
is *fresh oil*. [[Manna and Quail]] records the difference.

**Whose eyes.** The chapter counts by eyes. There are the people's eyes on the manna (6), the manna's *eye* (7), what was evil
in Moses' eyes (10), and favor in Yahweh's eyes (11, 15). *Bəʿênê mōšeh rāʿ* (10) does not say what displeased him, the
weeping or Yahweh's anger. His next word is *hărēʿōtā*, the same root, *you have done evil* (11), and *bərāʿātî*, *my
evil*, ends the speech (15). WEB's *displeased*, *treated badly*, and *wretchedness* hide that the root runs through. Later
Jewish tradition is reported to treat *my evil* here as a scribal softening of *your evil*. That is recalled, and it was not
checked in any edition.

**Moses' speech.** He has used *lāmâ hărēʿōtā* before, at Exodus 5:22, where it was for the people. Here it is for himself. The new element is the picture. *Hārâ* and *yālad* are
the verbs of a mother, *conceived* and *brought out*, and the question expects the answer *no*. The implied answer is that
Yahweh is the parent. Then the figure changes to *hāʾōmēn*, a masculine participle by the tags, a guardian who carries a
child, as at Esther 2:7 and 2 Kings 10:1, with the feminine form at Ruth 4:16. *ʾĀman* is the root of *believe* (see
[[Faith]]). Rahlfs has *τιθηνὸς*. Verse 14 repeats Exodus 18:18 almost word for word, and [[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]] sets out the table.
*Nāśāʾ*, *carry*, and *maśśāʾ*, *burden*, join the whole speech to the answer (11, 12, 14, 17).

**Kill me.** *Hārəgēnî nāʾ hārōg* (15, WLC) doubles the verb with the infinitive, and the plea *nāʾ* is courteous. This page
knows of no earlier request to God for death in the Torah, but no search was run for one. At Exodus 32:32 Moses offered to be blotted out for the people. Here he asks for it to get
away from them. Yahweh gives no rebuke. See [[Take My Life - Moses, Elijah, and Jonah]].

**Took of the Spirit.** *Wəʾāṣaltî min-hārûaḥ* (17, 25, WLC) is a rare verb. By the tags *ʾāṣal* occurs five times, here twice
and at Genesis 27:36, Ecclesiastes 2:10, and Ezekiel 42:6. WEB has *take*, and Rahlfs has *ἀφελῶ* and *παρείλατο*. *Min* is
partitive, *some of*. Nothing says Moses had less afterward, and nothing says he had the same. The text's interest is that
the source of the elders' gift is *the Spirit on you*, and that Moses is no longer the only one. Verse 17 uses the
definite *the Spirit*, and verse 29 *his Spirit*, so it is Yahweh's, on Moses. See [[The Spirit of God]].

**They prophesied.** *Wayyitnabbəʾû* (25, WLC) is, by the tags, the first use of the verb *nāḇāʾ* in the Hebrew Bible's order.
The noun *nāḇîʾ* has come only at Genesis 20:7 and Exodus 7:1, and verse 29 is its third use. The text does not say what
*prophesying* was here, whether speech or ecstatic behavior. No words are recorded. The action is visible or audible,
as a young man can report it (27), and that is all. See [[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].

**They did so no more.** *Wəlōʾ yāsāpû* (25, WLC) is plain: *they did not add*. Rahlfs agrees, *οὐκέτι προσέθεντο*. Some
ancient versions are reported to read the reverse, *did not cease*. That is recalled, and it was not checked. On the Hebrew,
the sign happened once. The burden-sharing of verse 17 is not said to stop, but it is also never shown happening.

**Written, and not gone out.** *Wəhēmmâ bakkətubîm* (26, WLC) says Eldad and Medad were *among those written*. No list has
been mentioned, and why they stayed is not given. Rahlfs has *τῶν καταγεγραμμένων*. If they are of the seventy, then the
number at the Tent was short (24–25 say seventy). If they are extra, then the seventy were full and two more had been listed.
The text does not settle it. Either way, the Spirit *rested* on them away from the Tent, by the same verb, *nûaḥ* (25, 26).

**Joshua and the word *forbid*.** *Kəlāʾēm* (28, WLC), *restrain them*, is the verb for the people held back from bringing
gifts at Exodus 36:6. Rahlfs has *κώλυσον αὐτούς*, the verb of Mark 9:38–39 (SBLGNT). *Mibbəḥurāw* (28, WLC) has two readings.
WEB gives *one of his chosen men*, and Rahlfs similarly has *ὁ ἐκλεκτὸς*, but morphhb's lemma 979 is the noun of Ecclesiastes
11:9 and 12:1, *youth*, so *from his youth* is also possible. *Məšārēt* is his title at Exodus 24:13 and 33:11.

**Are you jealous for my sake?** *Haməqannēʾ ʾattâ lî* (29, WLC) is the verb of Numbers 5:14 and of Phinehas at 25:11 and 13
(tags). Moses names the motive. Joshua's concern is for Moses' place, and Moses declines the protection. Then comes
*ûmî yittēn*, literally *who will give*, the Hebrew way of saying *I wish*. The people's question was *mî yaʾăkilēnû*, *who
will feed us* (4), and both are *who* questions. *Nātan*, *give*, runs through both stories as well (13, 18, 21, 25, 29).

**Wind.** *Wərûaḥ nāsaʿ* (31, WLC) gives the wind the verb of the march itself, *set out*, the same one as the people's in
verse 35 and throughout [[The March from Sinai]]. morphhb tags the noun as common in gender and the verb as masculine. Rahlfs has *πνεῦμα*, so the Spirit-and-wind pairing is kept in Greek. Psalm 78:26 names an east
wind and a south wind, and Numbers names no direction. Which sea is not said.

**Gathered.** *ʾĀsap* marks this chapter: Moses gathers the elders (16, 24), the fish of the sea could not be gathered (22),
Moses is *gathered* to the camp (30), and the people gather the quail (32). The elders are gathered for one thing, and the
quail for another.

**Two cubits.** *Kəʾammātayim ʿal-pənê hāʾāreṣ* (31, WLC) could give the depth of the fallen birds or the height they flew at,
and WEB's *above the surface of the earth* leaves that open. Rahlfs's *ὡσεὶ δίπηχυ ἀπὸ τῆς γῆς* is the same. The wiki does
not pick one.

**He who gathered least.** *Hammamʿît* (32, WLC) is the word of Exodus 16:17–18. There *he who gathered little had no lack*,
and everyone measured to an omer. Here the man with the least has ten homers. Using Exodus 16:36 and Ezekiel 45:11 together, a
homer is ten ephahs and an ephah ten omers, so ten homers is a thousand omers by volume. Rahlfs has *δέκα κόρους*. The comparison
of two different foods is rough, but the Exodus rule, *according to his eating*, is plainly gone.

**Meat between the teeth.** *Ṭerem yikkārēt* (33, WLC) is the hard phrase. WEB has *before it was chewed*. The verb is *kārat*,
usually *cut off*, and Rahlfs has *πρὶν ἢ ἐκλείπειν*, *before it ran out*. On WEB's reading the plague comes at the first
bite, which sits awkwardly with the month of verse 20. On the Greek reading it comes before the supply ended. See tension 3.

**The name.** *Qibrôt hattaʾăwâ* (34, WLC) has the same unnamed active verb as Taberah. The name uses the chapter's first noun,
and the last word of the verse is *hammitʾawwîm*, *those who craved*. Rahlfs translates the place, *μνήματα τῆς ἐπιθυμίας*,
and calls the dead *τὸν ἐπιθυμητήν*. That is the word family of 1 Corinthians 10:6 (SBLGNT). Who died, and how many, are not
stated, and verse 34 could be read as all who craved or only some.

**Same season, one year on.** Exodus 16:1 sets the first quail on the fifteenth of the second month, and Numbers 10:11 has the
march begin on the twentieth of the second month a year later. So the two quail stories fall about a year apart, at about
the same season by the dates given. Nothing in the text remarks on it. How long after 10:11 this unit falls is not stated,
and it is the wiki that sets the two dates side by side.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Where the Tent is.** Numbers 2:2, 17 places the Tent in the middle of the camp. Here Eldad and Medad *had not gone out to the Tent* and stayed *in the camp* (26), and
   Moses *went into the camp* (30). Verse 24's *went out* may point the same way, or he may only be leaving the Tent. That is
   the picture of Exodus 33:7, outside the camp, and it is not the picture of Numbers 2. See [[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]]. The wiki keeps both.
2. **The cloud.** Numbers 9:15–23 has the cloud resting over the Tent and rising to move the camp. Here Yahweh *came down in
   the cloud* (25), like Exodus 33:9. The two may be different movements of one cloud. The text does not explain them.
3. **A month, or the first bite.** Yahweh promises a whole month of meat (19–20), and verse 33 has the plague while the meat
   is between their teeth. Depending on *yikkārēt*, these are in tension or are not. Verse 33 also says nothing of how long
   the plague lasted.
4. **Moses unpunished.** Moses doubts the supply here (21–22) and is only answered with a question (23). At Numbers 20:12
   unbelief keeps him out of the land. The difference is not explained here, and this page does not try to.
5. **Moses' earlier burden.** Exodus 18 and Deuteronomy 1 tell of help for Moses differently, as
   [[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]] records.
6. **Two kinds of memory.** Psalm 105:40 keeps the quail as a gift, and Psalm 106:14–15 as a judgment, and neither says
   which quail it means by name. See [[Gift and Judgment - The Two Quail Stories of Exodus 16 and Numbers 11]].
7. **Paran.** Neither of this chapter's two places is in Paran by name. See [[Paran]].
