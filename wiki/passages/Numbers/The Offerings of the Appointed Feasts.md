# The Offerings of the Appointed Feasts

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 28:16–29:40

## Summary

The calendar begun in 28:1–15 continues from the recurring days to the annual feasts. Each feast is
given its own burnt offering, meal offering, and sin-offering goat, and each is stated as an addition
to the daily lambs.

**Passover and Unleavened Bread** (28:16–25): the fourteenth day of the first month *is Yahweh's
Passover*; a seven-day feast of unleavened bread follows from the fifteenth. The first and seventh
days are holy convocations without regular work. On each of the seven days, two young bulls, one ram,
and seven male lambs a year old are offered, with meal offerings at three tenths, two tenths, and one
tenth of an ephah, and one male goat *for a sin offering, to make atonement for you*.

**The day of first fruits, in the feast of weeks** (28:26–31): a holy convocation when a new meal
offering is brought; the same set of two bulls, one ram, seven lambs, and a goat.

**The first day of the seventh month** (29:1–6): a holy convocation and *a day of blowing of
trumpets*; one bull, one ram, seven lambs, and a goat, on top of both the new-moon offering and the
continual offering.

**The tenth day of the seventh month** (29:7–11): a holy convocation on which Israel is to *afflict
your souls* and do no work of any kind; one bull, one ram, seven lambs, and a goat, on top of *the sin
offering of atonement* and the continual offering.

**The fifteenth day of the seventh month** (29:12–34): seven days of feast. The bulls begin at
thirteen and fall by one each day to seven; every day has two rams, fourteen lambs, and a goat. From
the second day on the meal and drink offerings are given only as *according to their number, after
the ordinance*.

**The eighth day** (29:35–38): a solemn assembly without regular work; one bull, one ram, seven lambs,
and a goat.

A closing line sets all of this *besides your vows, and your freewill offerings* (29:39), and Moses
tells Israel everything Yahweh commanded (29:40).

## Notes

**What the unit adds to Leviticus 23.** Leviticus 23 gave the dates, the convocations, and the rest
from work, and for most days required only *an offering made by fire to Yahweh* (23:8, 25, 27, 36)
without saying what it was. Numbers 28:16–29:38 supplies the animals for every one of those days.
Where Leviticus did number an offering — at the first fruits of 23:18–19 — the numbers differ (see
below). Numbers does not cite Leviticus or explain the relation; the two read as complementary in most
places, and in one they disagree. See
[[The Feast Offerings Numbered - Leviticus 23, Numbers 28-29, Ezra 3, and Ezekiel 45]].

**The offerings at a glance.** Every set below is *besides the continual burnt offering*:

| Day | Bulls | Rams | Lambs | Goat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unleavened Bread, each of 7 days (28:19–22) | 2 | 1 | 7 | 1 |
| First fruits, feast of weeks (28:27–30) | 2 | 1 | 7 | 1 |
| Seventh month, day 1 (29:2–5) | 1 | 1 | 7 | 1 |
| Seventh month, day 10 (29:8–11) | 1 | 1 | 7 | 1 |
| Seventh month, days 15–21 (29:13–34) | 13 → 7 | 2 | 14 | 1 |
| Eighth day (29:36–38) | 1 | 1 | 7 | 1 |

The opening feasts repeat the new-moon set of 28:11–15. The three seventh-month days other than the
week of tents drop to one bull. The tents week doubles the rams and lambs and multiplies the bulls;
the eighth day returns to the single-bull set.

**No offering for the Passover itself.** Verse 16 names the fourteenth day as *Yahweh's Passover*
(Hebrew *pesaḥ laYHWH*, Miqra according to the Masorah via Sefaria) and then moves straight to the
fifteenth. No public offering is listed for the fourteenth. The Passover animal of Exodus 12 is a
household's lamb, not one of the calendar's public offerings, and this unit does not mention it. The
offerings begin with the feast of unleavened bread.

**A sin offering at every feast.** Each feast carries one male goat. At Unleavened Bread and the first
fruits it is *to make atonement for you* (28:22, 30); at the first of the seventh month the same
phrase appears (29:5); on the tenth day the goat is *besides the sin offering of atonement* (29:11) —
the goats of [[The Day of Atonement]], which this unit assumes and does not restate. From the fifteenth
of the seventh month onward the goat is called a sin offering without the atonement phrase (29:16,
19, 22, 25, 28, 31, 34, 38). The text does not name a sin that any of these goats addresses.

**The feast of weeks without a date.** Verse 26 calls the occasion *the day of the first fruits, when
you offer a new meal offering … in your feast of weeks* (Hebrew *bəšābuʿōtêkem*, *in your weeks*, MAM
via Sefaria). It gives no date and no count. The count of seven weeks to the fiftieth day is in
Leviticus 23:15–16; Numbers assumes it.

**A contradiction with Leviticus 23 on the first fruits.** Leviticus 23:18 requires, *with the bread,
seven lambs without defect a year old, one young bull, and two rams*, with a goat for a sin offering
and two lambs for peace offerings (23:19). Numbers 28:27 requires *two young bulls, one ram, seven male
lambs a year old*, with a goat (28:30) and no peace offering. The bull and ram numbers are reversed.
Both readings come from stored WEB, and the Hebrew of each verse has the same numbers (Leviticus 23:18
*par … ʾeḥād wəʾêlim šənāyim*; Numbers 28:27 *pārîm … šənayim ʾayil ʾeḥād*; MAM via Sefaria). Neither text says the two
sets are separate offerings to be brought on the same day, or that one replaces the other. The wiki
records both and does not harmonize them.

**The seventh month.** Three of the six occasions fall in one month. The first day is *yôm tərûʿâ*
(29:1, MAM via Sefaria), *a day of blowing* or shouting. No instrument is named; Leviticus 23:24 has
*zikrôn tərûʿâ*, *a memorial of blowing*, also without an instrument. WEB's *trumpets* supplies one in
both. See [[Trumpet and Ram's Horn - Two Instruments, One English Word]]. Because the day is also a new
moon, 29:6 adds its offering on top of the new-moon set as well as the continual one — the clearest
place in the calendar where three layers stack on one day.

**Two kinds of rest.** Most of the days forbid *kol-məleʾket ʿăbōdâ*, *all work of service*, which WEB
renders *regular work* (28:18, 25, 26; 29:1, 12, 35). The tenth of the seventh month alone forbids
*kol-məlāʾkâ*, *all work* (29:7), which WEB renders *no kind of work*. The Hebrew forms were checked in
MAM via Sefaria for 29:1, 7, 35. The same distinction is in Leviticus 23: *regular work* at 23:7–8, 21,
25, 35–36, and *no kind of work* at 23:28, 30–31 (stored WEB; *kol-məlāʾkâ* at 23:28, MAM via Sefaria),
where the Day of Atonement's rest matches the Sabbath's at 23:3.

**Afflict your souls.** Verse 29:7 has *wəʿinnîtem ʾet-napšōtêkem* (MAM via Sefaria); Leviticus 16:29
has the same verb and object in another form, *təʿannû ʾet-napšōtêkem* (MAM via Sefaria), and WEB
renders both *afflict your souls*. Numbers, like Leviticus, does not say what the affliction consists
of. The wiki does not supply a practice the text does not name.

**Seventy bulls.** The bulls of the seven days are thirteen, twelve, eleven, ten, nine, eight, seven:
seventy in all, beside fourteen rams, ninety-eight lambs, and seven goats. The series ends on seven on
the seventh day. Numbers does not comment on either number. In the Babylonian Talmud, Sukkah 55b, Rabbi
Elazar says the seventy bulls *correspond to the seventy nations of the world* and that the single bull
of the eighth day corresponds to Israel (William Davidson English translation via Sefaria, checked
2026-11-05). That is a later reading, not a statement of Numbers. See
[[Seventy Nations - Genesis 10 in Deuteronomy 32 and Luke 10]].

**The eighth day.** Verse 35 calls it *ʿăṣeret* (MAM via Sefaria), *a solemn assembly*, the word of
Leviticus 23:36. Its offerings match the first and tenth of the month rather than the week before. The
text sets it apart from the seven days without explaining why.

**After the ordinance.** From 29:18 the meal and drink offerings are given only as *bəmispārām
kammišpāṭ* (MAM via Sefaria), *according to their number, after the ordinance*. This assumes the
scale of Numbers 15:4–10 and 28:12–14, by which each bull, ram, and lamb carries a fixed measure.
The drink offerings are not given measures anywhere in this unit. See
[[Measures of Flour, Oil, and Wine - Exodus 29, Numbers 15, Numbers 28, and Ezekiel 46]].

**Public offerings and private ones.** Verse 39 ends the calendar by setting it *besides your vows, and
your freewill offerings*: the calendar is the community's fixed worship, and individual offerings come
on top of it. Leviticus 23:37–38 closes the same way. The next unit, [[Vows of Men and Women]], is about vows.

**Later keeping.** Ezra 3:4 says the returned exiles *kept the feast of tents, as it is written, and
offered the daily burnt offerings by number, according to the ordinance, as the duty of every day
required* — the closest echo in later Scripture of the daily numbers in 29:12–34. Nehemiah 8:18 records
the seven days of the same feast *and on the eighth day was a solemn assembly, according to the
ordinance*. 2 Chronicles 8:13 has Solomon offering *according to the commandment of Moses, on the
Sabbaths, on the new moons, and on the set feasts*. John 7:37 places Jesus' call *If anyone is thirsty*
*on the last and greatest day of the feast* — the feast of tents (John 7:2, not checked here); the text
does not say whether the seventh or eighth day is meant.

**Ezekiel's different numbers.** Ezekiel 45:21–25 also prescribes the Passover week and the
seventh-month feast, but has the prince provide *seven bulls and seven rams without defect daily the
seven days* and a goat, with an ephah of meal for each bull and ram — not the two bulls, one ram, and
seven lambs of Numbers 28, nor the descending bulls of Numbers 29. Ezekiel does not mention Numbers.
This is flagged as a discrepancy and not resolved; the parallel with Ezekiel 46 on the Sabbath and new
moon is in [[Sabbath and New Moon - Numbers 28, Ezekiel 46, Isaiah 66, and Colossians 2]].

**Verse numbering.** WEB numbers Moses' closing report as 29:40. Hebrew editions number the same
verse 30:1 (MAM via Sefaria), so Hebrew 30:2–17 corresponds to WEB 30:1–16. The plan's references
follow WEB.

**Boundary and naming.** The planned boundary, 28:16–29:40, is kept: the speech that began at 28:1
ends at 29:39, and 29:40 closes it. Ending with Moses' report keeps chapter 30's new speech (*Moses
spoke to the heads of the tribes*) separate. The provisional title is kept. No new person or place page
is created.
