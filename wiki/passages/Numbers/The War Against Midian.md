# The War Against Midian

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 31:1–54

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to *avenge the children of Israel for the Midianites*, and adds, *Afterward you
shall be gathered to your people* (2). Moses calls it *Yahweh's vengeance on Midian* (3) and sends a
thousand from every tribe, twelve thousand, with Phinehas the son of Eleazar the priest, *the vessels
of the sanctuary*, and *the trumpets for the alarm in his hand* (4–6).

The battle takes two verses. They kill every male, the five kings of Midian — Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur,
and Reba — and *Balaam the son of Beor with the sword* (7–8). They take the women and little ones
captive, take the livestock and goods, and burn the cities and encampments (9–11), then bring it all
to Moses, Eleazar, and the congregation at the plains of Moab (12).

The rest of the chapter is what happens after the battle, in four parts.

- **Moses' anger** (13–18). Moses meets the army outside the camp and is angry with the officers:
  *Have you saved all the women alive?* These, he says, caused Israel *through the counsel of Balaam*
  to trespass in the matter of Peor (15–16). He orders every male child killed and every woman who has
  lain with a man; the girls who have not are kept alive *for yourselves* (17–18).
- **Purification** (19–24). The soldiers and their captives stay outside the camp seven days and
  purify themselves on the third and seventh days, with their garments and everything of skin, goats'
  hair, and wood (19–20). Eleazar gives *the statute of the law*: metal goes through fire and is also
  purified *with the water for impurity*; whatever cannot bear fire goes through water (21–23). They wash
  their clothes on the seventh day and then come into the camp (24).
- **Dividing the plunder** (25–47). Yahweh orders the captured persons and animals counted and halved,
  between the fighters and the congregation (26–27). From the fighters' half, one in five hundred goes
  to Eleazar as *Yahweh's tribute*; from the congregation's half, one in fifty goes to the Levites
  (28–30). The totals follow: 675,000 sheep, 72,000 cattle, 61,000 donkeys, and 32,000 girls (32–35),
  each half and each tribute exactly computed (36–47).
- **The officers' gold** (48–54). The officers report that they have counted their men *and there
  lacks not one man of us* (49). They bring the gold jewellery each man found, *to make atonement for
  our souls before Yahweh* (50): 16,750 shekels (52). Moses and Eleazar bring it into the Tent of
  Meeting *for a memorial for the children of Israel before Yahweh* (54).

## Notes

**The command and its setting.** Numbers 25:17–18 had ordered Israel to *harass the Midianites, and
strike them*, *for they harassed you with their wiles … in the matter of Peor*. This chapter carries
that out, five chapters later, after the second census, the inheritance ruling, Joshua's commission,
the offering calendar, and the vow law. The command comes with a deadline: *Afterward you shall be
gathered to your people* (2), the phrase of 27:13. The war is the last campaign Moses directs.

**Whose vengeance.** Yahweh's words are *nəqōm niqmat bənê yiśrāʾēl mēʾēt hammidyānîm*, literally
*avenge the vengeance of the children of Israel from the Midianites* (2; Miqra according to the
Masorah, MAM). Moses, passing it on, calls it *niqmat-YHWH bəmidyān*, *Yahweh's vengeance on Midian*
(3; MAM). The same war is the vengeance of the people wronged at Peor and the vengeance of Yahweh.
The text does not choose between them. See [[Vengeance]].

**A thousand from each tribe, and a priest.** The army is small, equal by tribe, and drawn from the
counted men (4–5). Phinehas goes with it, not Eleazar, and *the vessels of the sanctuary and the
trumpets for the alarm* are *in his hand* (6). The text does not say what the vessels were. Rashi,
following earlier rabbinic sources he cites (Sifrei Bamidbar 157; Sotah 43a), takes them as the ark and
the high priest's golden plate; that is an interpretation, recorded here, not the text. The trumpets are
*ḥăṣōṣərôt hattərûʿâ* (MAM), the silver trumpets of Numbers 10:9, sounded *when you go to war*. Why
Phinehas goes is not said either; the reader knows him from Peor.

**Five kings and Balaam.** The battle report is spare: every male killed, five kings named, and then
Balaam (8). Numbers 24:25 had left him going *to his place*. The text does not say how he came to be
among Midian, or what the counsel of verse 16 was. Joshua 13:21–22 later names the same five as *the
chiefs of Midian … the princes of Sihon* and repeats that Israel killed *Balaam the son of Beor, the
soothsayer, with the sword* (WEB via bible-api.com). Numbers calls them kings; Joshua, chiefs and
Sihon's princes. The wiki records both titles without resolving them. One of the five, Zur, has the name
of Cozbi's father, *head of the people of a fathers' house in Midian* (25:15), called *the prince of
Midian* at 25:18. The wiki treats them as the same man, on the strength of the name and rank; the text
does not say so in as many words. See [[Zur]].

**Through the counsel of Balaam.** Moses' charge at 31:16 is *bidbar bilʿām*, *by the word of Balaam*
(MAM), the only statement in Numbers that Balaam had a part at Peor. Chapter 25 does not mention him,
and the wiki's page for it keeps him out of that scene. Here his role is stated, not narrated: the
women *caused the children of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass* (*limsor-maʿal*,
to act unfaithfully; MAM). The later readings — Deuteronomy 23:4, 2 Peter 2:15, Revelation 2:14 — build
on this verse. See [[The Counsel of Balaam - Numbers 31.16 and Its Later Readings]].

**Moses' anger, and the order that follows.** The officers have obeyed the command as stated: *they
killed every male* (7). Moses is angry that the women were spared (14–15), because at Peor it was women
who led Israel into trespass, and he orders the boys and every woman who has lain with a man killed
(17). The girls are kept *for yourselves* (18). The text reports this without comment, and it is among
the hardest commands in the Torah for a modern reader. The wiki states what the text says and does not
soften it. Three things the text supplies bear on it. The reason Moses gives is Peor and the plague,
not the war itself (16). The girls are counted, 32,000, and divided like the rest of the plunder (35,
40, 46), and thirty-two are given as *Yahweh's tribute* to Eleazar (40–41); the text does not say what
became of them. And Deuteronomy later regulates the taking of a captive woman as a wife: a month of
mourning for *her father and her mother*, and if the man has no delight in her, *you shall let her go
where she desires; but you shall not sell her at all for money* (Deuteronomy 21:10–14; now ingested
as [[Captives, Firstborn Sons, and Public Shame]]). Numbers 31 does not cite that law, and the wiki does not claim the
Deuteronomy law governed these captives.

**Purification after battle.** Killing and touching the slain make the soldier unclean, as touching a
corpse does in Numbers 19: *Whoever has killed any person, and whoever has touched any slain* purifies
on the third and the seventh day, *you and your captives* (19; *titḥaṭṭəʾû*, MAM). Victory does not
exempt the army from the law of the dead. Eleazar adds a rule for objects: what can withstand fire goes
through fire, *nevertheless it shall be purified with the water for impurity* (*bəmê niddâ*, 23; MAM),
the water of the red heifer (19:9). Metal is listed by kind: gold, silver, brass, iron, tin, and lead
(22). See [[The Red Heifer]] and [[Clean and Unclean]].

**Half to the fighters, half to the congregation.** Yahweh's division splits the plunder equally between
twelve thousand soldiers and everyone else (27). The soldiers' half pays one in five hundred to the
priest; the congregation's, one in fifty to the Levites (28–30) — the same ratio of ten to one as the
Levites' tithe of a tithe given to the priests at 18:26. That comparison is the wiki's, not the text's.
The levy is *mekes* (28, 37–41; MAM), a word found throughout the tribute lists of this chapter. The
numbers are exact: 675,000 sheep halve to 337,500, and one in five hundred of that is 675 (36–37); the
cattle, donkeys, and persons work out the same way. Verse 53 adds that *the men of war had taken booty,
every man for himself* — the personal plunder, apart from what was counted. See
[[Half to the Fighters - Numbers 31 and David's Statute at Ziklag]].

**Not one man missing.** The officers' report is *wəlōʾ-nipqad mimmennû ʾîš* (49; MAM): *not one man of us
is missing*, with *pāqad*, the verb of the census, *counted* or *missing* from the count. They bring gold
*ləkappēr ʿal-napšōtênû*, *to make atonement for our souls* (50; MAM), and it is put in the Tent *for a
memorial for the children of Israel before Yahweh* (54). Exodus 30:12–16 uses the same terms for the
half shekel paid at a census: *a ransom for his soul … that there be no plague among them when you count
them*, given *to make atonement for your souls* and appointed *for the service of the Tent of Meeting;
that it may be a memorial for the children of Israel before Yahweh* (stored WEB). Numbers 31 does not
cite Exodus 30, but the count, the atonement for souls, and the memorial in the Tent all match. The text
does not say what guilt the officers' gold atones for — the census, the killing, the plunder, or none
named — and the wiki leaves that open. See
[[The Half Shekel - Census, Ransom, and the Temple Tax]].

**No ḥērem.** The war is called vengeance, not *ḥērem*: no form of the root *ḥrm*, devote to
destruction, occurs in the Hebrew of Numbers 31 (MAM, searched by consonants). The plunder is kept,
divided, and tithed, unlike Jericho's (Joshua 6:17–19). Deuteronomy 20:13–14, in its law for distant
cities, allows males to be killed and *the women, the little ones, the livestock … all its plunder* to be
taken (now ingested as [[Laws for Going to War]]); Numbers 31 goes further in killing the boys and the women
who had lain with a man, and gives Peor as the reason. See
[[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]].

**Midian afterwards.** The chapter reads as the end of Midian: every male killed and the cities burned
(7, 10). Judges 6:1 has Yahweh deliver Israel *into the hand of Midian seven years* (WEB via
bible-api.com). The text of Numbers does not say every Midianite clan was involved; the wiki records the
later strength of Midian beside this chapter without harmonizing them. See [[Midian]].

**Boundary and naming.** The planned boundary, 31:1–54, is kept: chapter 32 turns to Reuben and Gad, in
[[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. The provisional title is kept. New person pages are created for four of the five kings — [[Evi]], [[Rekem]],
[[Reba]], and [[Hur (king of Midian)]], the last qualified because two Hur pages already exist. Zur's page
already existed.
