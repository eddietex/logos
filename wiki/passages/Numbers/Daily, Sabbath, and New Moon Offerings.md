# Daily, Sabbath, and New Moon Offerings

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 28:1–15

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to command Israel to take care to present his offering, *my food for my
offerings made by fire*, a pleasant aroma to him, each at its due season (1–2). The calendar of
public offerings follows, and this unit gives its first three layers.

**Every day** (3–8): two male lambs a year old without defect, one in the morning and one at
evening, as a continual burnt offering. Each lamb has a tenth of an ephah of fine flour mixed with
a fourth of a hin of beaten oil, and a drink offering of a fourth of a hin of strong drink poured to
Yahweh in the holy place. Verse 6 says this offering *was ordained in Mount Sinai*.

**Every Sabbath** (9–10): two more male lambs a year old without defect, with two tenths of an ephah
of fine flour mixed with oil and the drink offering, *besides the continual burnt offering*.

**Every new month** (11–15): a burnt offering of two young bulls, one ram, and seven male lambs a year
old without defect, with meal offerings on a fixed scale — three tenths of an ephah for each bull, two
tenths for the ram, one tenth for each lamb — and drink offerings of wine: half a hin for a bull, a
third for the ram, a fourth for a lamb. One male goat is offered as a sin offering to Yahweh. All of it
comes in addition to the continual burnt offering.

## Notes

**Why a calendar here.** The unit follows the second census and the commissioning of Joshua, and it
opens a two-chapter calendar (28:1–29:40) that the plan splits in two: this unit holds the recurring
offerings, and the next holds the annual feasts. Numbers does not say why the calendar is given at this
point. Coming after the count of the generation that will enter the land, it reads as the worship that
generation is to keep; that is an inference from placement, not a statement of the text.

**My offering, my food.** Verse 2 is in Yahweh's first person throughout. The Hebrew is
*qorbānî laḥmî ləʾiššay rêaḥ nîḥōḥî* — *my offering, my bread/food, for my fire-offerings, my pleasant
aroma* — and the time phrase is *bəmôʿădô*, *in its appointed time* (Miqra according to the Masorah,
via Sefaria). *Leḥem* is the ordinary word for bread or food. Leviticus had already called the altar
portions *the food of the offering made by fire* (3:11, 16) and the priests' offerings *the bread of
their God* (21:6, 8, 17). Psalm 50:12–13 later denies that God eats what is offered. Numbers makes no
comment on how the word is meant. See
[[The Bread of God - Leviticus 3 and 21, Numbers 28, Psalm 50, and Malachi 1]].

**The daily offering restated.** Verses 3–8 repeat the measures of Exodus 29:38–41: two lambs, a tenth
of an ephah of fine flour, a fourth of a hin of beaten oil, a fourth of a hin for the drink offering.
Numbers adds *without defect* (3) and the place of pouring, *in the holy place* (7). Verse 6 names its
source, *ordained in Mount Sinai*: the offering is not new, and this chapter treats it as the base on
which every other offering is laid. The evening lamb is offered *bên hāʿarbāyim*, *between the two
evenings* (4, 8), the phrase used at Exodus 29:39 for the same lamb and at Exodus 12:6 for the Passover
killing (MAM via Sefaria); WEB renders it *at evening* in all three.

**Strong drink and wine.** Verse 7 has *nesek šēkār*, *a drink offering of strong drink*; verse 14 has
*yayin*, wine (MAM via Sefaria). Exodus 29:40 has wine for the same daily lamb. The text does not
explain the different word, and the wiki does not decide whether *šēkār* here means a different liquid
or is used broadly.

**Besides the continual.** The refrain *besides the continual burnt offering* (10, 15; the next unit
repeats it) makes the calendar cumulative. The Sabbath does not replace the daily lambs; it adds two.
The new moon adds eleven animals and a goat. The phrase *every Sabbath* (10) is literally *the burnt
offering of a Sabbath in its Sabbath*, and *every month* (14) is *the burnt offering of a month in its
month* (*ʿōlat šabbat bəšabbattô*; *ʿōlat ḥōdeš bəḥodšô*; MAM via Sefaria).

**The first Sabbath offerings.** In the stored text of Exodus and Leviticus, the Sabbath is a day of
rest and a sign, and Leviticus 24:8 sets the bread of the presence in order every Sabbath, but no
Sabbath animal offering is numbered. Leviticus 23:38 mentions the Sabbaths alongside the feast offerings
without a figure. Numbers 28:9–10 is the first place in the wiki's sequence to set one: two lambs, the
same number as a single day's continual offering.

**The first new-moon offerings.** Numbers 10:10 had prescribed trumpets *in the beginnings of your
months* over the burnt offerings; this is the first text in the wiki's sequence to say what those
offerings are. The new-moon set — two bulls, one ram, seven lambs, one goat — is the same set the next
unit prescribes for each day of Unleavened Bread and for the Feast of Weeks (28:19–22, 27–30, stored
WEB). Later texts keep the Sabbath and new moon side by side: Ezekiel 46 prescribes different numbers
for the prince's offerings, Isaiah 66:23 pictures all flesh worshiping *from one new moon to another*,
and Colossians 2:16–17 calls the feast, new moon, and Sabbath *a shadow of the things to come*. See
[[Sabbath and New Moon - Numbers 28, Ezekiel 46, Isaiah 66, and Colossians 2]].

**A sin offering in the calendar.** The new month alone, in this unit, carries a sin offering: one male
goat *for a sin offering to Yahweh* (15; Hebrew *ləḥaṭṭāʾt laYHWH*, MAM via Sefaria). Verse 15 states no
sin it addresses and does not use the word *atonement*; the goats of the next unit do (28:22, 30). The
wiki does not supply a reason the text leaves out.

**The scale applied.** The meal and drink offerings of verses 12–14 follow the scale laid down in
Numbers 15:4–10 — three tenths, two tenths, one tenth of flour; a half, a third, a fourth of a hin of
wine. Verses 12–13 give no oil measure for the new-moon offerings, only *mixed with oil*. See
[[Measures of Flour, Oil, and Wine - Exodus 29, Numbers 15, Numbers 28, and Ezekiel 46]].

**Boundary and naming.** The planned boundary, 28:1–15, is kept: verse 16 begins the annual feasts with
the Passover. The provisional title is kept. No new person or place page is created; Mount Sinai is
covered by [[Horeb]].
