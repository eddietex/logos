# Vows of Men and Women

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 30:1–16

## Summary

Moses speaks to *the heads of the tribes* with *the thing which Yahweh has commanded* (1). The rule
for a man is one sentence: when he vows a vow to Yahweh or swears an oath to bind himself, *he shall
not break his word*; he does all that has gone out of his mouth (2).

The rest of the chapter is about a woman's vows, case by case.

- **A young woman in her father's house** (3–5). If her father hears her vow and *holds his peace*, it
  stands. If he forbids her *in the day that he hears*, it does not stand, and *Yahweh will forgive
  her, because her father has forbidden her*.
- **A woman who marries with vows already on her** (6–8). Her husband has the same choice on the day
  he hears: silence confirms; forbidding makes the vow *and the rash utterance of her lips* void, and
  Yahweh forgives her.
- **A widow or a divorced woman** (9). Everything she has bound herself to *shall stand against her*.
- **A wife who vows in her husband's house** (10–15). Silence confirms; voiding on the day he hears
  cancels, and Yahweh forgives her. Every vow and every binding oath *to afflict the soul* her husband
  may establish or make void (13). Silence *from day to day* establishes them (14). If he voids them
  later, after the day he heard, *he shall bear her iniquity* (15).

Verse 16 closes: *These are the statutes … between a man and his wife, between a father and his
daughter, being in her youth, in her father's house.*

## Notes

**Verse numbering.** The Hebrew chapter begins one verse earlier: WEB 29:40 is Hebrew 30:1, so WEB
30:1–16 is Hebrew 30:2–17 (Miqra according to the Masorah, MAM, via Sefaria). Verse numbers on this
page are the WEB's; Hebrew forms cited below are located by the WEB verse.

**Why here.** Numbers 29:39 closed the feast calendar *besides your vows, and your freewill offerings*:
the public offerings do not discharge a private vow. This chapter takes up vows next. The text does not
say why it comes at this point; following 29:39 is the visible link, and the wiki goes no further.

**To the heads of the tribes.** Most laws in Numbers go to *the children of Israel* or to Aaron. This one
is spoken to *rāʾšê hammaṭṭôt*, *the heads of the tribes* (1; MAM). The reason is not given. The law
concerns authority inside households, which may be why it goes to the tribes' heads; that is an inference,
and the wiki notes the address without explaining it.

**He shall not break his word.** The man's rule is *lōʾ yaḥēl dəbārô* (2; MAM). *Yaḥēl* is a form of the
root *ḥll*, the root of *profane* at Leviticus 19:12, *You shall not swear by my name falsely, and profane
the name of your God* (*wəḥillaltā*, MAM). A broken vow is, in this vocabulary, a word made common. The
chapter gives a man no way out: no father or husband stands over him, and the only rule for him is verse 2.
The wording *all that proceeds out of his mouth* (*kəkol-hayyōṣēʾ mippîw*, MAM) returns at Deuteronomy
23:23, *that which has gone out of your lips* (WEB, bible-api.com); see
[[Pay What You Vow - Deuteronomy 23.21-23, Proverbs 20.25, and Ecclesiastes 5.4-6]].

**The vocabulary of binding.** The chapter pairs *neder*, vow, with *ʾissār*, a bond, and the verb *ʾāsar*,
to bind, in *a bond with which she has bound her soul* (*ʾissār ʾăšer-ʾāsərâ ʿal-napšāh*, 4, 6, and
throughout; MAM). *ʾĀsar* is the ordinary verb for tying or imprisoning; here what is bound is the
*nepeš*, the self. The noun *ʾissār* recurs through the chapter; the wiki has not checked whether it occurs
anywhere else in the Hebrew Bible, a claim often made about it.

**Silence as consent.** The father's or husband's silence is a legal act. *Holds his peace* is
*heḥĕrîš* (4, 7, 11, 14; MAM), and verse 14 spells out the consequence: *he has established them,
because he held his peace*. The verbs of cancelling are *hēnîʾ*, to forbid or restrain (5, 8, 11), and
*hēpēr*, to make void (8, 12, 13, 15), the verb of Genesis 17:14, *He has broken my covenant*
(*ʾet-bərîtî hēpar*, MAM). The verb of
confirming is *hēqîm*, to make stand (13, 14), and the vows that are not cancelled *stand*, *yāqûm*
(4, 7, 11). Everything turns on one day: *in the day that he hears*. The law gives the man a window, not a
standing veto.

**Yahweh will forgive her.** Three times (5, 8, 12) the cancelled vow is followed by *Yahweh will forgive
her* (*wayHWH yislaḥ-lāh*, MAM), the verb *sālaḥ*. No offering is required. Leviticus 5:4–6 had required a
guilt offering, a lamb or goat, from anyone who *swears rashly with his lips* and later knows it. Here the
woman whose vow is cancelled brings nothing; the text says Yahweh forgives her, and verse 5 gives the reason,
*because her father has forbidden her*. The law does not say she sinned in vowing. *Forgive* may mean only
that she is released from what she said she would do; the text does not explain it, and the wiki leaves it
open. See [[Forgiveness]].

**He shall bear her iniquity.** A husband who says nothing on the day he hears and cancels later does not
make the vow void in Yahweh's sight; instead *wənāśāʾ ʾet-ʿăwōnāh*, *he shall bear her iniquity* (15; MAM).
The guilt of the broken vow passes to him. The phrase is the one Leviticus 5:1 uses of a silent witness, *he shall bear
his iniquity*: carrying guilt or its penalty.

**Two husband cases, not one.** Verses 6–8 begin *wəʾim-hāyô tihyeh ləʾîš* (MAM), literally *and if she
indeed becomes a man's*, with her vows *on her*: a woman who marries, or is betrothed, while carrying vows made
earlier. WEB renders it *If she has a husband, while her vows are on her*. Verses 10–12 then take a vow made *in
her husband's house*. The first case covers vows that pass from a father's authority to a husband's; the second,
vows made inside the marriage. The WEB's English is close to the first case and does not show the distinction
as sharply.

**The rash utterance of her lips.** Verses 6 and 8 add *mibṭāʾ śəpāteyhā* (MAM), *the rash utterance of her
lips*. The verb *biṭṭēʾ*, to speak rashly, is the one in Leviticus 5:4, *swears rashly with his lips*
(*ləbaṭṭēʾ biśpātayim*), and in Psalm 106:33, where Moses at Meribah *spoke rashly with his lips*
(*wayəbaṭṭēʾ biśpātāyw*; all MAM; Psalm 106:33 WEB via bible-api.com). See
[[Rash Words of the Lips - Leviticus 5.4, Numbers 30, and Psalm 106.33]].

**To afflict the soul.** Verse 13 names *every binding oath to afflict the soul*, *ləʿannōt nāpeš* (MAM): the
phrase of the Day of Atonement, *you shall afflict your souls* (Leviticus 16:29, *təʿannû ʾet-napšōtêkem*;
Numbers 29:7). Psalm 35:13 joins it to fasting: *I afflicted my soul with fasting* (WEB via bible-api.com;
*ʿinnêtî baṣṣôm napšî*, MAM). A vow of fasting or abstinence is the likely sense; Numbers does not define it, and
the wiki does not claim verse 13 restricts the husband's power to such vows.

**The widow and the divorced woman.** Verse 9 is the only case without a man. A woman no longer under a father or
husband answers for her vow herself: it *shall stand against her*. The law treats her as it treats the man of
verse 2. Elsewhere the widow is a figure of vulnerability; here the law simply treats her as answerable on her own.

**What the law does not say.** It does not say whether a woman may vow, only what happens after she does: verse 3
assumes she can. It does not say why a father or husband may cancel, beyond the household structure named in verse
16. It gives the woman no say over a man's vow. And it gives a man no release from his own: Jephthah, whose vow
fell on his daughter (Judges 11:30–31, 39), is a man vowing, under verse 2 and not verses 3–5. The wiki notes this
on [[A Person Vowed to Yahweh - Leviticus 27.1-8, Judges 11, and 1 Samuel 1]]; Judges is not yet ingested.

**Hannah.** The best-known wife's vow in the histories is Hannah's (1 Samuel 1:11). Her husband Elkanah later
says *only may Yahweh establish his word* (1:23), with *yāqēm*, the verb of confirming in verses 13–14 here (MAM).
1 Samuel does not cite this law. See [[May Yahweh Establish His Word - Numbers 30 and 1 Samuel 1]].

**Boundary and naming.** The planned boundary, 30:1–16, is kept: chapter 31 turns to the war against Midian. The
provisional title is kept. No new person or place page is created; the vowers are unnamed legal figures.
