# The Defeat at Hormah

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 14:39–45

## Summary

Moses tells the sentence to all Israel, and the people mourn greatly (39). In the morning they rise
early and go up to the top of the mountain, saying that they are here and will go up to the place
Yahweh spoke of, *for we have sinned* (40). The last thing they were told was to turn toward the Red
Sea tomorrow (14:25). They have heard the word *tomorrow* and gone the other way.

Moses asks why they are transgressing Yahweh's word. It will not succeed. He says not to go up,
because Yahweh is not among them; they will be struck before their enemies. The Amalekite and the
Canaanite are in front of them, and they will fall by the sword, because they have turned back from
following Yahweh and Yahweh will not be with them (41–43).

They go up anyway. The narrator puts one clause beside that: the ark of Yahweh's covenant and Moses
did not leave the camp (44). The Amalekite and the Canaanite who live in that hill country come down,
strike them, and beat them down as far as Hormah (45).

The unit is seven verses long and turns the whole of chapter 14 inside out. The day before, Yahweh
said go and the people said no. Today Yahweh says no and the people say go. Both times they answer
with the word that was not given. They say *we have sinned*, and the first thing they do after saying
it is the sin's mirror image. The text does not say whether the confession was sincere. It reports
only what they did next and what happened.

Verse 43 is the fear of 14:3 arriving. They refused the land so as not to *fall by the sword*, and
they fall by the sword in the attempt to take it back. Nothing is said about how many died, and no
further word comes from Yahweh. The chapter ends in the middle of a rout, and the next verse in the
book is law for a future in the land: [[Offerings in the Land and Sins of Ignorance]].

## Notes

**Going, in the wrong direction.** *Māḥār pənû ûsəʿû lākem hammidbār* (14:25, WLC), *tomorrow turn,
and journey into the wilderness*. Verse 40 opens *wayyaškimû babbōqer*. The text does not state that
this is that tomorrow, but the sequence reads so.

**Mourned.** *Wayyitʾabbəlû hāʿām məʾōd* (39, WLC). Exodus 33:4 has the same form of the same verb,
and the setting matches: Yahweh has just said *I will not go up among you* (33:3), and *the people
heard this evil news* and mourned. By the tags these are the second and third uses of the hithpael of
*ʾābal*, after Jacob for Joseph at Genesis 37:34. Exodus 33 then goes to the Tent with Moses, while
here they go to the mountain without him.

**The top of the mountain.** *Rōʾš hāhār* (40, 44, WLC). By the tags, the only other places in the
Torah with these two words together are Exodus 19:20 and 34:2, the top of Sinai. There Moses went up
when called and the people were forbidden to. Here the people go up when forbidden, and Moses stays
down. Also, at Exodus 17:9 Moses said he would stand on *the top of the hill*, with *gibʿâ* for the noun, while Joshua
fought Amalek below. These comparisons are the wiki's. *Hāhār* is the hill country of 13:17 and
13:29, and Rahlfs has *τὴν κορυφὴν τοῦ ὄρους*.

**We are here.** *Hinnennû wəʿālînû* (40, WLC). Rahlfs has *ἰδοὺ οἵδε ἡμεῖς ἀναβησόμεθα*. *Hinnennû*
is the plural of *hinnēnî*, the *here I am* of Genesis 22:1 and Exodus 3:4, but those answer a call,
and no one has called. That reading is the wiki's. Note that the verse has them already going up (40)
before Moses speaks (41), and going up again after he speaks (44). The second may be the same
ascent resumed; the text does not explain.

**The place Yahweh said.** *ʾEl-hammāqôm ʾăšer ʾāmar YHWH* (40, WLC). By the tags this sequence
occurs only here and at Numbers 10:29, where Moses invites Hobab to journey *to the place of which
Yahweh said, ‘I will give it to you’*. WEB's *promised* here is an interpretation of *said*. The
people quote Moses' own earlier sentence but stop before *I will give it*.

**We have sinned.** *Kî ḥāṭāʾnû* (40, WLC). It is what Aaron said at 12:11 and what Israel will say
at 21:7, where it is followed by Moses' prayer and an answer. Here *kî* attaches the confession to
the plan. Rahlfs has *ὅτι ἡμάρτομεν*. Read one way it says *we will go up, because we have sinned*,
which offers the ascent as repair. That is an inference from the syntax, not something the text
states. Deuteronomy 1:41 makes it explicit in its own way: *according to all that Yahweh our God
commanded us*.

**Transgressing the mouth.** *ʿŌbərîm ʾet-pî YHWH* (41, WLC), literally *crossing the mouth of
Yahweh*. WEB has *disobey the commandment*. By the tags, the phrase with this verb occurs in four
verses, here, 22:18, 24:13, and 1 Samuel 15:24. Rahlfs has *παραβαίνετε τὸ ῥῆμα κυρίου*. The word is
the one Joshua and Caleb used of the land, *ʿābarnû bāh* (14:7). At 9:18–23 the same *pî YHWH* was
the people's rule of march. See
[[Transgressing the Mouth of Yahweh - Numbers 14 and 1 Samuel 15]].

**It will not succeed.** *Wəhiwʾ lōʾ tiṣlāḥ* (41, WLC). The verb until now is the hiphil used of the
servant's errand (Genesis 24) and of Joseph (Genesis 39), where Yahweh makes the thing prosper. By the
tags, this is the only qal in the Torah. Rahlfs has *οὐκ εὔοδα ἔσται ὑμῖν*.

**Not among you.** *Kî ʾên YHWH bəqirbəkem* (42, WLC). Moses told Yahweh at 14:14 that the nations
had heard *that you Yahweh are among this people*, *bəqereb hāʿām hazzeh*. Numbers 11:20 called him
*Yahweh who is among you*. The word closes the unit too: the ark did not leave *miqqereb hammaḥăneh*
(44). So he is in the midst of the camp, not of the people who have left it. Rahlfs has *μεθ᾿ ὑμῶν*
at verse 42. See
[[Is Yahweh Among Us - Exodus 17, Numbers 14, 1 Samuel 4, and Micah 3]].

**Struck before your enemies.** *Wəlōʾ tinnāgəpû lipnê ʾōyəbêkem* (42, WLC). It is the covenant curse
of Leviticus 26:17 in the same wording, already. The noun *maggēpâ* killed the ten at 14:37. See
[[Struck Before Your Enemies - Leviticus 26, Numbers 14, Deuteronomy 28, and 1 Kings 8]].

**You will fall by the sword.** *Ûnəpaltem beḥāreb* (43, WLC) is the phrase of 14:3 turned from a
complaint into a forecast. Rahlfs uses *πεσεῖσθε* twice, at verses 42 and 43, rendering *nāgap* the
first time.

**Turned back from after.** *Šabtem mēʾaḥărê YHWH* (43, WLC). This stands against *wayəmallēʾ
ʾaḥăray*, Caleb's *filled after me*, at 14:24: following is measured by the same preposition. The
verb *šûb* is also what they proposed at 14:3–4 and the verb of the spies' return at 13:25. Rahlfs has
*ἀπεστράφητε ἀπειθοῦντες κυρίῳ*. *Kî-ʿal-kēn*, *because therefore*, joins the clauses. Numbers 32:15
and 1 Samuel 15:11 use the same verb and preposition, the second of Saul.

**They presumed.** *Wayyaʿpilû laʿălôt* (44, WLC). By the tags this hiphil, listed as 6075 b, occurs
only here. Deuteronomy 1:43, telling the same event, uses *wattāzīdû*, a different verb, and the two
witnesses agree in sense. The meaning here depends on context, and any derivation from *ʿōpel*,
*mound*, is recalled and was not checked in a lexicon. Rahlfs has *διαβιασάμενοι*, *forcing their way*.
See [[Puffed Up - Aphal in Numbers 14.44 and Habakkuk 2.4]].

**The ark and Moses.** *Waʾărôn bərît-YHWH ûMōšeh lōʾ-māšû miqqereb hammaḥăneh* (44, WLC). The full
title occurs in Numbers only here and at 10:33, where the ark went ahead three days' journey. The two
staying things are named together under one plural verb, so the narrator treats Moses and the ark as
one fact. *Mûš* is the verb of the pillar that *didn't depart* (Exodus 13:22) and of Joshua at the
Tent (33:11). Rahlfs has *οὐκ ἐκινήθησαν*. The narrator does not say whether anyone proposed to bring
the ark. Compare 1 Samuel 4:3–11, where it is brought and is lost.

**Beat them down.** *Wayyakkûm wayyakkətûm* (45, WLC). The second verb is *kātat*, *to crush*. By
the tags its hiphil occurs only here and at Deuteronomy 1:44. The same verb is used of the calf ground
to powder at Deuteronomy 9:21 and of swords beaten into plowshares at Isaiah 2:4 and Micah 4:3. The
word sounds like the one before it, and the English does not keep that. *Wayyēred* gives the Amalekite
the same direction as the people: they went up and he came down.

**Hormah.** *ʿAd-haḥormâ* (45, WLC), with the article, which by the tags this name has nowhere else.
Rahlfs has *ἕως Ερμαν* and adds *καὶ ἀπεστράφησαν εἰς τὴν παρεμβολήν*, *and they returned to the
camp*, which is not in WLC. The name is the root of *ḥērem*, and is explained only at 21:3. See
[[Even to Hormah - Numbers 14, Numbers 21, Deuteronomy 1, and Judges 1]] and [[Hormah]].

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Who gave the warning.** Here Moses speaks with no reported word from Yahweh. Deuteronomy 1:42
   has Yahweh tell Moses to say it, in the first person: *I am not among you*.
2. **Who struck them.** The Amalekite and the Canaanite here (43, 45), the Amorites at Deuteronomy
   1:44. Numbers 13:29 put the Amorite in the hill country and the other two elsewhere.
3. **Where the enemy lived.** Verse 25 says *in the valley*, and verse 45 says *in that mountain*,
   twenty verses apart. The Amalekite was in the Negev at 13:29.
4. **Hormah's name.** It is used here before the event that names it at Numbers 21:3. Judges 1:17
   names it again, for a different victory, from Zephath.
5. **The way they fled.** Deuteronomy 1:44 says *in Seir, even to Hormah*; Rahlfs there has *from
   Seir*. Numbers has neither Seir nor a starting point.
6. **What the mourning became.** Deuteronomy 1:45 puts weeping after the defeat, and Yahweh does
   not listen. Numbers puts mourning before the attempt and has no weeping afterward.
7. **The order to turn.** 14:25 says *tomorrow*, and Deuteronomy 1:46 says they stayed at Kadesh
   *many days*.
8. **Pardon.** Yahweh has pardoned (14:20), and Yahweh is not among them (14:42). See
   [[Forgiveness]].
