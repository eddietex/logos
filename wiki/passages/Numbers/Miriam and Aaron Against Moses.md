# Miriam and Aaron Against Moses

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 12:1–16

## Summary

The third complaint on the road comes from inside Moses' own family. At Hazeroth, Miriam and Aaron speak against him. The first
reason given is his Cushite wife (1). The words they actually say are about something else: *Has Yahweh indeed spoken only with
Moses? Hasn't he spoken also with us?* (2). The text does not connect the two. Then, flatly, *Yahweh heard it*.

The narrator stops to say that Moses was very humble, more than anyone on the face of the earth (3). Moses says nothing to
his brother and sister in this chapter, and he speaks only once, in verse 13, on behalf of the one who spoke against him.

Yahweh acts *suddenly* (4). He calls all three to the Tent, comes down in a pillar of cloud, stands at the door, and calls Aaron and
Miriam forward (5). His speech is the Torah's fullest statement of how he speaks to people (6–8). To a prophet he makes himself known
in a vision and speaks in a dream. Moses is not so. He is *faithful in all my house*, and with him Yahweh speaks *mouth to mouth*,
plainly and not in riddles, and he sees Yahweh's form. So why were they not afraid?

His anger burns and he leaves (9). When the cloud goes, Miriam is leprous, *as white as snow* (10). Aaron turns, sees her, and calls his
younger brother *my lord*. He confesses for both, *we have sinned*, and begs that she not be like a stillborn child with its flesh half
gone (11–12). Moses cries out in five Hebrew words: *Heal her, God, I beg you!* (13).

Yahweh's reply does not say yes or no. If her father had spat in her face, she would bear the shame seven days. So she is to be
shut outside the camp seven days and then brought in (14). That is what happens, and the whole people waits for her (15). Then they travel from
Hazeroth and camp in the wilderness of Paran (16), the place from which the spies will go out.

The chapter reads as an answer to the one before it. Moses has just wished that all Yahweh's people were prophets (11:29). Here two of
them claim that much, and Yahweh does not deny they have heard him. He says instead that prophets are not all alike.

> **11** Aaron said to Moses, “Oh, my lord, please don’t count this sin against us, in which we have done foolishly, and in which we have sinned.

## Notes

**Miriam first.** The chapter opens *wattədabbēr miryām wəʾahărōn* (1, WLC). The verb is feminine singular by the tags, with two subjects after it. A verb can agree with the first of two subjects (recalled, not from a grammar),
so the form alone does not prove she led, but she is named first, and she alone is struck. Rahlfs has the plain *ἐλάλησεν*. In verses 4 and 5
the order moves: Moses, Aaron, Miriam, then Aaron and Miriam, and she is first again only at the end.

**The stated cause and the spoken cause.** Verse 1 gives the reason as *ʿal-ʾōdôt hāʾiššâ hakkušît*, and repeats itself: *for he had married a Cushite woman*. Verse 2
says nothing about her. Readers have supposed that the marriage was a pretext for the real complaint, or that it was the real
complaint and the claim to prophecy was the argument. The text does not say which, and it does not say what was wrong with the marriage: that
she was foreign, that she was a new wife, or something else. Yahweh's answer never mentions her. *Kušît* in this sense occurs only in this verse by the
tags. See [[Who Is the Cushite Woman - Numbers 12.1, Exodus 2, and Habakkuk 3]].

**One idiom, two meanings.** *Dibbēr bə-* runs through the chapter. In verse 1 it means to speak against, in verse 2 it means Yahweh speaking with or through someone, and verse 8 has both:
*peh ʾel-peh ʾădabber-bô* and *lədabbēr bəʿabdî*. Verse 6 has the same form again. Numbers 21:5, 7 and Psalm 78:19 use it for speaking against (WLC). Rahlfs separates the senses, with
*κατὰ Μωυσῆ* against the plain dative *Μωυσῇ μόνῳ λελάληκεν*, so the wordplay is lost in Greek and in WEB. Those who spoke *against* Moses are told how Yahweh speaks *with* him.

**What they claim is true.** Miriam is *the prophetess* (Exodus 15:20). Aaron is called Moses' *prophet* (Exodus 7:1), and Yahweh spoke to him at Exodus 4:27. Yahweh does not
deny any of this. He sets it in a lower place. Their question has *raq ʾak*, *only, just* (2), two restrictive words in a row, and the reply answers that word.

**Yahweh heard it.** *Wayyišmaʿ yhwh* (2, WLC) closes verse 2. Numbers 11:1 has the same report, with anger following at once. Here the anger waits for verse 9, after a speech, and
verse 6 begins with *hear*, *šimʿû-nāʾ*. They were overheard, and now they are told to listen.

**Very humble.** *ʿĀnāw* (3) is written *ענו* and read *עניו* in WLC, which morphhb gives as two tokens under one lemma. By the tags this is its only use in the Torah, and the rest are Job, Psalms,
Proverbs, and the prophets, mostly of the poor and the lowly. So it may mean *afflicted* or *dependent* as much as *modest*; that range is recalled, not checked in a lexicon, but the
company the word keeps is from the tags. Rahlfs has *πραῢς σφόδρα*, the word of Matthew 11:29 and, in the plural, Matthew 5:5 (SBLGNT). The verse is a narrator's parenthesis, and
it explains the silence that follows. Exodus 11:3 has the same *hāʾîš mōšeh … məʾōd*. See [[The Last Plague Announced]].

**On the surface of the earth.** *ʿAl-pənê hāʾădāmâ* (3, WLC) ends Exodus 33:16 too, where Moses asks that he and Israel be *separated … from all the people who are on the surface of the earth*.
There the phrase sets Israel apart, and here it sets Moses apart. The echo is the wiki's observation.

**Who says it.** A sentence praising Moses' humility sits in a book that names him as the writer of only the journey list (33:2). Whether he wrote this about
himself has been argued for a long time. The verse does not address it. The wiki notes the question and does not settle it.

**Suddenly.** *Pitʾōm* (4, WLC) occurs twice in Numbers by the tags, and the other is the sudden death that defiles a Nazirite at 6:9. *You three come out* (4) is a summons. Then the two are called
forward and Moses is left standing back (5).

**The pillar at the door.** *Wayyēred yhwh bəʿammûd ʿānān wayyaʿămōd petaḥ hāʾōhel* (5, WLC) is nearly Exodus 33:9: *yērēd ʿammûd heʿānān wəʿāmad petaḥ hāʾōhel*. Rahlfs has *ἐν στύλῳ νεφέλης*.
The scene has the shape of the Tent outside the camp, as at 11:24–30. See tension 6.

**Vision and dream.** Verse 6 sets *bammarʾâ* and *baḥălôm* in parallel, with *ʾetwaddāʿ*, *I make myself known*. *Marʾâ* is rare: 10 verses by the tags, including Genesis 46:2, 1 Samuel 3:15, and the mirrors of Exodus 38:8. *Ḥălôm* is the
word of Genesis 20:3, Genesis 37, and Genesis 41, and Deuteronomy 13 pairs it with the prophet. Rahlfs has *ἐν ὁράματι* and *ἐν ὕπνῳ*, *in sleep*. The Hebrew phrase *nəbîʾăkem yhwh* is
awkward, *your prophet, Yahweh*. WEB takes *Yahweh* with what follows. Rahlfs has *προφήτης ὑμῶν κυρίῳ*, *a prophet of yours to the Lord*. The text may be disturbed; that is the wiki's judgment from the
two forms, and no apparatus was read.

**Not so.** *Lōʾ-kēn ʿabdî mōšeh* (7, WLC) gives Moses the title before the description. Exodus 14:31 had *his servant Moses*, in a verse where the people *believed*. Verse 7's word is *neʾĕmān*,
the niphal participle of the same root, used of Samuel at 1 Samuel 3:20 by the tags. So the people are told to trust the servant, and now the servant is called trustworthy. *Bəkol-bêtî*, *in
all my house*, could mean the household he manages or the tabernacle (the same word is used of the sanctuary elsewhere). The chapter does not explain it. Rahlfs has *ὁ θεράπων μου* and *ἐν ὅλῳ τῷ οἴκῳ μου πιστός*, and Hebrews 3:5 takes up those words. See
[[Faithful in All My House - Numbers 12.7 and Hebrews 3]].

**Mouth to mouth.** *Peh ʾel-peh* (8, WLC) is used of speech only here by the tags. The form recurs at Ezra 9:11, *mippeh ʾel-peh*, meaning from one end to the other. The usual phrase for Moses
is *face to face* (Exodus 33:11; Deuteronomy 34:10). *Ûmarʾeh* follows, which WEB gives as *even plainly*. The noun is *appearance*, a different lemma from verse 6's *marʾâ* by the tags, though close in sound, and it is
Leviticus 13's word for how a sore looks. Miriam's skin will shortly be something to look at. Rahlfs has *ἐν εἴδει*.

**Riddles.** *Ḥîdôt* (8, WLC) is Samson's word in Judges 14, the queen of Sheba's in 1 Kings 10:1, and the word of Psalm 78:2 and Proverbs 1:6. See [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]] and
[[Not in Riddles - Numbers 12.8 and 1 Corinthians 13.12]].

**Yahweh's form.** *Ûtəmunat yhwh yabbîṭ* (8, WLC) is the boldest clause. *Təmûnâ* is the word of Exodus 20:4 and Deuteronomy 4:12, 15, *you saw no form*. *Yabbîṭ* is the verb of Exodus 3:6, where Moses was afraid to look at God, and
of Exodus 33:8. Rahlfs changes the clause to *καὶ τὴν δόξαν κυρίου εἶδεν*, *and he saw the glory of the Lord*, which is the word of Moses' request at Exodus 33:18 and is past tense. That reading is
softer than the Hebrew. See tension 1.

**Not afraid.** The question *ûmaddûaʿ lōʾ yərēʾtem* (8, WLC) ends with *bəʿabdî bəmōšeh*, *against my servant, against Moses*, with the preposition twice. The speech has no sentence in it, and the penalty comes without a word.

**The cloud goes.** *Wəheʿānān sār* (10, WLC) has the verb *sûr*, not Numbers 9's *ʿālâ*. Yahweh *departs* first (9). The moment that shows Miriam is the moment he is gone.

**Like snow.** *Mᵉṣōraʿat kaššāleg* (10, WLC) has no word for *white*. WEB supplies it here and at Exodus 4:6 and 2 Kings 5:27, the only three verses where the two words stand together by the tags. Snow may point to
color or to flaking; the verse does not say. Rahlfs has *λεπρῶσα ὡσεὶ χιών*. Whatever the disease was, it was not necessarily what modern English calls leprosy. See [[Clean and Unclean]] and
[[As White as Snow - Moses' Hand, Miriam, and Gehazi]].

**Only Miriam.** Both spoke, and the anger burned against *them* (*bām*, 9, WLC), but only she is struck. No reason is given. Readers have said she began it, that the verb shows it, or that a
high priest could not be made unclean and go on serving. Those are reported and not decided. Aaron says *us* and *we* in verse 11 and is not corrected.

**Aaron looks.** *Wayyipen ʾahărōn* (10, WLC) is more than noticing. Leviticus 13:2 brings such cases to Aaron the priest, so the man who now looks is the man whose office is to look. He does not pronounce anything. He turns to
Moses instead.

**My lord.** *Bî ʾădōnî* (11, WLC) is the courtesy word of Exodus 4:10 and 13, where Moses says it to God, in a form differing by a vowel, with the Torah's other uses at Genesis 43:20 and 44:18. Aaron used *my lord* of Moses at Exodus 32:22 too, also after a sin.
His confession has two verbs: *nôʾalnû*, *we have done foolishly*, a niphal occurring four times by the tags, and *ḥāṭāʾnû*. Rahlfs softens the first to *ἠγνοήσαμεν*, *we acted in ignorance*.

**Half-consumed.** Verse 12 compares her to one born dead, whose flesh is half eaten. WLC has *ʾimmô* and *bəśārô*, *his mother* and *his flesh*. Rahlfs has *ὡσεὶ ἔκτρωμα*, with *τῶν σαρκῶν αὐτῆς*, *her* flesh. Later Jewish
tradition is reported to treat the Hebrew as a softening of *our mother* and *our flesh*, which would make Aaron's plea a family one; that is recalled, not checked in any edition. *Ἔκτρωμα* is the word Paul uses of himself at 1 Corinthians 15:8
(SBLGNT), and no link is claimed.

**The five words.** *ʾĒl nāʾ rəpāʾ nāʾ lāh* (13, WLC) is the Torah's shortest prayer the wiki has met, though no search was run for a shorter one. It comes after *ʾal nāʾ* in verses 11 and 12, and the consonants of *ʾl nʾ* are the same each time: Aaron's *not, please* twice, and then
Moses' *God, please*. The sound shift is the wiki's observation. *Ṣāʿaq* with *rāpāʾ* was also the pair at Marah (Exodus 15:25–26). The man who was attacked and who has not spoken is the one who asks. See
[[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].

**Her father.** *Wəʾābîhā yārōq yāraq bəpānêhā* (14, WLC) doubles the verb with the infinitive. *Yāraq* occurs only here and at Deuteronomy 25:9 by the tags. The argument moves from lesser to greater, and the father is a supposition, not a report.
*Tikkālēm* is *be ashamed*. So the sentence is shame, and Yahweh frames it as a family's discipline with an end date. See
[[Spit in Her Face - Numbers 12.14 and Deuteronomy 25.9]].

**Shut up.** *Tissāgēr* (14, WLC) is the verb of Leviticus 13:4–5, 21, 26, 31, 33, where the priest isolates a suspected case for seven days. But Leviticus 13:46 sends the leper outside the camp *all the days in which the plague is in him*, and Leviticus 14:8 has the cleansed person come in and stay outside his tent for seven days.
Numbers 12 uses the word and the number and does not fit either rule closely. No priest examines her, and no rite is told.

**Gathered in.** *Teʾāsēp* (14) and *hēʾāsēp* (15, WLC) are from *ʾāsap*, the verb that runs through chapter 11. Rahlfs has *εἰσελεύσεται*, then *ἕως ἐκαθαρίσθη*, *until she was cleansed*, which supplies what the Hebrew does not say.

**The people wait.** *Wəhāʿām lōʾ nāsaʿ* (15, WLC) uses the march verb. The whole camp stands still seven days for the one woman who stood far off for her brother at Exodus 2:4, if that was she. That link is an old reading and the text does not draw it.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Seeing God.** Verse 8 says Moses *shall see Yahweh's form*. Exodus 33:20 says *You cannot see my face*, and Exodus 33:23 *my face shall not be seen*, a few verses after Exodus 33:11 says *face to face*. Deuteronomy 4:12, 15 says Israel *saw no form*. Rahlfs's *glory* eases verse 8. The wiki keeps both claims. See [[Mouth to Mouth - Numbers 12.6-8, Exodus 33, and Deuteronomy 34]].
2. **The prophet like Moses.** Verses 6–8 set Moses above prophets as a class. Deuteronomy 18:15, 18 promises a prophet *like* him, and Deuteronomy 34:10 says none has arisen. These are recorded together on the same page.
3. **Who she is.** The Cushite woman may be Zipporah or a second wife. [[Zipporah]] and [[Cush]] now carry both readings.
4. **Was she healed?** Moses asks for healing. Yahweh's reply mentions seven days and shame and does not mention healing, and the Hebrew says only that she was *brought in*. Rahlfs says *cleansed*.
5. **Why not Aaron?** See the note above. The text gives no reason.
6. **Where the Tent is.** *Come out to the Tent* (4), and the camp's *outside* is a separate place (14–15). As with 11:24–30, this is not the picture of Numbers 2. See [[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]].
7. **The cloud.** It leaves (10) and the people stay (15). Compare Numbers 9. See [[Over the Tent and Before the People - Where the Cloud Stood]].
8. **The route.** Verse 16 goes from Hazeroth to the wilderness of Paran, while 33:18 goes to Rithmah and does not name Paran. See [[Paran]].
