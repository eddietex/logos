# Edom Refuses Passage

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 20:14–21

## Summary

Moses sends messengers from Kadesh to Edom’s king. Israel appeals as a brother nation,
recalls the ancestors’ descent into Egypt and the suffering there, and recounts Yahweh’s
answer to their cry: an angel sent and deliverance from Egypt (14–16).

The first request promises passage along the king’s highway without entering fields or
vineyards or drinking well water. Edom threatens armed resistance. Israel renews the request,
now explicitly offering payment if people or livestock drink water, but Edom repeats the
refusal and comes out in force. Israel turns away rather than forcing entry (17–21).

## Key verses

WEB, copied verbatim from the stored Numbers source.

> **14** Moses sent messengers from Kadesh to the king of Edom, saying: “Thus says your brother Israel: You know all the travail that has happened to us;

> **16** When we cried to Yahweh, he heard our voice, sent an angel, and brought us out of Egypt. Behold, we are in Kadesh, a city in the edge of your border.

> **17** “Please let us pass through your land. We will not pass through field or through vineyard, neither will we drink from the water of the wells. We will go along the king’s highway. We will not turn away to the right hand nor to the left, until we have passed your border.”

> **18** Edom said to him, “You shall not pass through me, lest I come out with the sword against you.”

> **19** The children of Israel said to him, “We will go up by the highway; and if we drink your water, I and my livestock, then I will give its price. Only let me, without doing anything else, pass through on my feet.”

> **20** He said, “You shall not pass through.” Edom came out against him with many people, and with a strong hand.

> **21** Thus Edom refused to give Israel passage through his border, so Israel turned away from him.

## Notes

### Brotherhood does not secure access

The appeal is diplomatic: shared ancestry, known suffering, and a promise of limited transit.
Edom’s refusal exposes the gap between kinship and cooperation. Israel does not claim Edom’s
land in this request. The narrator records the withdrawal without a battle or a divine command
within these eight verses. It does not explain Edom’s motives. National hostility here should
not be projected back onto Esau’s personal welcome in Genesis 33.

### Two proposals and a recalled rescue

The offer to pay for water (19) qualifies the earlier undertaking not to drink from the wells
(17); these are successive negotiations, not two descriptions silently harmonized into one.
The highway is a route through Edom, but the passage does not give its precise course. No
separate route page or speculative map is needed.

Verse 16 describes past rescue, not a new angelic appearance at Kadesh. The existing angel page
records the comparison without identifying this messenger more precisely than the WEB does.
The king receives a period qualifier because his personal name is not given; he is not equated
with any king in Genesis 36. All quotations come from stored WEB Numbers 20; comparisons were
checked in stored Genesis 33 and Exodus 3, 14, and 23. No source-language assertion is made.
