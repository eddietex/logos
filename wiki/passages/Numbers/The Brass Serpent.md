# The Brass Serpent

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 21:4–9

## Summary

Israel leaves Mount Hor to go around Edom and becomes discouraged on the journey.
The people speak against God and Moses, accusing them of bringing Israel out of Egypt
to die and rejecting the bread they have. Yahweh sends venomous snakes; many die.
The people confess their sin and ask Moses to pray for removal of the serpents.
He prays, and Yahweh commands a snake set on a pole. Moses makes it of brass;
the bitten person who looks at it lives.

The mediator they accused becomes the one who prays for them. The answer takes a different
form from the request: the narrative gives a remedy for the bitten without explicitly
reporting that the snakes are removed.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Numbers.md`. The complete six-verse unit follows.

> **4** They traveled from Mount Hor by the way to the Red Sea, to go around the land of Edom The soul of the people was very discouraged because of the journey.

> **5** The people spoke against God, and against Moses, “Why have you brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? For there is no bread, and there is no water; and our soul loathes this disgusting bread.”

> **6** Yahweh sent venomous snakes among the people, and they bit the people. Many people of Israel died.

> **7** The people came to Moses, and said, “We have sinned, because we have spoken against Yahweh, and against you. Pray to Yahweh, that he take away the serpents from us.” Moses prayed for the people.

> **8** Yahweh said to Moses, “Make a venomous snake, and set it on a pole. It shall happen, that everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live.”

> **9** Moses made a serpent of brass, and set it on the pole. If a serpent had bitten any man, when he looked at the serpent of brass, he lived.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh sends the snakes after the complaint, then directs Moses to make the sign through
  which the bitten may live (6–9). Judgment and rescue are both attributed to him.
- [[Moses]] — The people confess speaking against him and ask his intercession. He prays for them
  and makes the brass serpent as commanded (7–9), continuing to serve after his own sentence at
  Meribah.

## Places

- [[Mount Hor]] — Israel departs from Mount Hor on the way to the Red Sea (4). The snake episode
  belongs to the onward journey, not explicitly to the summit where Aaron died.
- [[Edom]] — Israel travels around Edom (4), continuing the narrative of refused passage in Numbers
  20:14–21. The detour occasions discouragement; this unit reports no new confrontation with Edom.
- [[Egypt]] — Egypt is the place the people accuse God and Moses of bringing them out of to die (5).
  It is remembered in the complaint, not the location of the snake episode.
- [[The Red Sea]] — The way to the Red Sea supplies the direction of travel from Mount Hor around
  Edom (4). The verse narrates no second sea crossing or exact route.

## Themes

- [[Murmuring]] — Discouragement on the detour becomes speech against God and Moses. The people
  reject the provision they have, then confess that their speech was sin (5–7).
- [[Prayer]] — The people ask Moses to pray for the serpents to be taken away. His prayer is
  reported without its words; the answer provides life for the bitten, without stating that the
  snakes disappear (7–9).
- [[Sin]] — The people identify their own speech against Yahweh and Moses as sin (7). Confession
  precedes intercession and rescue; no sacrificial rite is prescribed in this unit.
- [[Judgment]] — Yahweh sends the snakes, and many Israelites die (6). The remedy preserves bitten
  people who look; no restoration of those already dead is narrated.
- [[Faith]] — Looking at the appointed serpent brings life (8–9). Reading this response as trust is
  an interpretation of the action; the stored WEB describes looking, while John 3:14–17 explicitly
  develops belief.
- [[Life and Death]] — Many die from the bites, while bitten people who look at the brass serpent
  live (6–9). Rescue here is survival, not a narrated resurrection of the dead.
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — Moses prays for the people who have spoken against him, then makes
  the object Yahweh commands (7–9). His work mediates rescue without making him its independent
  source.
- [[The Wilderness]] — The journey around Edom produces discouragement and a renewed accusation that
  the exodus leads only to death (4–5). The remedy is given on the road, without a new campsite
  being named.
- [[Healing]] — The bitten person who looks lives (9). Bodily rescue follows intercession and a
  commanded sign, rather than a described medical procedure; the narrator does not explain the
  physical mechanism.
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — Here the endangered person must look at the object God appoints (8–9).
  Sight is associated with life, not because every image heals but because this act answers a
  particular promise.
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — The God who sends the deadly snakes also provides a way for the bitten
  to live (6–9). Mercy is a thematic description of this rescue; it does not cancel the deaths
  already narrated.
- [[Idolatry]] — Numbers 21:8–9 commands making and looking at a serpent, without commanding worship
  of it. In 2 Kings 18:4, incense offered to the same object leads to its destruction. The
  authorized sign and its later cultic use must be distinguished.

## Notes

**Complaint and provision.** The people's claim that there is no bread sits alongside
their rejection of the bread present (5). These are words of complaint, not two separate
narratorial inventories. The passage does not itself call the bread manna; its continuity
with the wilderness provision is a contextual reading.

**Looking and living.** Numbers gives a command and an outcome, not an explanation of
the object's power. It records neither worship of the serpent nor an instruction to preserve
it as a cult object. John 3 and 2 Kings 18 develop distinct later uses of the same story;
the connection pages retain those differences.

**Translation and limits.** The planned title retains brass, as in the stored WEB verse 9.
Verse 6 in this source says venomous snakes. No Hebrew etymology, species identification,
alloy identification, or Greek textual decision is asserted. Verse 4's missing punctuation
after Edom is preserved in the quotation from the immutable raw source.

**Scope.** The planned boundary is unchanged. Later-book comparisons were verified in
bible-api.com's WEB responses for John 3:14–17, 2 Kings 18:4, and 1 Corinthians 10:9 on
2026-11-04; those books remain un-ingested. Israel here is the people, not Jacob as an
individual. No new page for the unnamed collective or for the manufactured object is needed:
the passage and connection pages carry their roles.
