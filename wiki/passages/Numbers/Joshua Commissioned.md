# Joshua Commissioned

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 27:12–23

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to go up into the mountain of Abarim and see the land given to Israel. Once
he has seen it, he will be gathered to his people as Aaron was, because at the waters of
Meribah of Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin he rebelled against Yahweh's word and did not honor
him as holy before the people.

Moses answers with a request rather than a protest. He asks Yahweh, *the God of the spirits of
all flesh*, to appoint a man over the congregation who will go out and come in before them and
lead them out and bring them in, so that Yahweh's congregation will not be like sheep without a
shepherd. Yahweh names Joshua the son of Nun, *a man in whom is the Spirit*, and tells Moses to
lay his hand on him, set him before Eleazar the priest and the whole congregation, commission
him in their sight, and give him some of his authority so that Israel will obey. Joshua is to
stand before Eleazar, who will inquire for him by the judgment of the Urim; at that word Israel
goes out and comes in. Moses does as commanded: he sets Joshua before Eleazar and the
congregation, lays his hands on him, and commissions him.

## Key verses

Copied verbatim from stored WEB, raw/bible/Numbers.md.

> **12** Yahweh said to Moses, “Go up into this mountain of Abarim, and see the land which I have given to the children of Israel.

> **13** When you have seen it, you also shall be gathered to your people, as Aaron your brother was gathered;

> **14** because in the strife of the congregation, you rebelled against my word in the wilderness of Zin, to honor me as holy at the waters before their eyes.” (These are the waters of Meribah of Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin.)

> **15** Moses spoke to Yahweh, saying,

> **16** “Let Yahweh, the God of the spirits of all flesh, appoint a man over the congregation,

> **17** who may go out before them, and who may come in before them, and who may lead them out, and who may bring them in; that the congregation of Yahweh may not be as sheep which have no shepherd.”

> **18** Yahweh said to Moses, “Take Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the Spirit, and lay your hand on him.

> **19** Set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation; and commission him in their sight.

> **20** You shall give authority to him, that all the congregation of the children of Israel may obey.

> **21** He shall stand before Eleazar the priest, who shall inquire for him by the judgment of the Urim before Yahweh. At his word they shall go out, and at his word they shall come in, both he, and all the children of Israel with him, even all the congregation.”

> **22** Moses did as Yahweh commanded him. He took Joshua, and set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation.

> **23** He laid his hands on him, and commissioned him, as Yahweh spoke by Moses.

## Notes

**The sentence repeated, the death deferred.** Verses 12–14 restate the Meribah verdict of
Numbers 20:12 and the wording used at Aaron's death (20:24): *gathered to your people … because
you rebelled against my word*. Moses is shown the land but does not die in this chapter. The
narrative continues through the rest of Numbers, and Deuteronomy 32:49–50 repeats the command
with more detail — *"Go up into this mountain of Abarim, to Mount Nebo, which is in the land of
Moab, that is across from Jericho"*, and *"be gathered to your people, as Aaron your brother died
on Mount Hor"* (WEB, verified through bible-api.com; Deuteronomy not yet ingested). Numbers 27 does
not name Nebo or say which peak of Abarim is meant.

**Moses answers with the people's need.** His only recorded reply is a request for a leader
(15–17). He does not argue the sentence, and the text gives no report of his feelings. Whether
this is resignation, acceptance, or simply priority is not stated; the wiki records the words
alone. Deuteronomy 3:23–27 narrates a plea to cross over that Numbers does not include; that plea is now ingested in
[[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]]. See also
[[See the Land and Commission Joshua - Numbers 27 and Deuteronomy 3]].

**The God of the spirits of all flesh.** Moses addresses Yahweh with the title he and Aaron used in
Numbers 16:22, when they pleaded against the death of the whole congregation for one man's sin.
The Hebrew phrase *ʾĕlōhê hārûḥōt ləkol-bāśār* is identical in both verses (Miqra according to the
Masorah, via Sefaria). There it grounds an appeal for discrimination in judgment; here, an appeal
for a fitting appointment. See [[The God of the Spirits of All Flesh - Numbers 16.22 and 27.16]].

**Going out and coming in.** The leader Moses asks for is described four ways — going out and
coming in before them, leading them out and bringing them in (17). Verse 21 returns to the same
pair: Israel goes out and comes in at the word given through Eleazar. The phrase frames
leadership as conducting the congregation's movements, including in war; this unit does not
narrate a campaign.

**Sheep with no shepherd.** The image is Moses' own, and the first time in the wiki's sequence that
the people are pictured as a flock lacking a shepherd. Genesis had already called God a shepherd
(see [[The God Who Has Fed Me - The First Time God Is a Shepherd]]). The phrase recurs in Micaiah's
vision (1 Kings 22:17) and in the Gospels' compassion summaries (Matthew 9:36; Mark 6:34). See
[[Sheep Without a Shepherd - Numbers 27.17, 1 Kings 22.17, and the Gospels]].

**A man in whom is the Spirit.** WEB capitalizes *Spirit*; the Hebrew is *ʾîš ʾăšer-rûaḥ bô*, with
no article on *rûaḥ* (MAM via Sefaria). The capital is a translator's decision, and the text does
not say when or how Joshua received the Spirit. The phrase is the stated qualification; no other
reason is given. Joshua's earlier record — Amalek, the mountain, the tent, the spies — is not
mentioned. Numbers 11 had placed Joshua next to the Spirit's distribution, objecting when Eldad
and Medad prophesied (11:28–29); this verse is the first to say the Spirit is in him.

**One hand commanded, two hands laid.** Yahweh says *lay your hand* (18; Hebrew *yādəkā*,
singular); the report says Moses *laid his hands* (23; *yādāyw*, plural) (MAM via Sefaria; WEB
follows both). The text does not comment on the difference, and the wiki does not build a doctrine
on it. Deuteronomy 34:9 later connects the laying on of hands with Joshua being *"full of the
spirit of wisdom"* (WEB, verified through bible-api.com; not yet ingested). The act here is
public: before Eleazar and the whole congregation, *in their sight* (19, 22).

**Some of your authority.** WEB *give authority to him* (20) renders *wənātattâ mēhôdəkā ʿālāyw*;
the preposition *min* is prefixed to *hôd*, a word for splendor or majesty (MAM via Sefaria). That
construction is commonly read as partitive — *some of* Moses' majesty — but the partitive sense is
an interpretation of the preposition, not something the verse spells out. Either way, the stated
purpose is obedience: *that all the congregation … may obey*.

**A different kind of leader.** Moses spoke with Yahweh directly (Numbers 12:8). Joshua will
*stand before Eleazar*, who inquires for him by the Urim (21). Leadership passes; Moses' unmediated
access does not pass with it. The Hebrew here names only *the Urim* (*hāʾûrîm*), not the Thummim
(MAM via Sefaria); this is the first narrative in the wiki's sequence where the Urim is assigned a
use, though no inquiry is actually carried out here. See
[[Urim and Thummim - The Breastplate of Judgment and Later Inquiry]].

**Eleazar's role.** Aaron's successor (Numbers 20:25–28) becomes the stage and the oracle for Moses'
successor. The two transitions run in parallel: Aaron's garments put on Eleazar at Mount Hor; Moses'
hand laid on Joshua before Eleazar here. The text does not draw the comparison itself.

**Boundary and naming.** The planned boundary, 27:12–23, is unchanged. The provisional title is
kept. Abarim receives its own place page; the related camp [[Iyeabarim]] is a separate name and is
not identified with it here.
