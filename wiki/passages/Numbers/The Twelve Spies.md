# The Twelve Spies

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 13:1–33

## Summary

Israel has reached the wilderness of Paran, and the book's long march now has its object in view.
Yahweh tells Moses to send men to spy out the land of Canaan, *which I give to the children of
Israel*, one from each tribe (1–2). The twelve are named (4–15). None of them is one of the twelve
princes of Numbers 1, 2, 7, and 10, and two of them matter: Caleb of Judah, who appears here for the
first time, and Hoshea of Ephraim, whom Moses calls Joshua (16).

Moses gives them a list of questions: is the land good or bad, fat or lean, wooded or not, and are
its people strong or weak, few or many, in camps or strongholds. He adds *Be courageous*, and tells
them to bring fruit, because it is the season of the first-ripe grapes (17–20). They go the whole
length of the land, from the wilderness of Zin to Rehob by the entrance of Hamath (21). At Hebron
they find Ahiman, Sheshai, and Talmai, the children of Anak (22). In the valley of Eshcol they cut
one cluster of grapes and carry it on a staff between two, with pomegranates and figs (23–24).

After forty days they return to the whole congregation at Kadesh and show the fruit (25–26). The
first report is what Moses asked for. The land flows with milk and honey, and *this is its fruit*.
However, the people are strong, the cities large and fortified, the children of Anak are there, and
the land is held from the South to the hill country to the sea (27–29). Caleb quiets the people and
says, *Let us go up at once* (30). The others say, *We aren't able* (31).

Then the narrator changes the word. They bring out an *evil report of the land*: it is a land that
eats up its inhabitants, all its people are huge, the Nephilim are there, and *we were in our own
sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight* (32–33). The chapter ends on that sentence.
The people's answer, and Yahweh's, come in [[The People Refuse the Land]].

The chapter does not say the facts were false. The fruit is real, and so are the sons of Anak. The
first report is called no evil thing. The turn comes when *we aren't able* replaces *Yahweh gives*,
and Yahweh's phrase from verse 2 is absent from everything the ten say.

## Notes

**Send for yourself.** *Šəlaḥ-ləkā* (2, WLC) has the extra *to you*, the same form as *lek-ləkā* at
Genesis 12:1 in construction. Rahlfs keeps it, *ἀπόστειλον σεαυτῷ*. A rabbinic reading takes it as
*send if you wish*, which would ease the conflict with Deuteronomy 1; that is recalled, not checked
in any edition. The Hebrew does not require it. See tension 1.

**I give.** *ʾĂšer-ʾănî nōtēn* (2, WLC) is a participle, *which I am giving*. The mission is
therefore not to find out whether the land can be had. Moses' questions in verses 18–20 are about
what it is like, and whether it can be taken was not one of them.

**Which princes?** Verse 2 asks for *every one a prince* (*nāśîʾ*), and verse 3 says *heads*.
Numbers 1:5–15, 7:12–83, and 10:14–27 have already named the twelve princes, and none of those names
is here. Nahshon leads Judah there and Caleb goes for Judah here. So *nāśîʾ* is not one office with
one holder, at least here; the text does not explain it. See [[The Twelve Tribal Princes]].

**The list.** The order is Reuben, Simeon, Judah, Issachar, Ephraim, Benjamin, Zebulun, Manasseh,
Dan, Asher, Naphtali, Gad, and it matches neither the census order of Numbers 1 nor the camp order
of Numbers 2. Levi is absent, as the tribe is from the land allotments. Ephraim and Manasseh stand
apart, with Benjamin and Zebulun between them, and *the tribe of Joseph* is attached only to
Manasseh (11). Rahlfs's order was not compared name by name. *Igal the son of Joseph* is not the
patriarch's son. *Sethur the son of Michael* carries the first *Michael* in the Hebrew Bible by the
tags. By the tags Gaddiel, Gaddi, Sethur, Nahbi, and Geuel occur nowhere else, and the other five
names belong to other people elsewhere. The ten are named here once, and Numbers 14:37 records their
death with no names.

**Hoshea and Joshua.** *Wayyiqrāʾ mōšeh ləhôšēaʿ bin-nûn yəhôšuaʿ* (16, WLC). The form is narrative,
with no date, and it stands at the end of the list and not at the moment of sending. Exodus has used
*Joshua* since 17:9, and Numbers since 11:28. So either the narrator has used the later name all
along, or verse 16 reports a naming from earlier. The verse gives no reason, and it does not explain
the name. Rahlfs has *Αυση* and then *Ἰησοῦν* in one verse. Deuteronomy 32:44 has *wəhôšēaʿ bin-nûn*
in WLC, the old name long after this chapter, while WEB prints *Joshua* there and Rahlfs has
*Ἰησοῦς*. See tension 6 and [[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]].

**Six questions.** Verses 18–20 come in pairs. *Strong or weak, few or many, good or bad, camps or
strongholds, fat or lean, wood or none.* The first report answers most of them and the second
replaces them. *Fat or lean* gets *milk and honey*, *strong or weak* gets *strong*, and *camps or
strongholds* gets *fortified and very large*. Nothing in verses 27–29 is called evil.
*Wəhitḥazzaqtem* (20), *be courageous*, is the verb *ḥāzaq*, and the adjective *ḥāzāq* is the word
of *strong or weak* (18) and of *stronger than we* (31). The men are told to be strong in the same
terms as the thing they fear. The wordplay is the wiki's observation.

**This way by the South.** *ʿĂlû zeh bannegeb* (17, WLC). Rahlfs twice has *τῇ ἐρήμῳ*, *by the
wilderness*, where the Hebrew has *the Negev* (17, 22). *Wəʿălîtem ʾet-hāhār*, *go up into the hill
country*, sets up the chapter's main verb. *ʿĀlâ* recurs in verses 17, 21, 22, 30, and 31: Caleb
says it twice, and the others deny it.

**First-ripe grapes.** *Yəmê bikkûrê ʿănābîm* (20, WLC). *Bikkûrîm* is the first-fruits word of
Exodus 23:16 and Leviticus 23:17. The usual placing of the season in high summer is recalled, and
the chapter gives no date. Numbers 10:11 last gave one, the twentieth of the second month. Rahlfs
has *ἡμέραι ἔαρος πρόδρομοι σταφυλῆς*, *days of spring, forerunners of the grape*.

**The verb of the chapter.** *Tûr* occurs seven times in the chapter by the tags, and it is not the
ordinary word for spying. Genesis 42 and Joshua 2 use *rāgal* for spies. *Tûr* is to scout, to seek,
to go about, the word for the ark at 10:33 and for following the eyes at 15:39. WEB's *spy out*
hides this. See [[To Seek Out the Land - Tur in Numbers 10, 13, 15, and Ezekiel 20]].

**He came to Hebron.** *Wayyaʿălû bannegeb wayyābōʾ ʿad-ḥebrôn* (22, WLC). *Went up* is plural and
*came* is singular by the morphology. WEB and Rahlfs (*ἦλθον*) make both plural. Verse 23 is plural
again. Jewish tradition is reported to read Caleb alone at Hebron, which would fit Deuteronomy 1:36
and Joshua 14:9 (*the land where you walked*); that reading is recalled, and the singular could be
collective. The wiki notes the form and does not build on it. See
[[Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1]].

**A date for Hebron.** *Šebaʿ šānîm nibnətâ lipnê ṣōʿan miṣrāyim* (22, WLC). The aside fits no task
in the story. Hebron is the patriarchs' town, and Zoan is a royal city of Egypt, so the land is
given an antiquity measured against Israel's former masters. That reading is the wiki's. Rahlfs
renders Zoan as *Τάνιν*. The date cannot be used without knowing which founding of Zoan is meant.
See [[Zoan]].

**One cluster.** *Wəʾeškôl ʿănābîm ʾeḥād* (23, WLC) puts *one* last, and *bammôṭ bišnāyim*, *on a
pole, by two*, says why it matters. *Môṭ* is the carrying-frame of Numbers 4:10, 12, the same lemma,
on which the Kohathites bore the holy things. The fruit of the land travels as the sanctuary's
vessels travel. That link is the wiki's and the text does not press it. Numbers 20:5 will list
*figs*, *vines*, and *pomegranates* as what the wilderness lacks, and the three are all here.
Christian readers have seen the two bearers and the cluster as a figure of the cross; that is
recalled and is not in the text.

**Named for the fruit.** *Naḥal ʾeškôl* (23–24). The Israelites are said to have named a place in
Canaan a generation before they entered it. Verse 24 gives the etymology, against the [[Eshcol]] of
Genesis 14:13, the Amorite with the same word for a name in the same district. The tags give the two
words separate numbers. See [[The Valley of Eshcol]].

**Milk and honey.** *Zābat ḥālāb ûdəbaš* has so far been a promise, at Exodus 3:8, 17, 13:5, and
33:3 and Leviticus 20:24. Verse 27 is the first time anyone who has been there says it, and they
hold up the fruit as they do. In Numbers 16:13 Dathan and Abiram will use the same words of Egypt.

**However.** *ʾEpes kî* (28, WLC) turns the report. *ʿAz hāʿām*, *the people are strong*, uses
*ʿaz*, and it is Moses' question that used *ḥāzāq*. The peoples are named by region in verse 29, and
the list has *Hittite, Jebusite, Amorite* in the hills and *Canaanite* by the sea and the Jordan, a
tidier map than Exodus 3:8's list. Rahlfs adds *ὁ Ευαῖος*, the Hivite. Amalek stands first in the
list, in the South; Israel has fought them already. See [[The Battle with Amalek]].

**Caleb stills them.** *Wayyahas kālēb ʾet-hāʿām ʾel-mōšeh* (30, WLC) uses a rare word, eight verses
by the tags, most of them a bare *hush!* (Judges 3:19; Amos 6:10; Habakkuk 2:20). No uproar has been
narrated, so verse 30 implies one the text leaves out. *ʿĀlōh naʿăleh … yākôl nûkal* has the same
doubling twice, *going up we will go up, being able we are able*. *We shall possess it*
(*wəyārašnû*) is the verb of Yahweh's promise. Rahlfs keeps the doubled forms, with *ἀναβάντες
ἀναβησόμεθα* and *δυνατοὶ δυνησόμεθα*. Caleb says nothing about Yahweh here. In 14:8–9 he and Joshua
will.

**We aren't able.** *Lōʾ nûkal* (31, WLC) is the same verb in the same form as Caleb's, with a
negative. *Mimmennû*, *than we*, has the same spelling as *than he*, and a rabbinic reading is
reported to have the spies saying the people are stronger than God. That is recalled. The tags, WEB,
and Rahlfs (*ἡμῶν*) all take it as *than we*.

**The evil report.** *Wayyôṣîʾû dibbat hāʾāreṣ* (32, WLC). Up to here the report was *dābār* (26).
Now it is *dibbâ*, nine occurrences by the tags, the word of Joseph's report at Genesis 37:2.
*Report of the land* makes the land the thing maligned, and the verse sends it to the children of
Israel, not to Moses. Rahlfs has *ἔκστασιν*, *terror* or *astonishment*, which names the effect. See
[[An Evil Report - Dibbah in Genesis 37, Numbers 13, and Ezekiel 36]].

**A land that eats.** *ʾEreṣ ʾōkelet yôšəbêhā* (32, WLC). Its meaning is unclear. It may mean a poor
land, one that kills by war, or a land Israel's people could not survive. The statement also sits
badly with the verse's own *men of great stature*, since a land that ate its people would not raise
giants. Leviticus 18:25 said the land *vomited out her inhabitants*, with the same *yôšəbêhā*, and
Leviticus 26:38 said a land of enemies *will eat you up*. See
[[A Land That Eats Up Its Inhabitants - Numbers 13.32, Leviticus 18 and 26, and Ezekiel 36]].

**All the people.** *Wəkol-hāʿām* (32) is an exaggeration by its own terms: verse 22 had three named
men, and verse 28 said *children of Anak*. *ʾAnšê middôt*, *men of measures*, is a rare idiom;
compare *ʾîš middâ* at 1 Chronicles 11:23 and *ʾanšê middâ* at Isaiah 45:14 (WLC).

**The Nephilim.** *Wəšām rāʾînû ʾet-hannəpîlîm bənê ʿănāq min-hannəpilîm* (33, WLC). The word stands
twice in the verse, with and without the *y*, and *bənê ʿănāq min-hannəpilîm* identifies the Anakim
with them. Rahlfs has only *τοὺς γίγαντας*, and lacks the clause *the sons of Anak, who come from
the Nephilim*. So in that Greek text the identification with Anak is absent. No apparatus was read,
so whether the Hebrew clause is a gloss cannot be said here. See
[[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]].

**In their sight.** *Wannəhî bəʿênênû kaḥăgābîm wəkēn hāyînû bəʿênêhem* (33, WLC). *Ḥāgāb* is the
edible locust of Leviticus 11:22, and five occurrences by the tags include Isaiah 40:22. The first
half can be known. The second half cannot, and the spies state it as a fact. Rahab says otherwise at
Joshua 2:9, in the same land. See [[Like Grasshoppers - Numbers 13.33 and Isaiah 40.22]] and
[[Terror Has Fallen on Us - The Song of the Sea in Rahab's Mouth]].

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Who proposed it.** Here Yahweh commands the mission (1–2). In Deuteronomy 1:22–23 the people
   ask for it and Moses approves it. See [[Who Sent the Spies - Numbers 13 and Deuteronomy 1]].
2. **What they said.** Here the report is mixed and then evil. Deuteronomy 1:25 gives only *It is a
   good land*, and 1:28 puts the fear in the people's mouths, quoting *our brothers*.
3. **How far they went.** Verse 21 goes to Rehob and the entrance of Hamath. Verses 22–24,
   Deuteronomy 1:24, and Numbers 32:9 name nothing beyond the valley of Eshcol.
4. **Which wilderness.** Kadesh is in the wilderness of Paran here (26). It is in the wilderness of
   Zin at 20:1 and 33:36, and verse 21 treats Zin as the starting edge. See [[Kadesh]] and
   [[The Wilderness of Zin]].
5. **Caleb alone, or with Joshua.** Caleb alone speaks here. Numbers 14:24 and Deuteronomy 1:36 name
   him alone, while 14:6, 30, 38 and 32:12 name both, and Deuteronomy 1:38 gives Joshua a different
   reason.
6. **When he was renamed.** See the note above.
7. **Who holds what.** Verse 29 puts Amalek in the South and the Canaanite by the sea and the
   Jordan, while 14:25 and 14:45 put both in the valley and the hill country.
8. **Which princes.** See the note above.
9. **Who removed the Anakim.** Joshua (Joshua 11:21), Caleb (Joshua 15:14; Judges 1:20), or Judah
   (Judges 1:10). See [[Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1]].
10. **Whose son.** Caleb is *the son of Jephunneh* here and at 1 Chronicles 4:15, and 1 Chronicles
    2:18 has *Caleb the son of Hezron*. They may be two men. See [[Caleb (son of Jephunneh)]].
11. **After the flood.** Nephilim in Canaan after Genesis 7. See [[The Nephilim]].
