# The Rebellion of Korah

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 16:1–40

## Summary

Korah, Dathan, Abiram, and On rise with 250 leaders against Moses and Aaron, invoking the
holiness of the congregation (1–3). Moses proposes an incense test of Yahweh’s choice and
confronts the Levites’ demand for priesthood (4–11). Dathan and Abiram refuse his summons,
calling the land they left fertile and accusing him of domination and failed promises (12–15).

Korah gathers the congregation at the Tent; glory appears, and Moses and Aaron intercede
against its destruction (16–22). The people are told to leave the rebels’ tents. Moses declares
that an extraordinary death will attest his commission; the ground opens and swallows the
rebels and households, while fire consumes the 250 incense offerers (23–35). Eleazar recovers
the holy censers, which become an altar covering and a warning guarding Aaron’s priesthood
(36–40). The next day’s renewed complaint continues in
[[Aaron Stands Between the Dead and the Living]].

## Notes

**Two accusations, one coalition.** Korah’s challenge concerns holiness and access; Dathan and
Abiram emphasize failed deliverance, land, and coercive rule. Calling Egypt a land flowing with
milk and honey reverses the promise’s direction. Their accusation is not the narrator’s verdict;
Numbers 14 has already narrated the refusal to enter the land.

**An intercessor who also protests.** Moses denies taking even a donkey or harming anyone (15).
He asks that their offering not be respected, yet joins Aaron to plead for the congregation (22).
The plea’s distinction between one offender and the whole assembly does not eliminate the
troubling inclusion of households, wives, and little ones in verses 27–33. No individual offenses
are assigned to those children.

**Korah’s fate and On’s disappearance.** Verse 32 names Korah’s men rather than narrating a
separate death scene for Korah. Stored WEB Numbers 26:10 explicitly places Korah in the swallowing;
26:11 says his sons did not die. That retrospective qualifies an indiscriminate reading of
“all” in chapter 16; it is a checked cross-reference, not ingestion of Numbers 26. On is named
in 16:1 and then disappears from the account. His withdrawal, rescue, or death is not explained.

**Holy objects from condemned offerers.** The censers remain holy because they were offered before
Yahweh (38). Their reuse is commanded as a warning, not approval of their owners. The restriction
in verse 40 defines the excluded person by descent from Aaron, not simply foreign nationality.

**A punitive descent, not a map of the afterlife.** Going alive into Sheol is the extraordinary
judgment here (30–33). This qualifies the broad statement on the Sheol page that it is not a place
of punishment, without establishing ongoing torment there. The consuming fire is a separate act
against the 250 offerers (35).
