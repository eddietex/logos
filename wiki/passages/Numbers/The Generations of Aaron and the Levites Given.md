# The Generations of Aaron and the Levites Given

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 3:1–13

## Summary

Two chapters have placed the tribes. This one turns to the people at the center. It opens with a
heading, *the history of the generations of Aaron and Moses*, dated to the day Yahweh spoke with
Moses on Sinai (1). The list that follows holds only Aaron's four sons (2). They are the anointed
priests (3), and two of them are already dead: Nadab and Abihu died offering strange fire, and they
had no children, so Eleazar and Ithamar served under their father's eye (4).

Then Yahweh speaks twice. The first speech gives the tribe of Levi to Aaron. Moses is to bring them
near and stand them before the priest, and they will keep his duties and the duties of the whole
congregation at the Tent (5–8). They are *wholly given* to him on Israel's behalf (9). Aaron and his
sons keep the priesthood, and the outsider who comes near dies (10).

The second speech gives the reason in God's own terms. He has taken the Levites *instead of all the
firstborn*, because every firstborn is his from the night he struck Egypt's, man and animal alike
(11–13). The speech ends, *I am Yahweh*.

So the chapter sets three circles. The priests stand nearest, the Levites around them, and all
Israel beyond, and the circles are held in place by a death already suffered and a death still
threatened. The tribe that serves here does so as a substitute. Each Levite stands where an Israelite
family's eldest son would otherwise stand, in a claim that goes back to the Passover.

> **1** Now this is the history of the generations of Aaron and Moses in the day that Yahweh spoke with Moses in Mount Sinai.

> **2** These are the names of the sons of Aaron: Nadab the firstborn, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.

> **10** You shall appoint Aaron and his sons, and they shall keep their priesthood. The stranger who comes near shall be put to death.”

> **11** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **12** “Behold, I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel instead of all the firstborn who open the womb among the children of Israel; and the Levites shall be mine:

> **13** for all the firstborn are mine. On the day that I struck down all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I made holy to me all the firstborn in Israel, both man and animal. They shall be mine. I am Yahweh.”

## Notes

**Boundary.** The plan's break holds. Verses 1–4 are the heading and list, 5–10 and 11–13 are two
speeches each opened by the same formula, and 3:14 begins a new command to count.

**A heading with half its names missing.** *Wəʾēlleh tôlədōt ʾahărōn ûmōšeh* (WLC) promises both
brothers, and Aaron comes first. Exodus 6:26 has the same order, and the next verse reverses it. The
list then gives only Aaron's sons, and Moses' are not named here at all. 1 Chronicles 23:14–15 later
says Moses' sons, Gershom and Eliezer, *were named among the tribe of Levi*, which fits the silence
here, though Numbers does not explain it. Only this one verse and two others outside Genesis open with
*ʾēlleh tôlədōt*. See [[The Generations of Aaron and Moses - Toledot Outside Genesis]].

**Anointed.** Verse 3 calls the sons *the priests who were anointed*, *hakkōhănîm hamməšuḥîm* (WLC).
Exodus 28:41 and 40:15 command that, but the rites themselves pour oil only on Aaron's head (Exodus
29:7) and sprinkle the sons with oil and blood (29:21; Leviticus 8:30). The word covers both acts
here. The anointing of the sons is not in doubt, only its manner.

**Whom he consecrated.** WEB's *consecrated* is the idiom *millēʾ yādām*, *he filled their hand*
(WLC), and the verb's subject is not named. Moses performed the rite in Leviticus 8, so he is the
natural reading, though Aaron is the nearest named person. The same idiom stands in Moses' command to
the Levites at Exodus 32:29, *mil'û yedkem*. That is the wiki's observation; Numbers does not point
back to it.

**Nadab and Abihu, in one verse.** Verse 4 gives the deaths, the offense, the place, and something
Leviticus 10 did not mention: *they had no children*. That last clause is why the two names leave the
priestly line entirely, and why Eleazar and Ithamar inherit it. Numbers repeats *ʾēš zārâ* (WLC) and
adds nothing about why the fire was strange, and it makes no reference to the *which he had not
commanded them* of Leviticus 10:1.

*ʿAl-pənê ʾahărōn ʾăbîhem* (WLC), WEB's *in the presence of Aaron their father*, is usually taken
as in his sight or in his lifetime; that range is not checked here against a named lexicon. Genesis 11:28 uses *ʿal-pənê* of Haran dying before Terah, where *in his
lifetime* fits. 1 Chronicles 24:2 uses a different preposition, *lipnê*, of the deaths themselves.
The wiki leaves the phrase at WEB's rendering.

**One verb, three times.** *Qārab* runs through the unit. Nadab and Abihu died *bəhaqrîbām*, when
they brought near strange fire (4). Moses is told *haqrēb*, bring near, the tribe of Levi (6). The
outsider *haqqārēb*, who comes near, is put to death (10). All three forms are in the WLC. Whether
anything is meant by the repetition is a reading, but the unit's question is plainly who may come
near, by whose command.

**Keeping and serving.** The Levites' task is *šāmar … mišmeret*, to keep a charge, and *ʿābōd
ʿăbōdâ*, to serve a service (7–8, WLC). Genesis 2:15 pairs the same two roots, *ləʿobdāh
ûləšomrāh*, for the man in the garden. See [[Work]] and [[The Garden of Eden]]. The wiki notes the
shared pair and draws no conclusion about the design of the sanctuary from it.

**Counted, and appointed.** *Tipqōd* (10), WEB's *appoint*, is the verb of the census, *tipqədû*
at 1:3 (WLC), WEB's *count*. The priests are not numbered in this unit, only
assigned.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **To Aaron, or to Yahweh?** Verse 9 has *nətûnim nətûnim hēmmâ lô*, given to him, and the verse's
   context makes that Aaron. Numbers 8:16 has *lî*, *to me*, in Yahweh's voice. 18:6 holds both, *a
   gift to you, dedicated to Yahweh*. Ancient witnesses are reported to differ at 3:9, but that was
   not checked here. See [[Wholly Given - Netunim in Numbers and the Temple Servants]].
2. **Why Levi?** Exodus 32:26–29 gives Levi a moment of loyalty, and verse 12 gives a different
   account, in which the tribe is simply taken in place of the firstborn, with no merit named. The
   texts do not contradict each other, and neither cites the other. Jacob's curse (Genesis 49:5–7)
   is not mentioned either. See [[Levi]].
3. **Firstborn redeemed, or replaced?** Exodus 13:13 says *you shall redeem* every firstborn son, as
   a standing rule. Verse 12 replaces them with a tribe, once, at a census. 3:46–51 and 18:15–16 will
   show both in play. The text never says how the two relate. See
   [[In Place of All the Firstborn - Exodus 13 and Numbers 3, 8, and 18]].
4. **Which day?** Verse 13 says he made them holy *on the day* he struck Egypt, and 8:17 says the
   same. The command to sanctify them in Exodus 13:2 comes in a following speech that has no date of
   its own. The two can stand together if the claim precedes the command, and neither text addresses
   it.
