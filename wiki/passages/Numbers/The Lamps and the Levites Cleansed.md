# The Lamps and the Levites Cleansed

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 8:1–26

## Summary

The chapter has three speeches of Yahweh to Moses, each opening the same way (1, 5, 23).

The first is about light and is only four verses long. When Aaron sets up the lamps, the seven are to
light the area in front of the lampstand (2). *Aaron did so* (3). This is the first time the Bible
narrates Aaron lighting them. Exodus 30:8 said he would, and at Exodus 40:25 Moses lit them himself.
Verse 4 then recalls what the lampstand is: beaten gold, base to flowers, made by the pattern Yahweh
showed Moses.

The second speech is the long one. The Levites are to be made fit for the Tent (6–19), and verses 20–22
report that it was done. They are sprinkled, shaved over the whole body, and their clothes are washed
(7). All Israel gathers, the children of Israel lay hands on them, and Aaron offers the Levites
themselves before Yahweh as a wave offering (10–11). The Levites then lay hands on two bulls, a sin
offering and a burnt offering, *to make atonement for the Levites* (12). Only after this do they go in
to work (15, 22). The word of the rite is *cleanse*, not *sanctify*. Nobody anoints them, and they
receive no special clothes.

Verses 16–19 give the reason, and it repeats Numbers 3: they are taken instead of the firstborn, who
became Yahweh's on the night he struck Egypt. Then comes something new. The Levites are given to Aaron
*to make atonement for the children of Israel; that there be no plague* when Israel comes near the
sanctuary (19). The people have laid their hands on the Levites, and the Levites now stand between the
people and the danger of the holy place.

The third speech sets the limits of a Levite's working life: in from twenty-five, out at fifty (24–25).
After fifty a man may still *minister with their brothers* and keep watch, but he *shall perform no
service* (26). This is the age Numbers 4 gave as thirty, and the chapter does not comment on the
difference.

## Key verses

> **2** “Speak to Aaron, and tell him, ‘When you light the lamps, the seven lamps shall give light in front of the lamp stand.’”

> **3** Aaron did so. He lit its lamps to light the area in front of the lamp stand, as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **4** This was the workmanship of the lamp stand, beaten work of gold. From its base to its flowers, it was beaten work: according to the pattern which Yahweh had shown Moses, so he made the lamp stand.

> **7** You shall do this to them, to cleanse them: sprinkle the water of cleansing on them, let them shave their whole bodies with a razor, and let them wash their clothes, and cleanse themselves.

> **10** You shall present the Levites before Yahweh. The children of Israel shall lay their hands on the Levites,

> **11** and Aaron shall offer the Levites before Yahweh for a wave offering, on the behalf of the children of Israel, that it may be theirs to do the service of Yahweh.

> **12** “The Levites shall lay their hands on the heads of the bulls, and you shall offer the one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering to Yahweh, to make atonement for the Levites.

> **16** For they are wholly given to me from among the children of Israel; instead of all who open the womb, even the firstborn of all the children of Israel, I have taken them to me.

> **19** I have given the Levites as a gift to Aaron and to his sons from among the children of Israel, to do the service of the children of Israel in the Tent of Meeting, and to make atonement for the children of Israel; that there be no plague among the children of Israel, when the children of Israel come near to the sanctuary.”

> **21** The Levites purified themselves from sin, and they washed their clothes; and Aaron offered them for a wave offering before Yahweh; and Aaron made atonement for them to cleanse them.

> **24** “This is that which belongs to the Levites: from twenty-five years old and upward they shall go in to wait on the service in the work of the Tent of Meeting;

> **25** and from the age of fifty years they shall cease waiting on the work, and shall serve no more,

> **26** but shall minister with their brothers in the Tent of Meeting, to perform the duty, and shall perform no service. You shall do thus to the Levites concerning their duties.”

## Notes

**Why the lamps come here.** The chapter does not say why four verses on the lamps stand before the
Levites. Numbers 7 ends with Moses inside the Tent, and the lampstand is also inside, so the move is
easy to follow. *Yahweh spoke to Moses* (1) could be taken as what the voice of 7:89 said, but the
formula is the ordinary one and nothing ties the two together. The lamps and the Levites are both things
Aaron does *before Yahweh*, and verses 3 and 20–22 close on the same obedience wording. That link is the
wiki's.

**To make the lamps go up.** *Bəhaʿălōtəkā* (2, WLC) and *heʿĕlâ* (3) are hiphil forms of *ʿālâ*, *go
up*. WEB's *light* gives the sense, though the verb itself is *cause to go up*. The tags put this verb with
*nēr*, *lamp*, at Exodus 25:37; 27:20; 30:8; 40:4, 25; Leviticus 24:2; and Numbers 8:2–3. The same verb
is used for offering up a burnt offering. The wiki notes the shared verb and builds nothing on it, since *go up* is common.

**In front of the lampstand.** *ʾEl mûl pənê hammənôrâ* (2, 3, WLC). Exodus 25:37 had said the lamps were
to light the space in front of it, so this adds no new rule. The new thing is that Aaron is told and
that he does it. The text gives no reason for the direction, and nothing in Numbers says what the light
fell on, though Exodus 26:35 puts the table opposite.

**Who made it?** *Kēn ʿāśâ*, *so he made* (4, WLC), has no named subject. Exodus 37:1, 17 runs the
making under Bezalel. Verse 4 may mean Bezalel, or Moses as the one responsible, or be impersonal. *Pattern*
here is *mar'eh*, *appearance*, and not the *tabnît* of Exodus 25:9, 40. *Miqšâ*, *beaten work*, is
tagged in the Torah for the cherubim (Exodus 25:18; 37:7), the lampstand, and the silver trumpets
(Numbers 10:2), the next metal object in the book.

**Cleanse, not sanctify.** *Ṭāhēr* comes at verses 6, 7, 15, and 21. *Qādaš* comes only at verse 17, of
the firstborn. In the tags no verse joins *māšaḥ*, *anoint*, with the Levites anywhere in the Hebrew Bible.
See [[Cleansed, Not Anointed - The Levites of Numbers 8 and the Priests of Leviticus 8]]. Nothing suggests
the Levites were guilty of any particular thing. Verse 21 says *purified themselves from sin*, but the
word is the one used for clearing corpse uncleanness at Numbers 19:12 and 31:19.

**The water.** *Mê ḥaṭṭāʾt* (7, WLC) is literally *water of sin* or *of sin offering*. WEB's *water of
cleansing* gives the function. This exact pair is tagged only here. Numbers 19:9 has a near relative,
*ləmê niddâ ḥaṭṭāʾt hîʾ*, and 19:18–19 is the nearest parallel in the Torah for water sprinkled on people
to cleanse them. Numbers 8 gives no recipe. Whether it means the water of the red heifer, which the
book has not yet described, cannot be settled from this verse.

**Razor.** See [[A Razor over All Their Flesh - Leviticus 14, Numbers 6, and Numbers 8]]. Verse 7 is
written with the Levites as the subject, *they shall pass a razor over all their flesh*. Moses sprinkles,
and they do the rest.

**Hands.** The verb is *sāmak* at verses 10 and 12, the verb of the offerer's hand at Leviticus 1:4. Israel
puts hands on the Levites, Aaron presents them as Israel's offering, and the Levites put their hands on
the bulls. The chain is plain in the text, though it does not explain it. Verse 10 says *on the Levites*
and does not mention heads, and it does not say how a whole congregation did this. Elsewhere the wiki has
called verse 10 an appointment; the sacrificial sense fits at least as well. See
[[Hands on the Head - The First Blessing by Touch]].

**Men as a wave offering.** *Tənûpâ* occurs at verses 11, 13, 15, and 21. Everywhere else in the tags it
is an animal part, grain, oil, metal, or Yahweh's action against nations. Nowhere else is it people. See
[[The Levites as a Wave Offering - Tenufah from the Breast to the Tribe]]. The text does not say what was
physically done with them. Waving a tribe cannot have been done as a breast is waved, so the word is either
a gesture adapted or a term used for a status, and the wiki leaves that open.

**Atonement in both directions.** Verse 12 has atonement made *for* the Levites by bulls and verse 21 by
Aaron. Verse 19 has the Levites given *to make atonement for the children of Israel*. How they do this is
not said. The clause that follows gives the result, *no plague*, *negep*, when Israel comes near. That is
close to what Numbers 1:53 said of the camp cordon: *that there may be no wrath*. So the most the text
allows is that their presence and service protect, and it does not say they offer anything for Israel.
*Negep* also stands at Exodus 12:13 and 30:12, the night of the blood and the census ransom, which Exodus
30:15–16 calls atonement money. At Numbers 17:11–12 (WEB 16:46–47) it is the plague Aaron stops. These
links are from the tags and the reading of them is the wiki's.

**Five times.** Verse 19 says *children of Israel* five times in one verse. The Levites are given from them,
serve for them, atone for them, and protect them, and the verse ends with them coming near. The Levites
are mentioned once.

**Fifty.** *Yāšûb*, *he shall return* or *turn back* from the service (25, WLC). The Levite law is the
only rule the wiki has read so far that ends a man's work at an age. Verse 26 keeps him in the Tent:
*wəšērēt*, *minister*, the verb of 1:50 and 3:6, and *lišmōr mišmeret*, *to keep the charge*, while
*ʿăbōdâ*, *service*, stops. So the chapter treats *charge* and *service* as different things, though it
does not define them. Readers often take the first as guard duty and the second as labor. That report
is unchecked here. Numbers 3:7–8 and 18:3–4 use the same word for the Levites' *duty*.

**Unnamed houses.** Numbers 3–4 and 7 sort the Levites into Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. This chapter does
not. The cleansing is done to the tribe as one.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **When did this happen?** The unit has no date. The Levites are already given at 3:6–9 and 3:11–13,
   counted in chapters 3–4, and handed wagons at 7:5–9, which the book places in the first month. Yet
   here they do not *go in* to their service until they are cleansed (15, 22). Either the chapter comes
   earlier than its place in the book, or the wagon verses describe an arrangement before the men took
   up the work. The text does not say which.
2. **Thirty or twenty-five?** Numbers 4:3 and following against 8:24. See
   [[Thirty, Twenty-Five, and Twenty - The Age of Levite Service]].
3. **Who waves them?** Verse 11 and verse 21 say Aaron does. Verses 13 and 15 say *you*, that is Moses.
   The likely answer is that Moses is responsible and Aaron acts, but the verses do not say so.
4. **Whose are they?** Verse 14 *mine*, verse 16 *to me*, verse 19 *to Aaron*. This is the chain described
   in [[Wholly Given - Netunim in Numbers and the Temple Servants]]; it is resolved there as far as the
   texts allow.
5. **Which water?** See *The water* above.
