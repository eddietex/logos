# The Passover in the Second Year

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 9:1–14

## Summary

The unit goes back in time. It is dated to the first month of the second year (1), a month before the
census that opened the book (1:1). Yahweh tells Moses that Israel is to keep the Passover at its appointed
time, on the fourteenth at evening, by all its statutes (2–3). Moses tells the people, and they keep it in
the wilderness of Sinai, as Yahweh commanded (4–5). This is the first Passover the Bible narrates since
the night in Egypt.

Then a problem comes up. Some men are unclean from a dead body and cannot keep it on that day (6). They do
not ask to be excused. They come to Moses and Aaron and ask why they should be *kept back* from offering
*the offering of Yahweh* with the rest of Israel (7). Moses has no ruling, and he says so: *Wait, that I
may hear what Yahweh will command concerning you* (8).

The answer is wider than the question (9–14). It covers anyone unclean by a dead body, adds anyone on a
long journey, and holds for later generations (10). They keep the Passover a month later, on the fourteenth
of the second month, by the same rules (11–12). The new date is for those who could not come. It is no
excuse for those who could: the clean man who is not travelling and does not keep it is cut off and bears
his sin (13). The stranger living among them may keep it too, under the same statute (14).

So a law comes into being because a few people objected to being left out. The unit does not report
whether these men did keep the second-month Passover.

## Notes

**Why it comes here.** Verse 1 is earlier than 1:1, so this unit is out of date order, as Numbers 7 is. The
text gives no reason. The next dated event is the march at 10:11, and the second-month date of verse 11 falls
six days before it, so the placing may keep the Passover beside the departure. That is the wiki's inference.

**Kept at Sinai.** Exodus 12:25 and 13:5 speak of keeping this service once Israel has come to the land.
Here Yahweh orders it in the wilderness, and nothing in the unit addresses the difference. See
[[Kept in the Wilderness - The Passovers of Egypt, Sinai, and Gilgal]].

**What is not mentioned.** Verses 1–5 name no lamb, no blood, no doorpost, no priest, no Levite. *According
to all its statutes* (3) covers them without detail. How the Exodus rite worked in tents, with blood for
*the two door posts* (Exodus 12:7), the text does not say. Verse 11 repeats the unleavened bread and bitter
herbs and verse 12 the morning and the bones, while the roasting and the seven days go unmentioned.

**At evening.** *Bên hāʿarbayim* (3, 5, 11, WLC), the Passover time at Exodus 12:6 and Leviticus 23:5. The
tags also put it at the daily evening lamb (Exodus 29:39, 41; Numbers 28:4, 8), the lighting of the lamps
(Exodus 30:8), and the quail (Exodus 16:12).

**Bitter herbs.** The tags have *mərōrîm* three times: Exodus 12:8, Numbers 9:11, and Lamentations 3:15,
where it is not a food law. *Passover* with *appointed time*, *môʿēd*, in one verse is tagged at Numbers 9:2,
9:13, and Deuteronomy 16:6.

**A dead body is a *nepeš*.** The word in verses 6, 7, and 10 is *nepeš*, usually *soul* or *person*; verses
6–7 have *nepeš ʾādām* (WLC), and WEB's *dead body* gives the sense. In verse 13 the same word is the person
cut off: *that soul*. The law does not need the two uses to mean the same thing, and the wiki builds nothing
on it. The rules for this uncleanness come at Numbers 19:11–22, with seven days and the water. Numbers 9
gives none of them.

**Who had touched the dead?** The text does not say. The nearest reported deaths are Nadab and Abihu, whose
bodies [[Mishael (son of Uzziel)]] and [[Elzaphan (son of Uzziel)]] carried out (Leviticus 10:4–5). If those
deaths fell on the eighth day of Leviticus 9:1, and that day was near the eighth of the month, then seven days
of uncleanness would reach about the Passover. Each step there is an assumption, Leviticus 10 has no date, and
*carried them in their coats* may not mean touch. The arithmetic is the wiki's own and identifies no one.

**Not guilt.** The men say *we are unclean* and do not call it sin. They had probably done something
required: someone has to bury the dead. Their objection works because uncleanness is a condition and not an
offense, as [[Clean and Unclean]] tracks. Yahweh's answer accepts this by giving them a date, and it does not
ask how they became unclean.

**Kept back.** *Lāmmâ niggāraʿ* (7, WLC), *why are we reduced*, or *cut short*. The verb *gāraʿ* is the common
*take away*, but the tags join it with *lāmmâ*, *why*, in two verses only: here and Numbers 27:4, where the
daughters of Zelophehad ask why their father's name should be *taken away*. Both are protests against being
left out, and in both Moses goes to Yahweh and the law grows. See
[[Wait, That I May Hear - The Cases Moses Took to Yahweh]].

**Coming near.** The men *came near*, *wayyiqrəbû*, to Moses (6), because they cannot *bring near*, *haqrîb*,
Yahweh's *qorbān* (7). All three are from one root (*qrb*). Verse 13 repeats it, *lōʾ hiqrîb*. The wiki notes
the wordplay as its own observation. Exodus 12:48 uses the same verb for the stranger, *then let him come
near*.

**The offering of Yahweh.** *Qorban YHWH* (7, 13) is the phrase of Leviticus 1:2. By the tags, verse 13 is
the only verse in the Hebrew Bible with both *qorbān* and *pesaḥ*. Exodus 12:27 had called the Passover a
*zebaḥ*. So here it is named in the language of the altar laws, though the unit does not say what happened
at the altar.

**Moses does not know.** *ʿImdû wəʾešməʿâ* (8), literally *stand and let me hear*. The man who has received
every law so far says he has no answer yet, and the unit shows him asking without any recorded speech. How he
heard is not stated. Numbers 7:89 would suggest the Tent, but this unit does not say so.

**Far.** *Bəderek rəḥōqâ* (10, WLC), *on a far journey*. In the WLC the last letter of *rəḥōqâ* carries a
dot above it, a scribal mark the text does not explain; the wiki reports it and takes nothing from it. Verse
13 says only *on a journey*. How far is *far* is not defined. Israel at Sinai was one camp, so this clause and
*your generations* (10) look past the present moment. It reads most easily with a central place of sacrifice,
as in Deuteronomy 16:2, 5–7, which does not repeat the second date. That link is an inference.

**Cut off.** *Wəniḵrətâ hannepeš hahiwʾ mēʿammêhā* (13, WLC). At Exodus 12:15 and 19 the same penalty was
for eating leaven, and here it is for leaving the Passover undone. Here it comes with the phrase *ḥeṭʾô
yiśśāʾ*, *shall bear his sin*, as at Leviticus 24:15, in the case of the blasphemer. The text does not say
what *cut off* consists of. See [[Sin]].

**The stranger.** Verse 14 opens with the same words as Exodus 12:48, *wəkî-yāgûr ʾittəḵā gēr wəʿāśâ pesaḥ*,
with *you* now plural. WEB's *desires to keep* renders the verb *ʿāśâ*, *and he does*; *desire* is a
translator's gloss on a conditional clause. Exodus 12:48 adds circumcision, and Numbers 9:14 does not mention
it. See [[One Law for the Native and the Stranger]]; that page reads the silence as no repeal. *Native of the
land*, *ʾezraḥ hāʾāreṣ*, is said at Sinai, by a people with no land yet. By the tags, these are the only two
verses with *gēr* and *pesaḥ*.

**After this.** Hezekiah's Passover falls in the second month, with people eating unclean. See
[[The Second Month - Numbers 9 and Hezekiah's Passover]]. At John 11:55 people go up early to purify themselves
before the Passover, and at John 18:28 the accusers stay out of the Praetorium *that they might not be
defiled, but might eat the Passover*. Both assume the rule that uncleanness keeps a person from the meal.
Whether John has Numbers 9 in mind is not stated. The Greek of those verses was not checked.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Where were they living?** Numbers 5:2 sends the unclean by the dead out of the camp, and here such men
   come before Moses and Aaron. The text does not say from where. See
   [[Who Is Put Out of the Camp - Numbers 5 and the Purity Laws]].
2. **Exodus 12:25 and 13:5 against Numbers 9:2.** Land, or wilderness. See above.
3. **Exodus 12:47 against this law.** *All the congregation of Israel shall keep it* has no exception, and
   Numbers 9 now provides a later date for some. It adds to the law and does not revoke it.
4. **Circumcision.** Exodus 12:48 requires it and Numbers 9:14 is silent. Joshua 5:5 complicates the wilderness
   years; see [[Kept in the Wilderness - The Passovers of Egypt, Sinai, and Gilgal]].
5. **Did they keep it?** The law is given, and no second-month Passover is reported at Sinai.
