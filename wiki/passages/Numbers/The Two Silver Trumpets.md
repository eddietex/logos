# The Two Silver Trumpets

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 10:1–10

## Summary

This is the last thing given at Sinai before the camp moves. Yahweh tells Moses to make two trumpets of beaten
silver, for calling the congregation and for moving the camps (2).

The unit then works as a code. Both trumpets blown bring the whole congregation to the door of the Tent (3). One
trumpet brings only the princes (4). An alarm sets the eastern camps moving (5), and a second alarm the southern
camps (6). For an assembly they blow, but do not sound an alarm (7). So the camp can now receive four orders by
ear, and the people have to know which is which.

Verse 8 settles who holds the instruments: the sons of Aaron, the priests, by a statute forever. The last two
verses then look past the wilderness. In a war *in your land*, an alarm on the trumpets means Israel will be
remembered before Yahweh and saved from its enemies (9). On days of gladness, set feasts, and the first of each
month, the trumpets are blown over the burnt offerings and peace offerings, as a memorial before God (10). The unit
closes with *I am Yahweh your God*.

A tool made for a marching camp is therefore also given a place in the wars and worship of a settled people, who
do not yet exist. The next verse (10:11) has the cloud rise, and the journey starts.

## Key verses

> **2** “Make two trumpets of silver. You shall make them of beaten work. You shall use them for the calling of the congregation, and for the journeying of the camps.

> **3** When they blow them, all the congregation shall gather themselves to you at the door of the Tent of Meeting.

> **4** If they blow just one, then the princes, the heads of the thousands of Israel, shall gather themselves to you.

> **7** But when the assembly is to be gathered together, you shall blow, but you shall not sound an alarm.

> **8** “The sons of Aaron, the priests, shall blow the trumpets. This shall be to you for a statute forever throughout your generations.

> **9** When you go to war in your land against the adversary who oppresses you, then you shall sound an alarm with the trumpets. Then you will be remembered before Yahweh your God, and you will be saved from your enemies.

> **10** “Also in the day of your gladness, and in your set feasts, and in the beginnings of your months, you shall blow the trumpets over your burnt offerings, and over the sacrifices of your peace offerings; and they shall be to you for a memorial before your God. I am Yahweh your God.”

## Notes

**Which word.** *Ḥăṣōṣərâ*, here rendered *trumpet* (2, 8, 9, 10; WLC), is not the *šôpār* of Sinai, of Leviticus 25:9,
or of Jericho. WEB also uses *trumpet* for the *šôpār*, so the English hides the difference. The wiki reads the
*ḥăṣōṣərâ* as a made metal instrument because verse 2 says it is one. By the morphology tags it occurs 29 times in
27 verses, and in the Torah only here and at Numbers 31:6. See
[[Trumpet and Ram's Horn - Two Instruments, One English Word]].

**The verb of the unit.** *Tāqaʿ*, *blow*, comes eight times (3, 4, 5, 6 twice, 7, 8, 10), and *tərûʿâ*, *alarm*,
three times (5, 6 twice), with the verb *hērîaʿ*, *sound an alarm*, twice (7, 9; WLC, by the tags).
The text does not describe the sounds. What it shows is that two kinds of signal existed and that they were
distinct: verse 7 says *tiṯqəʿû wəlōʾ ṯārîʿû*, *you shall blow, but you shall not sound an alarm* (WLC). How the
two differed, in pitch or rhythm, the unit does not say, and the wiki does not guess. Joel 2 keeps the same
distinction on a different instrument. Joel 2:1 has both verbs for the alarm of the day of Yahweh, and 2:15, calling a
solemn assembly, has *tāqaʿ* alone (WLC). The comparison is the wiki's.

**Make for yourself.** *ʿĂśē ləḵā* (2) is singular, and so are *ləḵā*, *to you*, in the same verse and *ʾēleḵā* in
verses 3–4 (WLC). The making is laid on Moses, and the assembly gathers to him. The address turns plural at verse 5.
No maker is named, and no verse reports them being made. By verse 8 the blowing passes to the priests.

**Beaten work.** *Miqšâ* (2) is the word for the cherubim and the lampstand (Exodus 25:18, 31; Numbers 8:4). By
the tags it stands in the Torah only for those and these trumpets, and elsewhere only at Jeremiah 10:5. Those are
gold, these are silver, and the text does not explain why. The
atonement money of Exodus 30:16 is silver and is also called a *memorial*, but nothing here says the trumpets came
from it, so the wiki does not connect them as fact.

**Heads of the thousands.** *Rāʾšê ʾalpê yiśrāʾēl* (4) repeats Numbers 1:16 exactly (WLC). The men who carried the
census are the ones a single trumpet calls.

**Two trumpets, two sons.** At this date Aaron has two living sons (3:4), and there are two trumpets. The text
does not link them, and verse 8 is a statute for every generation of priests, so the wiki notes only the fit. 1
Chronicles 16:6 does later name two priests, Benaiah and Jahaziel, with trumpets continually before the ark.

**The Hebrew stops at the south.** Verses 5–6 give alarms for the east and the south, then stop, and the masoretic
text (WLC) has no west or north. The Greek of Rahlfs's Septuagint adds a third alarm for the camps *παρὰ θάλασσαν*
and a fourth for those *πρὸς βορρᾶν*, west and north, in verse 6. The wiki has compared Rahlfs's text and not the
Greek manuscripts, and does not decide whether the Greek preserves a longer text or fills out a list. One reading of
the Hebrew is possible from the march itself: the order at 10:14–25 is Judah, then the tabernacle, then Reuben, the
sanctuary, Ephraim, and Dan, which would leave the later camps to follow in turn. The unit does not say so.

**No trumpet on the march.** The first departure (10:11–28) names the cloud, the command, and the order of the
standards. It does not mention a trumpet, and neither does the rest of the book's journey narrative. The word returns in
Numbers only at 29:1 in WEB, where the Hebrew has no instrument word, and at 31:6. The wiki does not take that
silence as non-use, but the unit's main purpose is not narrated in operation.

**Sign and sound.** [[The Cloud over the Tabernacle]] ended with the question of why a visible sign needed Moses.
This unit gives one partial answer. A cloud can be seen by anyone who looks, but it cannot tell a tribe that its
turn has come, and it cannot call the princes without the people. The trumpet turns a sign from Yahweh into a
public order given in sequence. That is the wiki's reading. The text only sets the two units side by side.

**You will be remembered.** *Wənizkartem*, tagged niphal, says Israel *will be remembered* (9, WLC). The verse does not
say that God is otherwise forgetting. Of the 20 niphal tags for this verb, this is the only finite form with the
plural *you* as subject. The next verb, *wənôšaʿtem*, *you will be saved*, is also niphal, and the agent of both is left
unstated, though *before Yahweh your God* makes the sense plain. Verse 10 uses the noun: the trumpets are
*ləziḵḵārôn*, *for a memorial*, before God. Exodus 28:12, 29 has the same idea in stones carried on Aaron's shoulders
and heart, before Yahweh. The wiki reads these together: a human action is put before God, and the verse expects him to act. The Greek of
Rahlfs carries the link across with *ἀναμνησθήσεσθε* (9) and *ἀνάμνησις* (10). See
[[God Remembered - Noah, Abraham, Rachel, and Israel]].

**Gladness first.** The list in verse 10 begins with *yôm śimḥatḵem*, *the day of your gladness* (WLC), before the
fixed feasts and new moons. It is the only item not tied to a calendar. No verse in the unit says what days it means,
though the wiki notes that a return from war sits naturally here, as at 2 Chronicles 20:27–28. The same instruments
that sound in danger are blown in joy.

**The adversary.** *Haṣṣar haṣṣōrēr ʾetḵem* (9, WLC) sets two near-identical words side by side, which WEB gives as
*the adversary who oppresses you*. As worded, the verse has someone else pressing on Israel in its own land.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **In your land.** Verse 9 frames the alarm for a war in the land, and verse 10 likewise assumes settled feasts.
   Numbers 31:6 takes the trumpets to war against Midian, before the crossing. The verse need not be a limit, but the
   text does not say so.
2. **Who blows.** Verse 8 says the priests, as a statute. Verses 3, 4, and the end of 6 have an unspecified *they*, and
   verses 5, 6, 7, 9, and 10 a plural *you*. Later, 2 Kings 11:14 and 2 Chronicles 23:13 have the people of the land blowing
   *ḥăṣōṣərôt* (WLC). The wiki does not resolve this. See
   [[The Priests' Trumpets - Numbers 10 from the Camp to the Second Temple]].
3. **Which instrument at the new moon.** Verse 10 has *ḥăṣōṣərôt* at the beginnings of months. Psalm 81:3 (81:4 in the
   Hebrew) has *šôpār* at the new moon (WLC). Both can be true of different settings, but neither text mentions the other.
4. **What the alarm is for.** It sets out camps (5–6) and also brings Israel to mind before Yahweh (9). The unit uses
   the same word, *tərûʿâ* or its verb, for both, and does not relate them.
