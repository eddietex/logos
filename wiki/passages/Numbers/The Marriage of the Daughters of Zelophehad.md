# The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 36:1–13

## Summary

The heads of Gilead's households bring Moses and Israel's princes a consequence of the
daughters' inheritance ruling: if the women marry into another tribe, their land will leave
the ancestral allotment. The heads say even Jubilee will leave that loss in place.

Moses affirms the concern according to Yahweh's word. Zelophehad's daughters may choose whom
they think best, within their father's tribal family; the rule applies to every daughter
possessing an inheritance. Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Noah marry their paternal
cousins, and their inheritance remains in Manasseh. The final verse closes Numbers with
the commandments given through Moses in the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho.

## Key verses

Copied verbatim from stored WEB, raw/bible/Numbers.md.

> **1** The heads of the fathers’ households of the family of the children of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, of the families of the sons of Joseph, came near, and spoke before Moses, and before the princes, the heads of the fathers’ households of the children of Israel.

> **2** They said, “Yahweh commanded my lord to give the land for inheritance by lot to the children of Israel. My lord was commanded by Yahweh to give the inheritance of Zelophehad our brother to his daughters.

> **3** If they are married to any of the sons of the other tribes of the children of Israel, then will their inheritance be taken away from the inheritance of our fathers, and will be added to the inheritance of the tribe to which they shall belong. So will it be taken away from the lot of our inheritance.

> **4** When the jubilee of the children of Israel shall be, then will their inheritance be added to the inheritance of the tribe to which they shall belong. So their inheritance will be taken away from the inheritance of the tribe of our fathers.”

> **5** Moses commanded the children of Israel according to Yahweh’s word, saying, “The tribe of the sons of Joseph speaks right.

> **6** This is the thing which Yahweh does command concerning the daughters of Zelophehad, saying, ‘Let them be married to whom they think best; only they shall marry into the family of the tribe of their father.

> **7** So shall no inheritance of the children of Israel move from tribe to tribe; for the children of Israel shall all keep the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers.

> **8** Every daughter who possesses an inheritance in any tribe of the children of Israel shall be wife to one of the family of the tribe of her father, that the children of Israel may each possess the inheritance of his fathers.

> **9** So shall no inheritance move from one tribe to another tribe; for the tribes of the children of Israel shall each keep his own inheritance.’”

> **10** The daughters of Zelophehad did as Yahweh commanded Moses:

> **11** for Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Noah, the daughters of Zelophehad, were married to their father’s brothers’ sons.

> **12** They were married into the families of the sons of Manasseh the son of Joseph. Their inheritance remained in the tribe of the family of their father.

> **13** These are the commandments and the ordinances which Yahweh commanded by Moses to the children of Israel in the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho.

## Notes

**A right retained and a choice constrained.** Numbers 27:7–8 grants the daughters their
father's inheritance. Numbers 36:6–9 does not take it away; it limits the marriages through
which it might leave the tribe. Any earlier synthesis implying unrestricted marriage for
heiresses must be qualified by this passage. The text affirms both petitions but protects
tribal continuity by imposing the new restriction on the women.

**The rule and its fulfillment.** Verse 8 concerns daughters who possess an inheritance.
Verse 11 reports these sisters marrying first cousins; it does not require every heiress
to marry a first cousin rather than another member of the father's tribal family. No
general prohibition on all intertribal marriage is stated.

**Jubilee is part of the problem presented.** Verse 4 is the household heads' argument.
Leviticus 25:13, 28 restores possession after sale; it does not expressly address the tribal
affiliation of an heiress who marries. The distinction explains why a further provision is
needed without pretending either text supplies a complete legal account of every case.

**A legal ending.** Numbers ends with an obedient family preserving its inheritance,
not a conquest scene. The final location repeats the book's Moab setting. The future land
is governed before the narrative reports the western allotment.

**Names and scope.** The five sisters continue on their collective person page, and the
Gileadite household heads on the existing family register. The ancestors named in verse 1
are not new living participants. No new pericope boundary or individual biography is needed.
