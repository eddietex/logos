# The Cloud over the Tabernacle

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 9:15–23

## Summary

The unit goes back to the day the tabernacle was raised. The cloud covered it, and from evening until
morning there was something over it like fire (15). This did not stop: *so it was continually* (16).

Then the text sets out how Israel moved. When the cloud was taken up from the Tent, the people set out, and
where it settled, they camped (17). The cloud is what the people see, but the text says the order came from
Yahweh: *at the commandment of Yahweh* they travelled and camped (18). The rest of the unit goes through the
possible lengths of a stop. It could be many days (19), a few days (20), one night (21), a day and a night
(21), two days, a month, or longer (22). Whatever the length, the rule held. The people stayed while the cloud
stayed, and left when it rose.

The last verse does not mention the cloud. It says only that they camped and travelled at Yahweh's command,
kept his charge, and did so *by Moses* (23).

Nobody moves in this unit. Israel is still at Sinai, and the first departure comes at 10:11. So this is a
description of how every journey would work, given before the first one starts. It has no event, no speech,
and no named place.

## Key verses

> **15** On the day that the tabernacle was raised up, the cloud covered the tabernacle, even the Tent of the Testimony: and at evening it was over the tabernacle, as it were the appearance of fire, until morning.

> **16** So it was continually. The cloud covered it, and the appearance of fire by night.

> **17** Whenever the cloud was taken up from over the Tent, then after that the children of Israel traveled; and in the place where the cloud remained, there the children of Israel encamped.

> **18** At the commandment of Yahweh, the children of Israel traveled, and at the commandment of Yahweh they encamped. As long as the cloud remained on the tabernacle they remained encamped.

> **19** When the cloud stayed on the tabernacle many days, then the children of Israel kept Yahweh’s command, and didn’t travel.

> **21** Sometimes the cloud was from evening until morning; and when the cloud was taken up in the morning, they traveled: or by day and by night, when the cloud was taken up, they traveled.

> **22** Whether it was two days, or a month, or a year that the cloud stayed on the tabernacle, remaining on it, the children of Israel remained encamped, and didn’t travel; but when it was taken up, they traveled.

> **23** At the commandment of Yahweh they encamped, and at the commandment of Yahweh they traveled. They kept Yahweh’s command, at the commandment of Yahweh by Moses.

## Notes

**Verse 15 again.** *Bəyôm hāqîm ʾet-hammiškān* (WLC) goes back to the raising dated at Exodus 40:17, the
first of the first month. Numbers has now returned to that time at 7:1, 9:1, and here. *On the day* need not
be pressed to one day (see Numbers 7:84 at
[[On the Day Moses Finished - Exodus 40, Leviticus 8-9, and Numbers 1, 7, and 9]]), but here the event it
points to is the single one at Exodus 40:34.

**This is Exodus 40 expanded.** Exodus 40:36–38 gave the rule in three verses: cloud taken up, people travel;
cloud not taken up, people do not; cloud and fire in sight of all Israel. Numbers takes nine. *Taken up* is the
same verb in both, and in the same niphal infinitive form at Exodus 40:37 and Numbers 9:22 (*hēʿālōtô*, WLC).
What Numbers adds is the lengths of stay and the phrase *at the commandment of Yahweh*. Exodus does not use
that phrase here, and Exodus says the fire was *in* the cloud (40:38), where Numbers says *as the appearance of
fire* over the tabernacle.

**Habitual verbs.** After the perfect *kissâ*, *covered*, in verse 15, the morphology tags mark the verbs
for *was* and *covered* in verses 15–16 and for *travelled* at verse 17 as imperfects (WLC). English
translations can put these in the past. Read with *so it was continually*, the sense is what used to happen
again and again. The perfect comes back in verse 23, *šāmārû*, *they kept*. So the unit ends with a report,
not a rule: over the whole journey, they did this. Whether that is true of the whole journey is a question
the book raises itself, since Numbers goes on to record refusals.

**The mouth of Yahweh.** WEB's *at the commandment of Yahweh* is *ʿal-pî YHWH*, literally *at the mouth of
Yahweh* (WLC). It occurs seven times here: twice each in verses 18 and 20, three times in 23. The wiki has
counted these in the tags and draws no meaning from the number seven. Chapters 3 and 4 used the phrase for the
counting of the Levites (3:16, WEB *according to Yahweh's word*), and it returns at 10:13 and 33:2, 38. A cloud
has no mouth, and that is the odd thing about using this phrase here. No word is reported. The people see a
cloud, and the text calls that being spoken to.

**Words of the unit.** By the tags, *ʿānān*, *cloud*, comes eleven times, *nāsaʿ*, *travel*, nine, and *ḥānâ*,
*camp*, six (WLC). Verse 23 keeps the two verbs and loses the cloud. The unit has no *ʿammûd*, *pillar*, and no
*kābôd*, *glory*. See [[Over the Tent and Before the People - Where the Cloud Stood]].

**Settled.** *Šāḵan* (17, 18, 22) is the verb behind *miškān*, *tabernacle*, a link the Hebrew shows and WEB
does not. The verb was used of the glory on Sinai (Exodus 24:16) and of the cloud on the Tent (Exodus 40:35).
Here it is what the cloud does at each stop.

**Testimony.** *ʾŌhel hāʿēdut*, *Tent of the Testimony* (15, WLC), is rare. By the tags it stands at this verse,
Numbers 17:22–23 and 18:2 (17:7–8 in WEB numbering), and 2 Chronicles 24:6. Numbers 1:50, 53 and 10:11 use
*miškan hāʿēdut*. So just before the cloud rises at 10:11, the place is named for the tablets inside it. The
wiki notes the fit and does not claim the wording was chosen for it.

**Like fire.** *Kəmarʾēh-ʾēš* (15, WLC). Verse 16 has *marʾēh ʾēš*. The tags join those two words at Exodus 24:17,
where the glory on Sinai looked like devouring fire, and in Ezekiel 1:13, 27, 8:2, and Daniel 10:6. The people
at the camp see every night something of what they saw at the mountain. That link is the wiki's reading. Verse
16 in the Hebrew has no word matching *by day*; the wiki did not compare the ancient versions on this.

**The lengths.** Verse 19 has *yāmîm rabbîm*, *many days*, the phrase used of Kadesh at Deuteronomy 1:46. Verse
20 has *yāmîm mispār*, *days of number*, a few. Verse 22 runs *ʾô yōmayim ʾô ḥōdeš ʾô yāmîm* (WLC). WEB's
*a year* renders *yāmîm*, literally *days*, as it does at Leviticus 25:29, where the same word is WEB's *full
year*. Other readings would leave it as an unspecified longer time, and the wiki does not rest anything on the
year. One night and a long stay get the same treatment, and no verse says why any stop lasted
as long as it did.

**Waiting as obedience.** Verse 19 calls staying put *keeping Yahweh's charge*: *and didn't travel*. The rule is
hardest in the long stop, and verse 19 names that case first. Deuteronomy 1:2 sets the road from Horeb to Kadesh
at eleven days, so the long years ahead are not a matter of distance. Their cause is told at Numbers 14.

**Yahweh's charge.** *Mišmeret YHWH* (19, 23), adjacent, stands in the tags at these two verses, Leviticus 8:35,
1 Kings 2:3, and 2 Chronicles 13:11; 23:6. *Mišmeret* has been the word for the Levites' guard duty (Numbers
1:53; 3:7–8; 8:26). At Leviticus 8:35 Aaron and his sons *keep Yahweh's command* for seven days at the Tent door,
told to stay where they are or die. Now the same phrase is used of all Israel staying where they are. The
comparison is the wiki's. WEB's *command* hides the guard-duty sense of the word.

**By Moses.** *Bəyad-Mōšeh* (23), literally *by the hand of Moses*. With *ʿal-pî YHWH* the phrase closed the
Levite counts at 4:37, 45, and 49, and it comes again at 10:13 (WLC). The cloud is visible *in the sight of all the house of Israel* (Exodus 40:38), so the end of
this unit is unexpected: why would a sign anyone could see need Moses? The text does not say. The next unit
gives two silver trumpets to call the camp to move (10:1–10), and that may be part of how a visible sign became
a public order, but this unit does not make that link. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

**Verse 22 has no failure.** The unit reports no one leaving early or lagging behind. That is its picture of
the arrangement, and it will not survive the book unchanged. Numbers 14:44 has the people going up on their own
with the ark left in camp.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Where the cloud was.** Here it is on the tabernacle, and the Tent travels in the middle of the camp (2:17).
   Exodus 13:21, Numbers 14:14, and Deuteronomy 1:33 have Yahweh going *before* the people in it, and at 10:33–34
   the ark goes ahead while the cloud is *over them*. See
   [[Over the Tent and Before the People - Where the Cloud Stood]].
2. **Who picks the campsite.** The cloud (9:17), the ark (10:33), and Yahweh (Deuteronomy 1:33). Hobab is asked
   to be Israel's *eyes* at 10:31, right after this rule.
3. **Could anyone enter?** Exodus 40:35 says Moses could not enter *because the cloud stayed on it*. Verse 16
   says the cloud covered it continually, and Moses goes in at Numbers 7:89 and Leviticus 9:23. The change is not
   explained. See [[Cloud and Fire - From the Sea to the Filled Tabernacle]].
4. **Numbers 12:10.** The cloud *departed from over the Tent* and the people did not travel (12:15). The verb
   there is *sār*, not the *ʿālâ* of this unit (WLC), so the rule may not be in view; the text does not comment.
