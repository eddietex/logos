# Arad and Hormah

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 21:1–3

## Summary

The Canaanite king of Arad hears that Israel is approaching by the way of Atharim,
attacks, and takes captives. Israel addresses Yahweh collectively, promising to destroy
the enemy's cities if he gives victory. Yahweh hears and delivers the Canaanites;
Israel destroys both the people and their cities, and the place is named Hormah.

The movement is from captivity to petition to victory. Unlike the unauthorized ascent
in Numbers 14:39–45, the outcome is explicitly attributed to Yahweh's response. No battle
tactics, casualty figures, or report of the captives' release is supplied.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Numbers.md`. The complete three-verse unit follows.

> **1** The Canaanite, the king of Arad, who lived in the South, heard that Israel came by the way of Atharim. He fought against Israel, and took some of them captive.

> **2** Israel vowed a vow to Yahweh, and said, “If you will indeed deliver this people into my hand, then I will utterly destroy their cities.”

> **3** Yahweh listened to the voice of Israel, and delivered up the Canaanites; and they utterly destroyed them and their cities. The name of the place was called Hormah.

## Notes

**A vow, not a reported command to attack.** The king initiates hostilities. Israel initiates
the conditional vow; no divine speech commanding it is recorded. The narrator nevertheless
expressly credits Yahweh with hearing and delivering the enemy. This sequence should not
be flattened into either an independent military success or a quoted divine command.

**The extent of destruction.** Verse 2 names the cities in the promise; verse 3 names both
the Canaanites and their cities in its fulfillment. The brief account does not distinguish
combatants from other inhabitants. Its violence remains part of the passage, rather than
being reduced to a metaphor of spiritual victory.

**Two naming accounts remain.** Numbers 14:45 already uses Hormah before Numbers 21:3
explains the name. Judges 1:17 attributes the naming of Zephath as Hormah to Judah and
Simeon. These accounts differ in actors and narrative setting; this page does not decide
whether they describe renewed destruction, different traditions, or different sites.
Nor does Numbers 21:3 explicitly identify Hormah as Arad itself: a king, multiple cities,
and a named place should not be collapsed into one securely located town.

**Scope and sources.** The planned boundary is retained. Numbers and Leviticus comparisons
use stored WEB; Judges 1:16–17 was verified directly against bible-api.com's WEB response.
No source-language claims or modern archaeological identifications are added. Atharim's
meaning and route are left undecided.
