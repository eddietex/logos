# The Second Census

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 26:1–65

## Summary

After the plague, Yahweh commands Moses and Eleazar to count Israel's men eligible for war
from twenty years upward. The census is taken in the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho.
Twelve tribal registers yield 601,730 (1–51). Within the families, the narrator remembers
Dathan and Abiram's rebellion, Korah's death and his sons' survival, Er and Onan's deaths,
Zelophehad's five daughters, and Asher's daughter Serah.

The count anticipates inheritance: larger groups receive more and smaller groups less,
but the land is also divided by lot (52–56). Levi is separately counted from one month upward,
23,000 males, with no inheritance among the other tribes (57–62). The closing comparison with
Sinai reports the death of the former military census generation except Caleb and Joshua
(63–65). This is both a new beginning and a record of judgment.

## Notes

**Scope.** The census is not a total population count. Its military threshold differs from
Levi's one-month threshold. Women appear in the genealogy without being added to the military
total. Named ancestors and retrospective deaths must not be mistaken for people alive in the camp.

**Judgment and continuity.** The old military cohort has died; that statement is narrower than
the death of everyone who left Egypt. Moses remains alive and conducts the count. Korah's sons
survive although chapter 16 uses sweeping language about the rebels' households. The census
preserves this qualification, not an explanation of how they escaped.

**Inheritance.** Proportion and lot are both commanded. The text does not specify a procedure
that reconciles them, so neither instruction should be reduced to the other. Zelophehad's lack
of sons introduces a family situation that the following pericope will address.

**Numbers are not explanations.** Simeon's fall from 59,300 to 22,200 is striking, especially
after the Peor plague and the naming of a Simeonite leader in Numbers 25:14. But neither chapter
assigns the entire decrease to that event. Manasseh now exceeds Ephraim, so the younger brother's
promised greatness in Genesis 48:19 cannot mean numerical superiority at every census.

**Genealogical differences.** Genesis 46:21 lists Ard and Naaman among Benjamin's sons;
Numbers 26:40 places them under Bela. Genesis 46:10 names Jemuel and Zohar in Simeon's line,
where Numbers 26:12–13 has Nemuel and Zerah. These WEB lists differ. This ingestion neither
silently identifies every variant nor invents missing generations to harmonize them.
The family register is preserved in [[The Families of the Second Census]].

are copied from stored WEB. Comparisons use stored Numbers 1, 14, 16, 25; Genesis 46 and 48;
and Exodus 6. No new Hebrew or Greek wording claim or unverified external quotation is made.
