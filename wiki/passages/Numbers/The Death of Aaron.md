# The Death of Aaron

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 20:22–29

## Summary

Israel travels from Kadesh to Mount Hor, at Edom’s border. Yahweh announces that Aaron
will die without entering the land because of the rebellion at Meribah (22–24).
Moses must take Aaron and Eleazar up the mountain and transfer Aaron’s garments to his son.

The ascent occurs before the congregation. Moses clothes Eleazar in Aaron’s garments;
Aaron dies on the summit, and only Moses and Eleazar descend (25–28). All Israel mourns
Aaron for thirty days (29). His exclusion is fulfilled, but the priestly office continues
through his son: judgment on the priest does not abolish provision for the congregation.

> **25** Take Aaron and Eleazar his son, and bring them up to Mount Hor;

> **26** and strip Aaron of his garments, and put them on Eleazar his son. Aaron shall be gathered, and shall die there.”

> **27** Moses did as Yahweh commanded. They went up into Mount Hor in the sight of all the congregation.

> **28** Moses stripped Aaron of his garments, and put them on Eleazar his son. Aaron died there on the top of the mountain, and Moses and Eleazar came down from the mountain.

> **29** When all the congregation saw that Aaron was dead, they wept for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel.

## Notes

### Public succession, limited narration

The congregation sees the ascent (27); the text does not say it watches every act on the
summit. Three ascend and two return. The garments make continuity visible, but this brief
account does not narrate anointing or the seven-day rite prescribed in Exodus 29:29–30.
Their omission is not a statement that those instructions were revoked.

### Judgment does not erase mourning

Verse 24 identifies rebellion at Meribah as the reason Aaron will not enter. Numbers 20:12
had addressed both Moses and Aaron, although only Moses was narrated striking the rock.
This passage confirms shared responsibility without supplying Aaron’s unreported action.
The thirty days of national grief honor the man whose failure has just been recalled.

### What remains unstated

Being gathered to his people (24, 26) accompanies death here; it does not describe who buries
Aaron, where a grave is prepared, or the mechanics of the afterlife. Leviticus 21:10–12
forbids the high priest’s corpse defilement even for parents. Eleazar’s presence raises a
question, but Numbers narrates neither corpse contact nor a burial by him. A violation or
an exemption cannot be asserted from silence. Mount Hor is located only by the narrative’s
relation to Edom, without a modern-site identification.
