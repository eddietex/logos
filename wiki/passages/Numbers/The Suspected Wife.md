# The Suspected Wife

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 5:11–31

## Summary

The law that follows the clearing of the camp turns to something no one can see. Yahweh gives Moses
a case with no witness: a husband suspects his wife, and nobody can show whether she has been
unfaithful. The law states both possibilities side by side (14). She may be defiled, or he may be
jealous of a wife who is not.

The husband brings her to the priest, with an offering of barley meal that has no oil and no
frankincense, *bringing iniquity to memory* (15). What follows happens *before Yahweh* (16, 18). The
priest puts dust from the tabernacle floor into holy water in a clay vessel, loosens the woman's
hair, and puts the offering in her hands while he holds the water (17–18). He puts her under oath in
two halves: if she is innocent, she will be free of the water; if she is guilty, Yahweh will make her
a curse among her people, with her thigh falling away and her body swelling (19–22). She answers
*Amen, Amen* (22). The priest writes the curses, blots them into the water, burns a handful of the
offering, and makes her drink (23–26).

The two outcomes close the case (27–28). A guilty woman suffers what the oath named and becomes a
curse among her people. A woman who is not defiled is free and *shall conceive offspring*. A summary
restates the two triggers (29–30), and the last verse sets the two parties apart: the man is free
from iniquity, and the woman bears hers (31).

No court acts in this passage, and nobody is sentenced. The verdict is left to Yahweh, and the only
human actions are bringing her, swearing, and drinking. The law therefore both exposes a woman to a
frightening public rite and stops a husband's suspicion from being settled by the husband. The text
states neither of those as its purpose. That reading is the wiki's.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Numbers.md`. Complete unit:

> **11** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **12** “Speak to the children of Israel, and tell them: ‘If any man’s wife goes astray, and is unfaithful to him,

> **13** and a man lies with her carnally, and it is hidden from the eyes of her husband, and this is kept concealed, and she is defiled, and there is no witness against her, and she isn’t taken in the act;

> **14** and the spirit of jealousy comes on him, and he is jealous of his wife, and she is defiled: or if the spirit of jealousy comes on him, and he is jealous of his wife, and she isn’t defiled:

> **15** then the man shall bring his wife to the priest, and shall bring her offering for her: one tenth of an ephah of barley meal. He shall pour no oil on it, nor put frankincense on it, for it is a meal offering of jealousy, a meal offering of memorial, bringing iniquity to memory.

> **16** The priest shall bring her near, and set her before Yahweh;

> **17** and the priest shall take holy water in an earthen vessel; and of the dust that is on the floor of the tabernacle the priest shall take, and put it into the water.

> **18** The priest shall set the woman before Yahweh, and let the hair of the woman’s head go loose, and put the meal offering of memorial in her hands, which is the meal offering of jealousy. The priest shall have in his hand the water of bitterness that brings a curse.

> **19** The priest shall cause her to swear, and shall tell the woman, “If no man has lain with you, and if you haven’t gone aside to uncleanness, being under your husband, be free from this water of bitterness that brings a curse.

> **20** But if you have gone astray, being under your husband, and if you are defiled, and some man has lain with you besides your husband:”

> **21** then the priest shall cause the woman to swear with the oath of cursing, and the priest shall tell the woman, “Yahweh make you a curse and an oath among your people, when Yahweh allows your thigh to fall away, and your body to swell;

> **22** and this water that brings a curse will go into your bowels, and make your body swell, and your thigh fall away.” The woman shall say, “Amen, Amen.”

> **23** “‘The priest shall write these curses in a book, and he shall blot them out into the water of bitterness.

> **24** He shall make the woman drink the water of bitterness that causes the curse; and the water that causes the curse shall enter into her and become bitter.

> **25** The priest shall take the meal offering of jealousy out of the woman’s hand, and shall wave the meal offering before Yahweh, and bring it to the altar.

> **26** The priest shall take a handful of the meal offering, as its memorial, and burn it on the altar, and afterward shall make the woman drink the water.

> **27** When he has made her drink the water, then it shall happen, if she is defiled, and has committed a trespass against her husband, that the water that causes the curse will enter into her and become bitter, and her body will swell, and her thigh will fall away: and the woman will be a curse among her people.

> **28** If the woman isn’t defiled, but is clean; then she shall be free, and shall conceive offspring.

> **29** “‘This is the law of jealousy, when a wife, being under her husband, goes astray, and is defiled;

> **30** or when the spirit of jealousy comes on a man, and he is jealous of his wife; then he shall set the woman before Yahweh, and the priest shall execute on her all this law.

> **31** The man shall be free from iniquity, and that woman shall bear her iniquity.’”

## Notes

**Where this law starts.** Verse 13 piles up what is missing: *hidden from the eyes of her husband*,
*kept concealed*, *no witness against her*, *she isn't taken in the act*. The laws of Leviticus
20:10 and Deuteronomy 22:22 assume a case that has been found out, and Deuteronomy 19:15 wants two
or three witnesses. This one begins where those cannot. See
[[No Witness Against Her - Numbers 5, Exodus 22, and Deuteronomy 19]].

**Two cases, one rite.** Verse 14 gives the same jealousy twice and changes only the last clause:
*and she is defiled*, *and she isn't defiled*. Verses 29–30 repeat the division. The law allows from
the start that the husband may be wrong, and that a *spirit of jealousy* (*rûaḥ qinʾâ*, 14, 30; WLC)
is no evidence. *Qinʾâ* is the word of [[Envy]]. Yahweh's own name *Jealous* in Exodus 34:14 comes
from the same root, and the text does not bring the two together here.

**Going astray.** *Śāṭâ* (12, 19, 20, 29) occurs in the Hebrew Bible only in this passage and at
Proverbs 4:15 and 7:25, by Strong's tags (WLC). The second is the warning at the end of
[[The Young Man Void of Understanding]], *Don't go astray in her paths*.

**Trespass against a husband.** Verses 12 and 27 use the *maʿal* of verse 6. In the Torah's other
uses of the verb, the one wronged is Yahweh (Leviticus 5:21 in the Hebrew, 26:40; Numbers 5:6;
Deuteronomy 32:51) or his holy things (Leviticus 5:15). Only here is it a husband, by Strong's tags
(WLC). The wife's act is put in the vocabulary of breach of faith with God. The wiki does not press
this further, because the law also calls the act defilement.

**Seed, first and last.** *A man lies with her carnally* (13) is *šikbat zeraʿ* (WLC), the lying of
seed, the phrase of Leviticus 15:16–18 and 19:20. The outcome of innocence is *wənizrəʿâ zāraʿ* (28,
WLC), a niphal of the verb with the same noun: literally, she will be seeded with seed. The first is
what is suspected, and the second is what she is promised.

**A poor offering.** The Hebrew is *qemaḥ śəʿōrîm*, *barley meal* (15, WLC), not the *fine flour* of
Leviticus 2:1 or 5:11. The missing oil and frankincense match Leviticus 5:11, whose stated reason is
that it is a sin offering. Here the reason is that it is *a meal offering of jealousy*, and this
page does not treat the two as the same thing. The woman's guilt is unknown when it is brought. It
is called *her offering* (15), but her husband brings it. See
[[Flour for a Sin Offering - Leviticus 2 and 5]].

**Memory turned round.** *Zikkārôn*, *memorial*, is ordinarily the word of the Passover day (Exodus
12:14) and the stones on Aaron's shoulders (Exodus 28:12). Here it is joined to *mazkeret ʿāwōn*
(15, WLC), a causative participle of *zākar*: *bringing iniquity to memory*. WEB uses the same
phrase at Ezekiel 29:16, and the widow at 1 Kings 17:18 says *You have come to me to bring my sin to
memory* (both verified at bible-api.com). The *memorial* portion of verse 26 is the *ʾazkārâ* of
Leviticus 2:2, and this is the only time the word occurs outside Leviticus (WLC).

**Holy water, dust, clay.** *Mayim qədōšîm* (17) occurs only here, by Strong's tags (WLC), and the
text does not say where it comes from. The clay vessel recalls the rite for the cleansed leper
(Leviticus 14:5, 50), though there it is a bird that dies over the water. *Qarqaʿ*, *floor*, occurs
in the Torah only here (WLC); the other uses are building floors in 1 Kings 6–7 and the sea bottom in
Amos 9:3. The text gives no meaning for the dust. The obvious link to Genesis 3 is not one this law
makes.

**Loosened.** The Hebrew reads *ûp̄āraʿ ʾet-rōʾš hāʾiššâ* (18, WLC), he loosens the woman's head;
WEB supplies *hair*. The same verb and object describe the leper (Leviticus 13:45) and what priests
may not do (Leviticus 10:6; 21:10). The sign is one of disorder or uncleanness in those places, but
this verse does not say what it means, and whether it is uncovering or unbinding is not settled by
the word.

**The water's name.** It is *mê hammārîm* from verse 18 onward, before anything has been written
into it. That phrase occurs only in this chapter, by Strong's tags (WLC). Nothing named in its
making is harmful: holy water, dust, and the washed-off writing. The text attributes the result both
to Yahweh (21) and to the water (22, 24, 27). Compare [[Bitter Water at Marah]], and see
[[Two Bitter Waters - Marah and the Law of Jealousy]].

**Thigh and belly.** *Yārēk* and *beṭen* (21–22, 27) are the two body words, and what exactly is
described is uncertain. *Thigh* is used of descendants at Genesis 46:26 and Exodus 1:5, where those
who came out of Jacob's *yārēk* are his offspring (WLC; WEB has *direct offspring* and *body*). Set
against verse 28, the penalty may then concern childbearing. That is an inference, and the wiki does
not decide it. WEB's *allows* (21) renders a form of *nātan*, *gives*, so the Hebrew is more direct
than the English: Yahweh gives the thigh to fall. Translations vary, and none were compared in this
pass.

**The first Amen.** *ʾĀmēn* appears here first in canonical order, by Strong's tags (WLC). Its next
occurrences are Deuteronomy 27:15–26, where the people answer each curse. So the word's first two
settings are both people agreeing to a curse on themselves if they are guilty. A woman who says it
here has spoken two words of her own in the whole passage, and they are the same word.

**Writing washed off.** The verb of verse 23 is *māḥâ*, which is used with *book* at Exodus 17:14
and 32:32–33. There a name or memory is blotted out as a judgment. Here curses are blotted out and
what is removed from the page goes into the woman. The words themselves are what she drinks.

**The order of events.** Verse 24 has her drink, and verse 26 has her drink *afterward*, once the
offering is burned; the offering is taken from her hand in verse 25. Verse 27 then starts from when
she has drunk. Either verse 24 states the outcome ahead of the sequence, or the text has two
descriptions of one act. The wiki leaves that open.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Who is covered?** Verses 3 and 6 of this chapter name *male and female* and *man or woman*. This
   law has no case in which a wife suspects a husband. The wiki records the imbalance and does not
   explain it away.
2. **The penalty.** Leviticus 20:10 and Deuteronomy 22:22 require death for both parties. Here the
   guilty woman's stated end is to be a curse among her people (27), and the other man is never
   mentioned again. The text does not say whether anything further follows.
3. **The false accuser.** Deuteronomy 22:13–19 fines and binds a husband who slanders his bride. Here
   the man *shall be free from iniquity* (31). The verb is the *nāqâ* that clears the woman (19, 28), so
   he may be cleared of any guilt for bringing the charge, or of a share in her sin. The verse does
   not specify. The Deuteronomy case is different, and the two are compared on the connection page.
4. **Her consent.** The text records *Amen, Amen* but no alternative. It does not say what happens if
   she refuses, or if she confesses.
5. **Later practice.** The wiki has met no later narrative of this rite so far, and has not searched
   post-biblical sources on whether or how it was practiced.
