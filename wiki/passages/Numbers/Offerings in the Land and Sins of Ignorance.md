# Offerings in the Land and Sins of Ignorance

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 15:1–31

## Summary

The last chapter ended in a rout, with a generation sentenced to die in the wilderness. The next
words from Yahweh are *when you have come into the land of your habitations, which I give to you*
(2). No place or date is given for this speech, and no event is reported until verse 32. The law
simply assumes that Israel will arrive.

Three laws follow, and the first two open on that assumption.

**What goes with an animal (1–16).** Any burnt offering or sacrifice, whether for a vow, a freewill
gift, or a set feast, must come with flour and oil as a meal offering, and with wine as a drink
offering. The amounts rise with the animal: for a lamb, a tenth of an ephah and a fourth of a hin of
oil and of wine; for a ram, two tenths and a third of a hin; for a bull, three tenths and half a hin
(4–10). The rule applies per animal (11–12). The foreigner offers the same way, and the paragraph
closes on one statute, one law, and one ordinance for both: *as you are, so shall the foreigner be
before Yahweh* (13–16).

**The first of the dough (17–21).** When they eat the bread of the land, they are to set aside a cake
from the first of their dough for Yahweh, as the threshing floor's offering is set aside, throughout
their generations.

**Error, and the high hand (22–31).** If commands go undone by error, the congregation offers a young
bull as a burnt offering with its meal and drink offerings, and a male goat as a sin offering; the
priest makes atonement, and they are forgiven, the foreigner among them too (22–26). One person who
sins by error offers a year-old female goat, and is forgiven, under one law for native and foreigner
(27–29). Then the provision stops. The person who acts *with a high hand*, native or foreigner,
blasphemes Yahweh, and is to be cut off, *because he has despised Yahweh's word* (30–31). No offering
is named for that person. *His iniquity shall be on him.*

The unit is law, but its placement makes an argument. Yahweh has just refused to let this generation
enter, and he now legislates for what they will do in the land, including the wine of a country whose
grapes the spies carried back at 13:23. The speech is addressed to *the children of Israel*, and
it says *throughout your generations* (14, 15, 21, 23). The little ones of 14:31 are the likeliest
people to bring these offerings, although the text does not say so. Nor does it link the two chapters
itself. The link is one of sequence, and reading it as reassurance is the wiki's inference.

The word for forgiveness also carries over. At 14:19–20 Moses asks Yahweh to *pardon* and Yahweh says
*I have pardoned*. In this unit the same verb comes three times (25, 26, 28), now as the expected
result of a procedure. The chapter before showed a people who said *we have sinned* and then went up
against the word. The closing verses of this unit name what that kind of act is. The text does not
apply verses 30–31 to the attempt at Hormah, and the next unit is the case of a man gathering sticks,
which the text likewise does not label.

## Key verses

> **2** “Speak to the children of Israel, and tell them, ‘When you have come into the land of your habitations, which I give to you,

> **15** For the assembly, there shall be one statute for you and for the stranger who lives as a foreigner, a statute forever throughout your generations. As you are, so shall the foreigner be before Yahweh.

> **29** You shall have one law for him who does anything unwittingly, for him who is native-born among the children of Israel, and for the stranger who lives as a foreigner among them.

> **30** “‘But the soul who does anything with a high hand, whether he is native-born or a foreigner, the same blasphemes Yahweh. That soul shall be cut off from among his people.

> **31** Because he has despised Yahweh’s word, and has broken his commandment, that soul shall utterly be cut off. His iniquity shall be on him.’”

## Notes

**The land of your habitations.** *ʾEreṣ môšəbōtêkem* (2, WLC). By the tags, this pairing of the two
nouns occurs only here. Rahlfs has *τὴν γῆν τῆς κατοικήσεως ὑμῶν*. The verb is present: *ʾăšer ʾănî
nōtēn lākem*, *which I give to you*. Yahweh's giving is stated in a participle one chapter after the
people were told they would not go in. See
[[When You Come Into the Land - The Law Given Before Arrival]].

**A meal offering with every animal.** Leviticus 2 treats the meal offering as an offering in its own
right, brought with oil and frankincense. Here it is an accompaniment that the animal requires. The
verses name flour, oil, and wine, and do not name frankincense. Exodus 29:40 already gave the lamb's
amounts for the daily offering, and this unit extends the scale to all burnt offerings and sacrifices.
See [[Measures of Flour, Oil, and Wine - Exodus 29, Numbers 15, Numbers 28, and Ezekiel 46]].

**Mixed.** *Bālûl birbiʿît hahîn šāmen* (4, WLC), the same *bālal* of the flour mixed with oil in
Exodus 29:40. Rahlfs has *ἀναπεποιημένης ἐν ἐλαίῳ*.

**The assembly.** *Haqqāhāl ḥuqqâ ʾaḥat* (15, WLC). The noun stands at the head of the clause, and WEB
renders it *for the assembly*. Rahlfs has no word for it: *νόμος εἷς ἔσται ὑμῖν*. Rahlfs also uses *νόμος* for both
*ḥuqqâ* in verse 15 and *tôrâ* in verse 16. *Kākem kaggēr yihyeh lipnê YHWH*, *as you, as the
stranger, he shall be before Yahweh*, is the sharpest form of the rule in the Torah's lists on
[[One Law for the Native and the Stranger]]; that comparison is the wiki's.

**The first of your dough.** *Rēʾšît ʿărisōtêkem* (20, WLC). The noun *ʿărîsâ* occurs four times by the
tags, here twice, at Nehemiah 10:38 (10:37 in WEB), and at Ezekiel 44:30. Its sense as *dough* is
taken from WEB and Rahlfs's *φυράματος*, not from a lexicon. *Ḥallâ*, *cake*, is the singular here,
and the same word is used of the twelve cakes at Leviticus 24:5. See
[[The First of Your Dough - Numbers 15, Ezekiel 44, Nehemiah 10, and Romans 11]].

**WEB's "wave offering" here.** *Tārîmû tərûmâ* (19, 20, WLC), *lift up a lifted offering*. WEB's
*wave offering* in verses 19–21 translates *tərûmâ*, while at 8:11–21 the same English renders *tənûpâ*, a different noun
by the tags; see [[The Lamps and the Levites Cleansed]]. WEB's *heave it* in verse 20 is closer to
the verb. Rahlfs has *ἀφαίρεμα*.

**They err, and do not do.** *Wəkî tišgû wəlōʾ taʿăśû ʾēt kol-hammiṣwōt hāʾēlleh* (22, WLC). The
case is stated as commands not done. Leviticus 4:13 uses the same verb, *yišgû*, for doing what must
not be done. Compare Ezekiel 45:20, which uses the verb in a rite for the one who errs. See
[[The Congregation's Error - Leviticus 4 and Numbers 15]].

**From the eyes of the congregation.** *Mēʿênê hāʿēdâ* (24, WLC). WEB has *without the knowledge of
the congregation*. Leviticus 4:13 has *mēʿênê haqqāhāl*, *from the eyes of the assembly*. Rahlfs keeps
the idiom, *ἐξ ὀφθαλμῶν τῆς συναγωγῆς*, and adds *ἄμωμον* to the bull, which WLC does not have in this
verse.

**One person.** *Wəʾim nepeš ʾaḥat teḥĕṭāʾ bišgāgâ* (27, WLC) repeats Leviticus 4:27 word for word as
far as *bišgāgâ*. The next word in Leviticus is *mēʿam hāʾāreṣ*, *of the common people*; Numbers has
nothing there, and verse 29 widens it to native and stranger. Rahlfs has *ἀκουσίως* in verses 24, 27, 28, and
29 and *ἀκούσιον* in verse 25, and *ἑκούσιον* for *nədābâ*, the freewill offering, at verse 3.

**With a high hand.** *Bəyād rāmâ* (30, WLC). The same two words describe Israel leaving Egypt at
Exodus 14:8 and Numbers 33:3. The morphhb tags give the second word a different number here (7411 b)
from those two verses (7311), but the written words are the same, so the tag difference is likely a
tagging matter; this was not checked in a lexicon. Rahlfs separates them: *ἐν χειρὶ ὑψηλῇ* in Exodus
and Numbers 33, *ἐν χειρὶ ὑπερηφανίας*, *with a hand of arrogance*, here. WEB does not gloss it, and verse 31 explains
it by what the person has despised. See
[[With a High Hand - Exodus 14, Numbers 15, and Numbers 33]].

**Blasphemes.** *ʾEt-YHWH hûʾ məgaddēp* (30, WLC), with Yahweh placed first. By the tags *gādap* occurs
seven times, and this is its only use in the Torah. The others are 2 Kings 19:6, 22 with Isaiah 37:6,
23, of Sennacherib's men; Psalm 44:16 (44:17 in WLC); and Ezekiel 20:27. Rahlfs has *παροξύνει*,
*provokes*.

**Despised.** *Kî dəbar YHWH bāzâ* (31, WLC). By the tags, *bāzâ* occurs in one verse with *dəbar YHWH*
only here and at 2 Samuel 12:9, Nathan to David. Rahlfs uses *ἐφαύλισεν* here and *ἐφαύλισας* there.
See [[Despised the Word of Yahweh - Numbers 15.31 and 2 Samuel 12.9]].

**Broken.** *Wəʾet-miṣwātô hēpar* (31, WLC). *Pārar* with a commandment as its object occurs here and
at Ezra 9:14, by the tags. Psalm 119:126 has it with *tôrâ*.

**Cut off, cut off.** *Hikkārēt tikkārēt* (31, WLC), after *wənikrətâ* in verse 30. By the tags this
doubled form of the verb occurs only here. WEB gives the doubling as *utterly*. The Torah says
*cut off* often, but it does not define in this unit whether the action is the community's or God's.

**His iniquity on him.** *ʿĂwōnâ bāh* (31, WLC), feminine, agreeing with *nepeš*. At 14:18 Yahweh is
*nōśēʾ ʿāwōn*, *lifting iniquity*, and at 14:34 the people *bear* their own. Rahlfs has *ἡ ἁμαρτία
αὐτῆς ἐν αὐτῇ*.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **What the congregation brings.** Leviticus 4:14 has a young bull for the sin offering. Numbers
   15:24 has the bull as a burnt offering and a male goat for the sin offering.
2. **Doing or not doing.** Leviticus 4:13 speaks of what was done and should not have been. Numbers
   15:22 speaks of commands not done. Whether these are two cases or one is not stated.
3. **What one person brings.** Leviticus 4:28, 32 has a female goat or lamb, without defect. Numbers
   15:27 has a female goat a year old, and neither a lamb nor *without defect*.
4. **Who is covered.** Leviticus 4 also has the priest and the ruler. Numbers 15 has only the
   congregation and one person.
5. **The flour for a lamb.** One tenth here (4) and two tenths at Leviticus 23:13. Ezekiel 46:5–7, 11
   has a different scale altogether.
6. **Liquid at the daily offering.** Wine here and at Exodus 29:40. WEB at Numbers 28:7 has *strong
   drink*.
7. **No offering for the high hand.** Verses 30–31 name none, but Leviticus 6:1–7 provides an offering
   for deliberate fraud, and 2 Samuel 12:13 reports David's sin put away. See
   [[Unwitting and Presumptuous - Numbers 15, Psalm 19, and Hebrews 10]].
8. **One law, and its limits.** Verses 15–16 and 29 say one law for native and stranger. Exodus 12:48
   still sets circumcision as the condition for the Passover. This unit does not state one for
   offerings.
