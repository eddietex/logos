# Drive Out the Inhabitants

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 33:50–56

## Summary

From the plains of Moab, beside the Jordan at Jericho, Yahweh tells Moses to instruct Israel
for its entry into Canaan. Israel must drive out the inhabitants, destroy their stone idols and
molten images, and demolish their high places (50–52). Possession and dwelling rest on Yahweh’s
gift (53); the land is to be inherited by family and tribe, by lot, with larger shares for larger
groups and smaller shares for smaller groups (54).

The ending makes failure costly. Remaining inhabitants will become painful irritants and harass
Israel in the land (55). Yahweh then warns that what he intended to do to them he will do to
Israel (56). The people receiving the land can themselves become the objects of judgment.
This is instruction and warning before entry, not a report that conquest has occurred.

## Key verses

> **33:52** then you shall drive out all the inhabitants of the land from before you, destroy all their stone idols, destroy all their molten images, and demolish all their high places.

> **33:53** You shall take possession of the land, and dwell therein; for I have given the land to you to possess it.

> **33:54** You shall inherit the land by lot according to your families; to the more you shall give the more inheritance, and to the fewer you shall give the less inheritance. Wherever the lot falls to any man, that shall be his. You shall inherit according to the tribes of your fathers.

> **33:55** “But if you do not drive out the inhabitants of the land from before you, then those you let remain of them will be as pricks in your eyes and as thorns in your sides, and they will harass you in the land in which you dwell.

> **33:56** It shall happen that as I thought to do to them, so will I do to you.”

## Notes

- **A new speech after the itinerary.** Verse 50 begins a command at the journey’s endpoint.
  The existing boundary, 33:50–56, is retained; the detailed borders belong to the next unit.
- **Gift and responsibility.** Verse 53 supplies the reason for possession, while 54 regulates
  distribution. Like 26:52–56, it does not explain how the lot and proportional shares are
  administratively coordinated. No reconstructed allotment mechanism is asserted here.
- **The warning reverses the actors.** The distinction between the dispossessed and those who
  possess does not guarantee Israel’s security. Verse 56 places Israel under the same divine
  agency. This is a threat contingent on disobedience, not an assertion that it has already happened.
- **A tension in the conquest instructions.** Exodus 23:29–30 explicitly envisages gradual removal
  to protect the land from desolation. Numbers 33:55 warns against leaving inhabitants. Both
  stand: Numbers specifies neither a one-year deadline nor a replacement timetable. The existing
  connection page distinguishes planned delay from disobedience without pretending the texts
  supply a complete harmonization.
- **Read the violence accurately.** The charge is to expel the inhabitants and destroy their
  cult objects and sites. It neither narrates a campaign nor specifies the killing of every
  resident here. Expulsion itself remains a grave consequence for the people being displaced.
