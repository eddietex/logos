# The Stages of the Journey

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 33:1–49

## Summary

Moses records Israel’s stages at Yahweh’s command, from departure out of Egypt to the camp
beside the Jordan in the plains of Moab. The opening dates the departure to the day after
Passover, while Egypt buries its firstborn, and interprets it as judgment on Egypt’s gods.
The repeated travel-and-camp pattern preserves places without supplying a story for every stop.

The list occasionally slows: Elim has twelve springs and seventy palms; Rephidim lacks water;
Aaron dies on Mount Hor in year forty, month five, day one, at age 123. The king of Arad hears
of Israel’s approach. Finally the encampment extends from Beth Jeshimoth to Abel Shittim.
The passage looks backward before the instructions for occupation begin at verse 50.

> **39** Aaron was one hundred twenty-three years old when he died in Mount Hor.

> **49** They encamped by the Jordan, from Beth Jeshimoth even to Abel Shittim in the plains of Moab.

## People
- [[Moses]] — Moses and Aaron lead the departure (1), but Moses alone is named as the writer of the journeys at Yahweh’s command (2). This attribution concerns the itinerary; it does not by itself attribute every part of Numbers to him.
- [[Aaron]] — The opening recalls his leadership with Moses (1). His death on Mount Hor is dated to the first day of the fifth month of the fortieth year, at age 123 (38–39). The list pauses for this death before continuing toward Moab.
- [[God]] — Yahweh commands the written record (2), strikes Egypt’s firstborn and judges its gods (4), and commands Aaron’s ascent before his death (38). Deliverance and the death of a leader belong to the same remembered journey.
- [[King of Arad (of Moses' time)]] — His hearing of Israel’s arrival is recalled (40). Numbers 21:1–3 supplies the attack and victory omitted from this brief itinerary notice.

Israel is the community on the march, not a new appearance of the patriarch Jacob.

Other named regions and landmarks:

- [[Egypt]] — The departure takes place while the Egyptians bury their firstborn; Yahweh has judged their gods (3–4).
- [[Baal Zephon]] — A landmark before the sea crossing, locating Pihahiroth (7).
- [[Migdol]] — Israel camps before Migdol after turning back from Etham (7).
- [[The Wilderness of Zin]] — The itinerary explicitly locates Kadesh in this wilderness (36).
- [[Edom]] — Mount Hor is on the edge of Edom (37); this is a border description, not a modern location.
- [[Arad]] — The king hears of Israel’s coming (40); unlike Numbers 21:1–3, this notice gives no battle outcome.
- [[Canaan]] — The king of Arad lives in the South in Canaan (40). Israel’s final camp remains across the Jordan.
- [[The Negev]] — The South locates the king of Arad within Canaan (40).
- [[The Jordan]] — Israel camps beside the river at Jericho, from Beth Jeshimoth to Abel Shittim (48–49); no crossing is narrated.
- [[Jericho]] — The final camp is located by the Jordan at Jericho (48), not inside a conquered city.
- [[Beth Jeshimoth]] — One limit of the final encampment by the Jordan, with Abel Shittim as the other (49).
- [[Abel Shittim]] — One limit of the encampment in the plains of Moab (49). Compare [[Shittim]] in Numbers 25:1; the relationship is plausible but the verses do not expressly equate the names.
- [[Nebo (landmark)]] — The mountains of Abarim are described as before Nebo (47). This page retains the ambiguous landmark without deciding between the city and the mountain; compare [[Nebo (city)]].

## Themes
- [[Remembrance]] — The journey is preserved as a written record attributed to Moses at Yahweh’s command (2). Selective notices of departure, water, and Aaron’s death punctuate repeated travel stages.
- [[The Wilderness]] — A long sequence of named camps links liberation to the border of the land. Most receive no event or duration; the list cannot supply a precise modern route by itself.
- [[Redemption]] — The departure on the day after Passover is public, while Egypt buries its firstborn (3–4). The journey begins with deliverance rather than with Israel’s conquest of Canaan.
- [[Judgment]] — Egypt’s firstborn are struck and its gods judged (4). Aaron’s commanded death is recalled (38); its Meribah rationale is supplied by Numbers 20:24, not repeated here.
- [[Idolatry]] — The retrospective explicitly includes judgment on Egypt’s gods (4); it supplies neither a list of gods nor a one-to-one correspondence between gods and plagues.
- [[Life and Death]] — Egypt’s burial of the firstborn and Aaron’s dated death frame the long march (4, 38–39). Israel’s continuity does not mean that all its original leaders survive.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The movement ends in the plains of Moab, with an extended camp beside the Jordan (48–49). Arrival at the border is distinguished from entry and possession.
- [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] — Egyptians bury their firstborn as Israel departs (4), recalling the final plague rather than discussing inheritance rights.
- [[Burial and the Grave]] — The Egyptians are burying the firstborn when Israel departs (4). Aaron’s death is recorded later (38–39), but no burial procedure is described.
- [[Providence]] — Elim’s springs and palms and Rephidim’s lack of water are remembered together (9, 14). Provision is part of the remembered route without every hardship being retold.

## Notes

**A record, not a complete map.** Verse 2 attributes the itinerary to Moses at Yahweh’s command.
It does not date each intermediate stage or establish modern coordinates. Nor does the brief
Arad notice retell the conflict. No symbolism is imposed on the number of stops.

**Differences remain visible.** Exodus 15:22 calls the wilderness after the crossing Shur;
Numbers 33:8 calls it Etham. Numbers 12:16 has departure from Hazeroth into Paran, whereas
33:18 names Rithmah. These may describe regions and particular camps, but that is an inference,
not an identification made in either text. Numbers 21:11–20 continues from Iyeabarim through
Zered and the Arnon toward Pisgah; 33:44–48 uses Iyim, Dibon Gad, Almon Diblathaim, and Abarim.
The lists have different selections, and this page does not manufacture a continuous harmonized map.

**Naming decisions.** Terah the camp and Terah the patriarch now both have qualifiers.
Libnah is qualified as a wilderness camp to avoid conflating it with a later city. Dibon Gad
and Abel Shittim retain their full names, with comparisons to Dibon and Shittim rather than
unqualified assertions of identity. Nebo is retained as a landmark: verse 47 does not specify
whether the referent is the city or mountain. No Hebrew etymology is asserted.

**Scope and sources.** The planned 1–49 boundary is unchanged. All quoted verses are from
stored WEB. Comparisons use stored Exodus and Numbers; no external quotation or source-language
claim is added. The commands in 33:50–56 belong to the next invocation.

### Comparison with Deuteronomy 10:1–11

Deuteronomy 10:6–7 differs in the first pair’s order if the similar station names correspond, and
locates Aaron’s death at Moserah rather than Mount Hor. No return journey is asserted.
