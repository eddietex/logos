# The Levitical Cities and the Cities of Refuge

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 35:1–34

## Summary

Yahweh instructs Moses in the plains of Moab to have Israel give the Levites forty-eight
cities and their surrounding land, taking more from larger inheritances and fewer from
smaller ones. Six of these cities are refuges, three on each side of the Jordan (1–15).

The refuge prevents the killer’s death before congregational judgment. Weapons and hostile
intent distinguish murder from an unwitting killing; the congregation must rescue the latter
killer from the avenger and restore him to refuge (16–25). He remains there until the high
priest’s death. Leaving sooner exposes him to the avenger without bloodguilt for the avenger;
after the priest dies he may return to his own possession (26–29).

One witness cannot sustain a death sentence. Neither the murderer’s life nor the protected
killer’s early return can be purchased. The closing rationale concerns the land itself:
shed blood pollutes it, and Yahweh dwells among its inhabitants (30–34).

## Key verses

> **35:7** All the cities which you shall give to the Levites shall be forty-eight cities together with their suburbs.

> **35:12** The cities shall be to you for refuge from the avenger, that the man slayer not die, until he stands before the congregation for judgment.

> **35:15** For the children of Israel, and for the stranger and for the foreigner living among them, shall these six cities be for refuge; that everyone who kills any person unwittingly may flee there.

> **35:25** The congregation shall deliver the man slayer out of the hand of the avenger of blood, and the congregation shall restore him to his city of refuge, where he had fled. He shall dwell therein until the death of the high priest, who was anointed with the holy oil.

> **35:30** “‘Whoever kills any person, the murderer shall be slain at the mouth of witnesses; but one witness shall not testify against any person that he die.

> **35:33** “‘So you shall not pollute the land in which you are; for blood pollutes the land. No atonement can be made for the land for the blood that is shed in it, but by the blood of him who shed it.

> **35:34** You shall not defile the land which you inhabit, in the middle of which I dwell; for I, Yahweh, dwell in the middle of the children of Israel.’”

## People

- [[God]] — commands provision, adjudication, and protection of the land.
- [[Moses]] — receives and transmits the commands.
- [[Levi]] — the descendants receive cities; the patriarch is not an actor.

The high priest, avenger, killer, and witnesses are legal roles rather than named individuals.
No particular high priest’s death is narrated.

## Places

- [[Moab (place)]] — plains where the speech is given.
- [[The Jordan]] — crossing ahead and the division between the two sets of refuges.
- [[Jericho]] — geographical reference for the camp.
- [[Canaan]] — location of three future refuges.

None of the forty-eight cities is named here.

## Notes

- **Unit retained.** The city's double function joins the Levite settlement plan to refuge
  legislation; the existing 35:1–34 boundary is unchanged.
- **Cities without a tribal allotment.** Numbers 18:23–24 excludes Levi from the tribal
  inheritance, while 35:2–8 gives cities and surrounding land. Leviticus 25:32–34 already
  protects those holdings. Earlier wiki claims of absolute propertylessness are explicitly
  corrected in the related connection and priesthood pages.
- **Measurements left visible.** Verse 4 gives one thousand cubits outward from the wall;
  verse 5 gives two thousand cubits on each side with the city in the middle. The relation
  between these measurements is not explained here. No harmonized plan or modern area
  calculation is asserted.
- **Safety has limits.** Refuge is available to Israelite and resident foreigner alike, but
  is neither unconditional asylum for murder nor immediate freedom for accidental killing.
  The avenger's authority and the congregation's duty of rescue must both remain visible.
- **The priest’s death.** The text specifies an anointed high priest and a release at his
  death, but supplies no sacrificial explanation. A Christian typological reading would be
  a further interpretation, not an explanation stated by this chapter.
- **Blood and atonement.** Verse 33 concerns the polluted land. It must not be isolated
  from the preceding distinction of murder and unwitting killing, or turned into a general
  denial of the sacrificial atonement described elsewhere.
- **Sources.** All quotations are copied from stored WEB Numbers 35. Comparisons use
  stored WEB Exodus 21, Leviticus 25, and Numbers 18. No new quotation from an unstored
  book, or exact Hebrew or Greek claim, is introduced.
