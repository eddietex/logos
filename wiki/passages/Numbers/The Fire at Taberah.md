# The Fire at Taberah

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 11:1–3

## Summary

The verse after the ark's last cry, the people complain. Nothing has been said about thirst, hunger, or the enemy
since [[The March from Sinai]], and nothing is said here either. The text gives no reason and no words. It says only
where the complaint landed, *in the ears of Yahweh* (1).

Yahweh hears, and his anger burns. *Yahweh's fire* burns among them and consumes some of the outskirts of the camp (1).
No warning comes first, and Moses has no part in it. The text does not say what the fire was, how it started, or whether
anyone died.

The people then cry to Moses, and Moses prays to Yahweh, and the fire goes down (2). The prayer has no recorded words.
Verse 3 names the place Taberah, and gives the reason in the same words as verse 1: *Yahweh's fire burned among them*.

The whole unit is a small pattern: complaint, anger, fire, cry, prayer, relief, name. The book will use that pattern
again at larger size. It comes right after ten chapters of a camp that did everything as commanded, and it is the first
thing the people do on the road. Before Sinai, each complaint in Exodus was met with water or bread and no penalty. This
is the first one in the wiki's sequence that is met with fire, and it follows the covenant, the law, and the tabernacle.
The text does not state that link. The order of events is all it gives.

## Notes

**What WEB leaves out.** The Hebrew of the first clause is fuller than the English: *wayhî hāʿām kəmitʾōnənîm raʿ bəʾoznê
yhwh* (WLC). WEB has no word for *kə-*, usually *like* or *as*, or for *raʿ*, *evil* or *bad*. So the people are
*like complainers*, or *as those complaining*, and something is *evil in the ears of Yahweh*. Whether *raʿ* says what they
complained of (hardship) or how the complaint sounded to Yahweh (evil), the syntax here does not settle, and the wiki
does not decide. Rahlfs's Greek has *γογγύζων πονηρὰ ἔναντι κυρίου*, keeping *πονηρὰ* and, like WEB, nothing for *kə-*.

**A rare verb.** *Mitʾōnənîm* is lemma 596 in morphhb, a hitpolel participle, and by the tags the verb occurs only here
and at Lamentations 3:39. It is not *lûn*, the verb of Exodus 15–17 and Numbers 14–17. The page on [[Murmuring]] now
records the difference. The meaning of so rare a verb rests mainly on context, and this page did not consult a lexicon. English
*complaining* is WEB's choice. Rahlfs's *γογγύζων* does blur the line in Greek: the same word family stands in its
Exodus 16:7–12 and at Numbers 14:27; 16:11; and 17:20, 25 (its numbering), so a reader of the Greek would hear one verb.

**In the ears of Yahweh.** Elsewhere in the wilderness the complaint is said to Moses, and it takes him or God to say
where it really goes (Exodus 16:8). Here it starts in Yahweh's ears. *Bəʾoznê yhwh* returns at 11:18, *you have wept in
the ears of Yahweh*, so the phrase comes back within the chapter. *Wayyišmaʿ yhwh* is the plain verb. In Exodus
16:12 Yahweh had also *heard*, and answered with meat.

**Anger first.** *Wayyiḥar ʾappô* is the idiom for anger, with *ʾap* literally the nose. Verse 33 has the same two words at the
plague, and verse 10 has them too. In Exodus 32 it is the phrase Moses argues against (32:10–12), so the wiki does not read this anger as unusual
for Yahweh, only as new on the road. See [[Slow to Anger - Proverbs 14.29 and Exodus 34.6]].

**Yahweh's fire.** *ʾĒš yhwh* occurs twice here (1, 3), and with the same construct form at 1 Kings 18:38 (WLC, tags).
The search looked for *ʾēš* followed directly by *yhwh*; other wordings, like Leviticus 10:2's *from before Yahweh*, are
not counted. At Carmel the same phrase consumes an offering, and here it consumes the camp's edge. The text calls it
Yahweh's and does not describe it as lightning or anything else. The wiki leaves it there.

**It consumes.** *Wattōʾḵal* (1) is the verb of Exodus 3:2's bush, *ʾênennû ʾukkāl*, *was not consumed*, and *bāʿar*
stands in both as well (WLC). The comparison is the wiki's. The fire that did not consume at the bush, where the rescue was
announced, now consumes at the edge of the rescued camp. The text does not draw this line, and the two scenes differ in
every other way.

**Outskirts.** *Biqṣēh hammaḥăneh* (WLC) is *at the edge of the camp*, the phrase of Judges 7:17, 19 and 2 Kings 7:8.
WEB's *some of the outskirts* and Rahlfs's *μέρος τι τῆς παρεμβολῆς*, *some part of the camp*, are both looser. In a camp
drawn as a square around the Tent (see [[The Camp Around the Tent]]), the edge is as far from the sanctuary as the
camp goes, and the fire starts there. Which tribes camped at that place the text does not say, and on the march no
square is standing. Verse 1 gives no count of the dead, and does not say that any person died. *Bām* puts the fire *among* them,
and *consumed* has the edge of the camp as its object.

**Cried to Moses.** *Wayyiṣʿaq hāʿām ʾel-mōšeh* (2, WLC). By the tags this is the only place the verb *ṣāʿaq* or *zāʿaq*
takes Moses as the one cried to; he is otherwise the one who cries to Yahweh (Exodus 8:12 WEB; 15:25; 17:4; Numbers 12:13
WEB). In Exodus 14:10 the people had cried to Yahweh. Here they go to the man, and it works. Whether that is trust
or distance, the verse does not say.

**Moses' first *hitpallel*.** *Wayyitpallēl* (2, WLC) is the Bible's usual verb for prayer, and it comes very late in the
story of Moses. By the tags, Exodus and Leviticus never use it. The Torah's other uses are Abraham's at Genesis 20:7, 17,
then Numbers 21:7 and Deuteronomy 9:20, 26. Moses' prayers in Exodus use *ṣāʿaq*, *ʿātar*, and at 32:11 *ḥālâ*. Numbers
21:7 is the nearest parallel, where the people ask and he prays. Rahlfs has *ηὔξατο*.

**The fire goes down.** *Wattišqaʿ* (2, WLC) is more physical than WEB's *abated*. The verb (lemma 8257) is usually glossed *sink*, a
sense recalled here and not checked in a lexicon. By the tags it occurs elsewhere at Jeremiah 51:64, Amos 8:8 and 9:5, and
in causative forms at Ezekiel 32:14 and Job 40:25 (Hebrew numbering). Rahlfs has *ἐκόπασεν*.

**The name.** *Wayyiqrāʾ šēm-hammāqôm hahûʾ taḇʿērâ* (3, WLC) has an active verb with no named subject. WEB's passive is
a fair rendering. The same form names Massah and Meribah at Exodus 17:7, *He called*, and Kibroth Hattaavah at 11:34.
Rahlfs translates the name, *ἐμπυρισμός*, *burning*, instead of spelling it out. *Taḇʿērâ* is built on *bāʿar*, the verb of
verses 1 and 3, so the name repeats the event in a word. The noun occurs only here and at Deuteronomy 9:22 (tags).

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Where Taberah is.** Numbers 33:16 goes straight from Sinai to Kibroth Hattaavah, with no Taberah. The verse here says
   *that place*, not that Israel camped there. So Taberah may be a spot on the stage to Kibroth Hattaavah, or a stage
   the list drops, and neither is stated. See [[Taberah]].
2. **How long after the three days.** Numbers 10:33 gives a three-day march. No time is given for 11:1, and no word says
   the camp has stopped, though *outskirts of the camp* implies it.
3. **Paran.** See [[The March from Sinai]] and [[Paran]]. This stop is the first of three between 10:12 and 12:16.
4. **What was said.** Verse 1 has no content, and Psalm 78:18–21 links a kindled fire to the demand for meat, which in
   Numbers comes after this unit. See [[Taberah Remembered - Numbers 11, Deuteronomy 9, and Psalm 78]].
