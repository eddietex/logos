# The Nazirite Vow

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 6:1–21

## Summary

The laws of the camp so far have told people what must happen to them. This one starts with what a
person may choose. *Either man or woman* may make a special vow, the vow of a Nazirite, to separate
themselves to Yahweh (2). No tribe, family, or office is required, and no length of time is set; the
days are whatever the vow names.

Three things follow for as long as the vow lasts. The Nazirite takes nothing from the grapevine:
no wine, strong drink, vinegar, or grape juice, and no grapes, fresh or dried, down to seeds and
skins (3–4). No razor touches the head, and the hair grows long (5). The Nazirite goes near no dead
body, even for father, mother, brother, or sister, *because his separation to God is on his head*
(6–7). The summary is one line: *All the days of his separation he is holy to Yahweh* (8).

The law then takes up the one breach it expects, and it is not a chosen one. If someone dies suddenly
beside the Nazirite, the head is defiled. The Nazirite shaves on the seventh day and brings two
birds on the eighth, and the priest makes atonement *because he sinned by reason of the dead* (9–11).
A year-old lamb follows as a trespass offering, the vow starts again, and the days already kept
*shall be void* (12).

The vow ends at the door of the Tent of Meeting with a long list of offerings: burnt, sin, and peace
offerings, a basket of unleavened bread, meal and drink offerings (13–17). The Nazirite shaves the head there and puts the hair on the fire under the
peace offering (18). The priest puts the boiled shoulder and two pieces of bread on the Nazirite's
hands and waves them before Yahweh (19–20). *After that the Nazirite may drink wine* (20). The
closing line holds the person to the vow as spoken, and allows more besides (21).

Nothing in the passage gives the Nazirite a task. There is no service at the sanctuary and no
message to carry. The law describes only what the person refrains from, and the body shows it. Why
someone would take the vow is not stated either. That is the wiki's observation, and it should not
be read as a claim that no purpose existed.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Numbers.md`. Complete unit:

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **2** “Speak to the children of Israel, and tell them: ‘When either man or woman shall make a special vow, the vow of a Nazirite, to separate himself to Yahweh,

> **3** he shall separate himself from wine and strong drink. He shall drink no vinegar of wine, or vinegar of fermented drink, neither shall he drink any juice of grapes, nor eat fresh grapes or dried.

> **4** All the days of his separation he shall eat nothing that is made of the grapevine, from the seeds even to the skins.

> **5** “‘All the days of his vow of separation no razor shall come on his head, until the days are fulfilled, in which he separates himself to Yahweh. He shall be holy. He shall let the locks of the hair of his head grow long.

> **6** “‘All the days that he separates himself to Yahweh he shall not go near a dead body.

> **7** He shall not make himself unclean for his father, or for his mother, for his brother, or for his sister, when they die; because his separation to God is on his head.

> **8** All the days of his separation he is holy to Yahweh.

> **9** “‘If any man dies very suddenly beside him, and he defiles the head of his separation; then he shall shave his head in the day of his cleansing. On the seventh day he shall shave it.

> **10** On the eighth day he shall bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the priest, to the door of the Tent of Meeting.

> **11** The priest shall offer one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering, and make atonement for him, because he sinned by reason of the dead, and shall make his head holy that same day.

> **12** He shall separate to Yahweh the days of his separation, and shall bring a male lamb a year old for a trespass offering; but the former days shall be void, because his separation was defiled.

> **13** “‘This is the law of the Nazirite: when the days of his separation are fulfilled, he shall be brought to the door of the Tent of Meeting,

> **14** and he shall offer his offering to Yahweh, one male lamb a year old without defect for a burnt offering, and one ewe lamb a year old without defect for a sin offering, and one ram without defect for peace offerings,

> **15** and a basket of unleavened bread, cakes of fine flour mixed with oil, and unleavened wafers anointed with oil, and their meal offering, and their drink offerings.

> **16** The priest shall present them before Yahweh, and shall offer his sin offering, and his burnt offering.

> **17** He shall offer the ram for a sacrifice of peace offerings to Yahweh, with the basket of unleavened bread. The priest shall offer also its meal offering, and its drink offering.

> **18** The Nazirite shall shave the head of his separation at the door of the Tent of Meeting, and shall take the hair of the head of his separation, and put it on the fire which is under the sacrifice of peace offerings.

> **19** The priest shall take the boiled shoulder of the ram, and one unleavened cake out of the basket, and one unleavened wafer, and shall put them on the hands of the Nazirite, after he has shaved the head of his separation;

> **20** and the priest shall wave them for a wave offering before Yahweh. This is holy for the priest, together with the breast that is waved and the thigh that is offered. After that the Nazirite may drink wine.

> **21** “‘This is the law of the Nazirite who vows, and of his offering to Yahweh for his separation, besides that which he is able to get. According to his vow which he vows, so he must do after the law of his separation.’”

## Notes

**Whose vow.** *ʾÎš ʾô ʾiššâ* (2, WLC) is the same pair that verse 6 of the previous chapter uses,
and the law then runs in masculine singular forms. Those forms do not cancel the opening, since the
verse has just named both. Chapter 5 ended with a wife brought before the priest by her husband;
chapter 6 opens with a woman who may bring herself. The text does not draw that contrast. Numbers
30:3–15 later lets a father or husband void a woman's vow on the day he hears it, so her freedom
here is not the whole picture. How a woman's vow worked with the shaving of verse 18 is not stated.

**A special vow.** WEB's *make a special vow* is *yaplîʾ lindōr neder* (2, WLC). By Strong's tags, the
hiphil of *pālāʾ* with *neder* occurs only here and at Leviticus 27:2, where WEB has *consecrates*.
The piel with *neder* is at Leviticus 22:21 and Numbers 15:3, 8. The same root gives the *wonders* of
Exodus 3:20 and *is anything too hard* at Genesis 18:14. Whether that sense colors the verb in a vow
formula, or the verb simply means to mark something off, is not settled by the word, and this pass
consulted no lexicon.

**Three words from one root.** *Nāzîr* is the person (2, 13, 18–21). *Hizzîr* is the verb, to
separate (2, 3, 5, 6, 12). *Nēzer* is the state, thirteen times in this passage, and WEB renders it
*separation*. Outside this chapter *nēzer* usually means a crown: the high priest's (Exodus 29:6;
39:30; Leviticus 8:9; 21:12) and a king's (2 Samuel 1:10; 2 Kings 11:12; Psalm 132:18). So *nēzer
ʾĕlōhāyw ʿal-rōʾšô* (7, WLC) is rendered *his separation to God is on his head*, and the same words
could be heard as his God's crown on his head. WEB's rendering is a fair one, and the wiki does not
replace it, but the English hides the echo. Jeremiah 7:29 uses the noun of hair: WLC has *gozzî nizrēḵ*, shear your
*nēzer*. See [[The Nazirite and the Priest - Numbers 6 and Leviticus 8, 10, and 21]].

**The same word for a vine.** Leviticus 25:5 and 11 use *nāzîr* of the vine left unpruned in the
Sabbath and Jubilee years, which WEB renders *undressed vine*. So the word that names the person who
stays away from the vine also names a vine left uncut. See
[[The Unpruned Vine and the Unshaved Head - Leviticus 25 and Numbers 6]].

**Vine, not alcohol alone.** The list is wider than drink. Fresh grapes, raisins, and juice are not
intoxicants, so the rule cannot rest only on drunkenness. It is *everything made of the vine* (4).
*Ḥarṣannîm* and *zāg* (4) each occur only here, by Strong's tags (WLC), and with no other use to compare,
their meanings rest on tradition and context. WEB's *seeds* and *skins* is one rendering, and the
order of the two is not certain. The text does not explain why the vine is excluded. It is sometimes
suggested that the vine stands for settled life in the land, but the text does not say that and the
wiki does not adopt it.

**Razor.** *Taʿar* (5) passes over the head (*lōʾ yaʿăbōr*). The same word is used for the Levites'
shaving at Numbers 8:7, the chapter of their cleansing. *Peraʿ*, *locks*, is tagged only here and at
Ezekiel 44:20, where priests may neither shave their heads nor let their locks grow long. It looks
related to the verb *pāraʿ*, *let go loose*, which was used of the suspected wife's head in the law
just before this one (5:18), although morphhb tags the noun and verb separately. Both laws put the
head in view, and the signs do not mean the same thing.

**Near the dead.** *ʿAl-nepeš mēt lōʾ yābōʾ* (6, WLC) is nearly the wording of the high priest's rule,
*ʿal kol-napšōt mēt lōʾ yābōʾ* (Leviticus 21:11, WLC). An ordinary priest may defile himself for near
kin (Leviticus 21:2–3). The high priest may not, for father or mother, and the Nazirite may not, for
father, mother, brother, or sister. The Nazirite is the stricter by the length of the list. *Nepeš*
here means a dead person, not a *soul* in any later sense.

**Accident as sin.** Verse 9 could not be plainer that nothing was intended. Two words for suddenness
stand together, *bəp̄etaʿ pitʾōm* (WLC). *Petaʿ* is also the word for the unintended shove of Numbers
35:22. Yet verse 11 says *ḥāṭāʾ ʿal-hannāpeš*, *he sinned by reason of the dead*, and the priest
makes atonement. This is the verb of [[The Sin Offering for Ruler and Individual]], and, as there,
it does not require a guilty intention. The word may mean a failure or a missing of what was vowed,
and not a moral offense. The text does not decide, and the wiki leaves it as the text has it.

**Seven and eight.** The birds, the eighth day, and the door of the Tent match the man cleansed of a
discharge (Leviticus 15:14–15) and the woman (15:29). The leper also reaches an eighth day
(14:10–11). The seventh-day shaving matches the leper as well (14:9). Corpse uncleanness itself gets
its own law in Numbers 19, which has not been ingested.

**The trespass lamb.** A ram is the trespass offering of Leviticus 5:15, for sin in *the holy things of
Yahweh*. A male lamb serves for the leper (14:12) and here. The trespass offering usually implies
something owed. The days given to Yahweh and then lost may be that debt, but this is an inference,
because the text gives its reason as the defilement of the vow, not as a debt. *Yippĕlû*, WEB's
*shall be void*, is literally *shall fall* (12, WLC).

**A sin offering with no sin.** A successful vow also has a sin offering, a ewe lamb, the lay
person's animal of Leviticus 4:32 (14). No breach has occurred by then, and the text gives no reason.
Verse 14 lists burnt, sin, peace. Verse 16 has the priest offer the sin offering first. The offering
order of the rite comes in the second list, not the first.

**On the hands.** *Wənātan ʿal-kappê hannāzîr* (19, WLC) puts the portions on the Nazirite's palms,
and the priest waves them. In the rest of the Hebrew Bible, by Strong's tags, the palms and the wave
offering appear in one verse only at Exodus 29:24 and Leviticus 8:27, the consecration of the
priests. There too, a basket of unleavened bread and a ram are involved. Their differences matter:
Aaron's portions are burned, while this one goes to the priest, and the Nazirite is released, not
installed. The priest's share is also larger than usual, since the shoulder comes on top of the breast
and thigh of Leviticus 7:31–34.

**The hair on the fire.** The Nazirite does the shaving (18). The fire is under the peace offering,
but the text does not call the hair an offering. The hair is the visible thing that has been dedicated
through these days, and it goes to the altar's fire instead of being thrown away. That reading is
inference. Verse 20 ends the sequence
with an unceremonious line about wine.

**What the hand reaches.** *Besides that which he is able to get* (21) is *millĕbad ʾăšer taśśîg yādô*
(WLC). The same idiom sets the poor man's offering at Leviticus 5:11, 14:22, and 27:8. Those passages
use it for the lowest a poor person may bring. Here it allows a person to bring more. The list is
the minimum, and the vow's own words may go beyond it: *according to his vow which he vows*.

**Later Nazirites.** The noun appears at Judges 13:5, 7; 16:17 for Samson; Amos 2:11–12 sets Nazirites
beside prophets; and Lamentations 4:7 calls the *nəzîrîm* of Jerusalem purer than snow, where WEB has
*nobles*. Hannah's vow in 1 Samuel 1:11 has no *nāzîr* in WLC. *Nazarene* in Matthew 2:23 is tied to
the town of Nazareth, and the wiki does not equate it with *Nazirite*. See
[[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]].

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **No stated length.** The text never gives a minimum or a maximum, and it gives no reason. Any
   fixed period found in later tradition is not in this passage, and such sources were not checked.
2. **Abstaining and sin.** The law honors the person who holds off from wine, and also requires a sin
   offering at the end. Whether anything in the vow itself is being answered for is not stated.
3. **Mourning forbidden.** The vow overrides the duty to bury a parent. Genesis 50 and Exodus 20:12 show
   how much that duty weighs, and this law does not explain the cost. The person chose it, but the
   family did not.
4. **Lifelong cases.** Numbers 6 assumes days that are fulfilled and a vow chosen by the person. Samson
   has neither, and the law does not provide for that.
