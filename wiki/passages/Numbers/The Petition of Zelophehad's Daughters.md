# The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 27:1–11

## Summary

Zelophehad's five daughters approach Moses, Eleazar, the princes, and the whole congregation
at the Tent of Meeting's entrance. Their father died in the wilderness without sons; they
distinguish his own sin from participation in Korah's rebellion. They ask why the absence
of a son should erase his name from his family, and request a possession among his brothers.

Moses brings the case to Yahweh, who declares the daughters right and commands that their
father's inheritance pass to them. The answer becomes a general statute for Israel:
when a man dies without a son, his daughter inherits; if there is no daughter, the succession
passes to his brothers, then his father's brothers, then the nearest kinsman of his family.

## Key verses

Copied verbatim from stored WEB, raw/bible/Numbers.md.

> **1** Then the daughters of Zelophehad, the son of Hepher, the son of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, of the families of Manasseh the son of Joseph came near. These are the names of his daughters: Mahlah, Noah, and Hoglah, and Milcah, and Tirzah.

> **2** They stood before Moses, and before Eleazar the priest, and before the princes and all the congregation, at the door of the Tent of Meeting, saying,

> **3** “Our father died in the wilderness. He was not among the company of those who gathered themselves together against Yahweh in the company of Korah, but he died in his own sin. He had no sons.

> **4** Why should the name of our father be taken away from among his family, because he had no son? Give to us a possession among the brothers of our father.”

> **5** Moses brought their cause before Yahweh.

> **6** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **7** “The daughters of Zelophehad speak right. You shall surely give them a possession of an inheritance among their father’s brothers. You shall cause the inheritance of their father to pass to them.

> **8** You shall speak to the children of Israel, saying, ‘If a man dies, and has no son, then you shall cause his inheritance to pass to his daughter.

> **9** If he has no daughter, then you shall give his inheritance to his brothers.

> **10** If he has no brothers, then you shall give his inheritance to his father’s brothers.

> **11** If his father has no brothers, then you shall give his inheritance to his kinsman who is next to him of his family, and he shall possess it. This shall be a statute and ordinance for the children of Israel, as Yahweh commanded Moses.’”

## Notes

**A claim recognized, not a revolt condemned.** The sisters speak publicly and reason from
family continuity. Yahweh endorses their claim. The proximity to recollections of Korah
does not make every challenge or question rebellious.

**The scope of the law.** The order remains patrilineal and gives the son priority. Yet a
daughter precedes the deceased man's brothers. Calling the rule either universal equality
or a total exclusion of women would miss its precise terms. No equal-share rule among
siblings of both sexes is stated.

**Sin and inheritance.** The sisters acknowledge their father's sin but deny participation
in Korah's company. The text does not identify his offense. A connection to the Sabbath
breaker would be conjectural. The father's death does not erase the daughters' claim.

**Job's daughters.** Job 42:13–15 describes daughters receiving an inheritance alongside seven
brothers. Numbers 27 regulates succession when a son is absent; it does not say a father may
never give daughters property when sons exist. The two situations differ without constituting
a demonstrated legal contradiction. See the inheritance connection above.

**Boundary and naming.** The planned boundary, 27:1–11, is unchanged. The passage title adds
“Petition” to avoid ambiguity with the existing collective person page
[[The Daughters of Zelophehad]]. Gilead and Hepher remain in
[[The Families of the Second Census]]; no distinct action warrants splitting that register
in this unit. The five sisters also continue to act collectively.

### Qualification from Numbers 36

The inheritance and Jubilee provisions should not be read as unrestricted guarantees of
marital choice or automatic reversal of every tribal transfer.
[[The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad]] (36:1–13) preserves the daughters’ property
while requiring marriage within their paternal tribal family; verse 4 presents the
clan heads’ concern that Jubilee would not remedy an intertribal transfer.
