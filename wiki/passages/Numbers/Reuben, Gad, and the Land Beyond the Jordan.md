# Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 32:1–42

## Summary

The children of Reuben and of Gad have *a very great multitude of livestock*, and they see that the land
of Jazer and Gilead is *a place for livestock* (1). They come to Moses, Eleazar, and the princes of the
congregation, name nine towns of the conquered country, and ask: *let this land be given to your servants
for a possession. Don't bring us over the Jordan* (2–5).

Moses hears it as Kadesh again. *Shall your brothers go to the war, and shall you sit here?* (6). Their
fathers discouraged the heart of Israel when they came back from the valley of Eshcol, Yahweh swore that
none of that generation but Caleb and Joshua would see the land, and Israel wandered forty years until the
generation was consumed (7–13). Now they have *risen up in your fathers' place, an increase of sinful
men*; if they turn away, Yahweh will leave the whole people in the wilderness again (14–15).

The two tribes come near with a proposal. They will build sheepfolds and cities for their little ones, and
they themselves will go armed before Israel *until we have brought them to their place*, and not return
home until every Israelite has his inheritance (16–19). Moses accepts it as a condition. If every armed
man crosses the Jordan *before Yahweh* until the land is subdued, they will be *guiltless towards Yahweh,
and towards Israel*, and the eastern land will be theirs; *but if you will not do so … be sure your sin
will find you out* (20–23). He reverses their order — *Build cities for your little ones, and folds for
your sheep* — and tells them to do what they have said (24). They agree twice (25–27, 31–32).

Because Moses will not cross, he hands the agreement to Eleazar, Joshua, and the heads of the tribes, with
both outcomes spelled out: if the two tribes cross armed, give them Gilead; if not, they take their
possession *among you in the land of Canaan* (28–30).

Then Moses gives the kingdoms of Sihon and Og to Gad, Reuben, *and to the half-tribe of Manasseh the son
of Joseph* — a third party not mentioned in the request (33). Gad builds eight towns, Reuben six, and
Reuben renames some of them (34–38). Three Manassite groups take land by conquest: the children of Machir
take Gilead and dispossess the Amorites, Jair takes villages and calls them Havvoth Jair, and Nobah takes
Kenath and calls it by his own name (39–42).

## People

- [[Reuben]] — the tribe, joint petitioner for the eastern land (1–5); later named second, after Gad,
  through most of the chapter (2, 6, 25, 29, 31, 33); builds and renames six towns (37–38).
- [[Gad]] — the tribe, joint petitioner, named first from verse 2 on; builds eight towns (34–36).
- [[Manasseh]] — *the half-tribe of Manasseh the son of Joseph*, given land without having asked (33).
- [[Machir]] — his children take Gilead and dispossess the Amorites; Moses gives Gilead to Machir (39–40).
- [[Jair (son of Manasseh)]] — takes villages and calls them Havvoth Jair (41).
- [[Nobah (person)]] — takes Kenath and its villages and calls it Nobah (42).
- [[Moses]] — rebukes the request, sets the condition, and hands it to his successors (6–33).
- [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]] — addressed with Moses (2); one of the three charged to carry it out (28).
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — named among the two who *followed Yahweh completely* (12), and charged to
  carry out the agreement after Moses (28).
- [[Caleb (son of Jephunneh)]] — *the Kenizzite*, the other exception (12).
- [[The Twelve Tribal Princes]] — *the princes of the congregation* (2) and *the heads of the fathers'
  households of the tribes* (28); the text does not say these are the same twelve men.
- [[Sihon]] and [[Og]] — the kings whose kingdoms are given (33).
- [[Abraham]], [[Isaac]], [[Jacob]] — named in the oath Moses recalls (11).
- [[Joseph]] — named in Manasseh's patronymic (33).
- [[God]] — *struck* the land before Israel (4), swore the wilderness sentence (10–11), and is the one
  *before* whom the armed men cross (20–22, 27, 29, 32).
- Unnamed: the fathers sent from Kadesh Barnea (8); the Amorites of Gilead (39).

## Notes

**Where the chapter stands.** The war with Midian is over and the counted generation is camped in the
plains of Moab. The land east of the Jordan is already in Israel's hands from the victories over Sihon and
Og (21:21–35), and the two tribes call it *the land which Yahweh struck before the congregation of Israel*
(4). What they ask is to stop there, before the river the rest of Israel still has to cross.

**Livestock first.** The chapter opens with the cattle — the Hebrew word order puts *miqneh*,
*livestock*, first in verse 1 (Miqra according to the Masorah, MAM) — and the request repeats it: *a land
for livestock; and your servants have livestock* (4). The request is reasonable on its face. The tribes ask
politely, *if we have found favor in your sight* (5), and they name the land as Yahweh's victory, not
their own. The problem is the last clause: *Don't bring us over the Jordan.*

**Moses hears Kadesh.** His answer is the longest retelling of the spy story inside Numbers (8–13). He
makes the two tribes the spies' heirs: *you have risen up in your fathers' place, an increase of sinful
men* (*tarbût ʾănāšîm ḥaṭṭāʾîm*, 14; MAM). The danger he names is not that two tribes stay behind but that
their staying will *discourage the heart of the children of Israel* (7), as the spies' report did (9), and
that Yahweh will *yet again leave them in the wilderness* (15). The whole people's entry is at stake in
two tribes' choice. See [[The People Refuse the Land]] and [[Murmuring]].

**The verb of discouragement.** *Discourage* in verses 7 and 9 is the hiphil of *nwʾ* — written
*tnwʾwn* and read *təniʾûn* in verse 7 (ketiv and qere), *wayyānîʾû* in verse 9 (MAM). The same hiphil,
*hēnîʾ*, is the father's or husband's veto of a vow two chapters earlier: *if her father forbids her*
(30:5 WEB; *hēnîʾ* at 30:6 in the Hebrew numbering, MAM). And Numbers 14:34 has Yahweh's sentence end
*you shall know my alienation*, *tənûʾātî*, a noun built on the same root (MAM). The wiki notes the
repetition; the text does not draw it out. To discourage is to annul in advance what Yahweh has given.

**The proposal, and who made it.** The condition is often remembered as Moses' idea, but the text gives
the substance to the tribes. They offer to go *ready armed* before Israel *until we have brought them to
their place* and not to return home *until the children of Israel have each inherited their inheritance*
(17–18). Moses' terms in 20–22 ask for less: cross *until he has driven out his enemies* and *the land is
subdued*, *then afterward you shall return*. Rashi notices the gap: the tribes undertook more than Moses
required, stayed through the division of the land, *and so, indeed, they did* (Rashi on 32:24, citing
Joshua 22; Sefaria, Rosenbaum and Silbermann English). That is Rashi's reading; the text records both
formulations and does not comment.

***Before Yahweh.*** Moses' version is full of a phrase the tribes' version lacks: *arm yourselves to go
before Yahweh to the war*, *pass over the Jordan before Yahweh*, *the land is subdued before Yahweh*,
*a possession before Yahweh* (20–22). The tribes had said *before the children of Israel* (17). In their
second answer they take up his phrase — *before Yahweh to battle* (27), *armed before Yahweh* (32). The
war is Yahweh's, and the obligation is owed to him as well as to their brothers.

**Guiltless, and the sin that finds you.** *Guiltless towards Yahweh, and towards Israel* is *nəqiyyim
mēYHWH ûmiyyiśrāʾēl* (22; MAM): clear of obligation on both sides. The alternative is *behold, you have
sinned against Yahweh; and be sure your sin will find you out* — *ûdəʿû ḥaṭṭaʾtkem ʾăšer timṣāʾ ʾetkem*,
literally *know your sin, which will find you* (23; MAM). The proverb in English is about a hidden sin
being exposed. In context it is about a broken promise catching up with the one who made it: sin is
personified as something that comes looking. See [[Sin]] and [[Guilt and Conscience]].

***Subdued.*** *The land is subdued* is *wənikbəšâ hāʾāreṣ* (22, 29; MAM), from *kābaš*, the verb of
Genesis 1:28, *fill the earth, and subdue it* (*wəkibšuhā*, MAM). The wiki notes the shared verb; the
chapter does not cite Genesis.

**Children before sheep.** The tribes say *We will build sheepfolds here for our livestock, and cities for
our little ones* (16). Moses gives the instruction back in the opposite order: *Build cities for your
little ones, and folds for your sheep* (24). Rashi, following Midrash Tanchuma, reads the order as a
rebuke — they *paid more regard to their property than to their sons and daughters*, and Moses told them
to put first things first (Rashi on 32:16, Sefaria English). The reversal is in the text; the rebuke is
an interpretation. In their reply the tribes list *our little ones, our wives, our flocks, and all our
livestock* (26) — family first.

**A vow in all but name.** *Do that which has proceeded out of your mouth* (24) echoes the vow law,
*He shall do according to all that proceeds out of his mouth* (30:2). The tribes' promise is not called a
vow, but Moses treats it like one, binding before Yahweh, and Rashi makes the link explicit (on 32:24,
citing Numbers 30:3 in the Hebrew numbering). See [[Vows of Men and Women]] and [[Oaths and Vows]].

**Handed on.** Moses will not cross the Jordan (27:12–14), so the agreement needs witnesses who will. He
commands *concerning them* Eleazar, Joshua, and the heads of the fathers' houses (28), and gives them both
outcomes: Gilead if the two tribes cross, a possession *among you in the land of Canaan* if not (29–30).
The second outcome is not a punishment but a fallback; they would not lose their place in Israel. The
tribes' last answer turns Moses' words into Yahweh's: *As Yahweh has said to your servants, so will we do*
(31).

**Beyond the Jordan — from where?** Verse 19 uses one phrase for both banks: *we will not inherit with them
on the other side of the Jordan* (*mēʿēber layyardēn*, west), *because our inheritance has come to us on
this side of the Jordan eastward* (*mēʿēber hayyardēn mizrāḥâ*; MAM). *ʿĒber hayyardēn* means *across the
Jordan* and takes its direction from the speaker's position or from an added *eastward* or *westward*.
Here the speakers are on the east bank. The wiki's title keeps the conventional English phrase, *beyond the
Jordan*, for the east.

**The half-tribe of Manasseh.** Manasseh does not ask, is not rebuked, and is not party to the condition,
but verse 33 includes *the half-tribe of Manasseh the son of Joseph* (*waḥăṣî šēbeṭ mənaššeh*, MAM) in the
grant. Verses 39–42 then explain it after the fact: Manassite clans had taken land in Gilead and Bashan by
their own conquest. The chapter does not say whether the half-tribe was bound by the same condition.
Deuteronomy 3:18–20 and Joshua 1:12–15 address all two and a half tribes together, and Joshua 4:12 has the
half-tribe cross armed with the others (WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested). This is the first time
the expression *half-tribe of Manasseh* occurs in Genesis through Numbers (stored WEB); see
[[Two Tribes in Place of One - The Adoption of Ephraim and Manasseh]] and [[Manasseh]].

**Who built what — and a disagreement with Joshua.** Gad builds Dibon, Ataroth, Aroer, Atrothshophan,
Jazer, Jogbehah, Beth Nimrah, and Beth Haran; Reuben builds Heshbon, Elealeh, Kiriathaim, Nebo, Baal Meon,
and Sibmah (34–38). *Built* (*wayyibnû*, MAM) here probably means rebuilt or fortified, since these are
conquered towns already named in verse 3. Joshua 13's allotment does not match this list town for town:
it puts Dibon (13:17), Aroer *on the edge of the valley of the Arnon* (13:16), and Heshbon (13:17) inside
Reuben's border, while Gad's border runs *from Heshbon* and reaches *Aroer that is near Rabbah* (13:25–26)
(WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested). So Numbers 32 has Gad building Dibon and Aroer and Reuben
building Heshbon, and Joshua 13 gives Dibon to Reuben, names two Aroers, and lists Heshbon on both borders.
Possible explanations — building is not the same as allotment, the border lists describe a later state, or
two Aroers are meant in Numbers too — are not settled by the text. The wiki records both lists without
harmonizing them. See [[Dibon]], [[Aroer]], and [[Heshbon]].

**Names changed.** Verse 38 adds *(their names being changed)*, *mûsabbōt šēm*, after Nebo and Baal Meon,
and says *they gave other names to the cities which they built* (MAM). The text does not say what the new
names were. Both old names are divine names elsewhere: Nebo is the Babylonian god of Isaiah 46:1 (*Nebo
stoops*; WEB via bible-api.com), and Baal is the Canaanite god. Rashi takes these as idol names the
Reubenites changed (on 32:38, Sefaria English). That reading suits the parenthesis, but the text gives no
reason for the change. The old names stayed in use: Joshua 13:17 lists *Beth Baal Meon* and Deuteronomy
32:49 places *Mount Nebo* in Moab (WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested). Rashi also identifies the
Sebam of verse 3 with the Sibmah of verse 38; the wiki follows him, and does the same for Beon and Baal
Meon and for Nimrah and Beth Nimrah, on the spellings and the matching lists. See [[Names and Naming]].

**Machir, Jair, and Nobah.** The chapter closes with three conquests, each in a single verse or two, and
two of them end in a naming. Machir's children take Gilead (39–40) — the clan of the man who was the last
person named in Genesis, now holding the region where Genesis 31 set the heap of witness. Jair takes
*ḥawwōtêhem*, *their villages* (probably tent-villages; the meaning of *ḥawwôt* is not certain) and calls
them *Havvoth Jair* (41; MAM). Nobah takes Kenath *and its villages* — *bənōtêhā*, *its daughters* — and
calls it *Nobah, after his own name* (42; MAM). Deuteronomy 3:14 says Jair took *all the region of Argob*
in Bashan and called it Havvoth Jair; Judges 10:3–4 credits the name to *Jair, the Gileadite*, a judge
whose thirty sons had *thirty cities, which are called Havvoth Jair to this day* (WEB via bible-api.com,
not yet ingested). Whether these are one naming told twice or two Jairs and two explanations is not
settled; see [[Havvoth Jair]] and [[Jair (son of Manasseh)]].

**What the agreement became.** Deuteronomy 3:18–20 has Moses retell the command; Joshua 1:12–18 has Joshua
remind the two and a half tribes of it, and they answer *All that you have commanded us we will do*; Joshua
4:12–13 has them cross *armed before the children of Israel, as Moses spoke to them*, about forty thousand
men; and Joshua 22:1–9 has Joshua send them home: *You have not left your brothers these many days to this
day* (WEB via bible-api.com, not yet ingested). The condition was kept. What followed was less settled:
Joshua 22 continues with the altar by the Jordan and a near war; Deborah's song asks Reuben *Why did you
sit among the sheepfolds?* and says *Gilead lived beyond the Jordan* (Judges 5:16–17); and 1 Chronicles
5:25–26 reports that the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh were the ones carried away by
Assyria after they *played the prostitute after the gods of the peoples of the land* (WEB via
bible-api.com). See [[The Two and a Half Tribes - Numbers 32, Deuteronomy 3, and Joshua 1, 4, 22]].

**Outside the promise?** Moses' rebuke treats crossing the Jordan as entering *the land which Yahweh has
given them* (7, 9), and verse 30 calls the west bank *the land of Canaan*. Numbers 34 will draw Canaan's
border along the Jordan. So the eastern tribes hold land Israel has conquered but which is not called
Canaan here. The chapter does not say that the grant is a lesser inheritance, and Moses gives it as *a
possession before Yahweh* (22). The wiki records the question, not an answer. See [[Land and Sojourning]].

**Outside the Bible.** The Mesha Stele, a ninth-century BC Moabite inscription, reports that the men of Gad
had long lived in the land of Ataroth, and names several towns of this chapter as Moabite in Mesha's
time, Nebo, Baal Meon, and Kiriathaim among them. That is a paraphrase from memory of standard
translations of the stele, not a quotation; it was not checked against a published edition in this pass, and the wiki relies on it
only to show that these were real places contested between Israel and Moab.

**Boundary and naming.** The planned boundary, 32:1–42, is kept: chapter 33 begins the list of stages.
The provisional title is kept. Person pages are created for Jair, qualified as *(son of Manasseh)*
because Judges 10 has another Jair, and for Nobah, qualified as *(person)* because his name is also a
place name (42; Judges 8:11). Place pages are created for the eleven towns not yet in the wiki and for
Havvoth Jair and Kenath. *Nebo* is qualified as *(city)* because Mount Nebo and the god Nebo will need
their own treatment. Kenath takes its original name, with *Nobah* recorded as its other name, so that it
does not collide with the man. No page is created for the Amorites, who are named here and repeatedly
since Genesis 14; that is a lint candidate.
