# The March from Sinai

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 10:11–36

## Summary

Israel leaves Sinai. On the twentieth day of the second month of the second year, the cloud rises from the
tabernacle (11). That date falls six days after the second Passover of 9:11, and nineteen days after the census
order of 1:1. By Exodus 19:1 the people have been at the mountain since the third month of the first year.

The rule of [[The Cloud over the Tabernacle]] now happens for the first time. The people go *according to the
commandment of Yahweh by Moses* (13), the same phrase that closed 9:23. Verse 12 states the whole journey in a
line, out of the wilderness of Sinai, with the cloud stopping in the wilderness of Paran.

Verses 14–28 then report the march, and it is [[The Camp Around the Tent]] put into motion. Judah's standard goes
first, with Issachar and Zebulun. The tabernacle comes down, and Gershon and Merari carry it (17). Reuben's standard
follows, with Simeon and Gad. The Kohathites come next, *bearing the sanctuary*, and the text adds that the others
had the tabernacle up before they arrived (21). Ephraim's standard follows, with Manasseh and Benjamin, and Dan's
comes last with Asher and Naphtali, *the rear guard of all the camps* (25). Every tribe has its commander, and they
are the twelve men of the census. Verse 28 ends the list: *Thus were the travels of the children of Israel*.

The narrative then steps aside for a private conversation. Moses asks Hobab, the son of Reuel the Midianite, to come
with Israel, *for Yahweh has spoken good concerning Israel* (29). Hobab refuses. He will go to his own land and
relatives (30). Moses asks again: *you know how we are to encamp in the wilderness, and you can be our eyes* (31), and
whatever good Yahweh does for Israel, Israel will do for him (32). The text gives no answer. The next verse is the
camp moving.

The march lasts three days (33). The ark of Yahweh's covenant goes before them *to seek out a resting place*, and the
cloud is over them by day (34). The unit closes with two short cries of Moses, one for each thing the ark does.
When it sets out: *Rise up, Yahweh, and let your enemies be scattered!* (35). When it rests: *Return, Yahweh, to the ten
thousands of the thousands of Israel* (36).

The whole book up to here has been preparation, and this unit is the moment it was for. Everything has gone right so
far, with every tribe in place and every command kept. The next verse is 11:1, and the people complain.

## Notes

**The first use of the rule.** *ʿAl-pî yhwh bəyad-mōšeh* (13, WLC) repeats the last words of 9:23. Numbers 9 said how
every journey would work, and this verse says the first one worked that way. Exodus 40:36 had already given the same
rule for *all their journeys*. By the tags, the root *nāsaʿ*, *set out*, comes fourteen times in verses 12–35.

**No trumpet.** The unit before this one set up alarms for moving the camps (10:5–6), and this one never mentions
them. See the note at [[The Two Silver Trumpets]]. The wiki does not take the silence as non-use.

**Chapter 2 in motion.** The same four standards go in the same order. *Degel* comes four times here (14, 18, 22, 25;
WLC). Chapter 2 said where each camp goes, and this unit adds who is doing the moving. The twelve fathers' names repeat
exactly from chapter 1, with Deuel at verse 20, not the Reuel of 2:14. The commanders are each *over his army*, and
the word *nāśîʾ* is absent from verses 11–36 (tags), so the men called princes in chapters 1, 2, and 7 are named here only as commanders. The
differences over the Levites are set out at [[As They Encamp, So They Set Out - Numbers 2.17 and 10.14-28]].

**Rear guard.** *Məʾassēp* (25, WLC) is a piel participle of the verb for *gather*, so Dan closes the column as the
one who gathers it in. How that worked, the verse does not say. Isaiah 52:12 uses the same participle with a suffix of
God (*ûməʾassipḵem*, WLC, WEB *your rear guard*), with Yahweh also going before. It does not cite Numbers.

**Set up before they arrived.** Verse 21 is the only reason the unit gives for anything in the order. *Wəhēqîmû ʾet-hammiškān
ʿad-bōʾām* (WLC) has no named subject. WEB's *the others* is an interpretation, and in context Gershon and Merari,
who went ahead with the structure, are the likely subject. The Tent is standing when the holy things reach it.

**Hobab's relationship.** *Ḥōtēn mōšeh* (29, WLC) stands after both *Hobab* and *the son of Reuel the Midianite*, so the
words can describe either man. If they describe Reuel, Hobab is Moses' brother-in-law. If Hobab, he is the
father-in-law. The wiki keeps him on his own page, and sets out the names at [[Jethro]]. It
adopts neither reading. Judges 4:11 calls Hobab *ḥōtēn mōšeh* too (WLC). Rahlfs's Greek has *Ιωβαβ* here, and *γαμβρῷ*
for the relationship.

**The good, five times.** The conversation turns on one root. Verse 29 has *wəhēṭaḇnû*, *we will treat well*, and *ṭôḇ*,
*good*; verse 32 has *haṭṭôḇ*, *yêṭîḇ*, and *wəhēṭaḇnû* (WLC, tags). Moses offers Hobab a share in what Yahweh has
promised, and the offer does not ask him to do anything but come, until verse 31.

**Don't leave us.** The second request changes the ground. Verse 29 offers Hobab something, and verse 31 asks for
something: *ʿal-kēn yādaʿtā ḥănōtēnû bammidbār*, *you know our camping in the wilderness* (WLC). This comes one chapter
after the camp was told that Yahweh's cloud picks every stop. The wiki does not read that as unbelief. A cloud may
pick the place, and a desert man may still know its water and pasture, though the text does not say so. The tension is
left standing at [[Over the Tent and Before the People - Where the Cloud Stood]]. Moses asked at Exodus 33:15–16 for God's
presence to go with Israel, with no human guide in view; here he asks a human guide, a few verses before the ark goes ahead.

**Be our eyes.** *Ləʿênāyim* (31, WLC) is direct, and WEB follows it. Rahlfs's Greek differs throughout the verse: *οὗ
εἵνεκεν ἦσθα μεθ᾿ ἡμῶν ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ καὶ ἔσῃ ἐν ἡμῖν πρεσβύτης*, roughly *since you were with us in the wilderness, and you
will be an elder among us*. The wiki compared Rahlfs's text only, not the manuscripts, and does not decide how the
readings are related.

**No ending.** After verse 32 Hobab says nothing, and Numbers never mentions him again (tags: *ḥōḇāḇ* comes only here and at
Judges 4:11). The later Kenites in the land suggest that someone stayed, but that is an inference. See
[[Hobab and the Kenites - Numbers 10, Judges 1 and 4, and 1 Samuel 15]].

**The Mount of Yahweh.** *Har yhwh* (33, WLC) is unexpected. Exodus calls this mountain *the mountain of God*. By the tags,
in Exodus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy this is the only *har yhwh*, and elsewhere the phrase points to Zion or the temple
mountain (Psalm 24:3; Isaiah 2:3; 30:29). WEB's *On Yahweh's mountain* at Genesis 22:14 stands at Moriah, though its Hebrew was not checked for this page. The wiki notes the wording at the moment
Israel leaves, and draws nothing from it.

**The ark goes ahead.** The verse says *ʾărôn bərît-yhwh*, and that full title occurs in Numbers only here and at 14:44
(tags; the ark is otherwise *the ark* at 3:31 and 10:35, and *the ark of the Testimony* at 4:5 and 7:89). Numbers 14:44 is the
one place the ark stays behind, when Israel goes up without it, and is struck. *Lāṯûr*, *to seek out* (33), is the verb
for spying the land in chapters 13–14 (tags). So the ark does here what the spies will do there, and Deuteronomy 1:33
gives the same infinitive to Yahweh himself. The comparison is the wiki's. *Three days' journey* comes twice. It may be
the length of the march, or how far ahead the ark went, and the text does not say. At the Jordan the distance is about
two thousand cubits (Joshua 3:4).

**Two cries.** Verse 35 uses *qûmâ yhwh*, and verse 36 *šûbâ yhwh*. Both are short, both are spoken to Yahweh, and
they answer the ark's two motions, *binsōaʿ* and *ûḇənuḥōh* (WLC). *Yōʾmar* in verse 36 is imperfect (tags), and the
two lines read like a repeated use, though the first verb is *wayyōʾmer*. Whether the unit means every time, or this
time, the wording does not settle. The enemies are named as Yahweh's, *your enemies*, with no battle in sight. The ark moves
as if it were a king going out. The picture is the wiki's, and the verse gives only the cry. See
[[Rise Up, Yahweh - The Ark's Cry in Numbers 10, Psalm 68, and Psalm 132]].

**Return to whom.** Verse 36 has no preposition: *šûbâ yhwh riḇəḇôt ʾalpê yiśrāʾēl* (WLC). WEB supplies *to*. Rahlfs's
Greek makes a different sentence, *ἐπίστρεφε κύριε χιλιάδας μυριάδας ἐν τῷ Ισραηλ*. A reader can take *return* as
*return to*, or as *turn back* or *bring back* the thousands. The wiki reports WEB and does not settle it. Either
way, the number recalls the armies just listed.

**Resting place.** *Mənûḥâ* (33) and *bənuḥōh*, *when it rested* (36), are from one root, and the last paragraph of the unit
begins and ends on rest. Exodus 33:14 had promised it to Moses: *My presence will go with you, and I will give you rest*,
with *wahănīḥōtî lāḵ* (WLC). The people are not resting for long, as the next chapter shows.

**Marks in the text.** WLC prints an inverted nun (׆) after verse 34 and again after verse 36, so verses 35–36 are set off
from what surrounds them. The morphhb edition notes *Inverted nun in the text* at both places. Rahlfs's text has a different
order, with the two cries before the verse about the cloud (its 10:34–35 are the Hebrew 35–36, and its 10:36 is the Hebrew 34).
So the two witnesses agree that these lines are somehow marked or movable, though they show it differently. This wiki
did not consult the rabbinic discussion of the marks or the Greek manuscripts, and gives no account of why they exist.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Where the cloud stopped.** Verse 12 has it stopping in the wilderness of Paran. But Taberah (11:3), Kibroth
   Hattaavah (11:34), and Hazeroth (11:35) come first, and 12:16 reaches Paran only after Hazeroth. Numbers 33:16–18 lists
   Kibroth Hattaavah, Hazeroth, and then Rithmah, with no Paran. Verse 12 can be a summary of the whole stage, but the
   text does not say that. See [[Paran]].
2. **Where the ark was.** Verse 21 has the Kohathites carrying the sanctuary in the middle of the column, and the ark is
   part of their load (3:31; 4:5, 15). Verse 33 has the ark in front. Either the ark moved apart on this march, or these
   lines speak differently, and neither is stated.
3. **Who chooses the camp.** Cloud (9:17), Hobab (10:31), ark (10:33), and Yahweh (Deuteronomy 1:33).
4. **Who Hobab is.** See above, and [[Jethro]].
5. **Three days, then what.** Verse 33 gives three days, and Numbers 33:16 gives one stage from Sinai to Kibroth Hattaavah.
   How the two line up is not stated.
