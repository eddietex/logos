# Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 25:1–18

## Summary

Israel stays at Shittim, where sexual infidelity with Moabite women leads into sacrificial meals and
worship of their gods. Israel joins itself to Baal Peor; Yahweh commands judgment on the chiefs, and
Moses orders the judges to kill those attached to Baal Peor (1–5).

Before Moses and a congregation weeping at the Tent’s entrance, an Israelite brings a Midianite
woman. Phinehas follows with a spear and kills them both; the plague stops, with 24,000 dead (6–9).
Yahweh credits Phinehas with sharing his jealousy and making atonement, and grants him and his
offspring a covenant of peace and everlasting priesthood (10–13).

Only then are the pair named: Zimri son of Salu, a Simeonite household prince, and Cozbi daughter of
Zur, a Midianite leader. The final command is to harass and strike Midian because of its deception
in the Peor affair (14–18).

## Notes

**Blessing and accountability.** Coming immediately after Balaam’s blessings, this judgment shows
that protection from a hostile curse is not immunity from Israel’s own apostasy. Balaam is not named
in chapter 25; the later attribution in Numbers 31:16 must not be silently inserted into this scene.

**Distinct people and incomplete narrative details.** The opening names Moabite women; Cozbi is
Midianite. Neither ethnicity is a synonym for the other. Verses 4–5 command action, but do not
narrate completion of every execution. The precise relation of the two commands remains unstated.
The pavilion scene does not explicitly describe intercourse or identify Cozbi as a cult prostitute.

**Peace through killing.** The text explicitly praises Phinehas’s act and calls it atonement, while
describing two human deaths. The covenant’s designation as peace preserves a real moral tension with
the violence that precedes it. It is a particular narrated judgment, not a general authorization for
private religious violence. Earlier Aaronic appointment remains in force; this is a promise
concerning Phinehas and his offspring, not the first creation of priesthood.
