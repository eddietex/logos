# The Borders of the Land

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 34:1–29

## Summary

Yahweh gives Moses the boundaries of Canaan: the southern line beside Zin and Edom runs
past Kadesh Barnea to the brook of Egypt and the sea; the sea forms the west; the northern
line runs through Mount Hor and the entrance of Hamath to Hazar Enan; the east descends
past the sea of Chinnereth along the Jordan to the Salt Sea (1–12).

Moses explains that this is the inheritance by lot for nine and a half tribes. Reuben, Gad,
and half of Manasseh already have their inheritance east of the Jordan (13–15). A second
speech appoints Eleazar and Joshua to divide the land, with ten named tribal representatives,
including Caleb for Judah (16–29). The chapter defines a future distribution, not a completed
conquest or a record of fields already allocated.

## Key verses

> **34:2** “Command the children of Israel, and tell them, ‘When you come into the land of Canaan (this is the land that shall fall to you for an inheritance, even the land of Canaan according to its borders),

> **34:13** Moses commanded the children of Israel, saying, “This is the land which you shall inherit by lot, which Yahweh has commanded to give to the nine tribes, and to the half-tribe;

> **34:15** The two tribes and the half-tribe have received their inheritance beyond the Jordan at Jericho eastward, toward the sunrise.”

> **34:17** “These are the names of the men who shall divide the land to you for inheritance: Eleazar the priest, and Joshua the son of Nun.

> **34:29** These are they whom Yahweh commanded to divide the inheritance to the children of Israel in the land of Canaan.

## Places

- [[Canaan]] — The land is defined by a circuit of southern, western, northern, and eastern borders (2–12). Verses 13–15 distinguish this allotment for nine and a half tribes from the inheritance east of the Jordan; Canaan here does not include every Israelite possession.
- [[Edom]] — The southern border begins from the wilderness of Zin alongside Edom (3). Edom locates the boundary; it is not included as a new grant in this description.
- [[The Wilderness of Zin]] — Zin marks the southern frontier alongside Edom, with the boundary running south of Kadesh Barnea (3–4). This is a border description rather than another wilderness march.
- [[Kadesh]] — The southern border passes south of Kadesh Barnea toward Hazar Addar and Azmon (4). The place previously associated with refusal and Meribah now serves as a boundary marker.
- [[The Salt Sea]] — The southern boundary begins at its eastern end (3), and the eastern border descends along the Jordan to end here (12). It anchors two sides of the circuit.
- [[Hamath]] — The entrance of Hamath marks the northern line after Mount Hor and before Zedad (8). The boundary repeats a northern reference from the spies’ journey (Numbers 13:21); it does not report completed occupation.
- [[The Jordan]] — The eastern line descends along the Jordan to the Salt Sea (12). Verses 14–15 simultaneously recognize Israelite inheritance east of it, distinguishing Canaan’s boundary from all Israel’s holdings.
- [[Jericho]] — Jericho locates the already received inheritance beyond the Jordan eastward (15); no conquest of Jericho is narrated here.
- [[The Ascent of Akrabbim]] — Numbers 34:4. The southern border turns south of this ascent before passing to Zin.
- [[Hazar Addar]] — Numbers 34:4. A southern boundary marker between Kadesh Barnea and Azmon.
- [[Azmon]] — Numbers 34:4–5. A southern boundary marker after Hazar Addar, from which the border turns to the brook of Egypt.
- [[The Brook of Egypt]] — Numbers 34:5. The southern border reaches this brook from Azmon and ends at the sea. This page does not equate the brook with the Nile or identify it with Genesis 15:18’s river of Egypt.
- [[The Great Sea]] — Numbers 34:5–7. The sea defines the western border and provides the starting point for the northern line.
- [[Mount Hor (northern border)]] — Numbers 34:7–8. A northern boundary landmark between the great sea and the entrance of Hamath. Its role differs from [[Mount Hor]], the mountain beside Edom where Aaron died (Numbers 20:22–29). The shared name does not justify merging these geographical descriptions.
- [[Zedad]] — Numbers 34:8–9. The northern boundary passes here after the entrance of Hamath, continuing to Ziphron.
- [[Ziphron]] — Numbers 34:9. The northern boundary runs here before ending at Hazar Enan.
- [[Hazar Enan]] — Numbers 34:9–10. The endpoint of the northern border and the starting point of the eastern border toward Shepham.
- [[Shepham]] — Numbers 34:10–11. The eastern border runs from Hazar Enan here, then down to Riblah.
- [[Riblah]] — Numbers 34:11. An eastern boundary landmark located on the east side of Ain. No identification with another Riblah is assumed.
- [[Ain]] — Numbers 34:11. A landmark used to locate Riblah on its east side in the eastern boundary description.
- [[The Sea of Chinnereth]] — Numbers 34:11–12. The eastern border reaches the east side of this sea and then goes down to the Jordan and the Salt Sea.

## Notes

- **Two speeches, one distribution plan.** The retained unit joins the land’s extent (1–15)
  to the personnel responsible for distributing it (16–29).
- **Two Mount Hors.** The northern boundary landmark in 7–8 is kept distinct from Aaron’s
  death site beside Edom (20:22–29). Neither is assigned a modern summit here.
- **Ten representatives for nine and a half tribes.** Manasseh supplies a representative for
  its western share; Reuben and Gad already have eastern land. The list is not the earlier
  twelve-prince census roster. Elizaphan son of Parnach is not identified with Elzaphan son
  of Uzziel, and Kemuel son of Shiphtan is distinguished by patronymic.
- **Different borders remain different.** Genesis 15:18 names the Euphrates, while this chapter
  does not. Numbers 34 itself recognizes inheritance outside Canaan. A map that treats all
  biblical land formulas as identical would suppress those distinctions.
- **Lot and appointed officers.** Verse 13 and verses 17–29 require both; their exact procedure
  is not explained. No individual tribal boundary is assigned in this chapter.
