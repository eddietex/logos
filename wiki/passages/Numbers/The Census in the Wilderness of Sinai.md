# The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 1:1–54

## Summary

A month after the tabernacle was raised, Yahweh speaks to Moses in the Tent of Meeting and orders a
count. Every male of twenty and upward who can go out to war is to be registered by family and by
name, with Moses and Aaron doing the counting and one man from each tribe standing with them
(1–4). Those twelve men are named (5–16). The whole congregation assembles on the same day and
declares its ancestry (18), and Moses counts them *in the wilderness of Sinai* (19).

The tribes are then reported one by one, in a fixed formula, from Reuben's 46,500 to Naphtali's
53,400 (20–43). Joseph appears as two tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh, and the sum is 603,550 (44–46).

Levi is not in it (47). Yahweh's speech says why: the Levites are appointed over the Tabernacle of
the Testimony instead. They carry it, take it down, set it up, and camp around it. The other tribes
camp by their standards, and the Levites stand between them and the wrath that an outsider's
approach would bring (48–53). The chapter ends with Israel doing what was commanded (54).

This is the first unit of the book, so it sets the frame. Israel has been a family, then a crowd of
slaves, then a congregation at a mountain. Here it is enrolled as an army with a sanctuary at its
center, and it is about to march.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The plan takes the chapter whole. Verses 47–54 could stand alone as the appointment of
the Levites, but verse 47 needs them to explain itself, and verse 54 closes the whole command. The
next chapter opens with a new speech, to Moses and Aaron.

**The date.** *The first day of the second month, in the second year* (1). The tabernacle went up on
the first day of the first month of that year (Exodus 40:17), so Leviticus has filled about a month.
Israel leaves on the twentieth of this second month (Numbers 10:11). The book is not strictly in
order: the Passover speech at 9:1 is dated to the first month, before this verse.

**The name of the book.** The Hebrew title *Bəmidbar* comes from this first verse (WLC, *bəmidbar
sînāy*), and it fits the book better than the English title drawn from the censuses. Those two facts
about the titles are traditional and were not checked against an edition here.

**Two verbs turned over.** The census runs on *pāqad*, *to count, muster*, 22 times in the chapter,
and on the idiom *nāśāʾ rōʾš*, *lift the head* (2). Verses 49–50 reuse both. Yahweh tells Moses *lōʾ
tipqōd* and *lōʾ tiśśāʾ* of Levi: do not count them, do not lift their head. Then *hapqēd*, appoint
them, and *hēmmâ yiśʾû ʾet-hammiškān*, they shall lift the tabernacle (WLC). WEB's *count*, *take a
census*, *appoint*, and *carry* cannot show it. The tribe whose head is not lifted lifts the
dwelling. That is an observation about the wording; the text makes nothing of it.

**The register.**

The twelve figures add up to the stated total. Every one ends in hundreds except Gad's 45,650. The
text gives no reason for that rounding.

**Two orders in one chapter.** The name list (5–15) puts Leah's sons first, then Rachel's line, then
Dan, Asher, Gad, Naphtali. The census (20–43) moves Gad up to third, behind Simeon. That is where he
stands in the camp at 2:10–14, under Reuben's standard, so the census order may anticipate the camp.
Neither order is explained. See [[The Twelve Sons and the Lists That Reorder Them]].

**What the old lines look like now.** Reuben is still *Israel's firstborn* (20), with that phrase
given to no other tribe here, even after Genesis 49:4. Judah is the largest. Ephraim outnumbers
Manasseh, as Genesis 48:19 said the younger would be greater, but at Numbers 26:34, 37 the figures
are Manasseh 52,700 and Ephraim 32,500. Simeon, third here at 59,300, falls to 22,200 at 26:14. None
of this is commented on. Reading the numbers as verdicts is the reader's move.

**Who is not counted.** Women, boys under twenty, men who cannot fight, the Levites, and, as far as
the chapter says, the *mixed multitude* of Exodus 12:38. Verse 2 limits it to males, and verse 3 to
those fit for service. The total is therefore a muster and not a population.

**Why Levi is out.** The reason the chapter gives is service: *appoint the Levites over the
Tabernacle of the Testimony* (50). Nothing here mentions land. The landless Levi of Numbers 18:20 is
a later statement, and two wiki pages that gave landlessness as the reason for this exclusion have
been corrected. The chapter does not cite Levi's sword at Exodus 32:26–29 or the scattering of
Genesis 49:7 either.

**The stranger and the wrath.** *Zār* (51) means here anyone who is not a Levite. At 3:10 it means
anyone who is not of Aaron's house, so the word is relative to the line being guarded. *Strange
fire* at Leviticus 10:1 is the same word, *ʾēš zārâ* (WLC). *Qeṣep*, *wrath* (53), recurs in Numbers
only at 18:5 and WLC 17:11 (WEB 16:46), and the verb stands at Leviticus 10:6, *he not be angry with
all the congregation* (WLC lemma search). Nadab and Abihu are recent, and the Levite cordon answers
exactly what happened to them. The text does not draw the line itself. See
[[The Stranger Who Comes Near - Numbers 1.51, 3.10, 18.7, and Leviticus 10]].

**The thousands.** Verse 16 calls the twelve *rāʾšê ʾalpê yiśrāʾēl*, *heads of the thousands of
Israel* (WLC). The wiki recorded three answers to the size of the army at Exodus 12:37: take the
figure as it stands, read it as schematic or symbolic, or read *ʾelep* as a clan or unit. It still
adopts none. This chapter adds constraints that any answer must meet. The Hebrew total at 46 reads
as ordinary hundreds and thousands, and half of 603,550 is the 301,775 shekels of Exodus 38:25–26,
so that account also computes with plain numbers. On the other side, the
22,273 firstborn of 3:43 sit oddly against more than 603,550 males. See
[[Six Hundred Three Thousand Five Hundred Fifty - Exodus 38.26 and Numbers 1.46]].

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Before and after.** Exodus 38:26 reports 603,550 men paying silver for sockets, in an account
   placed before the raising of the tabernacle in the first month (Exodus 40:17). Numbers 1 orders
   the count in the second month and reaches the same number. Neither text reconciles this.
2. **The ransom.** Exodus 30:12 requires a ransom at a census *that there be no plague*. Numbers 1
   mentions no ransom and no plague. It may assume the silver or may be a different kind of count.
   The chapter does not say.
3. **Deuel or Reuel.** Eliasaph's father is *Dəʿûʾēl* at 1:14, 7:42, and 10:20, and *Rəʿûʾēl* at
   2:14 (WLC; WEB follows both). Daleth and resh are easily confused, which is a common explanation
   and not something the text confirms.
4. **Counted and uncountable.** Genesis 15:5 says the stars cannot be counted, and the offspring
   will be like them. Here the offspring are counted, by divine command. See
   [[Dust, Stars, and Sand - The Offspring Promises]].
