# The People Refuse the Land

**Book:** [[Numbers]]
**Reference:** Numbers 14:1–38

## Summary

The chapter picks up where the grasshoppers left off. The whole congregation cries out and weeps
through the night (1). They murmur against Moses and Aaron: *We wish that we had died in the land of
Egypt, or that we had died in this wilderness!* (2). Then they name the one they actually blame,
*Why does Yahweh bring us to this land, to fall by the sword?* (3), and they say to one another,
*Let us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt* (4). This is the exodus undone in a sentence.
It is said at the border of the land, with the fruit in front of them.

Moses and Aaron fall on their faces (5). Joshua and Caleb tear their clothes, and Joshua speaks for
the first time in this story. The land is *an exceeding good land*. If Yahweh delights in us he will
bring us in. *Don't rebel against Yahweh, neither fear the people of the land; for they are bread
for us* (6–9). The ten had called it a land that eats its inhabitants, and these two say that the
people in it are what will be eaten. The congregation's answer is to speak of stoning them, and at
that moment Yahweh's glory appears at the Tent (10).

Yahweh asks Moses how long the people will despise him and refuse to believe, and he makes the offer
he made at the calf. He will strike them with pestilence, disinherit them, and make a greater nation
from Moses (11–12). Moses declines without saying so. He argues from what the nations will say,
*Because Yahweh was not able to bring this people into the land* (13–16), and then from what Yahweh
himself said at Sinai, reciting the proclamation of Exodus 34 (17–18), and he asks for pardon (19).

*I have pardoned according to your word* (20). The next word is *but*. As Yahweh lives, the men who
saw his glory and his signs and tested him *these ten times* will not see the land (21–23). The
exception is *my servant Caleb*, who had another spirit (24). Tomorrow they are to turn back toward
the Red Sea (25).

A second speech gives the sentence in detail, and it takes their words as its terms. *As you have
spoken in my ears, so will I do to you* (28). They wished to die in this wilderness, and they will.
All who were counted from twenty years old upward will fall there, except Caleb and Joshua (29–30).
They said their little ones would be prey, and the little ones will go in (31). The children will be
shepherds in the wilderness for forty years, a year for each day of the search, until the fathers'
bodies are consumed (32–35).

The ten who brought the report die *by the plague before Yahweh*. Joshua and Caleb live (36–38).

The pardon and the sentence stand in adjacent verses and the chapter does not reconcile them. The
nation is not destroyed and the promise is not withdrawn. The men who refused it do not receive it.

## Notes

**They lifted up their voice.** *Wattiśśāʾ kol-hāʿēdâ wayyittənû ʾet-qôlām* (1, WLC) has the verb
*nāśāʾ*, with no stated object until the second verb supplies *voice*. It is the first of six uses
in these verses. The congregation lifts up its voice (1), Yahweh is the one who *lifts* iniquity
(18), Moses says he has done it from Egypt until now (19), he has lifted his hand to swear (30), and
now the children and the fathers will *lift*, that is bear, their own guilt (33, 34). WEB has
*lifted up*, *forgiving*, *forgiven*, *swore*, and *bear*, so the English cannot show that one verb
is carrying the chapter. Yahweh has carried Israel's iniquity all the way here. The sentence moves
that load onto them. That reading is the wiki's. The verb is common, and the argument rests only on
the concentration in one passage.

**That night.** The people weep *ballaylâ hahûʾ*. Rahlfs has *ὅλην τὴν νύκτα ἐκείνην*, *all that
night*. A rabbinic tradition ties this night to the ninth of Av, as a weeping without cause that was
repaid with cause; that is recalled and was not checked in any edition.

**The wish.** *Lû mattnû bəʾereṣ miṣrayim ʾô bammidbār hazzeh lû mātnû* (2, WLC). The sentence opens
and closes with *lû*. This is the wish of Exodus 16:3, with a new alternative added, *in this
wilderness*, and verses 28–29 grant exactly that half of it. Exodus 16:3 was answered with bread.

**They name Yahweh.** The narrator reports murmuring as being against Moses and Aaron (see
[[Murmuring]]), and does so again in verse 2. In the next verse the congregation itself says *lāmâ
YHWH mēbîʾ ʾōtānû*, *why is Yahweh bringing us* (3, WLC). The participle is the verb of the promise.
The hiphil of *bôʾ*, *bring*, comes five times and shapes the chapter: *why does Yahweh bring us*
(3); *he will bring us* (8); *not able to bring* (16); *him I will bring* (24); *them I will bring*
(31).

**A head.** *Nittənâ rōʾš wənāšûbâ miṣrāymâ* (4, WLC), literally *let us give a head*. The men of
Numbers 13:3 were *heads*, the same word in the plural. Israel was counted by heads of houses at
1:4. Rahlfs has *ἀρχηγόν*. The narrative does not say anyone was appointed. Nehemiah 9:17 says they
did. See [[A Captain to Return to Egypt - Numbers 14.4, Nehemiah 9.17, and Acts 7.39]].

**Falling.** *Nāpal* is a thread: the people fear they will *fall by the sword* (3), Moses and Aaron
*fall* on their faces (5), and the carcasses will *fall* in this wilderness (29, 32). The word will
return at verse 43, *you will fall by the sword*. Their fear is met, though in a different place.

**An exceeding good land.** *Ṭôbâ hāʾāreṣ məʾōd məʾōd* (7, WLC) doubles the intensive, and it
answers Moses' question of 13:19, *whether it is good or bad*. The phrasing of the clause before it,
*hāʾāreṣ ʾăšer ʿābarnû bāh lātûr ʾōtāh*, repeats the ten's words at 13:32 exactly, up to the point
where the two reports part. The two men begin with the other side's sentence and finish it
differently.

**If he delights in us.** *ʾIm-ḥāpēṣ bānû YHWH* (8, WLC). The condition is about Yahweh's
disposition, not the Anakim's height, so the matter is moved off the question of *able* altogether.
Joshua and Caleb supply what the ten left out at 13:31, the name of Yahweh, and they supply it three
times in two verses.

**They are bread for us.** *Kî laḥmēnû hēm* (9, WLC) is short and blunt: *for they are our bread*.
Rahlfs has *κατάβρωμα*, *food*. It answers *ʾereṣ ʾōkelet yôšəbêhā* (13:32): if the land eats, say
the two, we shall do the eating. Psalm 14:4 has the workers of iniquity eating God's people *as they
eat bread*, which shows the metaphor could run in either direction. See
[[A Land That Eats Up Its Inhabitants - Numbers 13.32, Leviticus 18 and 26, and Ezekiel 36]].

**Their shade.** *Sār ṣillām mēʿălêhem* (9, WLC) is literally *their shade has turned from over
them*. WEB has *their defense*. *Ṣēl* is the protecting shadow of Genesis 19:8 (*under the shadow of
my roof*) and, by the tags, of Psalm 91:1 and 121:5. Rahlfs has *ὁ καιρός*, *the time* or *the
season*, an unexpected rendering whose reasoning was not investigated. Set beside *wayhwh ʾittānû*,
*Yahweh is with us*, the clause says the nations are unprotected and Israel is not.

**Rebel.** *ʾAl-timrōdû* (9, WLC). *Mārad* has so far been used of the kings under Chedorlaomer
(Genesis 14:4) and occurs nowhere else in the Torah by the tags. It is a political verb, the word
for vassals who break with their overlord. It fits a people proposing to appoint a head of their
own.

**To stone them.** *Wayyōʾmərû kol-hāʿēdâ lirgôm ʾōtām bāʾăbānîm* (10, WLC). The verse reports
speech, and no stone is thrown; WEB's *threatened* is an interpretation of *said*. Moses said *They
are almost ready to stone me* at Exodus 17:4, with the verb *sāqal*. Here it is *rāgam*, the legal
verb of Leviticus 24:14–23. The glory comes at the moment the threat is made, and Rahlfs adds *in a
cloud, upon the tent*, against the Hebrew's *in the Tent of Meeting*.

**Despise.** *ʿAd-ʾānâ yənaʾăṣunî hāʿām hazzeh* (11, WLC). *Nāʾaṣ* first appears here by the tags,
and it recurs at verse 23 and 16:30; 1 Samuel 2:17 and Isaiah 1:4 will use it too. *ʿAd-ʾānâ* is the
*how long* of Exodus 16:28. *Lōʾ-yaʾămînû bî* is the verb of Exodus 4:31 and 14:31, where they did
believe.

**Pestilence and disinheriting.** *ʾAkkennû baddeber wəʾôrišennû* (12, WLC). *Deber* is the plague
of Egypt's livestock (Exodus 9:3), so Israel is threatened with what Egypt got. *Wəʾôrišennû* is the
hiphil of *yāraš*. It is the verb Caleb used for possessing the land (13:30), and the verb Israel's
later conquest narratives use for dispossessing peoples. The people who would not dispossess the
nations are threatened with being dispossessed. *Wəʿāṣûm mimmennû* echoes Exodus 1:9, where Pharaoh
says Israel is *mightier than we*. Rahlfs adds *you and the house of your father*, which the Hebrew
does not have.

**Moses does not answer the offer.** He never refers to the new nation. At Exodus 32:32 he asked to
be blotted out with the people. Here he simply goes past it. The argument has two parts, and neither
appeals to Israel's merit. Unlike Exodus 32:13 it does not name Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. See
[[What the Egyptians Will Hear - Moses' Argument in Exodus 32, Numbers 14, and Deuteronomy 9]].

**Not able.** *Mibbiltî yəkōlet YHWH ləhābîʾ* (16, WLC), with *yəkōlet* parsed as the infinitive of
*yākōl*. That verb has been the hinge since Caleb's *yākôl nûkal* and the ten's *lōʾ nûkal*
(13:30–31). What the ten said of themselves, the nations will say of Yahweh. Moses has heard the
logic of the report through to its end. Rahlfs keeps the same verb with *δύνασθαι*.

**Slaughtered.** *Wayyišḥāṭēm bammidbār* (16, WLC). *Šāḥaṭ* is the regular verb for killing a
sacrifice in Leviticus. In the nations' mouth it would be a contemptuous word. Rahlfs has
*κατέστρωσεν αὐτοὺς ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ*, and Paul has the same verb and phrase at 1 Corinthians 10:5. See
[[Whose Bodies Fell in the Wilderness - Numbers 14 in Psalms 95 and 106, 1 Corinthians 10, Hebrews 3, and Jude]].

**Eye to eye.** *ʿAyin bəʿayin* (14, WLC) occurs in this sense only here and at Isaiah 52:8 by the
tags; Deuteronomy 19:21 has the same two words in the law of retaliation. WEB has *face to face*,
which hides that this is not the *pānîm* of Exodus 33:11 and Deuteronomy 34:10, or the *peh* of
Numbers 12:8. See [[Mouth to Mouth - Numbers 12.6-8, Exodus 33, and Deuteronomy 34]]. Moses tells
Yahweh that the nations already know he is present. His argument works only if that reputation is
real.

**What Moses leaves out.** Moses says he is quoting (*kaʾăšer dibbartā lēʾmōr*, 17), and verse 18
follows Exodus 34:6–7 closely, but not exactly (WLC):

| Exodus 34:6–7 | Numbers 14:18 |
|---|---|
| *ʾēl raḥûm wəḥannûn* | omitted |
| *ʾerek ʾappayim wərab-ḥesed* | kept |
| *weʾĕmet* | omitted |
| *nōṣēr ḥesed lāʾălāpîm* | omitted |
| *nōśēʾ ʿāwōn wāpešaʿ wəḥaṭṭāʾâ* | *nōśēʾ ʿāwōn wāpāšaʿ*, without *sin* |
| *wənaqqēh lōʾ yənaqqeh* | kept |
| *ʿal-bānîm wəʿal-bənê bānîm* | *ʿal-bānîm*, without *children's children* |
| *ʿal-šillēšîm wəʿal-ribbēʿîm* | kept |

He drops *merciful and gracious*, where one would expect a plea to begin, and he keeps *he will by
no means clear the guilty*, where one would expect him to stop. The omission of *to thousands* and
the retention of the generational clause are harder still to explain, and verse 33 will enact that
clause. The text gives no reason. Rahlfs restores *καὶ ἀληθινός* and *καὶ ἁμαρτίας* in its version
of the verse, so the Greek stands closer to Exodus. See
[[Merciful and Gracious - Exodus 34, Numbers 14, and Jonah 4]].

**Pardon.** *Səlaḥ-nāʾ* (19, WLC). Exodus 34:9 is the first use by the tags, Moses asking; Leviticus
4–5 and 19:22 promise it through a priest's atonement. *Sālaḥtî kidbārekā* (20) is the only place in
the tags with the first-person perfect, *I have pardoned*. Jewish liturgy is reported to use these
words on the Day of Atonement; that is recalled. Rahlfs has *ἵλεως αὐτοῖς εἰμι*, *I am merciful to
them*, and adds *to Moses*.

**As I live.** *Wəʾûlām ḥay-ʾānî* (21, WLC). The oath *ḥay ʾānî* occurs in 22 verses by the tags,
and this is the first; 14:28 is the second. Sixteen are in Ezekiel, whose wilderness history in
chapter 20 takes up this chapter's sentence. Deuteronomy 32:40 has a different pronoun. *Nəʾum YHWH*
at verse 28 is the second use of the formula in the Hebrew Bible by the tags, after the oath at
Moriah in Genesis 22:16. Rahlfs adds *and my name living*.

**The earth and the land.** *Kol-hāʾāreṣ* (21) is the same word as *hāʾāreṣ* throughout the chapter.
A people has refused the land while Yahweh swears his glory will fill the earth. *Wəyimmālēʾ* has an
ordinary conjunction, and the syntax of the oath is not plain. WEB takes it as a second clause of
the oath. See [[Filled with His Glory - Numbers 14.21, Psalm 72, Isaiah 6, and Habakkuk 2]].

**Filled and filled after.** *Yimmālēʾ* (21) and *wayəmallēʾ ʾaḥăray* (24) are the same verb, three
verses apart: the glory will fill the earth, and Caleb *filled after me*. That wordplay is the
wiki's observation. See
[[Wholly Followed - Mille Ahare in Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, and 1 Kings]].

**Ten times.** *Zeh ʿeśer pəʿāmîm* (22, WLC). Genesis 31:7, 41 and Job 19:3 have used *ten times* as
a round number, and the wiki reads it so here. The chapter offers no list. The story so far could
supply one, including the sea (Exodus 14), Marah, the manna, Rephidim, the calf, Taberah, and
Kibroth Hattaavah, and a rabbinic list of ten is known (recalled, unchecked). Rahlfs has *τοῦτο
δέκατον*, *this tenth time*, which turns the idiom into a count. *Wayənassû ʾōtî* is the verb of
Massah. See [[Do Not Test Yahweh - Massah in Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and Hebrews]].

**They saw, and shall not see.** *Hārōʾîm ʾet-kəbōdî … ʾim-yirʾû ʾet-hāʾāreṣ* (22–23, WLC). *ʾIm*
with no apodosis is the oath form, *if they see*, meaning *they surely will not*. Verse 30 and Psalm
95:11 have the same form, and verses 28 and 35 have the positive form, *ʾim-lōʾ*. The men's crime is
partly that they saw, because Yahweh's verse sets the things seen against the thing refused. In
Rahlfs verse 23 has a long addition, the children *who are with me here, who do not know good or
evil, every inexperienced youth*, which is not in WLC. It resembles Deuteronomy 1:39. See
[[Your Little Ones - Numbers 14.31 and Deuteronomy 1.39]].

**Another spirit.** *ʿAbdî kālēb ʿēqeb hāytâ rûaḥ ʾaḥeret ʿimmô* (24, WLC). *My servant* was
Yahweh's word for Moses at Numbers 12:7–8, and here it is given to Caleb. The *rûaḥ* comes two
chapters after the Spirit was shared out at Numbers 11:25, but the text does not connect them.
Rahlfs has *πνεῦμα ἕτερον ἐν αὐτῷ*. *ʾEl-hāʾāreṣ ʾăšer bāʾ šāmmâ* uses the singular as 13:22 did,
and it is the base for Caleb's claim to Hebron. Joshua is absent from this verse.

**Rejected.** *Məʾastem bāh* (31, WLC). *Māʾas* is what Yahweh said they did to him at 11:20, *you
have rejected Yahweh who is among you*. Now they have rejected the land. Psalm 106:24 has the same
verb with the same preposition, *wayyimʾăsû bəʾereṣ ḥemdâ*, and WEB there has *despised*.

**All who were counted.** *Wəkol-pəqudêkem ləkol-misparkem mibben ʿeśrîm šānâ wāmāʿlâ* (29, WLC).
This is the language of the census: *pāqad*, the number, and the age limit of Numbers 1:3. The roll
that organized the army has become a list of the condemned. The arrangement of Numbers 1–10 now
turns out to have been made for men who will not fight this war. Numbers 26:64–65 closes the
account. The Levites were not in that count (1:47–49), which may be why Eleazar can enter the land;
the text does not say so, and that is the wiki's inference. See tension 4.

**The hand.** *Nāśāʾtî ʾet-yādî* (30, WLC) is Yahweh's oath gesture of Exodus 6:8, with WEB's
*swore* in both verses. It was the promise then, and the same gesture now excludes those who
received it. Ezekiel 20:15 and Psalm 106:26 pick up the gesture in order to refuse.

**Prey.** *Ṭappəkem ʾăšer ʾămartem lābaz yihyeh* (31, WLC) repeats verse 3's *ləbaz*. Deuteronomy
1:39 keeps these exact words. The little ones are the ones the fathers claimed to be protecting.

**Carcasses.** *Pigrêkem* (29, 32, 33, WLC). *Peger* is a harsh word, a corpse lying where it fell;
Leviticus 26:30 and Genesis 15:11 have used it, and Isaiah 66:24 will close with it. *Ûpigrêkem
ʾattem*, *but your carcasses, you* (32), is emphatic. Rahlfs has *τὰ κῶλα*, literally *limbs*, and
Hebrews 3:17 uses this word.

**Shepherds.** *Yihyû rōʿîm bammidbār ʾarbāʿîm šānâ* (33, WLC). WEB has *wanderers*, and the Hebrew
has *shepherds*, a participle of *rāʿâ*. Rahlfs has *νεμόμενοι*, *grazing*. Shepherding is the life
of the patriarchs and of Moses at Horeb, here imposed rather than chosen. That connection is the
wiki's.

**Your prostitution.** *Wənāśəʾû ʾet-zənûtêkem* (33, WLC). *Zənût* occurs nine times by the tags,
and this is the first; the rest are in Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Hosea. Exodus 34:15–16 has the verb of
the nations' gods. Here nothing sexual and no idol is in view. Refusing the land is called
unfaithfulness to a husband, and Numbers 15:39 will use the verb for following one's own eyes.
Jeremiah 2:2 remembers the same wilderness as the *love of your weddings*, which does not match this
verse, and the wiki keeps both.

**A year for each day.** *Yôm laššānâ yôm laššānâ* (34, WLC), with the phrase doubled. By the tags
it occurs twice in the Hebrew Bible, here and at Ezekiel 4:6, where the same phrase comes with
*nāśāʾ ʿāwōn*. See [[A Day for a Year - Numbers 14.34 and Ezekiel 4.6]]. The number is measured from
the spies' search, the sin being counted at its own length.

**My alienation.** *Wîdaʿtem ʾet-tənûʾātî* (34, WLC). *Tənûʾâ* occurs only here and at Job 33:10 by
the tags, where WEB has *occasions*. WEB here has *alienation*. Rahlfs has *τὸν θυμὸν τῆς ὀργῆς
μου*, which goes by sense. The meaning of the word cannot be settled from two uses. *Wəyādəʿû
ʾet-hāʾāreṣ* (31) stands beside it. The children will know the land, and the fathers will know this.

**Gathered against me.** *Hannôʿādîm ʿālāy* (35, WLC) uses *yāʿad*, the root of *môʿēd* in *ʾōhel
môʿēd* and likely of *ʿēdâ*. This congregation has met, and the meeting is against the one whose
Tent is named for meeting. That wordplay is the wiki's. *Hāʿēdâ hārāʿâ hazzōʾt* comes in verses 27
and 35.

**The ten die first.** *Bammaggēpâ lipnê YHWH* (37, WLC). The rest of the generation will die over
four decades. These men die at once. Numbers 13:32 had only *dibbat hāʾāreṣ*; verse 37 adds *rāʿâ*,
*dibbat hāʾāreṣ rāʿâ*, the phrasing of Genesis 37:2, so the noun alone may not have been evil, and
verse 36 still says *ləhôṣîʾ dibbâ ʿal-hāʾāreṣ*, with the land as the thing wronged. Verse 36 has a
*kətîb/qərê* in WLC, *wylwnw* against *wayyallînû*. The *qərê* gives the causative, *made … to
murmur*. Rahlfs renders *ῥήματα πονηρά*. See
[[An Evil Report - Dibbah in Genesis 37, Numbers 13, and Ezekiel 36]].

**They lived.** *Ḥāyû* (38, WLC), a single verb against *die* in 2, 15, 35, and 37. The story began
with people wishing they had died, and it ends with the two who wanted to go in being the ones who
lived.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Caleb alone, or with Joshua.** Verse 24 and Deuteronomy 1:36 have Caleb alone; verses 30 and 38
   and 32:12 have both, and verses 6–9 give both the speech. Two speeches of sentence stand side by
   side, 11–25 and 26–35. Source critics are reported to assign them to different strands; that is
   recalled, and the wiki reads the text as it stands.
2. **Pardon and sentence.** *I have pardoned* (20) and *they shall not see the land* (23) are
   adjacent. The word *but* holds them, and no theory is given. See [[Forgiveness]].
3. **Forty years or thirty-eight.** Verses 33–34 say forty, a year for each day. Deuteronomy 2:14
   counts thirty-eight from Kadesh Barnea to the Zered. The forty appears to include what has passed
   since the exodus, though verse 34 counts from the search. The numbers do not fit exactly.
4. **Who is under the sentence.** The *men* who saw (22); *all who were counted … from twenty years
   old* (29); *all this evil congregation* (35); *the men of war* (Deuteronomy 2:14, 16). The
   Levites were not counted, and the text never says the women were either included or exempted.
5. **Moses.** He is not sentenced here. Numbers 20:12 gives another reason for his exclusion, at
   Meribah. Deuteronomy 1:37 puts it in this speech: *Also Yahweh was angry with me for your sakes.*
   See [[Who Sent the Spies - Numbers 13 and Deuteronomy 1]].
6. **The children and the fathers.** Verse 33 has the children bear the fathers' *zənût*.
   Deuteronomy 24:16 and Ezekiel 18 forbid or deny a transfer of guilt. They bear the consequence,
   and verse 34 then says *you will bear your iniquities*. See
   [[Visiting the Iniquity of the Fathers - Job 21.19, Jeremiah 31, and Ezekiel 18]].
7. **Where the Amalekites are.** Verse 25 puts Amalek and the Canaanite in the valley. 13:29 puts
   them in the South and by the sea, and 14:45 will put them in the mountain.
8. **The weeping.** Here the congregation cries aloud. Deuteronomy 1:27 and Psalm 106:25 have them
   murmuring *in your tents*, indoors.
9. **Which sin fixed the oath.** Numbers has the oath at Kadesh. Psalm 95:8–11 sets it after Massah
   and Meribah, and Hebrews follows the psalm.
10. **Was a head appointed?** Verse 4 is a proposal, and Nehemiah 9:17 narrates it as done.
11. **The land's people.** Verse 14 has the nations knowing all about Israel, and 13:33 has Israel
    certain of how it looks to them. Joshua 2:9–11 will side with Moses.
