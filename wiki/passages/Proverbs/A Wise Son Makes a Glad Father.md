# A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 10:1–32

## Summary

A new heading names [[Solomon]], and the form changes. After the long instructions and the two
invitations of [[The Two Feasts]], chapter 10 offers short sayings that contrast wisdom and folly,
righteousness and wickedness. A son's conduct affects his parents; diligence matters at harvest;
speech can nourish neighbors or ruin the speaker; and Yahweh's blessing and judgment frame the
outcomes. There is no new narrative scene and no named city.

**10:1–5 — Household, livelihood, and moral limits.** The first saying pairs a glad father with a
grieving mother. It does not assign a wise son exclusively to fathers or a foolish one exclusively
to mothers: the paired lines describe the household's response. Verses 2–3 set righteousness and
Yahweh's provision before the work sayings of 4–5. Ill-gotten treasure cannot deliver from death,
while diligence brings wealth and timely gathering marks a wise son. The harvest example gives
wisdom a deadline; sleeping through it affects the family, not merely the sleeper.

**10:6–14 — A reputation and a mouth that outlast the moment.** Blessing rests on the righteous,
whose memory remains blessed; the wicked person's name decays. The wise accept commandments and
store knowledge. Verse 13 contrasts discerning lips with a rod for the back of the person lacking
understanding, placing bodily discipline beside the chapter’s calls to receive correction.
By contrast, the chattering fool falls (8, 10), the wink causes sorrow (10), and
violence covers the wicked mouth (6, 11). The WEB repeats that last clause exactly; this page
follows its rendering without deciding between possible source-language interpretations. Verse 11
describes the righteous mouth as a spring of life. Read beside the guarded heart in
[[Keep Your Heart]] (4:23–24), the image connects inward formation to outward speech: the wisdom
kept within can sustain other people.

**Love covers; hatred stirs up (10:12).** The contrast is between escalating conflict and responding
to wrongs in love. It does not say that the wrong never occurred. Verse 18 condemns hiding hatred
behind lying lips, so the chapter itself distinguishes love's covering from deceitful concealment.
Proverbs 17:9 connects covering an offense with promoting love, while 28:13 rejects concealing one's
own sins. These help interpret 10:12 as a relational response, not permission to suppress truth.
First Peter 4:8 closely parallels the saying; James 5:19–20 joins covering sins to turning back a
wanderer. See [[Love Covers Wrongs - Proverbs 10.12, 1 Peter 4.8, and James 5.20]].

**10:15–22 — Wealth protects, but words also feed.** Verse 15 acknowledges the real shelter wealth
supplies and the destruction poverty can bring. It does not call the poor lazy or wicked. Verses 2
and 16 prevent that observation from making possessions the final measure of a life: the source and
use of gain matter. Correction keeps a person on the way of life, while refusal leads others astray
in the WEB (17). Lying, slander, and uncontrolled talk are contrasted with restraint, a tongue like
choice silver, and lips that feed many (18–21). The best mouth is therefore neither constantly
talking nor always silent; it speaks what benefits others. Yahweh's blessing brings wealth (22),
complementing the diligent hand (4) without identifying every rich person as righteous.

**10:23–32 — Pleasure, expectation, and endurance.** The fool enjoys wickedness; the person of
understanding enjoys wisdom. The wicked face what they fear, while the righteous receive what they
desire (23–24). A whirlwind removes the wicked, but the righteous endure (25). The sluggard is an
irritation to those who send him, as vinegar is to teeth and smoke to eyes (26); idleness now has a
cost to those depending on the worker. Fear of Yahweh, joy, secure dwelling, and acceptable speech
close the portrait of the righteous; shortened days, failed hope, destruction, and perverse speech
close the opposing portrait (27–32). The way of Yahweh is one stronghold with different consequences
for the upright and evildoers (29).

**The tension with Job is explicit.** Proverbs 10:24 assigns realized fear to the wicked, yet the
upright Job says his own feared disaster has arrived (Job 1:1; 3:25). Proverbs 10:25 describes a
storm removing the wicked; Job 1:18–22 describes a wind destroying the children of an upright man,
followed by the narrator's refusal to charge him with sin. Proverbs 10:27 shortens the wicked
person's life; Job 21:7–13 describes wicked people growing old in prosperity. And the hungry
laborers in Job 24:5–11 cannot be explained by the lazy hand of Proverbs 10:4. These are conflicting
pictures of observed outcomes, not reasons to revise Job's character. Reading Proverbs as general
instruction rather than a diagnosis of each sufferer is the wiki's interpretive approach; chapter 10
itself does not state an exception clause. See [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]], [[Retribution]],
and [[Suffering]].

## Key verses

> **10:1** The proverbs of Solomon. A wise son makes a glad father; but a foolish son brings grief to his mother.

> **10:2** Treasures of wickedness profit nothing, but righteousness delivers from death.

> **10:4** He becomes poor who works with a lazy hand, but the hand of the diligent brings wealth.

> **10:11** The mouth of the righteous is a spring of life, but violence covers the mouth of the wicked.

> **10:12** Hatred stirs up strife, but love covers all wrongs.

> **10:15** The rich man’s wealth is his strong city. The destruction of the poor is their poverty.

> **10:17** He is in the way of life who heeds correction, but he who forsakes reproof leads others astray.

> **10:19** In the multitude of words there is no lack of disobedience, but he who restrains his lips does wisely.

> **10:21** The lips of the righteous feed many, but the foolish die for lack of understanding.

> **10:22** Yahweh’s blessing brings wealth, and he adds no trouble to it.

> **10:24** What the wicked fear, will overtake them, but the desire of the righteous will be granted.

> **10:27** The fear of Yahweh prolongs days, but the years of the wicked shall be shortened.

> **10:30** The righteous will never be removed, but the wicked will not dwell in the land.

  is not identified with a particular territory in this saying. See [[Land and Sojourning]].

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned Proverbs 10:1–32 unit is retained. The collection begins at 10:1, but its
individual sayings do not create a single narrative or sustained speech ending at 10:32.
Chapter-sized ingestion follows the book page’s existing plan for the sentence collections; the
clusters discussed above are reading aids, not a claim that each is an independently marked
composition.
