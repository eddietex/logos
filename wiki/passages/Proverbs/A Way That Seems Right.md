# A Way That Seems Right

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 14:1–35

## Summary

The chapter opens with a woman and a house: *"Every wise woman builds her house, but the foolish one
tears it down with her own hands"* (14:1). The verb and the noun are the ones the prologue closed on —
*"Wisdom has built her house"* (9:1) — and the line brings the two women of chapter 9 down from
personification into an ordinary household, where a wife's wisdom or folly builds or demolishes a
real home. The chapter then runs through thirty-four more sayings on witnesses, scoffers, the
prudent and the simple, temper, the poor, the king, and the fear of Yahweh. It is a collection of
related sayings, not a continuous argument; the groupings below are reading aids.

Where Proverbs 13 named no divine name, **Proverbs 14 names Yahweh three times and the Maker once**:
the upright *"fears Yahweh"* (2), *"In the fear of Yahweh is a secure fortress"* (26), *"The fear of
Yahweh is a fountain of life"* (27), and the oppressor of the poor *"shows contempt for his Maker"*
(31). All four are about how a man stands toward God; none narrates an act of God.

**14:1–9 — Houses, witnesses, and scoffers.** After the house (1) comes the walk: uprightness is
itself the fear of Yahweh, and perversity despises him (2). The fool's talk *"brings a rod to his
back"* (3), the rod of 13:24 now earned by the mouth. Verse 4 is a farmer's observation: *"Where no
oxen are, the crib is clean, but much increase is by the strength of the ox."* An empty stall is
tidy; a productive one is not, and the saying prefers the mess that comes with a harvest. The
truthful and false witness of verse 5 return at verse 25 and bracket most of the chapter. The
scoffer *seeks* wisdom and does not find it (6) — he is not idle, but his search is undone by his
contempt — while knowledge *"comes easily to a discerning person"*. Stay away from the fool (7); the
prudent think about their way (8). Verse 9's *"Fools mock at making atonement for sins"* is the
WEB's rendering of a line English versions render differently; this page follows the WEB and makes
no claim about the Hebrew.

**14:10–14 — What the heart keeps.** Verse 10 is one of the book's least moral sayings: *"The heart
knows its own bitterness and joy; he will not share these with a stranger."* Verse 13 continues it —
*"Even in laughter the heart may be sorrowful, and mirth may end in heaviness."* These two lines set
a limit on what anyone can know of another's inner life, and they bracket the chapter's most quoted
verse: **"There is a way which seems right to a man, but in the end it leads to death"** (14:12),
which gives this page its title. The way that *seems* right is the self-assessment the prologue warned
against — *"Don't be wise in your own eyes"* (3:7) — compressed into one line with no named villain:
the man is not wicked in his own estimate, and that is the danger. **The sentence recurs word for
word in the WEB at 16:25**, the first exact doublet the wiki has met inside the Solomonic collection;
the page for 16 will take it up. Between and around these, the house of the wicked falls and the
tent of the upright flourishes (11), and each man is repaid for his ways (14).

**14:15–19 — The simple, the prudent, and the quick-tempered.** *"A simple man believes everything"*
(15) makes credulity, not unbelief, the simple man's failing. *"A wise man fears and shuns evil"* (16)
is close in the WEB's English to the description of Job at Job 1:1, *"one who feared God, and turned
away from evil"*; this page has not verified whether the Hebrew verbs are identical. The quick to
anger commit folly (17), the simple inherit folly and the prudent are crowned with knowledge (18),
and the evil bow before the good (19).

**14:20–24 — The poor man's neighbours.** Verse 20 is a bare observation: *"The poor person is
shunned even by his own neighbor, but the rich person has many friends."* It describes the world
without approving it, and the very next line judges it: *"He who despises his neighbor sins, but
blessed is he who has pity on the poor"* (21). The pair is the chapter's clearest case of a proverb
that reports a social fact and a proverb that rules on it standing side by side; reading verse 20
as advice would invert verse 21. Planners of evil go astray, *"love and faithfulness belong to those
who plan good"* (22); hard work profits and mere talk impoverishes (23); the wise are crowned with
riches and fools with folly (24).

**14:25–27 — The witness and the fountain.** The truthful witness *"saves souls"* (25), giving
testimony life-and-death weight, as the law does (Exodus 20:16; 23:1–8). Then the fear of Yahweh,
twice: a *"secure fortress"*, and *"he will be a refuge for his children"* (26) — the WEB leaves it
open whether *his* means the God-fearer's children or Yahweh's, and this page does not decide it —
and *"a fountain of life, turning people from the snares of death"* (27). **Verse 27 repeats 13:14
with one change**: there, *"The teaching of the wise is a spring of life, to turn from the snares of
death"*. The two sayings put the teaching of the wise and the fear of Yahweh in the same place in the
same sentence, which is the motto of 1:7 stated as an equivalence.

**14:28–35 — King, temper, Maker, and nation.** The king's glory is in a populous people (28); the
slow to anger have *"great understanding"* (29), the phrase *slow to anger* being the one Exodus 34:6
uses of Yahweh himself; *"envy rots the bones"* (30). **Verse 31 grounds the treatment of the poor in
creation**: *"He who oppresses the poor shows contempt for his Maker, but he who is kind to the needy
honors him."* The poor man is not only a neighbour but someone's handiwork, and contempt for him
reaches his maker. Proverbs 17:5 repeats the thought; Job 31:15 had made the same argument from his
own servants — *"Didn't he who made me in the womb make him?"* The wicked is brought down in
calamity, *"but in death, the righteous has a refuge"* (32); *"Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin
is a disgrace to any people"* (34), the chapter's only saying about peoples rather than persons; and
the king favours the servant who deals wisely (35).

**Verse 32 and the refuge in death.** The WEB's *"in death, the righteous has a refuge"* is one of
the few sayings in the collection that looks past the end of life, and it stands against Job's
picture of the righteous and the wicked lying down *"alike in the dust"* (Job 21:26). Some modern
translations render the line differently, reportedly following the ancient Greek in place of the
Hebrew; that report has not been verified against a source-language edition here, so this page
records the WEB's reading and claims no more for it than the English says.

**The promises and their counterexamples.** *"The tent of the upright will flourish"* (11) and
*"a good man will be rewarded for his ways"* (14) are general sayings. Job's children died when their eldest
brother's house fell on them (Job 1:18–19), and Bildad turned the matching sentence — *"The tent of the
wicked shall be no more"* (Job 8:22) — into an argument against him. As on the pages for chapters
10–13, the wiki reads these as general instruction and keeps the tension explicit; the chapter itself
supplies two checks. Verse 12 says a man's sense of his own rightness can be mistaken, which cuts
against any confident verdict on a sufferer; and verse 20's poor man is shunned for his poverty, not
proven guilty by it. See [[Retribution]] and [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

## Key verses

> **14:1** Every wise woman builds her house, but the foolish one tears it down with her own hands.

> **14:4** Where no oxen are, the crib is clean, but much increase is by the strength of the ox.

> **14:5** A truthful witness will not lie, but a false witness pours out lies.

> **14:10** The heart knows its own bitterness and joy; he will not share these with a stranger.

> **14:12** There is a way which seems right to a man, but in the end it leads to death.

> **14:13** Even in laughter the heart may be sorrowful, and mirth may end in heaviness.

> **14:15** A simple man believes everything, but the prudent man carefully considers his ways.

> **14:20** The poor person is shunned even by his own neighbor, but the rich person has many friends.

> **14:21** He who despises his neighbor sins, but blessed is he who has pity on the poor.

> **14:26** In the fear of Yahweh is a secure fortress, and he will be a refuge for his children.

> **14:27** The fear of Yahweh is a fountain of life, turning people from the snares of death.

> **14:29** He who is slow to anger has great understanding, but he who has a quick temper displays folly.

> **14:30** The life of the body is a heart at peace, but envy rots the bones.

> **14:31** He who oppresses the poor shows contempt for his Maker, but he who is kind to the needy honors him.

> **14:32** The wicked is brought down in his calamity, but in death, the righteous has a refuge.

> **14:34** Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin is a disgrace to any people.

## People

- [[God]] — *Yahweh* in 14:2, 26, 27, and *his Maker* in 14:31: feared, a fortress and refuge, a
  fountain of life, and honoured or despised in the treatment of the poor.
- [[Solomon]] — attribution from the collection heading at 10:1; not named in this unit.
- The king of 14:28, 35 is a type, not an identified ruler. The wise woman, the witnesses, the
  scoffer, the simple, the poor man, and his neighbour are unnamed types.
