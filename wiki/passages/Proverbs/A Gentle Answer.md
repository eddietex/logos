# A Gentle Answer

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 15:1–33

## Summary

The chapter opens on the tongue and closes on the fear of Yahweh. Its first line is one of the
best-known sentences in the book: **"A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up
anger"** (15:1), which gives this page its title. Its last is a double motto: *"The fear of Yahweh
teaches wisdom. Before honor is humility"* (15:33). Between them run thirty-one more sayings, on
speech above all, and on the heart, reproof, the household, and the poor. As with chapters 10–14, it
is a collection of related sayings rather than an argument; the groupings below are reading aids.

**Proverbs 15 names Yahweh nine times** — at 15:3, 8, 9, 11, 16, 25, 26, 29, and 33 — more than any
chapter of the sentence collection so far. Where chapter 14's four references were about how a man
stands toward God, chapter 15's are about how God stands toward men: what he sees (3, 11), what he
detests and loves (8, 9, 26), whom he hears and keeps far from (29), and what he uproots and guards
(25). It is still a book of sayings, not of narrative; no divine act is told, but God is the subject
of the verbs.

**15:1–7 — The tongue, and the eyes that watch it.** The gentle answer and the harsh word (1); the
tongue of the wise and the gushing mouth of fools (2); and then, set among sayings about speech,
***"Yahweh's eyes are everywhere, keeping watch on the evil and the good"*** (3). The line widens 5:21,
*"the ways of man are before Yahweh's eyes"*, from the adulterer's road to every place, and the
position of the verse makes the speaker's words part of what is watched. Verse 4 returns to the
tongue: *"A gentle tongue is a tree of life, but deceit in it crushes the spirit"*. This is the fourth
tree of life in Proverbs, after Wisdom herself (3:18), the fruit of the righteous (11:30), and longing
fulfilled (13:12) — now a manner of speaking. The WEB has *gentle* at both 15:1 and 15:4; this page
does not assert that the Hebrew uses the same word in both. The fool despises his father's correction
(5); the righteous house holds treasure while wicked income brings trouble (6); and the lips of the
wise spread knowledge, *"not so with the heart of fools"* (7).

**15:8–11 — What Yahweh detests, loves, and sees.** ***"The sacrifice made by the wicked is an
abomination to Yahweh, but the prayer of the upright is his delight"*** (8). The contrast is not
sacrifice against prayer as forms — the wicked could pray and the upright sacrifice — but the worshipper
against the worship: the same God who prescribed offerings in Exodus finds the wicked man's offering
an *abomination*, the word 6:16 used for what Yahweh hates. Verse 9 states the ground: *"The way of the
wicked is an abomination to Yahweh, but he loves him who follows after righteousness."* The verse that
says what God loves is set beside what he refuses. Proverbs returns to this at 21:3, 21:27, and 28:9,
and the wiki has already watched it happen once: in Job 42:8 the friends must bring *"a burnt
offering"*, and the acceptance turns on *"my servant Job shall pray for you, for I will accept him"* —
offering and prayer together, the prayer of the upright the one accepted. See
[[The Prayer of the Upright - Proverbs 15.8, 29 and Job 42.8]]. Stern discipline waits for the one who
forsakes the way, and *"whoever hates reproof shall die"* (10). Then the chapter's widest claim:
***"Sheol and Abaddon are before Yahweh— how much more then the hearts of the children of men!"***
(11). The underworld itself is in Yahweh's view, so the human heart, which feels private, is not. Job
26:6 had said the first half — *"Sheol is naked before God, and Abaddon has no covering"* — and the
proverb draws the inference. See [[Abaddon Has No Covering - Job 26.6, Proverbs 15.11, and Revelation 9.11]].

**15:12–17 — The heart, glad and aching, and the better-than sayings.** The scoffer will not go to the
wise because he does not love reproof (12). Three sayings on the heart (13–15) are observations rather
than verdicts: *"A glad heart makes a cheerful face; but an aching heart breaks the spirit"* (13), and
*"All the days of the afflicted are wretched, but one who has a cheerful heart enjoys a continual feast"*
(15). Like 13:12 and 14:10, they report what the inside of a person is like without assigning blame,
and verse 15's *afflicted* is not said to deserve his wretchedness. Between them, the understanding
heart *seeks* knowledge while fools' mouths *"feed on folly"* (14). Then **the first pair of
"better than" sayings in the chapter**: *"Better is little, with the fear of Yahweh, than great treasure
with trouble"* (16), and *"Better is a dinner of herbs, where love is, than a fattened calf with hatred"*
(17). A *better than* saying concedes what an antithetical saying does not — that the righteous may have
little and the wicked much. The same chapter that put *"much treasure"* in the house of the righteous
(6) here ranks a small portion with the fear of Yahweh above a great one without it. See
[[Better Is Little - The Better-Than Sayings of Proverbs]].

**15:18–24 — Temper, the road, and the word in season.** *"A wrathful man stirs up contention, but one
who is slow to anger appeases strife"* (18) restates 15:1 as character rather than speech, and uses the
WEB phrase *slow to anger* of 14:29 and of Yahweh at Exodus 34:6; see
[[Slow to Anger - Proverbs 14.29 and Exodus 34.6]]. The sluggard's way is a thorn hedge and the upright's
path *"a highway"* (19). **Verse 20 is a near-doublet of the collection's opening line**: 10:1 has *"A
wise son makes a glad father; but a foolish son brings grief to his mother"*; 15:20 has *"A wise son
makes a father glad, but a foolish man despises his mother"* — the first half all but identical in the
WEB, the second moving from the grief the son causes to the contempt he shows. Folly is a joy to the
witless (21). *"Where there is no counsel, plans fail; but in a multitude of counselors they are
established"* (22) repeats 11:14's *multitude of counselors*, there for the nation, here for any plan.
**Verse 23 is the chapter's second saying about timing**: *"Joy comes to a man with the reply of his
mouth. How good is a word at the right time!"* The gentle answer of verse 1 is a matter of manner; the
word *"at the right time"* is a matter of season, and 25:11 will put it as *"apples of gold in settings
of silver"*. *"The path of life leads upward for the wise, to keep him from going downward to Sheol"*
(24): the prologue's road down to Sheol (5:5; 7:27; 9:18) given its opposite direction. The verse does
not say where the upward path ends; it says only that it is not Sheol.

**15:25–29 — The proud, the widow, the bribe, and the prayer.** ***"Yahweh will uproot the house of the
proud, but he will keep the widow's borders intact"*** (25). The widow is the person the law names as
having no protector — *"You shall not take advantage of any widow or fatherless child"* (Exodus 22:22) —
and her *borders* are the boundary of her land, the thing the powerful could move. Proverbs will forbid
moving *"the ancient boundary stone"* twice (22:28; 23:10), and Job 24:2–3 lists the people *"who remove
the landmarks"* and *"take the widow's ox for a pledge"*. Here Yahweh himself is the one who keeps the
boundary. See [[The Widow's Borders - Proverbs 15.25 and the Ancient Boundary Stone]]. Yahweh detests
the thoughts of the wicked (26); *"He who is greedy for gain troubles his own house, but he who hates
bribes will live"* (27), which takes the *greedy for gain* of the gang in 1:19 into the household and
the law's *"You shall take no bribe"* (Exodus 23:8) into a promise of life. The righteous heart *weighs*
its answers where the wicked mouth *"gushes out evil"* (28), and the speech that began the chapter ends in
deliberation. ***"Yahweh is far from the wicked, but he hears the prayer of the righteous"*** (29)
returns to verse 8: the prayer of the upright is a delight, and it is heard.

**15:30–33 — Good news, reproof, and the motto.** *"The light of the eyes rejoices the heart. Good news
gives health to the bones"* (30). The ear that listens to reproof *"will be at home among the wise"*
(31); refusing correction is despising *"his own soul"* (32). The chapter closes on ***"The fear of
Yahweh teaches wisdom. Before honor is humility"*** (33). The first half joins the motto of 1:7 and 9:10
— where the fear of Yahweh is the *beginning* of knowledge and of wisdom — with a verb of instruction:
here it is the teacher. The second half recurs word for word in the WEB at 18:12, *"before honor is
humility"*, there set against *"Before destruction the heart of man is proud"*; and 22:4 will name *"humility
and the fear of Yahweh"* together. See [[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]].

**Reproof as the chapter's thread.** Five sayings concern correction: the fool despises it (5), the one
who hates it dies (10), the scoffer avoids the wise because of it (12), the listening ear lives among the
wise (31), and the refuser despises his own soul (32). With 12:1, *"Whoever loves correction loves
knowledge"*, this makes the willingness to be corrected one of the sentence collection's most constant
tests.

**The gentle answer and Job's friends.** Verse 1 and verse 23 describe a kind of speech that the book of
Job is largely about the absence of. Eliphaz asks Job, *"Are the consolations of God too small for you,
even the word that is gentle toward you?"* (Job 15:11) — claiming for his own speech the quality
Proverbs 15:1 commends. Job's verdict was *"You are all miserable comforters!"* (Job 16:2), and *"How
long will you torment me, and crush me with words?"* (Job 19:2), with the friends' own view of their
words set against the sufferer's. Proverbs 15:4 names what Job describes: the deceitful tongue *"crushes
the spirit"*. Job also said what he would have done in their place: *"I would strengthen you with my
mouth. The solace of my lips would relieve you"* (Job 16:5). See [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]].

**The promises and their counterexamples.** *"In the house of the righteous is much treasure"* (6) and
*"Yahweh will uproot the house of the proud"* (25) are general sayings. Job, *"blameless and upright"*
(Job 1:1), lost the household that made him *"the greatest of all the children of the east"* (Job 1:3).
As on the pages for chapters 10–14, the wiki reads these as general instruction and keeps the tension
explicit. **This chapter supplies its own check more plainly than most:** the better-than sayings of
15:16–17 presuppose a righteous man with little and a wicked man with *"great treasure"*, and verse 15's
afflicted man is described without being accused. Verse 11's Yahweh who sees the heart is, in the book
of Job, also the reason a sufferer's case cannot be decided from the outside. See [[Retribution]] and
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned Proverbs 15:1–33 unit is retained. Chapter-sized units are the book page's
stated convention for the sentence collection, not a claim that the chapter is one original discourse.
Only this queue item is advanced.

**Source text.** All quotations are copied from `raw/bible/Proverbs.md`, `raw/bible/Job.md`, and
`raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB). Raw text is unchanged. No exact Hebrew or Greek wording is asserted on
this page. The repetitions noted — 15:20 with 10:1, 15:22 with 11:14, 15:33 with 18:12, 15:18 with
14:29 and Exodus 34:6, and 15:3 with 5:21 — are comparisons of the stored WEB English. Whether the two
*gentle*s of 15:1 and 15:4 render the same Hebrew word, and whether Eliphaz's *gentle* word in Job
15:11 shares vocabulary with Proverbs 15:1, has not been checked against a source-language edition; the
connection drawn is one of subject, not of wording.

**Comparisons.** Supporting verses were read in stored WEB: Proverbs 1:7, 19; 3:18; 5:5, 21; 6:16;
9:10; 10:1; 11:14, 30; 12:1, 25; 13:12; 14:10, 29; 16:8, 19; 17:1, 22; 18:12; 19:1; 21:3, 27;
22:4, 28; 23:10–11; 25:11; 28:6, 9; Job 1:1, 3, 5; 15:11; 16:2, 5; 19:2; 24:2–3; 26:6; 42:8–9;
Exodus 22:22–24; 23:8; 34:6. No unreached book was fetched.
