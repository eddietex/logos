# The Two Paths

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 4:10–19

## Summary

A new address — *"Listen, my son"* — turns from the remembered household of 4:1–9 to the road.
The father says he has already taught the son *"in the way of wisdom"* and led him *"in straight
paths"*, and promises an unhampered walk and a run without stumbling (4:10–12). The son must take
firm hold of instruction, because *"she is your life"* (4:13). Then the other road: do not enter
the path of the wicked, avoid it, turn from it, pass on (4:14–15). The wicked cannot sleep until
they have done evil, and they eat and drink wickedness and violence (4:16–17). The lecture closes
on two images of light: the path of the righteous like dawn brightening to full day, the way of
the wicked like a darkness in which they cannot see what trips them (4:18–19).

**4:10–13 — A road already begun.** The father does not offer the son a choice from nowhere. The
verbs of 4:11 are in the past: *"I have taught you … I have led you."* The son is already on the
straight paths, and the lecture is about staying there. The promise of *"many"* years (4:10)
repeats the *"length of days, and years of life"* of 3:2, and the unhampered step echoes 3:23,
*"Your foot won't stumble."* Instruction is personified for a moment in 4:13 — *"Don't let her
go. Keep her"* — in the language just used of wisdom herself (4:6, 8). The WEB's *"she"* makes
instruction and wisdom close to interchangeable here; this page does not rest any claim on the
grammatical gender of the underlying Hebrew, which has not been checked for this pass.

**4:14–15 — Six commands for one refusal.** *Don't enter, don't walk, avoid it, don't pass by
it, turn from it, pass on.* The density is the teaching. The father does not argue the son out of
the wicked path once he is on it; he tells him not to set foot there, not even to go near. This
is the first lecture's warning made general. In 1:15 the path was a particular gang's — *"don't
walk on the path with them"* — and here it is simply *"the path of the wicked"*. See
[[Do Not Consent to Sinners]].

**4:16–17 — The appetite of the wicked.** The portrait is of compulsion. The wicked *"don't
sleep, unless they do evil. Their sleep is taken away, unless they make someone fall"* (4:16).
Set beside 3:24 — the wise son's *"sleep will be sweet"* — the contrast is not between two
schedules but between rest and its absence. Evil does not satisfy those who do it; it is what
they need before they can lie down. Their bread and wine are *"wickedness"* and *"violence"*
(4:17), which can be read either as food gained by violence or as violence itself taken as food;
the verse does not choose between them and neither does this page. The meal anticipates the
two banquets of chapter 9, where Wisdom offers *"my bread"* and *"the wine which I have mixed"*
(9:5) and Folly praises water that is stolen and *"food eaten in secret"* (9:17).

**4:18–19 — Dawn and darkness.** The lecture ends without a threat. *"The path of the righteous
is like the dawning light, that shines more and more until the perfect day"* (4:18): the
righteous life is described as growth, not arrival — light that is real at first and fuller
later. *"The way of the wicked is like darkness. They don't know what they stumble over"*
(4:19). The punishment of the wicked here is not a blow from outside but ignorance of their own
road. They fall and cannot say why. This is the same darkness as 2:13's *"ways of darkness"*,
now with its consequence stated, and it brings back the stumbling the son was promised he would
not suffer (4:12). See [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] and [[Light and Darkness]].

**The wicked who love the night — Job 24.** Job 24:13–17 describes the same people from the
other side: *"those who rebel against the light"*, who *"don't know its ways, nor stay in its
paths"* — the murderer, the adulterer who waits for twilight, the burglars digging through
houses in the dark, for whom *"the morning is … like thick darkness"*. Proverbs and Job agree on
the portrait. **They disagree on what follows from it.** Proverbs 4:19 gives the wicked a
darkness that trips them; Job's speech begins by asking why the Almighty does not keep times of
judgment (24:1) and says that *"God doesn't regard the folly"* (24:12). The wiki keeps both: the
lecture describes where the dark road leads; Job testifies that the fall is not always seen
in his lifetime. Job 24:22–24 then speaks of the mighty as exalted and soon gone, so even
Job's chapter is not a simple denial. See
[[Rebels Against the Light - Proverbs 4 and Job 24]] and
[[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]].

**Two ways.** The pericope is Proverbs' plainest statement of the two-ways teaching that has run
under the book since 1:15 and 2:12–20: one road, one alternative, and nothing in between. It sits
in a line with Deuteronomy 30:15–19, where Moses sets *"life and death"* before Israel, and with
Psalm 1, which ends on *"the way of the righteous"* and *"the way of the wicked"* (1:6), and it
reaches Matthew 7:13–14's broad and narrow ways. See
[[The Two Ways - Proverbs 4, Psalm 1, and Matthew 7]] and [[Walking with God]].

## Key verses

> **4:10** Listen, my son, and receive my sayings. The years of your life will be many.

> **4:11** I have taught you in the way of wisdom. I have led you in straight paths.

> **4:12** When you go, your steps will not be hampered. When you run, you will not stumble.

> **4:13** Take firm hold of instruction. Don’t let her go. Keep her, for she is your life.

> **4:14** Don’t enter into the path of the wicked. Don’t walk in the way of evil men.

> **4:15** Avoid it, and don’t pass by it. Turn from it, and pass on.

> **4:16** For they don’t sleep, unless they do evil. Their sleep is taken away, unless they make someone fall.

> **4:17** For they eat the bread of wickedness, and drink the wine of violence.

> **4:18** But the path of the righteous is like the dawning light, that shines more and more until the perfect day.

> **4:19** The way of the wicked is like darkness. They don’t know what they stumble over.

## People

- The father and the son are unnamed, as in [[Get Wisdom]]. The collection heading names
  [[Solomon]] (1:1), but 4:10–19 names no one, and no person page is revised for this unit.
- *"Evil men"* and *"the wicked"* are a type, not identified individuals.

## Places

No named geographical place. The path and the way are metaphors for a manner of life.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 4:10–19 unit is kept. The renewed *"My son"* at 4:20 opens the next
lecture, which moves from the road to the heart, eyes, and feet.
