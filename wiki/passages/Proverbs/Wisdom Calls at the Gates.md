# Wisdom Calls at the Gates

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 8:1–21

## Summary

Wisdom's second speech, and the first half of the longest one in the book. Chapter 7 ended with
the strange woman's house as *"the way to Sheol"*; chapter 8 opens at once with a question that
answers it: *"Doesn't wisdom cry out? Doesn't understanding raise her voice?"* (8:1). **The
setting** (8:1–3): Wisdom stands on the heights by the road, where the paths meet, and beside the
city gates. **The call** (8:4–11): she calls to all people, the simple and fools included, to
learn prudence and understanding; her words are true, righteous, and straight, with *"nothing
crooked or perverse in them"*, and her instruction is to be taken *"rather than silver"*, because
*"wisdom is better than rubies"*. **Her self-description** (8:12–21): *"I, wisdom"* lives with
prudence, hates pride and the perverse mouth, and owns counsel, sound knowledge, understanding,
and power. *"By me kings reign"*; she loves those who love her, is found by those who seek her,
and brings riches, honour, and enduring wealth, walking in the paths of justice to fill her lovers'
treasuries. The speech continues at 8:22 with Wisdom's origin before creation; see the book page's
next pericope.

The speech is built so that **Wisdom answers the strange woman point for point**, in the same
places and often in the same words. Chapter 7's woman lurked at corners at nightfall; Wisdom stands
in the open at the gates. She spoke smooth words; Wisdom's words have nothing crooked in them. She
sought the young man and found him; Wisdom is found by those who seek her. She offered *loving*
until morning; Wisdom loves those who love her. See
[[Two Voices in the Street - The Strange Woman and Wisdom in Proverbs 7-9]].

**8:1–3 — Where she stands.** *"Doesn't wisdom cry out? Doesn't understanding raise her voice? On
the top of high places by the way, where the paths meet, she stands. Beside the gates, at the entry
of the city, at the entry doors, she cries aloud"* (8:1–3). The Hebrew (read from Sefaria's *Miqra
according to the Masorah*) repeats the opening of Wisdom's first speech: *tiqrāʾ*, *she calls*
(8:1; 1:21), *tittēn qôlāh*, *she gives her voice* (8:1; 1:20), and *tārōnnâ*, *she cries aloud*
(8:3; 1:20). The places are the same kind too: the *gates* (*šəʿārîm*, 8:3; 1:21) and the *entries*
(*pəṯāḥîm*, 8:3; *piṯḥê*, 1:21). What is new is height and the crossroads. *Bərōʾš-mərōmîm*, *on
the top of high places* (8:2), anticipates 9:3, where Wisdom calls *"from the highest places of the
city"* (*mərōmê qāreṯ*), and 9:14, where Folly sits *"on a seat in the high places of the city"* —
the same phrase, so the two women will compete for the same height. *Bêṯ nəṯîḇôṯ*, *the house of
paths*, is the WEB's *"where the paths meet"*: Wisdom stands where every road passes, so no one
can say they did not hear her. She *stands* (*niṣṣāḇâ*), where the strange woman *lurked*
(7:12).

The reading recorded on [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]] still applies. Job 28 asks *"where shall
wisdom be found?"* and answers that *"it is hidden from the eyes of all living"* (Job 28:12, 21).
Proverbs 8 opens with a question expecting the answer *yes*: wisdom does cry out, publicly, at every
crossroads. The wiki keeps both: Job 28's wisdom is the knowledge by which God made the world; the
wisdom of Proverbs 8:1–21 is a summons anyone can hear. The second half of the chapter (8:22–31)
will bring the two closer, because there Wisdom says she was with Yahweh at creation.

**8:4–9 — To all people, with straight words.** *"To you men, I call! I send my voice to the sons
of mankind"* (8:4). *ʾÎšîm*, *men*, and *bənê ʾāḏām*, *the sons of Adam*: the address is
universal, not to Israel or to a school. The first call (1:22) was to the *simple*, the *mockers*,
and the *fools*; this one is to the simple and fools again — *"You simple, understand prudence. You
fools, be of an understanding heart"* (8:5) — but its tone is invitation, not rebuke. *ʿOrmâ*,
*prudence*, was the book's first promise to the simple: *"to give prudence to the simple"* (1:4,
*lāṯēṯ lip̄ṯāʾyim ʿormâ*). Wisdom now offers in person what the preface said the book would give.

*"Hear, for I will speak excellent things. The opening of my lips is for right things. For my mouth
speaks truth. Wickedness is an abomination to my lips"* (8:6–7). *Nəḡîḏîm*, *excellent things*, is
from the word for a prince or leader (*nāḡîḏ*), so the phrase may mean *princely things*; the WEB
renders the sense. *Mêšārîm*, *right things*, is the *equity* of 1:3. *Tôʿăḇaṯ śəp̄āṯay*, *an
abomination to my lips*, uses the noun of 6:16, where the seven things are *"an abomination to"*
Yahweh (*tôʿăḇaṯ nap̄šô*): Wisdom hates what Yahweh hates. *"My mouth speaks"* is *yehgeh ḥikkî*,
literally *my palate murmurs* — *hāḡâ*, the verb of quiet speech or meditation.

*"All the words of my mouth are in righteousness. There is nothing crooked or perverse in them"*
(8:8, *nip̄tāl wəʿiqqēš*). The two adjectives are the two of Deuteronomy 32:5, where Moses calls
Israel *"a perverse and crooked generation"* (*dôr ʿiqqēš ûp̄ətaltōl*), and *ʿiqqēš* is the word for
the wicked men of 2:15, *"crooked in their ways"* (*ʿiqqəšîm*). Wisdom's speech is the reverse of the
speakers the son was warned against: *"the men who speak perverse things"* (2:12) and the strange
woman *"who flatters with her words"* (2:16; 7:5). *"They are all plain to him who understands"*
(8:9, *nəḵōḥîm*, *straight ahead*, *in front*): what Wisdom says is not riddling. The book's preface
promised to explain *"the words and riddles of the wise"* (1:6); Wisdom's own words need no
explaining.

**8:10–11 — Better than silver.** *"Receive my instruction rather than silver; knowledge rather than
choice gold. For wisdom is better than rubies. All the things that may be desired can't be compared
to it"* (8:10–11). *Qəḥû mûsārî wəʾal-kāsep̄*, *take my instruction and not silver*: the choice is
put as an exclusion, not a ranking. 8:11b is almost word for word 3:15b — *wəḵol-ḥăp̄āṣîm lōʾ
yišwû-ḇāh* (8:11), *wəḵol-ḥăp̄āṣeyḵā lōʾ yišwû-ḇāh* (3:15) — with *your desires* made general.
*Mippənînîm*, *than rubies*, is in both verses. The father's praise of wisdom in chapter 3 is
repeated by Wisdom herself, and in the third person even though she is speaking (*"wisdom is
better"*). See [[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]] and
[[Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth]].

**8:12–14 — "I, wisdom".** *"I, wisdom, have made prudence my dwelling. Find out knowledge and
discretion"* (8:12). *ʾĂnî-ḥoḵmâ šāḵantî ʿormâ*: *šāḵan* is *to dwell* or *settle*, the verb of
the cloud that *"settled"* on Sinai and of God's dwelling among Israel (Exodus 24:16; 25:8). The WEB
takes *ʿormâ* as the place Wisdom lives; the Hebrew can also mean that she dwells *with* prudence.
*Mezimmôṯ*, *discretion*, is the *mezimmâ* promised *"to the young man"* in 1:4. The preface's gifts
— prudence to the simple, knowledge and discretion to the young — are in Wisdom's household.

*"The fear of Yahweh is to hate evil. I hate pride, arrogance, the evil way, and the perverse
mouth"* (8:13). The only time Yahweh is named in 8:1–21, and the book's motto restated as a
definition. Proverbs 1:7 made the fear of Yahweh *"the beginning of knowledge"*; 3:7 joined it to
*"depart from evil"*, as Job 28:28 did (*"To depart from evil is understanding"*). Here it is
*śənōʾṯ-rāʿ*, *to hate evil*, and Wisdom immediately says what she hates. *Gēʾâ wəgāʾôn*, *pride
and arrogance*, are a sound-pair from the same root, *gāʾâ*, *to rise up*; *pî tahpuḵôṯ*, *the
perverse mouth*, is 2:12's *"men who speak perverse things"* (*məḏabbēr tahpuḵôṯ*). See
[[The Fear of God]], [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]], and
[[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]].

*"Counsel and sound knowledge are mine. I have understanding and power"* (8:14, *lî-ʿēṣâ
wəṯûšiyyâ ʾănî ḇînâ lî ḡəḇûrâ*). The verse claims for Wisdom four things Job claimed for God:
*"With God is wisdom and might. He has counsel and understanding"* (Job 12:13, *ʿimmô ḥoḵmâ
ûḡəḇûrâ lô ʿēṣâ ûṯəḇûnâ*). Wisdom, counsel, understanding, and might are also the spirit that rests
on the coming king in Isaiah 11:2: *"the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel
and might"* (*rûaḥ ʿēṣâ ûḡəḇûrâ*). Job said these belonged to God; Proverbs 8 gives them to Wisdom;
Isaiah gives them, by the Spirit, to a king. See
[[Counsel and Might - Job 12.13, Proverbs 8.14, and Isaiah 11.2]]. The word *ʾănî ḇînâ*, *I am
understanding*, is literally an identity statement, not *I have*; the WEB smooths it.

**8:15–16 — By me kings reign.** *"By me kings reign, and princes decree justice. By me princes rule;
nobles, and all the righteous rulers of the earth"* (8:15–16). *Bî məlāḵîm yimlōḵû*: *by me*, or
*through me*, kings are kings. Every title of rule is in these two verses — *məlāḵîm* (kings),
*rōzənîm* (rulers, princes), *śārîm* (princes, officials), *nəḏîḇîm* (nobles), *šōp̄əṭîm* (judges).
The claim is that good government is done by wisdom, and the book was put under the name of a king
who asked for exactly that: Solomon asked for *"an understanding heart to judge your people, that I
may discern between good and evil"* (1 Kings 3:9). See
[[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]] and [[Kingship and Empire]].
*Yəḥōqəqû ṣeḏeq*, *decree justice*, uses *ḥāqaq*, *to inscribe*, the root of *ḥōq*, *statute*.

*"All the righteous rulers of the earth"* (8:16) has a textual question. The Hebrew of the Masoretic
text, as printed in Sefaria's edition, reads *kol-šōp̄əṭê ṣeḏeq*, *all the judges of righteousness*,
with no word for *earth*. The WEB's *"of the earth"* evidently follows a different reading of the
line; the wiki has not checked which manuscripts or versions have it and takes no position.

**8:17 — Love and seeking.** *"I love those who love me. Those who seek me diligently will find
me"* (8:17). The verbs of 1:28 come back without the negative: there, those who refused her
*"will seek me diligently, but they will not find me"* (*yəšaḥărunənî wəlōʾ yimṣāʾunənî*); here,
*ûməšaḥăray yimṣāʾunənî*. Proverbs states both, and [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]] records the
reading the wiki takes: the difference is time — 1:28 is seeking after the storm has come. And the
strange woman used the same pair of the young man: she came *"to diligently seek your face, and I
have found you"* (7:15). **Wisdom is sought and found; the strange woman does the seeking
herself.** Jeremiah 29:13 makes the same promise of God: *"You shall seek me, and find me, when you
search for me with all your heart."*

The first line has a written-and-read variant in the Masoretic text. Sefaria's edition prints the
written form *ʾhbyh* and the read form *ʾōhăḇay*, *those who love me*; the written consonants are
usually taken as *those who love her*, which does not fit a first-person speech, and the read form
is the one translated. The WEB follows the read form. The love is mutual and conditional: *"Love her,
and she will keep you"* (4:6), the father said; Wisdom now says it herself. See [[Love]].

**8:18–21 — Riches and righteousness.** *"With me are riches, honor, enduring wealth, and
prosperity"* (8:18). *ʿŌšer wəḵāḇôḏ*, *riches and honour*, is the pair in Wisdom's left hand at
3:16, and the pair God gave Solomon though he had not asked: *"I have also given you that which you
have not asked, both riches and honor"* (1 Kings 3:13, *gam-ʿōšer gam-kāḇôḏ*). **The WEB's
*prosperity* translates *ṣəḏāqâ*, the ordinary word for *righteousness***, the same word that
returns two verses later in *"the way of righteousness"* (8:20). The WEB reads *ṣəḏāqâ* here as
its consequence, well-being; the Hebrew leaves the two together, so that the list of Wisdom's
goods ends on righteousness itself.

*"My fruit is better than gold, yes, than fine gold; my yield than choice silver"* (8:19) repeats
3:14 with Wisdom as speaker: *ṭôḇ piryî mēḥārûṣ … ûṯəḇûʾāṯî mikkesep̄ niḇḥār* against 3:14's *ṭôḇ
saḥrāh missəḥar-kāsep̄ ûmēḥārûṣ təḇûʾāṯāh*. *Təḇûʾâ*, *yield* or *harvest*, is in both. Her
*fruit* is the fruit of the tree of life she was called at 3:18.

*"I walk in the way of righteousness, in the middle of the paths of justice; that I may give wealth
to those who love me. I fill their treasuries"* (8:20–21). *Bəʾōraḥ-ṣəḏāqâ … bəṯôḵ nəṯîḇôṯ
mišpāṭ*: Wisdom is now a walker on the roads where she stood at the start (*nəṯîḇôṯ*, 8:2), and her
road is the one God guards at 2:8 (*ʾorḥôṯ mišpāṭ*, *"the paths of justice"*). *Ləhanḥîl*, *to
cause to inherit*, is the verb of the land given to Israel as an inheritance; what she gives is
*yēš*, a rare noun use of the word for *there is* — *substance*, *what really exists* — which the
WEB renders *wealth*. *ʾŌṣərōṯêhem ʾămallēʾ*, *I will fill their treasuries*.

**The promise and the exclusion stand side by side.** 8:10 says *take instruction and not silver*;
8:18–21 says wisdom brings riches, honour, and full treasuries. The speech does not explain how
both are meant. The wiki reads it as an order of desires — wisdom first, wealth as what may follow —
which is how 1 Kings 3:11–13 tells Solomon's case: he did not ask for riches, and was given them.
But the speech states the promise without exceptions, and the wiki has already met a man whom
wisdom and fear of God did not keep rich. See [[Wealth and Possessions]] and the next paragraph.

**Reading it after Job.** Job did what 8:13 defines as the fear of Yahweh — he *"feared God, and
turned away from evil"* (Job 1:1) — and lost everything 8:18–21 promises: riches (1:13–17), honour
(19:9, *"He has stripped me of my glory"*), and an inheritance (his children, 1:18–19). He also sat
at the city gate in honour before his losses (Job 29:7), where Wisdom now stands. The wiki keeps the
reserve it has kept since [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]]: Proverbs describes how things
ordinarily go when wisdom is loved; Job shows a case where they did not, and God said Job had spoken
rightly (Job 42:7). See [[Retribution]].

**Wisdom speaks; is she God?** As in 1:20–33, Wisdom speaks in a voice close to God's — she hates
what Yahweh hates, claims what Job gave to God, and makes kings reign — but she names Yahweh in the
third person (8:13) and does not say she is him. The wiki records that she speaks as God's own
wisdom personified, without deciding more. 8:22–31 will say where she came from.

## People

- Wisdom personified is the speaker, as at 1:20–33; she is treated on [[Wisdom]], not as a person
  page.
- [[God]] — named once, *Yahweh*, in *"The fear of Yahweh is to hate evil"* (8:13).
- [[Solomon]] — not named; 8:15–18 is set beside 1 Kings 3:9–13, the request for wisdom to judge and
  the riches and honour given besides.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 8:1–21 unit is kept. 8:1 opens the speech with a new question after 7:27,
and 8:22 begins Wisdom's account of her origin (*"Yahweh possessed me in the beginning of his
work"*). Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* marks a *petuḥah* after 7:27 and the next one
after 8:31, with none inside 8:1–21, so the division at 8:22 is the wiki's, by subject, not the
Masoretic paragraphing. The title is the plan's, from 8:1–3.
