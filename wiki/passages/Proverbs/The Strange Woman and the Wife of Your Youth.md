# The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 5:1–23

## Summary

The first lecture given wholly to one subject. Proverbs 2:16–19 introduced the strange woman in a
single sentence; chapter 5 gives her a whole lecture, and answers her with the son's own wife. It
moves in three steps. **Her mouth and her end** (5:1–6): lips that *"drip honey"*, a mouth
*"smoother than oil"*, and an end *"as bitter as wormwood"*, her feet going down to death and her
steps to [[Sheol]]. **The cost of her door** (5:7–14): stay far from it, or lose honour, years,
strength, and labour to strangers, and groan at the end over the instruction refused. **The well
at home** (5:15–20): drink from your own cistern, rejoice in *"the wife of your youth"*, and be
captivated by her love — so why by another's? A closing triplet sets all of it before Yahweh's
eyes: the wicked man is caught by his own sins and dies *"for lack of instruction"* (5:21–23).

**5:1–6 — Honey and wormwood.** The lecture opens as the last one did — attend, *"Turn your ear"*
(5:1; 4:20) — and its purpose is that *"your lips may preserve knowledge"* (5:2). The son's lips
are set at once against hers: *"For the lips of an adulteress drip honey"* (5:3). The WEB's
*"adulteress"* renders Hebrew *zārâ*, the same word it rendered *"the strange woman"* at 2:16; in
5:20 *zārâ* is paired again, as at 2:16, with *nokriyyâ*, *"another"* — *foreign*, or *belonging
to someone else* (Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*). The wiki keeps the
reading set out on [[If You Seek Her as Silver]]: she is another man's wife, *strange* because she
belongs to another household, and the lecture's own contrast — her against *your* wife — supports
it. Her danger is again her speech (2:16), and her speech is pleasant; the warning is about what
comes *after*. *"In the end"* (*ʾaḥărîṯāh*) she is wormwood and a two-edged sword (5:4), and
5:11 uses the same word for the son's own end, *"at your latter end"* (*bəʾaḥărîṯeḵā*): her end
becomes his. Her feet go down to death (5:5), the opposite of the son's feet in 4:26–27, and
*"She gives no thought to the way of life"* (5:6). The verb there, *təpallēs*, is the one 4:26
used for *"Make the path of your feet level"* (*pallēs*), and 5:21 uses it again of Yahweh, who
*"examines"* (*məpallēs*) every path a man walks: she does not weigh the way of life; Yahweh
weighs every way. The gloss *weigh* or *make level* is the conventional one and more than one
rendering is defended; the shared verb is read from the Hebrew text itself. The Masoretic text
closes this first section with a *petuḥah* after 5:6.

**5:7–14 — What her house costs.** *"Now therefore, my sons"* (5:7) — plural, like 4:1's
*"Listen, sons"* — and the command is distance: *"Don't come near the door of her house"* (5:8). The cost is listed as a
transfer of everything the son owns to others: honour to *"others"*, years to *"the cruel one"*,
wealth to *"strangers"* (*zārîm*, the masculine plural of her own name), labour to *"another man's
house"* (*bêṯ nokrî*) (5:9–10). Who *"the cruel one"* is the text does not say — the wronged
husband, a creditor, or the figure of death are all proposed, and this page does not choose. The
father then puts a confession in the son's mouth, spoken too late: *"How I have hated instruction,
and my heart despised reproof"* (5:12). It is the confession of the fool of 1:7, who *despises*
instruction, and of the refusers of [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]], who *"hated knowledge"* (1:29)
and *"despised all my reproof"* (1:30). He ends *"at the brink of utter ruin, among the gathered
assembly"* (5:14): the ruin is public. See [[Folly]].

**5:15–20 — Your own well.** The answer to the strange woman is not abstinence but a wife. *"Drink
water out of your own cistern, running water out of your own well"* (5:15). In a dry land a
cistern or well was a household's most guarded possession — Genesis's wells are fought over and
sworn to — and the image makes the wife the son's own spring, as Song of Solomon 4:12 calls the
bride *"a locked up spring, a sealed fountain"*. Verse 16 is uncertain. The WEB makes it a
question, *"Should your springs overflow in the streets?"*; the Hebrew has no interrogative
particle, and some read it as a statement, or of children spreading out from the household. The
WEB's question fits 5:17, *"Let them be for yourself alone, not for strangers with you"*, and the
page follows it with that caveat. Then the blessing: *"Let your spring be blessed. Rejoice in the
wife of your youth"* (5:18, *ʾēšeṯ nəʿûreḵā*) — the phrase Malachi 2:14 uses, and the reverse of
the strange woman of 2:17, who *"forsakes the friend of her youth"*. Verse 19 is the most openly
erotic sentence the wiki has yet reached: *"let her breasts satisfy you at all times. Be
captivated always with her love."* **Delight in marriage is commanded, not merely permitted.**
See [[Your Own Well - Proverbs 5.15-19, Song of Solomon 4.12-15, and Ecclesiastes 9.9]] and
[[The Wife of Your Youth - Proverbs 2.17, Malachi 2.14, and Jeremiah 3.4]].

**One verb three times.** *"Be captivated"* (5:19), *"be captivated"* (5:20), and *"go astray"*
(5:23) all render forms of one Hebrew verb, *šāḡâ* (*tišgeh*, *tišgeh*, *yišgeh*), which elsewhere
means to stagger, to reel, or to err (the forms are read from the Hebrew text; the sense range is
the conventional lexical one, not checked against a lexicon this pass). The lecture uses it as a
hinge. Be intoxicated with your wife's love; why be intoxicated with a stranger? — and the man
who is ends up staggering *"in the greatness of his folly"*. The same reeling is either delight
or ruin, depending on whose love it is.

**5:21–23 — Before Yahweh's eyes.** The lecture's last argument is not prudence but sight: *"For
the ways of man are before Yahweh's eyes. He examines all his paths"* (5:21). The secret the
adulterer counts on does not exist. Job 24:15 knew the type — *"The eye also of the adulterer
waits for the twilight, saying, 'No eye shall see me'"* — and answered with *"His eyes are on
their ways"* (24:23). Then the trap: *"The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him. The cords of his
sin hold him firmly"* (5:22). *"Hold"* here (*yittāmēḵ*) is the verb 5:5 used for her steps
that *"lead straight"* (*yiṯmōḵû*, *take hold of*) to Sheol: she holds to Sheol, and his sin holds
him. The self-springing trap is the one [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] turned on the gang (1:17–18).
He dies *"for lack of instruction"* (*mûsār*, 5:23), the instruction he confessed to hating
(5:12). See [[Seeing and Being Seen]] and [[Retribution]].

**Job swore to this lecture.** Job 31:9–12 is the same scene from the other side, under oath: *"If
my heart has been enticed to a woman, and I have laid wait at my neighbor's door, then let my wife
grind for another, and let others sleep with her. For that would be a heinous crime … For it is a
fire that consumes to destruction, and would root out all my increase."* The door (Proverbs 5:8),
the loss of everything to others (5:9–10; Job 31:8, *"let me sow, and let another eat"*), and the
end in destruction are all there. Proverbs warns the son what adultery will cost him; Job names
that cost as the penalty he accepts if he is guilty. See
[[The Neighbor's Door - Proverbs 5 and Job 31.9-12]].

**Bildad's net, and its misuse.** Proverbs 5:22's self-catching snare is the image Bildad pressed
on Job: *"he is cast into a net by his own feet … A snare will take him by the heel"* (Job
18:8–9). In Proverbs it describes a man the text has shown sinning; in [[Bildad's Second Speech]]
it is applied to a man God has called blameless (Job 1:8). The rule and the misapplication use
the same picture, and the wiki keeps them distinct: Proverbs states what sin does, not that every
trapped man has sinned. See [[Retribution]].

> **5:3** For the lips of an adulteress drip honey. Her mouth is smoother than oil,

> **5:4** but in the end she is as bitter as wormwood, and as sharp as a two-edged sword.

> **5:5** Her feet go down to death. Her steps lead straight to Sheol.

> **5:8** Remove your way far from her. Don’t come near the door of her house,

> **5:12** and say, “How I have hated instruction, and my heart despised reproof;

> **5:15** Drink water out of your own cistern, running water out of your own well.

> **5:18** Let your spring be blessed. Rejoice in the wife of your youth.

> **5:19** A loving doe and a graceful deer— let her breasts satisfy you at all times. Be
> captivated always with her love.

> **5:21** For the ways of man are before Yahweh’s eyes. He examines all his paths.

> **5:22** The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him. The cords of his sin hold him firmly.

> **5:23** He will die for lack of instruction. In the greatness of his folly, he will go astray.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 5:1–23 unit is kept. *"My son"* opens it at 5:1 and 6:1 opens a new
lecture on surety with another *"My son"*. Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* marks a
*petuḥah* after 5:6 and after 5:23; the internal break at 5:6 is treated as a section within one
lecture, since 5:7's *"Now therefore"* continues the same subject.
