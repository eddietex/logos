# The King's Heart

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 21:1–31

## Summary

The chapter opens and closes on the limits of human power before Yahweh. The most powerful heart
in the land, the king's, is in Yahweh's hand *"like the watercourses"* (1); at the other end, no
wisdom, understanding, or counsel stands *"against Yahweh"*, and though the horse is prepared for
battle, *"victory is with Yahweh"* (30–31). Between these frames the sayings test what people trust:
their own sense of being right (2), sacrifice (3, 27), treasure gained by a lying tongue (6), a
bribe (14), pleasure (17), stored oil (20), and a mighty city's confidence (22).

This is a collection of short sayings, not a continuous speech. The groups below are reading aids;
they do not claim that every neighboring verse belongs to one argument.

**21:1–8 — The weighed heart.** The king's heart is turned like irrigation channels (1). The image
does not say how the turning happens or that the king loses responsibility for his decisions, and
no particular king is named. Every way seems right *"in his own eyes"*, but Yahweh weighs the hearts
(2), very nearly repeating 16:2 with *hearts* where that verse reads *motives*. Doing righteousness
and justice is more acceptable than sacrifice (3): a ranking, not an abolition of sacrifice, which
verse 27 assumes is still offered. Pride is called *"the lamp of the wicked"* (4), an image Proverbs
elsewhere snuffs out (13:9; 20:20). Diligent plans profit while haste rushes to poverty (5);
treasure won by lies is *"a fleeting vapor for those who seek death"* (6). Violence drives its
makers away because they refuse right conduct (7), and the guilty way is crooked where the innocent
is upright (8).

**21:9–19 — Households, the poor, the bribe, and the end of the wanderer.** Better a corner of the
housetop than a house shared with a contentious woman (9), and better a desert than a contentious
and fretful woman (19). The two sayings frame the section and speak from a husband's point of view;
they are not a general estimate of women, and Proverbs elsewhere calls a prudent wife a gift from
Yahweh (19:14) and names a *contentious man* who kindles strife (26:21). See
[[The Contentious Woman - Proverbs 19.13, 21.9, 19, 25.24, and 27.15]]. Between them: the wicked
soul shows a neighbor no mercy (10); the mocker's punishment teaches the simple, while the wise
learn by instruction alone (11). *"The Righteous One"* considers the wicked house and brings it to
ruin (12) — the WEB capitalizes the title as a divine reference, while the KJV, checked at
bible-api.com, reads *"The righteous man wisely considereth"*, a human observer, and ascribes the
overthrow to God by supplying the subject; the wiki follows the stored WEB without deciding the
question. Whoever stops his ears at the poor man's cry will cry and not be heard (13), measure for
measure. A secret gift pacifies anger and a bribe in the cloak calms strong wrath (14): an
observation that bribery works, not an approval of it — 17:23 calls receiving a secret bribe the
perversion of justice. Doing justice is joy to the righteous and ruin to evildoers (15). The one
who wanders from understanding *"shall rest in the assembly of the departed spirits"* (16), the same
WEB phrase used for the strange woman's and Folly's guests (2:18; 9:18). Love of pleasure, wine, and
oil impoverishes (17). The wicked become *"a ransom for the righteous"* (18), a reversal like 11:8,
where the wicked takes the delivered righteous man's place; the saying names no payer, payee, or
legal procedure.

**21:20–31 — Treasure, the tongue, and the limits of strength.** The wise house holds treasure and
oil that the fool swallows (20). Pursuing righteousness and kindness finds life, righteousness, and
honor (21) — the pursuit receives what it pursues and more. The wise man scales the city of the
mighty and brings down *"the strength of its confidence"* (22), echoing the rich man's imagined
*unscalable wall* of 18:11. Guarding mouth and tongue keeps the soul from troubles (23). The proud
man's name is *scoffer* (24). The sluggard's desire kills him because his hands refuse work (25);
the covetous crave all day while the righteous give without withholding (26). The wicked man's
sacrifice is an abomination, still more when brought with wicked intent (27), repeating 15:8 and
intensifying it. The false witness perishes; the listener *"speaks to eternity"* (28). The wicked
hardens his face, the upright establishes his ways (29). Then the close: no counsel against Yahweh
(30), and victory not with the horse but with him (31). Verse 31 does not forbid preparation — the
horse *is* prepared — but locates the outcome elsewhere.

**Tensions kept explicit.** Verse 1's turned royal heart stands beside Exodus, where Yahweh says
*"I will harden his heart"* of Pharaoh (4:21) and the narrator also says Pharaoh *"hardened his
heart"* himself (9:34): divine direction and royal responsibility are both asserted and not
reconciled. See [[The King's Heart in Yahweh's Hand - Proverbs 21.1, Exodus, and Ezra 6.22]].
Verse 13's unheard cry is a rule about the one who ignores the poor; it is not a diagnosis that
everyone whose prayer goes unanswered has ignored the poor — Job cried out and was not answered
(Job 19:7; 30:20) though God affirmed his integrity. Verse 14's working bribe and 17:23's condemned
bribe are both retained: Proverbs reports what works and judges it separately. Verse 18's ransom
reverses fortunes; it is not developed into a theory of substitution here.

## Key verses

> **21:1** The king’s heart is in Yahweh’s hand like the watercourses. He turns it wherever he desires.

> **21:2** Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but Yahweh weighs the hearts.

> **21:3** To do righteousness and justice is more acceptable to Yahweh than sacrifice.

> **21:9** It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop, than to share a house with a contentious woman.

> **21:12** The Righteous One considers the house of the wicked, and brings the wicked to ruin.

> **21:13** Whoever stops his ears at the cry of the poor, he will also cry out, but shall not be heard.

> **21:14** A gift in secret pacifies anger; and a bribe in the cloak, strong wrath.

> **21:16** The man who wanders out of the way of understanding shall rest in the assembly of the departed spirits.

> **21:18** The wicked is a ransom for the righteous; the treacherous for the upright.

> **21:21** He who follows after righteousness and kindness finds life, righteousness, and honor.

> **21:22** A wise man scales the city of the mighty, and brings down the strength of its confidence.

> **21:23** Whoever guards his mouth and his tongue keeps his soul from troubles.

> **21:25** The desire of the sluggard kills him, for his hands refuse to labor.

> **21:27** The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination; how much more, when he brings it with a wicked mind!

> **21:30** There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against Yahweh.

> **21:31** The horse is prepared for the day of battle; but victory is with Yahweh.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh is named in verses 1–3, 30–31; *"the Righteous One"* (12) is a divine title on
  the WEB's capitalization, a righteous man in the KJV.
- [[Solomon]] — collection attribution from 10:1; not named in chapter 21.
- [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] — comparison only, through the hardened heart of Exodus.
- The king, the contentious woman, the mocker, the simple, the sluggard, the false witness, the
  scoffer, and the wise man who scales a city are generic figures; none receives an invented
  identity.

## Places

- [[Sheol]] — not named, but verse 16's *"assembly of the departed spirits"* is the WEB phrase that
  2:18 and 9:18 place in the realm of the dead (9:18: *"the depths of Sheol"*).
- The housetop corner, the desert land, and the city of the mighty are images, not identifiable
  locations requiring place pages.

## Themes

- [[Providence]] — Proverbs 21:1, 30–31; The king's heart turned, counsel outmatched, victory given.
- [[Kingship and Empire]] — Proverbs 21:1; The ruler's heart as directed water, without an exemption from responsibility.
- [[Hardening of the Heart]] — Proverbs 21:1, 29; A turned royal heart and a hardened wicked face.
- [[Righteousness]] — Proverbs 21:3, 12, 15, 18, 21, 26; Justice done with joy, pursued, and generous.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — Proverbs 21:3, 27; Justice ranked above sacrifice; the wicked man's offering refused.
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — Proverbs 21:2, 4, 24; Self-approval, the high look, and the scoffer's name.
- [[Light and Darkness]] — Proverbs 21:4; Pride named the lamp of the wicked.
- [[Work]] — Proverbs 21:5, 25; Diligent plans against haste; the sluggard's fatal desire.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Proverbs 21:5–6, 17, 20, 26; Lying treasure, love of pleasure, stored oil, and giving.
- [[Deception]] — Proverbs 21:6, 8, 28; Treasure by a lying tongue and the false witness.
- [[Marriage]] — Proverbs 21:9, 19; The contentious wife, spoken from the husband's side.
- [[Strife and Peacemaking]] — Proverbs 21:9, 14, 19; Domestic contention and anger pacified by a gift.
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — Proverbs 21:10, 13, 21, 26; The wicked show no mercy; kindness is pursued.
- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — Proverbs 21:13; Stopped ears answered by an unheard cry.
- [[Prayer]] — Proverbs 21:13, 27; The cry not heard, and worship refused from the wicked.
- [[Judgment]] — Proverbs 21:2, 12, 15; Hearts weighed and the wicked house considered.
- [[Wisdom]] — Proverbs 21:11, 20, 22, 30; Instruction, stored treasure, strategy, and wisdom's limit.
- [[Folly]] — Proverbs 21:16, 20, 24; The wanderer, the swallowing fool, and the scoffer.
- [[Life and Death]] — Proverbs 21:6, 16, 21, 25; Seekers of death, the departed spirits, and life found.
- [[Redemption]] — Proverbs 21:18; The wicked as ransom for the righteous, a reversal of places.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Proverbs 21:6, 23, 28; Lying tongues, guarded mouths, and witnesses.
- [[War]] — Proverbs 21:22, 31; The wise besieger and the prepared horse.
- [[Retribution]] — Proverbs 21:7, 13, 16, 18, 25; Expected outcomes, measure for measure, held beside Job.

## Notes

**Boundary and attribution.** The planned 21:1–31 unit is retained under the book page's
convention of taking the sentence collection a chapter at a time. Exactly one queue item is
advanced. The collection's Solomonic heading (10:1) supplies attribution, not the identity of the
chapter's king.

**Sources.** Every quotation from Proverbs and Exodus is copied from the stored WEB in `raw/`.
Comparisons were checked in Proverbs 2:18; 9:18; 11:8; 12:4; 13:9; 15:8, 29; 16:2; 17:23; 18:11,
16, 22; 19:13–14; 20:20; 24:12; 26:21; and Exodus 4:21; 9:12, 34; 14:14, 17, 28; 15:1, 21. External
WEB verses quoted on the new connection pages (1 Samuel 15:22; Hosea 6:6; Micah 6:8; Psalm 20:7;
33:17; Ezra 6:22) and Proverbs 25:24 and 27:15 (stored but not yet ingested) were verified at
bible-api.com with `translation=web` on 2026-10-26; the KJV reading of 21:12 was checked at the
same service with `translation=kjv`. Job 19:7 and 30:20 were checked in the stored WEB and are cited by reference. No
unreached book was fetched, and existing raw text is unchanged.

**Language and interpretation.** No claim about Hebrew wording is made in this pass. In particular,
the wiki does not assert here which Hebrew word underlies *departed spirits* at 21:16 or *ransom* at
21:18; earlier pages that discuss *rəp̄āʾîm* and *kōper* document their own sources, and their
identification with this chapter's wording has not been checked. The *Righteous One* of verse 12 is
recorded as a translation difference between the WEB and KJV, not resolved.
