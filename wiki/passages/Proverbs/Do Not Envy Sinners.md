# Do Not Envy Sinners

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 23:12–24:2

## Summary

The second block of *"the words of the wise"* opens as the first did, with a call to attention:
*"Apply your heart to instruction, and your ears to the words of knowledge"* (23:12), which repeats
the shape of 22:17's *"Turn your ear … Apply your heart to my teaching."* What follows sounds more
like chapters 1–9 than like the commands of 22:22–23:11. The address *"My son"* returns three times
(23:15, 19, 26), the father's own heart and gladness are named, the
mother is brought in beside him, and the two old dangers of the prologue — the strange woman and bad
company — come back, joined by a third the prologue never developed: wine. The block is framed by
two prohibitions of envy (23:17; 24:1), and the reason given for both is the same — the sinner has
no future worth wanting, and the son who fears Yahweh does.

**23:12–14 — The rod and Sheol.** *"Don't withhold correction from a child. If you punish him with
the rod, he will not die. Punish him with the rod, and save his soul from Sheol."* The fourth of
the book's rod sayings (13:24; 19:18; 22:15), and the one with the highest stakes. *"He will not
die"* answers the parent's fear that the rod will harm him; *"save his soul from Sheol"* answers it
by naming the worse harm. As at 19:18 (*"don't be a willing party to his death"*), the death in
view is the one folly leads to, the road the prologue walked to the strange woman's house (7:27).
These are sayings for a household in which corporal discipline was assumed; what they argue for is
correction, and the motive they give is the child's life. See
[[The Rod of Discipline - Proverbs 13.24 and the Father's Correction]].

**23:15–16 — The father's heart.** *"My son, if your heart is wise, then my heart will be glad,
even mine: yes, my heart will rejoice, when your lips speak what is right."* After two verses of
the rod, the father says what he wants from it, and it is not obedience but joy. The son's heart and
the father's heart are put side by side in one line. *"Even mine"* is the father refusing to speak
as a detached teacher. The heading saying of the Solomonic collection, *"A wise son makes a glad
father"* (10:1), is here spoken by the father himself in the first person.

**23:17–18 — Envy and a future.** *"Don't let your heart envy sinners; but rather fear Yahweh all
day long. Indeed surely there is a future hope, and your hope will not be cut off."* The block's
title saying. The prohibition supposes that sinners can look enviable — a supposition 3:31 had
already made of *"the man of violence"*, and that Job 21 and Psalm 73 press much harder (see
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]]). Proverbs does not argue that the sinner is unhappy now. It
replaces one direction of the heart with another — envy with the fear of Yahweh, *"all day long"* —
and gives a reason that lies ahead: *"a future"*. The Hebrew word is *ʾaḥărît*, the *latter end*
(see the Notes), and the same word returns fifteen verses later as the end of a cup of wine.

**23:19–21 — The drunkard and the glutton.** *"Listen, my son, and be wise, and keep your heart on
the right path! Don't be among ones drinking too much wine, or those who gorge themselves on meat:
for the drunkard and the glutton shall become poor; and drowsiness clothes them in rags."* The first
warning about drink is a warning about company, like the prologue's first warning about the gang
(1:10–15): *"Don't be among"* them. Its reason is economic and bodily — poverty and rags — and
*"drowsiness"* ties it to the sluggard of 6:9–11. The pair of words, *drunkard* and *glutton*, is
the pair a father and mother bring against a stubborn and rebellious son in Deuteronomy 21:20, and
the pair Jesus's critics use of him (Matthew 11:19). See
[[A Glutton and a Drunkard - Proverbs 23.20-21, Deuteronomy 21.20, and Matthew 11.19]].

**23:22–25 — Father and mother.** *"Listen to your father who gave you life, and don't despise your
mother when she is old."* The parents are named here not as teachers only but as the ones who gave
life, and the mother specifically in her old age — the stage at which a grown son might stop
listening. Between this and the gladness that follows sits a single line of commerce: *"Buy the
truth, and don't sell it. Get wisdom, discipline, and understanding"* (23). The verb rendered
*buy* and *get* is one Hebrew imperative, *qənēh*, the same one 4:5, 7 repeated (*"Get wisdom. Get
understanding"*); this verse adds its opposite — *don't sell*. Wisdom bought at any price is the
prologue's lesson; that it can afterwards be sold is this one's. Then the parents' joy is stated
twice, and the mother is given the last word: *"Let her who bore you rejoice!"* (25). Proverbs
began by setting the mother's teaching beside the father's (1:8); here her gladness is the goal.
See [[Honor Your Father and Mother - Exodus 20 and Ephesians 6]].

**23:26–28 — The deep pit.** *"My son, give me your heart; and let your eyes keep in my ways. For a
prostitute is a deep pit; and a wayward wife is a narrow well. Yes, she lies in wait like a robber,
and increases the unfaithful among men."* The father's request for the son's heart is the positive
form of everything in 4:23 (*"Keep your heart with all diligence"*). The reason is the strange woman,
compressed to three lines. *"A deep pit"* repeats 22:14's phrase (there of an adulteress's mouth),
and *"lies in wait"* is the verb the gang used in the first lecture (*"Let's lay in wait for blood"*,
1:11) and the strange woman's *"lurking"* at 7:12 — the two dangers of the prologue sharing one verb
(see the Notes). The pit and the well are images of a fall with no climbing out. *"Increases the
unfaithful among men"* says what she costs beyond the one victim: she multiplies betrayers. See
[[Two Voices in the Street - The Strange Woman and Wisdom in Proverbs 7-9]].

**23:29–35 — Who has woe?** The longest unit in *"the words of the wise"*, and the most vivid
portrait in Proverbs outside chapter 7. It begins with a riddle of six questions — *"Who has woe? Who
has sorrow? Who has strife? Who has complaints? Who has needless bruises? Who has bloodshot eyes?"* —
and answers it in one line: *"Those who stay long at the wine; those who go to seek out mixed wine"*
(30). Then a command against looking: *"Don't look at the wine when it is red, when it sparkles in
the cup, when it goes down smoothly"* (31). The temptation is presented through the eyes, as Eve's
was (*"a delight to the eyes"*, Genesis 3:6) and as the strange woman's eyelids were (6:25). Then the turn:
*"In the end, it bites like a snake, and poisons like a viper"* (32). Then the drunkard's experience
from the inside — strange sights, a confused mind, the sensation of lying *"in the middle of the
sea"* or *"on top of the rigging"* (33–34) — and finally his own voice: *"They hit me, and I was not
hurt! They beat me, and I don't feel it! When will I wake up? I can do it again. I can find
another."* (35). The portrait ends not with ruin but with the next drink. The point of letting the
drunkard speak last is that he has learned nothing from the bruises the opening riddle counted; the
appetite outlasts the evidence. Proverbs 20:1 had called wine *"a mocker"*; this is the mockery
staged. See [[The Vine and the Cup]] and
[[The Drunkenness of Noah and the Drunkenness of Lot]].

**24:1–2 — Envy again.** *"Don't be envious of evil men; neither desire to be with them: for their
hearts plot violence, and their lips talk about mischief."* The block closes where it began, with
envy forbidden (23:17). This time the reason is not the sinner's end but his present: his heart and
lips — the very organs whose wisdom gladdened the father at 23:15–16 — are busy with violence and
mischief. *"Neither desire to be with them"* names what envy leads to: wanting the company, and then
the ways (3:31). Verse 3 begins a new pair of sayings on building a house by wisdom, and the next
queue item. See [[Do Not Fret Because of Evildoers - Proverbs 23.17, 24.19, and Psalm 37.1]].

**What the block adds.** Read whole, 23:12–24:2 is the wisdom of chapters 1–9 restated as short
commands, and it is organised round the heart: *"apply your heart"* (12), *"if your heart is wise"*
(15), *"Don't let your heart envy"* (17), *"keep your heart on the right path"* (19), *"give me your
heart"* (26), and the evil men whose *"hearts plot violence"* (24:2). The three dangers — envy of
sinners, wine and gluttony, the prostitute — are each a desire that looks toward something present
and visible. The fear of Yahweh is set against them because it looks toward the *ʾaḥărît*, the end.
That is the block's single argument, made at 23:18 and enacted at 23:32: the wine's end is a snake,
and the God-fearer's end is a hope that will not be cut off.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, named once (23:17), as the one to be feared *"all day long"* in place of envy.
  The *"future"* and the *"hope"* of 23:18 are given without saying who secures them; the verse
  leaves that implied by its first line.
- The father, the mother, the son, the child under the rod, the sinners, the drunkard and the
  glutton, the prostitute and the *"wayward wife"*, and the evil men are generic figures; none
  receives an invented identity.

## Places

- [[Sheol]] — 23:14, the place the rod saves the child's soul from.
- The sea and the rigging (23:34) are images of the drunkard's sensation, not settings.

## Themes

- [[Envy]] — Proverbs 23:17; 24:1; Sinners and evil men not to be envied, and not to be joined.
- [[The Fear of God]] — Proverbs 23:17; Fear of Yahweh *"all day long"* as the alternative to envy.
- [[Hope]] — Proverbs 23:18; *"Your hope will not be cut off"*, grounded in an *ʾaḥărît*, a future.
- [[Life and Death]] — Proverbs 23:13–14; The rod that does not kill and saves from Sheol.

- [[Love]] — Proverbs 23:15–16, 24–25; A father's and mother's gladness in a wise child.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Proverbs 23:16; 24:2; Lips that speak what is right, and lips that talk mischief.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Proverbs 23:21, 23; The drunkard's poverty, and truth as the thing not to sell.
- [[Sexual Sin]] — Proverbs 23:27–28; The prostitute as a deep pit and the foreign woman as a narrow well.
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — Proverbs 23:26, 29, 31, 33; Eyes kept in the father's ways, bloodshot eyes, wine looked at, strange sights.
- [[Deception]] — Proverbs 23:31–32; Wine that goes down smoothly and bites at the end.
- [[Strife and Peacemaking]] — Proverbs 23:29; 24:2; Strife and needless bruises at the wine; hearts that plot violence.
- [[Walking with God]] — Proverbs 23:19, 26; The heart kept on the right path, the eyes kept in the father's ways.
- [[Retribution]] — Proverbs 23:18, 21, 32; The latter end as the measure: hope for the God-fearer, rags for the glutton, a snakebite for the drinker.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 23:12–24:2 unit is retained. Verse 12's call to attention echoes 22:17 and
opens the block; 24:1–2 closes it by returning to 23:17's prohibition of envy; 24:3 turns to wisdom
building a house and starts the next queue item ([[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]], 24:3–22). Exactly one
queue item is advanced.

**Language.** Read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* on 2026-10-26:
- *Future* at 23:18 is *ʾaḥărît* (*kî ʾim-yēš ʾaḥărît*), and *"In the end"* at 23:32 is *ʾaḥărîtô*,
  *its end* — the same noun, used of the God-fearer's future and the wine's aftermath. It recurs at
  24:14, where the WEB has *"there will be a reward, your hope will not be cut off"* for a clause
  identical to 23:18's except for its opening particle, and at 24:20, *"there will be no reward to the evil man"* (*lōʾ-
  tihyeh ʾaḥărît lārāʿ*). The WEB translates the one word three ways. Psalm 73:17 has *ʾaḥărîtām*,
  *their latter end*, in the same edition.
- *Stay long* at 23:30 is *məʾaḥărîm*, a participle with the same three consonants as *ʾaḥărît*;
  that they share a root is standard lexical knowledge, not checked against a lexicon in this pass.
- *What is right* at 23:16 and *smoothly* at 23:31 are the same word, *mêšārîm*: the son's lips speak
  *uprightly*, and the wine goes down *uprightly*. The WEB's *smoothly* is a fair sense; the echo is
  lost in English.
- *Envy* at 23:17 and 24:1 is the verb *qinnēʾ* (*ʾal-yəqannēʾ libbəḵā baḥaṭṭāʾîm*; *ʾal-təqannēʾ
  bəʾanšê rāʿāh*), the root of [[Envy]]'s *qānāʾ*. 24:19's two lines, *ʾal-titḥar bammərēʿîm ʾal-
  təqannēʾ bārəšāʿîm*, open with the same four words as Psalm 37:1 (*ʾal-titḥar bammərēʿîm ʾal-
  təqannēʾ*), which then ends differently.
- *Drinking … wine* and *gorge … meat* at 23:20 are *sōḇəʾê-yāyin* and *zōləlê ḇāśār*; 23:21 has
  *sōḇēʾ wəzōlēl*, and Deuteronomy 21:20 has *zōlēl wəsōḇēʾ*, the same two participles in the
  opposite order.
- *Wayward wife* at 23:27 is *noḵriyyāh*, *foreign woman*, the word paired with the strange woman at
  2:16; *prostitute* is *zônāh*. *Deep pit* is *šûḥāh ʿămuqqāh*, the phrase of 22:14.
- *Lies in wait* at 23:28 is *teʾĕrōḇ*, the form at 7:12 (WEB *"lurking"*), from the verb of the gang's
  *neʾerḇāh*, *"Let's lay in wait"* (1:11). *Robber* is *ḥeṯep̄*, a rare word whose sense is uncertain;
  the WEB's *robber* is one common rendering, and this pass did not check a lexicon.
- *Needless bruises* at 23:29 are *pəṣāʿîm ḥinnām* — *ḥinnām*, *without cause*, the word of 1:11's
  *"without cause"* for the gang's victims. Here the drinker is the one wounded for nothing.
- *Get* and *buy* at 23:23 are one verb, *qənēh*, the imperative of 4:5, 7.
- Three *ketiv*/*qere* differences in the block (23:24, 26, 29) and one at 23:31 (*bakkîs* written,
  *bakkôs*, *in the cup*, read) do not affect the WEB's sense.

**Corporal discipline.** The wiki records what 23:13–14 says and the reason it gives. It does not
treat the verses as settling modern questions of method; [[The Rod of Discipline - Proverbs 13.24 and the Father's Correction]]
keeps the book's rod sayings together, along with Job 5:17's misapplication of the related teaching.

**An Egyptian comparison, unverified here.** The note on [[Thirty Excellent Things]] about the
*Instruction of Amenemope* applies to 22:17–24:22 as a whole. Commentators usually say the close
parallels thin out after 23:11; this pass consulted no edition of Amenemope and makes no claim either
way about this block.
