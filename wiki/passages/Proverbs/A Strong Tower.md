# A Strong Tower

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 18:1–24

## Summary

Chapter 18 is the chapter of the fortified city. Its centre is a pair of sayings about where a man
runs when he needs to be safe: ***"Yahweh's name is a strong tower: the righteous run to him, and are
safe"*** (18:10), which gives this page its title, and directly after it *"The rich man's wealth is
his strong city, like an unscalable wall in his own imagination"* (11). Later in the chapter the
image comes back twice: an offended brother is *"more difficult than a fortified city"*, and disputes
are *"like the bars of a fortress"* (19). Around that centre, most of the chapter is about speech —
the fool who only wants to air his opinion (2), the gossip's *"dainty morsels"* (8), the man who
answers before he hears (13), the first speaker in a lawsuit who seems right until he is
questioned (17), and *"Death and life are in the power of the tongue"* (21). It ends on companions
and a friend (24). Yahweh is named twice (10, 22). As with chapters 10–17, it is a collection of
related sayings rather than an argument; the groupings below are reading aids.

The chapter repeats itself more than any so far. In the Hebrew (read from Sefaria's *Miqra according
to the Masorah*), 18:8 is identical to 26:22; the first half of 18:11 is the first half of 10:15; the
second half of 18:12 is the second half of 15:33; the second half of 18:22 is the second half of 8:35;
18:4 opens with the words of 20:5; 18:14's *crushed spirit* is the phrase of 15:13 and 17:22; and 18:20
opens like 12:14 and 13:2. The collection is built from a stock of lines that its editors set in
more than one place.

**18:1–8 — The man apart, the fool's mouth, and the gossip.**

- *"An unfriendly man pursues selfishness, and defies all sound judgment"* (1). The *unfriendly man*
  is *nip̄rāḏ*, *one who separates himself*, from the root of 16:28's and 17:9's *separating* of
  friends; *selfishness* is *taʾăwâ*, *desire*. The verb at the end, *yitgallāʿ*, is the *breaking out*
  of 17:14's quarrel (*hitgallaʿ hārîḇ*) and of 20:3's *"every fool will be quarreling"*: the man who
  cuts himself off from others is already on the way to a fight. The chapter opens with a man who
  will not be part of a community and closes with the friend who *"sticks closer than a brother"*.
- ***"A fool has no delight in understanding, but only in revealing his own opinion"*** (2). The
  Hebrew is a play on sound — *yitgallāʿ* in verse 1, *bəhitgallôt libbô*, *in the uncovering of his
  heart*, in verse 2. The fool does not want to learn anything. He wants to be heard.
- *"When wickedness comes, contempt also comes, and with shame comes disgrace"* (3).
- ***"The words of a man's mouth are like deep waters. The fountain of wisdom is like a flowing
  brook"*** (4). *Mayim ʿămuqqîm* opens 20:5 too, *"Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water"*;
  *məqôr ḥokmâ*, *the fountain of wisdom*, varies 10:11's *məqôr ḥayyîm*, *"The mouth of the righteous
  is a spring of life"*. *Nōḇēaʿ*, *flowing*, is the verb of 1:23's *"I will pour out my spirit on
  you"*. The two halves can be read as a contrast (ordinary speech is deep and murky, wisdom runs
  clear) or as synonyms (a wise man's words are deep water and a running stream at once); the
  Hebrew has no conjunction to decide it. See
  [[I Will Pour Out My Spirit - Proverbs 1.23, Joel 2.28, and Acts 2.17]].
- ***"To be partial to the faces of the wicked is not good, nor to deprive the innocent of
  justice"*** (5). *Śəʾēt pənê-rāšāʿ*, *lifting the face of the wicked*, is the idiom of Leviticus
  19:15, *"You shall not be partial to the poor, nor show favoritism to the great"* (*lōʾ-tiśśāʾ
  pənê-dāl*; WEB at bible-api.com, Hebrew from Sefaria), and *ləhaṭṭôt ṣaddîq bammišpāṭ* uses the verb
  of 17:23's bribe taken *"to pervert the ways of justice"*. The court of chapter 17 continues. See
  [[A Bribe Blinds - Proverbs 17.23, Exodus 23.8, and Deuteronomy 16.19]].
- *"A fool's lips come into strife, and his mouth invites beatings"* (6); *"A fool's mouth is his
  destruction, and his lips are a snare to his soul"* (7). Two sayings in which the fool's own
  speech punishes him. *Məḥittâ*, *destruction*, is the word of 10:14–15 (*"the destruction of the
  poor is their poverty"*).
- ***"The words of a gossip are like dainty morsels: they go down into a person's innermost
  parts"*** (8). The *gossip* is the *nirgān* of 16:28, the *whisperer*, and the line returns word for
  word in Hebrew at 26:22, where the WEB translates it *"The words of a whisperer are as dainty
  morsels"*. The *morsels* (*mitlahămîm*) are swallowed gladly; what makes gossip dangerous is that the
  hearer enjoys it.

**18:9–12 — The tower and the wall.**

- *"One who is slack in his work is brother to him who is a master of destruction"* (9). *ʾĀḥ hûʾ*,
  *he is a brother*: the first of three brothers in the chapter (9, 19, 24). The lazy man and the
  destroyer belong to the same family; neglect does slowly what the destroyer does at once. See
  [[Work]].
- ***"Yahweh's name is a strong tower: the righteous run to him, and are safe"*** (10). *Migdal-ʿōz
  šēm YHWH*. *Migdal-ʿōz* is the strong tower of Judges 9:51, into which the people of a besieged city *"fled
  … and shut themselves in"*, and of Psalm 61:3, *"a strong tower from the enemy"* (WEB at
  bible-api.com, Hebrew from Sefaria). The safety is *niśgāḇ*, *set on high*, from the root of Psalm
  18:2's *"my high tower"* (*miśgabbî*) and Psalm 20:1's *"May the name of the God of Jacob set you
  up on high"* (*yəśaggeḇkā šēm*). This is the only place in the WEB of Proverbs where *"Yahweh's
  name"* occurs; Agur's words speak of God's name at 30:4 and 30:9. Babel built a city and *"a
  tower"* to *"make a name for ourselves"* (Genesis 11:4); here the name
  is the tower, and nobody builds it. See
  [[The Name of Yahweh Is a Strong Tower - Proverbs 18.10-11, Psalm 61.3, and Genesis 11.4]],
  [[A Name for Ourselves and a Great Name]], and [[The Tower of Babel]].
- ***"The rich man's wealth is his strong city, like an unscalable wall in his own imagination"***
  (11). The first half is 10:15's first half word for word (*hôn ʿāšîr qiryat ʿuzzô*). There it was a
  plain observation set against the poverty of the poor. Here, directly after 18:10, the second
  half judges it: the wall is *niśgāḇâ*, *set on high* — the same word as the righteous man's
  safety in verse 10 — but only *bəmaśkîtô*, *in his imagination*. Two kinds of high place stand
  side by side, and only one of them exists. Job swore that he had not *"made gold my hope"* or said
  to fine gold, *"You are my confidence"* (Job 31:24).
- ***"Before destruction the heart of man is proud, but before honor is humility"*** (12). The first
  half rewrites 16:18 (*lip̄nê-šeḇer*, *"Pride goes before destruction"*), with the heart's *lifting
  up* (*yiḡbah*) for pride; the second half is 15:33's second half word for word. Pride follows
  straight on from the imaginary wall: the man who thinks his wall is high has a heart that is
  high. See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].

**18:13–17 — Hearing before answering.**

- ***"He who gives answer before he hears, that is folly and shame to him"*** (13). *Mēšîḇ dāḇār
  bəṭerem yišmāʿ*. The book of Job has a speaker who did the opposite: Elihu *"had waited to speak"*
  (Job 32:4), and tells the friends, *"I waited for your words, and I listened for your reasoning,
  while you searched out what to say"* (32:11). See
  [[He Who Answers Before He Hears - Proverbs 18.13, 17 and Elihu's Waiting]].
- ***"A man's spirit will sustain him in sickness, but a crushed spirit, who can bear?"*** (14).
  *Rûaḥ nəḵēʾâ*, *a crushed spirit*, is the phrase of 15:13 and 17:22, its third use in four
  chapters. This time the spirit is what holds up the sick body, and the question is what holds up
  the spirit. The proverb asks and does not answer. Job's chapters are the long form of that
  question. See [[Healing]] and [[Suffering]].
- *"The heart of the discerning gets knowledge. The ear of the wise seeks knowledge"* (15). *Yiqneh*,
  *gets*, is the *qnh* of 4:5, 7's *"Get wisdom"*; heart and ear together are the opposite of the
  fool in verses 2 and 13.
- ***"A man's gift makes room for him, and brings him before great men"*** (16). The *gift* is
  *mattān*, not 17:8's *šōḥad*, *bribe*: the saying is neutral, an observation that a present opens
  doors. Genesis has the gift that goes ahead of its giver — Jacob's *"I will appease him with the
  present that goes before me"* (Genesis 32:20) and Israel's *"carry down a present for the man"*
  (43:11) — though the noun there is *minḥâ* (Hebrew from Sefaria). Proverbs leaves the reader to
  ask, with 17:8 and 17:23 a page behind, when a gift turns into a bribe.
- ***"He who pleads his cause first seems right; until another comes and questions him"*** (17).
  *Ṣaddîq hāriʾšôn bərîḇô*: *righteous is the first in his case*. *Waḥăqārô*, *and searches him*, uses
  the verb Elihu used of the friends *searching out* what to say (Job 32:11, *taḥqərûn*). (The
  written text has *yāḇōʾ*, *comes*; the reading tradition has *ûḇāʾ*, *and comes*; Sefaria prints
  both, and the sense is the same.) Read with verse 13, the rule is to hear both sides. The book of
  Job is a long test of that rule: each friend's speech sounds right until Job answers it, and
  Job's sounds right until God answers him.

**18:18–19 — The lot and the offended brother.**

- *"The lot settles disputes, and keeps strong ones apart"* (18). The civic use of the lot whose
  decision, at 16:33, is *"from Yahweh"*. Where two *strong ones* (*ʿăṣûmîm*) will not give way to
  each other, a decision neither of them made can end the quarrel. See
  [[The Lot Is Cast into the Lap - Proverbs 16.33 and the Casting of Lots]].
- ***"A brother offended is more difficult than a fortified city; and disputes are like the bars of
  a fortress"*** (19). *ʾĀḥ nip̄šāʿ miqqiryat-ʿōz*: the *fortified city* is *qiryat-ʿōz*, the *strong
  city* of the rich man's wealth in verse 11 (*qiryat ʿuzzô*). The brother is *nip̄šāʿ*, from *pšʿ*, the
  root of *pešaʿ*, *transgression*: a brother who has been sinned against. Genesis closes on exactly
  that request — *"Now please forgive the disobedience of your brothers"* (Genesis 50:17), where
  *disobedience* is *pešaʿ* (Hebrew from Sefaria). Joseph wept and was not harder than a city, and
  Esau, who had planned to kill his brother (27:41), *"ran to meet him, embraced him"* (33:4). The
  proverb describes how hard reconciliation is; Genesis twice shows it achieved anyway. The verse is
  generally regarded as difficult in Hebrew, and the reading above follows the WEB. See
  [[Brotherhood]] and [[Strife and Peacemaking]].

**18:20–24 — The fruit of the tongue, the wife, the poor, and the friend.**

- *"A man's stomach is filled with the fruit of his mouth. With the harvest of his lips he is
  satisfied"* (20). *Mippərî p̄î-ʾîš*, *from the fruit of a man's mouth*, opens 12:14 and 13:2 as
  well. The difference is that here a man is filled with what he says whether it is good or bad.
- ***"Death and life are in the power of the tongue; those who love it will eat its fruit"*** (21).
  *Bəyaḏ-lāšôn*, literally *in the hand of the tongue*. The verse carries the eating image on from
  verse 20: the tongue gives its lovers food, and the food can be death. *Those who love it* most
  naturally means those who love to talk. See [[Language and Speech]] and [[Life and Death]].
- ***"Whoever finds a wife finds a good thing, and obtains favor of Yahweh"*** (22). *Wayyāp̄eq rāṣôn
  mēYHWH* is word for word the second half of 8:35, where Wisdom says *"whoever finds me, finds life,
  and will obtain favor from Yahweh"*. The verb *find* (*māṣāʾ*) is used twice in both verses. The
  wife is given the promise that Wisdom gave, and that anticipates the *"worthy woman"* who closes the
  book (31:10). *Finds a good thing* (*māṣāʾ ṭôḇ*) also answers Genesis 2:18, *"It is not good
  (lōʾ-ṭôḇ) for the man to be alone"*. See [[Marriage]], [[Favor and Grace]], and
  [[Wisdom at the Creation]].
- ***"The poor plead for mercy, but the rich answer harshly"*** (23). A plain observation that does
  not pass judgment, and the chapter's last *ʿz* word: *ʿazzôt*, *harshly*, *strong things*, from the
  root of the *strong* tower (10) and the *strong* cities (11, 19). The rich man who trusts his strong
  city speaks strongly. The poor man's *pleas* are *taḥănûnîm*, the noun God uses of Leviathan in Job
  41:3, *"Will he make many petitions to you?"* (Job 40:27 in Hebrew numbering; Hebrew from Sefaria).
  See [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]].
- ***"A man of many companions may be ruined, but there is a friend who sticks closer than a
  brother"*** (24). The first half is uncertain in Hebrew: *ʾîš rēʿîm ləhitrōʿēaʿ* can be read with a
  root meaning *to be broken* (so the WEB's *may be ruined*) or one meaning *to keep company*, giving
  something like *a man of many companions is only good for company*. Either way, many companions are
  set against one friend. That friend is *ʾōhēḇ*, *one who loves*, and he is *dāḇēq*, *clinging*,
  the verb of Genesis 2:24's *"will join with his wife"*. With verse 19, the chapter has an offended
  brother harder than a city and a friend nearer than a brother. See
  [[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]] and [[Love]].

**The promises and their counterexamples.** The chapter's two direct promises are that the
righteous who run to the name *"are safe"* (10) and that the man who finds a wife *"obtains favor of
Yahweh"* (22). Neither is a guarantee of outcome. Job ran to the name — *"Blessed be Yahweh's
name"* (Job 1:21) — and was not kept from harm. The chapter also corrects itself: verse 11 says the
rich man's security exists only in his imagination, verse 23 records his harshness without
approving it, and verse 14 asks a question it does not answer. As on the pages for chapters 10–17,
these are general sayings and not verdicts on any particular person. See [[Retribution]] and
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned Proverbs 18:1–24 unit is retained. Chapter-sized units are the book page's
stated convention for the sentence collection, not a claim that the chapter is one original
discourse. Only this queue item is advanced.

**Source text.** All English quotations of Proverbs, Genesis, and Job are copied from `raw/bible/`
(WEB). Raw text is unchanged. Leviticus 19:15; Judges 9:51; Psalm 18:2; 20:1; 61:3 were checked
against the WEB at bible-api.com in this pass; those books are not stored and were not fetched.

**Hebrew.** The Hebrew of Proverbs 18:1–24; 8:35; 10:11, 14–15; 12:14; 13:2; 15:13, 33; 16:18, 28; 17:14,
22; 20:3, 5; 26:22; Genesis 2:18, 24; 11:4; 27:41; 32:20 (32:21 Hebrew); 50:17; Leviticus 19:15; Judges
9:51; Psalm 18:2 (18:3 Hebrew); 20:1 (20:2 Hebrew); 61:3 (61:4 Hebrew); and Job 32:11; 41:3 (40:27
Hebrew) was read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25. The verbal repetitions
listed in the Summary rest on that comparison. Root identifications that are lexical judgements
rather than visible identity of letters — *ʿōz* / *ʿuzzô* / *ʿazzôt* as one root *ʿzz*; *niśgāḇ* /
*miśgabbî* / *yəśaggeḇkā* as *śgb*; *nip̄šāʿ* with *pešaʿ*; *ḥqr* in 18:17 and Job 32:11; *yitgallāʿ* in
18:1 and 17:14 — are standard but were not checked against a lexicon in this pass. 18:19's textual
difficulty and the two readings of 18:24a are reported as commonly proposed, not checked against
commentaries or the ancient versions. The claim that 18:10 is the only occurrence of *"Yahweh's name"* in
Proverbs rests on a search of the stored WEB text; 30:4 and 30:9 speak of God's name in other words.

**Comparisons.** Supporting verses were read in stored WEB: Proverbs 1:23; 4:5, 7; 8:35; 10:11,
14–15; 12:14; 13:2; 15:13, 33; 16:18, 28, 33; 17:8, 9, 14, 22, 23; 20:3, 5; 26:22; 30:4, 9; 31:10;
Genesis 2:18, 24; 11:4; 27:41; 32:20; 33:4; 43:11; 50:17; Job 1:21; 31:24; 32:4, 11; 41:3. No
unreached book was fetched.
