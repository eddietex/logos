# Do Not Withhold Good

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 3:21–35

## Summary

The renewed address to the son turns the praise of wisdom into daily conduct. Retained
wisdom and discretion give life, secure steps, and untroubled sleep (3:21–24). The reason
for confidence in sudden danger is Yahweh's protection (3:25–26). Then the teaching turns
toward the neighbor: give the good that is due when able, do not postpone available help,
do not betray another's trust, and do not manufacture a quarrel (3:27–30).

The son must also refuse to envy or imitate violence (3:31). The closing contrasts explain
why: Yahweh rejects perversity and befriends the upright, curses the wicked household and
blesses the righteous, mocks mockers and gives grace to the humble; wisdom ends in glory,
folly in shame (3:32–35). The apparent advantage of violence is weighed against God's judgment.

**3:21–26 — Wisdom retained, confidence located in Yahweh.** The eyes, neck, foot, and
sleep place wisdom in embodied life. The promise does not make discretion an independent
security system: verse 26 names Yahweh as the ground of confidence. The instruction not
to fear sudden disaster assumes that danger can come into view; it does not tell the son
that he will never see it. Compare [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]].

**3:27–30 — The neighbor's claim comes before convenience.** Verse 27 speaks of good
owed to another, within one's capacity to give. Verse 28 makes delay culpable when the
resource is already available. This can include material help, but the text does not
restrict the good to alms or identify the recipient only as a poor person. Verse 29's
neighbor lives securely beside the hearer: the security promised in verse 23 must not
become an opportunity to prey on another's security. Verse 30 prohibits groundless
strife; it does not prohibit every justified complaint.

**3:31–35 — What deserves admiration.** The man of violence may look worth imitating,
but Yahweh's evaluations reverse that attraction. Friendship, blessing, grace, and glory
are set against abhorrence, curse, mockery, and shame. Verse 34 later becomes an explicit
scriptural citation in James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5; see
[[Grace to the Humble - Proverbs 3.34, James 4.6, and 1 Peter 5.5]] . The interpretation here
follows the WEB wording and makes no claim about exact Hebrew or Greek forms.

**Tension with Job.** Proverbs 3:24 promises sweet sleep and 3:33 locates blessing in
the righteous home. Job 1:1, 13–19 portrays an upright man whose household is devastated;
Job 7:3–4, 13–14 describes nights of terror. Job 21:7–9, conversely, describes wicked
people whose homes are safe. These claims conflict if Proverbs is made an exceptionless
account of present circumstances. Reading Proverbs as formative wisdom about the way
to live, rather than evidence from which to infer a sufferer's guilt, is the wiki's
interpretation. Both the strong promises and Job's counterexamples remain on record.

## Key verses

> **3:21** My son, let them not depart from your eyes. Keep sound wisdom and discretion:

> **3:22** so they will be life to your soul, and grace for your neck.

> **3:23** Then you shall walk in your way securely. Your foot won’t stumble.

> **3:24** When you lie down, you will not be afraid. Yes, you will lie down, and your sleep will be sweet.

> **3:25** Don’t be afraid of sudden fear, neither of the desolation of the wicked, when it comes:

> **3:26** for Yahweh will be your confidence, and will keep your foot from being taken.

> **3:27** Don’t withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in the power of your hand to do it.

> **3:28** Don’t say to your neighbor, “Go, and come again; tomorrow I will give it to you,” when you have it by you.

> **3:29** Don’t devise evil against your neighbor, since he dwells securely by you.

> **3:30** Don’t strive with a man without cause, if he has done you no harm.

> **3:31** Don’t envy the man of violence. Choose none of his ways.

> **3:32** For the perverse is an abomination to Yahweh, but his friendship is with the upright.

> **3:33** Yahweh’s curse is in the house of the wicked, but he blesses the habitation of the righteous.

> **3:34** Surely he mocks the mockers, but he gives grace to the humble.

> **3:35** The wise will inherit glory, but shame will be the promotion of fools.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh protects, befriends the upright, blesses, judges, and gives grace.
- The father, son, neighbor, violent man, wise, foolish, mockers, and humble are instructional
  figures or groups, not newly identified historical individuals. Solomon's book-level
  attribution does not establish the identity of this particular father or son.

## Places

- No named geographical place. The road, house, and habitation are everyday settings;
  no new place page is warranted.

## Themes

- [[Wisdom]] — Discretion protects life and takes concrete form in dealings with a neighbor.
- [[Faith]] — Yahweh grounds confidence when sudden fear threatens.
- [[Life and Death]] — Retained wisdom gives life, secure steps, and sweet sleep.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Available resources must not be withheld or needlessly delayed.
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — Timely help is required when the good owed is within reach.
- [[Strife and Peacemaking]] — Protect the neighbor’s trust; refuse baseless disputes and violent
  example.
- [[Envy]] — The violent man must be neither envied nor imitated.
- [[Righteousness]] — Friendship and blessing accompany uprightness; Job limits inference from
  prosperity.
- [[Blessing]] — Yahweh blesses the righteous habitation; tension with Job remains explicit.
- [[Curse]] — The wicked house is under Yahweh’s curse, without making disaster proof of guilt.
- [[Favor and Grace]] — Grace is given to the humble, a saying cited in James and 1 Peter.
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — Mockery meets divine opposition; the humble receive grace.
- [[Folly]] — Fools end in shame; the wise inherit glory.
- [[Retribution]] — Security and household blessing are promised; Job supplies explicit
  counterexamples.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Words can postpone available help; mockery receives judgment.
- [[Judgment]] — Yahweh’s verdict counters the apparent attractiveness of violence.
- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — A broad duty of timely help includes, but is not restricted to,
  the vulnerable.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The existing 3:21–35 unit is retained. The address to the son opens it,
security leads into social duties, and the final contrasts explain the warning against
violence. The next address to sons at 4:1 remains for the next invocation.
