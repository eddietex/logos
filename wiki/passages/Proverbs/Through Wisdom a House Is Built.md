# Through Wisdom a House Is Built

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 24:3–22

## Summary

The last block of the first *"words of the wise"* (22:17–24:22) has no opening call to attention of
its own; it simply begins building. Twenty verses later it ends where the whole collection ends, on
a command to fear Yahweh and the king and a question nobody answers: *"who knows?"* (24:22). Between
the two it moves through a handful of short units — a house, a war, a fool silent in the gate, a
rescue refused, honey, a righteous man who falls and gets up, an enemy who falls and must not be
gloated over, and the wicked whose lamp goes out. The address *"My son"* returns twice (13, 21). What
holds the block together is less a topic than a single movement: from what wisdom builds (3–6) to
what the wise must do when someone else's house is under attack (10–18), and finally to whom the
son answers for it (12, 18, 21–22).

**24:3–4 — The house built.** *"Through wisdom a house is built; by understanding it is established;
by knowledge the rooms are filled with all rare and beautiful treasure."* Three nouns — wisdom,
understanding, knowledge — and three stages of building: raised, made firm, filled. The same three
nouns, in the same order, are how Yahweh made the world in 3:19–20 (*"By wisdom Yahweh founded the
earth. By understanding, he established the heavens. By his knowledge, the depths were broken up"*),
and how the Spirit of God equipped Bezalel to make the tabernacle (Exodus 31:3). A household built
by wisdom is therefore built the way the cosmos and the sanctuary were (see the Notes for the
Hebrew, and
[[Wisdom, Understanding, and Knowledge - The Builder's Gifts in Proverbs, Exodus, and Kings]]).
The house here is probably a household as much as a building — the rooms filled with treasure are
the goods of a family that prospers — and the saying stands beside 9:1, where Wisdom *"has built her
house"*, and 14:1, where *"every wise woman builds her house, but the foolish one tears it down with
her own hands."* Proverbs states the ordinary outcome, not a guarantee; Job's household, ended in
a day when a wind *"struck the four corners of the house"* where his children were feasting (Job
1:18–19), is the case the wiki keeps beside it.

**24:5–6 — Strength and counsel.** *"A wise man has great power; and a knowledgeable man increases
strength; for by wise guidance you wage your war; and victory is in many advisors."* The second line
of verse 6 is word for word the second line of 11:14 in Hebrew, and its first line reworks 20:18
(*"by wise guidance you wage war!"*). Here the argument is about strength: wisdom is stronger than
might, because even a war is won by planning rather than muscle. See
[[A Multitude of Counselors - Proverbs 11.14 and Exodus 18]].

**24:7–9 — The fool, the schemer, the mocker.** *"Wisdom is too high for a fool: he doesn't open his
mouth in the gate."* The gate is where the town's elders sat to judge and deliberate; the fool has
nothing to say there, because what is said there is out of his reach. Then two sayings name the
other kind of mind: one who *"plots to do evil will be called a schemer"* (8), and *"The schemes of
folly are sin. The mocker is detested by men"* (9). Proverbs usually calls things *"an abomination to
Yahweh"*; here the mocker is *"detested by men"* — the same noun, *tôʿēḇāh*, applied to human
disgust (see the Notes). The mocker loses his neighbours before he loses anything else.

**24:10–12 — The rescue.** The block's moral centre, and its sharpest demand. *"If you falter in the
time of trouble, your strength is small"* (10) — in Hebrew a pun, *the day of distress* (*ṣārāh*)
showing whether your strength is *narrow* (*ṣar*). Then the trouble is named, and it is someone
else's: *"Rescue those who are being led away to death! Indeed, hold back those who are staggering
to the slaughter!"* (11). The text does not say who they are or why they are being led away —
condemned unjustly, perhaps, or simply in danger — and the wiki does not supply it. What it does
say is that the bystander's excuse is foreseen and refused: *"If you say, 'Behold, we didn't know
this'; doesn't he who weighs the hearts consider it? He who keeps your soul, doesn't he know it?
Shall he not render to every man according to his work?"* (12). Three questions, each naming God by
what he does: he *weighs hearts* (the title of 21:2, and 16:2's *"weighs the motives"*), he *keeps*
the soul, and he *renders* to each according to his work. The claim not to have known is answered by
the one who does know — including whether the claim is true. The last question is the formula of
Psalm 62:12, which Romans 2:6 quotes and Elihu restates at Job 34:11. See
[[According to His Work - Proverbs 24.12, Psalm 62.12, and Romans 2.6]].

**24:13–14 — Honey.** *"My son, eat honey, for it is good; the droppings of the honeycomb, which are
sweet to your taste: so you shall know wisdom to be to your soul; if you have found it, then there
will be a reward, your hope will not be cut off."* After the hardest command in the block, the
gentlest. Honey is commended for its own sake — *"it is good"* — and then made a comparison: wisdom
is to the soul what honey is to the palate. The second half repeats 23:18 almost exactly; the
WEB's *"reward"* is the same Hebrew noun, *ʾaḥărît*, that 23:18 renders *"future"* — the latter end.
See [[In the Days to Come - Aharit Hayyamim from Genesis 49 to the Prophets]] and
[[Hope Deferred and the Tree of Life - Proverbs 13.12 and the Hope of Job]].

**24:15–16 — Seven times.** *"Don't lay in wait, wicked man, against the habitation of the
righteous. Don't destroy his resting place: for a righteous man falls seven times, and rises up
again; but the wicked are overthrown by calamity."* The only saying in Proverbs addressed to *the
wicked man* directly, and it warns him off the house of 24:3: the righteous man's *habitation* and
*resting place*. *"Lay in wait"* is the gang's verb from the first lecture (1:11) and the strange
woman's at 7:12 and 23:28. The reason is not that the righteous never fall — they fall *"seven
times"*, a number of completeness — but that they get up; the wicked fall once. Proverbs here does
not promise the righteous immunity, and that is the point at which it is nearest to Job.

**24:17–18 — The enemy's fall.** *"Don't rejoice when your enemy falls. Don't let your heart be glad
when he is overthrown; lest Yahweh see it, and it displease him, and he turn away his wrath from
him."* The two verbs of verse 16 — *falls* and *overthrown* (*stumble*) — return, applied now to the
son's own enemy (see the Notes). The prohibition reaches the heart, not just the mouth. Its reason
is startling, and it is the text's, not the wiki's: gloating may lead Yahweh to *turn his wrath
away* from the enemy. The verse does not soften this into concern for the enemy's welfare; it
treats the enemy's fall as Yahweh's business and the onlooker's gladness as an offence Yahweh sees.
17:5 had already said *"He who is glad at calamity shall not be unpunished,"* and Job swears he has
not done it: *"If I have rejoiced at the destruction of him who hated me…"* (Job 31:29). The same
book that lets Wisdom laugh at the calamity of those who refused her (1:26) forbids the son to laugh
at his enemy's. See [[Help for an Enemy - Exodus 23, Proverbs 25, and Matthew 5]].

**24:19–20 — The lamp snuffed out.** *"Don't fret yourself because of evildoers; neither be envious
of the wicked: for there will be no reward to the evil man; and the lamp of the wicked shall be
snuffed out."* The fourth prohibition of envying the wicked in Proverbs (3:31; 23:17; 24:1), and the
one whose first line is the opening of Psalm 37:1. The reason is again *ʾaḥărît* — *"no reward"* is
*no latter end* — and the image is the household lamp of 13:9 put out. Coming straight after 24:17–18
it reads as the other half of a balance: the son must neither gloat over the wicked when they fall
nor envy them while they stand. See
[[Do Not Fret Because of Evildoers - Proverbs 23.17, 24.19, and Psalm 37.1]] and
[[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]].

**24:21–22 — Yahweh and the king.** *"My son, fear Yahweh and the king. Don't join those who are
rebellious: for their calamity will rise suddenly; the destruction from them both—who knows?"* The
collection closes on the book's motto (1:7) with a second object added: the king. Yahweh and the
king are not made equal, but they are named together as the two whose wrath falls on the
*rebellious* — in Hebrew *šônîm*, *those who change* (see the Notes). *"The destruction from them
both"* means the ruin Yahweh and the king bring; *"who knows?"* leaves its timing and its form
unstated. The pairing goes back to the law, *"You shall not blaspheme God, nor curse a ruler of your
people"* (Exodus 22:28), and forward to *"Fear God. Honor the king"* (1 Peter 2:17). See
[[Fear Yahweh and the King - Proverbs 24.21, Exodus 22.28, and 1 Peter 2.17]].

**What the block adds.** Read whole, 24:3–22 sets the household that wisdom builds (3–4) against the
households that violence threatens (11, 15), and asks the wise man what his strength is for. The
answer is not war only (6) but rescue (11), and the measure of it is the *day of distress*, which
is always someone's. The block then fences that strength on both sides: it may not be withheld with
a plea of ignorance (12), and it may not turn into gladness at an enemy's ruin (17). Over all of it
stands the one *"who weighs the hearts"* — the block's real subject, named by function in verse 12,
by name in 18 and 21. Verse 23 opens a new heading, *"These also are sayings of the wise"*, and the
next queue item.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh, named twice (18, 21) and described three times by function at 24:12: the weigher
  of hearts, the keeper of the soul, the one who renders to each according to his work. He sees the
  gloater's heart (18) and is feared together with the king (21).
- The king (21) is unnamed and generic; no person page. [[Solomon]] is not named here, and this
  collection is attributed to *"the wise"* (22:17), not to him.
- The wise man, the fool, the schemer, the mocker, the condemned being led away, the wicked man, the
  righteous man, the enemy, and the rebellious are generic figures; none receives an invented
  identity.

## Places

- *The gate* (7) is the generic town gate where elders sat in judgment; it is not a named place.

## Notes

**Boundary and title.** The planned 24:3–22 unit is retained. 24:3 begins the house saying after
24:1–2 closed the envy frame; 24:22 ends the first *"words of the wise"* with a *petuchah* paragraph
break in the Masoretic text, and 24:23 opens with its own heading, *"These also are sayings of the
wise."* The queue's provisional title *By Wisdom a House Is Built* was changed to *Through Wisdom a
House Is Built* to match the WEB wording of 24:3. Exactly one queue item is advanced.

**Language.** Read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* on 2026-10-26:
- 24:3–4 has *bəḥoḵmāh yibbāneh bāyiṯ ûḇiṯḇûnāh yiṯkônān ûḇəḏaʿaṯ* — *by wisdom … and by
  understanding … and by knowledge*. 3:19–20 has *bəḥoḵmāh … biṯḇûnāh … bəḏaʿtô*, with the same verb
  *kûn* (*kônēn*, *established*; here *yiṯkônān*). Exodus 31:3 has *bəḥoḵmāh ûḇiṯḇûnāh ûḇəḏaʿaṯ*,
  identical to 24:3–4's three prepositional nouns. 1 Kings 7:14 fills Hiram of Tyre *ʾeṯ-haḥoḵmāh
  wəʾeṯ-hattəḇûnāh wəʾeṯ-haddaʿaṯ*, which the WEB renders *"wisdom and understanding and skill"*.
- 24:6b, *ûṯəšûʿāh bərōḇ yôʿēṣ*, is identical to 11:14b. 24:6a, *bəṯaḥbulôṯ taʿăśeh-ləḵā
  milḥāmāh*, varies 20:18b, *ûḇəṯaḥbulôṯ ʿăśēh milḥāmāh*.
- *Too high* at 24:7 is written *rāʾmôṯ*, with an unusual *aleph*; *wisdom* there is the plural
  *ḥoḵmôṯ*, the form used of Wisdom at 9:1.
- *Detested* at 24:9 is *tôʿăḇaṯ ləʾāḏām*, *an abomination to man*, the noun Proverbs usually
  pairs with Yahweh.
- *Trouble* and *small* at 24:10 are *ṣārāh* and *ṣar*, from the same consonants. That they are a
  deliberate pun is the usual reading; this pass did not check a lexicon on whether they share a root.
- *Hold back* at 24:11 renders *ʾim-taḥśôḵ*, literally *if you hold back*. The clause can be read as
  a command (the WEB's *"Indeed, hold back"*) or as a reproach (*if you hold back* from rescuing);
  the wiki notes both and does not decide.
- *He who weighs the hearts* at 24:12 is *tōḵēn libbôṯ*, identical to 21:2's *tōḵēn libbôṯ*; 16:2
  has *tōḵēn rûḥôṯ*. *He who keeps your soul* is *nōṣēr napšəḵā*. *Render to every man according to
  his work* is *wəhēšîḇ ləʾāḏām kəp̄ŏʿŏlô*; 24:29, in the next block, forbids the son to say *ʾāšîḇ
  lāʾîš kəp̄ŏʿŏlô*, *"I will render to the man according to his work"* — the same phrase, God's in
  24:12 and forbidden to a man in 24:29. And 24:18's *turn away his wrath* is *wəhēšîḇ … ʾappô*, the
  same verb *hēšîḇ*.
- 24:14 has *wəyēš ʾaḥărîṯ wəṯiqwāṯəḵā lōʾ ṯikkārēṯ*; 24:20 has *lōʾ-ṯihyeh ʾaḥărîṯ lārāʿ*. The
  earlier page records 23:18's near-identical clause.
- *Lay in wait* at 24:15 is *ʾal-teʾĕrōḇ*, the verb of 1:11's *neʾerḇāh* and 23:28's *teʾĕrōḇ*.
  *Habitation* is *nəwēh*, a word used of pasture and dwelling; *resting place* is *riḇṣô*, from a
  verb used of animals lying down. That last sense is standard lexical knowledge, not checked against
  a lexicon in this pass.
- 24:16 has *yippôl … wāqām* (*falls … and rises*) and *yikkāšəlû* (*stumble*, WEB *"are
  overthrown"*); 24:17 has *binpōl* and *ûḇikkāšəlô* — the same two verbs, *nāp̄al* and *kāšal*. 24:17
  is written *ʾwyḇyḵ* (*your enemies*) and read *ʾôyiḇḵā* (*your enemy*), a *ketiv*/*qere* difference
  that does not change the sense.
- *Calamity* at 24:22 is *ʾêḏām*, the *ʾêḏ* of 1:26 (*bəʾêḏəḵem*, Wisdom laughing *"at your disaster"*)
  and of 17:5 (*śāmēaḥ ləʾêḏ*, *glad at calamity*). *Destruction* at 24:22 is *pîḏ*, the noun of Job
  31:29 (*ʾeśmaḥ bəp̄îḏ məśanʾî*, *"rejoiced at the destruction of him who hated me"*).
- *Rebellious* at 24:21 is *šônîm*, a participle usually taken from *šānāh*, *to change*: those who
  change sides, or seek change. The WEB's *"rebellious"* is one common rendering; this pass did not
  check a lexicon on the derivation.
- Exodus 22:28 is 22:27 in the Hebrew numbering: *ʾĕlōhîm lōʾ ṯəqallēl wənāśîʾ ḇəʿamməḵā lōʾ ṯāʾōr*.
  Psalm 62:12 is 62:13 in the Hebrew numbering: *kî-ʾattāh ṯəšallēm ləʾîš kəmaʿăśēhû* — a different
  verb (*šillēm*) and noun (*maʿăśeh*) from 24:12's. Job 34:11 has *pōʿal ʾāḏām yəšallem-lô*, sharing
  24:12's noun root *pʿl* and Psalm 62's verb.

**Gloating and Yahweh's wrath.** 24:18 gives a reason the reader may find uncomfortable: not
*because your enemy is a person*, but *lest Yahweh turn his wrath away from him*. The wiki records
the verse's reason as given. It does not claim the verse wants the enemy to suffer; it says the
enemy's fall belongs to Yahweh, and that joy at it is seen. Romans 12:19–21 and Proverbs 25:21–22 (now
in [[The Glory of Kings]]),
both on [[Help for an Enemy - Exodus 23, Proverbs 25, and Matthew 5]], are the fuller treatments.

**An Egyptian comparison, unverified here.** As on [[Thirty Excellent Things]] and
[[Do Not Envy Sinners]], this pass consulted no edition of the *Instruction of Amenemope* and makes
no claim about parallels in 24:3–22.
