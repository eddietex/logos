# Keep Your Heart

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 4:20–27

## Summary

A fresh *"My son"* opens the last lecture of chapter 4. The father asks for attention and a turned
ear (4:20), and asks that his words stay before the son's eyes and *"in the center of your heart"*
(4:21), because they are *"life to those who find them, and health to their whole body"* (4:22).
Then the lecture's centre: *"Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring
of life"* (4:23). What follows is the heart's outworking, organ by organ — a mouth and lips put
away from perversity (4:24), eyes fixed straight ahead (4:25), feet on a level path that turns
neither right nor left and is removed from evil (4:26–27).

**4:20–22 — The words kept inside.** The opening repeats the lectures before it: listen, turn
your ear, do not let the words depart from your eyes. The last phrase is word for word the opening
of 3:21, *"let them not depart from your eyes"*, and *"Turn your ear"* opens the next lecture too
(5:1). What is new is where the words are to be kept — *"in the center of your heart"* — and what
they do there. They are *"life"* and *"health to their whole body"*. In 3:8 the fear of Yahweh was
*"health to your body"*; here the father's words are. The Hebrew of 4:22 has *marpēʾ*, a noun
conventionally derived from the same root *rāp̄āʾ*, *to heal*, as 3:8's *rip̄ʾûṯ* (Hebrew read from
Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*; the root relation is the standard lexical one, not
checked against a lexicon this pass). See [[Healing]].

**4:23 — The heart as a spring.** The verse that names the pericope ranks the heart above
everything else the son guards. The WEB's *"with all diligence"* renders Hebrew
*mikkol-mišmār* — literally *from every keeping*, which can be read *above all that you guard* or
*with all vigilance*; both readings are possible and this page does not choose. *"Wellspring"*
renders *tôṣəʾôṯ*, a plural noun formed from the verb *to go out*: life's outgoings, or its
sources. The image is the one this lecture then works out: the heart is not a container for life
but the place it flows from. Speech, sight, and walking are where it comes out. In Proverbs the
heart is the seat of thought, intention, and understanding as much as feeling — 3:1 and 3:3
asked the son to let his heart keep the commandments and to write kindness and truth on *"the
tablet of your heart"*. See
[[The Tablet of the Heart - Proverbs 3.3, Jeremiah 31.33, and 2 Corinthians 3.3]].

**4:24–27 — Mouth, eyes, feet.** The commands run down the body. *"Put away from yourself a
perverse mouth"* (4:24) comes first, because perverse speech was the first danger of 2:12 — *"the
men who speak perverse things"* — and the strange woman's danger is her mouth too (2:16; 5:3).
The eyes look *"straight ahead"* (4:25): not inward wisdom alone, but the outward discipline of
not looking aside. The feet walk a path made *"level"*, every way *"established"* (4:26), and
turn *"to the right hand nor to the left"* (4:27). The lecture ends with *"Remove your foot from
evil"* — the same turning from evil 3:7 commanded (*"Fear Yahweh, and depart from evil"*), and
the reverse of the gang whose *"feet run to evil"* (1:16). See
[[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] and [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]].

**Neither right nor left — Deuteronomy and Joshua.** The phrase in 4:27 has a history. Moses
tells Israel, *"You shall not turn away to the right hand or to the left. You shall walk in all
the way which Yahweh your God has commanded you, that you may live"* (Deuteronomy 5:32–33), and
Yahweh tells Joshua not to turn from the law *"to the right hand or to the left"* (Joshua 1:7;
both WEB, verified at bible-api.com, not yet ingested). In Deuteronomy the straight road is the
commanded way of the law; in Proverbs 4 it is the father's teaching. The overlap of language is
plain, and with it the reward — *that you may live*. Proverbs does not cite Deuteronomy here, and
the wiki does not claim a direct quotation; it records the shared idiom. See
[[The Two Ways - Proverbs 4, Psalm 1, and Matthew 7]] and [[Walking with God]].

**Job's oath walks the same body.** Job 31 is Job's sworn self-examination, and it inspects the
same parts in the same order of thought. *"I made a covenant with my eyes"* (31:1); *"if my step
has turned out of the way, if my heart walked after my eyes"* (31:7); *"if my heart has been
enticed to a woman"* (31:9). And at 23:11–12 he had already claimed what Proverbs 4:26–27
commands: *"My foot has held fast to his steps. I have kept his way, and not turned away"*.
Proverbs 4 gives the instruction; Job 31 is a man swearing he kept it. **The difference is what
it earned him.** Proverbs promises *"life"* and *"health to their whole body"* (4:22); Job kept
heart, eyes, and feet and was struck *"with painful sores from the sole of his foot to his head"*
(Job 2:7). The wiki keeps both: the lecture says where the kept heart leads; Job is the case in
which the body's health did not follow. See [[Heart, Eyes, and Feet - Proverbs 4 and Job 31]]
and [[Job's Oath of Innocence]].

**Out of the heart — the Gospels.** Jesus' teaching that defilement comes from within takes the
same direction as 4:23: *"the things which proceed out of the mouth come out of the heart"*
(Matthew 15:18), and *"out of the abundance of the heart, his mouth speaks"* (Luke 6:45). Proverbs
moves from heart to mouth in consecutive verses (4:23–24); the Gospels say the same thing in one
sentence, and apply it to a debate about ritual washing that Proverbs does not have. The wiki
records this as a thematic echo, not a quotation. See
[[Out of the Heart - Proverbs 4.23, Matthew 15, and Luke 6]].

## Key verses

> **4:20** My son, attend to my words. Turn your ear to my sayings.

> **4:21** Let them not depart from your eyes. Keep them in the center of your heart.

> **4:22** For they are life to those who find them, and health to their whole body.

> **4:23** Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring of life.

> **4:24** Put away from yourself a perverse mouth. Put corrupt lips far from you.

> **4:25** Let your eyes look straight ahead. Fix your gaze directly before you.

> **4:26** Make the path of your feet level. Let all of your ways be established.

> **4:27** Don’t turn to the right hand nor to the left. Remove your foot from evil.

No named geographical place. The path is a metaphor for a manner of life.

- [[Language and Speech]] — the perverse mouth and corrupt lips put away (4:24).
- [[Seeing and Being Seen]] — eyes fixed straight ahead (4:25); the words kept before the eyes.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 4:20–27 unit is kept. *"My son"* opens it at 4:20, and the Masoretic
text closes it with an open-paragraph marker (*petuḥah*) after 4:27 in Sefaria's *Miqra according
to the Masorah*; 5:1 opens the next lecture with a new *"My son"*.

the WEB at bible-api.com on 2026-10-25. The Hebrew words cited for 4:22–23 (*marpēʾ*,

the glosses offered for them are conventional and are qualified in the text where more than one
reading exists.
