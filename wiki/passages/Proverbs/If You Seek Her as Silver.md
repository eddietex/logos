# If You Seek Her as Silver

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 2:1–22

## Summary

The father speaks again, and this time his lecture is **one long conditional sentence**. Wisdom
has just finished her speech in the street ([[Wisdom Calls in the Street]]), threatening the people
who would not listen; now the father explains what listening looks like and what it gets. There is
no story in the chapter, no gang and no quoted invitation as in
[[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] — only *if*, *then*, *to deliver you*, and *so that*, built into a
single argument from the son's ear to the land he will live in.

- **2:1–4 — *If.*** Three times: receive my words, call out for discernment, seek her as silver.
- **2:5–8 — *Then* (1).** You will understand the fear of Yahweh — *for Yahweh gives wisdom*.
- **2:9–11 — *Then* (2).** You will understand righteousness, and wisdom will guard you.
- **2:12–15 — *To deliver you* (1).** From the men who speak perverse things.
- **2:16–19 — *To deliver you* (2).** From the strange woman, whose house sinks down to death.
- **2:20–22 — *So that.*** You walk in the way of the good; the upright dwell in the land, the
  wicked are torn out of it.

**The chapter is built on two letters.** It has twenty-two verses — the number of letters in the
Hebrew alphabet — and its first half is led by the first letter, *aleph*, its second by the
twelfth, *lamed*, which begins the alphabet's second half. *ʾim*, *if* (2:1, 3, 4), and *ʾāz*,
*then* (2:5, 9), open the first half; *ləhaṣṣîlḵā*, *to deliver you* (2:12, 16), and *ləmaʿan*,
*so that* (2:20), open the second. The letters are a fact of the text; that they are deliberate —
an "alphabetizing" poem that is not a full acrostic — is a common description by commentators,
reported here without being checked against a specific one. The structure matches the logic either
way: the *aleph* half is about getting wisdom, the *lamed* half about what it rescues you from.

**The three *ifs* climb (2:1–4).** *"My son, if you will receive my words, and store up my
commandments within you"* — receiving, the passive posture of a pupil. Then *"Yes, if you call out
for discernment, and lift up your voice for understanding"* — the son's own voice, calling for
understanding as Wisdom called in the street (1:20–21). Then *"If you seek her as silver, and search
for her as for hidden treasures"* — the miner's labour. **The son is asked to do for Wisdom what she
did for him**: she called out (*tiqrāʾ*, 1:21, *qārāʾṯî*, 1:24), and now he is to call out
(*tiqrāʾ*, 2:3) for her.

**Store it up: the gang's verb, turned round (2:1, 7).** *"Store up my commandments"* is *tiṣpōn*,
from *ṣāp̄an*, *to hide, treasure up*. It is the verb the gang used in the first lecture — *niṣpənâ*,
*"Let's lurk secretly for the innocent"* (1:11) — and the verb the father turned against them,
*yiṣpənû*, *"They lurk secretly for their own lives"* (1:18). The gang hid themselves in ambush; the
son is to hide commandments inside himself. And the same verb returns five verses later with God as
its subject: *"He lays up sound wisdom for the upright"* (2:7, *yiṣpōn*, the reading tradition's
correction of a written form with a different prefix). **The son stores up the father's words; God
stores up wisdom for the son.** The treasure the son is told to dig for (2:4) has been laid up for
him by the one who gives it (2:6).

**Seeking and finding, after 1:28 (2:4–5).** *"If you seek her as silver, and search for her as for
hidden treasures: then you will understand the fear of Yahweh, and find the knowledge of God."*
*Find* is *timṣāʾ*. In the speech just ended, Wisdom said of the refusers, *"They will seek me
diligently, but they will not find me"* (1:28, *wəlōʾ yimṣāʾunənî*). **Here seeking finds.** The
difference the two passages give is the seeker's posture: in 1:28, people looking for a way out of
calamity after refusing the call; in 2:1–4, a son who has received the words first and searches
as for treasure. The book has now said both — seek and not find (1:28), seek and find (2:4–5;
compare 8:17).

**The miner's picture, against Job 28.** *Hidden treasures* is *maṭmônîm*. Job used the word of
the bitter who *"long for death … and dig for it more than for hidden treasures"* (Job 3:21,
*mimmaṭmônîm*), and Job 28 describes the whole labour of mining silver (*"Surely there is a mine for
silver"*, 28:1) only to conclude that wisdom is not where miners dig: *"it is hidden from the eyes
of all living"* (28:21). Proverbs 2:4 takes the same search as the model for seeking wisdom and
says it succeeds. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The two texts meet at the end — Job 28:28
gives wisdom as *"the fear of the Lord"*, and Proverbs 2:5 says the seeker *"will understand the fear
of Yahweh"* — and the reason Proverbs gives for success is the same reason Job 28 gives for the
gift: *"For Yahweh gives wisdom"* (2:6), as in Job 28:23–28 God *"understands its way"* and tells it to
man. The wiki's reading is that Proverbs' search succeeds because it is a search for something God
gives, not something the earth contains; Job 28's fails because it looks in the earth. But Proverbs
uses the miner's image without Job's caveat, and both statements stand. See
[[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]] and [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]].

**Effort and gift in the same sentence (2:1–6).** The son must receive, store, incline, call, lift
up his voice, seek, and search — seven verbs of effort — and the result is explained by *kî*,
*for*: *"For Yahweh gives wisdom. Out of his mouth comes knowledge and understanding"* (2:6). The
chapter does not choose between wisdom as achievement and wisdom as gift. It makes the search the
way the gift is received. Out of his *mouth* (*mippîw*): the wisdom God gives arrives as words,
which is how the father's arrives (2:1, *ʾămāray*, *my words*). James 1:5 states the gift side on
its own: *"if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally"*. Solomon's
request at Gibeon is the narrative form — *"Give your servant therefore an understanding heart"*,
and the answer, *"I have given you a wise and understanding heart"* (1 Kings 3:9, 12). See
[[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]].

**The knowledge of God (2:5).** *Daʿat ʾĕlōhîm* is a prophetic phrase as well as a wisdom one:
Hosea's charge is that there is *"no truth, nor goodness, nor knowledge of God in the land"* (Hosea
4:1), and *"I desire mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings"*
(6:6). In Proverbs 2:5 it stands in parallel with *"the fear of Yahweh"*: knowing God and fearing
him are one thing seen from two sides, and the motto of 1:7 — *"The fear of Yahweh is the
beginning of knowledge"* — is now reversed in direction. **There, the fear of Yahweh is where
knowledge begins; here, seeking knowledge is how one comes to understand the fear of Yahweh.** See
[[The Fear of God]].

**Job's vocabulary, without Job's case (2:7).** *"He lays up sound wisdom for the upright. He is a
shield to those who walk in integrity."* *Sound wisdom* is *tûšiyyâ*, a word close to the book of Job's
heart (Eliphaz's *"So that their hands can't perform their enterprise"*, Job 5:12, is the same
noun); *the upright* is *yəšārîm*; *integrity* is *tōm*. The pair recurs in 2:21: *"the upright
(yəšārîm) will dwell in the land. The perfect (təmîmîm) will remain in it."* **They are the two
words with which the book of Job introduces its hero**: *"That man was blameless and upright"* (Job
1:1, *tām wəyāšār*). Proverbs 2 promises the *tām* and the *yāšār* a shield and a place in the land.
Job was both, and lost his land, his house, and his children. **Tension flagged, not resolved.** The
wiki's reading is the one stated on the [[Proverbs]] book page — that Proverbs states how things
ordinarily run, and Job is the case that shows the ordinary rule is not a guarantee in any one life
— but Proverbs 2:7 and 2:21 do not hedge, and Job's story does not soften them. See [[Integrity]]
and [[Retribution]].

**Wisdom as a guard (2:8–11).** God *"guard[s] the paths of justice"* (*linṣōr*, 2:8); then the
gift moves inside the son and does the same work: *"Discretion will watch over you. Understanding
will keep you"* (2:11, *tišmōr … tinṣərekkâ*). The verbs God performs for the upright in 2:8,
*nāṣar* and *šāmar*, are the verbs wisdom performs in 2:11. And the second *then* reproduces the
preface: *"Then you will understand righteousness and justice, equity"* (2:9, *ṣedeq ûmišpāṭ
ûmêšārîm*) is the same triplet as *"righteousness, justice, and equity"* (1:3, *ṣedeq ûmišpāṭ
ûmēšārîm*), and *"Discretion"* (2:11, *məzimmâ*) is the word the preface promised *"to the young
man"* (1:4). **The book's purpose statement is coming true in the son.** See [[Righteousness]].

**Two rescues, from a man and a woman (2:12–19).** The second half names the two dangers the rest
of Proverbs 1–9 returns to: men who speak perversity, and the strange woman. **Both are defined by
speech and by what they forsake.**

- **The men (2:12–15).** *"the men who speak perverse things"* — *tahpuḵôt*, from *h-p-k*, *to turn
  upside down*: speech that inverts. They *"forsake the paths of uprightness, to walk in the ways of
  darkness"* (2:13, *hāʿōzəḇîm*), *"rejoice to do evil"* (2:14), and are *"crooked in their ways"*
  (2:15). They are the gang of 1:10–19 seen from the outside, with the emphasis moved from violence
  to talk. Job used the same picture of the dead: *"the land of darkness and of the shadow of death"*
  (Job 10:21) — the men's road leads where the woman's does. See [[Light and Darkness]].
- **The woman (2:16–19).** *"To deliver you from the strange woman, even from the foreigner who
  flatters with her words; who forsakes the friend of her youth, and forgets the covenant of her
  God."* *Forsakes* is *hāʿōzeḇet* — the same participle as the men's *hāʿōzəḇîm* (2:13), in the
  feminine. **The men forsake the paths of uprightness; the woman forsakes her husband.** Her weapon
  is *ʾămāreyhā*, *her words*, made smooth (*heḥĕlîqâ*, *flatters*) — and *ʾămāray*, *my words*, is
  what the father asked the son to receive in 2:1. The chapter sets the father's words against
  hers. The couplet of 2:16 returns word for word at 7:5.

**Who the strange woman is (2:16–17).** She is *ʾiššâ zārâ*, a *strange* or *outside* woman, and
*nokriyyâ*, a *foreign* woman. Two readings are live, and the wiki keeps both open.

1. **Another man's wife.** 2:17 says she has left *"the friend of her youth"* (*ʾallûp̄ nəʿûreyhā*)
   and forgotten *"the covenant of her God"* (*bərît ʾĕlōheyhā*). That makes her a married woman
   — *strange* because she belongs to someone else's household, outside the son's own — and makes
   her marriage a covenant with God as its witness. Proverbs 5 and 7 develop her this way, with a
   husband away from home (7:19, a later pericope).
2. **A literally foreign woman**, whose *"God"* is her own; on this reading 2:17 means the covenant
   of whatever god she married under, and the warning has the same edge as the foreign-marriage
   texts of Ezra and Nehemiah.

The wiki's reading is (1), because the chapter's terms — *youth*, *covenant*, *forsake*, *forget*
— are the terms Malachi uses for marriage within Israel: *"Yahweh has been witness between you and
the wife of your youth, against whom you have dealt treacherously, though she is your companion,
and the wife of your covenant"* (Malachi 2:14, *ʾēšeṯ nəʿûreyḵā … ʾēšeṯ bərîṯeḵā*). The man
Malachi accuses has *bāgaḏtâ*, *dealt treacherously* — the root of the *bôḡəḏîm*, *the treacherous*,
who are uprooted from the land at Proverbs 2:22. **Malachi indicts the husband; Proverbs 2 the wife;
the vocabulary is one.** Jeremiah 3:4 puts Israel's covenant with Yahweh in the same terms, Israel
calling God *ʾallûp̄ nəʿuray*, *the guide of my youth* in the WEB — the same phrase as *"the friend of
her youth"* here. A third reading — that she is already a figure for Folly, who speaks in chapter 9
and whose guests *"are in the depths of Sheol"* (9:18) — is the direction the book itself takes,
but in chapter 2 she is described as a woman, not as an allegory. See
[[The Wife of Your Youth - Proverbs 2.17, Malachi 2.14, and Jeremiah 3.4]], [[Marriage]],
[[Covenant]], and [[Sexual Sin]].

**Her house sinks down to death (2:18–19).** *"For her house leads down to death, her paths to the
departed spirits. None who go to her return again, neither do they attain to the paths of life."*
*Leads down* is *šāḥâ*, *sinks, bows down*: the house itself is pictured going under. *The departed
spirits* is *rəp̄āʾîm*, the shades of the dead — the word of Job 26:5, *"The departed spirits
tremble, those beneath the waters"*, of Psalm 88:10, *"Do the departed spirits rise up and praise
you?"*, and of Isaiah 14:9, where Sheol *"stirs up the departed spirits"* to meet the king of
Babylon. (The same Hebrew word names a people of giants in Genesis 14:5 and Deuteronomy 2–3; see
[[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]]. In wisdom and poetry it means the dead.) **None return**:
*lōʾ yəšûḇûn* — Job's own description of death, *"before I go where I shall not return from"* (Job
10:21, *wəlōʾ ʾāšûḇ*). Proverbs makes adultery a one-way journey into the realm Job feared, and
returns to the image at 5:5, *"Her feet go down to death. Her steps lead straight to Sheol"*, and
9:18. See [[Sheol]] and [[Life and Death]].

**The land (2:20–22).** *"that you may walk in the way of good men, and keep the paths of the
righteous. For the upright will dwell in the land. The perfect will remain in it. But the wicked
will be cut off from the land. The treacherous will be rooted out of it."* *ʾEreṣ* can mean the
earth or the land; in a book addressed to Israelite sons, and beside the vocabulary of Deuteronomy,
it reads as the land.

- *"Cut off"* is *yikkārēṯû*, from *kāraṯ* — the verb of making a covenant (*kāraṯ bərîṯ*, to *cut*
  a covenant) and of the covenant penalty: the uncircumcised male *"shall be cut off from his
  people. He has broken my covenant"* (Genesis 17:14, *wənikrəṯâ*). The woman *forgot the
  covenant* (2:17); the wicked are *cut off*.
- *"Rooted out"* is *yissəḥû*, from *nāsaḥ*, *to tear away* — the verb of Deuteronomy's curse on a
  disobedient Israel: *"You will be plucked from the land that you are going in to possess"*
  (Deuteronomy 28:63, *wənissaḥtem*). Proverbs applies to the individual wicked what Deuteronomy
  threatens the nation with.
- Psalm 37 says the same thing five times in its own words — *"evildoers shall be cut off, but those
  who wait for Yahweh shall inherit the land"* (37:9, *yikkārēṯûn*), *"the humble shall inherit the
  land"* (37:11), *"The righteous shall inherit the land, and live in it forever"* (37:29,
  *wəyiškənû*, the *dwell* of Proverbs 2:21). The third beatitude takes up 37:11 — *"Blessed are the
  gentle, for they shall inherit the earth"* (Matthew 5:5) — in what is usually read as a near
  quotation of the psalm's Greek form (the Septuagint was not checked here).

**Read after Genesis and Job, the land promise has to be held with its exceptions.** The patriarchs
lived in the promised land as sojourners and owned only a grave in it ([[Land and Sojourning]]);
Job, *tām wəyāšār*, lost everything he had (Job 1:13–19). Proverbs 2:21–22 states the rule, and
the wiki records the tension under [[Retribution]] rather than resolving it here. See
[[The Upright Will Dwell in the Land - Proverbs 2.21-22, Psalm 37, and Matthew 5.5]].

**Ways and paths.** The chapter uses *derek*, *way*, *ʾōraḥ*, *path*, and *maʿgāl*, *track*,
twelve times between them (2:8–20) — *"every good path"* (2:9), *"the ways of darkness"* (2:13), *"her
paths to the departed spirits"* (2:18), *"the paths of life"* (2:19), *"the way of good men"*
(2:20). Life is a road with two ends. Psalm 1:6 closes on the same picture — *"Yahweh knows the way
of the righteous, but the way of the wicked shall perish"* — and Matthew 7:13–14 gives it its New
Testament form, the broad way *"that leads to destruction"* and the narrow one *"that leads to life"*.
The book develops the image at length in [[Proverbs]] 4:10–19, a later pericope. See
[[Walking with God]].

## Key verses

> **1** My son, if you will receive my words, and store up my commandments within you;
> **2** So as to turn your ear to wisdom, and apply your heart to understanding;
> **3** Yes, if you call out for discernment, and lift up your voice for understanding;
> **4** If you seek her as silver, and search for her as for hidden treasures:
> **5** then you will understand the fear of Yahweh, and find the knowledge of God.
> **6** For Yahweh gives wisdom. Out of his mouth comes knowledge and understanding.

> **7** He lays up sound wisdom for the upright. He is a shield to those who walk in integrity;
> **8** that he may guard the paths of justice, and preserve the way of his saints.
> **9** Then you will understand righteousness and justice, equity and every good path.
> **10** For wisdom will enter into your heart. Knowledge will be pleasant to your soul.
> **11** Discretion will watch over you. Understanding will keep you,

> **12** to deliver you from the way of evil, from the men who speak perverse things;
> **13** who forsake the paths of uprightness, to walk in the ways of darkness;
> **14** who rejoice to do evil, and delight in the perverseness of evil;
> **15** who are crooked in their ways, and wayward in their paths:

> **16** To deliver you from the strange woman, even from the foreigner who flatters with her words;
> **17** who forsakes the friend of her youth, and forgets the covenant of her God:
> **18** for her house leads down to death, her paths to the departed spirits.
> **19** None who go to her return again, neither do they attain to the paths of life:

> **20** that you may walk in the way of good men, and keep the paths of the righteous.
> **21** For the upright will dwell in the land. The perfect will remain in it.
> **22** But the wicked will be cut off from the land. The treacherous will be rooted out of it.

## People

- *The father and the son* — the speaker and addressee of the lecture (2:1). No pages, as in
  [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]].
- [[God]] — named *Yahweh* three times (2:5, 6) and *God* twice (2:5, 17): the giver of wisdom, the
  one who stores it up for the upright, their shield and guard.
- *The strange woman* — *ʾiššâ zārâ*, *nokriyyâ* (2:16). **Not given a person page**: like Wisdom,
  she is a type within the book's instruction rather than a character in a narrative. Her page is
  the theme [[Sexual Sin]]. Proverbs 7 gives her a scene and a speech, and was the pericope set to
  decide whether that called for a separate page; it was decided not to — she is still unnamed and
  still a type, a wife in a prostitute's dress — see [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]].
- *The men who speak perverse things* (2:12) — no page; see [[Folly]] and [[Language and Speech]].

## Places

- [[Sheol]] — not named in the chapter, but the realm of *"death"* and *"the departed spirits"*
  (2:18) to which the woman's house sinks; Proverbs names it in the same picture at 5:5 and 9:18.
- *The land* (2:21–22) — the land of promise, unnamed; see [[Canaan]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].

## Themes

- [[Wisdom]] — sought as silver, given by Yahweh; effort and gift in one sentence.
- [[The Fear of God]] — understood at the end of the search (2:5), in parallel with the knowledge of
  God.
- [[Righteousness]] — the preface's triplet *righteousness, justice, and equity* returning (2:9).
- [[Integrity]] — *tōm* and *tāmîm* (2:7, 21), the pair of Job 1:1, promised a shield and the land.
- [[Sexual Sin]] — the strange woman introduced (2:16–19).
- [[Marriage]] — *"the friend of her youth"* and *"the covenant of her God"* (2:17).
- [[Covenant]] — marriage as a covenant; *cut off* (2:22), the covenant penalty.
- [[Life and Death]] — her house sinking to death; the paths of life (2:18–19).
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — the upright dwelling in the land, the wicked torn out (2:21–22).
- [[Retribution]] — the rule stated without exception, read against Job.
- [[Language and Speech]] — the father's words against the perverse men's and the woman's.
- [[Light and Darkness]] — *"the ways of darkness"* (2:13).
- [[Walking with God]] — the chapter's dozen ways and paths.
- [[Providence]] — God as shield and guard of the paths of justice (2:7–8).

alphabetic description of the chapter is the commentators' (not checked against a named one); the
first letters it rests on were checked.

**Translations.** The WEB renders *ʾallûp̄* as *"friend"* at Proverbs 2:17 and *"guide"* at Jeremiah
3:4; the Hebrew phrase, *ʾallûp̄ nəʿûr-*, is the same in both.

**The unit.** 2:1 opens with *"My son"* and a new speaker after Wisdom's speech; the edition above
places a *pe* paragraph marker after 2:22, and 3:1 opens a new lecture with *"My son"*. The chapter
is one sentence in the Hebrew; the WEB's punctuation keeps it nearly so.
