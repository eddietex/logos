# Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 3:13–20

## Summary

The instruction pauses to praise the thing the son has been urged to seek. The happy person
finds wisdom (3:13); wisdom brings a return greater than silver, gold, rubies, or anything else
one might desire (3:14–15). She holds long life in one hand and riches and honor in the other,
and her roads lead through pleasantness and peace (3:16–17). The praise ends where it began,
with happiness, now attached to retaining her: she is a tree of life (3:18).

The last two verses enlarge the reason for valuing her. Wisdom is not merely an aid to a
successful household: Yahweh founded the earth by wisdom and established the heavens by
understanding (3:19). His knowledge governs the depths and the dew (3:20). The passage moves
from what wisdom gives a person to its place in God's making and sustaining the world.

**3:13–15 — Finding what no purchase can match.** Proverbs 2:4 urged the search for wisdom
as for silver; this poem says the result is worth more than silver. The language of profit
does not make wisdom a commodity with a price. It ranks her above the goods used to describe
her value. See [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] and [[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]].

**3:16–18 — Both hands full, and a tree to grasp.** Wisdom is pictured as a woman with gifts
in both hands, then as a road to walk and a tree to hold. These are complementary images of
life with wisdom, not a narrative about a named woman. Finding her in verse 13 becomes
retaining her in verse 18: the poem praises continued attachment as well as discovery.
The tree recalls Genesis 2–3, but Proverbs does not claim that Eden has reopened or that
the wise become immortal.

**3:19–20 — The ground of the praise.** Earth and heavens widen the horizon beyond human
profit. Wisdom, understanding, and knowledge belong to the Creator's work. Verse 20 joins
the depths with the skies' dew; the breaking up of the depths can recall the flood, but
the verse does not identify a flood event. In this setting it places the waters within
God's wise ordering. Compare [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] and [[Out of the Whirlwind]]
through [[By Wisdom Yahweh Founded the Earth - Proverbs 3 and Creation]].

**Tension with Job, kept explicit.** Proverbs 3:16–17 associates wisdom with riches, honor,
and peace. Job 1:1 and 1:13–19 describe a God-fearing, upright man losing his property and
children; Job 2:7 adds bodily suffering. Proverbs 3:11–12 has already allowed for the
reproof of a loved son. Reading this praise as instruction about wisdom's worth, rather
than a guarantee by which to diagnose each person's fortunes, is the wiki's interpretation.
It does not remove either the promises in Proverbs or Job's counterexample.

## Key verses

> **3:13** Happy is the man who finds wisdom, the man who gets understanding.

> **3:14** For her good profit is better than getting silver, and her return is better than fine gold.

> **3:15** She is more precious than rubies. None of the things you can desire are to be compared to her.

> **3:16** Length of days is in her right hand. In her left hand are riches and honor.

> **3:17** Her ways are ways of pleasantness. All her paths are peace.

> **3:18** She is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her. Happy is everyone who retains her.

> **3:19** By wisdom Yahweh founded the earth. By understanding, he established the heavens.

> **3:20** By his knowledge, the depths were broken up, and the skies drop down the dew.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh founds earth and establishes heavens through wisdom and understanding.
- Wisdom is personified, as in [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]], and is treated on [[Wisdom]];
  the poem does not identify her as a separate historical person. The finder is unnamed.

## Places

- No named geographical location. Earth, heavens, depths, and skies describe the created
  world; the tree of life is an image, not a statement that the speaker is in Eden.

## Themes

- [[Wisdom]] — incomparable worth, life-giving guidance, and the Creator's wisdom.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The existing 3:13–20 unit is retained: happiness frames 3:13–18, creation
grounds the praise in 3:19–20, and the renewed address to the son at 3:21 starts the next unit.
No later pericope is ingested here.

**Personification and interpretation.** The woman holding gifts and the tree are poetic
representations of wisdom. Identifying this figure directly with Christ would be a further
Christian theological reading, not an identification made in these eight verses.

**Sources.** All eight verses above are copied verbatim from the stored WEB in
`raw/bible/Proverbs.md`. Genesis, Exodus, Job, and later Proverbs comparisons were checked
in their stored WEB files. Jeremiah 10:12–13 and Psalm 104:24 were verified separately through
bible-api.com with `translation=web` on 2026-10-25, without adding raw books.
The analysis concerns the WEB wording; no claim about exact Hebrew or Greek forms is made.
