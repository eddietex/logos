# A Dry Morsel with Quietness

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 17:1–28

## Summary

After chapter 16's frame of plan and outcome, chapter 17 has no frame at all. It opens on a house
— ***"Better is a dry morsel with quietness, than a house full of feasting with strife"*** (17:1),
which gives this page its title — and it closes on a closed mouth: *"Even a fool, when he keeps
silent, is counted wise"* (28). Between them the sayings return again and again to four subjects:
the household and its strife, the bribe and the corrupted court, the fool, and the friend. Yahweh
is named only twice (3, 15), and referred to once more as *"his Maker"* (5), against eleven times in
chapter 16. As with chapters 10–16, it is a
collection of related sayings rather than an argument; the groupings below are reading aids.

**17:1–6 — The house, the furnace, and the crown.**

- *"Better is a dry morsel with quietness, than a house full of feasting with strife"* (1). The
  fourth *better than* saying about a poor table, after 15:16–17 and 16:8, and the closest to
  15:17's *"dinner of herbs, where love is"*. The *feasting* is *zibḥê-rîb*, literally *sacrifices
  of strife* (Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*): *zebaḥ* is the
  sacrificial meal, the kind of meat a household ate after a peace offering, and the strange
  woman's *"Sacrifices of peace offerings are with me"* (7:14) used the same noun. The WEB's
  *feasting* is right about the table; the Hebrew adds that it may be a religious feast, and that
  the house keeping it is quarrelling over it. See
  [[Better Is Little - The Better-Than Sayings of Proverbs]].
- *"A servant who deals wisely will rule over a son who causes shame, and shall have a part in the
  inheritance among the brothers"* (2). The pair is the one of 10:5, *ben maśkîl* and *ben mēbîš*,
  the wise son and the son who causes shame; here a slave (*ʿebed maśkîl*) takes the wise son's
  place, and the inheritance follows wisdom across the line of birth. Genesis has the fear this
  saying turns into a possibility: Abram, childless, said *"one born in my house is my heir"*
  (Genesis 15:3), and Sarah's *"the son of this servant will not be heir with my son"* (21:10) shut
  the door the proverb holds open. See [[Slavery and Servitude]] and
  [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].
- ***"The refining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold, but Yahweh tests the hearts"*** (3).
  *Bōḥēn libbôt YHWH*: *bḥn* is the assayer's verb, the one Job used at 23:10, *"When he has tried
  me, I shall come out like gold"* (*bəḥānanî*; Hebrew from Sefaria). Proverbs 27:21 repeats the first half word for word in Hebrew
  (*maṣrēp lakkesep wəkûr lazzāhāb*), though the WEB renders the same noun *"The crucible"* there,
  and finishes it differently: *"but man is refined by his praise"*. Jeremiah 17:10 says *"I try the
  heart"* (WEB at bible-api.com), with the same participle *bōḥēn* but *kəlāyôt*, *kidneys*, as its
  object (Hebrew from Sefaria). Where 16:2 had Yahweh *weigh* the motives, here he smelts them. See
  [[I Shall Come Out Like Gold - Job 23.10 and the Refiner's Fire]] and [[Testing]].
- *"An evildoer heeds wicked lips. A liar gives ear to a mischievous tongue"* (4) — a saying about
  the listener, not the speaker: lies need an audience that wants them.
- ***"Whoever mocks the poor reproaches his Maker. He who is glad at calamity shall not be
  unpunished"*** (5). The first half is 14:31 again, with *mocking* (*lōʿēg*) in the place of
  *oppressing* (*ʿōšēq*); *ḥērēp ʿōśēhû*, *reproaches his Maker*, is the same in both (Hebrew from
  Sefaria; the WEB has *"shows contempt for his Maker"* at 14:31). Mockery, which takes nothing,
  is treated as an attack on God as surely as oppression, which takes everything. The second half
  is the subject of Job's oath: *"If I have rejoiced at the destruction of him who hated me, or
  lifted up myself when evil found him"* (Job 31:29), and of 24:17, *"Don't rejoice when your enemy
  falls."* See [[Contempt for His Maker - Proverbs 14.31 and Job 31.13-15]].
- *"Children's children are the crown of old men; the glory of children are their parents"* (6).
  16:31 called grey hair *ʿăṭeret tipʾeret*, *a crown of glory*; 17:6 takes the phrase apart and
  gives one word to each line — *ʿăṭeret zəqēnîm*, the old men's *crown*, and *tipʾeret bānîm*, the
  children's *glory* (Hebrew from Sefaria). Genesis closes on the picture: *"Joseph saw Ephraim's
  children to the third generation"* (Genesis 50:23); and Job, after the restoration, *"saw his sons,
  and his sons' sons, to four generations"* (Job 42:16). Job is the reminder that the crown can be
  taken before it is given back, and that the children it was given back in were not the ones he
  lost. See [[The Crown Given and Taken - Proverbs 4 and Job 19]].

**17:7–8, 15, 23, 26 — The bribe and the court.** The chapter returns five times to the corruption
of judgment, and the sayings read best together.

- *"Arrogant speech isn't fitting for a fool, much less do lying lips fit a prince"* (7). The
  *prince* is *nādîb*, the noble; the standard rises with the office.
- ***"A bribe is a precious stone in the eyes of him who gives it; wherever he turns, he
  prospers"*** (8). The *precious stone* is *ʾeben-ḥēn*, a *stone of favour*, and the bribe is
  *haššōḥad*. The saying observes and does not commend: *in the eyes of* its owner (*bəʿênê
  bəʿālāyw*) the bribe works like a charm, and the line reports how it looks to him. Proverbs 21:14
  makes the same observation, *"a bribe in the cloak, strong wrath"* — it pacifies.
- ***"He who justifies the wicked, and he who condemns the righteous, both of them alike are an
  abomination to Yahweh"*** (15). *Maṣdîq rāšāʿ*, *justifying the wicked*, is the precise inversion
  of the judge's duty in Deuteronomy 25:1 — *"they shall justify the righteous, and condemn the
  wicked"* — and Exodus 23:7 grounds the prohibition in God's own practice: *"I will not justify the
  wicked"* (*lōʾ-ʾaṣdîq rāšāʿ*). Isaiah 5:23 pronounces woe on those *"who acquit the guilty for a
  bribe"*; the WEB's *acquit the guilty* translates *maṣdîqê rāšāʿ*, the participle of 17:15 (WEB at
  bible-api.com; Hebrew from Sefaria). *Both of them alike*: acquitting the guilty is no lighter an
  abomination than condemning the innocent.
- ***"A wicked man receives a bribe in secret, to pervert the ways of justice"*** (23). This is the
  judgment that 17:8 withheld. *Šōḥad … yiqqāḥ*, *takes a bribe*, is the prohibition of Exodus 23:8
  turned into a description — *"You shall take no bribe"*, *wəšōḥad lōʾ tiqqāḥ* — and *ləhaṭṭôt …
  mišpāṭ*, *to pervert justice*, is the verb of Exodus 23:6 (*"You shall not deny justice"*, *lōʾ taṭṭeh
  mišpaṭ*) and Deuteronomy 16:19 (*"You shall not pervert justice"*, *lōʾ-taṭṭeh mišpāṭ*); all read in
  Hebrew from Sefaria. *In secret* is *mēḥēq*, *from the bosom*, the fold of the garment where 21:14
  hides its bribe (*baḥēq*). See
  [[A Bribe Blinds - Proverbs 17.23, Exodus 23.8, and Deuteronomy 16.19]].
- *"Also to punish the righteous is not good, nor to flog officials for their integrity"* (26). The
  *officials* are again *nədîbîm*, the nobles of verse 7: the court can wrong the powerful too, when
  they are punished for their *yōšer*, their uprightness.

Together these make the chapter's sharpest point about law. Proverbs elsewhere is accused of
describing the world as it ordinarily runs; here it describes a world in which the ordinary
running is corrupt — the bribe works (8), the guilty are acquitted (15), the righteous punished
(26) — and calls that running an abomination rather than an order.

**17:9–14 — Covering, rebuke, and the breach.**

- ***"He who covers an offense promotes love; but he who repeats a matter separates best
  friends"*** (9). *Məkasseh-pešaʿ*, *covering transgression*, is 10:12's *"love covers all wrongs"*
  (*kol-pəšāʿîm təkasseh ʾahăbâ*) turned from a statement about love into an act that seeks it
  (*məbaqqēš ʾahăbâ*, WEB *promotes*). The second half is 16:28's whisperer who *"separates close
  friends"*, with the same verb (*mapriḏ ʾallûp̄*). Covering here is the refusal to retell, not the
  pretence that nothing happened; 28:13 will set the limit on the other side, *"He who conceals his
  sins doesn't prosper."* See [[Love Covers Wrongs - Proverbs 10.12, 1 Peter 4.8, and James 5.20]].
- *"A rebuke enters deeper into one who has understanding than a hundred lashes into a fool"* (10).
  Deuteronomy 25:3 limits a beating to *"no more than forty stripes"* (WEB at bible-api.com); the proverb's hundred is not a sentence but a
  hyperbole, and its point is the old one of 9:8 and 12:1: correction works on those who love it.
- *"An evil man seeks only rebellion; therefore a cruel messenger shall be sent against him"* (11).
  The *messenger* (*malʾāk*) may be a royal officer — 16:14's *"messenger of death"* — or an agent of
  God; the saying does not say who sends him, and both readings have defenders.
- ***"Let a bear robbed of her cubs meet a man, rather than a fool in his folly"*** (12). *Dōb
  šakkûl*, the bereaved bear, is a proverbial figure for fury: Hushai uses it of David's men,
  *"like a bear robbed of her cubs in the field"* (2 Samuel 17:8), and Yahweh uses it of himself in
  Hosea 13:8, *"I will meet them like a bear that is bereaved of her cubs"* — Hosea's verb *ʾepgəšēm*,
  *I will meet them*, is the root of Proverbs' *pāgôš*, *let … meet* (WEB at bible-api.com; Hebrew
  from Sefaria). The fool is worse than the bear because the bear's rage has a cause and an end.
- ***"Whoever rewards evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house"*** (13). *Mēšîb rāʿâ taḥat
  ṭôbâ*: the noun phrase *rāʿâ taḥat ṭôbâ* is Joseph's steward's charge at Genesis 44:4, *"Why have
  you rewarded evil for good?"*, though Genesis has a different verb (*šillamtem*); the phrase, not
  the whole clause, is shared (Hebrew from Sefaria). The consequence lands on the *house*, not only the
  man. See [[Rewarding Evil for Good - A False Charge That Is Exactly True]].
- *"The beginning of strife is like breaching a dam, therefore stop contention before quarreling
  breaks out"* (14). *Pôṭēr mayim*, *letting water loose*: the image is a small opening that cannot
  be closed once it widens. *Rēʾšît mādôn*, *the beginning of strife*, borrows the word for the
  beginning of knowledge (1:7) and of wisdom (4:7, *rēʾšît ḥokmâ*, WEB *"Wisdom is supreme"*); here the
  beginning is the moment to act.

**17:16–20 — The fool's money, the friend, and the pledge.**

- *"Why is there money in the hand of a fool to buy wisdom, since he has no understanding?"* (16).
  *Liqnôt ḥokmâ*, *to get wisdom*, is the command of 4:5, 7 (*qənê ḥokmâ*) — *"Yes, though it costs
  all your possessions, get understanding"* — and 23:23 says *"Buy the truth."* The fool has the
  price and lacks the *lēb*, the heart, which is the one thing the purchase requires. Wisdom is for
  sale and cannot be bought. See [[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]].
- ***"A friend loves at all times; and a brother is born for adversity"*** (17). The two halves can
  be read as synonymous (the friend *is* a brother in trouble) or as progressive (a friend loves
  always, and a brother is what trouble proves him to be); the Hebrew allows both and the wiki does
  not choose. 18:24 adds that *"there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother"*, and 19:7 the
  other side: the poor man's *"friends avoid him!"* Job is the wiki's test of the saying. His
  *"three friends"* came *"to sympathize with him and to comfort him"* (Job 2:11); by chapter 6 he
  says *"To him who is ready to faint, kindness should be shown from his friend"* and *"My brothers
  have dealt deceitfully as a brook"* (6:14–15), and by chapter 19 *"All my familiar friends abhor
  me"* (19:19). See [[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]] and
  [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]].
- *"A man void of understanding strikes hands, and becomes collateral in the presence of his
  neighbor"* (18). The fourth saying against suretyship, after 6:1–5 and 11:15, with the same verbs
  as 6:1 (*tôqēaʿ kāp̄*, *ʿōrēb ʿărubbâ*). Placed directly after 17:17, it limits it: love the friend
  at all times, but do not sign for him. Judah's *"I'll be collateral for him"* (Genesis 43:9,
  *ʾeʿerbennû*) is the wiki's case of a pledge that was not folly. See
  [[I Will Be Surety for Him - Judah's Pledge and the Guarantor in Scripture]].
- *"He who loves disobedience loves strife. One who builds a high gate seeks destruction"* (19). The
  *high gate* is *maḡbîah pitḥô*, *who makes his door high*: either a house built for display, or —
  on a reading that takes *door* as the mouth — the man who opens his mouth wide; both are
  proposed, and the WEB takes the first.
- *"One who has a perverse heart doesn't find prosperity, and one who has a deceitful tongue falls
  into trouble"* (20).

**17:21–28 — The father of a fool, and the closed mouth.**

- *"He who becomes the father of a fool grieves. The father of a fool has no joy"* (21) and *"A
  foolish son brings grief to his father, and bitterness to her who bore him"* (25). The heading
  saying of the collection, *"A wise son makes a glad father; but a foolish son brings grief to his
  mother"* (10:1), told twice more from the parents' side. See [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]].
- ***"A cheerful heart makes good medicine, but a crushed spirit dries up the bones"*** (22). The
  third saying in three chapters on the spirit and the body, with 15:13 (*"an aching heart breaks the
  spirit"*) and 18:14 (*"a crushed spirit, who can bear?"*). Job's *"In the night season my bones are pierced
  in me"* (Job 30:17) is the crushed spirit seen from inside; the proverb names the
  connection without promising that a cheerful heart is always available. See [[Healing]].
- *"Wisdom is before the face of one who has understanding, but the eyes of a fool wander to the ends
  of the earth"* (24). Wisdom is near and the fool looks for it far away — or does not look for it at
  all, and lets his eyes go everywhere else.
- ***"He who spares his words has knowledge. He who is even tempered is a man of understanding"***
  (27). The written text (*ketiv*) has *qar-rûaḥ*, *cool of spirit*; the reading tradition (*qere*)
  has *yəqar-rûaḥ*, *precious of spirit* (Sefaria prints both). The WEB's *even tempered* follows
  the first.
- ***"Even a fool, when he keeps silent, is counted wise. When he shuts his lips, he is thought to be
  discerning"*** (28). Job said the same to his friends, and meant it as an insult: *"Oh that you
  would be completely silent! Then you would be wise"* (Job 13:5). The two verses share the root
  *ḥrš*, *be silent* — *maḥărîš* in Proverbs, *haḥărēš taḥărîšûn* in Job — and the same
  conclusion, wisdom (*ḥākām*, *ləḥokmâ*); Hebrew from Sefaria. The friends had been wisest in the
  seven days they said nothing (Job 2:13). See
  [[Lay Your Hand on Your Mouth - Job 21.5, 29.9, and 40.4]].

**The promises and their counterexamples.** The chapter's retribution sayings — the gloater *"shall
not be unpunished"* (5), the rebel meets *"a cruel messenger"* (11), evil *"shall not depart from his
house"* (13), the perverse heart *"doesn't find prosperity"* (20) — are general sayings, read here as
on the pages for chapters 10–16. But this chapter qualifies its own promises more sharply than most:
the bribe that *prospers* its giver wherever he turns (8) and the righteous who are punished and
flogged (15, 26) are admissions, inside the collection, that the court can make the righteous suffer
and the wicked thrive. Job's friends spoke as though no such court existed. See [[Retribution]]
and [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

> **17:3** The refining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold, but Yahweh tests the hearts.

> **17:22** A cheerful heart makes good medicine, but a crushed spirit dries up the bones.

## Notes

stated convention for the sentence collection, not a claim that the chapter is one original
discourse. Only this queue item is advanced.

`raw/bible/` (WEB). Raw text is unchanged. Quotations of Deuteronomy 16:19; 25:1; 2 Samuel 17:8;
Isaiah 5:23; Jeremiah 17:10; and Hosea 13:8 were checked against the WEB at bible-api.com in this
pass; those books are not stored and were not fetched. Deuteronomy 25:3 was checked the same way.
