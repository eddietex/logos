# Wisdom at the Creation

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 8:22–36

## Summary

The second half of Wisdom's speech in chapter 8, and the passage the book is best known for
outside itself. In 8:1–21 Wisdom said what she offers; here she says where she comes from. **Her
origin** (8:22–26): *"Yahweh possessed me in the beginning of his work, before his deeds of
old"*; she was set up *"from everlasting"*, and *"born"* before the depths, the springs, the
mountains, the hills, and the dust of the world. **Her presence** (8:27–29): when he established
the heavens, set a circle on the deep, made the clouds firm, and gave the sea its boundary, *"I was
there"*. **Her delight** (8:30–31): *"then I was the craftsman by his side"*, rejoicing before him
day by day, rejoicing in his world, with her delight among *"the sons of men"*. **Her appeal**
(8:32–36): *"Now therefore, my sons, listen to me"*; blessed is the one who keeps her ways and
watches daily at her doors, *"For whoever finds me, finds life, and will obtain favor from
Yahweh"*, and *"All those who hate me love death"*.

The father said at 3:19 that *"By wisdom Yahweh founded the earth"*. Here Wisdom says the same
thing in the first person, and adds that she was there to see it. The question Job 28 asked —
*"where shall wisdom be found?"* — has two answers in this chapter. She was with God before
anything was made, so no living person could find her by searching (Job 28:12–22). And she calls
at the city gates, so anyone can hear her (8:1–4). The speech joins them: the wisdom that was
present at creation is the wisdom that now speaks to *"the sons of men"*, where she delights to be
(8:31).

**8:22 — "Yahweh possessed me".** *YHWH qānānî rēʾšîṯ darkô qeḏem mip̄ʿālāyw mēʾāz*. Three words
need comment.

*Qānānî*, *possessed me*, is from *qānâ*, whose ordinary meaning is *to acquire* or *to get*,
often by purchase. It is the verb the father used when he told the son to get wisdom: *"Get
wisdom. Yes, though it costs all your possessions, get understanding"* (4:7, *qənēh ḥoḵmâ …
qənēh ḇînâ*). What the son must acquire, Yahweh had at the beginning. The same verb is used where
God is called *"possessor of heaven and earth"* (Genesis 14:19, 22, *qōnēh šāmayim wāʾāreṣ*), and
by Eve at the birth of Cain: *"I have gotten a man with Yahweh's help"* (Genesis 4:1, *qānîṯî*).
Those two uses show the range — owning, and bringing into being — and translations divide on 8:22
along that line: *possessed*, *acquired*, *brought forth*, or *created*. The WEB's *possessed*
takes the first. The Greek translation is usually reported as reading *ektisen*, *created*; the
wiki has not checked a Septuagint edition for that. The choice mattered later, because in the
fourth-century debates about the Son of God, this verse was read of Christ, and *created* was taken
by one side to mean that the Son was a creature. That history is mentioned here as commonly
reported; the wiki has not read the primary sources for it. The Hebrew verb itself does not decide
between *acquired* and *made*. The words that follow — *born* (8:24–25) — suggest an origin, but
they are poetry about birth, not a doctrine of it.

*Rēʾšîṯ darkô*, *the beginning of his way* (WEB *"the beginning of his work"*), uses the word that
opens Genesis: *bərēʾšîṯ*, *"In the beginning"* (Genesis 1:1). It is also the book's word for the
place of the fear of Yahweh — *"the beginning of knowledge"* (1:7, *rēʾšîṯ dāʿaṯ*) — and of wisdom
herself in 4:7 (*rēʾšîṯ ḥoḵmâ*, WEB *"Wisdom is supreme"*). The Hebrew can be read *as the
beginning of his way* — Wisdom herself is the first of his works — or *at the beginning of his
way*; the WEB takes the second, with *"in"*. *Qeḏem … mēʾāz*, *before … of old*: before anything he
made. The same phrase is given in Job to Behemoth: *"He is the chief of the ways of God"* (Job 40:19,
*rēʾšîṯ darkê-ʾēl*, as read on the wiki's Job pages), where the WEB takes it as rank rather than time.
See [[The First of the Ways of God - Job 40.19 and Proverbs 8.22]].

**8:23–26 — Before the world.** *"I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning, before the
earth existed"* (8:23). *Nissaḵtî*, *I was set up*, is the verb of Psalm 2:6, *"Yet I have set my
King on my holy hill of Zion"* (*nāsaḵtî malkî*), so the word may mean *installed*, as a king is.
Some read it from a different root meaning *woven* or *formed*; the wiki notes both, from the usual
lexical discussion, without checking a lexicon. *Mēʿôlām*, *from everlasting*; *mērōʾš*, *from the
head*, the beginning.

*"When there were no depths, I was born, when there were no springs abounding with water. Before
the mountains were settled in place, before the hills, I was born"* (8:24–25). *Ḥôlāltî*, twice,
is from *ḥûl*, *to writhe* in labour, *to be born*. It is the verb Eliphaz used when he asked Job,
*"were you brought out before the hills?"* (Job 15:7), and *gəḇāʿôṯ*, *hills*, is in both. See
[[The First Man and Wisdom Before the Hills]]. Eliphaz was asking Job whether he thought he was
Wisdom; here Wisdom says she is the one who was.

The list runs from below upward and from water to land: *təhōmôṯ*, *depths*; *maʿyānôṯ*,
*springs*; mountains *hoṭbāʿû*, *sunk* or *settled* into place; hills; *ʾereṣ wəḥûṣôṯ*, *earth and
fields* (literally *outside places*); and *rōʾš ʿap̄rôṯ tēḇēl*, *the first of the dust of the world*
(8:26). *Tēḇēl* is the inhabited world, and it comes back in 8:31.

**8:27–29 — "I was there".** *"When he established the heavens, I was there; when he set a circle
on the surface of the deep"* (8:27). *Šām ʾānî*, *there was I*. *Bahăḵînô šāmayim*, *when he
established the heavens*, is the verb of 3:19, *"By understanding, he established the heavens"*
(*kônēn šāmayim*). *Ḥûg ʿal-pənê ṯəhôm*, *a circle on the face of the deep*, takes the phrase of
Genesis 1:2 — *"Darkness was on the surface of the deep"* (*ʿal-pənê ṯəhôm*) — and draws the
horizon on it. Job 26:10 has the same picture: *"He has described a boundary on the surface of the
waters"* (*ḥōq-ḥāḡ ʿal-pənê-māyim*), and Isaiah 40:22 uses *ḥûg* of the earth: *"he who sits above
the circle of the earth"*.

*"When he established the clouds above, when the springs of the deep became strong, when he gave to
the sea its boundary, that the waters should not violate his commandment, when he marked out the
foundations of the earth"* (8:28–29). *Šəḥāqîm*, *clouds* or *skies*. *Ḥuqqô*, *its boundary*,
is *ḥōq*, *decree* or *statute*, the noun of 8:15's *yəḥōqəqû*; *pîw*, translated *his
commandment*, is literally *his mouth*. The sea is held by a word. God says the same to Job: *"Here
you may come, but no further"* (Job 38:11), and Jeremiah 5:22 has Yahweh place *"the sand for the
bound of the sea, by a perpetual decree, that it can't pass it"* (*ḥāq-ʿôlām wəlōʾ
yaʿaḇrenhû*, the verb *pass over* as in 8:29's *yaʿaḇrû*). Psalm 104:9: *"You have set a boundary
that they may not pass over."* The wiki has followed the sea's boundary from Genesis 1:9 to the
flood and the Red Sea; see [[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]].

**8:30–31 — The craftsman and the delight.** *"Then I was the craftsman by his side. I was a
delight day by day, always rejoicing before him, rejoicing in his whole world. My delight was with
the sons of men"* (8:30–31). *Wāʾehyeh ʾeṣlô ʾāmôn*: *I was beside him, ʾāmôn*. The WEB's
*craftsman* is one reading of *ʾāmôn*, connecting it with *ʾommān*, *artisan*. Another reads it as
*ʾāmûn*, *nursling* or *little child*, which suits the birth language of 8:24–25 and the play of
8:30–31. The consonants allow both, and translations divide. If *craftsman*, Wisdom had a hand in
the work, as 3:19 implies (*"By wisdom"*). If *nursling*, she was a child playing beside the maker.
The wiki records both and takes no position.

*Šaʿăšuʿîm*, *delight*, is a plural of intensity, and it is used twice: she *was* delight (8:30),
and her delight was with humanity (8:31). Whose delight she was in 8:30 — God's in her, or hers
before God — the Hebrew does not say; the WEB's *"I was a delight"* leaves it open as well.
*Məśaḥeqeṯ*, *rejoicing*, twice, is from *śāḥaq*, which means *to laugh* and *to play*. The same
verb is used in Job of the animals that *"play"* on the mountains (Job 40:20, *yəśaḥăqû-šām*) and
of Leviathan, whom no one can *"play with … as with a bird"* (Job 41:5; Hebrew 40:29,
*hăṯəśaḥeq-bô*). God's speeches in Job showed a creation full of creatures he delights in and humans
cannot tame; Proverbs 8 puts a delighting figure beside him at its making. Job 38:7 adds the same
note from a different word: *"when the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted
for joy"*. Genesis 1 said God *"saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good"*
(1:31); 8:30–31 gives that verdict a voice.

*Bəṯēḇēl ʾarṣô*, *in his whole world* — literally *in the world of his earth* — returns to the
*tēḇēl* of 8:26. *Wəšaʿăšuʿay ʾeṯ-bənê ʾāḏām*: *and my delights were with the sons of Adam*. The
speech that began *"I send my voice to the sons of mankind"* (8:4, *bənê ʾāḏām*) ends its creation
account on the same people. **She calls to humanity at the gates because humanity was her delight
from the beginning.** Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* marks a *petuḥah*, an open
paragraph break, after 8:31.

**8:32–33 — "Now therefore, my sons".** *"Now therefore, my sons, listen to me, for blessed are
those who keep my ways. Hear instruction, and be wise. Don't refuse it"* (8:32–33). *Wəʿattâ
ḇānîm šimʿû-lî* is the father's formula, word for word: it opens 5:7 and 7:24 in the same Hebrew
(*wəʿattâ ḇānîm šimʿû-lî*). In 7:24 the father used it before his conclusion about the strange
woman; Wisdom now uses it before hers. **She speaks to the sons as the father does.** *ʾAšrê*,
*blessed* or *happy*, is the word of 3:13 and 3:18 (*"Happy is the man who finds wisdom"*); it
names a condition, not a pronounced blessing. *Wəʾal-tip̄rāʿû*, *don't refuse it*, is from
*pāraʿ*, *to let loose* or *neglect* — the verb of 1:25, *"you have ignored all my counsel"*
(*wattip̄rəʿû*), in her first speech. There it was the refusal already made; here it is the warning
before.

**8:34–35 — At her doors.** *"Blessed is the man who hears me, watching daily at my gates, waiting
at my door posts"* (8:34). *Dalṯōṯay*, *my doors* (WEB *gates*), and *məzûzōṯ pəṯāḥāy*, *the door
posts of my entrances*. *Məzûzôṯ* is the word of Deuteronomy 6:9, *"You shall write them on the door
posts of your house"* (*ʿal-məzûzōṯ bêṯeḵā*) — the commands of the Shema written where one goes in
and out. The wiki has already noted Deuteronomy 6:7 behind 6:22 (see
[[The Lamp of the Commandment]]); here the doorposts are Wisdom's own. The picture is a man waiting
at a house each morning as a servant or petitioner does. **It is set against the strange woman's
door**: *"Don't come near the door of her house"* (5:8, *petaḥ bêṯāh*), and Folly *"sits at the door
of her house"* (9:14, *ləp̄eṯaḥ bêṯāh*). *Yôm yôm*, *day by day*, is the phrase of 8:30: Wisdom
delighted before God *day by day*, and the blessed man waits at her doors *day by day*.

*"For whoever finds me, finds life, and will obtain favor from Yahweh"* (8:35). The second half,
*wayyāp̄eq rāṣôn mêYHWH*, is identical in Sefaria's Hebrew to the second half of 18:22: *"Whoever
finds a wife finds a good thing, and obtains favor of Yahweh"*. The first half of each also uses
*māṣāʾ*, *to find*. The book that set the wife of the son's youth against the strange woman (5:18)
and called wisdom *"my sister"* (7:4) uses the same words for finding a wife and finding Wisdom.
3:13 had already joined *finding* and *getting*: *ʾašrê ʾāḏām māṣāʾ ḥoḵmâ … yāp̄îq təḇûnâ*,
*"Happy is the man who finds wisdom, the man who gets understanding"* — *yāp̄îq* is the verb
translated *obtain* in 8:35. *Rāṣôn*, *favour* or *acceptance*, is the word of 12:2, *"A good man
shall obtain favor from Yahweh"* (*yāp̄îq rāṣôn*). See [[Favor and Grace]].

The verse has a written-and-read variant. Sefaria's edition prints *mōṣʾî*, *whoever finds me*,
followed by the written form *mṣʾy* and the read form *māṣāʾ*, *finds*. The written form would be
read as a plural, *the finders of me are finders of life*; the read form is singular and is the one
translated. The sense is the same.

**8:36 — Missing her.** *"But he who sins against me wrongs his own soul. All those who hate me love
death"* (8:36). *Wəḥōṭəʾî*, *he who sins against me*, is from *ḥāṭāʾ*, whose basic sense is usually
given as *to miss* a mark — the usual lexical meaning, not checked against a lexicon here. Against
*mōṣʾî*, *whoever finds me*, the contrast in 8:35–36 is between finding Wisdom and missing her.
*Ḥōmēs nap̄šô*, *wrongs his own soul*, is from *ḥāmas*, the root of *ḥāmās*, *violence*, the word
for the earth *"filled with violence"* before the flood (Genesis 6:11). He does violence to his own
life. This is how the book has spoken all along: the gang ambush their own lives (1:18, *"They lurk secretly for their own lives"*), *"the
backsliding of the simple will kill them"* (1:32), and the adulterer *"destroys his own soul"*
(6:32). Sin against Wisdom is self-harm before it is anything else.

*"All those who hate me love death."* The chapter ends on the same pair of verbs with which it
began its promises: *"I love those who love me"* (8:17). Now there are two loves: love of Wisdom,
and love of death. Chapter 7 ended the same way — *"Her house is the way to Sheol, going down to
the rooms of death"* (7:27) — and chapter 9 will end with Folly's guests *"in the depths of
Sheol"* (9:18). Between them Wisdom's speech ends with life and death set before the hearer. See
[[Life and Death]] and [[Two Voices in the Street - The Strange Woman and Wisdom in Proverbs 7-9]].

**Who is Wisdom?** The question left open on [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]] is not settled here.
Wisdom says she was *possessed* by Yahweh, *set up*, and *born* — so she has an origin and is not
simply Yahweh under another name. She was there before anything else was made, beside him, at the
making of everything — so she is not one of the creatures she watched being made. The poem holds
both. **The wiki reads her, as before, as God's own wisdom personified**: the poem's way of saying
that God's wisdom is older than the world, was the means of its making (3:19), and is now offered
to people. It does not decide more than the text does.

**How the New Testament reads it.** The New Testament does not quote Proverbs 8:22–31, but several
passages describe Christ in its terms. John 1:1–3: *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word
was with God … All things were made through him."* Colossians 1:15–17 calls him *"the firstborn of
all creation"*, through whom *"all things have been created"*, who *"is before all things, and in
him all things are held together"*. Hebrews 1:2 says God *"made the worlds"* through his Son;
Revelation 3:14 calls him *"the Beginning of God's creation"*; and 1 Corinthians 1:24 says that
*"Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God"*. None of these cites Proverbs, and whether
their writers had Proverbs 8 in mind (or later Jewish writings that developed it) is a question of
interpretation, not something the texts state. The wiki records the pattern and the uncertainty;
see [[Possessed in the Beginning - Proverbs 8.22-31, John 1, and Colossians 1]] and
[[In the Beginning - Genesis 1 and John 1]].

**Reading it after Job.** God's speeches in Job 38–41 ask Job where he was when the earth's
foundations were laid. Proverbs 8 gives the answer for Wisdom: *"I was there"*. Job was told he
could not know the order of creation because he did not see it made. Wisdom saw it, and she
offers herself to people. The two books do not contradict on this point. Job 28:27 says God
*"saw"* wisdom and *"established it"* at creation; Proverbs 8 says the same with Wisdom speaking.
Where they differ is in what follows for humans. Job 28:28 gives them the fear of the Lord and
departure from evil as their share of wisdom. Proverbs 8:35 promises that whoever finds Wisdom
finds life. Job's righteous suffering remains the case where finding wisdom did not keep the
finder from loss; see [[Retribution]].

## People

- Wisdom personified is the speaker, as at 8:1–21; she is treated on [[Wisdom]], not as a person
  page.
- [[God]] — *Yahweh*, named twice (8:22, 35): the one who possessed Wisdom at the beginning,
  established the heavens, bounded the sea, and gives favour to whoever finds her.

## Places

- No named place. The depths, springs, mountains, hills, heavens, and sea (8:24–29) are the
  created world, not locations; Wisdom's doors and doorposts (8:34) are figurative.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned 8:22–36 unit is kept. 8:22 begins Wisdom's account of her origin, and
8:36 ends the chapter and the speech; 9:1 opens a new scene, Wisdom's house and feast. Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah* marks a *petuḥah* after 8:31, so the Masoretic paragraphing groups
8:32–36 with what follows rather than with 8:22–31; the wiki keeps 8:32–36 here because it is the
conclusion of Wisdom's speech, opened by the father's own formula of conclusion (5:7; 7:24). The
title is the plan's, from 8:22–31.
