# Whoever Loves Correction

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 12:1–28

## Summary

The opening contrast makes a person’s response to correction a test of wisdom. The sayings then
follow that test through household honor, honest counsel, animals, cultivation, testimony, insults,
and anxious hearts. Righteousness is recognizable in what one does with words and with dependents,
as well as in the stability promised to one’s life. This is a collection of related short sayings
rather than a narrated event or a continuous speech with one argument.

**12:1–8 — Correction, roots, and households.** Loving correction means loving knowledge; the harsh
description of someone who hates reproof targets refusal to learn. Yahweh’s favor and condemnation
set the moral frame (2). The righteous root endures (3), a worthy wife crowns her husband (4), and
just thoughts and rescuing words contrast with deceit and bloodshed (5–6). The standing house of the
righteous and the commendation of wisdom close the cluster (7–8). The wife is an unnamed type; no
personal biography is supplied.

**12:9–14 — Provision, animals, and productive work.** Modest standing with actual resources is
preferable to self-honor without bread (9). Verse 10 extends righteous conduct to an animal under
one’s care: the standard is respect for its life, while even the wicked person’s apparent mercy is
cruel. Cultivation supplies bread where chasing fantasies fails (11). Desired plunder contrasts with
a flourishing root (12), and the next sayings connect the consequences of lips and hands: speech can
entrap or satisfy, while work receives its reward (13–14). The text does not turn animals into mere
illustrations of human speech; their treatment matters in its own right.

**12:15–23 — Counsel, restraint, and truth.** The fool trusts his own assessment; wisdom listens
(15). Overlooking an insult is prudent (16), but restraint is not permanent silence: truthful
testimony is required (17), and wise words heal where rash speech pierces like a sword (18). Truth
endures while lies fail (19). Plotting deceit contrasts with promoting peace (20), righteous safety
with wicked trouble (21), and Yahweh’s rejection of lying lips with his delight in truthful conduct
(22). Keeping knowledge prudently contrasts with a fool broadcasting foolishness (23); the chapter
distinguishes useful speech from indiscriminate speech.

**12:24–28 — Hands, hearts, friendships, and the path of life.** Diligence promises rule while
laziness ends in slave labor (24). A kind word can lighten an anxious heart (25). The WEB commends
caution in friendship against the misleading way of the wicked (26), then contrasts neglected game
with the possessions of the diligent (27). Righteousness closes the chapter as a road of life with
no death in its path (28). The statement does not explain a mechanism of immortality or narrate
anyone escaping death.

**The promises and their counterexamples.** Proverbs 12:7 promises that the righteous household
stands, and 12:21 says that no mischief happens to the righteous. Job 1:1, 18–22 narrates an upright
man’s children dying in a collapsed house; Job 2:3, 7 joins an explicit declaration of undeserved
ruin to painful bodily affliction. Job 21:7–13 describes the wicked and their offspring prospering.
These contradict an exceptionless reading of the proverbial outcomes. Reading Proverbs as general
moral instruction is the wiki’s interpretation; the verses themselves do not supply exception
clauses. Likewise, 12:24 cannot be reversed to diagnose every enslaved person as lazy: Exodus
1:11–14 attributes forced labor to oppression, and Job 24:5–11 depicts workers who remain hungry.
See [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]], [[Retribution]], and [[Slavery and Servitude]].

## Key verses

> **12:1** Whoever loves correction loves knowledge, but he who hates reproof is stupid.

> **12:2** A good man shall obtain favor from Yahweh, but he will condemn a man of wicked plans.

> **12:4** A worthy woman is the crown of her husband, but a disgraceful wife is as rottenness in his bones.

> **12:7** The wicked are overthrown, and are no more, but the house of the righteous shall stand.

> **12:9** Better is he who is little know, and has a servant, than he who honors himself, and lacks bread.

> **12:10** A righteous man respects the life of his animal, but the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel.

> **12:15** The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but he who is wise listens to counsel.

> **12:17** He who is truthful testifies honestly, but a false witness lies.

> **12:18** There is one who speaks rashly like the piercing of a sword, but the tongue of the wise heals.

> **12:21** No mischief shall happen to the righteous, but the wicked shall be filled with evil.

> **12:22** Lying lips are an abomination to Yahweh, but those who do the truth are his delight.

> **12:24** The hands of the diligent ones shall rule, but laziness ends in slave labor.

> **12:25** Anxiety in a man’s heart weighs it down, but a kind word makes it glad.

> **12:28** In the way of righteousness is life; in its path there is no death.
