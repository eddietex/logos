# A Good Name

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 22:1–16

## Summary

The last sixteen verses of *"The proverbs of Solomon"* (10:1) close the collection with wealth on
both ends. It opens by ranking a good name above *"great riches"* (1) and setting rich and poor
before one Maker (2); it ends with two ways of handling wealth that both *"come to poverty"* (16).
Verse 17 then turns to a new voice — *"Turn your ear, and listen to the words of the wise"* — and a
new heading begins the next queue item, so 22:16 is a real boundary, not a chapter convenience.

As in the rest of 10:1–22:16, this is a collection of sayings, not a speech. The groups below are
reading aids only.

**22:1–5 — Name, Maker, prudence, humility.** A good name is *"more desirable than great riches"*
and loving favor *"better than silver and gold"* (1): a better-than saying that does not despise
wealth but ranks reputation and goodwill above it. *"The rich and the poor have this in common:
Yahweh is the maker of them all"* (2). The verse states a common origin, not a common condition; it
does not say the gap between them is unimportant or will close, only that neither stands outside
the Maker's claim — the same ground on which 14:31 and 17:5 judge contempt for the poor as contempt
for God (see [[Contempt for His Maker - Proverbs 14.31 and Job 31.13-15]]). 29:13 will repeat the
pattern with *"the poor man and the oppressor"*. The prudent man sees danger and hides while the
simple pass on and suffer (3), a saying repeated almost word for word at 27:12 (*"takes refuge"*
there for *"hides himself"*). *"The result of humility and the fear of Yahweh is wealth, honor, and
life"* (4) joins the pair that 15:33 and 18:12 had placed *before honor*. Thorns and snares lie in
the wicked man's path, and whoever guards his soul keeps away from them (5).

**22:6–9 — The child, the lender, the sower, the generous eye.** *"Train up a child in the way he
should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it"* (6). This is the passage's most quoted
line, and the most often read as a guarantee. The collection's own form argues against that
reading: these are sayings about how things tend to go, set beside others that admit exceptions,
and the same book is full of scoffing, foolish, and shameful sons (13:1; 17:21, 25; 19:26). The verse states the
long reach of early formation; it does not promise that every trained child will stay, nor diagnose
every parent whose grown child departs. What *"the way he should go"* means — the right way in
general, or a way fitted to the particular child — is not settled by the WEB rendering, and the wiki
leaves it open. See [[Train Up a Child - Proverbs 22.6, Genesis 18.19, and Deuteronomy 6.7]].

*"The rich rule over the poor. The borrower is servant to the lender"* (7) is an observation, not a
recommendation: the verse describes the power debt gives, which is exactly why the Torah limits
what a creditor may do to a poor neighbor (Exodus 22:25–27; see
[[Lending Without Interest - Exodus 22.25 and the Poor Brother]]). *"He who sows wickedness reaps
trouble, and the rod of his fury will be destroyed"* (8) uses the harvest image Eliphaz had already
used at Job 4:8 — and used against a man the narrator calls blameless; see
[[Sow Wickedness, Reap Trouble - Proverbs 22.8, Job 4.8, and Hosea 8.7]]. *"He who has a generous
eye will be blessed; for he shares his food with the poor"* (9): the *eye* here is a disposition, the
opposite of the grudging eye, and the reason given is concrete — food shared.

**22:10–16 — Mocker, king, sluggard, pit, rod, and two roads to poverty.** Drive out the mocker and
strife, quarrels, and insults stop (10) — a social remedy, where 21:11 had made the mocker's
punishment a lesson for the simple. The one who loves purity of heart and speaks gracefully is
*"the king's friend"* (11), closing the collection's long run of royal sayings (14:35; 16:13; 20:28;
21:1). Yahweh's eyes watch over knowledge and frustrate the words of the unfaithful (12). The
sluggard's excuse is a lion in the street (13), repeated with variation at 26:13; the fear is absurd,
and that is the joke. *"The mouth of an adulteress is a deep pit"*, and the one under Yahweh's wrath
falls in (14) — the strange woman of chapters 2, 5, 7, and 9 reduced to a single image, which 23:27
repeats with *"a prostitute is a deep pit"*. The saying makes the fall a consequence of Yahweh's
wrath already resting on the man, without saying what brought it there. *"Folly is bound up in the
heart of a child: the rod of discipline drives it far from him"* (15), the third of the book's rod
sayings after 13:24 and 19:18 (see
[[The Rod of Discipline - Proverbs 13.24 and the Father's Correction]]). The collection ends:
*"Whoever oppresses the poor for his own increase and whoever gives to the rich, both come to
poverty"* (16). The two halves are paired — one takes from those with little, the other gives to
those with much — and both are told they lose. The verse does not say whether *gives to the rich*
means a bribe, flattery, or simply misplaced generosity.

**Tensions kept explicit.** Verse 4 promises wealth, honor, and life to humility and the fear of
Yahweh; Job, who *"fears God"* in the narrator's own words (Job 1:1, 8), loses all three in two
chapters. The saying states the expected outcome and is not a diagnostic of anyone who lacks it —
the limit the wiki keeps throughout the book (see [[Retribution]]). Verse 6's trained child and the
book's many foolish sons are both kept, and verse 8's sower is kept beside Eliphaz's misuse of
the same image. Verse 7's observation that the borrower serves the lender stands beside the Torah's
limits on lending and Proverbs' own repeated warnings against standing surety (6:1–5; 11:15; 17:18;
20:16), which the next pericope continues at 22:26–27 (see
[[Thirty Excellent Things]]).

## Key verses

> **22:1** A good name is more desirable than great riches, and loving favor is better than silver and gold.

> **22:2** The rich and the poor have this in common: Yahweh is the maker of them all.

> **22:3** A prudent man sees danger, and hides himself; but the simple pass on, and suffer for it.

> **22:4** The result of humility and the fear of Yahweh is wealth, honor, and life.

> **22:6** Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it.

> **22:7** The rich rule over the poor. The borrower is servant to the lender.

> **22:8** He who sows wickedness reaps trouble, and the rod of his fury will be destroyed.

> **22:9** He who has a generous eye will be blessed; for he shares his food with the poor.

> **22:11** He who loves purity of heart and speaks gracefully is the king’s friend.

> **22:13** The sluggard says, “There is a lion outside! I will be killed in the streets!”

> **22:14** The mouth of an adulteress is a deep pit. He who is under Yahweh’s wrath will fall into it.

> **22:15** Folly is bound up in the heart of a child: the rod of discipline drives it far from him.

> **22:16** Whoever oppresses the poor for his own increase and whoever gives to the rich, both come to poverty.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh is named in verses 2, 4, 12, and 14: maker of rich and poor, the one whose fear
  yields life, whose eyes watch over knowledge, and whose wrath lies behind the fall into the pit.
- [[Solomon]] — collection attribution from 10:1; this pericope closes the collection. He is not
  named in chapter 22.
- The prudent, the simple, the child, the borrower and lender, the mocker, the king, the sluggard,
  and the adulteress are generic figures; none receives an invented identity.

## Places

- None. The streets of verse 13 and the pit of verse 14 are images, not locations.

## Notes

**Boundary and attribution.** The planned 22:1–16 unit is retained. It is the end of the
collection headed at 10:1; verse 17 begins *"the words of the wise"*, a separate collection and the
next queue item. Exactly one queue item is advanced.

**Sources.** Every quotation from Proverbs, Genesis, Exodus, and Job is copied from the stored WEB in
`raw/`. Comparisons were checked in Proverbs 6:1–5, 10–11; 11:15, 18, 24–25; 13:1, 24; 14:31;
15:33; 17:5, 18, 21, 25; 18:12; 19:18, 26; 20:4, 16; 21:11, 24; 23:27; 26:13; 27:12; 29:13; Genesis
18:19; Exodus 22:25–27; and Job 1:1, 8; 4:7–9 (Proverbs 23–29 stored but not yet ingested). External
WEB verses quoted on the two new connection pages (Deuteronomy 6:6–7; Ephesians 6:4; Hosea 8:7;
10:13; Galatians 6:7) were verified at bible-api.com with `translation=web` on 2026-10-26. No
unreached book was fetched, and existing raw text is unchanged.

**Language and interpretation.** No claim about Hebrew wording is made in this pass. The link
between 22:6's *train up* and Genesis 14:14's *trained men* rests on a root claim made on
[[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]; it has not been re-verified against a Hebrew edition here.
Whether *"the way he should go"* (6) means the right way generally or a way suited to the child, and
what *"gives to the rich"* (16) refers to, are left open.
