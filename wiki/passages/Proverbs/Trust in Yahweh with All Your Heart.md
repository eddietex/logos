# Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 3:1–12

## Summary

The father's third lecture is the most compact in the book: **six pairs of verses, each a command
followed by its reason.** The odd verse says what to do; the even verse says what comes of it.
Where chapter 2 was one long conditional sentence ([[If You Seek Her as Silver]]), chapter 3 is a
row of short ones, and its vocabulary moves from the classroom to the temple: teaching,
commandments, trust, fear, honour, first fruits, discipline.

- **3:1–2 — Keep my teaching**, for length of days, years of life, and peace.
- **3:3–4 — Bind kindness and truth on you**, and find favour with God and man.
- **3:5–6 — Trust in Yahweh with all your heart**, and he will make your paths straight.
- **3:7–8 — Fear Yahweh and depart from evil**, and it will be health to your body.
- **3:9–10 — Honour Yahweh with your first fruits**, and your barns will be full.
- **3:11–12 — Do not despise Yahweh's discipline**, for he reproves the son he loves.

**The last pair breaks the pattern, and the break is the lecture's own.** Five times the reason
given is a good thing received — long life, favour, straight paths, health, full barns. The sixth
time the reason given is that Yahweh *reproves*. The edition checked for this page (Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*) places a *pe* paragraph marker after 3:10, so the Masoretic
layout itself sets 3:11–12 apart from the promises before it, and 3:11 opens with *bənî*, *my son*,
as 3:1 did. **Within twelve verses, Proverbs supplies its own qualification of the promise of full
barns**: the son whom Yahweh loves may receive correction instead. The wiki keeps this unit as
3:1–12 rather than splitting at the marker, because 3:11–12 still has the command-and-reason shape
of the five pairs before it and the next verse begins a new form, the beatitude *"Happy is the man
who finds wisdom"* (3:13). See [[Do Not Despise the Chastening - Job 5.17, Proverbs 3.11-12, and Hebrews 12]].

**Teaching and commandments, as at Sinai (3:1).** *"My son, don't forget my teaching; but let your
heart keep my commandments"* — *tôrāṯî … ûmiṣwōṯay*. The pair *tôrâ* and *miṣwâ* is the pair of
Exodus 24:12, where Yahweh promises Moses *"the stone tablets with the law and the commands"*
(*wəhattôrâ wəhammiṣwâ*). In Proverbs the speaker is a father and *tôrâ* is his *teaching*, as the
WEB renders it; the words are still the words of the law at Sinai, and two verses later the son is
told where to write them. *Keep* is *yiṣṣōr*, from *nāṣar* — the verb God and wisdom performed for
the son in 2:8 and 2:11. Now the son's own heart is to do it. And *"don't forget"* (*ʾal-tiškāḥ*)
answers the strange woman of the previous lecture, who *"forgets the covenant of her God"* (2:17,
*šāḵēḥâ*). See [[Law and Obedience]] and [[Remembrance]].

**The fifth commandment's promise (3:2, 9).** *"For length of days, and years of life, and peace,
will they add to you"* — *ʾōreḵ yāmîm*. The only commandment of the Ten that carries a promise of
long days is the fifth: *"Honor your father and your mother, that your days may be long in the
land"* (Exodus 20:12, *yaʾărīḵûn yāmeḵā*). The lecture, spoken by a father, promises length of days
to the son who keeps his teaching. And at 3:9 it uses the commandment's verb: *"Honor Yahweh"* is
*kabbēḏ ʾeṯ-YHWH*, letter for letter the form of *kabbēḏ ʾeṯ-ʾāḇîḵā*, *"Honor your father"*. **The
command to honour father turns, in this chapter, into the command to honour Yahweh** — and 3:12 will
call Yahweh a father. The link is the wiki's reading of the shared forms; the chapter does not cite
the commandment. See [[The Ten Commandments]].

**Kindness and truth must not forsake you (3:3).** *"Don't let kindness and truth forsake you"* —
*ḥeseḏ weʾĕmeṯ ʾal-yaʿazḇuḵā*. The pair is the one Yahweh proclaims of himself at Sinai, *"abundant
in loving kindness and truth"* (Exodus 34:6, *rav-ḥeseḏ weʾĕmeṯ*), and the one Abraham's servant
blessed him for at the well: *"who has not forsaken his loving kindness and his truth toward my
master"* (Genesis 24:27, *lōʾ-ʿāzaḇ ḥasdô waʾămittô*) — the same pair with the same verb, *ʿāzaḇ*.
Genesis says God did not forsake his *ḥeseḏ* and *ʾĕmeṯ*; Proverbs tells the son not to let them
forsake him. And *ʿāzaḇ* was the verb of both dangers in the last lecture: the men *"forsake the
paths of uprightness"* (2:13), the woman *"forsakes the friend of her youth"* (2:17). **The son is
held by what the wicked let go.** See [[Merciful and Gracious - Exodus 34, Numbers 14, and Jonah 4]]
and [[A Wife for Isaac]].

**Around the neck, on the heart (3:3).** *"Bind them around your neck. Write them on the tablet of
your heart."* *Tablet* is *lûaḥ*, the word for the stone tablets of the testimony (Exodus 24:12;
31:18, *luḥōṯ*). The first image belongs to the father's lectures — instruction as *"chains around
your neck"* (1:9), *"Tie them around your neck"* (6:21) — and the second recurs at 7:3 word for word
(*kāṯḇēm ʿal-lûaḥ libbeḵā*). The prophets take the image both ways. Jeremiah 17:1 says the sin of
Judah is *"engraved on the tablet of their heart"* (*ʿal-lûaḥ libbām*); Jeremiah 31:33 has Yahweh
promise *"I will write it in their heart"* — and the *it* is *tôrāṯî*, *my law*, the same word, in
the same form, as the father's *"my teaching"* at 3:1. **In Proverbs the son is told to write; in
Jeremiah God says he will.** Paul gives the last turn: *"not in tablets of stone, but in tablets
that are hearts of flesh"* (2 Corinthians 3:3). Whether the *"them"* of *"bind them"* is kindness and
truth, or the teaching and commandments of 3:1, the verse does not say. See
[[The Tablet of the Heart - Proverbs 3.3, Jeremiah 31.33, and 2 Corinthians 3.3]].

**Favour with God and man (3:4).** *"So you will find favor, and good understanding in the sight of
God and man"* — *ûməṣāʾ-ḥēn … bəʿênê ʾĕlōhîm wəʾāḏām*. *Find favour in the sight of* is the Genesis
idiom for Noah, for Jacob's pleas, and above all for Joseph, who *"found favor in his sight"* with
Potiphar (Genesis 39:4, *wayyimṣāʾ yôsēp̄ ḥēn bəʿênāyw*), and of whom it is said in prison that
*"Yahweh was with Joseph, and showed kindness to him, and gave him favor in the sight of the keeper
of the prison"* (39:21) — *ḥeseḏ* and *ḥēn* in one verse, as in Proverbs 3:3–4. Samuel *"increased in
favor both with Yahweh, and also with men"* (1 Samuel 2:26), and Luke says the same of the boy
Jesus: *"in favor with God and men"* (Luke 2:52). See
[[Favor with God and Man - Proverbs 3.4, 1 Samuel 2.26, and Luke 2.52]] and [[Favor and Grace]].

**Trust, with all your heart (3:5–6).** *"Trust in Yahweh with all your heart, and don't lean on your
own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight."* Four
observations.

- ***With all your heart*** — *bəḵol-libbeḵā*, the phrase of the Shema, *"You shall love Yahweh your
  God with all your heart"* (Deuteronomy 6:5, *bəḵol-ləḇāḇəḵā*, the longer spelling of the same
  noun). The Shema asks for love with the whole heart; Proverbs asks for trust with it.
- ***Don't lean*** — *ʾal-tiššāʿēn*. It is the verb Bildad used of the godless man: *"He shall lean on
  his house, but it shall not stand"* (Job 8:15, *yiššāʿēn ʿal-bêṯô*). Leaning is resting your
  weight; what you lean on is what you trust. Bildad's godless man leans on his house; Proverbs'
  foolish son would lean on his own *bînâ*, *understanding*. **The book that was written to give
  understanding (1:2) tells the son not to lean on it.** It is not a contradiction: the
  understanding wisdom gives is the kind that knows it is not the ground. See
  [[Bildad's First Speech]].
- ***Acknowledge him*** — *dāʿēhû*, literally *know him*. It is the verb of *"the knowledge of God"*
  that the seeker finds in 2:5. Knowing God is not only an end of the search but a practice *"in
  all your ways"*.
- ***He will make your paths straight*** — *yəyaššēr*, from *yāšar*, the root of *the upright*,
  *yəšārîm*, whom 2:7 and 2:21 promised a shield and the land. God makes straight the paths of the
  one who trusts him. Proverbs 16:9 states the same order as a sentence — *"A man's heart plans his
  course, but Yahweh directs his steps"* — and Psalm 37:5 as a command: *"Commit your way to Yahweh.
  Trust also in him, and he will do this."* Jeremiah 17:5–8 gives the curse and the blessing: the
  man *"who trusts in man"* is a desert bush, the man *"who trusts in Yahweh"* a tree by the water.

See [[Faith]] and [[Walking with God]].

**Do not be wise in your own eyes (3:7).** *"Don't be wise in your own eyes. Fear Yahweh, and depart
from evil."* The first clause is the book's warning against self-assessed wisdom, which it returns
to with some force: *"Do you see a man wise in his own eyes? There is more hope for a fool than for
him"* (26:12); *"One who trusts in himself is a fool"* (28:26). Isaiah makes it a woe — *"Woe to those
who are wise in their own eyes"* (5:21, *ḥăḵāmîm bəʿênêhem*) — and Paul an exhortation: *"Don't be
wise in your own conceits"* (Romans 12:16, *μὴ γίνεσθε φρόνιμοι παρ' ἑαυτοῖς*). That Paul's wording
follows the Septuagint of Proverbs 3:7 is commonly said; the Septuagint was not checked here. See
[[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].

**Job's description, as a command (3:7).** *"Fear Yahweh, and depart from evil"* — *yərāʾ
ʾeṯ-YHWH wəsûr mērāʿ*. The book of Job introduces its hero with the same two verbs: *"one who
feared God, and turned away from evil"* (Job 1:1, *wîrēʾ ʾĕlōhîm wəsār mērāʿ*), and closes its
wisdom poem with them: *"the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom. To depart from evil is
understanding"* (Job 28:28). What Job was, the son is told to be. The verse gives the reason in the
next line — *"It will be health to your body"* — and the reader of Job knows that the man who did
both was struck *"with painful sores from the sole of his foot to his head"* (Job 2:7). The tension
is the one flagged on [[If You Seek Her as Silver]]: Proverbs states the ordinary result; Job is the
case where it did not follow. **Flagged, not resolved.** See [[The Fear of God]],
[[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]], and [[Retribution]].

**Health to your body (3:8).** *"It will be health to your body, and nourishment to your bones."*
*Health* is *rip̄ʾûṯ*, *healing*, from *rāp̄āʾ* — the root of Yahweh's name at Marah, *"I am Yahweh who
heals you"* (Exodus 15:26, *rōp̄əʾeḵā*), given with a condition very like this one: *"If you will
diligently listen to Yahweh your God's voice … I will put none of the diseases on you"*. *Body* is
*šōr*, which elsewhere means the *navel*; the WEB generalises it, and how the Hebrew should be taken
is uncertain (the Septuagint is reported to read a different word, not checked here). *Nourishment*
is *šiqqûy*, literally *drink* or *refreshment*. The picture is of the fear of Yahweh as medicine
and water to the frame. Proverbs repeats the promise of the father's words at 4:22, *"health to
their whole body"*. See [[Healing]] and [[Bitter Water at Marah]].

**Honour Yahweh with your first fruits (3:9–10).** *"Honor Yahweh with your substance, with the first
fruits of all your increase: so your barns will be filled with plenty, and your vats will overflow
with new wine."* **This is one of the few places in Proverbs that tells the reader to bring an
offering**; the book mentions sacrifice elsewhere mostly to rank it below justice (15:8; 21:3, later
pericopes). *First fruits* is *rēʾšîṯ*, the word of the Sinai law: *"The first of the first fruits of
your ground you shall bring into the house of Yahweh your God"* (Exodus 23:19; 34:26, *rēʾšîṯ
bikkûrê*). *Substance* is *hôn*, wealth of any kind, so the father extends the harvest offering to whatever a man owns. *Hôn* last appeared in the
gang's invitation, *"We'll find all valuable wealth. We'll fill our houses with plunder"* (1:13, *kol-hôn
yāqār … nəmallēʾ*); here it is given to Yahweh, and the barns *"will be filled"* (*wəyimmāləʾû*, the same
verb, *mālēʾ*). **The gang filled houses by taking; the son's barns are filled after giving.** *Barns* is *ʾăsāmîm*, the word
of Deuteronomy's covenant blessing, *"Yahweh will command the blessing on you in your barns"*
(Deuteronomy 28:8, *baʾăsāmeḵā*). Malachi will make the same promise a challenge — *"Bring the whole
tithe into the storehouse … and test me now in this"* (Malachi 3:10). Jesus' parable of the rich
fool runs the other way: a man whose barns overflowed, who built bigger ones and said *"Soul, you
have many goods laid up"*, and was called *"You foolish one"* (Luke 12:18–20). See
[[Honor Yahweh with Your Substance - First Fruits in Exodus 23, Proverbs 3.9-10, and Malachi 3.10]],
[[Festivals and First Fruits]], [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]], and [[Wealth and Possessions]].

**Discipline, as from a father (3:11–12).** *"My son, don't despise Yahweh's discipline, neither be
weary of his reproof: for whom Yahweh loves, he reproves; even as a father reproves the son in whom
he delights."* *Discipline* is *mûsār* — the *instruction* the preface promised (1:2) and the fool
despises (1:7). *Despise* is *timʾās*; *reproof* is *tôḵaḥtô*, Wisdom's own word in the street,
*"Turn at my reproof"* (1:23). *Reproves* is *yôḵîaḥ*.

- **Eliphaz said this to Job.** *"Behold, happy is the man whom God corrects. Therefore do not despise
  the chastening of the Almighty"* (Job 5:17) — *yôḵiḥennû* and *mûsar … ʾal-timʾās*, the same verbs
  and the same noun. **The same teaching, applied to the wrong man, was a false diagnosis**: the
  prologue had already told the reader that Job's suffering was *ḥinnām*, *without cause* (2:3).
  Proverbs states the teaching for a son who can be trained by it; the book of Job shows it can be
  misapplied. See [[Eliphaz's First Speech]].
- **What Proverbs has that Eliphaz left out is the father.** *"Whom Yahweh loves"* (*yeʾĕhaḇ*), and
  *"as a father … the son in whom he delights"* (*ûḵəʾāḇ ʾeṯ-bēn yirṣeh*). The discipline is a sign
  of love and of sonship. Deuteronomy 8:5 gives the same comparison: *"as a man disciplines his son,
  so Yahweh your God disciplines you"*. And the speaker of the lecture is a father, so the
  comparison is spoken from inside it.
- **Hebrews quotes it, with a different last line.** *"My son, don't take lightly the chastening of
  the Lord, nor faint when you are reproved by him; For whom the Lord loves, he chastens, and
  scourges every son whom he receives"* (Hebrews 12:5–6, WEB; *μαστιγοῖ δὲ πάντα υἱὸν ὃν
  παραδέχεται*, SBLGNT). Where the Hebrew has *"even as a father"*, Hebrews has *"scourges"*. The usual
  explanation is that the Greek translator read the same consonants, *wkʾb*, as a verb from
  *kāʾaḇ*, *to cause pain*, rather than as *"and as a father"*; that is reported here and was not
  checked against a Septuagint edition. Hebrews introduces the quotation by saying its readers *"have
  forgotten the exhortation"* (12:5, *ἐκλέλησθε*) — the lecture had opened *"don't forget my
  teaching"*. Revelation 3:19 gives the risen Christ the same words: *"As many as I love, I reprove
  and chasten."*

See [[Do Not Despise the Chastening - Job 5.17, Proverbs 3.11-12, and Hebrews 12]], [[Suffering]],
and [[Love]].

**The name.** *Yahweh* is named five times in the lecture (3:5, 7, 9, 11, 12) and *God* once (3:4);
chapter 2 named him five times in twenty-two verses. **In this lecture the son's relationship to
Yahweh is the content of nearly every command**: trust him, know him, fear him, honour him, accept
his reproof. Wisdom is not mentioned by name until 3:13. See [[God]] and [[Wisdom]].

## Key verses

> **1** My son, don't forget my teaching; but let your heart keep my commandments:
> **2** for length of days, and years of life, and peace, will they add to you.
> **3** Don't let kindness and truth forsake you. Bind them around your neck. Write them on the tablet
> of your heart.
> **4** So you will find favor, and good understanding in the sight of God and man.

> **5** Trust in Yahweh with all your heart, and don't lean on your own understanding.
> **6** In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight.
> **7** Don't be wise in your own eyes. Fear Yahweh, and depart from evil.
> **8** It will be health to your body, and nourishment to your bones.

> **9** Honor Yahweh with your substance, with the first fruits of all your increase:
> **10** so your barns will be filled with plenty, and your vats will overflow with new wine.

> **11** My son, don't despise Yahweh's discipline, neither be weary of his reproof:
> **12** for whom Yahweh loves, he reproves; even as a father reproves the son in whom he delights.

## People

- *The father and the son* — speaker and addressee (3:1, 11). No pages, as in the earlier lectures.
- [[God]] — *Yahweh* five times and *God* once: the object of trust, knowledge, fear, and honour; the
  one who makes paths straight, and who reproves the son he loves, *"as a father"*.

## Places

- No places are named. *The house of Yahweh* to which first fruits were brought (Exodus 23:19) is
  implied by 3:9, but the verse does not name it.

## Themes

- [[Faith]] — *"Trust in Yahweh with all your heart"* (3:5), the trust of the Shema's *whole heart*.
- [[Wisdom]] — not named until 3:13; here the son's own understanding is not to be leaned on.
- [[The Fear of God]] — *"Fear Yahweh, and depart from evil"* (3:7), Job 1:1's description as a command.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — *tôrâ* and *miṣwâ* (3:1), the Sinai pair; written on the heart (3:3).
- [[Remembrance]] — *"don't forget"* (3:1), against the woman who forgot the covenant (2:17).
- [[Favor and Grace]] — *ḥēn* in the sight of God and man (3:4).
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — *"Don't be wise in your own eyes"* (3:7).
- [[Healing]] — *rip̄ʾûṯ* (3:8), the root of *"Yahweh who heals you"*.
- [[Festivals and First Fruits]] — *rēʾšîṯ* (3:9), the harvest offering of Exodus 23:19.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — *hôn* honouring Yahweh; barns and vats (3:9–10).

**Translations.** The WEB's *"teaching"* (3:1) and *"law"* (Jeremiah 31:33) translate the same form,
*tôrāṯî*. Its *"favor"* at 1 Samuel 2:26 is not *ḥēn*: the Hebrew there says the boy grew *wāṭôḇ*,
*and good*, *"both with Yahweh, and also with men"*. Its *"body"* at 3:8 generalises *šōr*.

**The unit.** 3:1 opens with *"My son"* after the *pe* marker at the end of chapter 2. The edition
places another *pe* after 3:10; the wiki keeps 3:11–12 in this unit for the reasons given in the
Summary, and 3:13 opens the next pericope with a beatitude.
