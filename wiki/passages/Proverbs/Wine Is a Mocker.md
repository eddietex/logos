# Wine Is a Mocker

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 20:1–30

## Summary

The opening saying personifies wine as a mocker and beer as a brawler, making the loss of sound
judgment the chapter's first warning. The remaining sayings repeatedly distinguish what people
claim from what conduct reveals: professions of loyalty, assertions of purity, a buyer's
disparagement, reliable counsel, and the king's ability to judge. Yahweh is the maker of
hearing and sight, the judge of dishonest measures, the one awaited for rescue, the director of
steps, and the one whose lamp searches inwardly.

This is a collection of short sayings, not a continuous speech. The groups below provide
reading aids; they do not claim that every neighboring verse belongs to a single argument.

**20:1–7 — Sobriety, restraint, work, and character.** Being led astray by drink opposes wisdom
(1); the saying warns about its power without stating a universal ban on every use. Royal anger
threatens like a lion (2), while ordinary people gain honor by avoiding quarrels (3). The
sluggard neglects winter plowing and finds nothing at harvest (4). Understanding draws counsel
from a person's heart as from deep water (5), so discernment requires patient work rather than
merely accepting assertions. Many claim unfailing love; finding the faithful person is harder
(6). Integrity then receives an intergenerational blessing (7). The warning about idleness
describes a particular cause of need, not all poverty: Proverbs 13:23 already attributes
destroyed provision to injustice.

**20:8–13 — Judgment, purity, and the created senses.** The seated king scatters evil with his
eyes (8), an ideal of discerning judgment rather than a claim that rulers cannot err. Verse 9
questions who can claim a purified heart and freedom from sin. It does not cancel verse 7's
righteous person or verse 11's recognition of pure and right conduct in a child. Dishonest
weights and measures are an abomination to Yahweh (10); even equipment can carry a deception.
Yahweh makes the hearing ear and seeing eye (12), grounding the faculties of discernment in
creation. Opening one's eyes instead of loving sleep brings bread (13), an admonition to action
rather than a condemnation of all rest.

**20:14–19 — Trade, knowledge, guarantees, and counsel.** The buyer dismisses the goods, then
boasts after departing (14); the contrast exposes speech tailored to advantage. Knowledgeable
lips are a rarer treasure than gold and rubies (15). Verse 16 addresses the person taking
security from a guarantor, naming a stranger and, in the WEB's second clause, a wayward woman.
It gives no permission to disregard Exodus 22:26–27's return of a neighbor's covering at
sunset. Fraudulent food changes from sweetness to gravel (17): dishonest benefit is judged by
its end. Advice establishes plans and guides warfare (18); neither the moral legitimacy of a
particular war nor guaranteed success is specified. The tale-bearer reveals secrets, so wisdom
also chooses its companions (19).

**20:20–25 — Parents, inheritance, revenge, steps, and vows.** The lamp of the person cursing
either parent goes out in blackness (20). Exodus 21:17 states a death penalty for the offense;
the proverb gives no procedure and does not expressly define the lost lamp as childlessness. An
inheritance quickly gained lacks final blessing (21), though the means of acquisition are not
specified. Instead of promising repayment of evil, the hearer must wait for Yahweh's salvation
(22). The promise is rescue, not explicitly the enemy's destruction. Differing weights are
condemned again (23). Steps come from Yahweh, raising a question about how fully a person can
understand the way (24); the counsel of verse 18 still matters. A rash dedication considered
only after the vow has been made becomes a snare (25). No vowed object or named votary is
supplied.

**20:26–30 — The ruler and the inward person.** A wise king winnows the wicked and drives a
threshing wheel over them (26), forceful judgment pictured through agricultural work. The
saying does not narrate a particular execution technique. The human spirit is Yahweh's lamp,
searching the inward person (27); this is an image of disclosure, not an explanation of an
infallible psychological faculty. Love and faithfulness protect the king and sustain his throne
(28), balancing terror and punitive force with a different basis of security. Young men's
strength and old men's gray hair each receive honor (29), without either age group being
declared morally superior. Wounding blows cleanse evil and beatings purge inwardly (30). Unlike
a saying expressly about a father and son, this verse names no child or agent of punishment in
the WEB.

**Tensions kept explicit.** Proverbs 20:7's blessing on righteous offspring cannot be an
exceptionless guarantee of children's survival beside Job 1:1, 18–19 and 2:3: the upright man's
children die, and God affirms his retained integrity. Job 21:7–9 also describes secure children
of the wicked. Verse 9's purity question cannot be used to nullify that narrative evidence.
Verse 30's cleansing blows likewise stand beside Proverbs 17:26's condemnation of flogging
officials for integrity and Job 2:3, 7's undeserved affliction. The purpose assigned to
correction does not establish that every blow is just or every sufferer guilty. These tensions
are recorded, not solved by supplying hidden wrongdoing.

## Key verses

> **20:1** Wine is a mocker, and beer is a brawler. Whoever is led astray by them is not wise.

> **20:5** Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water; but a man of understanding will draw it out.

> **20:6** Many men claim to be men of unfailing love, but who can find a faithful man?

> **20:7** A righteous man walks in integrity. Blessed are his children after him.

> **20:9** Who can say, “I have made my heart pure. I am clean and without sin?”

> **20:10** Differing weights and differing measures, both of them alike are an abomination to Yahweh.

> **20:12** The hearing ear, and the seeing eye, Yahweh has made even both of them.

> **20:17** Fraudulent food is sweet to a man, but afterwards his mouth is filled with gravel.

> **20:20** Whoever curses his father or his mother, his lamp shall be put out in blackness of darkness.

> **20:22** Don’t say, “I will pay back evil.” Wait for Yahweh, and he will save you.

> **20:24** A man’s steps are from Yahweh; how then can man understand his way?

> **20:25** It is a snare to a man to make a rash dedication, then later to consider his vows.

> **20:27** The spirit of man is Yahweh’s lamp, searching all his innermost parts.

> **20:28** Love and faithfulness keep the king safe. His throne is sustained by love.

> **20:30** Wounding blows cleanse away evil, and beatings purge the innermost parts.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh is named in verses 10, 12, 22–24, and 27.
- [[Solomon]] — collection attribution from 10:1; not named in chapter 20.
- [[Job (person)]] — a comparison for integrity, bereavement, purity, and undeserved suffering.
- Kings, children, parents, buyers, guarantors, strangers, the wayward woman, the sluggard,
  tale-bearers, young and old men are generic figures; none receives an invented identity.

## Places

No geographical place is named. The throne, field, market, and inward chambers are settings or
images, not identifiable locations requiring place pages.

- [[Work]] — Proverbs 20:4, 13; Neglected plowing and excessive sleep jeopardize provision.
- [[Love]] — Proverbs 20:6, 28; Love must be faithful; it also sustains the throne.
- [[Integrity]] — Proverbs 20:6–7, 9, 11; Character known by faithful conduct, without a boast of sinlessness.
- [[Righteousness]] — Proverbs 20:7, 9, 11; Righteous conduct remains recognizable beside the purity question.

- [[Kingship and Empire]] — Proverbs 20:2, 8, 26, 28; Royal terror, discerning judgment, threshing, and sustaining love.

- [[Sin]] — Proverbs 20:7, 9, 27, 30; No confident claim to a self-purified heart.
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — Proverbs 20:9, 11, 30; Moral purity language distinguished from ritual status.

- [[Creation]] — Proverbs 20:12; Yahweh makes the capacities by which people hear and see.

- [[Deception]] — Proverbs 20:6, 10, 14, 17, 23; False measures and buyers whose boasting contradicts their bargaining.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Proverbs 20:4, 10, 13–17, 21, 23; Honest acquisition, risky guarantees, and inheritance without blessing.

- [[Theft and Restitution]] — Proverbs 20:10, 14, 17, 23; Fraudulent gain condemned without a restitution tariff.

**Language and interpretation.** Analysis follows the stored WEB, including winter in verse 4,
the wayward woman in 16, and the human spirit in 27. No new claim about Hebrew wording or
textual variants is made; images of the inward person are not assigned an unverified
original-language anatomy. Existing connection pages retain their earlier source documentation

WEB, and the claim that it necessarily predicts no descendants is narrowed to a possible
comparison. Physical punishment is recorded as the saying describes it, without deriving a
