# The Plans of the Heart

**Book:** [[Proverbs]]
**Reference:** Proverbs 16:1–33

## Summary

The chapter opens and closes on the same claim: people make the plans, Yahweh decides the outcome.
Its first line is ***"The plans of the heart belong to man, but the answer of the tongue is from
Yahweh"*** (16:1), which gives this page its title, and its last is ***"The lot is cast into the
lap, but its every decision is from Yahweh"*** (16:33). Between them stand the first cluster of
royal sayings in the collection (10–15), the best-known line in the book, *"Pride goes before
destruction"* (18), and a run on speech, temper, and grey hair. As with chapters 10–15, it is a
collection of related sayings rather than an argument; the groupings below are reading aids.

**16:1–9 — The Yahweh sayings.** Chapter 15 named Yahweh nine times across the chapter. Chapter 16
gathers its references at the start: he is named in every verse from 1 to 7, and again in 9 and
11. Verse 8, a *"better than"* saying, is the only verse of the opening nine without his name. These
are mostly about the relation between what a person intends and what happens.

- *"The plans of the heart belong to man, but the answer of the tongue is from Yahweh"* (1). The
  Hebrew *maʿarkê-lēb* is the *arrangements* of the heart, the ordering of what to say, and
  *maʿănēh lāšôn* is the *answer* the tongue actually gives (Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra
  according to the Masorah*). Even when the speech comes out as the speaker meant it, the proverb
  credits Yahweh with it. The *answer* echoes the *"gentle answer"* that opened chapter 15.
- ***"All the ways of a man are clean in his own eyes; but Yahweh weighs the motives"*** (2). The
  Hebrew is *tōkēn rûḥôt*, *weighs spirits*. Proverbs 21:2 repeats the line almost exactly with
  *right* for *clean* and *hearts* (*libbôt*) for *spirits*, and 24:12 calls Yahweh *"he who weighs
  the hearts"*. Self-assessment is not the last word; the measurement is his. Job asked for exactly
  that measurement — *"let me be weighed in an even balance, that God may know my integrity"* (Job
  31:6) — and 1 Samuel 2:3 says *"By him actions are weighed"* (WEB at bible-api.com). See
  [[Honest Weights - Proverbs 11.1, the Torah, and Job 31.6]].
- *"Commit your deeds to Yahweh, and your plans shall succeed"* (3). The verb is *gōl*, *roll*:
  roll your works onto Yahweh. Psalm 37:5 uses the same imperative, *gôl ʿal-YHWH darkekā*, *"Commit
  your way to Yahweh"* (WEB at bible-api.com; Hebrew from Sefaria). The proverb is the one place in
  this opening group where human planning is *joined* to Yahweh, not set against him.
- ***"Yahweh has made everything for its own end— yes, even the wicked for the day of evil"*** (4).
  The hardest line in the chapter. The Hebrew *lammaʿănēhû* can be read *for its purpose* or *for
  his purpose*, and the WEB's *"for its own end"* chooses the first; the verse is not explicit about
  whose end is meant. What it does say plainly is that the wicked are not outside Yahweh's ordering:
  they have a *day of evil* that belongs to it. It is a statement about the scope of providence, not
  a doctrine of how the wicked became wicked, and the wiki does not draw one out of it. Exodus 9:16
  is the nearest narrative case — Pharaoh told *"for this cause I have made you stand"* — see
  [[For This Very Purpose - Exodus 9.16 in Romans 9.17]]. Job's complaint about the wicked's *day*
  runs the other way: Job 21:30 reports the claim *"that the evil man is reserved to the day of
  calamity"*, in a chapter whose argument is that Job cannot see that day arriving; see
  [[Why Do the Wicked Live]].
- *"Everyone who is proud in heart is an abomination to Yahweh"* (5) — the vocabulary of 6:16–19,
  where *"haughty eyes"* head the list of the seven. See
  [[Six Things Yahweh Hates - Proverbs 6.16-19 and the Ten Commandments]].
- ***"By mercy and truth iniquity is atoned for. By the fear of Yahweh men depart from evil"*** (6).
  The verb is *yəkuppar*, a passive form of the root *kpr* that Exodus and Leviticus use for
  sacrificial atonement, and the subject is not a sacrifice but *ḥesed we'ĕmet*, *mercy and truth*.
  The same pair is what the son is told to bind round his neck at 3:3 (WEB *"kindness and truth"*)
  and what Yahweh proclaims himself *"abundant in"* at Exodus 34:6 (WEB *"loving kindness and
  truth"*); the English differs in all three, the Hebrew pair does not (all read from Sefaria). The
  verse does not say whose mercy and truth atone, God's or the penitent's, and both readings have
  defenders; it says only that atonement comes *by* them. Set beside 15:8, where the wicked man's
  sacrifice is an abomination, the sentence collection is consistent: the offering is not what
  makes the difference. Proverbs 21:3 will say it outright: *"To do righteousness and justice is more
  acceptable to Yahweh than sacrifice."* See [[Atonement]] and [[Sacrifice and Offering]].
- *"When a man's ways please Yahweh, he makes even his enemies to be at peace with him"* (7).
- *"Better is a little with righteousness, than great revenues with injustice"* (8) — the third of
  the collection's *better than* sayings about small means, after 15:16–17. See
  [[Better Is Little - The Better-Than Sayings of Proverbs]].
- ***"A man's heart plans his course, but Yahweh directs his steps"*** (9). The second half,
  *wa-YHWH yākîn ṣaʿădô*, uses the same verb and noun as Jeremiah's prayer, *"It is not in man who
  walks to direct his steps"* (Jeremiah 10:23, *wəhākîn ʾet-ṣaʿădô*; WEB at bible-api.com, Hebrew
  from Sefaria). Proverbs states the division of labour; Jeremiah confesses it. See
  [[Man Plans, Yahweh Directs - Proverbs 16.1, 9 and 19.21]].

**16:10–15 — The king.** Here the collection turns to the king for the first time at length; 14:28,
35 had touched him. Six sayings in a row:

- *"Inspired judgments are on the lips of the king. He shall not betray his mouth"* (10). The noun
  the WEB renders *inspired judgments* is *qesem*, a word elsewhere in the Old Testament used for
  forbidden divination; this page has not checked those other occurrences in this pass, so the
  claim is offered only as the reason the WEB's choice is notable. Here it is applied, without
  censure, to the king's verdict. The second half has *bəmišpāṭ*, *in judgment*, which the WEB
  folds into *"shall not betray his mouth"*.
- *"Honest balances and scales are Yahweh's; all the weights in the bag are his work"* (11). The
  merchant's weights belong to Yahweh, not just the merchant's honesty; 11:1 had called a false
  balance *"an abomination to Yahweh"*. The scales are *mōʾzənê mišpāṭ*, *scales of justice*.
- *"It is an abomination for kings to do wrong, for the throne is established by righteousness"*
  (12) — *yikkôn*, the same root *kûn* as Yahweh's *directing* of steps in 9 and the plans
  *established* in 3. A throne stands the way a step does: by being made firm.
- *"Righteous lips are the delight of kings"* (13); *"The king's wrath is a messenger of death, but
  a wise man will pacify it"* (14).
- ***"In the light of the king's face is life. His favor is like a cloud of the spring rain"*** (15).
  This is the image Job used of himself at the height of his old life: *"They waited for me as for
  the rain. Their mouths drank as with the spring rain. I smiled on them when they had no
  confidence. They didn't reject the light of my face"*, and *"I lived as a king in the army"* (Job
  29:23–25). The Hebrew shares both terms — *malqôš*, the spring rain, and *ʾôr pānîm*, the light of
  the face. See [[The Light of the King's Face - Proverbs 16.15 and Job 29.23-25]].

These are sayings about a king in general, not about Solomon in particular, though the collection
is headed *"The proverbs of Solomon"* (10:1). The ideal they draw — a king who judges righteously,
loves truthful speech, and whose favour is life — is the one Wisdom claimed for herself at 8:15:
*"By me kings reign, and princes decree justice."* They also admit the king's danger plainly:
his wrath kills (14), and 19:12 and 20:2 will compare it to a lion's roar.

**16:16–19 — Wisdom, the highway, and the fall.** *"How much better it is to get wisdom than
gold!"* (16) — the price comparison of 3:14 and 8:10–11, 19, now put as an exclamation; see
[[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]]. *"The highway of the upright is to depart from evil"*
(17) answers 15:19's *highway* of the upright. Then ***"Pride goes before destruction, and a
haughty spirit before a fall"*** (18), and its pair, *"It is better to be of a lowly spirit with
the poor, than to divide the plunder with the proud"* (19). Verse 18 is the chapter's most-quoted
line and states the principle that 11:2, 15:33 (*"Before honor is humility"*), and 18:12 (*"Before
destruction the heart of man is proud, but before honor is humility"*) restate. It is also the
line the wiki has watched enacted: the builders of Babel who would *"make a name"* for themselves
(Genesis 11:4), and Pharaoh's *"Who is Yahweh, that I should listen to his voice?"* (Exodus 5:2)
before the sea. Isaiah 14:13–15 gives it its fullest form: *"I will ascend into heaven! … Yet you
shall be brought down to Sheol"* (WEB at bible-api.com). Verse 19 is a *better than* saying that
ranks poverty with humility above shared plunder: the alternative it rejects is the gang of 1:13–14,
*"We'll fill our houses with plunder… We'll all have one purse."* See
[[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] and [[Grace to the Humble - Proverbs 3.34, James 4.6, and 1 Peter 5.5]].

**16:20–24 — Instruction and pleasant words.** *"He who heeds the Word finds prosperity. Whoever
trusts in Yahweh is blessed"* (20) — the trust of 3:5, *"Trust in Yahweh with all your heart"*,
now pronounced a blessing. The WEB capitalises *Word*; the verse does not say whose word, and the
Hebrew *dābār* can mean simply *the matter* or *instruction*. Then four sayings on teaching by
speech: the wise in heart called prudent, and *"Pleasantness of the lips promotes instruction"*
(21); understanding *"a fountain of life"* (22), the WEB phrase of 14:27's fear of Yahweh, where 10:11 and
13:14 had *"a spring of life"* for the righteous mouth and the teaching of the wise; *"The heart of the wise instructs his
mouth"* (23) — where verse 1 set the heart's plans against the tongue's answer, verse 23 puts the
wise man's heart in charge of his mouth, and the two lines stand together rather than against each
other; and ***"Pleasant words are a honeycomb, sweet to the soul, and health to the bones"*** (24),
which 24:13 will extend to wisdom itself: *"eat honey, for it is good."*

**16:25 — A doublet.** *"There is a way which seems right to a man, but in the end it leads to
death"* is word for word in the WEB the same line as 14:12, which gave the page
[[A Way That Seems Right]] its title. Placed after 16:2 (*"clean in his own eyes"*), it gives the
same warning from the end of the road rather than the start: the way that seems right is the way a
man is clean in his own eyes about, and Yahweh's weighing is what it has not reckoned with.

**16:26–30 — The worker and the worthless man.** *"The appetite of the laboring man labors for him;
for his mouth urges him on"* (26) — hunger as the motive for work, observed without praise or
blame. Then four sayings that read as a portrait: the *worthless man* who devises mischief, with
speech *"like a scorching fire"* (27); the perverse man who stirs up strife, and the *whisperer* who
*"separates close friends"* (28); the man of violence who entices his neighbour (29); and the one
*"who winks his eyes to plot perversities, one who compresses his lips"* (30). Together they draw
again the *"worthless person"* of 6:12–14, *"who winks with his eyes"* and *"always sows discord"*;
10:10 has the winking eye too. Verse 29's enticing neighbour repeats the prologue's first danger,
the gang that says *"Come with us"* (1:11).

**16:31–33 — Grey hair, self-rule, and the lot.** ***"Gray hair is a crown of glory. It is attained by
a life of righteousness"*** (31). *ʿăṭeret tipʾeret*, *crown of glory*, is the phrase Wisdom's gift
was given at 4:9 (WEB *"a crown of splendor"*; Hebrew from Sefaria), and here old age itself is
the crown. Leviticus 19:32 commands *"You shall rise up before the gray head"* (WEB at
bible-api.com), and 20:29 will set the splendour of grey hair against the young man's strength. The
second half is a general promise, and Genesis has already shown both its keeping — Abraham promised
burial *"at a good old age"* (Genesis 15:15) and dying *"in a good old age"* (25:8) — and Jacob's fear of the other end of it, *"you will
bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to Sheol"* (Genesis 42:38; 44:29). See
[[Buried in a Good Old Age - The Promise at Genesis 15.15 and Its Fulfilment]]. Job's friends
invoked grey hair as authority — *"With us are both the gray-headed and the very aged men"* (Job
15:10) — and Elihu answered that *"It is not the great who are wise, nor the aged who understand
justice"* (Job 32:9). Grey hair is a crown in Proverbs; it is not, in Job, a credential.

***"One who is slow to anger is better than the mighty; one who rules his spirit, than he who takes a
city"*** (32). The divine attribute of Exodus 34:6 again, as at 14:29 and 15:18, here ranked above
military conquest. See [[Slow to Anger - Proverbs 14.29 and Exodus 34.6]].

Then the close: ***"The lot is cast into the lap, but its every decision is from Yahweh"*** (33). The
lot (*gôrāl*) was how Israel took decisions it would not take itself: Aaron cast lots for the two
goats on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:8), Joshua divided the land by lot *"before Yahweh"*
(Joshua 18:10), sailors found Jonah by it (Jonah 1:7), and the apostles chose Matthias by it after
praying to the Lord *"who know the hearts of all men"* (Acts 1:24–26) — all WEB at bible-api.com,
none yet ingested. Proverbs 18:18 adds its civic use: *"The lot settles disputes"* (see
[[A Strong Tower]]). The
*decision* is *mišpāṭ*, the same word as the *"breastplate of judgment"* (*ḥōšen hammišpāṭ*) in which
the Urim and Thummim were kept (Exodus 28:30; Hebrew from Sefaria), so the closing verse joins the
king's *mišpāṭ* (10), the scales' *mišpāṭ* (11), and the priestly lot's under one rule. See
[[The Lot Is Cast into the Lap - Proverbs 16.33 and the Casting of Lots]] and
[[Urim and Thummim - The Breastplate of Judgment and Later Inquiry]].

**The frame: plans and outcomes.** Verses 1, 9, and 33 make the chapter's frame, and the sayings
between them mostly fit it. Man arranges, Yahweh answers (1); man is clean in his own eyes, Yahweh
weighs (2); man plans, Yahweh directs (9); man casts, Yahweh decides (33). Verse 3 is the one
reconciliation offered — commit the works to him and the plans are established — and 19:21 will
put the frame in one line: *"There are many plans in a man's heart, but Yahweh's counsel will
prevail."* Genesis supplied the wiki's great narrative instance in Joseph's words to his brothers:
*"you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good"* (Genesis 50:20). See
[[Providence]].

**The promises and their counterexamples.** *"When a man's ways please Yahweh, he makes even his
enemies to be at peace with him"* (7), *"Whoever trusts in Yahweh is blessed"* (20), and grey hair
*"attained by a life of righteousness"* (31) are general sayings. Job is the wiki's standing
counterexample: a man whose ways pleased Yahweh (Job 1:8) and whose enemies, the Sabeans and
Chaldeans, were not at peace with him (Job 1:15, 17). As on the pages for chapters 10–15, the wiki
reads these as general instruction and keeps the tension explicit. **This chapter offers its own
qualification more than most**: if *"Yahweh weighs the motives"* (2) and *"its every decision is from
Yahweh"* (33), then outcomes are his to assign, and the frame of the chapter is exactly the ground
on which Job could not read his own case from the outside. See [[Retribution]] and
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

  16:10–15 is a type, not identified with him.

  the grey-haired are unnamed types.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The planned Proverbs 16:1–33 unit is retained. Chapter-sized units are the book page's
stated convention for the sentence collection, not a claim that the chapter is one original
discourse. Only this queue item is advanced.

**Source text.** All English quotations of Proverbs, Job, Genesis, and Exodus are copied from
`raw/bible/` (WEB). Raw text is unchanged. Quotations of Leviticus 16:8; 19:32; Joshua 18:10; 1
Samuel 2:3; Psalm 37:5; Isaiah 14:13–15; Jeremiah 10:23; Jonah 1:7; and Acts 1:24–26 were checked
against the WEB at bible-api.com in this pass; those books are not stored and were not fetched.

**Hebrew.** The Hebrew of Proverbs 3:3; 4:9; 16:1–4, 6, 9–12, 15, 31, 33; 18:18; 21:2; 24:12; Psalm
37:5; Jeremiah 10:23; Job 29:23–24; and Exodus 28:30; 34:6 was read from Sefaria's *Miqra according
to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25. The claims that rest on it are: *ḥesed we'ĕmet* in 16:6, 3:3, and Exodus
34:6; *yəkuppar* in 16:6; *gōl* in 16:3 and Psalm 37:5; *yākîn ṣaʿădô* in 16:9 and *hākîn ʾet-ṣaʿădô*
in Jeremiah 10:23; *tōkēn rûḥôt* in 16:2 against *tōkēn libbôt* in 21:2 and 24:12; *mišpāṭ* in 16:10,
11, 33 and Exodus 28:30; *malqôš* and *ʾôr pānîm* in 16:15 and Job 29:23–24; and *ʿăṭeret tipʾeret* in
16:31 and 4:9. That *qesem* (16:10) is used elsewhere for forbidden divination is not checked against
those passages here and is marked as such in the text. *Kpr* as the root of sacrificial atonement in
