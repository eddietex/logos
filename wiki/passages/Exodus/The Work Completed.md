# The Work Completed

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 39:32–43

## Summary

All the work of the tabernacle is finished, and the children of Israel are credited with
carrying out Yahweh’s commands to Moses (39:32). They bring the finished articles to him:
the tent and its structural parts and coverings; the ark and mercy seat; table and show bread;
lampstand, lamps, and oil; golden altar, anointing oil, and incense; bronze altar and basin;
court and its fittings; and garments for Aaron and his sons (39:33–41).

The narrator repeats that Israel has done all the commanded work (39:42). Moses sees the
work, recognizes its conformity to the command, and blesses the people (39:43). The making
account ends with recognition of communal obedience. The erection, arrangement, and filling
of the tabernacle remain the separate subject of chapter 40.

## Notes

### The whole inventory is an answer to the command

The planned 39:32–43 boundary is retained: the declaration of completion introduces the
presentation, and Moses’ blessing closes it. The inventory recalls the public commission in
35:10–19 and gathers the preceding detailed making reports into one whole. It includes supplies
such as bread, oil, and incense as well as durable structures and clothing. It is not another
metal-weight account like 38:21–31, and it gives no new measurements or cost totals.

The declarations in 39:32, 42, and 43 surround the inventory with obedience. These are three
framing notices in the checked WEB, not a newly verified count of a particular Hebrew formula.
The verdict does not require the instructions and making reports to be identical transcripts;
the descriptive differences already recorded in the earlier pages remain visible.

### Collective credit and a mediator’s inspection

The children of Israel receive credit for doing the work (39:32, 42). That does not mean each
person made every object: 35:25–26 and 36:1–2 distinguish skilled participants, and 38:22–23
names Bezalel and Oholiab. Neither craftsman is named in this unit. Aaron is named as priest
in the garment inventory; his sons are collective, identified from 28:1 without inventing
individual acts here. Israel is the people in this account, not a personal appearance of Jacob.

Moses has been the recipient and transmitter of the design. Now the work comes to him and he
assesses it. The blessing follows his recognition of obedience, but no words of the blessing
or inspection procedure are supplied. It should not be confused with anointing the objects or
ordaining the priests.

### After the calf, a different verdict

In 32:8 the people had departed from the commanded way; in 32:19 Moses saw the calf and
dancing and broke the tablets in anger. Here the work meets the command and his response is
blessing. The contrast belongs after intercession and covenant renewal. It neither denies
the earlier breach nor claims that gifts and craftsmanship bought forgiveness. Aaron’s ongoing
priestly designation likewise does not erase his responsibility for the calf.

### Finished, and still to be raised

Verse 39:32 and the later statement that Moses finished the work in 40:33 concern different
stages: completed articles brought for review, then erection and arrangement. Bread and oil
appear in the inventory, but their placement and use are separately reported in 40:23–25.
The anointing oil is delivered in 39:38; anointing is separately commanded in 40:9–15. No cloud,
manifestation of glory, or journey is narrated in this unit.

The completion, seeing, and blessing resemble Genesis 1:31–2:3 at the level of the verified
WEB narrative. Their order, subjects, and objects differ. This is a literary comparison,
not a claim of identical Hebrew vocabulary, a six-day building schedule, or an explicit
cosmic meaning assigned to every sanctuary component.
