# Water from the Rock

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 17:1–7

## Summary

**The third need of the wilderness is water again, and this time the complaint has a new question in it:
*"Is Yahweh among us, or not?"*** (17:7). Marah asked *"What shall we drink?"* (15:24); the wilderness of Sin
wished it had died in Egypt (16:3); at Rephidim the people *quarrel* with Moses, *test* Yahweh, and come close
to stoning the man who led them out. **God's answer is still not punishment.** He sends Moses ahead with the
elders and the rod that struck the Nile, says ***"I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb"***, and
water comes out of the rock when Moses strikes it. **The place is named for what the people did, not for what
God gave**: *Massah*, *testing*, and *Meribah*, *quarreling*.

The pericope moves in four steps:

- **The camp without water (17:1).** From the wilderness of Sin, *"by their journeys, according to Yahweh's
  commandment"*, to Rephidim — ***"but there was no water for the people to drink."***
- **The quarrel (17:2–3).** *"Give us water to drink."* Moses: ***"Why do you quarrel with me? Why do you test
  Yahweh?"*** The people murmur: *"Why have you brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us, our children, and our
  livestock with thirst?"*
- **The cry and the command (17:4–6).** Moses cries to Yahweh — *"They are almost ready to stone me"* — and is
  told to pass on ahead with elders and the rod, to the rock where God will stand. *"Moses did so in the sight
  of the elders of Israel."*
- **The naming (17:7).** *"Massah, and Meribah, because the children of Israel quarreled, and because they
  tested Yahweh, saying, 'Is Yahweh among us, or not?'"*

**It is the third turn of the cycle Marah began** — need, complaint, Moses' cry, God's provision (see
[[Bitter Water at Marah]], [[Manna and Quail]], and [[Murmuring]]) — **and the first in which the narrator
names the people's sin rather than God's test.** At Marah God *"tested them"* (15:25); in the wilderness of
Sin the manna was given *"that I may test them"* (16:4); **at Rephidim the people test God**, the first time
in Scripture a human being is the subject of *nissâ* with God as its object (17:2, 7; WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The direction of the verb has turned round.** See [[Testing]].

**Exodus does not punish it, and the rest of Scripture never forgets it.** Deuteronomy makes the place a
commandment — *"You shall not tempt Yahweh your God, as you tempted him in Massah"* (Deuteronomy 6:16) — and
Jesus quotes the first half of that verse in the wilderness (Matthew 4:7). Psalm 95 makes it a warning,
Hebrews 3 a sermon. **And Numbers 20 tells a second water-from-the-rock story, also named Meribah, that ends
with Moses barred from the land.** See [[Do Not Test Yahweh - Massah in Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and Hebrews]]
and [[The Rock That Followed Them - Exodus 17, Numbers 20, and 1 Corinthians 10]].

### Where it sits

| Elim | 15:27 | twelve springs, seventy palms | — |

**Two stories happen at Rephidim**, and the second begins in the same place with the next verse: *"Then
Amalek came and fought with Israel in Rephidim"* (17:8). **The rod is in both**: in Moses' hand at the rock
(17:5) and on the hill (17:9). **Sinai is next after Rephidim** — *"When they had departed from Rephidim, and
had come to the wilderness of Sinai"* (19:2; *not yet ingested*). See [[Rephidim]].

## Key verses

### No water at Rephidim (17:1)

> **1** All the congregation of the children of Israel traveled from the wilderness of Sin, by their
> journeys, according to Yahweh's commandment, and encamped in Rephidim; but there was no water for the people
> to drink.

***"By their journeys"*** — ***ləmasʿêhem***, *by their stages*. **Exodus goes from Sin to Rephidim in one
verse; Numbers 33 makes it three stages**: *"They traveled from the wilderness of Sin, and encamped in Dophkah.
They traveled from Dophkah, and encamped in Alush. They traveled from Alush, and encamped in Rephidim, where
there was no water for the people to drink"* (Numbers 33:12–14; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **The plural noun
may be the narrator's acknowledgement of the stops it skips.** That is a reading; the text does not say so.
Dophkah and Alush are named nowhere else in the Torah (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy).

***"According to Yahweh's commandment"*** — ***ʿal-pî YHWH***, literally *at the mouth of Yahweh*. **The camp
without water is the one God led them to.** The march is not Moses' mistake. Like Marah, three days beyond
the sea (15:22), **the need comes on God's road.**

***"But there was no water for the people to drink"*** — ***wəʾên mayim lištōt hāʿām***. **Numbers 33:14 keeps
the clause in its itinerary** — *wəlōʾ-hāyâ šām mayim lāʿām lištôt* (WLC) — **one of only two camps in that list
whose water is mentioned**, the other being Elim's twelve springs (33:9; WLC). *"Rephidim"* is named five times in the Torah: 17:1, 8; 19:2; Numbers 33:14, 15 (WLC
lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). See [[Rephidim]].

### Why do you test Yahweh? (17:2–3)

> **2** Therefore the people quarreled with Moses, and said, "Give us water to drink." Moses said to them, "Why
> do you quarrel with me? Why do you test Yahweh?"
>
> **3** The people were thirsty for water there; and the people murmured against Moses, and said, "Why have you
> brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us, our children, and our livestock with thirst?"

***"Quarreled"*** — ***wayyāreb***, from ***rîb***, *to strive, contend, bring a case*. **The verb's first
occurrences in Scripture are over water too**: *"The herdsmen of Gerar argued (wayyārîbû) with Isaac's
herdsmen, saying, 'The water is ours'"* (Genesis 26:20), and again at the second well (26:21) — then Jacob
*"contended (wayyāreb) with Laban"* (31:36) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **Isaac's quarrel was with
strangers over a well; Israel's is with its own leader over no well at all.** *Rîb* is also the word of the
lawsuit — the noun returns in the laws of the court, *"Neither shall you favor a poor man in his cause
(bərîbô)"* (23:3; WLC). **The people bring a case against Moses.** See [[Isaac in Gerar]] and [[Esek]].

***"Give us water to drink"*** — ***tənû-lānû mayim wəništê***. **The plural imperative, *give*, is addressed to
Moses** — and in the Hebrew, as in the WEB, it is plural; the text does not say whom besides Moses the people
mean. Aaron is not named in the pericope.

***"Why do you quarrel with me? Why do you test Yahweh?"*** — ***mah-tərîbûn ʿimmādî mah-tənassûn ʾet-YHWH***.
**Two parallel questions, and the second is the first explained**: a quarrel with Moses is a test of God. **It
is the same move Moses made in the wilderness of Sin** — *"Your murmurings are not against us, but against
Yahweh"* (16:8) — **now made in a single sentence.** **And it is the first time in Scripture a human being
tests God.** *Nissâ* has occurred three times before, all with God as the one testing: Abraham (Genesis 22:1),
Marah (Exodus 15:25), the manna (16:4); here it is *tənassûn ʾet-YHWH*, *you test Yahweh* (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). See [[Testing]].

***"The people were thirsty"*** — ***wayyiṣmāʾ šām hāʿām lammayim***. **The verb *ṣāmēʾ*, *to thirst*, occurs
only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy); the noun *ṣāmāʾ*, *thirst*, at the end of the
verse, appears again in the Torah only among the curses — *"in hunger, in thirst (ûbəṣāmāʾ), in nakedness"*
(Deuteronomy 28:48, by reference; WLC). **Deuteronomy 8:15 remembers the country itself as *thirst*** —
*ṣimmāʾôn*, *"thirsty ground where there was no water"* — and the rock: *"who poured water for you out of the
rock of flint"* (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).

***"The people murmured against Moses"*** — ***wayyālen hāʿām ʿal-mōšeh***. **The third *lûn* of the
wilderness, after Marah (15:24) and Sin (16:2).** At Sin it was against *"Moses and … Aaron"*; **here it is
against Moses alone again**, as at Marah. See [[Murmuring]].

***"Why have you brought us up out of Egypt"*** — ***lāmmâ zeh heʿĕlîtānû mimmiṣrayim***. **At Sin they said
*"you have brought us out (hôṣēʾtem)"* (16:3), the verb *yāṣāʾ*; here it is *heʿĕlîtānû*, the causative of
*ʿālâ*, *to go up*** (WLC) — **God's verb at the bush**: *"to bring them up (ûləhaʿălōtô) out of that land"*
(3:8; WLC). **The promise of the bush is turned into the charge.**

***"To kill us, our children, and our livestock"*** — ***ləhāmît ʾōtî wəʾet-bānay wəʾet-miqnay***. **The Hebrew is
singular** — *me, and my sons, and my livestock* (WLC) — **and the WEB, like most English versions, renders it
as the plural it means.** The whole people speaks as one man, as *"the people"* (*hāʿām*) is grammatically
singular throughout the verse. **The children and the livestock are the ones they insisted on bringing**: when
Pharaoh tried to keep them back, Moses said *"Our livestock also shall go with us. Not a hoof shall be left
behind"* (10:26). See [[Locusts and Darkness]].

### They are almost ready to stone me (17:4)

> **4** Moses cried to Yahweh, saying, "What shall I do with these people? They are almost ready to stone me."

***"Moses cried to Yahweh"*** — ***wayyiṣʿaq mōšeh ʾel-YHWH***. **The third time Moses is the subject of
*ṣāʿaq***: over the frogs (8:12 WEB; 8:8 Hebrew), at Marah (15:25), and here (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus);
and at the sea God asked him *"Why do you cry to me?"* (14:15). **He answers the people the way he did at Marah,
by turning from them to God.** See [[Prayer]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

***"What shall I do with these people?"*** — ***mâ ʾeʿĕśeh lāʿām hazzeh***. ***"These people"*** — ***lāʿām hazzeh***, *to this
people*: **the same two words as Moses' complaint after the bricks**, *"why have you brought trouble on this
people (lāʿām hazzeh)?"* (5:22; WLC). **Then he was protesting on their behalf; now he is asking what to do
with them.** The phrase will be used of Israel by God and by Moses at the golden calf (32:9, 21, 31; WLC; *not
yet ingested*). At the bush his questions were *"Who am I?"* and *"What should I tell them?"* (3:11, 13); in
Exodus's text this is **the first *"What shall I do?"*** he asks.

***"They are almost ready to stone me"*** — ***ʿôd məʿaṭ ûsəqālūnî***, *a little more and they will stone me*.
**The first stoning in Scripture was one Moses feared from Egypt**: *"shall we sacrifice the abomination of the
Egyptians before their eyes, and won't they stone us?"* (8:26 WEB; 8:22 Hebrew). **Here it is the second
*sāqal* in Scripture, and the threat comes from Israel** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **The next is God's
own law at Sinai** — whoever touches the mountain *"shall surely be stoned"* (19:13; *not yet ingested*). Joshua
and Caleb will be threatened with it in Numbers 14:10 (by reference, *not yet ingested*).

### I will stand before you there on the rock (17:5–6)

> **5** Yahweh said to Moses, "Walk on before the people, and take the elders of Israel with you, and take the
> rod in your hand with which you struck the Nile, and go.
>
> **6** Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb. You shall strike the rock, and water will
> come out of it, that the people may drink." Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel.

***"Walk on before the people"*** — ***ʿăbōr lipnê hāʿām***. **Moses is not told to answer the crowd or to hide
from it, but to walk out in front of the people who want to stone him.** The text records no rebuke of the
people from God at all.

***"Take the elders of Israel with you"*** — ***wəqaḥ ʾittəkā mizziqnê yiśrāʾēl***, literally *some of the elders*.
**The elders are the first audience Moses was ever sent to** (3:16, 18); they were gathered when the signs
were done *"in the sight of the people"* and *"the people believed"* (4:29–31); and they were the first to be
told to *"kill the Passover"* (12:21). **Here they are made witnesses.** The miracle is done *"in
the sight of the elders of Israel"* (17:6) — ***ləʿênê ziqnê yiśrāʾēl*** — **as the first plague was done *"in
the sight of Pharaoh, and in the sight of his servants"*** (7:20). **The signs of 4:30 were done *"in the sight of the people"* (*ləʿênê hāʿām*); this one the text
does not say the people saw.**
The elders are a collective here and have no page; they return at Sinai (19:7; 24:1, 9–11; *not yet ingested*).

***"The rod … with which you struck the Nile"*** — ***ûmaṭṭəkā ʾăšer hikkîtā bô ʾet-hayʾōr***. **The verb is
second-person masculine singular, said to Moses** (WLC). **That settles, in God's words, a question the first
plague left open**: at 7:19 God told Moses to tell *Aaron*, *"Take your rod"*, and at 7:20 *"he lifted up the
rod, and struck the waters"* with the subject unnamed. **Exodus 17:5 calls it Moses' rod and Moses' blow.** The
wiki records both and does not harmonize them: 7:19 gives the rod to Aaron, 17:5 gives the striking to Moses.
See [[Water Turned to Blood]] and [[The Nile]]. **The rod that turned the river to blood now draws water out
of stone.**

***"Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock"*** — ***hinənî ʿōmēd ləpānêkā šām ʿal-haṣṣûr***. **God
stands *before* Moses**, in front of him and in his sight. **No glory, cloud, or fire is mentioned**, as the
glory was mentioned in the wilderness of Sin (16:10). **What Moses strikes is the rock God is standing on.**
**The text does not say whether anyone besides Moses saw him.**

***"The rock"*** — ***haṣṣûr***. **The first *ṣûr* in Scripture** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus; Torah-wide,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Its next uses in Exodus are also God standing beside Moses on a rock**: *"there is a
place by me, and you shall stand on the rock (haṣṣûr)"*, and *"I will put you in a cleft of the rock"* while
his glory passes (33:21–22; WLC; *not yet ingested*). **Deuteronomy turns the word into a name of God** — *"The
Rock (haṣṣûr): his work is perfect"* (Deuteronomy 32:4, by reference; WLC) — and the word runs through the
same song (32:13, 15, 18, 30, 31, 37; WLC), at 32:31 and 32:37 for the *"rock"* of other peoples' gods. **The rock Moses struck becomes the title of the God who stood on it.** That
line is drawn by later readers, beginning with Paul (1 Corinthians 10:4); Deuteronomy itself does not mention
Massah in the song. See [[The Rock That Followed Them - Exodus 17, Numbers 20, and 1 Corinthians 10]].

***"In Horeb"*** — ***bəḥōrēb***. **The second *Horeb* in Exodus, after the bush (3:1).** **It creates a
geographical tension the text does not resolve**: Israel is camped at Rephidim (17:1), and only *"when they had
departed from Rephidim"* do they come to the wilderness of Sinai (19:2). **Readings reported, not adopted**:
*Horeb* names a region wider than the mountain, which Rephidim lay at the edge of; Moses and the elders walk on
ahead (*ʿăbōr lipnê hāʿām*) as far as the mountain; or the episode has been placed by theme rather than by
stage. See [[Horeb]].

***"You shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it"*** — ***wəhikkîtā baṣṣûr wəyāṣəʾû mimmennû mayim***.
**The water *comes out* (*yāṣəʾû*) — the exodus verb**, which the people turned against Moses at Sin (*"you have
brought us out"*, 16:3). **At Rephidim it is the water that goes out.** **The striking is commanded here**; at
Meribah in Numbers 20 Moses will be told to *"speak to the rock"* and will strike it twice instead (Numbers
20:8–11; WEB; *not yet ingested*). See the note at 17:7.

***"Moses did so"*** — ***wayyaʿaś kēn mōšeh***. **The miracle itself is not described.** The text reports
Moses' obedience and leaves the water to be inferred from the promise. **Psalm 78 fills it in**: *"He split
rocks in the wilderness, and gave them drink abundantly as out of the depths. He brought streams also out of
the rock, and caused waters to run down like rivers"* (Psalm 78:15–16; WEB; *not yet ingested*).

### Is Yahweh among us, or not? (17:7)

> **7** He called the name of the place Massah, and Meribah, because the children of Israel quarreled, and
> because they tested Yahweh, saying, "Is Yahweh among us, or not?"

***"Massah, and Meribah"*** — ***massâ ûmərîbâ***. **Two names, each built on one of the two verbs of 17:2**:
*massâ* from ***nissâ***, *test*, and *mərîbâ* from ***rîb***, *quarrel*. **The second half of the verse glosses
them in order** — *ʿal-rîb bənê yiśrāʾēl wəʿal nassōtām ʾet-YHWH*, *because of the quarrel of the children of
Israel and because of their testing Yahweh* (WLC). **Marah was named for the water** (15:23); **Massah and
Meribah are named for the people.** **The subject of *"He called"* is unnamed**; Moses is the natural reading.
See [[Names and Naming]] and [[Massah and Meribah]].

**Later Scripture uses the two names apart, together, and for two different places.** *Massah* alone stands for
this day in Deuteronomy 6:16 and 9:22; *Meribah* is also the name of the water at Kadesh in Numbers 20:13, 24,
which Numbers 27:14 calls *Meribah of Kadesh* (WLC); and Deuteronomy 33:8 puts the two in parallel. **Psalm 95:8
uses them as common nouns** — *kimrîbâ kəyôm massâ*, which the WLC tags as the nouns *quarrel* and *testing*
rather than as the place names (WLC) — **and the WEB renders them as names**: *"as at Meribah, as in the day of
Massah in the wilderness"*. See [[Massah and Meribah]] and [[Kadesh]].

***"Is Yahweh among us, or not?"*** — ***hăyēš YHWH bəqirbēnû ʾim-ʾāyin***. **The question of the whole
wilderness**, asked after the cloud, the sea, the manna, and the glory. ***Bəqirbēnû***, *in our midst*, **occurs
twice in Exodus — here, and in Moses' prayer after the golden calf**: *"please let the Lord go among us
(bəqirbēnû)"* (34:9; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus; *not yet ingested*), after God has said *"I will not go up
among you (bəqirbəkā)"* (33:3). **The question the people use as a test is the one Moses will later ask as a
plea.** **The answer the tabernacle gives** is *"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them"*
(25:8; *not yet ingested*). See [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].

**Who tested whom at Massah?** Three answers in Scripture, and the wiki records all three without choosing:

| Passage | Who tests | Who is tested |
|---|---|---|
| Exodus 17:2, 7; Deuteronomy 6:16; Psalm 95:9 | Israel | Yahweh |
| Psalm 81:7 | God | Israel: *"I tested you at the waters of Meribah"* |
| Deuteronomy 33:8 | God | Levi's *"godly one"*: *"whom you proved at Massah, with whom you contended at the waters of Meribah"* |

**Psalm 81:7 may mean the Kadesh Meribah of Numbers 20**, and **Deuteronomy 33:8 says nothing about the
people's sin at all** — in Levi's blessing the testing and the contending are God's, and its object is Levi's
*ḥăsîd*, his *"godly one"* (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). Which of Levi's men is meant (Moses and Aaron are the
usual candidates) the verse does not say.

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 17:1 opens a new stage and 17:7 closes on the naming; 17:8 stays in
  Rephidim but opens a new event (*"Then Amalek came"*), so the two are separate pericopes despite sharing a
  camp and the rod.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** *"the rock in Horeb"* (17:6) while Israel is camped at Rephidim, which
  it leaves before reaching Sinai (19:2); the rod of the first plague given to Aaron at 7:19 and called the rod
  *"with which you struck the Nile"* to Moses at 17:5; Massah as Israel's test of God (Exodus 17; Deuteronomy
