# Moses Returns to Egypt

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 4:18–31

## Summary

**Fourteen verses, three meetings, and in the middle of them the strangest scene in the book.**
[[Moses]] comes down from the mountain and asks [[Jethro]] to let him go back to his brothers in
Egypt, *"and see whether they are still alive"* — without a word about the bush. Jethro answers *"Go
in peace"* (4:18). Yahweh confirms the road: *"all the men who sought your life are dead"* (4:19).
Moses sets his wife and sons on a donkey and takes *"God's rod"* in his hand (4:20).

**Then God gives Moses the end of the story before the first request has been made.** Do the wonders
before Pharaoh, *"but I will harden his heart and he will not let the people go"* (4:21); and say to
him, ***"Israel is my son, my firstborn"*** — *"Let my son go, that he may serve me … Behold, I will
kill your son, your firstborn"* (4:22–23). **The tenth plague is announced here, before the first.**

**And then, at a lodging place on the road, *"Yahweh met Moses and wanted to kill him"*** (4:24).
[[Zipporah]] takes a flint, cuts off her son's foreskin, touches it to *"his feet"*, and says *"Surely
you are a bridegroom of blood to me"*; *"so he let him alone"* (4:25–26). No reason is given for the
attack, and the Hebrew does not say whom it was against — only *him*.

**The last movement is everything Moses said would not happen, happening.** [[Aaron]], sent by
Yahweh, meets him *"on God's mountain"* and kisses him (4:27); Moses tells him the words and the
signs (4:28); they gather the elders; Aaron speaks, the signs are done, and ***"the people
believed"*** — and when they hear that Yahweh has *visited* them and *seen their affliction*, they
bow and worship (4:29–31). **Moses' third objection was *"they will not believe me"* (4:1). They
do, at the first telling.**

### The shape of it

| | verses | where | who meets Moses | what is said or done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4:18 | Midian | Jethro | *"Let me go"* — *"Go in peace"* |
| 2 | 4:19–20 | Midian → the road | Yahweh | *"the men who sought your life are dead"*; wife, sons, donkey, rod |
| 3 | 4:21–23 | the road | Yahweh | wonders; *"I will harden his heart"*; *"Israel is my son, my firstborn"* |
| 4 | 4:24–26 | a lodging place | Yahweh | *"wanted to kill him"*; the flint; *"a bridegroom of blood"* |
| 5 | 4:27–28 | God's mountain | Aaron | a kiss; *"all Yahweh's words"* |
| 6 | 4:29–31 | Egypt | the elders and the people | the words, the signs; *"the people believed"* |

**The passage is a set of meetings, and two of them share a verb.** *"Yahweh met him"* at the lodging
place and Aaron *"met him"* at the mountain are the same word, ***pāgaš*** (*wayyipgəšēhû*, 4:24 and
4:27): one meeting is to kill and the other to kiss. **The pericope also runs the plan of 3:16–18
forward in order** — gather the elders, tell them *"I have surely visited you"*, and they will
*"listen to your voice"* — and every step comes off, except that Aaron, and not Moses, does the
speaking.

## Key verses

### Leave from Jethro (4:18)

> **18** Moses went and returned to Jethro his father-in-law, and said to him, "Please let me go and
> return to my brothers who are in Egypt, and see whether they are still alive." Jethro said to
> Moses, "Go in peace."

**Moses asks the priest of Midian for leave and does not mention God.** He has been commissioned at
length (3:10–4:17), but the reason he gives his father-in-law is family: *ʾēləkâ nāʾ wəʾāšûbâ
ʾel-ʾaḥay*, *let me go and return to my brothers* — **the same *brothers* he went out to at 2:11**,
twice in that verse. Why he says nothing of the bush is not explained. It may be reticence before a
foreign priest, a man who later has to be told *"all that Yahweh had done to Pharaoh"* (18:8), or
simply the narrator's economy. The wiki records the silence and does not read a motive into it.

***"See whether they are still alive"*** — ***hăʿôdām ḥayyîm*** — **is Joseph's question**, asked of
his father twice: *"Is he yet alive?"* (Genesis 43:27) and *"Does my father still live?"* (45:3).
**Like Joseph, Moses has been years away from the family he asks after** — forty, on Stephen's count
(Acts 7:30).

***"Jethro"*** — the Hebrew writes ***Yeter*** in the first clause and ***Yitrô*** in the second,
within one verse; the WEB normalizes both. See [[Jethro]] for the names.

***"Go in peace"*** — ***lēk ləšālôm*** — **is what Laban never said.** Jacob had to steal away from
his father-in-law (Genesis 31:20); Moses asks, and is sent. See [[Moses Flees to Midian]].

### The men who sought your life (4:19–20)

> **19** Yahweh said to Moses in Midian, "Go, return into Egypt; for all the men who sought your
> life are dead."
> **20** Moses took his wife and his sons, and set them on a donkey, and he returned to the land of
> Egypt. Moses took God's rod in his hand.

**The verse answers the fear of 2:14–15.** *"Pharaoh … sought to kill Moses"* (2:15) — ***bāqaš***;
*"the men who sought your life"* — ***hamməbaqšîm ʾet-napšekā***, the same verb — *"are dead"*. The
king's death was narrated at 2:23; now God draws the conclusion for Moses. **It is the one objection
Moses never voiced at the bush** — fear of the warrant still on him — and God answers it unasked.

**Why God says *"Go, return"* after Moses has already taken his leave** is a question of order the
text does not settle: 4:19 may be a further word given in Midian to confirm the departure (*"in
Midian"* is the narrator's note), or the narrative may run its scenes thematically rather than in
sequence. Both readings are recorded.

**Matthew uses the sentence of the infant Jesus.** An angel tells Joseph in Egypt, *"Arise and take
the young child and his mother, and go into the land of Israel, for those who sought the young
child's life are dead"* (Matthew 2:20). **The plural is Exodus's**: in Matthew only Herod has died
(2:19). Matthew has Jesus relive Moses' rescue from a king's massacre, his flight, and his return on
a word that the seekers are dead. See [[Every Son Who Is Born - Pharaoh's Decree and Herod's]].

***"His sons"*** — **plural, though only one son, [[Gershom]], has been named** (2:22). The second,
Eliezer, is named only at 18:4. **The family goes too**, set on a donkey (*wayyarkibēm
ʿal-haḥămōr*). By 18:2 Zipporah and the sons are back with Jethro, *"after he had sent her away"*;
when that happened is not narrated, and the wiki does not guess.

***"God's rod"*** — ***maṭṭēh hāʾĕlōhîm*** — **is the shepherd's staff of 4:2**, *"What is that in
your hand?" … "A rod"*, **renamed by what it has become**. It was Moses' at 4:2 and *"this rod"* at
4:17; from here it is God's. See [[Signs and Wonders]].

### My son, my firstborn (4:21–23)

> **21** Yahweh said to Moses, "When you go back into Egypt, see that you do before Pharaoh all the
> wonders which I have put in your hand, but I will harden his heart and he will not let the people
> go.
> **22** You shall tell Pharaoh, 'Yahweh says, Israel is my son, my firstborn,
> **23** and I have said to you, "Let my son go, that he may serve me"; and you have refused to let
> him go. Behold, I will kill your son, your firstborn.'"

***"Wonders"*** — ***hammōpətîm*** — **is a third word for what God does through Moses.** The signs
for Israel were *ʾōtōt* (4:8–9, 17); God's acts against Egypt were *niplāʾōt* (3:20); here they are
*môpətîm*, *portents*. 7:3 joins two of them, *"my signs and my wonders"*. See
[[Signs and Wonders]].

***"I will harden his heart"*** — ***ʾăḥazzēq ʾet-libbô***, ***I will make his heart strong*** — **is
the first hardening statement in Exodus, and God makes it before Moses has met Pharaoh.** Three
verbs carry the theme through chapter 14: *ḥāzaq*, *to be strong* (here); *kābēd*, *to be heavy*
(7:14; the word of Moses' *"heavy"* mouth, 4:10); and *qāšâ*, *to be hard* (7:3). **The order of
agents in the narrative is the old problem.** God says he will harden (4:21; 7:3); then Pharaoh's
heart *is* hardened, without an agent, or Pharaoh hardens it himself (7:13–8:32); and only at 9:12,
the sixth plague, does the narrator first say *"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"*. The wiki
records the sequence and the readings on [[Hardening of the Heart]] — a theme page created with this
passage — and adopts none.

***"Yahweh says"*** — ***kōh ʾāmar YHWH***, *thus says Yahweh* — **is the first occurrence of the
messenger formula with God's name in Scripture.** It is not in Genesis; checked against the
Westminster Leningrad Codex, Exodus 4:22 is its first instance, and its next is 5:1, Moses' first
words to Pharaoh. **The formula that will open the speeches of the prophets is first given to Moses
to say to a king.** See [[Prophecy and Vision]].

***"Israel is my son, my firstborn"*** — ***bənî bəkōrî yiśrāʾēl*** — **is the first time Scripture
calls Israel, the people, God's son.** Three things in it are new.

- **Sonship is said of a nation, not a king or a man.** The *"sons of God"* of Genesis 6 and Job 1
  are not Israel; Adam is not called God's son in the Old Testament. The relation is announced to a
  foreign king as the reason for a demand, and it is never explained or derived — not from creation,
  not from the covenant. **Hosea makes it the beginning of Israel's history**: *"When Israel was a
  child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt"* (Hosea 11:1). Paul lists *"the
  adoption"* first among Israel's privileges (Romans 9:4).
- ***Firstborn* is said of a younger son's name.** *Israel* was given to Jacob (Genesis 32:28), the
  younger twin who bought the birthright and stole the blessing. **Genesis spends six generations
  setting the firstborn aside; Exodus opens its account of the nation by calling the whole people,
  under the younger brother's name, God's firstborn.** Jeremiah does the same with the younger son of
  Joseph: *"I am a father to Israel. Ephraim is my firstborn"* (Jeremiah 31:9). See
  [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].
- ***"That he may serve me"*** — ***wəyaʿabdēnî*** — **is the book's one-word thesis.** Egypt made
  Israel *serve* (*ʿābad*, 1:13–14); God claims the same service for himself. **The demand is not
  freedom but a change of master**, and the master is a father. See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"You have refused"*** — ***wattəmāʾēn*** — **is past tense, of a refusal that has not happened
yet.** The speech is to be delivered after the refusal; the word returns in God's mouth when it has
(*"He refuses to let the people go"*, 7:14).

***"I will kill your son, your firstborn"*** — ***hōrēg ʾet-binkā bəkōrekā*** — **is measure for
measure.** Pharaoh's house killed Israel's sons (1:16, 22); God names Israel his firstborn son and
claims Pharaoh's. **It is fulfilled in 12:29** — *"from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his
throne to the firstborn of the captive"* — and Israel's own firstborn are thereafter redeemed because
of it (13:15). See [[Israel My Firstborn - Exodus 4.22, Hosea 11.1, and Matthew 2.15]] and
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]].

### The bridegroom of blood (4:24–26)

> **24** On the way at a lodging place, Yahweh met Moses and wanted to kill him.
> **25** Then Zipporah took a flint, and cut off the foreskin of her son, and cast it at his feet; and
> she said, "Surely you are a bridegroom of blood to me."
> **26** So he let him alone. Then she said, "You are a bridegroom of blood," because of the
> circumcision.

**Three verses, no explanation, and almost every noun ambiguous.** The scene follows directly on
*"I will kill your son, your firstborn"*, and it is placed there on purpose or not at all.

***"Yahweh met Moses"*** — **the Hebrew does not name Moses.** It reads *wayyipgəšēhû YHWH
wayəbaqqēš hămîtô*, *Yahweh met him and sought to kill him*; the WEB supplies *Moses*, as most
translations do. The candidates are Moses — the man just named in 4:21–23 — or the son, whose
circumcision ends the attack, and who has just been set beside Pharaoh's firstborn.

***"Wanted to kill him"*** — ***wayəbaqqēš hămîtô***, *sought to put him to death* — **is *bāqaš*
again, the verb of 2:15 and 4:19.** Pharaoh *sought* to kill Moses; *"the men who sought your life
are dead"*; and five verses later **Yahweh *seeks* his life.** The WEB's *"wanted"* hides the
repetition.

***"A flint"*** — ***ṣōr*** — **is the instrument Joshua uses for the second circumcision at the
border of the land**: *"Make flint knives, and circumcise again the sons of Israel"* (Joshua 5:2).

***"Cut off the foreskin of her son"*** — ***wattikrōt ʾet-ʿorlat bənāh*** — **is the verb of the
penalty.** The uncircumcised male *"shall be cut off from his people. He has broken my covenant"*
(Genesis 17:14) — *wənikrətâ*, *kārat*. **Zipporah cuts the foreskin off so that no one is cut
off.** This is the reading most commentators take of the attack: the son of the man sent to deliver
the covenant people had not been given the covenant sign, and Moses is held to it. The text never
says so; it says only that the circumcision ended it.

***"Cast it at his feet"*** — ***wattaggaʿ lərağlāyw***, ***she touched it to his feet*** — **is
*nāgaʿ*, *to touch*, not *to throw*.** Whose feet is not said: Moses', the son's, or the attacker's.
*Feet* is sometimes a euphemism in Hebrew, and some readers take it so here. **At the Jabbok the
assailant *"touched"* Jacob** — *wayyiggaʿ*, the same verb — the hollow of his thigh (Genesis 32:25).
See below.

***"Surely you are a bridegroom of blood to me"*** — ***kî ḥătan-dāmîm ʾattâ lî*** — **is the
centre of the riddle.** *Ḥātān* is *bridegroom*, or more broadly a man related by marriage — **it
is the root of the word that has just named Jethro, *ḥōtənô*, *his father-in-law* (4:18).** To whom
she says it — Moses, the child, or the attacker — and what it means are all disputed. Three standard
readings, none adopted:

- **To Moses**: the blood of the son's circumcision redeems the husband, and she claims him back as
  her bridegroom by it — *you are mine again by blood*.
- **To the son**: on a proposal often made, circumcision was once a rite before marriage (the
  related Arabic verb, commonly cited, means *to circumcise*), and she names the infant by what the
  rite made him. The comparative evidence was not checked for this page.
- **An old formula** that the narrator explains in 4:26b: *"You are a bridegroom of blood," because
  of the circumcision* — ***lammûlōt***, a form found only here — **the narrator himself treating the
  phrase as needing a gloss.**

***"So he let him alone"*** — ***wayyirep mimmennû***, *he slackened from him*. **The attack ends with
a blood rite performed by a Midianite woman**, and **it is the first time in Exodus that blood
averts Yahweh's killing**. The Passover will do it on a national scale: *"when I see the blood, I will
pass over you"* (12:13), and *"will not allow the destroyer to come in"* (12:23). The wiki records
this as a resonance commentators frequently draw, not a link the text states.

**The lodging place and the Jabbok.** Twice in the Torah a man whom God has just sent home is
attacked on the road — at the Jabbok by *"a man"* whom Jacob identifies with God (Genesis 32:30), and
here by Yahweh.

| | Jacob — Genesis 31–33 | Moses — Exodus 4 |
|---|---|---|
| the sending | *"Return to the land of your fathers … and I will be with you"* (31:3) | *"Go, return into Egypt"* (4:19); *ʾehyeh ʿimmāk* (3:12) |
| the leave from a father-in-law | stolen away from Laban (31:20) | *"Go in peace"* from Jethro (4:18) |
| the attack | *"a man wrestled with him"* (32:24) | *"Yahweh met him and wanted to kill him"* (4:24) |
| *nāgaʿ* | *"he touched the hollow of his thigh"* (32:25) | *"she touched it to his feet"* (4:25) |
| the outcome | a wound, a blessing, a new name | a wound to the son, a release, a name: *bridegroom of blood* |
| the brother | Esau *"ran to meet him … kissed him"* (33:4) | Aaron *"met him … and kissed him"* (4:27) |

**Both men are sent back toward a brother, attacked by God on the road, marked in the body, and then
met by the brother with a kiss.** The table records what the texts share; it does not claim that one
was written with the other in view. See
[[Wrestlings of God - Rachel at Naphtali's Birth and Jacob at the Jabbok]].

### Aaron meets him (4:27–28)

> **27** Yahweh said to Aaron, "Go into the wilderness to meet Moses." He went, and met him on God's
> mountain, and kissed him.
> **28** Moses told Aaron all Yahweh's words with which he had sent him, and all the signs with which
> he had instructed him.

**4:14 kept to the letter.** God said *"he comes out to meet you"*; now he is sent — *liqraʾt Mōšeh*,
*to meet Moses* — and *"met him on God's mountain"* (*bəhar hāʾĕlōhîm*). **Moses is back at
[[Horeb]]** — whether on his road to Egypt or as a deliberate return the text does not say, and since
the mountain's location is unknown, the geography cannot settle it. Either way the brothers' mission
begins where the call did.

***"And kissed him"*** — ***wayyiššaq-lô***. Esau kissed Jacob after twenty years of a murder threat
(Genesis 33:4); Joseph kissed his brothers after they had sold him (45:15). **Aaron kisses Moses with
no quarrel to get past** — the gladness God foretold (4:14). See [[Brotherhood]].

***"All Yahweh's words with which he had sent him"*** — ***ʾăšer šəlāḥô*** — ***šālaḥ*** **once more,
the verb of the sending and of *"Let my son go"*.** Moses does at once what God said Aaron's office
would be: *"You shall speak to him, and put the words in his mouth"* (4:15).

### The people believed (4:29–31)

> **29** Moses and Aaron went and gathered together all the elders of the children of Israel.
> **30** Aaron spoke all the words which Yahweh had spoken to Moses, and did the signs in the sight
> of the people.
> **31** The people believed, and when they heard that Yahweh had visited the children of Israel, and
> that he had seen their affliction, then they bowed their heads and worshiped.

**Moses says nothing in these three verses.** He gathers the elders with Aaron, but **Aaron speaks
and Aaron does the signs** — the grammar keeps Aaron as the subject of *"did"* (*wayyaʿaś*) — signs
that were given to Moses, in Moses' hand, at the bush. **The man who was *"heavy of mouth"* has his
mouth, and it is his brother.**

***"The people believed"*** — ***wayyaʾămēn hāʿām*** — **answers the third objection word for word.**
*"Behold, they will not believe me"* (4:1): *ʾāman* came five times in 4:1–9, all of it provision for
disbelief, and the signs were given so that *"they may believe"* (4:5). **They believe at the first
telling.** The same verb returns at the sea, enlarged: *"they believed in Yahweh, and in his servant
Moses"* (14:31). See [[Faith]].

**The belief does not last a chapter.** After Pharaoh's answer the officers say to Moses and Aaron,
*"May Yahweh look at you, and judge"* (5:21), and at 6:9 *"they didn't listen to Moses for anguish of
spirit, and for cruel bondage"*. **This verse is the high point of Israel's faith before the sea, and
it is reached before anything has been done.**

***"Yahweh had visited the children of Israel"*** — ***pāqad*** — **is Joseph's word, delivered.**
*"God will surely visit you"* (Genesis 50:24), *"I have surely visited you"* (Exodus 3:16), and now the
people hear it. See [[Yahweh Visited Sarah - Paqad and the Visitations of God]].

***"That he had seen their affliction"*** — ***rāʾâ ʾet-ʿonyām*** — **is 3:7 in the third person**:
*"I have surely seen the affliction of my people"*. **The people's reason for worship is the same
pair of verbs, *seeing* and *visiting*, with which the narrator and God have spoken of them** (2:25;
3:7, 16). See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

***"They bowed their heads and worshiped"*** — ***wayyiqqədû wayyištaḥăwû*** — **is the posture of
Abraham's servant at the well when his prayer was answered** (*"The man bowed his head, and worshiped
Yahweh"*, Genesis 24:26), **and of the people again at the first Passover** (*"The people bowed their
heads and worshiped"*, Exodus 12:27). **The first act of the people of Israel as a people toward God
in Exodus is worship** — and it is done in Egypt, before the three days' journey asked for *"that we
may sacrifice to Yahweh"* (3:18).

**And it reverses 2:14.** Forty years before, a Hebrew answered Moses' first intervention with *"Who
made you a prince and a judge over us?"* **Now the elders of the same people accept the commission
without a question.** The difference is the one the bush made: Moses comes this time sent. See
[[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].

## The three sons

**The passage is held together by sons.** Moses' *sons* go on the donkey (4:20); **Israel** is God's
*son*, his *firstborn* (4:22–23); **Pharaoh's** *son*, his *firstborn*, is to die (4:23); and
**Zipporah's** *son* is cut for the life of — whoever it was (4:25). **The firstborn of God, the
firstborn of Pharaoh, and the firstborn of Moses are named within four verses of each other**, and
only the last is touched by blood in this chapter. Whether the juxtaposition means that Moses' house
had to bear the covenant's sign before he could demand God's son from Pharaoh's house, the text does
not say; it is the reading that best explains why the scene is here, and the wiki records it as that.

## People

- [[Moses]] — takes leave of Jethro, sets out with his family and God's rod, is told the end of the
  contest, is attacked at the lodging place, meets Aaron, and gathers the elders (4:18–31)
- [[God]] — confirms the return; names Israel his firstborn and announces the death of Pharaoh's;
  promises to harden Pharaoh's heart; *"met him and wanted to kill him"*; sends Aaron
- [[Jethro]] — *"Go in peace"*; named *Yeter* and *Yitrô* in one verse (4:18)
- [[Zipporah]] — takes a flint, circumcises her son, and speaks her only words in Scripture:
  *"a bridegroom of blood"* (4:25–26)
- [[Gershom]] — one of *"his sons"* (4:20); the son circumcised at 4:25, if he is the one meant
- [[Aaron]] — sent by Yahweh, meets Moses at the mountain, kisses him, and speaks and does the signs
  before the people (4:27–30)
- [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] — whose heart God will harden and whose firstborn God will kill
  (4:21–23)
- *Eliezer* — Moses' second son, implied by *"his sons"* (4:20) but named only at 18:4; no page until
  then
- *The elders of Israel* (4:29) and *the people* (4:30–31) — a body, not given a page

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** 4:18 changes scene from the mountain to Jethro's house; 5:1 opens the
  first audience with Pharaoh.
- **Whom Yahweh attacked (4:24)** — the Hebrew has only *him*; Moses (WEB, most translations) or the
  son. Both recorded; none adopted.
- **Why the attack** — the uncircumcised son (the majority reading, from Genesis 17:14 and the
  remedy); the deliverer's own unfitness; or an apotropaic rite whose reason the narrator did not
  know or did not give. Recorded; none adopted.
- ***Ḥătan-dāmîm*** — three readings (to Moses, to the son, an old formula glossed in 4:26b);
  none adopted.
- **The hardening** — the sequence of agents is recorded on [[Hardening of the Heart]], counted from
  the Westminster Leningrad Codex; the theological readings are recorded there and not adopted.
- **The order of 4:18 and 4:19** — recorded as an open question.
- **Hebrew forms on this page were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Open
  Scriptures Hebrew Bible, `morphhb`), including *Yeter* (4:18), the unnamed object of 4:24,
  *nāgaʿ* (4:25; Genesis 32:25), *kārat* (4:25; Genesis 17:14), *bāqaš* (2:15; 4:19, 24), *pāgaš*
  (4:24, 27), and the first *kōh ʾāmar YHWH* (4:22; not in Genesis or Job). Hebrew verse numbers in
  Genesis 32 run one ahead of the English.
- **First occurrences claimed on this page** — the messenger formula (4:22), Israel as God's son
  (4:22), blood averting Yahweh's killing (4:25–26) — were checked against Genesis and Exodus 1–4 in
  `raw/bible/` and the WLC.
