# The Altar and the Court

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 27:1–21

## Summary

God continues the instructions to Moses with an altar of acacia wood overlaid with brass,
five cubits square and three high. Its horns belong to the same piece; its utensils, grating,
and carrying poles are specified. The altar is hollow and must follow what Moses was shown
on the mountain (27:1–8). This is a command to make it, not a report of its construction.

A court one hundred cubits long and fifty wide surrounds the tabernacle. Linen hangings five
cubits high are supported by pillars with brass sockets and silver fittings. The east side has
a twenty-cubit embroidered entrance screen between two fifteen-cubit lengths of hanging
(27:9–19). The court adds an outer enclosure to the tent and its inner veil.

The closing command moves from making the sanctuary to maintaining its light. Israel must supply
pure beaten olive oil; Aaron and his sons must tend the lamp outside the veil, from evening to
morning before Yahweh, as an enduring statute for the people (27:20–21). These two verses bridge
the furniture instructions and the priestly appointments of chapter 28. Neither the altar's
sacrificial ritual nor the priests' ordination is carried out here.

## Notes

### A portable altar, without a complete reconstruction

The five-by-five-cubit altar is three cubits high and has four horns. Utensils, a brass grating,
and rings are specified. Poles go through the rings when it is carried (27:1–8).
The text locates the grating under the ledge, reaching halfway up, but does not supply every
detail of the fire surface or explain how the wood is protected. It calls for hollow planks;
it does not say to fill the altar with earth. No modern dimensions or exact engineering
reconstruction are asserted here.

The WEB uses *brass* repeatedly and *bronze rings* at 27:4. Those are the translation's labels;
this page makes no claim about alloy composition or an exact Hebrew term. The horns receive
no ritual explanation in this unit. The gold incense altar of 30:1–10 is a different object,
with different dimensions and use. Exodus 38:1 and 40:29 identify this larger altar with
burnt offerings, and 40:6–8 places it outside the tent entrance within the court.

### Two altar commands remain in view

Exodus 20:24 commands an altar of earth, while 20:25 permits uncut stone and says a tool would
pollute it. Exodus 27:1–8 commands skilled fabrication in wood and metal. These provisions
differ materially; neither should overwrite the other. A distinction between local altars
and the portable sanctuary altar is a contextual explanation, but Exodus does not expressly
formulate it. A proposed earth filling would also require information this chapter omits.
See [[An Altar of Uncut Stones - Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 27, and Joshua 8]].

### The outer entrance is a third hanging

The long sides are each one hundred cubits, the west fifty, and the east fifty divided as
fifteen plus twenty plus fifteen (27:9–18). The twenty-cubit screen is the gate of the court,
not the entrance to the tent or the inner veil. Like the tent entrance screen it is embroidered
with blue, purple, scarlet, and linen; unlike the inner veil, no cherubim are specified for it.
The court pillars' sockets are brass and their hooks and fillets silver. The text does not
specify the material of the court pillar shafts, so chapter 26's acacia pillars should not
silently fill that gap. No symbolic interpretation of the colors, metals, or measurements is
supplied by this chapter.

### A lamp rule at the transition to priesthood

The planned pericope is kept intact. Verses 20–21 return from the outer court to the lamp
inside the tent but outside the veil. They join the construction instructions to the service
of the family whose garments are prescribed next. Oil was already requested in 25:6; now its
quality and its providers are stated, and its attendants are named.

The command to burn continually and the explicit evening-to-morning schedule must be read
together. The page records the required nightly tending without claiming these verses alone
prove either uninterrupted twenty-four-hour burning or daytime extinguishing. Exodus 30:7–8
later mentions morning tending and evening lighting. The near repetition in Leviticus 24:1–4
names Aaron, where Exodus says Aaron and his sons, and explicitly names the lamps on the pure
gold lampstand. Neither passage explains oil as a symbol of the Spirit; the later prophetic
lampstand image is discussed separately in
[[Seven Lamps - The Lampstand in Zechariah 4 and Revelation 1]].
