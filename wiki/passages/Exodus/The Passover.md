# The Passover

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 12:1–28

## Summary

**The story stops on the eve of the last blow and turns into law.** Chapter 11 ended with the death of
the firstborn announced and Moses gone out *"in hot anger"*. Chapter 12 does not go on to midnight. It
goes back to God speaking *"to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt"* (12:1), and what he gives them is
a calendar, a meal, and a rule for a festival — **the first law given to Israel as a people is given
while they are still slaves, before the event it commemorates has happened.**

**God to Moses and Aaron (12:1–20).** *"This month shall be to you the beginning of months"* (12:2). On
the tenth day every household takes a lamb — a male, a year old, *"without defect"*, sheep or goat,
shared with a neighbour if the household is small (12:3–5). On the fourteenth, at evening, *"the whole
assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it"* (12:6). Its blood goes on the two doorposts and
the lintel; its meat is eaten that night, roasted whole, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, and
nothing is left to morning (12:7–10). **They eat dressed to leave**: *"with your belt on your waist,
your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and you shall eat it in haste: it is Yahweh's
Passover"* (12:11). That night God will go through Egypt and strike every firstborn, *"Against all the
gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am Yahweh"* (12:12); and ***"when I see the blood, I will pass
over you"*** (12:13). The day becomes *"a memorial"* and *"a feast to Yahweh … forever"* (12:14). Then a
second rite, seven days of unleavened bread, with leaven put out of the house, a *"holy convocation"*
on the first day and the seventh, no work, and a penalty: whoever eats leaven *"shall be cut off from
Israel"* (12:15–20).

**Moses to the elders (12:21–27).** Moses passes it on, and adds what God did not say: hyssop to apply
the blood, *"none of you shall go out of the door of his house until the morning"* (12:22), and **a name
for the agent at the door** — *"Yahweh will pass over the door, and will not allow the destroyer to come
in to your houses to strike you"* (12:23). The rite is to be kept in the land to come (12:25), and when
the children ask *"What do you mean by this service?"*, the parents answer: ***"It is the sacrifice of
Yahweh's Passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt, when he struck the
Egyptians, and spared our houses"*** (12:26–27).

**The people (12:27–28).** *"The people bowed their heads and worshiped"* — the response of 4:31 — and
*"as Yahweh had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did"* (12:28). **They have done nothing yet except
agree.** The lamb is not yet killed when the pericope ends; the blow falls at 12:29.

### The shape of it

| | 12:1–14 | 12:15–20 | 12:21–27 | 12:27b–28 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speaker | Yahweh to Moses and Aaron | Yahweh to Moses and Aaron | Moses to the elders | the narrator |
| Subject | the lamb, the blood, the meal | seven days of unleavened bread | the blood applied; the children's question | worship and obedience |
| Time | that night, and *"forever"* | *"throughout your generations"* | *"when you have come to the land"* | now |
| Says why | *"I will pass over you"* (12:13) | *"in this same day I have brought your armies out"* (12:17) | *"he … spared our houses"* (12:27) | — |

**Three audiences are addressed at once.** The slaves in Egypt are told what to do tonight. Their
descendants are told to keep it *"throughout your generations"* (12:14, 17). And the reader is told what
the children will ask (12:26). **12:17 speaks of the exodus as already done** — *"in this same day I
have brought (hôṣēʾtî) your armies out"* — though in the story it is still hours away. The festival is
written from the far side of the event, and the event has not happened yet.

## Key verses

### The beginning of months (12:1–2)

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying,
> **2** "This month shall be to you the beginning of months. It shall be the first month of the year to
> you.

***"In the land of Egypt"*** — *bəʾereṣ miṣrāyim*. Nearly every later law Israel receives is given
at Sinai, in the wilderness, or on the plains of Moab. **This one is given in the land of bondage**, and the verse
says so as if the location were part of the law.

***"The beginning of months"*** — ***rōʾš ḥŏdāšîm*** (WLC). **Israel's year is renumbered from the
night it leaves Egypt.** The month is not named here; at 13:4 it is *"the month Abib"*, and Deuteronomy
16:1 opens the Passover law with it: *"Observe the month of Abib, and keep the Passover to Yahweh your
God; for in the month of Abib Yahweh your God brought you out of Egypt by night"* (WEB). Genesis gave the
lights *"for signs to mark seasons, days, and years"* (Genesis 1:14); **here, for the first time, God fixes a date in
Israel's calendar by an event in its history** rather than by the sky. See [[Creation]] and
[[Remembrance]].

### A lamb for a household (12:3–6)

> **3** Speak to all the congregation of Israel, saying, 'On the tenth day of this month, they shall take
> to them every man a lamb, according to their fathers' houses, a lamb for a household;
> **4** and if the household is too little for a lamb, then he and his neighbor next to his house shall
> take one according to the number of the souls; according to what everyone can eat you shall make your
> count for the lamb.
> **5** Your lamb shall be without defect, a male a year old. You shall take it from the sheep, or from
> the goats:
> **6** and you shall keep it until the fourteenth day of the same month; and the whole assembly of the
> congregation of Israel shall kill it at evening.

***"All the congregation of Israel"*** — ***kol-ʿădat yiśrāʾēl***. **The first time Israel is called an
*ʿēdâ*, a congregation** — the word that will name it through the wilderness books. Until now it has been
*"the children of Israel"* and *"the people"*. It becomes a congregation in the act of preparing a
meal.

***"A lamb"*** — ***śeh***. **The last time the word stood in Scripture as a question, it was Isaac's**:
*"where is the lamb (haśśeh) for a burnt offering?"*, and Abraham's answer, *"God will provide himself
the lamb"* (Genesis 22:7–8). In Genesis–Deuteronomy *śeh* occurs before this only there and in Jacob's
wages (Genesis 30:32) (WLC lemma search). **On the page on
[[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]] the wiki noted that God provided an *ʾayil*, a ram, not the *śeh* that was named** — the lamb
left outstanding is one of the two readings recorded there, and this chapter is the next *śeh* in the
Torah. Whether the writer meant the echo cannot be shown. *Śeh* is a single head of the flock, sheep
or goat (12:5), not specifically a lamb; the WEB's *lamb* is the traditional rendering.

***"According to their fathers' houses"*** — *ləbêt-ʾābōt*, the phrase that headed the genealogy of
[[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]] (6:14). **The unit of the rite is the household**, and a household
too small for a whole animal joins *"his neighbor next to his house"* — the count is made by souls and by
appetite (12:4), so that nothing is left over (12:10).

***"Without defect"*** — ***tāmîm***. **The word Genesis used of two men**: Noah, *"blameless among the
people of his time"* (6:9), and Abram, told *"walk before me and be blameless"* (17:1). **This is its
first use of an animal** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy); from Exodus 29:1 and Leviticus 1:3 on,
it is the standard requirement of a sacrifice. See [[Integrity]] and [[Sacrifice and Offering]].

***"At evening"*** — ***bên hāʿarbāyim***, literally *between the two evenings*. The phrase belongs to
the cult: in the Torah it marks the Passover (here; Leviticus 23:5; Numbers 9:3, 5, 11), the quail
(16:12), and the daily evening offering and incense (29:39, 41; 30:8; Numbers 28:4, 8) (WLC lemma search).
Which two evenings are meant — sunset and dark, or afternoon and sunset — is not stated, and later
practice divided over it. Deuteronomy 16:6 says *"at evening, at the going down of the sun"*.

***"The whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it"*** — every household kills its own
lamb, and the text counts it as one act of the whole people. **There is no priest and no altar.** The
doorframe of each house serves for both.

### The blood and the meal (12:7–11)

> **7** They shall take some of the blood, and put it on the two door posts and on the lintel, on the
> houses in which they shall eat it.
> **8** They shall eat the meat in that night, roasted with fire, and unleavened bread. They shall eat it
> with bitter herbs.
> **9** Don't eat it raw, nor boiled at all with water, but roasted with fire; with its head, its legs and
> its inner parts.
> **10** You shall let nothing of it remain until the morning; but that which remains of it until the
> morning you shall burn with fire.
> **11** This is how you shall eat it: with your belt on your waist, your shoes on your feet, and your
> staff in your hand; and you shall eat it in haste: it is Yahweh's Passover.

***"The two door posts and … the lintel"*** — ***šətê hammǝzûzōt … hammašqôp***. ***Mašqôp*, the
lintel, occurs in the Torah only in this chapter** (12:7, 22, 23; WLC lemma search). *Məzûzâ*, the
doorpost, occurs here, at the slave who chooses to stay, whose ear is bored *"to the door or to the doorpost"*
(21:6), and in the command to write God's words *"on the door posts of your house, and on your
gates"* (Deuteronomy 6:9; 11:20; WEB) (WLC lemma search). **The doorpost marks the house that belongs to
Yahweh here, a slave who belongs to his master for life at 21:6, and the house that keeps the law in
Deuteronomy.**

***"Roasted with fire … not … boiled at all with water"*** — ***ṣəlî-ʾēš … ûbāšēl məbuššāl
bammāyim***. **The animal is cooked whole, over fire, without a pot.** No reason is given. Readers have
suggested haste (no time to cut and boil), the household's poverty of vessels, or a deliberate
difference from other rites; the text says none of these. **See the note on Deuteronomy 16:7 below.**

***"Unleavened bread … bitter herbs"*** — ***maṣṣôt … mərōrîm***. *Maṣṣôt* occurs once in Genesis, the
bread Lot baked for the angels on the night Sodom was destroyed (Genesis 19:3; WLC lemma search) — see
[[The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night]]. The bitter herbs are not explained
here. **The root *mrr* is the word of the oppression**: the Egyptians *"made their lives bitter (wayəmārərû)
with hard service"* (1:14), as [[The Oppression in Egypt]] noted.

***"With your belt on your waist, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand"*** — **a meal
eaten in travelling clothes**, ready to walk out. No other meal in Genesis or Exodus is given a dress
code.

***"In haste"*** — ***bəḥippāzôn***. **The noun occurs in the Torah only here and at Deuteronomy 16:3**,
*"for you came out of the land of Egypt in haste"* (WEB; WLC lemma search). In Exodus the haste is
commanded before it is forced: at 12:33 the Egyptians are *"urgent … to send them out of the land in
haste"*, and at 12:39 the dough is unleavened *"because they were thrust out of Egypt, and couldn't
wait"*.

***"It is Yahweh's Passover"*** — ***pesaḥ hûʾ laYHWH***. **The first use of *pesaḥ* in Scripture.** The
noun is introduced as a name before the verb that explains it, which comes two verses later.

### I will pass over you (12:12–13)

> **12** For I will go through the land of Egypt in that night, and will strike all the firstborn in the
> land of Egypt, both man and animal. Against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am
> Yahweh.
> **13** The blood shall be to you for a token on the houses where you are: and when I see the blood, I
> will pass over you, and there shall no plague be on you to destroy you, when I strike the land of
> Egypt.

***"I will go through"*** — ***wəʿābartî***, from *ʿābar*, *to pass through* or *cross*. **God passes
through Egypt and passes over Israel** — two different verbs, *ʿābar* and *pāsaḥ*, which the English
*pass* makes look alike.

***"Against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments"*** — ***ûbəkol-ʾĕlōhê miṣrayim ʾeʿĕśeh
šəpāṭîm***. ***Šəpāṭîm*, *judgments*, occurs in the Torah only four times** (WLC lemma search): God's
promise to redeem Israel *"with great judgments"* (6:6), his forecast to bring them out *"by great
judgments"* (7:4), this verse, and its fulfilment — Numbers 33:4, *"while the Egyptians were burying all
their firstborn, whom Yahweh had struck among them. Yahweh also executed judgments on their gods"*
(WEB). **This is the first time the plagues are said to be aimed at Egypt's gods.** No god of Egypt is
named anywhere in Exodus, and the text does not say how the death of the firstborn judges them. Readers
have mapped individual plagues onto individual Egyptian deities (the Nile, the sun at the darkness, the
divine Pharaoh at the firstborn); that mapping is later interpretation, not the text's. See
[[Idolatry]] and [[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

***"I am Yahweh"*** — ***ʾănî YHWH***, the signature of 6:2, 6, 8, 29, closing the sentence that
announces the blow.

***"The blood shall be to you for a token"*** — ***wəhāyâ haddām lākem ləʾōt***. ***ʾÔt*, *sign***, is
the word of the bow in the cloud (Genesis 9:12–17) and of circumcision (Genesis 17:11), and in Exodus of
the signs given to Moses (4:8–9, 17, 28, 30) and the plagues (7:3; 10:1–2) (WLC lemma search). **Like the
bow, this sign is looked at by God**: *"I will look at it, that I may remember the everlasting
covenant"* (Genesis 9:16); *"when I see the blood, I will pass over you"*. It is a sign *"to you"*, and
God is the one who reads it. See [[The Bow in the Cloud]] and [[Signs and Wonders]].

***"I will pass over you"*** — ***ûpāsaḥtî ʿălēkem***. **The verb *pāsaḥ* occurs in the Torah only in
this chapter, three times** (12:13, 23, 27; WLC lemma search). **What it means is debated.** The
traditional sense, which the WEB follows, is *pass over*, *skip*: God goes through Egypt and passes by
the marked houses. A second reading takes it as *protect* or *stand guard over*, on the strength of
Isaiah 31:5: *"As birds hovering, so Yahweh of Armies will protect Jerusalem. He will protect and deliver
it. He will pass over and preserve it"* (WEB) — where *pāsōaḥ* stands beside *gānôn*, *protecting*, and
*hiṣṣîl*, *delivering* (WLC). **Isaiah 31:5 pairs *pāsaḥ* with *hiṣṣîl*, as Exodus 12:27 does** (*pāsaḥ*
… *hiṣṣîl*). 12:23 can be read either way: Yahweh *"will pass over the door, and will not allow the
destroyer to come in"* sounds like a guard at the door. **The wiki records both senses and adopts
neither.** There is a third use of the root, *limping* (1 Kings 18:21, 26, as commonly noted; not
checked in this pass), whose connection with the Passover verb is uncertain.

***"There shall no plague be on you to destroy you"*** — ***wəlōʾ-yihyeh bākem negep ləmašḥît***. ***Negep***
is the noun of *nāgap*, the verb of the frogs (8:2) and of 12:23, 27. In the Torah it is a plague that
breaks out from God on the people when the boundary of the holy is crossed: at the census, *"that there
be no plague among them"* (30:12), and twice more in Numbers (8:19; 16:46–48 in English, 17:11–12 in
Hebrew) (WLC lemma search). ***Ləmašḥît***, *for destruction*, is the noun *mašḥît*, found nowhere else in the Torah (WLC); the
same consonants as a participle are *"the destroyer"* of 12:23.

### A memorial and a feast (12:14–20)

> **14** This day shall be to you for a memorial, and you shall keep it a feast to Yahweh: throughout
> your generations you shall keep it a feast by an ordinance forever.
> **15** "'Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread; even the first day you shall put away yeast out of
> your houses, for whoever eats leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall
> be cut off from Israel.
> **16** In the first day there shall be to you a holy convocation, and in the seventh day a holy
> convocation; no kind of work shall be done in them, except that which every man must eat, that only
> may be done by you.
> **17** You shall observe the feast of unleavened bread; for in this same day I have brought your armies
> out of the land of Egypt: therefore you shall observe this day throughout your generations by an
> ordinance forever.
> **18** In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at evening, you shall eat unleavened
> bread, until the twenty first day of the month at evening.
> **19** There shall be no yeast found in your houses for seven days, for whoever eats that which is
> leavened, that soul shall be cut off from the congregation of Israel, whether he is a foreigner, or one
> who is born in the land.
> **20** You shall eat nothing leavened. In all your habitations you shall eat unleavened bread.'"

***"A memorial"*** — ***ləzikkārôn***. **The first *zikkārôn* in Scripture** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). It returns at 13:9 for the same festival, at 17:14 for Amalek, and then for the
stones on the high priest's shoulders and breastplate, *"a memorial before Yahweh"* (28:12, 29). Genesis
had God remember Noah, Abraham, and Rachel (8:1; 19:29; 30:22); **this is the first time Israel is given
something to remember with.** See [[Remembrance]] and
[[God Remembered - Noah, Abraham, Rachel, and Israel]].

***"A feast to Yahweh"*** — ***ḥag laYHWH***. *Ḥag* was Moses' word to Pharaoh — *"we must hold a feast
to Yahweh"* (10:9) — the only earlier use in the Torah (WLC lemma search). **The feast that was the
pretext for leaving becomes the feast that remembers it.**

***"By an ordinance forever"*** — ***ḥuqqat ʿôlām***. Repeated at 12:17 and, in Moses' words, 12:24
(*ləḥoq … ʿad-ʿôlām*). **The only earlier *ʿôlām* in Exodus is God's name**: *"This is my name
forever"* (3:15; WLC lemma search). **The Passover is the first ordinance in Scripture called
*forever*.**

***"Seven days"*** — the festival is a week with a holy day at each end and *"no kind of work"* on them
(12:16). **Seven-day spans have appeared before** — the creation week (Genesis 2:2–3), the waits of
the flood (Genesis 7:4, 10; 8:10, 12), Jacob's week with Leah (Genesis 29:27), the mourning for Jacob
(Genesis 50:10), the seven days after the river was struck (Exodus 7:25) — **but this is the first week
God appoints to be kept, and the first with days on which work stops since Genesis 2:2–3.** The
Sabbath is not yet named in Exodus; it is first named at the manna (16:23; WLC lemma search). See [[Sabbath]] and [[Work]].

***"A holy convocation"*** — ***miqrāʾ qōdeš***. **The first time *qōdeš* is said of time in Exodus.**
The only holy thing so far has been the ground at the bush (3:5). See [[Holiness]].

***"That soul shall be cut off"*** — ***wənikrətâ hannepeš hahiwʾ***, twice (12:15, 19). **The same
formula stood once in Genesis, for the uncircumcised male**: *"that soul shall be cut off from his
people; he has broken my covenant"* (Genesis 17:14) — the exact clause *wənikrətâ hannepeš hahiwʾ* stands in Genesis–Exodus only at
Genesis 17:14, here, and at 31:14 for the Sabbath-breaker; 30:33, 38 use the same verb without *nepeš*
(WLC). **It is a sentence with
no court attached** — what it means in practice (execution, early death, exclusion, childlessness) is
not stated, and interpreters have differed. See [[Circumcision]] and
[[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]].

***"Whether he is a foreigner, or one who is born in the land"*** — ***baggēr ûbəʾezraḥ hāʾāreṣ***.
**The first *ʾezraḥ* in Scripture** (WLC lemma search). **A law spoken in Egypt to a people who own no
land already distinguishes the native of the land from the stranger in it**, and puts both under the
same rule. *Gēr* is the word Abraham used of himself among the Hittites (Genesis 23:4) and Moses for his
son's name (*Gershom*, *"I have lived as a foreigner"*, 2:22). At 12:48–49 the stranger may keep the
Passover itself if he is circumcised, under *"one law"*. See [[Land and Sojourning]] and
[[The Nations]].

***"In this same day I have brought your armies out"*** — ***bəʿeṣem hayyôm hazzeh hôṣēʾtî ʾet-ṣibʾôtêkem***.
*Bəʿeṣem hayyôm hazzeh*, *on this very day*, is the phrase of the day Noah entered the ark (Genesis 7:13)
and the day Abraham circumcised his household (Genesis 17:23, 26); in Exodus it returns at 12:41 and
12:51 (WLC lemma search).
*Ṣibʾôt*, *armies*, is the word of 6:26 and 7:4. **The verb is perfect, and the event is future in the
narrative**: the festival law is spoken as though the exodus had already happened.

### Moses to the elders (12:21–23)

> **21** Then Moses called for all the elders of Israel, and said to them, "Draw out, and take lambs
> according to your families, and kill the Passover.
> **22** You shall take a bunch of hyssop, and dip it in the blood that is in the basin, and strike the
> lintel and the two door posts with the blood that is in the basin; and none of you shall go out of the
> door of his house until the morning.
> **23** For Yahweh will pass through to strike the Egyptians; and when he sees the blood on the lintel,
> and on the two door posts, Yahweh will pass over the door, and will not allow the destroyer to come in
> to your houses to strike you.

***"All the elders of Israel"*** — gathered at 4:29, absent at 5:1 (see [[Bricks Without Straw]]), and
now the channel through which the rite reaches every house. **Moses relays only part of what he was told**
— nothing of the month, the tenth day, or the seven days of unleavened bread. He adds three things God
did not say in 12:1–20: the hyssop, the order to stay inside, and the destroyer.

***"A bunch of hyssop"*** — ***ʾăguddat ʾēzôb***. **The first *ʾēzôb* in Scripture.** In the rest of the
Torah hyssop appears only in two rites of cleansing: the healed leper and the house with mould (Leviticus
14:4–6, 49–52) and the water of the red heifer for corpse impurity (Numbers 19:6, 18) (WLC lemma search).
**Each time it is the instrument that applies blood or water to the thing to be protected or
cleansed.**

***"The basin"*** — ***bassap***. **The only *sap* in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). The WEB's *basin*
is the usual rendering. The word is also used elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible for a *threshold*, and some
ancient and modern readers take it that way here — the lamb killed at the threshold, the blood already at
the door (not checked in this pass beyond the Torah).

***"None of you shall go out of the door of his house until the morning"*** — ***wəʾattem lōʾ tēṣəʾû
ʾîš mippetaḥ-bêtô ʿad-bōqer***. **The only safe place is inside.** The night of the exodus is spent
indoors, and no one leaves until Pharaoh's summons (12:31). Compare Lot, who *"went out to them to the
door, and shut the door after him"*, and is pulled back inside by the angels before the city is
destroyed (Genesis 19:6, 10). See
[[The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night]].

***"Yahweh will pass through to strike … and will not allow the destroyer to come in"*** — ***wəʿābar
YHWH lingōp … wəlōʾ yittēn hammašḥît lābōʾ***. **In 12:12–13 God says *I* will strike; here Yahweh
passes through to strike, and there is also *the destroyer*, whom Yahweh will not allow in.** The text
does not say who the destroyer is. Readers have taken it as a description of Yahweh himself acting as
destroyer, as an angel sent by him (as in the destroying angel of 2 Samuel 24:16, paraphrased; not
quoted), or as a power that God restrains. **The definite article — *the* destroyer — is used as though
the reader already knows.** *Mašḥît* is the participle of *šāḥat*, the verb of the flood (*"Behold, I will
destroy them and the earth"*, Genesis 6:13) and of the angels at Sodom, *"we will destroy (mašḥitîm)
this place"* (Genesis 19:13) (WLC). Hebrews 11:28 takes up the figure: *"By faith, he kept the
Passover, and the sprinkling of the blood, that the destroyer of the firstborn should not touch them"*
(WEB). See [[The Angel of Yahweh]] and [[Judgment]].

### This service (12:24–28)

> **24** You shall observe this thing for an ordinance to you and to your sons forever.
> **25** It shall happen when you have come to the land which Yahweh will give you, according as he has
> promised, that you shall keep this service.
> **26** It will happen, when your children ask you, 'What do you mean by this service?'
> **27** that you shall say, 'It is the sacrifice of Yahweh's Passover, who passed over the houses of the
> children of Israel in Egypt, when he struck the Egyptians, and spared our houses.'" The people bowed
> their heads and worshiped.
> **28** The children of Israel went and did so; as Yahweh had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.

***"This service"*** — ***hāʿăbōdâ hazzōʾt***. **This is the word the book has used for slavery.** In
Exodus 1–6 *ʿăbōdâ* is Egypt's: the *"hard service"* that made their lives bitter (1:14, three times), the
*"bondage"* they groaned under (2:23, twice), the *"labor"* Pharaoh made heavier (5:9, 11), the
*"bondage"* God promised to rid them of (6:6), and the *"cruel bondage"* that kept them from listening
(6:9) (WLC lemma search). **Its next uses are here, twice, and the thing named is a rite** — and after
13:5 the word belongs to the tabernacle (27:19; 30:16; 35–39). **Israel trades one *ʿăbōdâ* for another
in a single chapter.** The book's demand was *"Let my son go, that he may serve me"* (4:23); this is the
first service Israel is told to render. See [[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[Work]].

***"When you have come to the land"*** — **the rite looks two ways at once**, back to Egypt and forward
to Canaan. *"According as he has promised"* points to the oath to the fathers (6:8). See [[Canaan]].

***"When your children ask you"*** — ***kî-yōʾmərû ʾălêkem bənêkem***. **The question is built into the
rite.** At 10:2 God had said the plagues were done *"that you may tell in the hearing of your son, and of
your son's son"*; here the son asks first. The pattern recurs at 13:8 and 13:14 and in Deuteronomy 6:20.
See [[Remembrance]].

***"The sacrifice of Yahweh's Passover"*** — ***zebaḥ-pesaḥ hûʾ laYHWH***. ***Zebaḥ***, a sacrifice
whose meat is eaten, is what Moses asked Pharaoh for — *"that we may sacrifice (wənizbəḥâ) to
Yahweh"* (3:18; 5:3), and *"sacrifices (zəbāḥîm) and burnt offerings"* (10:25) (WLC). **The
sacrifice that was the pretext for leaving is the first one Exodus prescribes, and it is offered in
Egypt.** The Passover is a *zebaḥ*, not an *ʿōlâ*, a burnt
offering: the household eats it. See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].

***"And spared our houses"*** — ***wəʾet-bāttênû hiṣṣîl***. **The WEB's *spared* is *hiṣṣîl*, *rescued*
or *delivered*** — the verb of Moses' accusation, *"you have not rescued (wəhaṣṣēl lōʾ-hiṣṣaltā) your
people at all"* (5:23), and of God's answer, *"I will rescue (wəhiṣṣaltî) you from their bondage"* (6:6)
(WLC). **The answer the children are to be given uses the word of the complaint.** See
[[Bricks Without Straw]] and [[I Am Yahweh]].

***"The people bowed their heads and worshiped"*** — ***wayyiqqōd hāʿām wayyištaḥăwû***. **The same two
verbs as 4:31**, when the people first believed Moses and Aaron (*wayyiqqədû wayyištaḥăwû*). The pair
occurs in Genesis–Numbers only at Genesis 24:26, 48; 43:28; Exodus 4:31; 12:27; 34:8; Numbers 22:31 (WLC
lemma search of *qādad*). **The people's two acts of worship in Exodus 1–12 frame the contest with
Pharaoh**: once when they hear that God has seen their affliction (4:31), once when they hear how he will
deliver them (12:27). See [[Moses Returns to Egypt]].

***"As Yahweh had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did"*** — the formula of obedience, used before of
Noah, of Moses, and of the brothers (see
[[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]]), **used here for the first time of the whole
people.**

### Deuteronomy 16 and Exodus 12 — two forms of the rite

**Deuteronomy's Passover law differs from this one, and the wiki records the differences rather than
harmonizing them.**

| | Exodus 12 | Deuteronomy 16:1–8 |
|---|---|---|
| Where | each household, at its own door (12:7, 22) | *"You may not sacrifice the Passover within any of your gates … but at the place which Yahweh your God shall choose"* (16:5–6) |
| Animal | *"from the sheep, or from the goats"* (12:5) | *"of the flock and the herd"*, *ṣōʾn ûbāqār* (16:2) |
| Cooking | *"Don't eat it … boiled at all with water, but roasted with fire"* (12:9), *bāšēl məbuššāl* | *ûbiššaltā* (16:7), the piel of the same verb *bāšal*, which the WEB renders *"You shall roast"* |
| Blood on the door | central (12:7, 13, 22–23) | not mentioned |

**The cooking verb is the sharpest of these.** Deuteronomy 16:7 uses *bāšal* (WLC), the verb Exodus 12:9
forbids; the WEB, like many translations, renders it *roast*. *Bāšal* can mean *cook* in general, and on
that reading there is no conflict. 2 Chronicles 35:13 — *"They roasted the Passover with fire according to
the ordinance. They boiled the holy offerings in pots"* (WEB) — is commonly read as combining the two
laws, its Hebrew using *bāšal* with *"fire"* (a claim about the Chronicles Hebrew not checked in this
pass). **Readers who see composite sources take Exodus 12 and Deuteronomy 16 as two stages of the rite —
a household rite and a central one; others take Deuteronomy as adapting the first Passover to life in the
land.** The wiki records both and adopts neither.

## Notes

- **The pericope ends before the lamb is killed.** The plan drawn at the first Exodus ingest put the
  break at 12:28, and this ingest kept it: 12:1–28 is speech and assent, 12:29 is the blow. The second
  Passover law (12:43–49, the foreigner and circumcision) falls in the next pericope, *The Death of the
  Firstborn and the Departure*, as planned.
- **No page for *the destroyer*.** The figure appears once in Exodus and is not identified; giving it a
  person page would decide the question the text leaves open. It is recorded on [[Judgment]] and
  [[The Angel of Yahweh]], and Hebrews 11:28's use is on
  [[Christ Our Passover - Exodus 12 in the New Testament]].
- **No page for the elders of Israel.** A group named at 3:16, 18; 4:29; and here; the wiki will revisit
  this if they gain a role at Sinai (24:1, 9–11). They do — seventy of them see God and eat
  ([[The Blood of the Covenant]]) — but still as an unnamed group, so no page was created there either.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** *pāsaḥ* as *pass over* or *protect*; *sap* as *basin* or
  *threshold*; the identity of the destroyer; the reasons for roasting whole; the plagues as judgments on
  named Egyptian gods; the meaning of *cut off*; the relation of Deuteronomy 16 to Exodus 12; the *śeh*
  echo of Genesis 22:7–8.
- **Sources.** Exodus and Genesis are quoted from `raw/bible/`. Verses outside `raw/` (Numbers 33:3–4;
  Deuteronomy 6:9; 16:1–7; 2 Chronicles 35:13; Isaiah 31:5; Hebrews 11:28) are quoted from the WEB as
  served by bible-api.com (`translation=web`). **Hebrew forms and counts were checked against the
  Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures `morphhb`)**; lemma searches were run over
  Genesis–Deuteronomy, and Isaiah 31:5 was checked in the same edition. Claims about 1 Kings 18,
  2 Samuel 24, 2 Chronicles 35 Hebrew, and *sap* outside the Torah are marked at their point as not
  checked in this pass.
