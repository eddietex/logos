# The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 26:1–37

## Summary

The furniture now receives its enclosure. God instructs Moses to make ten linen curtains decorated
with cherubim, joined as two sets of five by fifty gold clasps. A larger covering of eleven goats’
hair curtains hangs over them, with coverings of red-dyed rams’ skins and what the WEB calls sea cow
hides above (26:1–14). The joined pieces make one tabernacle; the larger covering protects its sides
and rear.

Upright acacia boards stand in silver sockets and are held together by bars. Twenty boards form each
long side; six boards and two corner boards close the west end. The boards and bars are overlaid
with gold (26:15–29). Moses must erect the dwelling according to what he was shown on the mountain
(26:30). The chapter describes instructions, not construction already carried out.

Two different hangings order entry. An inner veil with cherubim hangs on four gold-covered pillars
in silver sockets. It separates the holy place from the most holy, where the ark and mercy seat
belong. Outside this veil, the table stands to the north and the lampstand to the south. A screen
at the tent entrance hangs on five gold-covered pillars in brass sockets (26:31–37).

**The dwelling promised in 25:8 has boundaries within it.** Nearness to God does not mean that every
part of the dwelling is equally accessible. Exodus 26 establishes the spatial distinction;
Leviticus 16:2 will state the restriction on entering within the veil.

## Notes

### The boundary and the assembly

The planned boundary is retained. Chapter 26 treats the enclosure, its supports, its partition,
and its doorway. Chapter 27 turns to the altar and court. The curtains and frames belong together
because they form the dwelling that receives chapter 25's furniture.

The ten linen curtains measure twenty-eight by four cubits each; the eleven goats’ hair curtains
measure thirty by four. Both sets are joined into larger assemblies, but their clasps differ:
gold for the linen, brass for the goats’ hair (26:6, 11). Verses 9–13 explain the folding and
extra overhang. Verse 14 specifies two further coverings without giving their dimensions. The WEB's
animal label, *sea cow hides*, is retained as this translation's wording; no species identification
is established here from a source-language or zoological study.

The long sides each contain twenty boards of one and a half cubits width, giving thirty cubits
along each side; each board is ten cubits tall. There are forty-eight boards and ninety-six silver
sockets under them, counting the six western boards and two corners (26:18–25). Four more silver
sockets support the inner veil's pillars (26:32). These totals are arithmetic from the instructions,
not symbolic meanings assigned by the text. The corner arrangement in 26:24 is difficult to
reconstruct from the WEB alone; this page does not claim an exact internal width, roof profile,
board thickness, or modern-unit conversion.

### One dwelling, two hangings

The inner veil repeats the colored linen and cherubim of the inner curtains (26:1, 31).
It hangs under the clasps and divides two named holy spaces (26:33). The entrance screen shares
the colors and linen but is described as embroidery; no cherubim are specified for it (26:36).
The inner veil has four pillars in silver sockets; the entrance has five in brass sockets.
Neither hanging should silently substitute for the other in a later cross-reference.

The ark and mercy seat stand within the veil. The table and lampstand stand outside it, north
and south respectively (26:33–35). The incense altar is not introduced until 30:1–10.
The separation is explicit, but rules about who enters and on what occasions come in later texts.
Leviticus 16:2 forbids Aaron unrestricted entry within the veil; Hebrews 9:6–8 describes the
priestly restriction. See [[The Veil - Separation in Exodus and Access in Hebrews]].

### Cherubim, images, and access

Genesis 3:24 puts cherubim at the guarded approach to the tree of life. Exodus 25:18–22 puts their
figures over the ark, and 26:1, 31 extends them to the curtains and dividing veil. The combination
makes an Eden comparison plausible, especially beside the lampstand's botanical design. Exodus
never calls the tent Eden or the lampstand the tree of life. Nor does it say the textile cherubim
literally guard the veil. The connection is thematic interpretation; see
[[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]].

The image prohibition in Exodus 20:4 and the commanded cherubim must both be accounted for.
The chapter commands figures but does not command their worship. This supports a distinction
between authorized sanctuary art and idols while leaving the scope of the prohibition a matter
for interpretation; see [[Idolatry]]. No appearance, number, or arrangement of the textile
cherubim is supplied, so the two gold figures of chapter 25 should not determine a reconstruction.

### The later text does not merely repeat the instruction

Exodus 36:8–38 closely follows these materials and measurements in a report of making. It does not
repeat every instruction: for example, the detailed folding and overhang of 26:9, 12–13 are not
restated there. The erection and furniture placements belong to 40:18–28. Repetition demonstrates
the correspondence of command and execution without making the accounts verbatim duplicates.
See [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

Hebrews 10:19–22 interprets entry through the veil in relation to Jesus' flesh and blood. The
Gospels report a torn temple veil at his death. The later temple curtain is not the same physical
fabric as the wilderness tent, and the Gospel verses do not themselves explain the event in
Hebrews' terms. [[The Veil - Separation in Exodus and Access in Hebrews]] records the connection and the limits of the identification.
