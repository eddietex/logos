# Livestock, Boils, and Hail

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 9:1–35

## Summary

**Three more plagues, and in this chapter the plagues begin to touch life and death.** The
pestilence kills Egypt's herds, the boils fall on the bodies of people and animals, and the hail
kills whoever stays out in the field. **This is also the chapter where Yahweh is first said to
harden Pharaoh's heart**, where Pharaoh first says *"I have sinned"*, and where some of Pharaoh's own
servants first fear Yahweh's word.

**The livestock (9:1–7).** [[Moses]] carries the demand again — *"Let my people go, that they may
serve me"* — and the threat: *"Yahweh's hand is on your livestock which are in the field … with a
very grievous pestilence"* (9:1–3). **The line drawn at the flies is drawn again**: *"Yahweh will make
a distinction between the livestock of Israel and the livestock of Egypt"* (9:4). **Yahweh sets the
hour himself this time**, *"Tomorrow"* (9:5). The next day *"all the livestock of Egypt died, but of
the livestock of the children of Israel, not one died"* (9:6). **Pharaoh sends to check, and finds it
so, and his heart is still heavy** (9:7).

**The boils (9:8–12).** No warning. Moses and [[Aaron]] take *"handfuls of ashes of the furnace"*, and
Moses throws them toward the sky *"in the sight of Pharaoh"*. The ashes become fine dust over Egypt,
and boils break out *"on man and on animal"* (9:8–10). ***"The magicians couldn't stand before Moses
because of the boils"*** (9:11). This is the last time they appear. **And then, for the first time,
the narrator says that Yahweh did the hardening**: ***"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"***
(9:12).

**The hail (9:13–35).** Moses is again told to rise early and stand before Pharaoh (9:13). **The speech
is the longest of the plagues so far.** God says he will now send *"all my plagues against your
heart"*. He could already have *"cut off"* Pharaoh *"from the earth"*, but *"for this cause I have
made you stand: to show you my power, and that my name may be declared throughout all the
earth"* (9:14–16). He announces a hail *"such as has not been in Egypt since the day it was
founded"*. **For the first time a plague comes with a way out**: bring the livestock and the
servants inside, or they will die (9:18–19). **Egypt splits.** *"Those who feared Yahweh's word
among the servants of Pharaoh"* bring theirs in; those who *"didn't respect Yahweh's word"* leave
theirs in the field (9:20–21). Moses stretches out his rod. Thunder, hail, and fire run along the
ground. The hail strikes people, animals, plants, and trees *"throughout all the land of Egypt"* —
***"Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, there was no hail"*** (9:22–26).
**Pharaoh confesses**: ***"I have sinned this time. Yahweh is righteous, and I and my people are
wicked"*** (9:27). He asks for prayer again, and promises again. Moses agrees to pray *"that you may
know that the earth is Yahweh's"*, and adds, *"I know that you don't yet fear Yahweh God"* (9:29–30).
The narrator then notes which crops were lost: the flax and barley, but not the wheat and spelt
(9:31–32). Moses spreads out his hands and the storm stops. ***Pharaoh "sinned yet more, and
hardened his heart, he and his servants"*** (9:33–35).

### The shape of it

| | Pestilence (9:1–7) | Boils (9:8–12) | Hail (9:13–35) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warning | yes, at the palace | none | yes, in the morning |
| Agent | Yahweh, no rod | Moses throws ashes | Moses' rod |
| Israel spared | stated (9:4, 6) | not stated | stated (9:26) |
| Magicians | — | can't stand before Moses | — |
| Pharaoh | sends to check | — | confesses, asks for prayer, promises |
| The heart | *kābēd* (qal), no agent | **Yahweh** *ḥāzaq* (piel) | Pharaoh *kābēd* (hiphil), then *ḥāzaq* |

**The pattern of 7:14–8:32 repeats**: a warning in the palace, a warning in the morning, then a third
plague with no warning at all. The boils, like the lice, come with no warning, and the magicians are
beaten at the third plague of each set: they fail at the lice, and leave at the boils. **The hail opens the third set**, and it gets the longest speech
of all — God explaining why Pharaoh is still standing.

## Key verses

### The livestock (9:1–7)

> **3** behold, Yahweh's hand is on your livestock which are in the field, on the horses, on the
> donkeys, on the camels, on the herds, and on the flocks with a very grievous pestilence.
> **4** Yahweh will make a distinction between the livestock of Israel and the livestock of Egypt; and
> nothing shall die of all that belongs to the children of Israel."'"

***"Yahweh's hand"*** — ***yad-YHWH***. **The hand God promised at the bush comes down**: *"I will reach
out my hand and strike Egypt with all my wonders"* (3:20), and *"when I stretch out my hand on Egypt"*
(7:5). **In this plague nobody uses a rod or stretches out a hand. The hand is Yahweh's own.**

***"A very grievous pestilence"*** — ***deber kābēd məʾōd***. ***Deber*** is the word Moses and Aaron
used to Pharaoh in their first audience: *"lest he fall on us with pestilence, or with the sword"*
(5:3). **The danger they gave as their reason for going to sacrifice now falls on Egypt's herds.** In
Genesis through Numbers *deber* occurs only at Exodus 5:3; 9:3, 15; Leviticus 26:25; and Numbers 14:12
(WLC lemma search). The adjective is ***kābēd*** again, the word for Pharaoh's heart (7:14), now
describing the plague. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"Will make a distinction"*** — ***wəhiplâ***, the *pālâ* of 8:22, where Goshen was *"set apart"*.
**The flies set apart a land; the pestilence sets apart herds.**

***"Yahweh appointed a set time"*** — ***wayyāśem YHWH môʿēd***. At the frogs Pharaoh chose
*"Tomorrow"* (8:10); this time Yahweh chooses it. ***Môʿēd*** is the word of Genesis 1:14 —
*"let them be for signs to mark seasons"* — and of Sarah's son: *"At the set time I will return to you"*
(Genesis 18:14; also 17:21; 21:2). **After this it becomes the word of the feasts and of the *ʾōhel
môʿēd*, the Tent of Meeting**, which accounts for most of its uses in Exodus 27–40 (WLC lemma search).

> **6** Yahweh did that thing on the next day; and all the livestock of Egypt died, but of the livestock
> of the children of Israel, not one died.
> **7** Pharaoh sent, and, behold, there was not so much as one of the livestock of the Israelites dead.
> But the heart of Pharaoh was stubborn, and he didn't let the people go.

***"Not one died"*** — ***lōʾ-mēt ʾeḥād***; and Pharaoh's messengers find *"not so much as one"*, ***ʿad-ʾeḥād***.
**The count is exact on both sides.** The same *not one* closes the story at the sea: *"There remained
not so much as one of them"* (14:28).

***"All the livestock of Egypt died."*** **The text does not resolve this, so it is noted here.** The
boils fall *"on animal"* (9:9–10). The hail warning assumes Egypt still has livestock in the field
(9:19–21). The firstborn of livestock die at the Passover (11:5; 12:29). Readers have handled this in
three ways. One reads *all* as hyperbole. One reads the threat's *"which are in the field"* (9:3) as
the limit. One supposes the Egyptians restocked, some from Goshen. The narrative does not say. The
same difficulty recurs with the hail's *"every herb of the field"* (9:25) and the locusts' *"every
herb"* (10:15).

***"The heart of Pharaoh was stubborn"*** — ***wayyikbad lēb parʿōh***, the qal of *kābēd*, *was
heavy*, with no agent. **He has the proof he sent for, and does nothing with it.**

### The boils (9:8–12)

> **8** Yahweh said to Moses and to Aaron, "Take to you handfuls of ashes of the furnace, and let Moses
> sprinkle it toward the sky in the sight of Pharaoh.
> **9** It shall become small dust over all the land of Egypt, and shall be a boil breaking out with
> boils on man and on animal, throughout all the land of Egypt."
> **10** They took ashes of the furnace, and stood before Pharaoh; and Moses sprinkled it up toward the
> sky; and it became a boil breaking out with boils on man and on animal.
> **11** The magicians couldn't stand before Moses because of the boils; for the boils were on the
> magicians, and on all the Egyptians.
> **12** Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he didn't listen to them, as Yahweh had spoken to
> Moses.

***"Ashes of the furnace"*** — ***pîaḥ kibšān***. *Pîaḥ*, *soot* or *ashes*, occurs only here in Genesis
through Numbers (WLC lemma search). **The *kibšān* is the furnace of Sodom**: *"the smoke of the land
went up as the smoke of a furnace"* (Genesis 19:28). **It is also the furnace of Sinai**: *"its smoke
ascended like the smoke of a furnace"* (Exodus 19:18). In Genesis through Numbers those are its only
other occurrences (WLC lemma search). Some readers connect
the kiln with Israel's labour in brick (1:14; 5:7–19). The text never says what the furnace was for,
so that link is a reading, not the narrator's claim. See
[[Fire and Sulfur from the Sky]] and [[Smoking Furnace and Flaming Torch - Fire as Theophany]].

***"Sprinkle it toward the sky"*** — ***zərāqô … haššāmaymâ***. ***Zāraq*** is the verb of the altar,
of blood *"sprinkled"* or splashed against it, though it is not yet used that way in Exodus (WLC). The
gesture is reported, not explained.

***"Small dust"*** — ***ʾābāq***, fine dust, found only here in Genesis through Numbers (WLC lemma search).
**The lice came from *ʿăpar hāʾāreṣ*, the dust of the earth (8:16); the boils come from ash.** ***"A
boil breaking out with boils"*** — ***šəḥîn pōrēaḥ ʾăbaʿbuʿōt***. ***Šəḥîn*** is the skin disease of
Leviticus 13:18–23, which the priest examines for leprosy (WLC). **It is also what Job is struck
with**: *"So Satan went out from the presence of Yahweh, and struck Job with painful sores from the
sole of his foot to his head"* (Job 2:7) — *bišḥîn rāʿ* (WLC). Deuteronomy names the plague after its
place: *"Yahweh will strike you with the boils of Egypt"* (Deuteronomy 28:27, WEB). See
[[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]].

***"The magicians couldn't stand before Moses"*** — ***wəlōʾ-yākəlû haḥarṭummîm laʿămōd lipnê
mōšeh***. **This is their last appearance.** *Ḥarṭummîm* occurs nowhere in Exodus after this verse
(WLC lemma search), so they leave the story without being dismissed. **Moses and Aaron *"stood before
Pharaoh"*** (*wayyaʿamdû*, 9:10); **the magicians cannot stand at all.** Their confession at the lice
was in words (8:19); here their own bodies show it. ***ʿĀmad*, *to stand*, runs through the chapter**:
here, at 9:16 (*"I have made you stand"*), and at 9:28 (*"you shall stay no longer"*, *lōʾ tōsipûn
laʿămōd*). See [[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]] and
[[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]].

***"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"*** — ***wayḥazzēq YHWH ʾet-lēb parʿōh***. **This is the first
time in Exodus that the narrator says Yahweh did it.** God had said he would (*"I will harden his
heart"*, 4:21; 7:3). The narrator had reported it with no agent (7:13, 22; 8:19; 9:7). Pharaoh himself
was the subject at 8:15 and 32. **Now, after the sixth plague, the narrator says Yahweh did it**, with
the piel of *ḥāzaq*. A WLC search of Exodus for *lēb* with *ḥāzaq*, *kābēd*, or *qāšâ* finds Yahweh as
the subject of a narrated hardening first here, and then at 10:20, 27; 11:10; 14:8. **The order is
God's forecast, then no stated agent, then Pharaoh, then God.** Readers have long argued over what
that order means. The text gives the sequence and does not explain it. See
[[Hardening of the Heart]].

### The hail announced (9:13–21)

> **14** For this time I will send all my plagues against your heart, against your officials, and
> against your people; that you may know that there is no one like me in all the earth.
> **15** For now I would have stretched out my hand, and struck you and your people with pestilence,
> and you would have been cut off from the earth;
> **16** but indeed for this cause I have made you stand: to show you my power, and that my name may be
> declared throughout all the earth;
> **17** as you still exalt yourself against my people, that you won't let them go.

***"All my plagues against your heart"*** — ***ʾet-kol-maggēpōtay ʾel-libbəkā***. ***Maggēpâ*** is the
noun of *nāgap*, the verb of the frogs (*"I will plague"*, 8:2) and of the Passover (12:23, 27). It does
not occur in Genesis, and in Exodus only here; in Numbers it is the word for the plagues in the
wilderness (Numbers 14:37; 16:48–50 [Hebrew 17:13–15]; 25:8–9, 18–19; 31:16) (WLC lemma search). **The
plagues are aimed *at his heart***, the organ being hardened. ***"That you may know that there is no
one like me in all the earth"*** sharpens 8:10's *"no one like Yahweh our God"*: *in all the earth*.
See [[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

***"You would have been cut off from the earth"*** — ***wattikkāḥēd min-hāʾāreṣ***. **God tells Pharaoh
that the pestilence could already have been for him.**

***"For this cause I have made you stand"*** — ***baʿăbûr zōʾt heʿĕmadtîkā***, the hiphil of ***ʿāmad***.
**The magicians could not stand (9:11); Pharaoh stands because God made him stand.** The WEB's
*"made you stand"* is literal. Whether it means *kept you alive* or *raised you up* is debated, and
Paul's quotation takes the second sense. ***"To show you my power"*** (*harʾōtəkā ʾet-kōḥî*) ***"and that
my name may be declared throughout all the earth"*** (*ûləmaʿan sappēr šəmî bəkol-hāʾāreṣ*). **The
purpose is no longer only that Pharaoh should know, but that the whole earth should hear.** Paul
quotes the verse in Romans 9:17: *"For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, 'For this very purpose I caused
you to be raised up, that I might show in you my power, and that my name might be proclaimed in all
the earth'"* (WEB). He follows it with *"he has mercy on whom he desires, and he hardens whom he
desires"* (Romans 9:18, WEB). See
[[For This Very Purpose - Exodus 9.16 in Romans 9.17]].

***"As you still exalt yourself against my people"*** — ***ʿôdəkā mistôlēl bəʿammî***. *Mistôlēl*
occurs only here in Genesis through Numbers (WLC lemma search). Lexicons connect the root *sālal* with
heaping up, as in building a raised road, so the image would be Pharaoh setting himself up like a
barrier against God's people. **That etymology is reported from standard lexicons and was not
verified here.** See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].

> **18** Behold, tomorrow about this time I will cause it to rain a very grievous hail, such as has
> not been in Egypt since the day it was founded even until now.
> **19** Now therefore command that all of your livestock and all that you have in the field be
> brought into shelter. Every man and animal that is found in the field, and isn't brought home, the
> hail shall come down on them, and they shall die."'"
> **20** Those who feared Yahweh's word among the servants of Pharaoh made their servants and their
> livestock flee into the houses.
> **21** Whoever didn't respect Yahweh's word left his servants and his livestock in the field.

***"I will cause it to rain"*** — ***mamṭîr***, the hiphil of ***māṭar***. In Genesis and Exodus its
subject is always God:

- Genesis 2:5 — *"Yahweh God had not caused it to rain on the earth"*.
- Genesis 7:4 — the flood: *"I will cause it to rain on the earth for forty days and forty nights"*.
- Genesis 19:24 — Sodom: *"Then Yahweh rained on Sodom and on Gomorrah sulfur and fire"*.
- Exodus 9:18, 23 — the hail.
- Exodus 16:4 — the manna: *"I will rain bread from the sky for you"*.

Those are its only occurrences in the two books (WLC lemma search). **Twice God rains destruction
mixed with fire — on Sodom and on Egypt — and soon after he rains bread on Israel.** See
[[Fire and Sulfur from the Sky]] and [[The Flood as Un-Creation]].

***"Such as has not been in Egypt since the day it was founded"*** — *hiwwāsədâ*, the only *yāsad*
in Genesis and Exodus (WLC lemma search). ***"Such as has not been"*** (*lōʾ-hāyâ kāmōhû*) recurs at
9:24, for the locusts (10:14), and for the *"great cry"* of the last night: *"such as there has not
been, nor shall be any more"* (11:6).

***"Brought into shelter"*** — ***hāʿēz***, *bring to safety*. **This is the first plague announced with
a way of escape.** It is offered to Pharaoh and to anyone in Egypt who will listen.

***"Those who feared Yahweh's word among the servants of Pharaoh"*** — ***hayyārēʾ ʾet-dəbar YHWH
mēʿabdê parʿōh***. **These are the first Egyptians in Exodus said to fear Yahweh**, and the court
divides. In 1:17 *"the midwives feared God"* and defied *"the king of Egypt"*, though whether they were
Hebrews or Egyptians serving Hebrews is debated (see [[Shiphrah and Puah]]). **Here members of
Pharaoh's own court fear Yahweh's word while Pharaoh does not** (9:30). ***"Whoever didn't
respect"*** is literally ***lōʾ-śām libbô***, *did not set his heart*. That is near the wording of
Pharaoh at the river, who *"didn't even take this to heart"*, *lōʾ-šāt libbô* (7:23), though with a
different verb (*śîm* here, *šît* there; WLC). See [[The Fear of God]].

### The hail falls (9:22–26)

> **23** Moses stretched out his rod toward the heavens, and Yahweh sent thunder, hail, and lightning
> flashed down to the earth. Yahweh rained hail on the land of Egypt.
> **24** So there was very severe hail, and lightning mixed with the hail, such as had not been in all
> the land of Egypt since it became a nation.
> **25** The hail struck throughout all the land of Egypt all that was in the field, both man and
> animal; and the hail struck every herb of the field, and broke every tree of the field.
> **26** Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, there was no hail.

***"Thunder"*** — ***qōlōt***, literally *voices*. **The same plural is the thunder at Sinai**: *"there
were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud on the mountain"* (19:16; also 20:18). Pharaoh will call
it ***qōlōt ʾĕlōhîm***, *"mighty thunderings"* in the WEB, literally *voices of God* (9:28). **The storm
that ruins Egypt sounds like the mountain where Israel will meet God.** ***"Lightning flashed down to the earth"***
is ***wattihălak-ʾēš ʾārṣâ***, *fire walked to the ground*. In 9:24 it is *ʾēš mitlaqqaḥat*, fire *taking
hold of itself* within the hail, a hithpael of *lāqaḥ* (WLC). **Hail and fire together, rained by
Yahweh, recall Sodom's *"sulfur and fire"* (Genesis 19:24).**

***"Since it became a nation"*** — ***mēʾāz hāytâ ləgôy***. **Egypt is called a *gôy*, a nation**, the
word of the promise to Abram, *"I will make of you a great nation"* (Genesis 12:2). See
[[The Nations]].

***"Every herb of the field"*** — ***kol-ʿēśeb haśśādeh***. **Genesis 2:5 puts the same two things side
by side**: *"no herb of the field had yet sprung up; for Yahweh God had not caused it to rain on the
earth"*. There, the herb of the field waits for God's rain. Here, God's rain destroys it. The hail
*"broke every tree of the field"*, and the locusts will eat *"all the fruit of the trees which the
hail had left"* (10:15). See [[Creation]].

***"Only in the land of Goshen … there was no hail."*** **The third time the text says Israel is
spared**: Goshen at the flies (8:22–23), the herds (9:4, 6), and here. It will be said again at the
darkness (10:23) and the last night (11:7). See [[Goshen]]
and [[Call and Election]].

### The confession, and the prayer (9:27–30)

> **27** Pharaoh sent, and called for Moses and Aaron, and said to them, "I have sinned this time.
> Yahweh is righteous, and I and my people are wicked.
> **28** Pray to Yahweh; for there has been enough of mighty thunderings and hail. I will let you go,
> and you shall stay no longer."
> **29** Moses said to him, "As soon as I have gone out of the city, I will spread abroad my hands to
> Yahweh. The thunders shall cease, and there will not be any more hail; that you may know that the
> earth is Yahweh's.
> **30** But as for you and your servants, I know that you don't yet fear Yahweh God."

***"I have sinned this time"*** — ***ḥāṭāʾtî happaʿam***. **Pharaoh confesses for the first time.**
***Happaʿam***, *this time*, echoes God's *"For this time"* (*bappaʿam hazzōʾt*, 9:14) and Pharaoh's
hardening *"this time also"* (8:32). He will confess again at 10:16: *"I have sinned against Yahweh
your God, and against you"*.

***"Yahweh is righteous, and I and my people are wicked"*** — ***YHWH haṣṣaddîq waʾănî wəʿammî
hārəšāʿîm***. **These are the terms of Abraham's argument over Sodom**: *"Will you consume the righteous
with the wicked? … Shouldn't the Judge of all the earth do right?"* (Genesis 18:23–25). *Ṣaddîq* and
*rāšāʿ* are paired there, and *ṣaddîq* occurs nowhere else in Exodus before this verse (WLC lemma
search). **At Sodom Abraham asked whether the righteous would die with the wicked; at the hail
Pharaoh, a king, puts himself among the wicked.** The confession is accurate, but it does not last
(9:34). See [[Shall Not the Judge of All the Earth Do Right]], [[Righteousness]], and [[Sin]].

***"Pray to Yahweh"*** — ***haʿtîrû*** again, the *ʿātar* of 8:8, 28. ***"I will let you go, and you shall
stay no longer"*** — the third promise of release (8:8, 28; 9:28). ***"Enough"*** — ***rab***.

***"I will spread abroad my hands to Yahweh"*** — ***ʾeprōś ʾet-kappay ʾel-YHWH***. **This is the first
time Scripture describes this gesture of prayer.** In Genesis through Numbers, *pāraś* with the palms
occurs only here and at 9:33; the verb is otherwise used of the cherubim's wings and the coverings of
the tabernacle (WLC lemma search). Moses prays outside *"the city"*, which is not named. See
[[Prayer]] and [[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].

***"That you may know that the earth is Yahweh's"*** — ***kî laYHWH hāʾāreṣ***. **The knowledge refrain
again, now about ownership.** Israel will hear it at Sinai, *"for all the earth is mine"* (19:5), and
the psalm sings it: *"The earth is Yahweh's, with its fullness"* (Psalm 24:1, WEB). Here it is said to
a king who claims to own Egypt's land. Genesis 47:20 records how *"the land became Pharaoh's"*. See
[[The Land Is Mine - Pharaoh's Freehold and the Jubilee]].

***"You don't yet fear Yahweh God"*** — ***ṭerem tîrəʾûn mippənê YHWH ʾĕlōhîm***. **Outside Genesis 2–3,
this is the only *YHWH ʾĕlōhîm*, Yahweh God with *ʾĕlōhîm* standing alone, in Genesis and Exodus**
(WLC search). Elsewhere in those books *ʾĕlōhîm* after the name always carries a suffix (*your God*,
*our God*) or a following genitive (*God of the Hebrews*). **The compound name of the garden is used
once more, to tell Pharaoh what he lacks.** Whether the echo is intended cannot be shown. See
[[The Names of God]] and [[The Fear of God]].

### The crops, and the heart (9:31–35)

> **31** The flax and the barley were struck, for the barley was in the ear, and the flax was in bloom.
> **32** But the wheat and the spelt were not struck, for they had not grown up.
> ...
> **34** When Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunders had ceased, he sinned yet more,
> and hardened his heart, he and his servants.
> **35** The heart of Pharaoh was hardened, and he didn't let the children of Israel go, just as Yahweh
> had spoken through Moses.

**An agricultural note in the middle of the story.** ***"The barley was in the ear"*** — ***haśśəʿōrâ
ʾābîb***. ***ʾĀbîb*** is the name of the month of the Exodus itself: *"Today you go out in the month
Abib"* (13:4; also 23:15; 34:18), and in Leviticus it is the fresh grain (2:14) (WLC lemma search).
**Barley ruined while in the ear: the month of Abib is close.** The surviving wheat and spelt are
what the locusts will eat (10:5, 15). The farming calendar of Egypt implied here is not pursued.

***"He sinned yet more"*** — ***wayyōsep laḥăṭōʾ***. **This is the verb of his confession.** *"I have
sinned"* (9:27) turns into sinning again as soon as the danger is gone. ***"And hardened his heart, he
and his servants"*** — ***wayyakbēd libbô hûʾ waʿăbādāyw***. **For the first time the servants are
included.** Some of them had feared Yahweh's word (9:20); now they share the hardening, and God will
say so at 10:1: *"I have hardened his heart, and the heart of his servants"*. Then ***wayyeḥĕzaq lēb
parʿōh***, *ḥāzaq* again with no agent (9:35). **One verse uses Pharaoh's own *kābēd* and the next an
unstated *ḥāzaq*, as at 8:15 and 8:19.**

***"Just as Yahweh had spoken through Moses"*** — ***bəyad-mōšeh***, literally *by the hand of Moses*.
**This is the first time Exodus uses the phrase** (WLC search; next at 34:29; 35:29). **Moses' hand
has stretched out the rod; now God's word is said to come by that hand.**

## People

- [[God]] — strikes the livestock with his own hand; hardens Pharaoh's heart (9:12); makes him
  *"stand"* to show his power and declare his name; rains the hail; spares Goshen
- [[Moses]] — carries both warnings; throws the ashes; stretches the rod to the sky; prays outside
  the city with spread hands; tells Pharaoh *"you don't yet fear Yahweh God"*
- [[Aaron]] — takes the ashes with Moses (9:8–10); summoned with him (9:27); silent
- [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] — sends to check the herds; is made to *"stand"*; confesses *"I have
  sinned"*; promises the third release; *"sinned yet more"*
- [[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]] — *"couldn't stand before Moses because of the boils"*: their
  last appearance
- *Pharaoh's servants* — divided at the warning (9:20–21), and hardened with him (9:34); not given a
  page

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — its herds dead, its people boiled, its field, trees, flax and barley broken; called a
  *gôy* *"since it became a nation"*
- [[Goshen]] — no hail (9:26); Israel's herds untouched (9:4–7, not naming Goshen)
- *"The city"* (9:29, 33) — the royal city, unnamed

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** Chapter 9 holds the second set's first two plagues and the
  third set's first. The hail's speech (9:14–16) works as a hinge into the last set, so it is kept
  with the hail rather than moved into chapter 10.
- **"All the livestock" (9:6) against 9:9, 19–21, and 12:29** is noted as an internal tension in the
  text, not as a contradiction between wiki pages. No solution is chosen.
- **The order of hardening statements** (forecast, no agent, Pharaoh, Yahweh) is taken from a WLC
  search of Exodus for *lēb* with *ḥāzaq*, *kābēd*, or *qāšâ*. Theological readings of that order
  (judicial hardening, confirmation of a choice already made, and so on) are not chosen here.
- ***Mistôlēl*'s etymology** (*sālal*, heap up) is reported from standard lexicons, not checked.
- **Romans 9:17 is quoted only in the WEB English.** No claim is made about its Greek, or about how it
  relates to the Septuagint of Exodus 9:16.
- **The *YHWH ʾĕlōhîm* observation** is a result of a WLC search of Genesis and Exodus for the name
  followed by the absolute form *ʾĕlōhîm*. Its significance is a reading.
- **Natural explanations** — anthrax for the pestilence, a severe spring storm for the hail — are a
  standing line of interpretation and are not pursued here.
- **Verse quotations outside `raw/`** (Romans 9:17–18; Psalm 24:1; 78:47–48; 105:32–33; Deuteronomy
  28:27; Revelation 16:21) were fetched from bible-api.com (`translation=web`), one reference at a
  time. Genesis, Exodus, and Job are quoted from `raw/bible/`.
- **Hebrew forms on this page were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Open Scriptures
  Hebrew Bible, `morphhb`): all of Exodus 9, and Job 2:7. Occurrence counts come from lemma searches of
  the WLC of Genesis and Exodus, with Leviticus and Numbers where stated.
