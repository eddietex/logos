# The Tabernacle Built

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 36:8–38

## Summary

The wise-hearted workers make the enclosure prescribed in Exodus 26. Ten linen curtains with
blue, purple, scarlet, and cherubim are joined in two groups of five, with matching loops and
fifty gold clasps. The joined curtains form a unit (36:8–13). Eleven larger goats’ hair curtains
form the tent covering, joined in groups of five and six with brass clasps; red-dyed rams’ skins
and the hides named in the WEB complete the coverings (36:14–19).

Acacia boards, each ten by one and a half cubits, have paired tenons and stand in silver sockets.
There are twenty boards on each long side, six on the west, and two at the western corners.
Acacia bars bind the boards together, with a middle bar running from end to end; gold covers the
boards and bars, and gold rings hold the bars (36:20–34).

The makers produce the inner veil with cherubim and four gold-covered pillars in silver sockets,
then the embroidered entrance screen and five pillars with brass sockets (36:35–38). The detailed
report makes the correspondence between instruction and work visible. It does not yet report
the furniture in place, the tent's final erection, or the glory filling it.

## People

- The wise-hearted workers are the explicit collective makers (36:8); no human is named within
  this unit. The following singular descriptions do not establish solitary manufacture.
- [[Bezalel]] and [[Oholiab]] — the named makers in 36:1–2 supply the immediate context, not
  fresh named appearances in 36:8–38. No individual task is assigned to Oholiab here.
- [[Moses]] — contextual recipient and transmitter of the design; no action is assigned to him
  in these verses. His later erection of the tent is a separate stage (40:18–28).
- [[God]] — the design and workers' wisdom are his in the preceding instructions (26; 36:1–2);
  these verses report human work without a new divine speech or appearance.
- [[Cherubim]] — figures made on the linen curtains and inner veil (36:8, 35), not living
  beings appearing at the worksite.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — the Sinai camp by narrative continuity from 34:29 and 36:6. This unit names no
  new location; the tabernacle being made is the prescribed sanctuary, not a newly named town.

## Themes

- [[Work]] — Skilled making and joining turn the supplied materials into components.
- [[Wisdom]] — The wise-hearted company makes its competence visible in workmanship.
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — The enclosure is made; erection and indwelling glory remain ahead.
- [[Holiness]] — The inner veil and entrance screen preserve distinct boundaries.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The work closely follows the earlier specifications, with differences

- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — Gold, silver, brass, textiles, skins, and wood become useful parts.

## Notes

### Boundary, agency, and completion

The planned 36:8–38 boundary is retained. It starts with the collective makers and ends with
the entrance screen's supports; 37:1 turns to the ark and names Bezalel. The WEB shifts from the
plural makers in 36:8 to repeated singular descriptions of making. Reading these within Bezalel's
commission is reasonable, but the passage does not say he alone wove, cast, carved, and joined
every object. Oholiab's and the wider company's roles in 36:1–2 remain part of the context.

The curtain assemblies are joined here (36:13, 18); this should not be reduced to collecting
loose materials. But making and joining components is not the final raising of the dwelling.
Exodus 40:17–21 separately reports the erection, spreading of the coverings, and placing of the
ark and veil. Neither completion of all sanctuary work nor the glory's arrival is narrated here.

### A close correspondence, not an identical transcript

Exodus 26:1–6 corresponds to 36:8–13; 26:7–14 to 36:14–19; 26:15–29 to 36:20–34;
and 26:31–32, 36–37 to 36:35–38. The same curtain counts, measurements, loops, clasps,
boards, sockets, and bars make compliance visible. The detailed folding and overhang directions
of 26:9, 12–13 are not restated. Their omission from the report does not establish that they
were disobeyed. Erection and furniture placement in 26:30, 33–35 belong to the later account.

**A difference in description remains explicit.** Exodus 26:37 commands overlaying the five
entrance pillars with gold; 36:38 specifies gold on their capitals and fillets. The latter does
not say that only these parts received gold, so it need not contradict the command, but neither
does it explicitly repeat a full-pillar overlay. Both statements are retained; this page does
not infer either disobedience or an exact reconstruction from the difference.

The obedience connection concerns the correspondence of the reports. It does not claim that
the full completion formula analyzed on that connection page occurs in this unit.

### Measured components without invented symbolism

The linen curtains are twenty-eight by four cubits; the goats’ hair curtains are thirty by four.
Fifty gold clasps join the linen assemblies; fifty brass clasps join the goats’ hair assemblies.
The forty-eight boards have ninety-six silver sockets in total, and the veil's four pillars
supply four more silver sockets (36:23–30, 36). These totals are arithmetic from the report,
not meanings assigned to the numbers by the text. The doorway's five sockets are brass.

The corner join in 36:29 is not enough, in the WEB alone, to settle a modern reconstruction's
board thickness, exact internal width, or roof profile. No cubit-to-meter conversion is needed.
Likewise, the animal identification in the WEB's wording at 36:19 is preserved as translation,
not independently established zoology. No symbolism of colors, skins, clasps, or sockets is
asserted by these verses.

### Two hangings, and represented cherubim

The inner veil has cherubim and four pillars with silver sockets (36:35–36). The embroidered
entrance screen has five pillars with brass sockets (36:37–38), and no cherubim are specified
for it. Their distinct functions are supplied by the earlier instructions, especially 26:33.
Manufacture of the veil is not an account of priestly entry, and later readings about access
should not be presented as explanations spoken by the makers.

The image prohibition (20:4–5), the condemned calf (32:4–8), and the commanded textile cherubim
(26:1, 31; 36:8, 35) must all remain visible. The narrative represents these figures as part of
the commissioned sanctuary; it does not authorize their worship or give a general theory of
religious art. Neither their number nor their visual form on the textiles is described.
