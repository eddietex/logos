# The Burning Bush

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 3:1–4:17

## Summary

**The God who heard, remembered, saw, and knew at the end of chapter 2 now speaks — and, after two
chapters of near-silence, he speaks from 3:4 to 4:17 almost without a break.** [[Moses]], keeping
[[Jethro]]'s flock, leads it to *"the back of the wilderness"* and comes to *"God's mountain, to
Horeb"* (3:1). [[The Angel of Yahweh]] appears to him *"in a flame of fire out of the middle of a
bush"*, and the bush burns and is not consumed. Moses turns aside to look; God calls him by name
twice from the bush; Moses answers *"Here I am"*, is told to take off his sandals on holy ground, and
hides his face. Then the commission: God has seen the affliction, heard the cry, and knows the
sorrows; he has *"come down to deliver them"* and bring them to *"a land flowing with milk and
honey"*; and he is sending Moses to [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] (3:7–10).

**Moses answers with five objections, and God answers every one.**

| | the objection | God's answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh?"* (3:11) | *"Certainly I will be with you"*, and a sign that comes afterwards (3:12) |
| 2 | *"What is his name? What should I tell them?"* (3:13) | *"I AM WHO I AM"*; *Yahweh, the God of your fathers*; and the whole plan, Pharaoh's refusal included (3:14–22) |
| 3 | *"They will not believe me"* (4:1) | three signs: the rod and the snake, the leprous hand, the water turned to blood (4:2–9) |
| 4 | *"I am not eloquent … I am slow of speech"* (4:10) | *"Who made man's mouth? … I will be with your mouth"* (4:11–12) |
| 5 | *"Oh, Lord, please send someone else"* (4:13) | anger — and Aaron, *"your brother, the Levite"*, as his mouth (4:14–17) |

**The objections get shorter and more honest as they go.** The first is modesty; the second and third
are about the people; the fourth is about Moses' own body; the fifth is simply *no*. **It is the
fifth, not any of the others, that makes Yahweh angry — and the anger does not withdraw the call.** It
adds a second man to it.

**The passage answers chapter 2 point for point.** There Moses saw his brothers' burdens and acted
without a commission, by his own hand, and was asked *"Who made you a prince and a judge over us?"*
(2:14). **Here the commission comes, and Moses — offered at last the authority that question denied
him — refuses it four times and asks for a substitute the fifth.** The man who once acted without
being sent will not go now that he is. Stephen sees the turn: *"This Moses, whom they refused, saying,
'Who made you a ruler and a judge?'—God has sent him as both a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the
angel who appeared to him in the bush"* (Acts 7:35). See
[[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].

**And the burning bush is the book in miniature.** Fire that does not consume what it burns in — a
holy presence that a creature cannot look at, settling in something lowly without destroying it — is
the problem the whole second half of Exodus works on: whether Yahweh can live in the middle of a camp.
Moses' blessing of Joseph will call God *"him who lived in the bush"* (Deuteronomy 33:16), with the
verb *šākan*, the root of *miškān*, the tabernacle. **The God who dwelt in a bush on this mountain will
end the book dwelling in a tent at its foot** (40:34–35).

### The shape of it

| | verses | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3:1–6 | the bush, the call by name, *"Here I am"*, holy ground, the God of the fathers |
| 2 | 3:7–10 | seen, heard, known; *"I have come down"*; *"I will send you"* |
| 3 | 3:11–12 | *"Who am I?"* — *"I will be with you"* |
| 4 | 3:13–22 | the name, the elders, the three days' journey, the refusal foretold, the wonders, the plunder |
| 5 | 4:1–9 | *"They will not believe me"* — three signs |
| 6 | 4:10–12 | *"I am not eloquent"* — *"Who made man's mouth?"* |
| 7 | 4:13–17 | *"Send someone else"* — anger, Aaron, the rod |

## Key verses

### The bush that is not consumed (3:1–3)

> **1** Now Moses was keeping the flock of Jethro, his father-in-law, the priest of Midian, and he led
> the flock to the back of the wilderness, and came to God's mountain, to Horeb.
> **2** Yahweh's angel appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the middle of a bush. He looked, and
> behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed.
> **3** Moses said, "I will go now, and see this great sight, why the bush is not burned."

***"Moses was keeping the flock"*** — ***rōʿeh***, a participle of continuing action — **is how the
forty years Stephen counts (Acts 7:30) are told: as one unbroken activity.** The prince of Egypt is a
shepherd of someone else's sheep, which is what Joseph's brothers told Pharaoh their family had always
been, and what Egypt despised (Genesis 46:34). **The future shepherd of Israel learns the trade on a
Midianite's flock** — David, too, will be taken *"from following the sheep"* (the wiki records this as
an allusion for when 2 Samuel 7:8 is reached).

***"The back of the wilderness"*** — ***ʾaḥar hammidbār*** — **is *behind* the wilderness**, or, since
Hebrew orients by facing east, *to the west of it*. The phrase is used nowhere else. **Moses has gone
past the edge of the grazing land, and the mountain is where nobody goes.**

***"God's mountain, to Horeb"*** — ***har hāʾĕlōhîm*** — **the narrator names the mountain by what it
will become before it has become it.** Nothing has happened on it yet. *Horeb* is the name
Deuteronomy uses for the mountain Exodus will mostly call *Sinai*; Stephen puts the bush *"in the
wilderness of Mount Sinai"* (Acts 7:30). See [[Horeb]] *(new)*.

***"Yahweh's angel appeared to him"*** — **and from the next verse it is Yahweh and God who speak.**
The alternation [[The Angel of Yahweh]] page has tracked since Genesis 16 is here at its plainest:
*the angel* appears (3:2), *Yahweh* sees him turn aside, *God* calls (3:4), and the voice says *"I am
the God of your father"* (3:6). **No seam is explained; Stephen keeps both** — *"an angel of the Lord
appeared to him"* and *"a voice of the Lord came to him"* (Acts 7:30–31).

***"A bush"*** — ***sənê*** — **is a word used only here and in Deuteronomy 33:16**, a thorny desert
shrub of uncertain species. **Its sound is almost *Sinai*** (*sînay*), and many commentators think the
narrator means the pun: the mountain of the law is named, in effect, for the bush. The wiki records
the assonance; the etymology of *Sinai* is not known.

***"The bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed"*** — ***bōʿēr bāʾēš … ʾênennû
ʾukkāl*** — **the miracle is not the fire but the bush.** Fire in Scripture so far has been a sword at
Eden's gate (Genesis 3:24), a torch between the pieces (15:17), and sulfur on Sodom (19:24): **the
presence of God at a boundary, or as judgment.** Here, for the first time, it is fire that dwells in
something without destroying it. See
[[Smoking Furnace and Flaming Torch - Fire as Theophany]].

***"I will go now, and see"*** — ***ʾāsurâ-nnāʾ wəʾerʾeh***, literally ***let me turn aside and
see***. The WEB's *go* and the next verse's *came over* both render ***sûr***, *to turn aside*. **The
whole encounter waits on a detour**: *"When Yahweh saw that he turned aside to see, God called"* (3:4).
**Moses' first act in the call narrative is curiosity, and God's first act is to notice it.** See
[[Seeing and Being Seen]].

### Moses, Moses (3:4–6)

> **4** When Yahweh saw that he came over to see, God called to him out of the middle of the bush, and
> said, "Moses! Moses!" He said, "Here I am."
> **5** He said, "Don't come close. Take off your sandals, for the place you are standing on is holy
> ground."
> **6** Moreover he said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the
> God of Jacob." Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look at God.

***"Moses! Moses!" … "Here I am"*** — ***hinnēnî*** — **is the third doubled name in Scripture**, after
*"Abraham, Abraham!"* at the knife (Genesis 22:11) and *"Jacob, Jacob!"* at Beersheba, on the way down
to Egypt (46:2). **Jacob was told there, *"I will go down with you into Egypt. I will also surely bring
you up again"*** (46:4) — and the next man God calls this way is the one he sends to do it. See
[[Jacob, Jacob - The Doubled Name and Hinneni]].

***"Don't come close"*** — **the first word God says to Moses after his name is a boundary.** The
mountain will have one too: *"Be careful that you don't go up onto the mountain, or touch its border"*
(19:12). **The holiness that Exodus spends twenty chapters building a structure for is introduced here
as a distance.**

***"Take off your sandals, for the place you are standing on is holy ground"*** — ***ʾadmat-qōdeš***
— **is the first occurrence of the noun *qōdeš*, *holiness*, in the Bible.** The root has appeared
once before as a verb: God *"made it holy"*, the seventh day (Genesis 2:3). **So the first holy thing
in Scripture is a time, and the second is a place — a patch of desert ground, holy for no reason the
text gives except that God is speaking from it.** The removal of the sandals is not explained either;
priests in the tabernacle are never told to wear shoes, and Joshua is given the same command, almost
word for word, by *"the prince of Yahweh's army"* outside Jericho: *"Take off your shoes; for the
place on which you stand is holy"* (Joshua 5:15). See [[Holiness]] *(new)*.

***"I am the God of your father"*** — ***ʾĕlōhê ʾābîkā***, ***father*** in the singular — **is the
formula of Genesis 26:24 and 46:3**, where *your father* meant Abraham and then Isaac. **For Moses it
can only mean Amram, a man who is not yet named and has done nothing in the story but marry** (2:1) —
or it means *your ancestor* collectively, and the triad that follows says which ancestors. **God
introduces himself to the man raised by an Egyptian princess by naming his Hebrew father first.** The
formula has been growing a name at a time since Genesis 26:24 (*Abraham*) and 28:13 (*Abraham* and
*Isaac*); **this is the first time God himself says all three.** See [[The Names of God]].

**Jesus will argue the resurrection from this verse.** *"Haven't you read in the book of Moses, about
the Bush, how God spoke to him, saying, 'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of
Jacob'?"* (Mark 12:26). The argument is that God says *I am*, not *I was*, their God, centuries after
their deaths — *"God is not the God of the dead, but of the living"* (Matthew 22:32). **Mark's *"about
the Bush"* is a section title**: in Jesus' day the passage was known by the name this wiki has given
it. See [[I Am the God of Abraham - Exodus 3.6 and the Resurrection]] *(new)*.

***"Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look at God"*** — **the man who was afraid of Pharaoh
(2:14) is now afraid in the other sense.** The fear of chapter 2 made him run; this one makes him stay
and cover his face. Hagar had asked *"Have I even stayed alive after seeing him?"* (Genesis 16:13);
Jacob said *"my life is preserved"* (32:30); God will tell Moses outright that *"man may not see me and
live"* (Exodus 33:20). **The same Moses will ask to see God's glory, and be shown his back** (33:18–23).
See [[The Fear of God]].

### I have come down (3:7–10)

> **7** Yahweh said, "I have surely seen the affliction of my people who are in Egypt, and have heard
> their cry because of their taskmasters, for I know their sorrows.
> **8** I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of
> that land to a good and large land, to a land flowing with milk and honey; to the place of the
> Canaanite, the Hittite, the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite.
> **9** Now, behold, the cry of the children of Israel has come to me. Moreover I have seen the
> oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them.
> **10** Come now therefore, and I will send you to Pharaoh, that you may bring my people, the children
> of Israel, out of Egypt."

**God repeats the narrator's four verbs from 2:24–25 in his own first person** — *seen*, *heard*,
*know*, and (by the cry that *"has come to me"*) remembered.

| 2:24–25, the narrator | 3:7–9, God |
|---|---|
| *God heard their groaning* | *"have heard their cry"* (3:7); *"the cry … has come to me"* (3:9) |
| *God saw the children of Israel* | *"I have surely seen the affliction"* (3:7); *"I have seen the oppression"* (3:9) |
| *God knew* | *"I know their sorrows"* (3:7) — the object 2:25 left out |

***"I have surely seen"*** — ***rāʾōh rāʾîtî***, the verb doubled for emphasis. ***"My people"*** —
***ʿammî*** — **is the first time God calls Israel *my people*.** In chapter 1 they were *"the people
of the children of Israel"* in Pharaoh's mouth (1:9); in God's, they are his.

***"I have come down"*** — ***wāʾērēd*** — **is the verb of Babel and Sodom.** *"Yahweh came down to
see the city and the tower"* (Genesis 11:5); *"I will go down now, and see whether their deeds are as
bad as the reports which have come to me"* (18:21). **Both times God came down to see an outcry and
judge it. Here he has come down having seen, to deliver** — and the outcry is from the victims, not
about the guilty. It is also the promise to Jacob kept: *"I will go down with you into Egypt"*
(Genesis 46:4).

***"To deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians"*** — ***ləhaṣṣîlô miyyad miṣrayim*** — **is the
Midianite daughters' sentence with the nouns exchanged**: *"An Egyptian delivered us out of the hand of
the shepherds"* (2:19). See [[Moses Flees to Midian]].

***"A land flowing with milk and honey"*** — ***ʾereṣ zābat ḥālāb ûdəbāš*** — **the phrase's first
occurrence in Scripture**, and it will be said about twenty times more, all of them about this land.
*Milk* is pastoral wealth — goats and sheep, a shepherd's measure of good country — and *honey* is
probably date or fig syrup as much as bees'. **It is a shepherd's description, given to a shepherd.**

***"The Canaanite, the Hittite, the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite"*** — **five
of them are from the ten peoples of Genesis 15:19–21**, the list God gave Abram the night he foretold
the four hundred years; the Hivite is new, and the list of six becomes the standard one. **The land is described twice in one verse, once by what it yields and once by who
owns it**, and the second description is the problem. See [[Canaan]] and [[The Nations]].

***"I will send you"*** — ***wəʾešlāḥăkā*** — **the verb of the call, *šālaḥ*, will run through the
whole passage** (3:10, 12, 13, 14, 15; 4:13) **and then become the word of the confrontation**: *"Let my
people go"* is *šallaḥ ʾet-ʿammî*, the same verb in the intensive, *send away my people*. **God sends
Moses so that Pharaoh will send Israel.** See [[Call and Election]].

### Who am I? (3:11–12)

> **11** Moses said to God, "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh, and that I should bring the
> children of Israel out of Egypt?"
> **12** He said, "Certainly I will be with you. This will be the token to you, that I have sent you:
> when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain."

***"Who am I?"*** — ***mî ʾānōkî*** — **is the first objection of the called in Scripture**, and the
pattern it starts is one of the Bible's most regular. Gideon: *"how shall I save Israel? Behold, my
family is the poorest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house"* (Judges 6:15). Saul: *"Am
I not a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel?"* (1 Samuel 9:21). Isaiah: *"Woe is me!
For I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips"* (Isaiah 6:5). Jeremiah: *"Behold, I don't know
how to speak; for I am a child"* (Jeremiah 1:6). **And God answers Gideon in nearly the words he gives
Moses**: *"Surely I will be with you"* (Judges 6:16). See
[[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]] *(new)*.

**It is also the answer to 2:14.** *"Who made you a prince and a judge over us?"* asked who Moses was
to act. **Moses now asks the same question of himself, and God does not answer it** — he does not tell
Moses who he is. He tells him who will be with him.

***"Certainly I will be with you"*** — ***kî-ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*** — **is the key to the name two verses
later.** *ʾEhyeh*, *I will be*, is the first person of *hāyâ*, *to be*, and it is exactly the word God
will give as his name in 3:14. **Before Moses asks the name, he has already heard it — as a promise of
company.** It is the promise made to Isaac (*"I will be with you"*, Genesis 26:3) and to Jacob twice
(28:15; 31:3), now made to the man who will bring their descendants out. It comes back twice more in
this passage, to the mouth (4:12, 15).

***"This will be the token to you … when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God
on this mountain"*** — **a sign that can be checked only after Moses has obeyed.** Every other sign in
the passage (4:2–9) is given to persuade in advance; this one confirms in arrears. **The proof that God
sent Moses is that Moses will be back here, with all of them** — which happens at 19:1–2.

***"You shall serve God"*** — ***taʿabdûn*** — **is *ʿābad*, the book's governing root.** Egypt made
Israel *serve* with hard *service* (1:13–14); the first word God says about Israel's future is that
they will *serve* him, on this mountain. **The exodus is announced, before anything else, as a change
of master.** See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

### I AM WHO I AM (3:13–15)

> **13** Moses said to God, "Behold, when I come to the children of Israel, and tell them, 'The God of
> your fathers has sent me to you;' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' What should I tell them?"
> **14** God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM," and he said, "You shall tell the children of Israel this:
> 'I AM has sent me to you.'"
> **15** God said moreover to Moses, "You shall tell the children of Israel this, 'Yahweh, the God of
> your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is
> my name forever, and this is my memorial to all generations.

***"What is his name?"*** — ***mah-šəmô*** — **is a strange question for Israel to ask.** The God of
the fathers was not nameless; Genesis has the patriarchs calling on *Yahweh* from 4:26 onward. **The
question is probably not *what is he called* but *what does his name mean for us now*** — *mah*, *what*,
asks about character where *mî*, *who*, would ask for identity. That is the standard reading; the other
is that the people in Egypt have forgotten the name, which 6:3 can be read to support. See
[[Calling on the Name of Yahweh]].

***"I AM WHO I AM"*** — ***ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh*** — **the Hebrew is the first person imperfect of
*hāyâ*, and the imperfect is not tied to a time.** It can be *I am who I am*, *I will be what I will
be*, or *I will be who I am*, and the three readings each have their defenders:

1. **Self-existence** — *I am the one who is.* This is the Septuagint's ***egō eimi ho ōn***, *I am the
   one who is*, and through it the classical theology of God as being itself.
2. **Faithful presence** — *I will be there, as who I will be.* This takes the ***ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*** of
   3:12 as the key: the name is the promise of company, made into a noun. It is the reading most Old
   Testament commentary now prefers.
3. **Reticence** — *I am who I am, and that is all you will be told.* The same construction (*X ʾăšer
   X*) elsewhere closes a subject rather than opening it, and the scene belongs with the two in which a
   divine figure refuses a name outright (Genesis 32:29; Judges 13:18). See
   [[Why Do You Ask My Name - Genesis 32, Exodus 3, and Judges 13]].

**The wiki records all three and adopts none.** What the text does make plain is the link to 3:12:
*ʾehyeh* is said there as a promise before it is said here as a name.

***"I AM has sent me to you"*** — ***ʾehyeh šəlāḥanî*** — **the name used as a subject, once, and never
again.** In the next verse it becomes ***YHWH***, the third person of the same verb — *he is*, or *he
will be* — **so that God calls himself *I am* and Israel will call him *He is*.** That relation between
*ʾehyeh* and *Yahweh* is the gloss Exodus gives the name; it is not an etymology, and the original
meaning of the four letters is not recoverable. See [[The Names of God]].

**John's Gospel reaches for the verse.** *"Most certainly, I tell you, before Abraham came into
existence, I AM"* (John 8:58) — the Septuagint's *egō eimi*, said without a predicate, by a man
arguing about Abraham. **The hearers' response — they take up stones (8:59) — shows how they heard
it.** Revelation's *"who is and who was and who is to come"* (Revelation 1:8) is widely read as an
expansion of the same name. See [[I AM WHO I AM - Exodus 3.14 and John 8.58]] *(new)*.

***"This is my name forever, and this is my memorial to all generations"*** — ***zeh-šəmî ləʿōlām
wəzeh zikrî lədōr dōr*** — ***zēker***, *memorial*, *the way I am to be remembered*, **is the noun of
*zākar*, the verb of 2:24.** God remembered his covenant; now he gives Israel the means to remember
him. **The name is the people's half of remembrance.** See [[Remembrance]] and
[[God Remembered - Noah, Abraham, Rachel, and Israel]].

### The plan, the refusal, and the plunder (3:16–22)

> **16** Go and gather the elders of Israel together, and tell them, 'Yahweh, the God of your fathers,
> the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, has appeared to me, saying, "I have surely visited you,
> and seen that which is done to you in Egypt;
> **17** and I have said, I will bring you up out of the affliction of Egypt to the land of the
> Canaanite, the Hittite, the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite, to a land flowing
> with milk and honey."'
> **18** They will listen to your voice, and you shall come, you and the elders of Israel, to the king
> of Egypt, and you shall tell him, 'Yahweh, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. Now please let us
> go three days' journey into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to Yahweh, our God.'
> **19** I know that the king of Egypt won't give you permission to go, no, not by a mighty hand.
> **20** I will reach out my hand and strike Egypt with all my wonders which I will do among them, and
> after that he will let you go.
> **21** I will give this people favor in the sight of the Egyptians, and it will happen that when you
> go, you shall not go empty-handed.
> **22** But every woman shall ask of her neighbor, and of her who visits her house, jewels of silver,
> jewels of gold, and clothing; and you shall put them on your sons, and on your daughters. You shall
> plunder the Egyptians."

***"I have surely visited you"*** — ***pāqōd pāqadtî*** — **is Joseph's dying sentence, kept word for
word.** *"God will surely visit you"* — *pāqōd yipqōd* — he said twice, the second time making his
brothers swear to carry his bones (Genesis 50:24–25). **The password Moses is to give the elders is
the last thing Joseph said.** When the people hear *"that Yahweh had visited the children of Israel"*,
they believe (4:31). See [[Joseph]] and [[Remembrance]].

***"The elders of Israel"*** — **the first mention of any institution among the Hebrews in Egypt.** The
people who had no leader in chapters 1–2 turn out to have heads of families whom Moses can gather.
Moses and Aaron gather them at 4:29.

***"Yahweh, the God of the Hebrews"*** — **the name the message to Pharaoh uses, and the only one he
could be expected to know.** *Hebrew* has been the outsider's word all along — Potiphar's wife's
(*"a Hebrew"*, Genesis 39:14), the Egyptians' (43:32), Pharaoh's daughter's (Exodus 2:6). **God
introduces himself to Egypt by the name Egypt uses for his people.** See
[[Abram the Hebrew - The First Use of the Name]].

***"Please let us go three days' journey into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice"*** — **is the
request less than the plan?** God has just told Moses he will bring Israel out to Canaan (3:8, 17); the
request to Pharaoh asks only for a three-day festival. **The readings divide:**

1. **A test, not a deception.** The request is modest on purpose, so that Pharaoh's refusal of even
   this exposes him; it is never withdrawn in favour of a larger one, and Pharaoh himself will later
   name the larger stakes (10:10–11).
2. **A deliberate understatement**, which some ancient and modern readers have found troubling and
   others have defended as a legitimate ruse against an oppressor, in the line of the midwives' answer
   (1:19).
3. **The sacrifice is real, and its place is this mountain** (3:12) — three days' journey being a way
   of saying *out of Egypt's sight*, since what Israel sacrifices Egypt holds sacred (8:26).

**The wiki records the three and does not decide.** See [[Deception]] and
[[Sacrifice and Offering]].

***"I know that the king of Egypt won't give you permission to go, no, not by a mighty hand"*** —
***wəlōʾ bəyād ḥăzāqâ*** — **the Hebrew is ambiguous, and so are the translations.** The WEB takes it
as *not even under compulsion*; the Septuagint and many modern versions read *unless by a mighty hand*
— **which fits the next verse**, where God's hand does compel him. **Either way, the refusal is
foreknown before the request is made**, and Moses is sent to ask anyway. See
[[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] *(new)*.

***"I will reach out my hand and strike Egypt with all my wonders"*** — ***wəhikkêtî***, *I will
strike*, **is *nākâ*, the verb Moses used on one Egyptian in secret** (2:12). **What Moses did to one
man, by his own hand, God will do to the country, by his.** *"My wonders"* — ***niplāʾōtay*** — is
the first naming of the plagues. See [[Signs and Wonders]] *(new)* and [[Judgment]].

***"You shall not go empty-handed"*** — ***lōʾ tēləkû rêqām*** — **is the promise of Genesis 15:14
dated and specified**: *"Afterward they will come out with great wealth"*. **And it becomes the law of
release**: a Hebrew slave set free *"you shall not let him go empty"* (Deuteronomy 15:13), the same
*rêqām*. **Israel is to leave Egypt the way an Israelite slave is to leave Israel — paid.**

***"You shall plunder the Egyptians"*** — ***wəniṣṣaltem*** — **is *nāṣal*, the verb of 3:8, in another
stem.** *Hiṣṣîl* (3:8) is *to rescue, to snatch out*; *niṣṣēl* (3:22) is *to strip*. **God delivers
Israel from Egypt, and Israel strips Egypt, in one root.** The narrator reports it done in the same
words (12:35–36). See [[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]] *(new)* and
[[Wealth and Possessions]].

***"Every woman shall ask of her neighbor, and of her who visits her house"*** — ***miggərat
bêtāh***, *from the one sojourning in her house* — **uses *gûr*, the root of *gēr*, the word Gershom
was named for** (2:22). **The Egyptian woman is a *sojourner* in the Hebrew woman's house.** The
plunder is collected by women from women, as the deliverance began with women; and the clothes go on
the sons and daughters — **on the children Pharaoh ordered drowned.**

### They will not believe me (4:1–9)

> **1** Moses answered, "But, behold, they will not believe me, nor listen to my voice; for they will
> say, 'Yahweh has not appeared to you.'"
> **2** Yahweh said to him, "What is that in your hand?" He said, "A rod."
> **3** He said, "Throw it on the ground." He threw it on the ground, and it became a snake; and Moses
> ran away from it.
> **4** Yahweh said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand, and take it by the tail." He stretched out his
> hand, and took hold of it, and it became a rod in his hand.
> **5** "That they may believe that Yahweh, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of
> Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you."
> **6** Yahweh said furthermore to him, "Now put your hand inside your cloak." He put his hand inside
> his cloak, and when he took it out, behold, his hand was leprous, as white as snow.
> **7** He said, "Put your hand inside your cloak again." He put his hand inside his cloak again, and
> when he took it out of his cloak, behold, it had turned again as his other flesh.
> **8** "It will happen, if they will not believe you or listen to the voice of the first sign, that
> they will believe the voice of the latter sign.
> **9** It will happen, if they will not believe even these two signs or listen to your voice, that you
> shall take of the water of the river, and pour it on the dry land. The water which you take out of
> the river will become blood on the dry land."

***"They will not believe me"*** — ***lōʾ-yaʾămînû lî*** — **is *ʾāman*, the verb of Abram's faith**
(*"He believed in Yahweh"*, Genesis 15:6), **and it appears five times in nine verses** (4:1, 5, 8 twice,
9). **The third objection contradicts what God has just said** — *"They will listen to your voice"*
(3:18) — and God does not point that out. He gives signs. **The passage closes the question at 4:31:
*"The people believed."*** See [[Faith]].

***"What is that in your hand?"*** — ***mazzeh bəyādekā***, written as one word — **and the answer is
*maṭṭeh*, a rod.** Rabbinic readers heard the near-rhyme (*mazzeh* / *maṭṭeh*); the wiki notes it. **The
question is about what Moses already has.** A shepherd's staff becomes *"God's rod"* (4:20), the
instrument of the plagues and the sea; nothing new is put into his hand. See [[Providence]].

***"It became a snake; and Moses ran away from it"*** — ***nāḥāš*** — **the word of Genesis 3**, the
serpent of the garden. **When Aaron's rod becomes a serpent before Pharaoh, the word is different —
*tannîn*** (7:9–10), the word for the sea-monsters of creation (Genesis 1:21). **The private sign is a
snake; the public one is a dragon.** *"Moses ran away from it"* is the narrator's only comic touch in
the passage: the man who will face Pharaoh runs from his own walking-stick.

***"Take it by the tail"*** — **the one place no snake-handler would grab.** The command is to do the
dangerous thing first and find it safe afterwards, which is the shape of the whole commission.

***"His hand was leprous, as white as snow"*** — ***məṣōraʿat kaššāleg*** — ***ṣāraʿat*** **is a
skin disease the Torah treats as ritual uncleanness** (Leviticus 13–14), not necessarily what is now
called leprosy, and *as white as snow* is its formula: **Miriam is struck with it for speaking against
Moses** (*"Miriam was leprous, as white as snow"*, Numbers 12:10), **and Gehazi for lying to Elisha**
(*"He went out from his presence a leper, as white as snow"*, 2 Kings 5:27). **Moses' hand is the only
one in Scripture that goes white and is restored within a verse, with no fault attached.** The sign
shows that the God who sends him can make clean and unclean at will. See [[Clean and Unclean]].

***"The voice of the first sign"*** — ***ləqōl hāʾōt hāriʾšôn*** — **a sign has a voice.** Moses had
feared they would not *"listen to my voice"* (4:1); God answers that the signs will speak where he
cannot. **The fourth objection, about his mouth, is already half-answered by the third's reply.** See
[[Signs and Wonders]].

***"The water which you take out of the river will become blood on the dry land"*** — **the first
plague, in a cup.** The river of 1:22, into which the sons were cast, and of 2:3–5, from which Moses
was drawn, **will be turned to blood at 7:17–21** — and this sign is the preview. *"On the dry land"*
— ***bayyabbāšâ*** — is the *dry land* of Genesis 1:9, and the *dry ground* Israel will cross the sea
on (14:16, 22). See
[[The Nile]] and [[Blood]].

### Not a man of words (4:10–12)

> **10** Moses said to Yahweh, "O Lord, I am not eloquent, neither before now, nor since you have
> spoken to your servant; for I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue."
> **11** Yahweh said to him, "Who made man's mouth? Or who makes one mute, or deaf, or seeing, or blind?
> Isn't it I, Yahweh?
> **12** Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak."

***"I am not eloquent"*** — ***lōʾ ʾîš dəbārîm ʾānōkî***, ***not a man of words am I*** — **and he
says so in one of the longest speeches anyone makes in Exodus.** The man who will speak Deuteronomy —
which opens *"These are the words (haddəbārîm) which Moses spoke"* (Deuteronomy 1:1) — begins by
denying he is a man of *dəbārîm*.

***"Slow of speech, and of a slow tongue"*** — ***kəbad-peh ûkəbad lāšôn***, ***heavy of mouth and
heavy of tongue*** — **is *kābēd*, the word that will describe Pharaoh's heart.** *"Pharaoh's heart is
stubborn"* (7:14) is *kābēd lēb*, *heavy of heart*. **The plagues will be a contest between a man with
a heavy mouth and a king with a heavy heart.** What Moses' heaviness was — a speech impediment, a lack
of fluency, an Egyptian-raised man's poor Hebrew, or simply reluctance — **the text does not say**, and
he repeats the objection in another form twice later: *"who am of uncircumcised lips"* (6:12, 30).

**Stephen says the opposite.** *"Moses was instructed in all the wisdom of the Egyptians. He was mighty
in his words and works"* (Acts 7:22). **The wiki records the tension and two standard readings**: that
Stephen describes Moses as he became, after God was *"with his mouth"*, and Exodus as he saw himself; or
that *mighty in words* refers to what Moses said and wrote — the Torah — rather than his delivery.
Neither is adopted. See [[Language and Speech]].

***"Who made man's mouth?"*** — ***mî śām peh lāʾādām*** — ***śām***, *put, set*, **is the verb of
2:14: *"Who made (śāmkā) you a prince and a judge?"*** **Both questions begin *mî śām*, *who set*. The
first denied Moses a commission; the second gives it, and gives it on the ground that the one who set
the mouth in the man can set the man in the office.** *"Mute, or deaf, or seeing, or blind"* — **God
claims the disabilities with the faculties**, a claim Jesus' disciples will put to him as a question
(*"who sinned, this man or his parents?"*, John 9:2; recorded for when John is reached). See
[[Creation]].

***"I will be with your mouth"*** — ***ʾehyeh ʿim-pîkā*** — **the third *ʾehyeh* of the passage**:
*I will be with you* (3:12), *I AM* (3:14), *I will be with your mouth* (4:12). **The name is a promise
that keeps narrowing to the point of need.** Jesus makes the same promise to disciples brought before
kings: *"I will give you a mouth and wisdom which all your adversaries will not be able to withstand or
to contradict"* (Luke 21:15).

### Send someone else (4:13–17)

> **13** He said, "Oh, Lord, please send someone else."
> **14** Yahweh's anger burned against Moses, and he said, "What about Aaron, your brother, the Levite?
> I know that he can speak well. Also, behold, he comes out to meet you. When he sees you, he will be
> glad in his heart.
> **15** You shall speak to him, and put the words in his mouth. I will be with your mouth, and with his
> mouth, and will teach you what you shall do.
> **16** He will be your spokesman to the people; and it will happen, that he will be to you a mouth,
> and you will be to him as God.
> **17** You shall take this rod in your hand, with which you shall do the signs."

***"Please send someone else"*** — ***šəlaḥ-nāʾ bəyad-tišlāḥ***, ***send, please, by the hand of
whomever you will send*** — **is *šālaḥ* twice, thrown back at the God who said *"I will send you"*
(3:10).** It is not an objection; there is nothing in it to answer. **It is the only one of the five
God does not answer with an argument.**

***"Yahweh's anger burned against Moses"*** — ***wayyiḥar-ʾap YHWH bəMōšeh*** — **the first time in
Scripture the narrator says Yahweh's anger burned.** Abraham had pleaded *"Oh don't let the Lord be
angry"* (Genesis 18:30, 32) of an anger that did not come. **And it is spent on a man who will not go —
and then it does nothing.** God does not withdraw the call or punish the refusal; he gives Moses
help. Whether the anger costs Moses anything — the rabbinic view that the priesthood passed from Moses'
line to Aaron's here (Babylonian Talmud, Zevachim 102a) — the text does not say, and the wiki records
the tradition without endorsing it. See [[Divine Grief and Relenting]].

***"Aaron, your brother, the Levite"*** — **why *the Levite*, when Moses is one too?** Three readings:
that it marks Aaron's future office, since the priesthood will be *Levite* in a special sense; that
*Levite* already meant a professional function, not only a descent; or that it is simply full
identification, *your brother Aaron, of the house of Levi*. **The wiki records them and adopts none.**
[[Aaron]] *(new)* is introduced as already on his way: *"behold, he comes out to meet you"*. **Before
Moses has agreed to anything, the help is travelling.** It meets him at 4:27, *"on God's mountain"*.

***"I know that he can speak well"*** — ***dabbēr yədabbēr hûʾ***, *he can surely speak*. ***"When he
sees you, he will be glad in his heart"*** — **after Genesis, a startling thing to say of a brother.**
Cain killed Abel; Ishmael was sent away from Isaac; Esau planned to kill Jacob, and their meeting
twenty years later was weeping on the neck (Genesis 33:4); Joseph's brothers sold him. **Here, for
the first time, a brother's coming is announced as gladness before it happens**, and there is no
birthright between them to contend for. Rivalry does come, later and over the prophetic office, when
Aaron joins Miriam against Moses (Numbers 12). See [[Brotherhood]].

***"He will be to you a mouth, and you will be to him as God"*** — ***yihyeh-lləkā ləpeh wəʾattâ
tihyeh-llô lēʾlōhîm*** — **the clearest definition of a prophet in the Torah, given before the word
*prophet* is used of either man.** God puts words in Moses' mouth; Moses puts words in Aaron's (4:15).
At 7:1 the sentence is made explicit: *"I have made you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother shall
be your prophet."* **And Deuteronomy turns it into the promise of a prophet like Moses**: *"I will put
my words in his mouth, and he shall speak to them all that I shall command him"* (Deuteronomy 18:18);
Jeremiah is told *"Behold, I have put my words in your mouth"* (Jeremiah 1:9). See
[[Prophecy and Vision]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

***"You shall take this rod in your hand, with which you shall do the signs"*** — **the passage ends
on the object, not the man.** Moses has not said yes. The next verse has him go to his father-in-law
(4:18), and the narrator never records his consent: **the call narrative of the Bible's greatest
prophet ends with him still objecting, and simply going.**

## Moses' objections and God's *ʾehyeh*

**Read whole, the passage is an argument Moses loses and does not concede.** He has a reason for each
refusal, and each reason is answered with something God will be or do; none is answered with
something Moses is.

| Moses says | God answers |
|---|---|
| *who am I* (3:11) | ***ʾehyeh ʿimmāk***, *I will be with you* (3:12) |
| *what is his name* (3:13) | ***ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh*** (3:14) |
| *they will not believe* (4:1) | signs that have a *voice* (4:8) |
| *I am heavy of mouth* (4:10) | ***ʾehyeh ʿim-pîkā***, *I will be with your mouth* (4:12) |
| *send someone else* (4:13) | *I will be with your mouth, and with his mouth* (4:15) |

**The God of the Torah does not choose the competent.** Abraham was childless and old, Jacob a
deceiver, Joseph a boastful younger son; Moses is a fugitive, a killer, a shepherd, and by his own
account no speaker. **What makes the call work in every case is the clause God attaches to it, and in
Exodus that clause is his name.** See [[Call and Election]].

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** The pericope ends at 4:17, with the rod, before Moses goes to Jethro
  (4:18). The call narrative is one continuous dialogue on the mountain, and 4:18 changes scene.
- **The angel and God** — recorded as alternating, per the standing practice on
  [[The Angel of Yahweh]]; not harmonized.
- **The meaning of *ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh*** — three standard readings (self-existence, faithful
  presence, reticence) recorded; none adopted. The link to *ʾehyeh ʿimmāk* (3:12) is grammatical and
  is stated as such.
- **The three days' journey** — three readings recorded (a test, a ruse, a literal festival at the
  mountain); none adopted.
- **3:19, *wəlōʾ bəyād ḥăzāqâ*** — *not even by* (WEB) or *unless by* (LXX and others) a mighty hand;
  both recorded.
- **Acts 7:22 against Exodus 4:10** — *"mighty in his words"* against *"not a man of words"*;
  recorded as a tension with two readings.
- **Zevachim 102a** on the priesthood passing to Aaron is cited as a rabbinic tradition, not as the
  text's claim.
- **First occurrences claimed on this page** — *qōdeš* (3:5), *"milk and honey"* (3:8), *"my people"*
  in God's mouth (3:7), Yahweh's anger burning as narrated fact (4:14), and all three patriarchs named
  in God's own self-introduction (3:6) — were checked against Genesis and Exodus 1–2 in `raw/bible/`.
  The *sənê*/*Sinai* assonance is recorded as a commonly noted pun, not an etymology.
