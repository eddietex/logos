# The Last Plague Announced

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 11:1–10

## Summary

**The shortest chapter of the plague narrative, and the hinge of it.** Nothing happens in Exodus 11.
No rod is stretched out and nothing falls on Egypt. God speaks to Moses, Moses speaks to Pharaoh, and
the narrator closes the whole sequence of wonders in two verses. **The tenth blow is announced here and
struck in chapter 12, with the Passover in between.**

**God to Moses (11:1–3).** *"Yet one plague more will I bring on Pharaoh, and on Egypt; afterwards he
will let you go. When he lets you go, he will surely thrust you out altogether"* (11:1). Israel is to
ask its Egyptian neighbours, men and women, for *"jewels of silver, and jewels of gold"* (11:2). The
narrator adds two facts, as if they were already true: *"Yahweh gave the people favor in the sight of
the Egyptians. Moreover the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's
servants, and in the sight of the people"* (11:3).

**Moses to Pharaoh (11:4–8).** *"About midnight I will go out into the middle of Egypt, and all the
firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die"*, from the heir on the throne to the slave girl at the mill,
and the firstborn of the livestock (11:4–5). There will be *"a great cry throughout all the land of
Egypt, such as there has not been, nor shall be any more"* (11:6). **Against Israel *"a dog won't even
bark or move its tongue"***, *"that you may know that Yahweh makes a distinction between the Egyptians
and Israel"* (11:7). Pharaoh's servants *"will come down to me, and bow down themselves to me, saying,
'Get out'"* (11:8). ***"He went out from Pharaoh in hot anger"*** (11:8).

**The narrator's close (11:9–10).** *"Pharaoh won't listen to you, that my wonders may be multiplied in
the land of Egypt"*; and *"Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, and Yahweh hardened
Pharaoh's heart, and he didn't let the children of Israel go out of his land"* (11:9–10). **These two
verses repeat the forecast of 7:3–4 nearly word for word, and so close the plague cycle where it was
opened.**

### The shape of it

| | 11:1–3 | 11:4–8 | 11:9–10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speaker | Yahweh to Moses | Moses to Pharaoh | Yahweh to Moses; the narrator |
| Subject | the release and the plunder | the death of the firstborn | the whole cycle of wonders |
| Looks back to | 3:20–22; 6:1 | 4:22–23 | 4:21; 7:3–4 |
| Looks ahead to | 12:31–36, 39 | 12:29–30 | 14:4, 8 |

**The chapter is all forecast.** Every promise in it is kept in chapter 12, most of them in the same
words: the midnight and the firstborn *"from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne"* (12:29);
the *"great cry in Egypt"* (12:30); Pharaoh's *"get out"* (12:31); the Egyptians *"urgent … to send them
out of the land in haste"* (12:33); the silver, gold, and favour (12:35–36); and *"they were thrust out
of Egypt"* (12:39).

## Key verses

### One plague more (11:1)

> **1** Yahweh said to Moses, "Yet one plague more will I bring on Pharaoh, and on Egypt; afterwards he
> will let you go. When he lets you go, he will surely thrust you out altogether.

***"Yet one plague more"*** — ***ʿôd negaʿ ʾeḥād***. **This is the only *negaʿ* in Exodus** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy);
the one collective name the blows have had so far is *maggēpōtay*, *"my plagues"* (9:14).
Elsewhere in the Torah *negaʿ* is overwhelmingly the *"plague"* of skin disease and mould in Leviticus
13–14, picked up in Deuteronomy 17:8; 21:5; 24:8. **Its one use in Genesis is the other Pharaoh's**:
*"Yahweh afflicted Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai"* (Genesis 12:17), where
the verb and the noun are the same root, *wayənaggaʿ … nəgāʿîm gədōlîm*. **The first Pharaoh in
Scripture was struck with *nəgāʿîm* and let a patriarch go with his wealth; the last blow on this
Pharaoh is a *negaʿ*, and Israel goes with Egypt's.** Whether the writer chose the word for the echo
cannot be shown. See [[Abram in Egypt]] and
[[Down to Egypt and Up Again - Abram's Descent as Proto-Exodus]].

***"He will surely thrust you out altogether"*** — ***kəšalləḥô kālâ gārēš yəgārēš ʾetkem***.
***Gārēš yəgārēš*** is an infinitive with its finite verb, the Hebrew way of saying *surely*. It is
God's promise at 6:1 come back — *"by a strong hand he shall drive them out (yəgārəšēm) of his land"* —
and the verb of the narrator at 12:39, *"they were thrust out (gōrəšû) of Egypt"*. **Pharaoh, who has
been asked again and again to *send* (*šālaḥ*), will finally do more than send.** ***Kālâ***, *altogether* or
*completely*, is a rare noun: **in the Torah it occurs only here and at Genesis 18:21**, where God goes
down to see whether Sodom has done *"altogether"* (*kālâ*) according to its outcry (WLC lemma search;
the WEB renders 18:21 differently). The second word of 18:21 is the cry. See below, at 11:6.

### Ask, and the favour given (11:2–3)

> **2** Speak now in the ears of the people, and let every man ask of his neighbor, and every woman of
> her neighbor, jewels of silver, and jewels of gold."
> **3** Yahweh gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover the man Moses was very great
> in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants, and in the sight of the people.

***"In the ears of the people"*** — ***bəʾoznê hāʿām***, the phrase of 10:2, *"that you may tell in the
hearing (bəʾoznê) of your son"*. **The telling begins before the event: the people are told what to ask
for before the night it is given.**

***"Every man … and every woman"*** — at the bush only women were to ask, *"every woman shall ask of her
neighbor, and of her who visits her house"* (3:22). **Here the men ask too, of *rēʿēhû*, his
neighbour, and the women of *rəʿûtāh*.** The order is carried out at 12:35. This is the middle term of
the chain recorded at [[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]]: promised to Abram
(Genesis 15:14), specified at the bush (3:21–22), ordered here, done in 12:35–36.

***"Yahweh gave the people favor"*** — ***wayyittēn YHWH ʾet-ḥēn hāʿām***. **The narrator tells it as
already done, in a speech about what is still to come.** The same clause stands at 3:21 (as promise) and
12:36 (as fact); in Exodus *ḥēn* occurs only at these three verses before chapter 33, where it becomes
Moses' word with God, *"I have found favor in your sight"* (33:12–17; WLC lemma search). Joseph was
given favour *"in the sight of the keeper of the prison"* (Genesis 39:21); Israel is given it in the
sight of the people who enslaved it. See [[Favor and Grace]].

***"The man Moses was very great"*** — ***gam hāʾîš mōšeh gādôl məʾōd***. **The same three words open
Numbers 12:3 with a different adjective**: *"Now the man Moses was very humble (wəhāʾîš mōšeh ʿānāw
məʾōd), more than all the men who were on the surface of the earth"* (WEB; Hebrew checked in the WLC).
**Exodus calls him great in Egypt's eyes, and Numbers calls him humble in God's.** *Gādôl məʾōd* is
said of Isaac at Gerar, *"until he became very great"* (Genesis 26:13). **And he is great *"in the sight
of Pharaoh's servants"*** — the court that called him *"a snare"* one chapter earlier (10:7). See
[[Moses]] and [[Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)]].

### About midnight (11:4–6)

> **4** Moses said, "This is what Yahweh says: 'About midnight I will go out into the middle of Egypt,
> **5** and all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on
> his throne, even to the firstborn of the female servant who is behind the mill; and all the firstborn
> of livestock.
> **6** There shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there has not been, nor
> shall be any more.

**The seam with 10:28–29.** Pharaoh has just said *"in the day you see my face you shall die!"*, and
Moses *"I will see your face again no more"*. Here Moses is speaking to Pharaoh, and at 11:8 he *"went
out from Pharaoh"*. **The simplest reading takes 11:4–8 as the rest of the same audience**: Moses'
answer at 10:29 and his last speech are one scene, and 11:1–3 is a word God had already given, set
here because the speech depends on it. Readers who see composite sources in Exodus take the seam as a
join. The text does not resolve it, and neither does this page — see the matching note on
[[Locusts and Darkness]].

***"About midnight"*** — ***kaḥăṣōt hallaylâ***. **The form *ḥăṣōt* occurs nowhere else in the Torah**
(WLC lemma search); when the blow falls, the narrator says *baḥăṣî hallaylâ*, *in the half of the
night* (12:29). **The *k-*, *about*, is Moses' and not the narrator's**: the man announcing the hour
gives it as an approximation; the account of the event gives it exactly. No day is named.

***"I will go out into the middle of Egypt"*** — ***ʾănî yôṣēʾ bətôk miṣrāyim***. **The exodus verb,
*yāṣāʾ*, used of God before it is used of Israel.** It comes four times in 11:4–8: God *goes out* into
Egypt (11:4); the servants will say *"Get out"* (*ṣēʾ*); Moses *"after that I will go out"* (*ʾēṣēʾ*);
and *"He went out"* (*wayyēṣēʾ*) from Pharaoh (11:8). **At 12:31 Pharaoh says it himself**: *"Rise up,
get out (ṣəʾû) from among (mittôk) my people"* — God goes out *into the middle* of Egypt, and Israel
goes out *from the middle* of it.

***"From the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne, even to the firstborn of the female servant
who is behind the mill"*** — ***mibbəkôr parʿōh hayyōšēb ʿal-kisʾô ʿad bəkôr haššipḥâ ʾăšer ʾaḥar
hārēḥāyim***. **A merism: the top of Egypt and the bottom, and everyone between.** The firstborn of
Pharaoh *"who sits on his throne"* is the heir, so the blow strikes the succession. The *šipḥâ*, the
slave girl, is the word of Hagar (Genesis 16:1) and occurs nowhere else in Exodus (WLC lemma search). The
mill, *rēḥayim*, the pair of hand-stones, is in the Torah only here, at the manna *"ground … in mills"*
(Numbers 11:8), and in the law that forbids taking *"the mill or the upper millstone as a pledge; for he
takes a life in pledge"* (Deuteronomy 24:6; both WEB, Hebrew checked in the WLC). **When the night
comes, the bottom of the merism changes**: *"to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon"*
(12:29), *bêt habbôr*, the *bôr* of Joseph's pit and prison (Genesis 37:24; 40:15; 41:14; WLC).

**The slave girl's son is the hardest line in the chapter.** She has no part in Pharaoh's decisions,
and her firstborn dies with his. The text gives no comment. Readers have answered in several ways —
that a nation is judged as a body with its king, as Genesis 18 debated whether a city could be spared
for its righteous; that Egypt's own decree against Israel's sons (1:16, 22) was a whole people's act
(*"Pharaoh commanded all his people"*, 1:22); that the firstborn of every household, high and low,
stood for Egypt's future as Israel's firstborn did for Israel's (4:22–23). The wiki records the
answers and adopts none. **The decree of 1:22 was the earlier Pharaoh's**, [[Pharaoh (of the oppression)]],
who died at 2:23 — so *measure for measure*, if it is read that way, falls on his successor's house.
See [[Retribution]].

***"All the firstborn of livestock"*** — after the pestilence killed *"all the livestock of Egypt"*
(9:6) and the hail struck the animals left in the field (9:25), there are still livestock to die. The
page on [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]] records the ways readers have handled this; this verse adds a
third count to the two.

***"A great cry"*** — ***ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ***. **The cry has changed sides.** *Ṣəʿāqâ* was Israel's: God
*"heard their cry because of their taskmasters"* (3:7) and *"the cry of the children of Israel has come
to me"* (3:9). Now it is Egypt's, and at 12:30 the narrator repeats the phrase: *"there was a great cry
in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one dead"*. **The only other *ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ*, the noun
with the adjective, in the Torah is Esau's**, when he learns the firstborn's blessing is gone: *"he cried with an exceeding
great and bitter cry"* (Genesis 27:34, *wayyiṣʿaq ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ ûmārâ ʿad-məʾōd*; WLC lemma search).
**Both great cries in the Torah are cries over a firstborn.** And the word's first use is Sodom's
*outcry* (Genesis 18:21), which *"has grown so great"* — *gādəlâ*, the verb — at 19:13 — the cry of the wronged reaching God, which Exodus 22:23 makes law:
*"if they cry at all to me, I will surely hear their cry"*. See
[[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]].

***"Such as there has not been, nor shall be any more"*** — ***ʾăšer kāmōhû lōʾ nihyātâ wəkāmōhû lōʾ
tōsip***. The formula of the hail (*"such as has not been in Egypt since the day it was founded"*,
9:18, 24) and the locusts (*"Before them there were no such locusts as they, nor will there ever be
again"*, 10:14), each with *kāmōhû*, *like it* (WLC). **The hail had no equal in the past; the locusts
none in past or future; and the cry none in past or future.**

### Not a dog's tongue (11:7–8)

> **7** But against any of the children of Israel a dog won't even bark or move its tongue, against man
> or animal; that you may know that Yahweh makes a distinction between the Egyptians and Israel.
> **8** All these servants of yours will come down to me, and bow down themselves to me, saying, "Get
> out, with all the people who follow you"; and after that I will go out.'" He went out from Pharaoh in
> hot anger.

***"A dog won't even bark or move its tongue"*** — ***lōʾ yeḥĕraṣ-keleb ləšōnô***, literally *a dog
will not sharpen* (or *point*) *its tongue*. **The Hebrew has no word for *bark***; the WEB supplies it
to give the sense. The idiom recurs in Joshua 10:21 after Israel's victory at Gibeon: *"None moved his
tongue against any of the children of Israel"* (WEB; *lōʾ-ḥāraṣ … ʾet-ləšōnô*, WLC). Of the books
searched (Genesis–Joshua), these are the only two uses of *ḥāraṣ* with *lāšôn*. **On a night of
universal wailing, Israel's houses will be so undisturbed that not even a dog will growl.** Dogs are
otherwise rare in the Torah: the torn meat thrown *"to the dogs"* (22:31), and *keleb* at Deuteronomy
23:18, which the WEB renders *"male prostitute"* (WLC lemma search).

***"That you may know that Yahweh makes a distinction"*** — ***ləmaʿan tēdəʿûn ʾăšer yaplê YHWH***. The
verb is ***pālâ***, the *setting apart* of Goshen from the swarms (8:22) and of Israel's livestock from
Egypt's (9:4). **In the Torah it occurs only at those two verses, here, and at 33:16**, where Moses asks
God to go with Israel *"so that we are separated, I and your people, from all the people who are on the
surface of the earth"* (WLC lemma search). **The distinction made in the plagues becomes the thing Moses
pleads for after the calf.** *Tēdəʿûn* is plural: the knowledge is for Pharaoh and his court together —
after 10:2 had addressed it to Israel. See
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]] and [[Goshen]].

***"All these servants of yours will come down to me, and bow down themselves to me"*** —
***wəyārədû … wəhištaḥăwû-lî***. **The court that bowed to Pharaoh will bow to Moses.** The verb is the
one of Joseph's dreams, *"your sheaves came around, and bowed down to my sheaf"* (Genesis 37:7), and of
their fulfilment in Egypt (42:6; 43:26, 28). **Joseph, a Hebrew, was bowed to by his brothers in Egypt;
now Egyptians are to bow to a Hebrew.** Exodus 12 does not narrate the bowing; it narrates the urgency
(12:33). See [[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]].

***"With all the people who follow you"*** — ***wəkol-hāʿām ʾăšer bəraglêkā***, *the people at your
feet*. **Everyone, with nothing held back** — the answer to every bargain of 8:25–28 and 10:8–11, 24.

***"He went out from Pharaoh in hot anger"*** — ***wayyēṣēʾ mēʿim-parʿōh bāḥŏrî-ʾāp***. **The first time
Exodus says Moses is angry.** Until now *ʾap*, *anger* — literally *nostril* — has been God's, and
against Moses: *"Yahweh's anger burned against Moses"* (4:14). After this it will be Moses' again at the
calf, *"Moses' anger grew hot"* (32:19; WLC lemma search of *ʾap* in Exodus). ***Ḥŏrî-ʾap***, *heat of
nostril*, occurs in the Torah only here and in Deuteronomy 29:24, where it is God's — *"What does the
heat of this great anger mean?"* (WEB; Hebrew 29:23, WLC). **Moses leaves the last audience with the
anger Scripture elsewhere gives to God.** The text does not say what angers him; the nearest causes are
Pharaoh's threat (10:28) and the knowledge of what is coming.

### The cycle closed (11:9–10)

> **9** Yahweh said to Moses, "Pharaoh won't listen to you, that my wonders may be multiplied in the land
> of Egypt."
> **10** Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, and Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and
> he didn't let the children of Israel go out of his land.

**The bracket.** Before the first sign God said: *"I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply my signs
and my wonders in the land of Egypt. But Pharaoh will not listen to you"* (7:3–4). After the ninth, he
says: *"Pharaoh won't listen to you, that my wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt"* (11:9).
The Hebrew is closer still — *wəlōʾ-yišmaʿ ʾălēkem parʿōh* (7:4) and *lōʾ-yišmaʿ ʾălêkem parʿōh*
(11:9); *wəhirbêtî … môpətay bəʾereṣ miṣrāyim* (7:3) and *rəbôt môpətay bəʾereṣ miṣrāyim* (11:9) (WLC).
**What was forecast as a threat is restated as a summary.** *Môpēt*, *wonder*, occurs in Exodus only at
4:21; 7:3, 9; 11:9, 10 (WLC lemma search) — **the word frames the plague cycle and does not reappear in
the book.**

***"He didn't let the children of Israel go out of his land"*** — ***wəlōʾ-šillaḥ ʾet-bənê-yiśrāʾēl
mēʾarṣô***. **The commission of 6:11 and 7:2, negated**: *"that he let the children of Israel go out of
his land"* (*wəšillaḥ ʾet-bənê-yiśrāʾēl mēʾarṣô*, 7:2; WLC). **The cycle that began with that phrase ends
with it, and with *not*.** The hardening is *wayəḥazzēq*, the piel of *ḥāzaq*, as at 10:20, 27 — **the third narrated
hardening in a row with God as subject** (10:20, 27; 11:10), and the fourth in all (9:12). The last
clause also repeats the end of the hail: *"he didn't let the children of Israel go"* (9:35). See
[[Hardening of the Heart]] and [[Signs and Wonders]].

## Notes

- **The chapter is short, and the wiki keeps it as its own pericope.** The plan drawn at the first
  Exodus ingest kept 11:1–10 apart from 12:1–28, and this ingest kept the boundary: 11 closes the
  plague cycle with its own bracket (11:9–10 ↔ 7:3–4), and 12:1 opens with a new speech, a calendar,
  and a rite. **The tenth plague is therefore split across three pericopes** — announced here, struck in
  *The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure* (12:29–51, not yet ingested), with the Passover instituted between.
- **The *"distinction"* is Israel's protection, not its innocence.** [[The Passover]] (12:1–28) makes
  the protection conditional on blood on the doorposts. This chapter says only that Yahweh *"makes a
  distinction"*; the next says how it is marked.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** that *negaʿ* was chosen to echo Genesis 12:17; that *kālâ* links
  11:1 to Genesis 18:21; that 11:1–3 is a pluperfect (*God had said*) rather than a new speech. None is
  shown by the text, and each is marked at its point.
- **Sources.** Exodus and Genesis are quoted from `raw/bible/`. Verses outside `raw/` (Numbers 11:8;
  12:3; Deuteronomy 24:6; 29:24; Joshua 10:21) are quoted from the WEB as served by bible-api.com
  (`translation=web`). **Hebrew forms and counts were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex
  (Open Scriptures `morphhb`).** Lemma searches were run over Genesis–Deuteronomy, and over Joshua for
  *ḥāraṣ*; a claim that a word occurs *only* in certain places is limited to those books.
