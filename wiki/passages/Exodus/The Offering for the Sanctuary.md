# The Offering for the Sanctuary

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 25:1–9

## Summary

Moses has gone into the cloud on the mountain for forty days (24:18). These are the first words God
speaks to him there, and they are not about law. **They are about a building.** God tells Moses to
have Israel *"take an offering for me"*, and only from those who want to give it: *"From everyone whose
heart makes him willing you shall take my offering"* (25:2).

**Then a list of materials** (25:3–7). Metals: *"gold, silver, brass"*. Dyed yarn and cloth: *"blue,
purple, scarlet, fine linen, goats' hair"*. Leather and timber: *"rams' skins dyed red, sea cow hides,
acacia wood"*. Supplies for worship: *"oil for the light, spices for the anointing oil and for the sweet
incense"*. And stones: *"onyx stones, and stones to be set for the ephod and for the breastplate"*. The
list names everything the next six chapters will build with, and some things — the ephod, the
breastplate — that the reader has not yet been told about.

**Then the purpose, in one sentence:** ***"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among
them"*** (25:8). **And the standard:** everything is to be made *"according to all that I show you, the
pattern of the tabernacle, and the pattern of all of its furniture"* (25:9). Israel supplies the
materials. God supplies the design.

Nine verses introduce seven chapters (25–31) of instructions, and through them the last sixteen
chapters of the book. **What Exodus has been moving toward since the mountain is now named: God living
in the middle of the camp.**

## People

- [[God]] — who asks for an offering, accepts it only from the willing, says why he wants a sanctuary
  (*"that I may dwell among them"*), and shows the pattern himself.
- [[Moses]] — the one spoken to in the cloud (24:18; 25:1), and the one to whom the pattern is shown
  (*"all that I show you"*, singular; MAM).
- *"The children of Israel"* (25:2) are the givers, and *"everyone whose heart makes him willing"* is
  the only qualification. No named person gives here; the giving is narrated at 35:20–29.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — the setting by context: Moses is *"on the mountain"* inside the cloud (24:18). The
  mountain is not named in 25:1–9. *"On the mountain"* is added at 25:9 in the Septuagint (see Notes)
  and appears in the Hebrew at 25:40.

## Themes

- [[The Dwelling of God]] — *"that I may dwell among them"* (25:8): the purpose of the tabernacle and,
  in a sense, of the exodus (29:45–46). **The theme page is created with this pericope.**
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — the *terûmâ*, a contribution set apart for God, given voluntarily.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — gold, silver, cloth, and stones, most plausibly from what Israel carried
  out of Egypt (3:22; 12:35); the text of 25:1–9 does not say where they came from.
- [[Holiness]] — *miqdāš*, *sanctuary*, a holy place made by human hands.
- [[The Glory of God]] — the glory that *"settled"* (*šākan*) on Sinai (24:16) is now to *dwell*
  (*šākan*) among the people (25:8).
- [[Walking with God]] — God living among people, not only meeting them at a distance; compare
  Leviticus 26:11–12.
- [[Work]] — the materials are for a work to be made *"according to … the pattern"* (25:9); the
  craftsmen are named at 31:1–11.

## Notes

### The boundary

The planned boundary is kept. 25:1 opens with a new speech formula, *"Yahweh spoke to Moses,
saying"*, and 25:9 ends with a closed paragraph marker in the MAM text (*{ס}*). 25:10 begins the first
object, the ark. These nine verses are an introduction to the whole set of instructions, not to the
ark alone.

### "Take an offering for me" — *tərûmâ*

The word for *offering* in 25:2–3 is ***tərûmâ*** (MAM), a noun usually connected with the verb *rûm*,
*to be high*, in the hiphil *to lift off* or *set apart*. It is something taken out of what a person
has and set aside for God. This is its first occurrence in Scripture: a search of Genesis and Exodus
1–25 in the MAM text finds it only at 25:2 and 25:3. **The verb is odd.** Israel is told to
*"take"* (*wəyiqḥû*) an offering *"for me"*, not to *give* one. The phrase is plain Hebrew idiom for
collecting a contribution. Some readers also see the point that what is given to God is first taken
from what God gave.

The Septuagint translates *tərûmâ* as ***aparchas***, *first fruits* (Rahlfs, 25:2), and its *"from
everyone"* clause reads *to whomever it seems good in the heart*.

### "Whose heart makes him willing"

*ʾăšer yiddəbennû libbô* (25:2; MAM) — literally *whom his heart moves to give freely*. The verb is
***nādab***, and its noun ***nədābâ*** is the *freewill offering* of 35:29, *"The children of Israel
brought a freewill offering to Yahweh"*. **No amount is set, and no one is required to give.** That
sets this offering apart from the laws just given. Those required three appearances a year, with *"none
shall appear before me empty"* (23:15), and the firstborn and first fruits were owed (22:29–30;
23:19). The sanctuary's materials are asked for, not levied. (The half-shekel *"ransom"* of 30:11–16 is a
different, compulsory payment, and the wiki will treat it when it arrives.)

**The narrative of 35–36 bears it out.** The verb comes back again and again: *"Whoever is of a
willing heart"* (*nədîb libbô*, 35:5); *"everyone whom his spirit made willing"* (35:21); *"as many as
were willing-hearted"* (35:22); *"every man and woman, whose heart made them willing"* (35:29; all MAM).
And it ends with the craftsmen telling Moses *"The people bring much more than enough"*, and Moses
proclaiming through the camp, *"Let neither man nor woman make anything else for the offering for the
sanctuary"* (36:5–6). That proclamation is where this page takes its title.

**The name Nadab is spelled with the same three consonants**, *n-d-b* (6:23; 24:1; MAM). It is usually
explained from this root as *generous* or *willing*; that is the standard lexical report, not checked
against a named lexicon in this pass, and Exodus does not make the connection. See
[[Nadab (son of Aaron)]].

**The same verb returns at the building of the temple.** In 1 Chronicles 29, David and the leaders give
for the house of God, and *"the people rejoiced, because they offered willingly, because with a perfect
heart they offered willingly to Yahweh"* (29:9). The verb there is the hitpael of *nādab*,
*hitnaddəbām*, *hitnaddəbû* (MAM). David adds, *"For all things come from you, and of your own have we
given you"* (29:14). Paul's *"Let each man give according as he has determined in his heart; not
grudgingly, or under compulsion; for God loves a cheerful giver"* (2 Corinthians 9:7) is about a
collection for people, not a sanctuary, but it gives the same rule. (Both are *not yet ingested*.)

### The materials

The list moves from metals to yarn and cloth, to skins and wood, and then to oil, spices, and stones
for worship (25:3–7). **Several words are uncertain.**

- *"Blue, purple, scarlet"* — *təkēlet*, *ʾargāmān*, *tôlaʿat šānî* (MAM). The first two are dyes
  usually identified with dyes from sea snails; *tôlaʿat šānî* is literally *worm of scarlet*, a dye
  from an insect. These are the usual lexical identifications, not checked against a named lexicon.
  *Šānî* is the scarlet of the thread tied on [[Zerah]]'s hand (Genesis 38:28, 30).
- *"Sea cow hides"* — *ʿōrōt təḥāšîm* (MAM). The animal or material is unknown. The WEB's *"sea cow"*
  (the dugong) is one guess. Older English versions have *badgers' skins*; others suggest porpoise,
  dolphin, a fine leather, or a colour. The wiki does not decide.
- *"Acacia wood"* — *ʿăṣê šiṭṭîm* (MAM), usually identified as a hardwood that grows in desert country
  like the Sinai. It is the main timber of the tabernacle.
- *"The ephod and … the breastplate"* (25:7) — priestly garments described in chapter 28. The text
  names them here without explaining them.

**Where did a slave people get gold, silver, and dyed linen in the desert?** Exodus never says outright
in this passage. The most natural answer, inside the book, is the jewels of silver and gold and the
clothing asked of the Egyptians (3:22; 11:2; 12:35–36). When the offering is made, the people bring
*"brooches, earrings, signet rings, and armlets, all jewels of gold"* (35:22). That is the wiki's
reading of the sequence; 25:1–9 does not draw the connection. See
[[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]].

**The same gold has two uses ahead.** Before the offering is made, Aaron asks for the people's
golden earrings and makes the calf (32:2–4). The book sets the two side by side: gold given for a god
of the people's making, and gold given for the place God asked for. The juxtaposition is Exodus's own;
whether the narrator means it as a judgment is a reading.

### "That I may dwell among them"

***Wəʿāśû lî miqdāš wəšākantî bətôkām*** (25:8; MAM) — *and they shall make me a holy place, and I
will dwell in their midst*. Three points:

- **It says *among them*, not *in it*.** God does not say he will live in the sanctuary. He says he
  will live among the people, and the sanctuary is the means. Many readers have made this point; the
  wording supports it, though the Hebrew may simply mean the same thing in shorter form.
- **The verb is *šākan*, and it has just been used of the glory on Sinai** (24:16; MAM). The noun
  from the same root, ***miškān***, *dwelling place*, names the tabernacle in the next verse (25:9),
  its first occurrence in Scripture (MAM search, Genesis–Exodus 25). The holy place is called by what
  it is for. The other noun, ***miqdāš***, *holy place*, is from *qādaš*, *to be holy*; it occurred
  once before, in the Song of the Sea (15:17).
- **It is the purpose clause the book has been waiting for.** At 29:45–46 God says he brought Israel
  out of Egypt *"that I might dwell among them"*. At 40:34–35 the glory fills the *miškān*. See
  [[I Will Take You as My People - The Covenant Formula]] and
  [[That I May Dwell Among Them - Tabernacle, Temple, Flesh, and New Creation]].

**The Septuagint does not say *dwell*.** Rahlfs' text of 25:8 reads *kai poiēseis moi hagiasma, kai
ophthēsomai en hymin* — *and you shall make me a sanctuary, and I will be seen among you*. The verb
*make* is singular, addressed to Moses, where the Hebrew has *they shall make*. And *dwell* has become
*be seen*. The reason is not stated anywhere; a common explanation is reluctance to say that God
*lives* in a place, the same care the Septuagint showed at 24:10–11.

### "The pattern"

*"According to all that I show you, the pattern (tabnît) of the tabernacle"* (25:9; MAM). ***Tabnît***
is from *bānâ*, *to build* — a *building plan*, *model*, or *form*. This is its first occurrence in
Scripture (MAM search, Genesis–Exodus 25); the next is 25:40. **The verb *show* is a participle**,
*marʾeh*, *I am showing* (MAM): the showing is going on as God speaks.

Exodus does not say what Moses saw — a heavenly original, a model, a vision, or a set of drawings. The
text says only that God showed something and that the work must follow it. **Hebrews reads it as a
copy of a heavenly reality**: the priests on earth *"serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things,
even as Moses was warned by God when he was about to make the tabernacle"* (Hebrews 8:5, quoting
Exodus 25:40). In Acts, Stephen recalls *"the tabernacle of the testimony in the wilderness, even as
he who spoke to Moses commanded him to make it according to the pattern that he had seen"* (Acts
7:44). The wiki treats the pattern texts with 25:40, the verse Hebrews quotes, which closes the
next pericope: see [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] and
[[After Their Pattern - Exodus 25.40 in Acts 7 and Hebrews 8]].

The Septuagint has *paradeigma* for *tabnît* and adds *en tō orei*, *on the mountain*, in 25:9
(Rahlfs). The Hebrew of 25:9 has no *on the mountain* (MAM); it has the phrase at 25:40.

### "Even so you shall make it"

*Wəkēn taʿăśû* (25:9; MAM). These are the words of the obedience formula — *kēn*, *so*, with
*ʿāśâ*, *do* or *make* — that Exodus uses at the end of the book: *"The children of Israel did
according to all that Yahweh commanded Moses; so they did"* (39:32; compare 39:42–43; 40:16). **The
instruction ends with the formula that will describe its completion.** As with Noah's ark (Genesis
6:22), a structure is built exactly to God's design. See
[[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

### What comes first

God's first words in the cloud are about an offering, not about sacrifice or priests. The order of the
instructions that follow is also striking: the ark, the table, and the lampstand first (25:10–40),
then the tent (26), then the altar and court (27), and only then the priests (28–29). The wiki notes
that the furniture of God's presence comes before the structure that houses it, and before the people
who serve in it. The text does not explain the order.
