# Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 31:1–18

## Summary

Yahweh names the people who will make the sanctuary before ending the instructions with the day on
which work must stop. Bezalel, son of Uri and grandson of Hur, belongs to Judah. God has called him
by name and filled him with the Spirit of God for wisdom, understanding, knowledge, and skilled work
in metal, stone, and wood (31:1–5). Oholiab, son of Ahisamach, belongs to Dan and is appointed
beside him. The wider company of wise-hearted workers also receives wisdom from God (31:6).

Their commission recapitulates the instructions of chapters 25–30: tent, ark and mercy seat,
furniture, table, lampstand, both altars, basin, priestly garments, anointing oil, and incense
(31:7–11). The list gives the work to a company rather than making Moses personally manufacture
everything he has been commanded to make. No construction is reported yet.

A fresh address makes Sabbath keeping a sign between Yahweh and Israel throughout their generations
(31:12–17). Its purpose is knowledge of Yahweh as the one who sanctifies them. The day is holy;
profanation carries death, and work brings the sanction of being cut off. Six days are for work, the
seventh for solemn rest. Israel's observance is a perpetual covenant, grounded in God's making
heaven and earth in six days and resting on the seventh.

When the speaking ends, God gives Moses the two stone tablets of the testimony on Mount Sinai,
written with God's finger (31:18). The tablets promised in 24:12 are now delivered. This closes the
mountain instructions; the following pericope will return to the people below.

> **31:1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **31:2** “Behold, I have called by name Bezalel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of

> **31:5** and in cutting of stones for setting, and in carving of wood, to work in all kinds of
> workmanship.

> **31:6** Behold, I myself have appointed with him Oholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of
> Dan; and in the heart of all who are wise-hearted I have put wisdom, that they may make all that I
> have commanded you:

> **31:7** the Tent of Meeting, the ark of the testimony, the mercy seat that is on it, all the
> furniture of the Tent,

> **31:8** the table and its vessels, the pure lamp stand with all its vessels, the altar of
> incense,

> **31:9** the altar of burnt offering with all its vessels, the basin and its base,

> **31:10** the finely worked garments—the holy garments for Aaron the priest—the garments of his
> sons to minister in the priest’s office,

> **31:11** the anointing oil, and the incense of sweet spices for the holy place: according to all
> that I have commanded you they shall do.”

> **31:12** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **31:13** “Speak also to the children of Israel, saying, ‘Most certainly you shall keep my
> Sabbaths: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that you may know that
> I am Yahweh who sanctifies you.

> **31:14** You shall keep the Sabbath therefore; for it is holy to you. Everyone who profanes it
> shall surely be put to death; for whoever does any work therein, that soul shall be cut off from
> among his people.

> **31:15** Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day is a Sabbath of solemn rest, holy to
> Yahweh. Whoever does any work on the Sabbath day shall surely be put to death.

> **31:16** Therefore the children of Israel shall keep the Sabbath, to observe the Sabbath
> throughout their generations, for a perpetual covenant.

> **31:17** It is a sign between me and the children of Israel forever; for in six days Yahweh made
> heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.’”

> **31:18** He gave to Moses, when he finished speaking with him on Mount Sinai, the two tablets of
> the testimony, stone tablets, written with God’s finger.

## Notes

### Gifted work is shared work

Bezalel is named and filled; Oholiab is appointed with him; wisdom is also given to the unnamed
workers (31:2–6). The text does not oppose skill to spiritual life. It also does not say that every
worker receives the precise description given to Bezalel, or explain how much skill each possessed
beforehand. Exodus 28:3 had already mentioned wise-hearted makers. The work here includes invention
within a command: Bezalel can devise skillful works (31:4), while the finished work must conform to
everything God commands (31:11).

In *Miqra according to the Masorah* (MAM), Exodus 31:3 has *rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm*, Spirit of God, followed
by *ḥokmâ*, *təbûnâ*, and *daʿat*: wisdom, understanding, and knowledge. The connection with Joseph
is substantive, but not an identical pair of nouns: Genesis 41:33, 39 has *nābôn wəḥākām*,
discreet/understanding and wise (MAM). See [[A Man in Whom Is the Spirit of God]] for the correction
of that page's earlier stronger claim.

### Which Hur?

Exodus 31:2 names Bezalel's grandfather Hur of Judah. It does not identify him as the Hur who
supported Moses in 17:10–12 and remained with Aaron in 24:14. The wiki therefore keeps
[[Hur (grandfather of Bezalel)]] separate from [[Hur (companion of Moses)]], with the possible
identification stated on both pages. 1 Chronicles 2:19–20 supplies Caleb and Ephrath as the
grandfather Hur's parents, and repeats Hur–Uri–Bezalel (verified WEB); it does not expressly
identify him with Moses' companion.

### A holy task does not cancel a holy day

The juxtaposition of work and rest is the chapter's central discipline. The makers have a
comprehensive commission, and the next speech still requires Sabbath observance. The text states no
exception for tabernacle work. This is a contextual inference from the order of the commands; Exodus
35:1–3, 30–35 will place Sabbath and the makers together again when Moses addresses the assembly
(local WEB, not yet ingested).

The sign's stated parties are Yahweh and the children of Israel (31:17). Its ground reaches back to
creation, but the passage does not itself legislate Sabbath observance for every nation. Nor does
the word perpetual settle later Christian debates about the command's application. The page records
the scope and duration claimed here.

### Death and being cut off

The law expressly says that the profaner must be put to death (31:14), repeats death for Sabbath
work (31:15), and also says the worker is cut off from the people (31:14). It does not specify the
procedure, identify an executioner, or explain whether the two formulas describe the same sanction
from different perspectives. The distinction matters when comparing Genesis 17:14, which states
cutting off but does not itself prescribe a human execution.

### God rested and was refreshed

The WEB's last words about the seventh day are *“he rested, and was refreshed”* (31:17). MAM has
*šābat wayyinnāpaš*. The language is more bodily than the bare cessation of work in Genesis 2:2–3;
Exodus 23:12 likewise uses refreshment for human dependents in the WEB. **The existing Sabbath and
God pages' “not exhaustion” synthesis must be qualified by this verse.** Isaiah 40:28, on the other
hand, describes the Creator as neither fainting nor weary (verified WEB). An anthropomorphic reading
can relate the descriptions, but it is an interpretation, not a removal of Exodus's wording. The
wiki retains both statements.

### The tablets delivered

The promise of 24:12 is fulfilled at the close of the speaking (31:18). Moses receives testimony in
stone; the narrator attributes its writing to God's finger. The verse does not yet describe the
writing's layout or recite the tablets' contents. The phrase recalls the magicians' confession in
Exodus 8:19, now used for writing rather than the plague. See
[[The Finger of God - Exodus 8.19, Exodus 31.18, and Luke 11.20]].
