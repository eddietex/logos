# Manna and Quail

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 16:1–36

## Summary

**A month out of Egypt the food runs out, and the people say they wish they had died there.** Marah's
complaint was two words about water (15:24); this one remembers Egypt as *"the meat pots"* and *"our fill of
bread"*, and accuses Moses and Aaron of bringing the whole assembly out *"to kill … with hunger"* (16:3). God's
answer is not a rebuke. It is bread from the sky every morning, quail one evening, and a rule attached to
the bread: gather a day's worth each day, and twice as much on the sixth day, because on the seventh there
will be none. **The rule is called a test** — *"that I may test them, whether they will walk in my law, or
not"* (16:4) — and **the chapter in which Israel is first tested by a daily rule is also the one where the
word *Sabbath* first appears in Scripture** (16:23; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy).

The pericope moves in eight steps:

- **The complaint (16:1–3).** The wilderness of Sin, *"between Elim and Sinai"*, on *"the fifteenth day of the
  second month"*. **The whole congregation murmurs *"against Moses and against Aaron"*** — Aaron's first time
  as an object of the complaint — and wishes it had died *"by Yahweh's hand in the land of Egypt"*.
- **God's answer to Moses (16:4–5).** *"Behold, I will rain bread from the sky for you"*; a day's portion each
  day; the test; the sixth day *"twice as much"*.
- **Moses and Aaron answer the people (16:6–8).** *"At evening … you shall know that Yahweh has brought you
  out"*; *"in the morning … you shall see Yahweh's glory"*; and the complaint redirected: ***"Your murmurings
  are not against us, but against Yahweh."***
- **The glory (16:9–12).** Aaron calls the congregation to *"Come near before Yahweh"*; they look toward the
  wilderness and ***"Yahweh's glory appeared in the cloud"*** (16:10) — the first time Scripture uses the
  phrase (WLC); God repeats the promise and adds its purpose, *"you shall know that I am Yahweh your God"*.
- **Quail and dew (16:13–15).** Quail cover the camp in the evening; in the morning, under the dew, *"a small
  round thing"*. ***"What is it?"*** — and Moses: *"It is the bread which Yahweh has given you to eat."*
- **The omer (16:16–21).** An omer a head; some gather more and some less; measured, *"he who gathered much
  had nothing over, and he who gathered little had no lack"*. Kept overnight, *"it bred worms, and became
  foul: and Moses was angry with them"*. In the heat it melts.
- **The sixth day and the Sabbath (16:22–30).** A double portion; the rulers report it; Moses: *"Tomorrow is a
  solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to Yahweh."* Kept overnight, it does not spoil. Some go out on the seventh day
  and find nothing. God: ***"How long do you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?"*** *"So the people
  rested on the seventh day."*
- **The name and the jar (16:31–36).** *"The house of Israel called its name Manna"*; an omer kept *"before
  the Testimony"* for the generations; manna for *"forty years, until they came to an inhabited land"*; and a
  note on the measure.

**It is the second turn of the cycle Marah began**: need, complaint, provision, and a lesson attached (see
[[Bitter Water at Marah]] and [[Murmuring]]). **It is sharper than Marah and still not punished.** The
complaint now has Egypt in it and a charge of murder; Moses and Aaron say it is really against God; God
says he has *"heard"* it (16:7, 8, 9, 12) — and feeds them. **The only anger in the chapter is Moses', at
people who kept the bread overnight (16:20)**, and the only rebuke from God is for the Sabbath-breakers, in
a question (16:28). Numbers 11 will tell a second quail story that ends very differently (WEB; *not yet
ingested*).

**The chapter also looks forward past itself, twice.** The jar is laid up *"before the Testimony"* (16:34),
which will not exist until 25:16; and the narrator says Israel *"ate the manna forty years"* (16:35), which
the people of the story cannot yet know. **The pericope is told from the end of the journey.** See the note
at 16:34–35.

**The date places it a month after the Passover.** The lamb was killed on *"the fourteenth day"* of the first
month (12:6); Israel reaches the wilderness of Sin *"on the fifteenth day of the second month after their
departing"* (16:1). **Sinai is named here for the first time in Scripture**, as the end of a stretch of road
(16:1; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy); Israel arrives there *"in the third month"* (19:1; *not yet
ingested*). See [[The Wilderness of Sin]] and [[Horeb]].

## Key verses

### The wilderness of Sin (16:1)

> **1** They took their journey from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came to the
> wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their
> departing out of the land of Egypt.

***"The wilderness of Sin"*** — ***midbar-sîn***. **Sin is named four times in Scripture's first five books**
— here, 17:1, and Numbers 33:11–12 (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy) — **always as a stage of the
march and never as the scene of anything but this chapter.** **The name has the same consonants as
*Sinai* (*sînay*), and the verse names both**; the WLC files them under separate numbers (5512, 5514), and the
wiki records the resemblance and draws nothing from it. The English place-name has nothing to do with the
English word *sin*. See [[The Wilderness of Sin]].

***"Between Elim and Sinai"*** — ***bên-ʾêlim ûbên sînāy***. **The first *Sinai* in Scripture** (WLC lemma
search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The mountain is named as a point on the road before anything has happened
on it**, just as Horeb was called *"God's mountain"* before the bush (3:1). See [[Horeb]].

***"All the congregation"*** — ***kol-ʿădat bənê-yiśrāʾēl***. ***ʿĒdâ*, *congregation*, first appeared in the
Passover law** (*"all the congregation of Israel"*, 12:3; WLC) and **comes five times in this chapter**
(16:1, 2, 9, 10, 22; and *qāhāl*, *"assembly"*, at 16:3; WLC). **The congregation that kept the Passover
together now complains together.**

### We wish that we had died in Egypt (16:2–3)

> **2** The whole congregation of the children of Israel murmured against Moses and against Aaron in the
> wilderness;
>
> **3** and the children of Israel said to them, "We wish that we had died by Yahweh's hand in the land of
> Egypt, when we sat by the meat pots, when we ate our fill of bread, for you have brought us out into this
> wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger."

***"Murmured against Moses and against Aaron"*** — ***wayyillônû … ʿal-mōšeh wəʿal-ʾahărōn*** (the WLC
records a *kethiv* *wylynw* beside the *qere* *wayyillônû*). **The second murmuring in Scripture, and the
first against both brothers** (WLC lemma search). See [[Murmuring]] and [[Aaron]].

***"We wish"*** — ***mî-yittēn***, literally *who will give …?* **The idiom of wishing**, here with
***mûtēnû***, *our dying*: the first time in Exodus that anyone in Israel wishes to have died.

***"By Yahweh's hand"*** — ***bəyad-YHWH***. **The phrase of the fifth plague**: *"Yahweh's hand is on your
livestock … with a very grievous pestilence"* (*yad-YHWH*, 9:3; WLC). **The death they say they would have
preferred is the death Yahweh's hand dealt Egypt.** The text does not draw the line; the phrase is the same.
See [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]].

***"When we sat by the meat pots"*** — ***bəšibtēnû ʿal-sîr habbāśār***. ***Sîr*, *pot*, occurs only here in
Exodus outside the tabernacle's bronze *"pails"* (27:3; 38:3; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus).** **The
verb is *yāšab*, *sit*, *dwell* — the verb God will use for the Sabbath: *"Everyone stay (šəbû) in his
place"* (16:29; WLC).** They remember sitting by pots in Egypt; they will be told to sit still on the
seventh day.

***"When we ate our fill of bread"*** — ***ləśōbaʿ***, *to satiety*. **Egypt is remembered as a full table.**
**Exodus has said nothing about Egypt's food for the slaves**; it has said *"they made their lives bitter with
hard service"* (1:14). **Numbers 11:5 will extend the memory**: *"We remember the fish, which we ate in Egypt
for nothing; the cucumbers, and the melons, and the leeks, and the onions, and the garlic"* (WEB; *not yet
ingested*). **God's answer takes the word up: *"bread to satisfy you"* (16:8), *"you shall be filled with
bread"* (16:12)** — the verb ***śābaʿ***, which occurs in Exodus only in these two verses (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Exodus). **They said they were full in Egypt; he says they will be full here.** See
[[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"You have brought us out"*** — ***hôṣēʾtem***, plural, of Moses and Aaron. **The exodus verb, *yāṣāʾ*, with
the brothers as its subject.** **Moses answers it in the next breath, in the same verb with God as subject**:
*"you shall know that Yahweh has brought you out (hôṣîʾ)"* (16:6; WLC). **The people credit the exodus to the
men; the men give it back.**

***"To kill this whole assembly with hunger"*** — ***bārāʿāb***. ***Rāʿāb*, *famine*, *hunger*, is Genesis's
word for every famine that drove the patriarchs toward Egypt** — Abraham's (12:10), Isaac's (26:1), and
Joseph's seven years (41:27–57) (WLC lemma search). **This is its first occurrence since Genesis 47:20, and
its only one in Exodus** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **Hunger brought Israel into Egypt; the first
hunger out of it makes Israel want Egypt back.** See [[Famine and Plenty]].

### I will rain bread from the sky (16:4–5)

> **4** Then Yahweh said to Moses, "Behold, I will rain bread from the sky for you, and the people shall go out
> and gather a day's portion every day, that I may test them, whether they will walk in my law, or not.
>
> **5** It shall come to pass on the sixth day, that they shall prepare that which they bring in, and it shall
> be twice as much as they gather daily."

***"I will rain"*** — ***mamṭîr***, the hiphil participle of ***māṭar***. **Every earlier rain of God's is
judgment or its absence.** *"Yahweh God had not caused it to rain on the earth"* (Genesis 2:5); *"I will cause
it to rain on the earth forty days and forty nights"* (7:4, the same participle, *mamṭîr*); *"Yahweh rained on
Sodom and on Gomorrah sulfur and fire from Yahweh out of the sky"* (19:24); and the seventh plague, *"I will
cause it to rain a very grievous hail"* (9:18, again *mamṭîr*; also 9:23) (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Exodus). **This is the verb's sixth occurrence in Scripture and the first time God rains something to
eat.**

***"Bread from the sky"*** — ***leḥem min-haššāmāyim***. **Sodom's fire came *"from Yahweh out of the sky (min-haššāmāyim)"*** (Genesis 19:24; WLC) — **the same verb and the same phrase.** **The sky that rained
fire on a city rains bread on a camp.** See [[Fire and Sulfur from the Sky]] and
[[Bread from Heaven - The Manna and John 6]].

***"Gather"*** — ***wəlāqəṭû***, from ***lāqaṭ***, *glean*, *pick up*. **It is the chapter's verb — nine
verses between 16:4 and 16:27** (WLC lemma search). **Its two uses in Genesis are Jacob's *"Gather stones"* at Galeed
(31:46) and Joseph's *"Joseph gathered up all the money that was found in the land of Egypt, and in the land
of Canaan … and Joseph brought the money into Pharaoh's house"*** (47:14; WLC). **The last gathering in
Genesis was a famine economy drawing everything into Pharaoh's house; the first in Exodus is a ration that
cannot be stored.** Leviticus will use the verb for the gleanings left for the poor (19:9–10; 23:22; WLC;
*not yet ingested*). See [[Joseph and the Famine]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]].

***"A day's portion every day"*** — ***dəbar-yôm bəyômô***, *the matter of a day in its day*. **The rule is a
ration, not a storehouse.** Many readers hear it in the Lord's Prayer's *"Give us today our daily bread"*
(Matthew 6:11 WEB; *not yet ingested*); the meaning of the Greek word rendered *daily* is disputed, and the
connection is the readers', not the text's (reported, not checked in this pass).

***"That I may test them"*** — ***ləmaʿan ʾănassennû***, the piel of ***nāsâ***, singular *him* again, as at
Marah (WLC). **The third occurrence of the verb in Scripture** — Abraham (Genesis 22:1), Marah (15:25), and
here (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **Marah named a test and stated a condition; here the test is
named and its content is given — a daily rule about food.** See [[Testing]].

***"Whether they will walk in my law, or not"*** — ***hăyēlēk bətôrātî ʾim-lōʾ***. ***Tôrâ* has occurred three
times before: Genesis 26:5's *"my laws"*, the Passover's *"One law"* (12:49), and *"Yahweh's law"* in the
mouth (13:9)** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **This is the first time God says *my law* in Exodus — and
what he calls *my law* is a rule about gathering bread.** The wording *"whether … or not"* will return in
Deuteronomy's summary of the wilderness: *"to prove you, to know what was in your heart, whether you would
keep his commandments, or not"* (Deuteronomy 8:2; WEB; *not yet ingested*). See [[Law and Obedience]].

***"They shall prepare"*** — ***wəhēkînû***, *make ready*, *set in order*. ***"Twice as much"*** —
***mišneh***, *double*. **Its uses in Genesis are Joseph's chariot *"the second (hammišneh)"* (41:43) and the
*"double money"* the brothers carry back to Egypt (43:12, 15)** (WLC lemma search). See
[[The Second Journey]].

**God tells Moses about the sixth day here, and Moses is not reported passing it on.** At 16:22 the rulers
come to Moses when the double portion appears, and only then does he say *"This is that which Yahweh has
spoken"* (16:23). **Whether the people gathered double on their own, or Moses had told them and the text
does not say so, is not stated** (readings reported, not decided).

### You shall know, and you shall see (16:6–8)

> **6** Moses and Aaron said to all the children of Israel, "At evening, then you shall know that Yahweh has
> brought you out from the land of Egypt;
>
> **7** and in the morning, then you shall see Yahweh's glory; because he hears your murmurings against
> Yahweh. Who are we, that you murmur against us?"
>
> **8** Moses said, "Now Yahweh shall give you meat to eat in the evening, and in the morning bread to satisfy
> you; because Yahweh hears your murmurings which you murmur against him. And who are we? Your murmurings
> are not against us, but against Yahweh."

***"You shall know"*** — ***wîdaʿtem***. **The plagues' formula has turned around.** It was promised to Israel
once, before the plagues — *"you shall know that I am Yahweh your God, who brings you out"* (6:7) — and then
said of Egypt — *"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* (7:5; 14:4, 18) — with one aside to Israel's
children (10:2). **Here it is said to Israel again, about the exodus itself**, and at 16:12 in its full
form, *"you shall know that I am Yahweh your God"*. **Then 16:15: *"they didn't know (lōʾ yādəʿû) what it was"***
(WLC). Deuteronomy draws the line itself: *"fed you with manna, which you didn't know, neither did your
fathers know; that he might teach you"* (8:3; WEB; WLC — the same verb, *yādaʿ*, in the hiphil for
*teach*; *not yet ingested*). See [[The Names of God]] and [[I Am Yahweh]].

***"You shall see Yahweh's glory"*** — ***ûrəʾîtem ʾet-kəbôd YHWH***. **The first *kəbôd YHWH*, *Yahweh's
glory*, in Scripture** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **The noun's three Genesis uses are human**:
Jacob's *"wealth"* (31:1), Joseph's *"all my glory in Egypt"* (45:13), and Jacob's *"my glory"* in the curse on
Simeon and Levi (49:6) (WLC). **And its root, *kbd*, is the book's word for Pharaoh's *heavy* heart and for
God's *"I will get honor (ʾikkābdâ) over Pharaoh"*** (14:4, 17; see [[The Crossing of the Sea]]). **God got
glory over Pharaoh at the sea; a month later he shows his glory to Israel.** See [[The Glory of God]].

***"Your murmurings"*** — ***təlunnōtêkem***. **The noun's first occurrence in Scripture; it comes five times
in 16:7–12 and three more in Numbers (14:27; 17:20, 25 Hebrew)** (WLC lemma search). **God *"hears"* them —
*šāmaʿ*, four times (16:7, 8, 9, 12; WLC)** — the verb of *"God heard their groaning"* in Egypt (2:24). **He
heard Israel groan under Pharaoh and hears Israel complain against him, and answers both with deliverance.**

***"Who are we?"*** — ***wənaḥnû māh***, *and we, what?* **The shortest self-description Moses gives in
Exodus.** His first question at the bush was *"Who am I (mî ʾānōkî)?"* (3:11; WLC); here, with Aaron, it is
*what*, not *who*. **And it answers the people's *"you have brought us out"* (16:3)**: the men are nothing; the
complaint passes through them to God.

***"Your murmurings are not against us, but against Yahweh"*** — ***lōʾ-ʿālênû təlunnōtêkem kî ʿal-YHWH***.
**The narrator has never reported a murmuring against God.** **It is Moses who says where the murmuring goes**,
and God will say it himself in Numbers 14:27 (*not yet ingested*). See [[Murmuring]] and
[[Mediation and Advocacy]].

**The speeches run in an unusual order.** Moses and Aaron announce meat and bread (16:6–8) before God is
reported promising meat at all (16:12), and the glory they promise for *"the morning"* (16:7) appears while
Aaron is still speaking (16:10). **Many readers see either a displacement of verses or a deliberate
announcement-then-confirmation** (reported, not decided). The text is left as it stands.

### Yahweh's glory appeared in the cloud (16:9–12)

> **9** Moses said to Aaron, "Tell all the congregation of the children of Israel, 'Come near before Yahweh,
> for he has heard your murmurings.'"
>
> **10** As Aaron spoke to the whole congregation of the children of Israel, they looked toward the wilderness,
> and behold, Yahweh's glory appeared in the cloud.

***"Come near before Yahweh"*** — ***qirbû lipnê YHWH***. **The language of approach to a sanctuary** —
*qārab* is the verb of bringing near an offering in Leviticus — **before there is a sanctuary to approach**.
**They come near, and they look *"toward the wilderness"***: the presence is not in a tent or on a mountain
yet, but out where they are going.

***"Behold, Yahweh's glory appeared in the cloud"*** — ***wəhinnēh kəbôd YHWH nirʾâ beʿānān***. ***ʿĀnān*,
*cloud*, first held the rainbow** — *"I set my rainbow in the cloud"* (Genesis 9:13–16) — **and then the pillar
that went before Israel and stood between Israel and Egypt** (13:21–22; 14:19–20, 24) (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Exodus). **This is the first time the glory is seen in it.** **The book ends in the same pair**: *"the
cloud covered the Tent of Meeting, and Yahweh's glory filled the tabernacle"* (40:34; [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]);
and Sinai between: *"Yahweh's glory settled on Mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days"* (24:16;
[[The Blood of the Covenant]]). **Exodus's whole movement of the glory — appearing, settling, filling — starts over a
complaining congregation in the wilderness of Sin.** See [[The Glory of God]] and
[[The Bow in the Cloud]].

> **12** "I have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel. Speak to them, saying, 'At evening you shall
> eat meat, and in the morning you shall be filled with bread: and you shall know that I am Yahweh your
> God.'"

***"At evening"*** — ***bên hāʿarbayim***, *between the two evenings*. **The hour of the Passover lamb**:
*"the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it at evening (bên hāʿarbāyim)"* (12:6; WLC).
**The meat comes at the hour the lamb was killed.** WEB renders both *"at evening"*.

***"I am Yahweh your God"*** — ***ʾănî YHWH ʾĕlōhêkem***. **The promise of 6:7, *"I will be to you a God; and
you shall know that I am Yahweh your God"*, reached in the wilderness** (WEB; the same formula). See
[[I Am Yahweh]].

### Quail, dew, and a small round thing (16:13–15)

> **13** In the evening, quail came up and covered the camp; and in the morning the dew lay around the camp.
>
> **14** When the dew that lay had gone, behold, on the surface of the wilderness was a small round thing,
> small as the frost on the ground.

***"Quail"*** — ***haśśəlāw***. **In the Torah only here and in Numbers 11:31–32** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Here the quail get one clause and are never mentioned again**; no one eats them on the
page. **In Numbers 11 they come by *"a wind from Yahweh"*, the people gather them for two days, and *"While the
meat was still between their teeth … Yahweh struck the people with a very great plague"*** (Numbers 11:31–33;
WEB; *not yet ingested*). **The first quail are a gift to a complaint; the second are a judgment on one.** The
psalm keeps the first: *"They asked, and he brought quails, and satisfied them with the bread of the sky"*
(Psalm 105:40; WEB; *not yet ingested*).

***"Covered the camp"*** — ***wattəkas***, *kāsâ*. **The verb of the plagues and the sea**: the frogs
*"covered the land of Egypt"* (8:6 WEB; 8:2 Hebrew, the same form *wattəkas*), the locusts *"covered the
surface of the whole earth"* (10:5, 15), and the waters *"covered the chariots"* (14:28; 15:5, 10) (WLC lemma
search, Genesis–Exodus). **What covered Egypt was a plague; what covers Israel's camp is supper.** The text
does not draw the line; the verb is the same.

***"The dew"*** — ***haṭṭal***. **Isaac's blessing was *"of the dew of the sky (miṭṭal haššāmayim)"*** (Genesis
27:28), and the half-blessing left for Esau *"of the dew of the sky from above"* (27:39) (WLC). **Its next
use is here; it is the vehicle of the bread** — Numbers 11:9 makes it explicit: *"When the dew fell on the
camp in the night, the manna fell on it"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*). See [[The Stolen Blessing]].

***"A small round thing"*** — ***daq məḥuspās***. ***Daq*, *thin*, *small*, is the word of Pharaoh's dreams —
the *"thin"* cows and the *"thin and blasted"* heads of grain that swallowed the fat ones** (Genesis 41:3–7,
23–24; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). ***Məḥuspās*, rendered *round* or *flake-like*, occurs nowhere else
in the books checked** (WLC, Genesis–Deuteronomy, Joshua, Nehemiah, Psalms) and is usually reported as unique
in the Hebrew Bible; its meaning is guessed from context (reported, not checked in this pass).
**The thin things of Pharaoh's dream were the famine; the thin thing on the wilderness is the food.**

***"Small as the frost"*** — ***kakkəpōr***. ***Kəpōr*, *hoarfrost*, is another word found only here in the
Torah** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The chapter needs words it has not used before to describe
a thing no one has seen before.**

> **15** When the children of Israel saw it, they said to one another, "What is it?" For they didn't know what
> it was. Moses said to them, "It is the bread which Yahweh has given you to eat."

***"What is it?"*** — ***mān hûʾ***. **The WLC parses *mān* here as an interrogative, not a noun** — a separate
sense from the *mān* of 16:31, 33, 35 — **and the narrator glosses it at once with the ordinary Hebrew *mah*:
*"for they didn't know what (mah) it was"*** (WLC). **So the name of the food is the question asked about
it.** *Mān* as *what?* is usually explained as an Aramaic or older form of the interrogative (reported, not
checked in this pass); the folk etymology is the text's own, at 16:31. See [[Names and Naming]].

***"It is the bread which Yahweh has given you"*** — ***hûʾ halleḥem ʾăšer nātan YHWH lākem***. **Moses
answers *What is it?* with what it is for, and whose gift.** John's crowd will quote the Psalms' and
Nehemiah's version of this — *"He gave them bread out of heaven to eat"* — and Jesus will answer *"it wasn't
Moses who gave you the bread out of heaven, but my Father gives you the true bread out of heaven"* (John
6:31–32; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **Moses had said as much here: *"which Yahweh has given."*** See
[[Bread from Heaven - The Manna and John 6]].

### He who gathered much had nothing over (16:16–21)

> **16** This is the thing which Yahweh has commanded: "Gather of it everyone according to his eating; an
> omer a head, according to the number of your persons, you shall take it, every man for those who are in his
> tent."
>
> **17** The children of Israel did so, and gathered some more, some less.
>
> **18** When they measured it with an omer, he who gathered much had nothing over, and he who gathered little
> had no lack. They gathered every man according to his eating.

***"An omer"*** — ***ʿōmer***. **The measure is named six times in this chapter and nowhere else in Scripture
as a measure**; elsewhere in the Torah the same letters are the *sheaf* of firstfruits (Leviticus 23:10–15)
and the forgotten sheaf left for the poor (Deuteronomy 24:19) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy; the
WLC marks the measure as a separate sense). **The chapter's last verse explains it: *"an omer is one tenth of
an ephah"* (16:36)** — a note for readers who no longer used it.

***"A head"*** — ***laggulgōlet***, *skull*, *head*. **The word of the census** — the
half-shekel *"a head"* (38:26; *not yet ingested*) and the head-count of Numbers 1:2, 18–22 (by reference,
*not yet ingested*) (WLC lemma search). **Everyone is counted
for bread before anyone is counted for war.** Its Aramaic cognate is usually connected with *Golgotha*,
*"the place of a skull"* (reported, not checked in this pass).

***"Some more, some less"*** — ***hammarbeh wəhammamʿîṭ***, *the one making much and the one making little*.
***"He who gathered much had nothing over"*** — ***lōʾ heʿdîp***, from ***ʿādap***, *be in excess*. ***"He
who gathered little had no lack"*** — ***lōʾ heḥsîr***, from ***ḥāsēr***, *lack*. **The verb *lack* is the flood's
*"The waters decreased"* (Genesis 8:3, 5) and Abraham's *"What if there will lack five of the fifty
righteous?"* (18:28)** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **And Moses will use it of the whole forty years:
*"These forty years, Yahweh your God has been with you. You have lacked nothing"*** (Deuteronomy 2:7; WEB;
WLC; *not yet ingested*) — as will the shepherd psalm: *"Yahweh is my shepherd: I shall lack nothing"*
(*lōʾ ʾeḥsār*, Psalm 23:1; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). See
[[The God Who Has Fed Me - The First Time God Is a Shepherd]].

**How the equality came about is not said.** One reading takes it as a miracle in the measuring, every
portion coming out to an omer whatever was gathered; another as the household sharing out its gathering, so
that each tent ended with what it needed (readings reported, not decided). **Paul quotes the verse for the
second sense**, as a pattern of *"equality"* in giving: *"As it is written, 'He who gathered much had nothing
left over, and he who gathered little had no lack'"* (2 Corinthians 8:15; WEB; *not yet ingested*). See
[[He Who Gathered Much - Exodus 16.18 and 2 Corinthians 8.15]].

> **19** Moses said to them, "Let no one leave of it until the morning."
>
> **20** Notwithstanding they didn't listen to Moses, but some of them left of it until the morning, and it bred
> worms, and became foul: and Moses was angry with them.
>
> **21** They gathered it morning by morning, everyone according to his eating. When the sun grew hot, it melted.

***"Let no one leave of it until the morning"*** — ***ʾîš ʾal-yôtēr mimmennû ʿad-bōqer***. **The Passover
rule, nearly word for word**: *"You shall let nothing of it remain (lōʾ-tôtîrû) until the morning"* (12:10;
WLC). **The lamb and the manna are both eaten the day they are given.**

***"They didn't listen to Moses"*** — ***wəlōʾ-šāməʿû ʾel-mōšeh***. **The first disobedience in the wilderness**,
and the first of the test's two failures (16:20, 27).

***"It bred worms, and became foul"*** — ***wayyārum tôlāʿîm wayyibʾaš***. ***Bāʾaš*, *stink*, is the verb of
Egypt's plagues**: *"the river shall become foul"* (7:18), *"the river became foul"* (7:21), and the frogs heaped
up until *"the land stank"* (8:14 WEB; 8:10 Hebrew) — **and of the foremen's *"you have made us a stench"*
(5:21)** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **Kept against the command, Israel's bread stinks the way Egypt's
river stank.** ***Tôlāʿ*, *worm*, will be the scarlet dye of the tabernacle** (*tôlaʿat šānî*, 25:4 and
throughout; WLC) — the same noun, from the insect the dye was made from (reported, not checked in this pass).

***"Moses was angry with them"*** — ***wayyiqṣōp ʿălēhem mōšeh***. ***Qāṣap* in Genesis is Pharaoh's verb**:
*"Pharaoh was angry with his two officers"* (40:2; also 41:10) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **This is
Moses' first anger against Israel**, and its object is hoarding. His anger at Pharaoh at 11:8 was a different
phrase (*ḥŏrî-ʾap*, *hot anger*; WLC). See [[The Cupbearer and the Baker]].

***"When the sun grew hot, it melted"*** — ***wəḥam haššemeš wənāmās***. ***Māsas*, *melt*, is otherwise the
verb of hearts that melt** — the spies' report, *"Our brothers have made our heart melt"* (Deuteronomy 1:28; WEB),
and the fearful soldier (20:8) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy; *not yet ingested*). **What was not
gathered by mid-morning was gone.**

### A holy Sabbath to Yahweh (16:22–26)

> **22** On the sixth day, they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for each one, and all the rulers of the
> congregation came and told Moses.
>
> **23** He said to them, "This is that which Yahweh has spoken, 'Tomorrow is a solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to
> Yahweh. Bake that which you want to bake, and boil that which you want to boil; and all that remains over
> lay up for yourselves to be kept until the morning.'"

***"All the rulers of the congregation"*** — ***kol-nəśîʾê hāʿēdâ***. ***Nāśîʾ*, *prince*, *ruler*, in Genesis is
Ishmael's *"twelve princes"* (17:20; 25:16), the Hittites' name for Abraham — *"a prince of God"* (23:6) — and
Shechem, *"the prince of the land"* (34:2)** (WLC lemma search). **Here it appears for the first time of
Israel's own leaders.**

***"A solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to Yahweh"*** — ***šabbātôn šabbat-qōdeš laYHWH***. **Both nouns occur here
for the first time in Scripture**: ***šabbāt*** has no occurrence in Genesis, and ***šabbātôn*** none before this
verse (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The seventh day had been blessed and made holy at creation
— *"he rested (šābat) on the seventh day … God blessed the seventh day, and made it holy"*** (Genesis 2:2–3)
— **with the verb but not the noun.** **The noun arrives in the wilderness, as a fact about bread.** No
commandment precedes it; the commandment comes at 20:8–11 and points back to creation (*not yet ingested*).
See [[Sabbath]] and [[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]].

***"Holy"*** — ***qōdeš***. **Its earlier uses in Exodus are the bush's *"holy ground"* (3:5), the feast's
*"holy convocation"* on its first and seventh days (12:16), and the Song's *"glorious in holiness"* and *"holy
habitation"* (15:11, 13)** (WLC lemma search). **Here, as in Genesis 2:3, the holy thing is a day** — and this
time a day of every week. See [[Holiness]].

***"Bake … boil"*** — ***tōʾpû … təbaššəlû***. **The manna can be cooked**; Numbers 11:8 lists the ways —
*"ground it in mills, or beat it in mortars, and boiled it in pots, and made cakes of it"* (WEB; *not yet
ingested*).

***"Lay up for yourselves to be kept"*** — ***hannîḥû lākem ləmišmeret***. ***Mišmeret*, *keeping*, *charge*, is
the word of the Passover lamb — *"you shall keep it (ləmišmeret) until the fourteenth day"*** (12:6) — **and of
Genesis 26:5's *"kept my requirements (mišmartî)"*** (WLC lemma search). **It returns three times at the end of
the chapter for the jar kept for the generations** (16:32, 33, 34; WLC). **What may not be kept overnight on
the other five days is to be kept on the sixth, and it does not spoil.** ***Hinnîaḥ*, *lay up*, *set down*, is
the verb of *"put him in the garden of Eden"*** (Genesis 2:15; WLC); the WLC files it under a separate number
(3240) from the *nûaḥ* of *"rested the seventh day"* in 20:11 (5117), though they are usually taken as one root
(reported, not checked in this pass).

> **24** They laid it up until the morning, as Moses asked, and it didn't become foul, and there were no worms
> in it.
>
> **25** Moses said, "Eat that today, for today is a Sabbath to Yahweh. Today you shall not find it in the field.
>
> **26** Six days you shall gather it, but on the seventh day is the Sabbath. In it there shall be none."

***"It didn't become foul, and there were no worms in it"*** — ***wəlōʾ hibʾîš wərimmâ lōʾ-hāyətâ bô***. **The
same verb, *bāʾaš*, negated** — and a second word for worm, ***rimmâ***, found only here in the Torah (WLC lemma
search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The test has a visible answer: the same bread, kept by permission, is sound.**

***"Today … today … today"*** — ***hayyôm*** three times in one verse (WLC). **Moses' instruction is all
present tense.** *"Six days you shall gather it, but on the seventh day is the Sabbath"* — **the rhythm of
the fourth commandment, *"You shall labor six days … but the seventh day is a Sabbath to Yahweh your God"***
(20:9–10; *not yet ingested*), **given here as a description of where the food will and will not be.**

### How long do you refuse? (16:27–30)

> **27** On the seventh day, some of the people went out to gather, and they found none.
>
> **28** Yahweh said to Moses, "How long do you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?
>
> **29** Behold, because Yahweh has given you the Sabbath, therefore he gives you on the sixth day the bread of
> two days. Everyone stay in his place. Let no one go out of his place on the seventh day."
>
> **30** So the people rested on the seventh day.

***"Some of the people went out to gather, and they found none"*** — **the second failure**, and no
punishment follows it except finding nothing. **Numbers 15 will tell of a man *"gathering sticks on the
Sabbath day"* who is stoned at God's command** (Numbers 15:32–36; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **The first
Sabbath-breakers go home empty-handed.** See [[Judgment]].

***"How long do you refuse"*** — ***ʿad-ʾānâ mēʾantem***. ***Māʾēn*, *refuse*, has been Pharaoh's verb through
the plagues**: *"you have refused to let him go"* (4:23), *"he refuses to let the people go"* (7:14), and
***"How long will you refuse (ʿad-mātay mēʾantā) to humble yourself before me?"*** (10:3) (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Exodus). **The question God put to Pharaoh is put to Israel, a month out of Egypt, about bread on the
seventh day.** The interrogative differs (*ʿad-ʾānâ* here, *ʿad-mātay* there) and the verb is the same. The
*you* is plural — addressed to Moses, meant for the people. See [[Hardening of the Heart]] and
[[Locusts and Darkness]].

***"My commandments and my laws"*** — ***miṣwōtay wətôrōtāy***. **The exact two words of Genesis 26:5, in the
same order and the same forms**: *"Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my requirements, my commandments
(miṣwōtay), my statutes, and my laws (wətôrōtāy)"* (WLC). **Genesis 26:5 is the only other verse in Genesis or
Exodus with the plural *tôrôt* and a first-person suffix** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **Abraham was
said to have kept them; Israel is asked why it refuses them — and the commandment in question is the Sabbath,
not yet written down.** See [[Because Abraham Obeyed My Voice - Law Before Sinai]] and
[[Law and Obedience]].

***"Yahweh has given you the Sabbath"*** — ***YHWH nātan lākem haššabbāt***. **The Sabbath is first named as
a gift**, with the verb of *"the bread which Yahweh has given you"* (16:15; WLC). **Bread and rest are given in
the same verb.** Its later grounding is twofold — creation (20:11) and the exodus (Deuteronomy 5:15) — and its
later status is *"a sign between me and you"* (31:13) (*not yet ingested*); **its first is neither: it is
given, like the food.**

***"Everyone stay in his place"*** — ***šəbû ʾîš taḥtāyw***, *sit, each under himself*. **The verb of
*"when we sat (bəšibtēnû) by the meat pots"*** (16:3; WLC). ***"Let no one go out (ʾal-yēṣēʾ)"*** — **the exodus
verb, *yāṣāʾ*, forbidden for a day.** **They came out of Egypt by going out; on the seventh day they are told
not to go out at all.**

***"So the people rested"*** — ***wayyišbətû hāʿām***. **The first time in Scripture that people are the
subject of *šābat*, *rest*.** The verb's earlier subjects are God (Genesis 2:2–3), day and night that *"will
not cease"* (8:22), and — in the causative — Pharaoh's charge *"you make them rest"* (5:5) and the leaven to be
put away (12:15) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **The rest Pharaoh called idleness, God gives as a day.**
See [[Bricks Without Straw]] and [[Sabbath]].

### Manna (16:31)

> **31** The house of Israel called its name Manna, and it was like coriander seed, white; and its taste was
> like wafers with honey.

***"The house of Israel"*** — ***bêt yiśrāʾēl***. **The phrase's first occurrence in Genesis or Exodus; its only
other one in Exodus is the book's last verse** — *"in the sight of all the house of Israel, throughout all
their journeys"* (40:38) (WLC search, Genesis–Exodus). **The people name the food themselves**: it is the
first thing in Exodus that Israel names.

***"Called its name Manna"*** — ***wayyiqrəʾû … ʾet-šəmô mān***, **the naming formula of Genesis** (WLC). **The
name keeps the question of 16:15.** See [[Names and Naming]].

***"Like coriander seed"*** — ***kəzeraʿ gad***. ***Gad*, *coriander*, occurs only here and in Numbers 11:7**
(WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). ***"White"*** — ***lābān***, the colour word of Laban's name and
Jacob's peeled rods (Genesis 30:35–37).

***"Like wafers with honey"*** — ***kəṣappîḥit bidbāš***. ***Ṣappîḥit* occurs nowhere else in the books checked** (WLC), and is usually reported as unique.
***Dəbaš*, *honey*, is the promised land's word** — *"a land flowing with milk and honey"* (3:8, 17; 13:5) — and
of Jacob's gift to the man in Egypt, *"a little honey"* (Genesis 43:11) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus).
**The wilderness bread tastes of the land it is leading to.** The text does not draw the line; the word is
the same.

**Numbers 11 describes it differently**: *"The manna was like coriander seed, and its appearance like the
appearance of bdellium … Its taste was like the taste of fresh oil"* (Numbers 11:7–8; WEB; *not yet
ingested*). **Exodus has honey wafers; Numbers has fresh oil.** Readers reconcile them as raw and cooked
manna — Numbers' taste comes after grinding and boiling — or let them stand as two traditions (reported, not
decided). **The wiki records both descriptions without harmonizing.**

### An omer kept before the Testimony (16:32–34)

> **32** Moses said, "This is the thing which Yahweh has commanded, 'Let an omer-full of it be kept throughout
> your generations, that they may see the bread with which I fed you in the wilderness, when I brought you out
> of the land of Egypt.'"
>
> **33** Moses said to Aaron, "Take a pot, and put an omer-full of manna in it, and lay it up before Yahweh, to
> be kept throughout your generations."
>
> **34** As Yahweh commanded Moses, so Aaron laid it up before the Testimony, to be kept.

***"That they may see the bread"*** — ***ləmaʿan yirʾû ʾet-halleḥem***. **The third memorial in three
chapters**: the Passover *"memorial"* (12:14), the unleavened bread as *"a sign … and … a memorial between your
eyes"* (13:9), and now a jar. **The first two are rites; this one is an object**, and its purpose is seeing,
the verb of *"you shall see Yahweh's glory"* (16:7). See [[Remembrance]].

***"With which I fed you"*** — ***heʾĕkaltî***, *I caused you to eat*. **God speaks in the first person
inside Moses' quotation**, and dates the feeding to *"when I brought you out"* — **the exodus verb again, with
God as subject, against the people's *"you have brought us out"* (16:3).**

***"A pot"*** — ***ṣinṣenet***. **The word occurs nowhere else in the books checked** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy, Joshua, Nehemiah, Psalms), and is usually reported as unique in the Hebrew Bible
(reported, not checked in this pass). **Hebrews remembers it as *"a
golden pot holding the manna"* in the ark, with *"Aaron's rod that budded, and the tablets of the covenant"***
(Hebrews 9:4; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **Exodus does not say the pot was gold, and does not say it was put in
the ark** — only *"before Yahweh"* and *"before the Testimony"*. **1 Kings 8:9 says that at the dedication of
the temple *"There was nothing in the ark except the two stone tablets"*** (WEB; *not yet ingested*). **The
three statements are recorded as they stand** — *before the Testimony* (Exodus), *in the ark* (Hebrews), and
*nothing in the ark but the tablets* (Kings). Readers reconcile them by placing the jar beside the ark, or by
a loss before Solomon's time (reported, not decided). The detail *golden* is usually traced to the Greek
translation of Exodus (reported, not checked in this pass).

***"Before the Testimony"*** — ***lipnê hāʿēdut***. **The first *ʿēdût* in Scripture** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Exodus). **The Testimony is the tablets placed in the ark** — *"You shall put the testimony which I shall
give you into the ark"* (25:16; [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]]) — **and neither tablets nor ark exist yet.** **The verse
is told from after Sinai.** Aaron is not yet a priest, and there is no tent to lay anything up in; *"before
Yahweh"* (16:33) is followed by an act that could only happen later. **Readers take 16:32–34 as a note placed
with the manna story by subject rather than by date** (reported, not decided); the wiki records the
anachronism as the text's own.

### Forty years (16:35–36)

> **35** The children of Israel ate the manna forty years, until they came to an inhabited land. They ate the
> manna until they came to the borders of the land of Canaan.
>
> **36** Now an omer is one tenth of an ephah.

***"Forty years"*** — ***ʾarbāʿîm šānâ***. **The narrator states the length of the wilderness before the
reason for it has happened** — the sentence of Numbers 14:33–34 (*not yet ingested*). **The flood's number
again** (Genesis 7:4, 12), now in years. **Joshua 5:12 closes it**: *"The manna ceased on the next day, after
they had eaten of the produce of the land … but they ate of the fruit of the land of Canaan that year"* (WEB;
*not yet ingested*).

***"An inhabited land … the borders of the land of Canaan"*** — ***ʾereṣ nôšābet … qəṣê ʾereṣ kənāʿan***. **Two
end points for one diet.** *Nôšābet*, *inhabited*, is the passive participle of ***yāšab*** — the verb of the
meat pots and of *"stay in his place"* (16:3, 29; WLC). **Israel complained of a land where it sat; the manna
lasts until it reaches a land where others sit.** See [[Canaan]].

***"An omer is one tenth of an ephah"*** — ***wəhāʿōmer ʿăśirît hāʾêpâ hûʾ***. **An explanatory note for
readers to whom the omer was no longer a familiar measure.** **The ephah is the measure of the meal offering
and of the poor man's sin offering** (Leviticus 5:11; *not yet ingested*); **a tenth of an ephah of fine
flour** becomes the standard grain offering (Numbers 28:5 and elsewhere; by reference, *not yet ingested*).
**The wilderness ration is the size of a later offering.** The text does not draw the line.

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 16:1 opens a new stage (*"They took their journey from Elim"*) and 17:1
  opens the next (*"from the wilderness of Sin"*). The chapter is one unit; its quail half is a single verse,
  and the plan's title is kept for continuity with Numbers 11.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** the order of 16:6–12, where Moses and Aaron announce the provision
  before God is reported promising it; the sixth-day rule given to Moses (16:5) and not reported passed on
  before 16:23; *"before the Testimony"* (16:34) and *"forty years"* (16:35), both told from after the events;
  the pot *"before the Testimony"* (Exodus) against *"in the ark"* (Hebrews 9:4) and *"nothing in the ark
  except the two stone tablets"* (1 Kings 8:9); the taste *"like wafers with honey"* (16:31) against *"like
  the taste of fresh oil"* (Numbers 11:8).
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** the equal omer as miracle or as sharing; *mān* as an Aramaic or older
  interrogative; *məḥuspās* as *round* or *flake-like*; *hinnîaḥ* and *nûaḥ* as one root; the scarlet *tôlaʿat*
  as named from a worm; *gulgōlet* and *Golgotha*; *golden* in Hebrews 9:4 as from the Greek Exodus; the
  daily-bread petition as an echo of *dəbar-yôm bəyômô*.
- **New pages:** the place [[The Wilderness of Sin]] (named so because the theme page [[Sin]] holds the bare
  title); the theme [[The Glory of God]], since *kəbôd YHWH* first appears here and runs to the end of the book;
  the connections [[Bread from Heaven - The Manna and John 6]] and
  [[He Who Gathered Much - Exodus 16.18 and 2 Corinthians 8.15]]. **Sinai was not given its own page**: the
  [[Horeb]] page already carries *Sinai* as an alias and defers the decision to the Sinai pericopes (19:1),
  so this pass records the first *Sinai* there.
