# The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 38:1–31

## Summary

The making account moves outside the tent: a hollow acacia altar, five cubits square and three high,
is overlaid with brass and fitted with horns, utensils, grating, rings, and carrying poles (38:1–7).
The basin and its base are made from the mirrors of women who ministered at the Tent of Meeting’s
door (38:8). Linen court hangings, pillars, sockets, silver fittings, an embroidered entrance
screen, and brass pins follow (38:9–20).

The account then turns from objects to responsibility and amounts. Moses commands the count, Ithamar
is named in the Levites’ service, Bezalel receives credit for the commanded work, and Oholiab is
named alongside him with his skills (38:21–23). The gold, silver, and brass are totaled. Census
silver from 603,550 men supplies a hundred silver sockets and the pillars’ fittings; the brass
supplies the listed sockets, altar, grating, utensils, and pins (38:24–31).

The chapter joins skilled making, women’s contributions, obedience, and accounting. Its fixed census
payment is distinct from the freewill gifts. Construction does not itself narrate sacrifice,
washing, the final erection, or the arrival of glory; those stages remain separately described in
Exodus 40.

## Notes

### Boundary and responsibility

The planned boundary, 38:1–31, is retained. The inventory is a distinct closing movement within the
unit: it accounts for materials used across the preceding construction, not only the objects made in
verses 1–20. The next pericope turns to priestly garments. Verse 22’s summary credit to Bezalel does
not require every later narrative event to have already been described. Oholiab is with him (38:23),
and 36:1–2 has already named the wider company.

Ithamar is named for the count under Moses’ command (38:21), not as the maker of the altar. Aaron
identifies his father and priestly family without a new action here. Uri, Hur, Ahisamach, Judah, and
Dan identify the makers through ancestry and tribe; the patriarchs are not present. The Levites are
the later group, not Levi himself. No new identity between Bezalel’s grandfather and Moses’
companion Hur is established.

### Command and manufacture

Exodus 38:1–7 corresponds to 27:1–8; 38:9–20 to 27:9–19; and the basin in 38:8 to 30:18. The altar
is explicitly for burnt offering and is distinct from the smaller gold incense altar of 37:25–28.
The court is one hundred by fifty cubits, with a twenty-cubit gate between two fifteen-cubit lengths
on the east. Its gate is distinct from the tent entrance and the inner veil. Silver-covered capitals
are expressly mentioned in 38:17, 19; the earlier court instructions do not give that detail.

The WEB uses both brass and bronze in this chapter (compare 38:29–30). Those English labels are
preserved without identifying an exact alloy or claiming a Hebrew distinction. The hollow altar is
not said to contain earth. The different earth-or-stone command in 20:24–25 therefore remains in
view beside the wood-and-metal altar of 27:1–8 and 38:1–7; the text does not explicitly explain
their relationship. See [[An Altar of Uncut Stones - Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 27, and Joshua 8]].

### The women and their mirrors

Verse 38:8 gives an unusually specific source for an object’s metal: the mirrors belonged to
ministering women at the Tent of Meeting’s door. The verse supplies neither personal names nor the
duties, frequency, or organization of their ministry. It does not call the mirrors sinful, describe
a renunciation of vanity, or make the women priests. Their identity with the spinners of 35:25–26 is
possible but not stated. Their contribution and ministry deserve a collective person page without
invented biographies.

There is also a question of setting. Exodus 33:7–11 describes a Tent of Meeting before the
sanctuary’s final erection in 40:17. The women’s designation in 38:8 could refer to that earlier
tent or describe them from a later perspective; this verse does not choose between those readings.
It should not silently become proof that the completed tabernacle was already functioning. See
[[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]].

### Amounts and uses

The text reports gold of 29 talents and 730 sanctuary shekels (38:24), census silver of 100 talents
and 1,775 shekels (38:25), and brass of 70 talents and 2,400 shekels (38:29). No modern weight
conversion is attempted. At half a shekel each, 603,550 men contribute 301,775 shekels. Comparing
that amount with the stated silver total implies 3,000 shekels to the talent within this account;
this is arithmetic from verses 25–26, not a separately verified history of weight standards.

One hundred talents go into a hundred sockets, one talent each; the remaining 1,775 shekels supply
hooks, capitals’ overlay, and fillets (38:27–28). The hundred sockets correspond to the earlier 96
board sockets and four veil sockets (26:19–25, 32; 36:24–30, 36). The tent entrance and court have
brass sockets, so the account does not make every sanctuary support silver.

The silver’s fixed half-shekel source matches 30:11–16, where the payment is ransom and atonement
money, equal for rich and poor. Exodus 35:24 also mentions silver among the willing contributions.
Both descriptions are retained: chapter 38 expressly identifies its counted silver as census silver,
but does not separately reconcile every voluntary silver gift with that tally. It is unwarranted to
describe every metal in the inventory as coming from an identical voluntary collection.

**The basin is absent from the brass-use list in 38:30–31**, although it was made of brass in 38:8.
Its separate mirror source may explain separate treatment, but the text supplies neither its weight
nor a statement that its metal was excluded from the total. The omission does not cancel its
manufacture. Likewise, the inventory does not disclose the weight or disposal of the surplus
reported in 36:7. These limits prevent treating the chapter as a fully itemized modern audit.

### Preparation and service

The basin is made here, filled with water in 40:30, and used by Moses, Aaron, and his sons in
40:31–32. The altar is made here and receives offerings in 40:29. The court is made here and raised
in 40:33; the glory follows in 40:34–35. The inventory’s precision should not erase those narrative
stages or suggest that material expenditure purchases God’s presence.
