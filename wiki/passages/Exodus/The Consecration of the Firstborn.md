# The Consecration of the Firstborn

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 13:1–16

## Summary

**Israel is out of Egypt, and the story stops a third time for law.** 12:51 ended with *"That same day,
Yahweh brought the children of Israel out"*; the march does not resume until 13:17. In between, God
speaks two verses and Moses speaks fourteen.

**God's word (13:1–2) is a single command and a claim.** *"Sanctify to me all the firstborn, whatever
opens the womb among the children of Israel, both of man and of animal. It is mine."* **The night before,
God struck the firstborn of Egypt *"both man and animal"* (12:12, 29); the morning after, he claims the
firstborn of Israel in the same pair of words.** Nothing in the two verses says what *sanctify* will mean
in practice.

**Moses' word (13:3–16) is in two panels, each built the same way.** He does not begin with the
firstborn. He begins with the day:

- **Unleavened bread (13:3–10).** *"Remember this day, in which you came out of Egypt, out of the house of
  bondage"*; the month is named, *Abib*; when Yahweh brings them into the land of the five peoples, seven
  days of unleavened bread and a feast on the seventh; and the father's word to the son, *"It is because
  of that which Yahweh did for me when I came out of Egypt"*. **It shall be a sign on the hand and a
  memorial between the eyes, *"that Yahweh's law may be in your mouth"*** (13:9).
- **The firstborn (13:11–16).** When Yahweh brings them into the land: every male firstborn of animals is
  Yahweh's; the firstborn donkey is redeemed with a lamb or has its neck broken; **every firstborn son is
  redeemed**. The son asks *"What is this?"*, and the father tells him that when Pharaoh would not let
  them go, *"Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt … Therefore I sacrifice to Yahweh all
  that opens the womb, being males; but all the firstborn of my sons I redeem"* (13:15). **It shall be a
  sign on the hand and *ṭôṭāpōt* between the eyes** (13:16).

**So God's two verses become a festival, a catechism, and a law of redemption, none of which God is
reported saying here.** It is the pattern of 12:21–27, where Moses relayed the Passover to the elders and
added the hyssop, the door, and the destroyer. **The text does not say whether Moses is expanding God's
word or relaying parts of it the narrator did not record**; the wiki notes the gap and does not fill it.

### The shape of it

| | 13:3–10 — unleavened bread | 13:11–16 — the firstborn |
|---|---|---|
| Opening memory | *"by strength of hand Yahweh brought you out"* (13:3) | — |
| *"When Yahweh brings you into the land"* | 13:5 — *"of the Canaanite, … which he swore to your fathers"* | 13:11 — *"of the Canaanite, as he swore to you and to your fathers"* |
| The practice | seven days unleavened; a feast on the seventh | animals given; donkey and son redeemed |
| The son | the father tells him unasked (13:8) | the son asks, *"What is this?"* (13:14) |
| The answer | *"what Yahweh did for me"* | *"Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt"* |
| The sign | *ʾôt* on the hand, *zikkārôn* between the eyes (13:9) | *ʾôt* on the hand, *ṭôṭāpōt* between the eyes (13:16) |
| The close | *"with a strong hand Yahweh has brought you out"* (13:9) | *"by strength of hand Yahweh brought us out"* (13:16) |

**Both panels close on the hand twice: the sign on the worshipper's hand and the strength of Yahweh's.**
The phrase *bəḥōzeq yād*, *by strength of hand*, occurs three times in this pericope (13:3, 14, 16) and
nowhere else in Genesis–Deuteronomy (WLC lemma search). **The Hebrew speech also changes number**: 13:3–4
addresses the people as plural *you* (*yəṣāʾtem*, *"you came out"*); from 13:5 every *you* is singular
(*yəbîʾăkā*, *"he brings you"*) (WLC). **The festival is commanded to a crowd and then handed to each
household, and finally to each father.**

## Key verses

### It is mine (13:1–2)

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,
> **2** "Sanctify to me all the firstborn, whatever opens the womb among the children of Israel, both of
> man and of animal. It is mine."

***"Yahweh spoke to Moses"*** — to Moses alone. The two Passover laws were spoken *"to Moses and Aaron"*
(12:1, 43); **Aaron is not named anywhere in 13:1–16.**

***"Sanctify"*** — ***qaddeš***, the piel imperative of *qādaš*. **The last time Scripture used this verb in
the piel was the seventh day**: *"God blessed the seventh day, and made it holy"* (Genesis 2:3,
*wayəqaddēš*). In Genesis–Exodus the piel of *qādaš* occurs at Genesis 2:3, then here, then not again until
Sinai (19:10, 14), where Moses is told to *"sanctify"* the people (WLC lemma search). **The first thing God
made holy was a day; the second is a class of persons and animals** — and this pericope will tie the two
together, since the firstborn law is given inside the law of a seven-day feast.

***"Whatever opens the womb"*** — ***peṭer kol-reḥem***. **The phrase is new here.** *Peṭer*, *that which
opens* or *firstling*, occurs first at 13:2 and, in the Torah, only in this pericope (13:2, 12, 13, 15),
Exodus 34:19–20, Numbers 3:12, 8:16 (in a feminine form), and 18:15 — every time in the firstborn law (WLC
lemma search). **In Genesis God was the one who opened wombs** — *"he opened her womb"* of Leah (29:31) and
of Rachel (30:22). **Here the child that opens the womb is God's.** See [[Barrenness]].

***"Both of man and of animal"*** — ***bāʾādām ûbabbəhēmâ***. The pair of 12:12 (*"both man and animal"*)
and 12:29. The firstborn struck in Egypt and the firstborn claimed in Israel are measured with the same
words.

***"It is mine"*** — ***lî hûʾ***, two words. **No reason is given in God's speech.** The reason is given
by Moses, at 13:15, and it is the night of 12:29. Numbers 3:13 gives it in God's voice: *"On the day that I
struck down all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I made holy to me all the firstborn in Israel, both
man and animal. They shall be mine"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*). See
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]].

### Remember this day (13:3–4)

> **3** Moses said to the people, "Remember this day, in which you came out of Egypt, out of the house of
> bondage; for by strength of hand Yahweh brought you out from this place. No leavened bread shall be
> eaten.
> **4** Today you go out in the month Abib.

***"Remember"*** — ***zākôr***, the infinitive absolute used as a command. **In the Torah this form
occurs five times: here; *"Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy"* (20:8); and three times in
Deuteronomy** — remember what Yahweh did to Pharaoh (7:18), what he did to Miriam (24:9), and what
Amalek did (25:17) (WLC lemma search). **This is the first of them, and the only one whose object is a day
other than the Sabbath.** See [[Remembrance]] and [[Sabbath]].

***"The house of bondage"*** — ***bêt ʿăbādîm***, literally *a house of slaves*. **This is the phrase's first
occurrence in this sense**; earlier strings of the same two nouns are *"your servant's house"* (Genesis
19:2) and the house of Pharaoh's servants (Exodus 8:3, 24; 7:28 and 8:20 in Hebrew), with *ʿebed*
in the construct (WLC). From here it becomes a formula: 13:14; the opening of the commandments,
*"who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage"* (20:2); and six times in
Deuteronomy (5:6; 6:12; 7:8; 8:14; 13:5, 10 in English) (WLC). **Exodus first named the thing in the
mouth of the people being freed from it.** See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"By strength of hand"*** — ***bəḥōzeq yād***. *Ḥōzeq* is from ***ḥāzaq***, *to be strong* — **the root
of Pharaoh's hardened heart** (7:13, 22; 9:35; and elsewhere) and of the Egyptians' urgency to send Israel
out (12:33) (WLC). **The noun *ḥōzeq* occurs in the Torah only in this pericope** (13:3, 14, 16; WLC lemma
search). **The strength that held Israel is answered by a strength that brought it out**, in the same
root. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"The month Abib"*** — ***ḥōdeš hāʾābîb***. **The first month of 12:2, named for the first time.**
*ʾĀbîb* means *fresh ears* of grain, and the word's first occurrence in Scripture is the hail: *"the barley
was in the ear"* — *ʾābîb* (9:31; WLC lemma search). **The month of the exodus is named for the crop the
seventh plague destroyed.** Whether the writer meant the echo cannot be shown; the word is the same. The
name recurs at 23:15 and 34:18 (*"for in the month Abib you came out of Egypt"*) and at Deuteronomy 16:1
(WLC). It is the older name; the later Babylonian month name *Nisan* (Nehemiah 2:1; Esther 3:7) is not
used in the Torah *(not yet ingested; not checked in this pass)*.

### When Yahweh brings you into the land (13:5–7)

> **5** It shall be, when Yahweh shall bring you into the land of the Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the
> Amorite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite, which he swore to your fathers to give you, a land flowing
> with milk and honey, that you shall keep this service in this month.
> **6** Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, and in the seventh day shall be a feast to Yahweh.
> **7** Unleavened bread shall be eaten throughout the seven days; and no leavened bread shall be seen
> with you. No yeast shall be seen with you, within all your borders.

***"The Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the Amorite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite"*** — **five
peoples, where the bush gave six.** At 3:8 and 3:17 the list was *"the Canaanite, the Hittite, the
Amorite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite"*; **here the Perizzite is missing** (WLC agrees
with the WEB). The text gives no reason. Lists of the land's peoples vary in length and order across the
Torah — ten in Genesis 15:19–21, six at Exodus 3:8, seven at Deuteronomy 7:1 (WLC; *not yet ingested*) — and
the wiki records the variation without reading a meaning into this one. See [[Canaan]] and
[[The Nations]].

***"Which he swore to your fathers"*** — ***nišbaʿ laʾăbōtêkā***. **The first time Exodus speaks of an oath
to the fathers.** The verb *šābaʿ* first occurs in Exodus here (13:5) and at 13:11, and then at 13:19 for
Joseph's oath (WLC). Genesis ended on it: *"the land which he swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob"*
(Genesis 50:24). At 13:11 the oath is sworn *"to you and to your fathers"* — **the generation that left is
counted among the ones sworn to.** See [[Oaths and Vows]].

***"A land flowing with milk and honey"*** — the phrase of 3:8, 17.

***"You shall keep this service"*** — ***wəʿābadtā ʾet-hāʿăbōdâ hazzōʾt***, verb and noun of the same root.
**The *ʿăbōdâ* that was Egypt's bondage (1:14; 2:23; 6:6, 9) became the Passover *"service"* at 12:25–26,
and here becomes the festival of unleavened bread.** The book's root, *ʿābad*, keeps changing masters.

***"In the seventh day shall be a feast to Yahweh"*** — ***ḥag laYHWH***. At 12:16 the seventh day was *"a
holy convocation"*; here it is a *ḥag*, a pilgrimage feast, the word of Moses' demand *"we must hold a feast
to Yahweh"* (10:9). **The feast Moses asked Pharaoh to let them keep in the wilderness is the feast they
are now told to keep in the land.**

***"No yeast shall be seen with you"*** — ***śəʾōr***, *leaven* or *starter dough*, beside *ḥāmēṣ*,
*leavened bread*. **12:15–20 forbade leaven *in the house*; here it may not be *seen* in all *your
borders*** (*gəbūlekā*). The rule grows with the territory.

### You shall tell your son (13:8–10)

> **8** You shall tell your son in that day, saying, 'It is because of that which Yahweh did for me when I
> came out of Egypt.'
> **9** It shall be for a sign to you on your hand, and for a memorial between your eyes, that Yahweh's
> law may be in your mouth; for with a strong hand Yahweh has brought you out of Egypt.
> **10** You shall therefore keep this ordinance in its season from year to year.

***"You shall tell"*** — ***wəhiggadtā***, the hiphil of *nāgad*. **Later Jewish tradition named the Passover
liturgy *Haggadah*, *the telling*, from this root, and the Passover text reads this verse at the
seder** (a matter of later practice, reported here and not checked against a source in this pass). In the
pericope itself, **this is the third telling commanded to a son**: *"that you may tell (təsappēr) in the
hearing of your son, and of your son's son"* (10:2, a different verb, *sāpar*); *"when your children ask
you"* (12:26); and here, **where the father speaks without being asked.** See
[[When Your Son Asks - Telling the Exodus to the Next Generation]].

***"What Yahweh did for me when I came out of Egypt"*** — ***lî … bəṣēʾtî***, first person singular. **The
answer is commanded for every future father, and every future father is to say *I* came out.** The
generation that left is not the speaker; the speaker is whoever keeps the feast. The same move is made at
13:14–16 (*"brought us out"*) and in Deuteronomy 6:21, *"We were Pharaoh's slaves in Egypt"* (WEB; *not yet
ingested*).

***"A sign to you on your hand, and … a memorial between your eyes"*** — ***ləʾôt ʿal-yādəkā
ûləzikkārôn bên ʿênêkā***. **The *ʾôt* that God set in the sky (Genesis 9:12–17), in the flesh
(17:11), and on the doorposts (12:13) is now set on Israel's own hand.** *Zikkārôn*, *memorial*, is the word
of 12:14 — *"This day shall be to you for a memorial"*. **Whether the verse means a physical object or a
figure of speech the text does not say**; at 13:9 *"It shall be"* has no expressed subject, and the
nearest thing it can refer to is the keeping of the feast in 13:6–8. See the note at 13:16.

***"That Yahweh's law may be in your mouth"*** — ***tôrat YHWH bəpîkā***. **The first *tôrat YHWH*, *law of
Yahweh*, in Scripture** (WLC search, Genesis–Deuteronomy; the construct with the divine name occurs only
here in the Torah). The singular *tôrâ* had appeared at Genesis 26:5 (*"my laws"*, plural) and at 12:49 as
*"One law"* for native and stranger. **Here, a day after leaving Egypt, before Sinai, the law of Yahweh is
something to have *in the mouth*** — that is, to recite. Luke 2:23 uses *"the law of the Lord"* (*nomos
kyriou*; SBLGNT) to introduce this very pericope. See
[[Holy to the Lord - The Firstborn Law and the Presentation of Jesus]].

***"With a strong hand"*** — ***bəyād ḥăzāqâ***, the adjective of *ḥāzaq*: the phrase of 3:19 and 6:1.

***"From year to year"*** — ***miyyāmîm yāmîmâ***, literally *from days to days*. The idiom is used of
Elkanah's yearly pilgrimage to Shiloh (1 Samuel 1:3; WLC; *not yet ingested*).

### Cause to pass over to Yahweh (13:11–13)

> **11** "It shall be, when Yahweh shall bring you into the land of the Canaanite, as he swore to you and
> to your fathers, and shall give it you,
> **12** that you shall set apart to Yahweh all that opens the womb, and every firstborn that comes from an
> animal which you have. The males shall be Yahweh's.
> **13** Every firstborn of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb; and if you will not redeem it, then you
> shall break its neck; and you shall redeem all the firstborn of man among your sons.

***"You shall set apart"*** — ***wəhaʿăbartā***, the hiphil of ***ʿābar***, literally *you shall cause to
pass over*. **The WEB's *"set apart"* is an interpretation; the verb is the common verb of crossing.** It
is the verb of God making the wind *"pass over the earth"* (Genesis 8:1) and of Jacob sending his family
across the Jabbok (32:23 in English) (WLC). **But in the law it is also the verb of child sacrifice**:
*"You shall not give any of your children (lit. of your seed) to make pass over (ləhaʿăbîr) to Molech"*
(Leviticus 18:21), and *"anyone who makes his son or his daughter to pass through (maʿăbîr) the fire"*
(Deuteronomy 18:10) (WLC; WEB renders Leviticus 18:21 *"as a sacrifice to Molech"*). **The firstborn law and
the prohibition of child sacrifice use one verb**, and this pericope is where the redemption clause is
first attached to it. The wiki's page on the problem was written before this ingest from the later texts;
see [[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]].

***"The males shall be Yahweh's"*** — ***hazzəkārîm laYHWH***. **The claim of 13:2 (*"all the firstborn"*) is
narrowed to males**, and it is narrowed again in 13:15 (*"being males"*). 13:2 does not say so; 13:12 does.

***"Every firstborn that comes from an animal"*** — ***peṭer šeger bəhēmâ***. *Šeger*, *offspring* of
cattle, is rare: in the Torah only here and in Deuteronomy's blessing and curse, *"the increase of your
livestock"* (7:13; 28:4, 18, 51) (WLC lemma search).

***"Every firstborn of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb"*** — ***tipdeh bəśeh***. **The first *pādâ*,
*redeem* or *ransom*, in Scripture** — Genesis has none (WLC lemma search). The word Moses' answer will use
of sons (*"all the firstborn of my sons I redeem"*, 13:15), and the word Deuteronomy uses for the exodus
itself: Yahweh *"redeemed you out of the house of bondage"* (Deuteronomy 7:8, *wayyipdəkā*; WEB, WLC).
**The donkey is the one unclean animal named**: it cannot be offered, so it is bought back with a *śeh* —
the word of the Passover lamb (12:3–5) and of Isaac's question, *"where is the lamb?"* (Genesis 22:7).
See [[The Ram Instead of the Son - Substitution at Moriah]].

***"Then you shall break its neck"*** — ***waʿăraptô***. *ʿĀrap*, from *ʿōrep*, *neck*, occurs in the Torah
only here, at the repetition in 34:20, and for the heifer whose neck is broken over an unsolved murder
(Deuteronomy 21:4, 6) (WLC lemma search). **An unredeemed firstborn donkey may not be kept and may not be
offered; it is killed.** The law closes off a third option. **What it does not do is give the son a
*"if you will not redeem"* clause** — the sentence turns from the donkey to the sons with no alternative
stated: *"and you shall redeem all the firstborn of man"*.

***"You shall redeem all the firstborn of man"*** — ***wəkōl bəkôr ʾādām bəbānêkā tipdeh***. No price or age
is given here. Numbers 18:16 gives both — *"from a month old … for five shekels of money"* — and Numbers 3
substitutes the Levites for the firstborn (WEB; *not yet ingested*). **Exodus 22:29 will state the claim
without this clause** — *"You shall give the firstborn of your sons to me"* — and 34:20 will restate it with
the clause. The tension between those two verses is recorded on
[[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]].

### What is this? (13:14–16)

> **14** It shall be, when your son asks you in time to come, saying, 'What is this?' that you shall tell
> him, 'By strength of hand Yahweh brought us out from Egypt, from the house of bondage.
> **15** When Pharaoh stubbornly refused to let us go, Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of
> Egypt, both the firstborn of man, and the firstborn of livestock. Therefore I sacrifice to Yahweh all
> that opens the womb, being males; but all the firstborn of my sons I redeem.'
> **16** It shall be for a sign on your hand, and for symbols between your eyes: for by strength of hand
> Yahweh brought us out of Egypt."

***"When your son asks you in time to come, saying"*** — ***kî-yišʾālkā binkā māḥār lēʾmōr***. **Deuteronomy
6:20 repeats the clause word for word** (WLC), with a longer question — *"What do the testimonies, the
statutes, and the ordinances … mean?"* (WEB). Joshua 4:6 and 4:21 use it for the stones from the Jordan:
*"when your children ask in the future, saying, 'What do you mean by these stones?'"* (WEB; WLC
*kî-yišʾālûn bənêkem māḥār lēʾmōr*; *not yet ingested*). ***Māḥār***, *tomorrow*, is the word WEB renders
*"in time to come"*. See [[When Your Son Asks - Telling the Exodus to the Next Generation]].

***"What is this?"*** — ***mah-zzōʾt***. **The shortest of the children's questions**: 12:26 asked *"What do
you mean by this service?"*; Deuteronomy 6:20 asks about *"the testimonies, the statutes, and the
ordinances"*. Here the son sees a donkey bought back with a lamb, or a firstling given, and asks only what
it is.

***"When Pharaoh stubbornly refused to let us go"*** — ***kî-hiqšâ parʿōh ləšalləḥēnû***, literally *when
Pharaoh made it hard to send us away*. **The verb is *qāšâ* in the hiphil — the verb of God's own forecast,
*"I will harden (ʾaqšeh) Pharaoh's heart"* (7:3)** — and those are its only two uses in Exodus (WLC lemma
search). **In God's forecast God is the subject; in the father's answer, Pharaoh is.** Exodus has used both
— God hardens and Pharaoh hardens — and this pericope puts them in one verb fifteen verses apart without
comment. Deuteronomy uses it again with God as subject, of Sihon: *"Yahweh your God hardened his spirit"*
(2:30) *(not yet ingested; WLC checked)*. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"Yahweh killed all the firstborn"*** — ***wayyahărōg YHWH***. **The narrator's verb at 12:29 was *nākâ*,
*struck*; the father's is *hārag*, *killed* — the verb of God's threat, *"I will kill (hōrēg) your son, your
firstborn"* (4:23)** (WLC). **The catechism uses the word of the promise, not of the report.** Then the
firstborn law follows as its consequence: *ʿal-kēn*, *therefore*. **Every redemption of a son is
grounded in the death of a son.** See [[Israel My Firstborn - Exodus 4.22, Hosea 11.1, and Matthew 2.15]].

***"Therefore I sacrifice … but … I redeem"*** — ***ʾănî zōbēaḥ … ʾepdeh***, a participle and an imperfect,
both first person singular. **The father describes himself as doing it now**, as if the law were already
kept in the land. The pair *sacrifice / redeem* is the whole law in two verbs: the animal goes to the altar,
the son is bought back.

***"Symbols between your eyes"*** — ***ṭôṭāpōt***. **The word occurs only three times in Scripture, all in
this phrase**: here, Deuteronomy 6:8, and Deuteronomy 11:18 (WLC lemma search, Torah; not checked outside
it). WEB renders it *"symbols"* here and *"frontlets"* in Deuteronomy 6:8 (*"You shall bind them for a sign
on your hand, and they shall be for frontlets between your eyes"*, WEB; *not yet ingested*). Its etymology
is uncertain. **Later Jewish practice took these verses literally, as the commandment of *tefillin*,
phylacteries, which contain passages including Exodus 13:1–16** (a matter of later practice, reported and
not checked in this pass); many readers take the verses as a figure, like *"Bind them around your
neck"* (Proverbs 3:3, WEB). **The text does not decide between them**, and at 13:9 the subject of *"It shall be"* is
a festival, not an object. The wiki adopts neither reading.

***"Your hand"*** — at 13:16 written ***yādəkâ*** with a final *he*, where 13:9 has the ordinary *yādəkā*
(WLC). The spelling is an orthographic variant and does not change the sense.

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 13:1–16 is the firstborn law with the unleavened-bread law
  inside it; 13:17 resumes the march (*"When Pharaoh had let the people go"*) and belongs with the sea.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** God's two verses against Moses' fourteen (expansion or unreported
  speech); *"all the firstborn"* (13:2) narrowed to males (13:12, 15); the five peoples against the bush's
  six; the hardening verb *qāšâ* with God as subject (7:3) and Pharaoh as subject (13:15); the redemption
  clause here against its absence at 22:29.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** the sign on the hand and between the eyes as literal (the later
  *tefillin*) or figurative; *ʾābîb* as a deliberate echo of 9:31; the meaning of *ṭôṭāpōt*.
- **No new person pages.** The father and son are generic; the peoples of the land are recorded on
  [[Canaan]] and [[The Nations]] until a pericope gives one of them a role.
- **Sources.** Exodus and Genesis are quoted from `raw/bible/`. Verses outside `raw/` (Leviticus 18:21;
  Numbers 3:12–13; 18:15–16; Deuteronomy 6:8, 20–21; 7:8; 18:10; 21:4; Joshua 4:6–7, 21–22; Proverbs 3:3;
  Luke 2:22–24)
  are quoted from the WEB as served by bible-api.com (`translation=web`). **Hebrew forms and counts were
  checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures `morphhb`)**: all of Exodus 13:1–16;
  Exodus 3:19; 4:23; 6:1; 7:28; 8:7, 20 (Hebrew numbering); 9:31; 10:2, 9; 34:19–20; Leviticus 18:21;
  Deuteronomy 6:8, 20–21; 7:1; 18:10; Joshua 4:6, 21; 1 Samuel 1:3; and
  lemma searches over Genesis–Deuteronomy as marked at each claim. **Luke 2:22–24's Greek was checked
  against the SBLGNT (MorphGNT).** Claims about later Jewish practice (the Haggadah, *tefillin*), the
  month name *Nisan*, and *ṭôṭāpōt* outside the Torah are marked at their point as not
  checked in this pass.
