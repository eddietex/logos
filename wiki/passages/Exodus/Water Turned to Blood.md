# Water Turned to Blood

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 7:14–25

## Summary

**The first blow falls on the river.** God tells [[Moses]] that Pharaoh's heart is *heavy* —
*kābēd*, the third of Exodus's hardening words — and sends him to meet the king *"in the morning"*
as he goes out to the water, standing *"by the river's bank"* with the rod *"which was turned to a
serpent"* (7:14–15). The message is the demand of 5:1 with a charge attached: *"until now you
haven't listened"* (7:16). **Then the purpose of the plagues is put to Pharaoh's face for the first
time**: *"In this you shall know that I am Yahweh"* (7:17). The river will be struck and turned to
blood, the fish will die, the river will stink, and *"the Egyptians shall loathe to drink water from
the river"* (7:18).

**The command widens the sign before it is done.** [[Aaron]] is to stretch his rod over *"the waters
of Egypt"* — rivers, streams, pools, and *"all their ponds of water"* — so that there is blood even
*"in vessels of wood and in vessels of stone"* (7:19). Moses and Aaron obey, in the formula of
obedience, and the rod strikes the water *"in the sight of Pharaoh, and in the sight of his
servants"* (7:20). **Everything forecast at 7:17–18 happens, word for word** (7:21).

**Then the magicians do the same** (7:22). Pharaoh's heart *"was hardened"*, *"as Yahweh had
spoken"*; he *"turned and went into his house, and he didn't even take this to heart"* (7:23). **The
Egyptians dig for water around the river** (7:24), and the narrator counts the days: *"Seven days were
fulfilled, after Yahweh had struck the river"* (7:25).

**Nobody in the pericope asks for relief, and none is given.** No prayer, no bargain, no lifting of the
plague is reported. The frogs that follow end in a request for prayer and a set time (8:8–9); the
blood ends with no exchange at all. It does not end in the text; it is outlasted.

### The shape of it

| | verses | what happens | turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7:14 | *"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn"* (*kābēd*) | the diagnosis |
| 2 | 7:15–16 | *"in the morning"*; *"by the river's bank"*; *"Let my people go"* | the meeting set |
| 3 | 7:17–18 | *"In this you shall know"*; strike, blood, fish die, river stinks, loathe to drink | **the forecast** |
| 4 | 7:19 | all the waters of Egypt, to vessels of wood and stone | the sign widened |
| 5 | 7:20–21 | *"as Yahweh commanded"*; struck in Pharaoh's sight; blood everywhere | **the forecast fulfilled** |
| 6 | 7:22 | the magicians match it; heart *ḥāzaq*, *"as Yahweh had spoken"* | the match and the hardening |
| 7 | 7:23 | *"he didn't even take this to heart"* | Pharaoh walks away |
| 8 | 7:24–25 | the Egyptians dig; *"seven days were fulfilled"* | the plague outlasted |

**The pericope repeats the pattern of [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]]**: a forecast, its exact
fulfilment, a match by the magicians, and a heart hardened *"as Yahweh had spoken"*. **The difference
is that this time the land is damaged.** The rods harmed nobody; the river kills its fish and leaves a
country digging for water.

## Key verses

### The heavy heart, and the morning at the river (7:14–16)

> **14** Yahweh said to Moses, "Pharaoh's heart is stubborn. He refuses to let the people go.
> **15** Go to Pharaoh in the morning. Behold, he goes out to the water; and you shall stand by the
> river's bank to meet him; and the rod which was turned to a serpent you shall take in your hand.
> **16** You shall tell him, 'Yahweh, the God of the Hebrews, has sent me to you, saying, "Let my
> people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness:" and behold, until now you haven't listened.

***"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn"*** — ***kābēd lēb parʿōh***, an adjective, not a verb: *Pharaoh's
heart is heavy*. **It is the word Moses used of himself at the bush**: *"I am slow of speech, and of a
slow tongue"* — *kəbad-peh ûkəbad lāšôn*, *heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue* (4:10). **The deliverer
heavy of mouth is sent to a king heavy of heart.** And the root is the one of Pharaoh's first order,
*tikbad hāʿăbōdâ*, *"Let heavier work be laid on the men"* (5:9): **the king who made Israel's
service heavy now has a heavy heart.** (All three verified in the WLC; WEB renders *kābēd* in three
different ways.) The adjective recurs in Exodus for the later plagues' severity — the *"grievous swarms"* of
flies (8:24, Hebrew 8:20), the *"very grievous pestilence"* (9:3), the *"very grievous hail"* (9:18, 24), and the locusts that *"were very
grievous"* (10:14) (WLC lemma search) — so the heaviness of Pharaoh's heart is answered, plague by
plague, by heavy blows. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"He refuses to let the people go"*** — ***mēʾēn ləšallaḥ hāʿām***. *Mēʾēn*, *he has refused*: the
narrative now treats refusal as Pharaoh's settled stance, not a single answer.

***"In the morning. Behold, he goes out to the water"*** — ***hinnēh yōṣēʾ hammaymâ***. **Pharaoh has a
habit, and God knows it.** The text does not say why the king goes to the water — whether to bathe, to
inspect the flood, or for a rite — and the wiki does not supply a reason. **It is the same water where
a daughter of an earlier Pharaoh *"came down to bathe"* and found the basket** (2:5).

***"You shall stand by the river's bank"*** — ***wəniṣṣabtā liqrāʾtô ʿal-śəpat hayəʾōr***. **The phrase
*śəpat hayəʾōr*, *the lip of the Nile*, occurs only three times in Genesis and Exodus before this, and
all three are at turning points** (WLC lemma search):

- **Genesis 41:3, 17** — the thin cattle stand *"on the brink of the river"*, and Pharaoh tells Joseph
  *"I stood on the brink of the river"*. **The river's edge is where a Pharaoh first learned that a God
  he did not know was speaking to him.** See [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]].
- **Exodus 2:3** — Moses' mother lays the basket *"in the reeds by the river's bank"*. **Moses was set
  down as a baby on the bank where he now stands as a messenger.** See [[The Birth of Moses]].

*Śāpâ* is also the word of Moses' objection, *ʿăral śəpātayim*, *"uncircumcised lips"* (6:12, 30).
**The man of uncircumcised lips stands on the river's lip.** The noun is the same; whether the
narrator intends the play is not something the text says, and the wiki records it as a resonance.

***"The rod which was turned to a serpent"*** — ***nehpak lənāḥāš***. **The rod is identified by the
bush, not by the court**: *nāḥāš*, the snake of 4:3, not the *tannîn* of 7:9–12. See
[[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]]. **And the verb is *hāpak*, *to turn*, which will turn the water at
7:17 and 7:20.** In Genesis the same verb is the overthrow of Sodom — *"He overthrew those cities"*
(19:25, *wayyahăpōk*; also 19:21, 29) — and the *"flaming sword which turned every way"* at Eden
(3:24) (WLC). **The rod that turned is the rod that turns.**

***"Yahweh, the God of the Hebrews"*** — the title of 3:18 and 5:3, the name God told Moses to use
before the king. **And *"that they may serve me"*** — *wəyaʿabdunî*, the *ʿābad* of the whole book,
now with a charge appended: ***"until now you haven't listened"*** — *lōʾ-šāmaʿtā ʿad-kōh*. 7:13 said
he *"didn't listen"*; here God says it to his face. See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

### In this you shall know (7:17–18)

> **17** Yahweh says, "In this you shall know that I am Yahweh. Behold, I will strike with the rod
> that is in my hand on the waters which are in the river, and they shall be turned to blood.
> **18** The fish that are in the river shall die, and the river shall become foul; and the Egyptians
> shall loathe to drink water from the river."'"

***"In this you shall know that I am Yahweh"*** — ***bəzōʾt tēdaʿ kî ʾănî YHWH***. **The refrain is now
addressed to Pharaoh, in the second person singular.** At 6:7 it was said to Israel (*"you shall
know"*); at 7:5 it was said about Egypt (*"the Egyptians shall know"*). **Here it is said to the man who
said *"I don't know Yahweh"* (5:2).** Each plague will be a lesson set to that sentence. See
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

***"I will strike with the rod that is in my hand"*** — ***ʾānōkî makkeh bammaṭṭeh ʾăšer-bəyādî***.
**The speaker is Yahweh, but the rod is in Moses' hand** (7:15), and in 7:19 it will be in Aaron's.
The sentence is God's speech quoted inside Moses' message, and *my hand* collapses the chain of
speech of 7:1–2 into a single *I*. **God strikes, and the hand that holds the rod is the messenger's**;
the text does not separate them. And at 7:25 the narrator settles whose blow it was: *"after Yahweh had
struck the river"*.

***Nākâ***, *to strike*, has a history in Exodus already (WLC lemma search):

- **2:11–13** — an Egyptian *striking* a Hebrew; Moses *striking* the Egyptian (WEB *"killed"*, 2:12);
  a Hebrew *striking* his fellow.
- **3:20** — God's promise: *"I will reach out my hand and strike Egypt with all my wonders"*.
- **5:14, 16** — Israel's foremen *"were beaten"* by Pharaoh's taskmasters.

**So the verb has gone from Egyptian against Hebrew, to Moses' private blow, to Pharaoh's foremen
beating Israel, and now to God striking Egypt.** The promise of 3:20 is kept with its own verb. Moses'
secret blow at 2:12, which failed and drove him out, is replaced by a public one at God's command.
See [[Moses Flees to Midian]].

***"The fish that are in the river shall die"*** — ***wəhaddāgâ … tāmût***. *Dāgâ*, *fish*, occurs in
Genesis only at 1:26, 28: *"let them have dominion over the fish of the sea"* (WLC lemma search). **The
first creatures put under the image of God's rule are the first to die in the plagues.**

***"The river shall become foul"*** — ***ûbāʾaš hayəʾōr***, *the river will stink*. **This is the verb of
the foremen's curse at 5:21**: *"you have made us a stench (hibʾaštem) … in the eyes of Pharaoh"*.
**Israel was made to stink to Pharaoh; now Pharaoh's river stinks.** Before Exodus, *bāʾaš* occurs only
at Genesis 34:30, Jacob's *"to make me odious"* after Shechem; after this it is the land stinking with dead frogs
(8:14, Hebrew 8:10) and the hoarded manna (16:20, 24) (WLC lemma search). See [[Bricks Without Straw]].

***"The Egyptians shall loathe to drink"*** — ***wəniləʾû miṣrayim lištôt***, *Egypt will weary of
drinking*. *Lāʾâ* is to be exhausted: at Genesis 19:11 the men of Sodom *"wearied themselves"* trying
to find Lot's door (WLC).

### All the waters of Egypt (7:19)

> **19** Yahweh said to Moses, "Tell Aaron, 'Take your rod, and stretch out your hand over the waters
> of Egypt, over their rivers, over their streams, and over their pools, and over all their ponds of
> water, that they may become blood; and there shall be blood throughout all the land of Egypt, both
> in vessels of wood and in vessels of stone.'"

**The forecast to Pharaoh named the river; the command to Aaron names every water there is.**
Four kinds, in Hebrew ***nahărōtām*** (*their rivers*, the Nile's branches), ***yəʾōrêhem*** (*their
streams*, the canals), ***ʾagmêhem*** (*their pools*, the marshes), and ***kol-miqwēh mêmêhem*** (*all
their ponds of water*).

***Miqwēh mêmêhem*** — ***the gathering of their waters***. **The phrase is Genesis 1:10**: *"the
gathering together of the waters (ûləmiqwēh hammayim) he called 'seas'."* In Genesis, Exodus,
Leviticus, Numbers, and Job the noun occurs only at Genesis 1:10, here, and Leviticus 11:36, of a pool
of gathered water (WLC lemma search; Leviticus is not yet in `raw/`, so it is not quoted). **The waters God gathered on the third day are the waters Aaron's rod turns to blood.** With
the fish of 1:26 dying in them, **the first plague reads as a small undoing of the third and fifth days
of creation in one country.** Exodus does not say so; the vocabulary does. See [[Creation]] and
[[The Flood as Un-Creation]].

***"Stretch out your hand"*** — ***nəṭēh yādəkā***, the verb of God's *"outstretched arm"* (6:6) and of
*"when I stretch out my hand on Egypt"* (7:5). **Aaron's hand does what God said his own would do.**

***"In vessels of wood and in vessels of stone"*** — ***ûbāʿēṣîm ûbāʾăbānîm***, literally *"and in the
woods and in the stones"*. **The WEB supplies *vessels***, and that is the usual reading: water already
drawn and stored in jars and troughs. Some interpreters read *wood and stone* as Egypt's images of its
gods. The Hebrew allows both; the wiki takes neither.

### The blow (7:20–21)

> **20** Moses and Aaron did so, as Yahweh commanded; and he lifted up the rod, and struck the waters
> that were in the river, in the sight of Pharaoh, and in the sight of his servants; and all the waters
> that were in the river were turned to blood.
> **21** The fish that were in the river died; and the river became foul, and the Egyptians couldn't
> drink water from the river; and the blood was throughout all the land of Egypt.

***"Moses and Aaron did so, as Yahweh commanded"*** — ***wayyaʿăśû-kēn … kaʾăšer ṣiwwâ YHWH***, the
obedience formula of 7:6 again. See [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

***"And he lifted up the rod, and struck"*** — ***wayyārem bammaṭṭeh wayyak***. **The subject is singular
and unnamed.** 7:19 told Aaron to stretch out the rod; 7:17 had God say *"the rod that is in my
hand"*; 7:15 put it in Moses' hand. **The text does not say whose hand lifts it**, and the
ambiguity follows the chain of speech of 7:1–2 into action.

***"In the sight of Pharaoh, and in the sight of his servants"*** — *ləʿênê parʿōh ûləʿênê ʿăbādāyw*.
**The same pair as 5:21**: the foremen said Moses had made them stink *"in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in
the eyes of his servants"* (*bəʿênê parʿōh ûbəʿênê ʿăbādāyw*). The blow is struck before the audience
in whose eyes Israel was made odious.

**7:21 fulfils 7:18 almost word for word**: fish dead, river stinking, Egyptians unable to drink. ***"And
the blood was throughout all the land of Egypt"*** fulfils 7:19. **The narrator adds nothing and
subtracts nothing.** As at 7:13, prediction and event are told in the same words.

**This is the third sign of the bush, enlarged.** At 4:9 God gave Moses a sign for Israel: *"take of
the water of the river, and pour it on the dry land. The water which you take out of the river will
become blood on the dry land."* That sign was for the elders, in a cup's worth. **Here it is for
Pharaoh, and it is the whole river.** See [[The Burning Bush]] and [[Signs and Wonders]].

**And it is the river of 1:22.** *"You shall cast every son who is born into the river"* — *hayəʾōrâ*,
the same river. **Exodus does not draw the link.** Later readers did: Wisdom of Solomon 11:6–7 says
the enemies were *"troubled with clotted blood instead of a river's ever-flowing fountain, to rebuke
the decree for the slaying of babes"* (WEB, deuterocanonical; quoted from bible-api.com). Revelation's
third bowl turns *"the rivers and springs of water"* to blood with the comment *"they poured out the
blood of the saints and prophets, and you have given them blood to drink"* (16:4–6, WEB). **The wiki
records the measure-for-measure reading as a reading.** See [[The Oppression in Egypt]] and
[[Waters Turned to Blood - The First Plague in the Psalms and Revelation]].

### The magicians, and the heart (7:22–23)

> **22** The magicians of Egypt did the same thing with their enchantments; and Pharaoh's heart was
> hardened, and he didn't listen to them; as Yahweh had spoken.
> **23** Pharaoh turned and went into his house, and he didn't even take this to heart.

***"The magicians of Egypt did the same thing"*** — ***wayyaʿăśû-kēn ḥarṭummê miṣrayim***. **The verb
phrase is the obedience formula's own** — *wayyaʿăśû-kēn*, *"they did so"*, the words used of Moses and
Aaron at 7:20. **The magicians "do so" too, and what they do is make more blood.** The text does not
ask where they found clean water to turn; 7:24 implies the Egyptians could still dig for it.
**Their match does not reverse the plague; it adds to it.** See
[[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]].

***"With their enchantments"*** — ***bəlāṭêhem***, from *lāṭ*, *secrecy*. At 7:11 the same act was
*bəlahăṭêhem*, which the morphology tags as *lahaṭ*, *flame*. **Here the spelling is the one most
lexicons use for *secret arts*.** (Both forms verified in the WLC.)

***"Pharaoh's heart was hardened … as Yahweh had spoken"*** — ***wayyeḥĕzaq lēb parʿōh***, the qal of
*ḥāzaq*, exactly as at 7:13. **The plague opened with *kābēd* in God's mouth and closes with *ḥāzaq* in
the narrator's**, and again no agent is named. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"He didn't even take this to heart"*** — ***wəlōʾ-šāt libbô gam-lāzōʾt***, literally *"he did not
set his heart even to this"*. **The *zōʾt*, *this*, is the *zōʾt* of 7:17**: *"In this (bəzōʾt) you
shall know"*. **God offered *this* as the lesson; Pharaoh does not set his heart to *this*.** The verb
*šît* returns at 10:1, where God *sets* his signs among them (*šitî ʾōtōtay*), and Job uses *šît* with
*lēb* when he asks what man is, *"that you should set your mind on him"* (Job 7:17, *tāšît … libbekā*)
(WLC).

***"Pharaoh turned and went into his house"*** — **he leaves the bank.** The king who came out to the
water in the morning goes home, where presumably there was water already drawn — though 7:19 had blood
*"in vessels of wood and in vessels of stone"*. The text does not resolve it.

### The digging, and the seven days (7:24–25)

> **24** All the Egyptians dug around the river for water to drink; for they couldn't drink the river
> water.
> **25** Seven days were fulfilled, after Yahweh had struck the river.

***"All the Egyptians dug"*** — ***wayyaḥpərû kol-miṣrayim***. **The king goes home; his people dig.**
*Ḥāpar* in Genesis is the verb of Abraham's and Isaac's wells (21:30; 26:15–32) — the patriarchs
digging for water in a dry land, and the Philistines stopping it up (WLC lemma search). **In Egypt, the
land of the river, people are reduced to digging like herdsmen in the Negev.** See
[[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]].

***"Seven days were fulfilled"*** — ***wayyimmālēʾ šibʿat yāmîm***. **The verb is *mālēʾ*, *to be
filled*, the last verb of 1:7**: *"the land was filled with them"* (*wattimmālēʾ hāʾāreṣ*) (WLC).
The Israelites filled the land; now the days are filled. **Seven days is a week**, and the narrator
does not say whether the plague ended then, or only that a week passed before the next word to
Pharaoh. The *"seven days"* of the flood's warning (Genesis 7:4, 10) and of creation's week stand
behind the number; the text makes no comment.

***"After Yahweh had struck the river"*** — ***ʾaḥărê hakkôt-YHWH ʾet-hayəʾōr***. **The narrator names
the striker.** Whoever lifted the rod at 7:20, the blow was Yahweh's.

**The pericope ends on time passing, not on a resolution.** 7:26 in the Hebrew (8:1 in the English)
opens the next demand. The WLC's chapter 7 runs four verses further than the English — Hebrew
7:26–29 is English 8:1–4 — so all verse numbers on this page are English, which agree with the Hebrew
up to 7:25.

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** 7:14 opens a new divine speech and a new scene; 7:25 closes the
  first plague with the seven days, and 7:26 (Hebrew) / 8:1 (English) opens the second.
- **The first cycle of three** (blood, frogs, lice) is often read with the second and third as a
  scheme of three triads, each opening with Moses sent to Pharaoh *"in the morning"* (7:15; 8:20;
  9:13) — the first two at the water, the third without it. The plan's units follow chapter
  divisions, so the triads are not reflected in the unit boundaries; this page notes the pattern only
  as it bears on 7:15.
- **Whose hand lifts the rod at 7:20** is left open, as the text leaves it.
- **Natural explanations** — red earth or algae in the Nile flood — are a standing line of
  interpretation and are not pursued here. Exodus calls it *dām*, blood, and the wiki reports what the
  text says.
- **The John 2 contrast** — Moses' first public sign turns water to blood, Jesus' first sign turns it
  to wine — is a later reading and is recorded as such; no claim is made about the Greek of John 2.
- **Hebrew forms on this page were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Open Scriptures
  Hebrew Bible, `morphhb`): all of Exodus 7:14–25, and Exodus 1:7; 2:3; 4:9–10; 7:26, 29; 8:1. Word
  counts and occurrences (*kābēd* adjective and verb, *śāpâ*, *hāpak*, *nākâ*, *dāgâ*, *bāʾaš*,
  *lāʾâ*, *ʾăgam*, *miqwēh*, *ḥāpar*, *šît*, *mālēʾ*, *dām*) come from lemma searches of the WLC of
  Genesis and Exodus, with Job, Leviticus, and Numbers where stated. Psalms, Revelation, John, and
  Wisdom of Solomon are quoted only from the WEB at bible-api.com; no claim is made about their Hebrew
  or Greek.
