# The Birth of Moses

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 2:1–10

## Summary

**Pharaoh's third measure is defeated in ten verses, by three women and a basket, on the river he
made a grave.** Exodus 1 ended with a public decree — *"You shall cast every son who is born into
the river"* (1:22) — and no word of anything stopping it. Chapter 2 opens with a marriage and a son.
His mother hides him three months; when she can hide him no longer she puts him *into the river*,
exactly as ordered, but inside a sealed basket. His sister watches. Pharaoh's own daughter comes
down to bathe, finds the basket, hears the baby cry, has pity on him, and knows at once that he is
a Hebrew. The sister offers a Hebrew nurse; the nurse is the mother; Pharaoh's household pays her
wages to nurse her own son. When the child is weaned he is brought back, *"he became her son"*, and
she names him [[Moses]] — *"Because I drew him out of the water"* (2:10).

**Nobody in the passage has a name except the child, and the child is named by the enemy's
daughter.** The father is *"a man of the house of Levi"*, the mother *"a daughter of Levi"*, the
sister *"his sister"*, the rescuer *"Pharaoh's daughter"*. Their names arrive later —
[[Amram]] and [[Jochebed]] at 6:20, [[Miriam]] at 15:20 — and the wiki gives them pages under
those names, with what this passage alone says kept distinct from what is supplied from elsewhere.
**Exodus 1 named the two midwives and left the king anonymous; Exodus 2:1–10 leaves everyone
anonymous except the one who will matter, and lets an Egyptian choose what he is called.** See
[[Names and Naming]].

**God is not mentioned once.** Not in the hiding, the basket, the timing of the bath, the
princess's pity, or the sister's quick tongue. The passage is the purest case in the Bible so far
of [[Providence]] told without a single divine verb — more so than [[The Oppression in Egypt]],
where God at least *"dealt well with the midwives"* (1:20). The narrator's silence is broken only
at 2:23–25, where God hears, remembers, sees, and knows.

### The shape of it

| | verses | who acts | what she does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2:1–2 | the mother | marries, bears, *sees that he is good*, hides him three months |
| 2 | 2:3 | the mother | makes a *tēbâ*, seals it, sets it in the reeds by the river |
| 3 | 2:4 | the sister | stands far off *to know* what will be done to him |
| 4 | 2:5–6 | Pharaoh's daughter | comes down, sees, sends, opens, sees, pities, recognizes |
| 5 | 2:7–8 | the sister | offers a Hebrew nurse, and is told *"Go"* |
| 6 | 2:9 | Pharaoh's daughter and the mother | the mother hired to nurse her own son |
| 7 | 2:10 | Pharaoh's daughter | adopts and names him |

**Every verb of rescue in the passage belongs to a woman.** The only male subjects are the
anonymous father, who marries in 2:1 and then disappears, and the infant, whose one act is to cry
(2:6). **It is the arithmetic Pharaoh got wrong in 1:16 and 1:22** — *"if it is a daughter, then she
shall live"* — carried to its conclusion: the daughters he spared, including his own, are the ones
who undo the decree.

## Key verses

### A son born and hidden (2:1–2)

> **1** A man of the house of Levi went and took a daughter of Levi as his wife.
> **2** The woman conceived, and bore a son. When she saw that he was a fine child, she hid him
> three months.

**The chapter begins with a household, the thing Pharaoh's measures were built to stop.** The
midwives were rewarded with *bāttîm*, households (1:21); 2:1 is *"a man of the house of Levi"*,
*mibbêt lēwî*. **Both parents are Levites**, which the narrator records twice in one verse without
explaining it. It will matter: Moses and Aaron's priestly line comes out of this marriage (6:16–27),
and the tribe of [[Levi]] — cursed by its father for the sword at Shechem (Genesis 49:5–7) — will
take its place at Sinai by the sword again (Exodus 32:26–29). See [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]] and
[[Priesthood]].

**The passage is told as if this were a first marriage and a first child**, and it is not. The
sister of 2:4 is old enough to stand watch and to speak to a princess, and Aaron is three years
older than Moses (*"Moses was eighty years old, and Aaron eighty-three years old"*, 7:7). **The
narrator strips the family to the one birth that matters** and lets the older children appear only
when they are needed. Nothing is said of how Aaron, born before 1:22 or in defiance of it, survived.

*"A daughter of Levi"* — ***bat-lēwî*** — can be read as *a Levite woman* or literally as *Levi's
daughter*. Numbers 26:59 takes it literally: *"The name of Amram's wife was Jochebed, the daughter
of Levi, who was born to Levi in Egypt."* **Read that way, Moses' mother is Jacob's granddaughter**,
and Exodus 6:20 makes her *"his father's sister"* to Amram — a marriage Leviticus 18:12 later
forbids. The literal reading also bears on the length of the sojourn, since it leaves very few
generations between the descent into Egypt and the exodus. See [[Jochebed]] and
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

***"When she saw that he was a fine child"*** — ***wattēreʾ ʾōtô kî-ṭôb hûʾ***, literally *she saw
him, that he was good* — **is the sentence of the creation week.** *"God saw the light, and saw that
it was good"* (Genesis 1:4) is *wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm ʾet-hāʾôr kî-ṭôb*: the same verb, the same object
marker, the same *kî-ṭôb*. The mother looks at her son the way God looked at the light. **It is not
a statement that she hid him because he was handsome** — every mother of every drowned boy saw that
her son was good — **but the narrator's way of marking a beginning.** The New Testament reads the
adjective as beauty: Stephen has Moses *"exceedingly handsome"* (Acts 7:20), and Hebrews says his
parents hid him *"because they saw that he was a beautiful child, and they were not afraid of the
king's commandment"* (Hebrews 11:23). See [[Creation]].

**Hebrews makes the hiding an act of faith and of fearlessness**, and attributes it to both
parents, where Exodus gives it to the mother alone. *"Not afraid of the king's commandment"* sets
Moses' parents beside the midwives, who *"feared God"* rather than the king (1:17). See [[Faith]]
and [[The Fear of God]].

### The ark on the river (2:3–4)

> **3** When she could no longer hide him, she took a papyrus basket for him, and coated it with
> tar and with pitch. She put the child in it, and laid it in the reeds by the river's bank.
> **4** His sister stood far off, to see what would be done to him.

***Tēbat gōmeʾ***, ***a papyrus basket***, **is an ark.** *Tēbâ* occurs in the Bible only of Noah's
vessel (Genesis 6–9, the WEB's *ship*) and of this basket (2:3, 5). Both are sealed against water;
both carry one rescued life — or one rescued family — through a water that is killing everyone else;
both float without oar or sail, at the mercy of what carries them. **Noah's is built by God's
command to God's specification; this one is improvised by a mother with no word from God at all.**
See [[The Two Arks - Noah's Ship and Moses' Basket]] *(new)* and [[Noah and the Flood]].

***"Coated it with tar and with pitch"*** — ***wattaḥmərâ baḥēmār ûbazzāpet*** — **are not Noah's
words.** Genesis 6:14 seals the ark with *kōper*. **The *ḥēmār*, *tar*, is Babel's**: *"they used
tar for mortar"* (Genesis 11:3), and the *"tar pits"* of the valley of Siddim (Genesis 14:10) — the
only other uses of the word in the Torah. **Babel bound bricks with it to make a name; here it binds
papyrus to save a child, in the country that has just put Israel to making bricks.** See
[[Come, Let Us - Babel's Bricks and Pharaoh's]] and [[The Tower of Babel]].

***Gōmeʾ***, ***papyrus***, is the reed Bildad says cannot grow without water (Job 8:11) — the plant
of the Nile marsh, the material of Egypt's writing. See [[Bildad's First Speech]].

***"In the reeds"*** — ***bassûp*** — **names the sea Israel will cross.** The body of water the
WEB calls the *Red Sea* (13:18; 15:4) is in Hebrew *yam sûp*, *the sea of reeds*. **Moses is laid
among the *sûp* as an infant and leads the people through the *sûp* as a man**; and the *sûp* is where
*"Pharaoh's chariots and his army"* are cast (15:4) — the water that was meant for Hebrew sons
receiving Egypt's soldiers instead. See [[The Nile]] and [[Retribution]].

**The mother obeys the decree to the letter.** Pharaoh said to cast every son *into the river*; she
puts him in the river. **The verb is different** — 1:22's *šālak*, *cast*, against 2:3's *śîm*,
*put*, *laid* — and the difference is the whole act: a son thrown away and a son placed. See
[[Deception]], where this is the gentlest case the theme has.

***"His sister stood far off, to see what would be done to him"*** — ***wattētaṣṣab ʾăḥōtô mērāḥōq
lədēʿâ mah-yyēʿāśeh lô*** — **is a watch at a distance over a child in danger, and Genesis has one
before it.** Hagar, with Ishmael dying of thirst, *"sat down opposite him, a good way off, about a
bow shot away. For she said, 'Don't let me see the death of the child'"* (Genesis 21:16) —
*harḥēq*, the same root *rāḥaq*. **Hagar sits so as not to see; the sister stands in order to
know.** In both scenes the child cries (*"God heard the voice of the boy"*, Genesis 21:17; *"the baby
cried"*, Exodus 2:6), and in both the child is saved near water by someone who opens her eyes. See
[[Watching from Far Off - Hagar and Moses' Sister]] *(new)* and
[[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]].

*"To see"* is the WEB's rendering of ***lədēʿâ***, ***to know*** — **the verb Pharaoh failed in
1:8.** The king *"didn't know Joseph"*; the sister stations herself *to know* what will become of
Joseph's people's son.

### Pharaoh's daughter (2:5–6)

> **5** Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe at the river. Her maidens walked along by the
> riverside. She saw the basket among the reeds, and sent her servant to get it.
> **6** She opened it, and saw the child, and behold, the baby cried. She had compassion on him, and
> said, "This is one of the Hebrews' children."

**The rescuer is the person in Egypt closest to the decree.** She is unnamed, she is the king's
daughter, and she comes *down* (*yārad*) to the river her father has filled with Hebrew boys. **She
sees the basket, sends for it, opens it, and sees the child** — the narrator slows to five verbs,
and then gives the one sound in the passage: ***wəhinnēh-naʿar bōkeh***, *and behold, a boy
weeping*. See [[Pharaoh's Daughter (of Moses' time)]] *(new)* and [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

***"She had compassion on him"*** — ***wattaḥmōl ʿālāyw*** — **is *ḥāmal*, to pity or to spare.**
It is the verb of sparing someone one has the power to kill: Saul *"spared Agag"* (1 Samuel 15:9),
and David is angry with the rich man who *"had no pity"* (2 Samuel 12:6). **The princess has the
power of her father's decree in her hands and spares.** It is not the Joseph cycle's word for
compassion — that is *raḥămîm*, the yearning of the inward parts (Genesis 43:14, 30) — and the
[[Compassion and Mercy]] page has been corrected to say so. See [[Kingship and Empire]].

***"This is one of the Hebrews' children"*** — **she knows at once, and saves him anyway.** The
recognition comes *after* the pity in the verse, and does not undo it. Whether she knew by
circumcision, by the basket, or by the plain fact that no Egyptian baby would be in the river, the
narrator does not say; what he records is that **the princess's first words in Scripture are the
identification that her father's law makes a death sentence.** *Hebrews* is, as everywhere in
Genesis and Exodus, the word a foreigner uses. See
[[Abram the Hebrew - The First Use of the Name]].

### The sister and the nurse (2:7–9)

> **7** Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Should I go and call a nurse for you from the
> Hebrew women, that she may nurse the child for you?"
> **8** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Go." The young woman went and called the child's mother.
> **9** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child away, and nurse him for me, and I will give
> you your wages." The woman took the child, and nursed it.

**The sister speaks one sentence, and it is a masterpiece of tact.** She does not ask that the
child be spared, does not say he is her brother, and does not presume the princess will keep him.
She offers a service — *"for you"*, twice — and leaves the decision where it already lies. **The
princess answers with one word, *"Go"*** (*lēkî*), and the sister is renamed by the narrator in the
same verse: no longer *his sister* but ***hāʿalmâ***, ***the young woman***. See [[Miriam]] *(new)*.

***ʿAlmâ*** is a young woman of marriageable age; the word is the one translated *virgin* in the
Greek of Isaiah 7:14, and the wiki notes the occurrence here without taking up that debate.

***"Take this child away, and nurse him for me, and I will give you your wages"*** — **Pharaoh's
house pays a Hebrew mother to keep alive the son Pharaoh ordered drowned.** *Śəkārēk*, *your wages*,
is the word of Jacob and Laban's long wage-quarrel (*"Appoint me your wages"*, Genesis 30:28); in
Exodus it will come back as the plunder Israel takes on the way out (*"jewels of silver, jewels
of gold, and clothing"*, 3:22; 11:2; 12:35–36) — **Egypt paying for Israel's service at the beginning and
the end.** The mother is hired to do the thing she was risking her life to do. **No text says she
told the princess who she was**, and the princess never asks. See [[Work]] and
[[Wealth and Possessions]].

### He became her son (2:10)

> **10** The child grew, and she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named
> him Moses, and said, "Because I drew him out of the water."

**The child grows up twice in this chapter**: *"The child grew"* here, and *"when Moses had grown
up"* (2:11) — the same verb *gādal* — first in his mother's arms, then in Pharaoh's house. How long
the nursing lasted is not said; in the ancient Near East weaning commonly came at two or three
years, which is the usual reading, and the text does not fix it.

***"He became her son"*** — ***wayhî-lāh ləbēn*** — **is the language of adoption**, the
claim Jacob makes, in the reverse direction, over Ephraim and Manasseh: *"Ephraim and Manasseh,
even as Reuben and Simeon, will be mine"* (Genesis 48:5).
**A Hebrew boy is adopted into the royal house of the nation enslaving his people.** Stephen: *"Pharaoh's
daughter took him up, and reared him as her own son. Moses was instructed in all the wisdom of the
Egyptians"* (Acts 7:21–22). Hebrews makes his later renunciation of that sonship the act of faith:
*"By faith, Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter"*
(Hebrews 11:24). See [[Adoption]] and [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]].

**The parallel with Joseph is close and inverted.** Joseph came into Egypt a Hebrew slave and was
made Egyptian by Pharaoh's grant — an Egyptian name, an Egyptian wife, a place in the court
(Genesis 41:45). Moses is born a Hebrew in Egypt and is made Egyptian by Pharaoh's daughter — an
adoptive mother and, if the usual derivation is right, an Egyptian name. **Joseph's Egyptian
standing saved Israel's household; Moses' will be thrown away to save Israel's people.** See
[[Joseph]] and [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]].

***"She named him Moses, and said, 'Because I drew him out of the water'"*** — **the name is a pun
that runs the wrong way, and the wrong way is the point.** *Mōšeh* is set against ***məšîtihû***,
*I drew him out*, from *māšâ*, a rare verb. But *mōšeh* is not the passive form (*drawn out* would be
*māšûy*); it is the active participle: ***the one who draws out*.** The princess explains the name by
what was done to him; the form of the name says what he will do. The verb's other use in the Hebrew
Bible is the psalmist's *"He drew me out of many waters"* (Psalm 18:16; 2 Samuel 22:17), and Isaiah
remembers *"the days of old, Moses and his people"* as the days of *"he who brought them up out of
the sea"* (Isaiah 63:11). See [[Drawn Out of the Water - The Name of Moses]] *(new)*.

**That an Egyptian princess explains her son's name with a Hebrew pun is part of the irony, not a
problem the narrator overlooked.** The name is usually connected with Egyptian *msy*, *to bear*,
*-mose*, *son* or *born*, familiar from royal names like Ahmose and Thutmose and from **Ramesses**,
*Ra has borne him* — the name of the store-city the Hebrews were building (1:11). On that
derivation the princess gave the boy a common Egyptian name, half of a theophoric name with the god
left off, and **the narrator heard in it a Hebrew word for what the boy would do**. The derivation
is the standard scholarly proposal and is not settled; the wiki records it as such. See
[[Rameses]] and [[Names and Naming]].

## The decree and the women

**Read against Exodus 1, the passage is a point-by-point undoing of the decree.**

| Exodus 1 | Exodus 2:1–10 |
|---|---|
| *"if it is a daughter, then she shall live"* (1:16) | three daughters save him: mother, sister, princess |
| *"You shall cast every son … into the river"* (1:22) | the son is *put* into the river, in an ark |
| *"Pharaoh commanded all his people"* (1:22) | Pharaoh's own daughter disobeys |
| the king *"didn't know Joseph"* (1:8) | the sister stations herself *to know* (2:4) |
| the Hebrews *made to serve* in Egypt (1:13–14) | a Hebrew mother *paid wages* by Egypt (2:9) |
| the midwives *saved the boys alive* (1:17) | the princess *spares* (*ḥāmal*) one (2:6) |

**The deliverer of Israel is raised at the expense of the king who meant to kill him, in the king's
own house.** That is the pattern Exodus will follow on a national scale — Egypt pays for the exodus
(12:35–36), and Pharaoh's army ends in the water — and it is the pattern of
[[You Meant Evil Against Me - The Two Intentions at Genesis 50.20]]: what was meant for evil turned to
good, with no visible hand turning it.

**Matthew's infancy narrative uses this passage as well as Exodus 1.** The child is hidden, the
decree is evaded, and the refuge is a foreign country — but Matthew's refuge is Egypt itself
(Matthew 2:13–15). See [[Every Son Who Is Born - Pharaoh's Decree and Herod's]].

**Hebrews 11:23 is the New Testament's one comment on the passage**, and it reads the mother's
improvisation as faith: hiding the child *"by faith"*, *"not afraid of the king's commandment"*.
**The faith Hebrews sees is a faith that acts without a word from God** — Exodus gives no promise,
no vision, no instruction to Moses' parents. See [[Faith]].

## Notes

- **Unnamed figures given named pages.** The passage names only Moses. The wiki creates
  [[Jochebed]] and [[Amram]] under the names Exodus 6:20 supplies, and [[Miriam]] under the name
  Numbers 26:59 implies — it lists only one sister of Aaron and Moses. **The identification of the
  sister of 2:4 with Miriam is an inference**, near-universal but not stated in Exodus, and the
  Miriam page says so. Pharaoh's daughter is never named in Exodus and takes the schema's
  narrative-keyed qualifier; later tradition's names for her (Josephus's *Thermuthis*, the
  rabbinic identification with the *Bithiah* of 1 Chronicles 4:18) are recorded on her page and
  not adopted.
- **The derivation of *Moses* from Egyptian *msy*** is the standard proposal, not a settled fact.
- **The wage-and-plunder link (2:9 and 3:22; 12:35–36)** is thematic, not lexical: the plunder
  passages do not use *śākār*.
- **Correction made in this pass**: [[Compassion and Mercy]] described the princess's verb as the
  Joseph cycle's; the Joseph cycle's word is *raḥămîm*, and Exodus 2:6 uses *ḥāmal*.
