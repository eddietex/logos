# The Crossing of the Sea

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 13:17–14:31

## Summary

**The march resumes where 12:51 left it, and in forty-one verses Israel goes from Succoth to the far
shore of the sea.** The law of the firstborn (13:1–16) is over; *"When Pharaoh had let the people go"*
(13:17) picks the story up again. The pericope moves in three stages:

- **The route (13:17–22).** God does not take the short road through *"the land of the Philistines"*, for
  fear the people will *"see war"* and turn back; he leads them round *"by the way of the wilderness by the
  Red Sea"*. Moses carries Joseph's bones. They camp at Etham, and **Yahweh goes before them in a pillar of
  cloud by day and of fire by night**, which *"didn't depart from before the people"*.
- **The trap (14:1–14).** God tells them to **turn back** and camp by the sea, so that Pharaoh will think
  them *"entangled in the land"*; he will harden Pharaoh's heart, Pharaoh will pursue, and God will *"get
  honor"* over him. Pharaoh's heart and his servants' change — *"What is this we have done?"* — and six
  hundred chariots overtake Israel by the water. The people cry out to Yahweh and turn on Moses: *"Because
  there were no graves in Egypt, have you taken us away to die in the wilderness?"* Moses answers: *"Don't
  be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of Yahweh … Yahweh will fight for you, and you shall be
  still."*
- **The crossing (14:15–31).** *"Why do you cry to me? … go forward."* The angel of God and the pillar
  move behind the camp and stand between the two armies all night. Moses stretches out his hand; **an
  east wind blows all night, the sea becomes dry land, and the waters stand as a wall on the right and on
  the left.** Egypt follows. In the morning watch Yahweh looks out through the pillar, throws the army into
  confusion, and clogs the chariot wheels; the Egyptians say *"Yahweh fights for them"*. Moses stretches out
  his hand again, the sea returns, and *"There remained not so much as one of them."* **Israel sees the
  Egyptians dead on the shore, fears Yahweh, and believes *"in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses."***

**The pericope is built on announcement and fulfilment, twice.** God says what will happen (14:1–4, 15–18);
the narrator reports it happening, often in the same words. **The difference this time is that God
announces the outcome to Israel's enemy's discredit and Israel's benefit *before* the people know there is
an enemy** — and the people, who have not heard 14:1–4, react as if the trap were real.

### The shape of it

| | God's word | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| The route | *"turn back and encamp before Pihahiroth"* (14:2) | *"They did so"* (14:4) |
| Pharaoh's thought | *"They are entangled in the land"* (14:3) | *"the people had fled"* (14:5) |
| The hardening | *"I will harden Pharaoh's heart"* (14:4) | *"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"* (14:8) |
| The pursuit | *"he will follow after them"* (14:4) | *"The Egyptians pursued them"* (14:9) |
| The sea | *"stretch out your hand over the sea, and divide it"* (14:16) | *"Moses stretched out his hand … the waters were divided"* (14:21) |
| Dry ground | *"into the middle of the sea on dry ground"* (14:16) | *"into the middle of the sea on the dry ground"* (14:22) |
| The Egyptians' heart | *"I myself will harden the hearts of the Egyptians, and they shall go in after them"* (14:17) | *"The Egyptians pursued, and went in after them"* (14:23) |
| The return | *"that the waters may come again on the Egyptians"* (14:26) | *"The waters returned, and covered the chariots"* (14:28) |
| The knowledge | *"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* (14:4, 18) | *"Yahweh fights for them against the Egyptians!"* (14:25) |

**The last row is the only one where the fulfilment is not stated in the announcement's words.** God says
twice that Egypt *"shall know that I am Yahweh"*; what the narrator records is the Egyptians naming Yahweh
as the one fighting, with Moses' own verb from 14:14 (*lāḥam*, WLC), as they flee. **Whether that is the
knowledge 14:4 and 14:18 mean, the text does not say.**

**Three words thread the chapter in Hebrew** (all WLC):

- ***ḥāzaq***, *harden* — God the subject at 14:4, 8, and 17. **At 14:17 the object widens from
  *"Pharaoh's heart"* to *lēb miṣrayim*, singular, *"the heart of Egypt"*** (WEB *"the hearts of the
  Egyptians"*).
- ***kābad***, *be heavy* — the root of Pharaoh's *heavy* heart at 7:14; 8:15, 32; 9:7, 34; 10:1, here in
  the niphal of God's *"I will get honor"* (*ʾikkābədâ*, 14:4, 17; *bəhikkābədî*, 14:18), and in the noun
  of the chariots driven ***"heavily"*** (*kəbēdut*, 14:25, its only occurrence in the Torah; WLC lemma
  search). **The heaviness that was Pharaoh's heart becomes God's glory and the drag on Pharaoh's wheels.**
  See [[Hardening of the Heart]].
- ***rāʾâ***, *see* — *"lest … they see war"* (13:17); *"see the salvation of Yahweh … the Egyptians whom
  you have seen today, you shall never see them again"* (14:13); *"Israel saw the Egyptians dead"* (14:30);
  *"Israel saw the great work"* (14:31). See [[Seeing and Being Seen]].

## Key verses

### Not by the way of the Philistines (13:17–18)

> **17** When Pharaoh had let the people go, God didn't lead them by the way of the land of the
> Philistines, although that was near; for God said, "Lest perhaps the people change their minds when they
> see war, and they return to Egypt";
> **18** but God led the people around by the way of the wilderness by the Red Sea; and the children of
> Israel went up armed out of the land of Egypt.

***"When Pharaoh had let the people go"*** — ***bəšallaḥ parʿōh***, the piel of *šālaḥ*, *send away*: the
verb of every *"Let my people go"* (5:1; 7:16; WLC), **now reported as done.** The verb of 14:5 will be the
same: *"that we have let Israel go"* (*šillaḥnû*).

***"God didn't lead them"*** — ***nāḥām***, from ***nāḥâ***, *to lead, guide*. **Its first occurrences are
in the mouth of Abraham's servant: *"Yahweh has led me on the way"*** (Genesis 24:27, 48; WLC lemma search).
In the Torah it occurs at those two verses, here and at 13:21, in the Song (15:13), at 32:34, Numbers 23:7,
and Deuteronomy 32:12 (WLC lemma search). See [[Providence]].

***"The land of the Philistines"*** — the coast road. **The Philistines of Genesis were a settled people at
Gerar who swore treaties** (Genesis 21:32–34; 26); the reason God gives for avoiding their road is war.
**The narrator calls it *"near"*, *qārôb*, and says God chose the longer road on purpose** — the first time
in the exodus that God's choice of route is explained. See [[The Land of the Philistines]].

***"Lest perhaps the people change their minds"*** — ***pen-yinnāḥēm hāʿām***. **The niphal of *nāḥam* is
the verb of God's grief at the flood: *"Yahweh was sorry that he had made man"*** (Genesis 6:6–7; WLC). In
the Torah it is used of God at Genesis 6:6–7, Exodus 32:12, 14, Numbers 23:19, and Deuteronomy 32:36, and of
people as *comfort* or *regret* elsewhere (Genesis 24:67; 27:42; 37:35; 38:12; 50:21) (WLC lemma search).
**Here it is a possibility God foresees in the people and plans around** — the first time in Exodus it is
used, and at 32:12–14 it will be Moses asking God to *"turn from your fierce wrath, and repent"*. See
[[Divine Grief and Relenting]].

***"When they see war"*** — ***milḥāmâ***. **The word of Pharaoh's first fear**: *"when any war breaks out,
they also join themselves to our enemies, and fight against us"* (1:10; *milḥāmâ … wənilḥam*, WLC).
**Pharaoh feared Israel would fight; God fears Israel will run from fighting; at 14:14 Yahweh fights for
them** (*yillāḥēm*, the same verb). See [[War]].

***"God led the people around"*** — ***wayyassēb***, the hiphil of *sābab*, *turn about*. **The detour is
God's, not an error** — the verb returns in Deuteronomy 2:1, 3 for the wilderness circuit around Seir
(WLC; *not yet ingested*).

***"By the way of the wilderness by the Red Sea"*** — ***derek hammidbār yam-sûp***. **The second *yam sûp*
in the book, after the locusts were thrown into it** (10:19). See [[The Red Sea]].

***"Went up armed"*** — ***waḥămušîm***. **The word occurs only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). Its
sense is uncertain; the WEB's *"armed"* is one reading, and *"in battle array"* or *"in ranks"* — some
connect it with *ḥāmēš*, *five*, as *organised in fives* — are others (reported, not checked in this pass).
**Whatever it means, the people who *"went up"* ready for war are the people God has just routed away from
war.** The text does not comment on the tension.

### The bones of Joseph (13:19)

> **19** Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, for he had made the children of Israel swear, saying,
> "God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up my bones away from here with you."

**The oath of Genesis 50:25, quoted back, with two differences in the Hebrew** (both WLC):

- **The swearing is doubled.** Genesis 50:25 has *wayyašbaʿ*, *he made swear*; Exodus 13:19 has
  ***hašbēaʿ hišbîaʿ***, the infinitive absolute with the finite verb — *he had surely made swear* — **the
  same doubled form as the promise inside the oath, *pāqōd yipqōd*, *"God will surely visit"*.** The
  narrator gives the oath the emphasis Joseph gave the promise.
- **A word is added: *ʾittəkem*, *"with you"*.** Genesis 50:25: *"you shall carry up my bones from here"*;
  Exodus 13:19: *"… from here with you"*. **Joseph is to go up as one of the people, not after them.**

**The verse is placed after the march has begun, not on the night of departure.** Exodus 13:17 opens
*"When Pharaoh had let the people go"*, and 13:20 records the stage from Succoth to Etham; **Moses takes the
bones somewhere on that road.** The wiki's earlier pages placed the act *"on the night of the departure"*;
the refinement is recorded on [[Carry Up My Bones - Joseph's Oath and Its Keeping]] and [[Joseph]].

***"God will surely visit you"*** — ***pāqōd yipqōd*** — **the third time the phrase has been kept**: at the
bush (*pāqōd pāqadtî*, 3:16), when the people believed (*"Yahweh had visited"*, 4:31), and now by Moses'
own act. See [[Yahweh Visited Sarah - Paqad and the Visitations of God]] and [[Oaths and Vows]].

### Succoth to Etham, and the pillar (13:20–22)

> **20** They took their journey from Succoth, and encamped in Etham, in the edge of the wilderness.
> **21** Yahweh went before them by day in a pillar of cloud, to lead them on their way, and by night in a
> pillar of fire, to give them light, that they might go by day and by night:
> **22** the pillar of cloud by day, and the pillar of fire by night, didn't depart from before the
> people.

***"Etham, in the edge of the wilderness"*** — ***ʾētām biqṣēh hammidbār***. **Numbers 33:6 repeats the
stage in the same words**: *"They traveled from Succoth, and encamped in Etham, which is in the edge of the
wilderness"* (WLC; *not yet ingested*). See [[Etham]] and [[Succoth (Egypt)]].

***"Yahweh went before them"*** — ***wYHWH hōlēk lipnêhem***, a participle: **he was going before them.**
**The narrator does not say *the pillar went before them* and Yahweh was in it; he says Yahweh went, *in*
(*bə-*) a pillar.** At 14:19 the same going-before is given to *"the angel of God"* and to the pillar, side
by side. See the note there.

***"A pillar of cloud … a pillar of fire"*** — ***ʿammûd ʿānān … ʿammûd ʾēš***. ***ʿAmmûd*, *pillar*, occurs
here for the first time in Scripture** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy); **the pillars of Genesis
are *maṣṣēbâ*, set up by men** (28:18; 35:14, 20) — see
[[The Pillar Jacob Raised and the Pillars the Law Forbids]]. **This one is not set up; it moves.** The word
will serve both the cloud (33:9–10; Numbers 12:5; 14:14; Deuteronomy 31:15) and the tabernacle's posts
(26:32 onward) (WLC). ***ʿĀnān*, *cloud*, last appeared in the bow** — *"I set my rainbow in the cloud"*
(Genesis 9:13–16) — and from here it becomes the sign of the presence at Sinai and over the tent (19:9, 16;
24:15–18; 40:34–38; WLC). See [[The Bow in the Cloud]] and [[Light and Darkness]].

***"To give them light, that they might go by day and by night"*** — ***ləhāʾîr lāhem***, the hiphil of
*ʾôr*, *light*: **the verb of the creation's lights, *"to give light on the earth"*** (Genesis 1:15, 17;
WLC). **At the ninth plague *"all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings"* (10:23); now the
light travels with them.**

***"Didn't depart"*** — ***lōʾ-yāmîš***. In the Torah *mûš* occurs here, of Joshua who *"didn't depart from
the tent"* (33:11), and of the ark and Moses, which did not leave the camp when the people went up against the
hill country (Numbers 14:44; *not yet ingested*) (WLC lemma search). **The first thing said never to leave Israel is the pillar.**

### Turn back (14:1–4)

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,
> **2** "Speak to the children of Israel, that they turn back and encamp before Pihahiroth, between Migdol
> and the sea, before Baal Zephon. You shall encamp opposite it by the sea.
> **3** Pharaoh will say of the children of Israel, 'They are entangled in the land. The wilderness has
> shut them in.'
> **4** I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and he will follow after them; and I will get honor over Pharaoh,
> and over all his armies; and the Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh." They did so.

***"That they turn back"*** — ***wəyāšūbû***. **God's instruction is to go back** — the verb of *"they
return (wəšābû) to Egypt"*, the outcome he avoided at 13:17 (WLC). **The route that kept them from war now
turns them toward it**, deliberately and with the reason given. Numbers 33:7 records the move: *"They
traveled from Etham, and turned back to Pihahiroth, which is before Baal Zephon: and they encamped before
Migdol"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*).

***"Pihahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, before Baal Zephon"*** — **three place-names given only to fix
a camp**, none of them named before. ***Migdol*** means *tower*, the noun of Babel's *"tower"*
(*migdāl*, Genesis 11:4; WLC) in a related form. ***Baʿal ṣəpōn*** carries the name of a god — *Baal of the
north*, or *of Zaphon* (the sense of *ṣāpôn* here is reported, not checked in this pass); *baʿal* itself is
an ordinary noun for *owner* or *husband* in Genesis (e.g. 20:3). **None of the three has an agreed location**; see [[Pihahiroth]],
[[Migdol]], and [[Baal Zephon]].

***"They are entangled in the land"*** — ***nəbukîm hēm bāʾāreṣ***. *Nəbukîm*, the niphal participle of
*būk*, *to be confused, perplexed*, **occurs only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). **Pharaoh's
reading of the situation is what God means him to read**: the people turned back and lost.

***"I will harden Pharaoh's heart"*** — ***wəḥizzaqtî***, the piel of *ḥāzaq*, the verb of 4:21. **The
plagues are over, and the hardening is not**: God says it once more, and the narrator confirms it at 14:8.
See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"I will get honor over Pharaoh"*** — ***wəʾikkābədâ bəparʿōh***, the niphal of *kābad*, *I will be made
heavy*, *glorified*. **The root of Pharaoh's heavy heart** (7:14; 8:15, 32; 9:7, 34; 10:1; WLC) **turned to
God's glory.** **It is the first time in Scripture God speaks of getting glory for himself** (the niphal of
*kābad* with God as subject first appears here and at 14:17–18 in the Torah, then at Leviticus 10:3; WLC
lemma search). See [[For This Very Purpose - Exodus 9.16 in Romans 9.17]].

***"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"*** — the formula of 7:5 (*"when I stretch out my hand on
Egypt"*), answered here for the last time in the contest.

***"They did so"*** — ***wayyaʿăśû-kēn***. **The people obey a route they do not understand.** Nothing says
they were told why.

### What is this we have done? (14:5–9)

> **5** The king of Egypt was told that the people had fled; and the heart of Pharaoh and of his servants
> was changed towards the people, and they said, "What is this we have done, that we have let Israel go
> from serving us?"
> **6** He prepared his chariot, and took his army with him;
> **7** and he took six hundred chosen chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt, and captains over all of
> them.
> **8** Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and he pursued the children of Israel; for the
> children of Israel went out with a high hand.
> **9** The Egyptians pursued them. All the horses and chariots of Pharaoh, his horsemen, and his army
> overtook them encamping by the sea, beside Pihahiroth, before Baal Zephon.

***"The people had fled"*** — ***bāraḥ hāʿām***. **The verb of every flight in the family**: Hagar *"fled
from her face"* (Genesis 16:6, 8), Jacob from Esau and from Laban (27:43; 31:20–22), Moses *"from the face
of Pharaoh"* (Exodus 2:15) (WLC lemma search). **What was released with Pharaoh's blessing asked (12:32) is
reported to him as a flight.** The text does not say who told him, or whether the report was that they had
gone further than three days. See [[Affliction and Flight - Hagar and the Exodus]].

***"The heart of Pharaoh and of his servants was changed"*** — ***wayyēhāpēk ləbab***, the niphal of
*hāpak*, *turn over*. **The verb of the rod *"turned to a serpent"* (7:15), of the Nile *"turned to blood"*
(7:17, 20), of the wind *"turned"* to the west (10:19), and of Sodom's *overthrow* (Genesis 19:21, 25, 29)**
(WLC lemma search). **The servants who begged *"Let the men go"* (10:7) turn with their
king.** See [[Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)]].

***"What is this we have done?"*** — ***mah-zzōʾt ʿāśînû***. **The question of Genesis turned inward.** The
string *mah-zzōʾt* with *ʿāśâ* is asked by God of the woman (Genesis 3:13), by a Pharaoh of Abram (12:18),
by Abimelech of Isaac (26:10), by Jacob of Laban (29:25), and by the brothers of God (42:28) — and **in this
pericope twice, by Egypt of itself (14:5) and by Israel of Moses (14:11)** (WLC; those seven are its only
uses in Genesis–Joshua). See [[What Is This You Have Done - The Accusing Question from Eden to the Sea]].

***"From serving us"*** — ***mēʿobdēnû***. **The book's root, *ʿābad*, once more.** Pharaoh's regret is not
for his firstborn but for his workforce. At 14:12 the people will use the same verb twice — *"that we may
serve the Egyptians"*. See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"Six hundred chosen chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt"*** — ***šēš-mēʾôt rekeb bāḥûr***. **Six
hundred chariots against *"about six hundred thousand on foot"*** (12:37). The narrator does not draw the
numbers together. ***"Captains"*** — ***šālīšīm***, from *šālôš*, *three*, often explained as the third man
in a chariot (reported, not checked in this pass).

***"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"*** — ***wayḥazzēq YHWH***. **The fifth narrated hardening with
Yahweh as subject** (9:12; 10:20, 27; 11:10; 14:8; WLC).

***"With a high hand"*** — ***bəyād rāmâ***. **Numbers 33:3 uses the phrase of the same day**: *"the children
of Israel went out with a high hand in the sight of all the Egyptians"* (WEB; WLC). **Numbers 15:30 uses it of
defiant sin**: *"the soul who does anything with a high hand … blasphemes Yahweh"* (WLC; *not yet
ingested*). **The phrase marks open, unhidden action**, and in Israel's mouth the same openness is either
freedom or contempt. *Rāmâ* is from *rûm*, the root of God's command at 14:16: ***"Lift up (hārēm) your
rod"*** (WLC). **The people go out with a raised hand; Moses will raise his rod over the sea.**

***"Overtook them"*** — ***wayyaśśîgû***. **Laban too *"overtook"* Jacob after a pursuit** (*wayyaśśēg*,
Genesis 31:25; WLC), in the scene the wiki reads as a proto-exodus — see
[[Served, Released, Pursued - The Laban Years as Proto-Exodus]].

### No graves in Egypt (14:10–12)

> **10** When Pharaoh came near, the children of Israel lifted up their eyes, and behold, the Egyptians were
> marching after them; and they were very afraid. The children of Israel cried out to Yahweh.
> **11** They said to Moses, "Because there were no graves in Egypt, have you taken us away to die in the
> wilderness? Why have you treated us this way, to bring us out of Egypt?
> **12** Isn't this the word that we spoke to you in Egypt, saying, 'Leave us alone, that we may serve the
> Egyptians?' For it would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness."

***"They were very afraid"*** — ***wayyîrəʾû məʾōd***. **The verb of 14:31, *"the people feared
Yahweh"*.** The chapter begins with Israel fearing Egypt and ends with Israel fearing God, in the same
verb (WLC). See [[The Fear of God]].

***"Cried out to Yahweh"*** — ***wayyiṣʿăqû***, the verb of the officers who *"cried to Pharaoh"* (5:15) and
of Moses at the frogs (8:12, WEB; 8:8 Hebrew) (WLC). **They cry to Yahweh and in the same breath accuse
Moses.** See [[Prayer]].

***"Because there were no graves in Egypt"*** — ***hamibbəlî ʾên-qəbārîm bəmiṣrayim***, a double negative,
*is it for lack of there being no graves*. **The sarcasm is the people's.** Readers often add that Egypt
was the land of tombs above all others; that is a point about Egyptian history, reported and not drawn
from the text. **What the text supplies is Genesis's own record**: Jacob begged *"Please don't bury me in
Egypt"* (Genesis 47:29), and Joseph's coffin has just been taken up out of it (13:19). **The people who
carry a man out of Egypt rather than bury him there accuse Moses of bringing them out to die unburied.** See
[[Burial and the Grave]].

***"Why have you treated us this way"*** — ***mah-zzōʾt ʿāśîtā llānû***, literally *"What is this you have
done to us?"* **The words of Pharaoh to Abram** (*mah-zzōʾt ʿāśîtā llî*, Genesis 12:18) and of Abimelech to
Isaac (*mah-zzōʾt ʿāśîtā llānû*, 26:10, identical; WLC). See the connection above.

***"Isn't this the word that we spoke to you in Egypt"*** — **no such word is recorded.** The nearest are
the foremen's curse on Moses and Aaron after the straw was withdrawn — *"May Yahweh look at you, and
judge"* (5:21) — and the people's not listening *"for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage"* (6:9).
**Neither says *"Leave us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians"*.** The wiki records the claim as the
people's, and does not decide whether it reports something the narrator omitted or remembers 5:21 as
more than it was.

***"Better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness"*** — **the first of the
wilderness complaints, and it comes before the wilderness.** The pattern — fear, *"in Egypt"*, *"to die in
the wilderness"* — will recur at 16:3 and 17:3 and through Numbers (by reference; *not yet ingested*).

### Stand still, and see (14:13–14)

> **13** Moses said to the people, "Don't be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of Yahweh, which he
> will work for you today: for the Egyptians whom you have seen today, you shall never see them again.
> **14** Yahweh will fight for you, and you shall be still."

***"Don't be afraid"*** — ***ʾal-tîrāʾû***. **The word God said to Abram (Genesis 15:1), to Hagar (21:17), to
Isaac (26:24), and to Jacob (46:3), and Joseph to his brothers (50:19, 21)** (*ʾal-tîrāʾ*; WLC). **It is
Moses' first *"Don't be afraid"*, and the next is at Sinai (20:20).**

***"Stand still"*** — ***hityaṣṣəbû***, the hithpael of *yāṣab*, *take your stand*. **The verb of Miriam,
who *"stood far off, to see what would be done to him"*** (2:4, *wattētaṣṣab … lədēʿâ*; WLC) **— a sister
standing at the water's edge to see a rescue.** It returns at Sinai, where the people *"stood at the lower
part of the mountain"* (19:17; WLC). The command is to stand and *see*: **the people are given nothing to
do.**

***"The salvation of Yahweh"*** — ***yəšûʿat YHWH***. **The second *yəšûʿâ* in Scripture**, after Jacob's
*"I have waited for your salvation, Yahweh"* (Genesis 49:18); the third is the Song, *"he has become my
salvation"* (15:2) (WLC lemma search, Torah). **Genesis left the noun with no object; here it has one, and
a date — *"today"*.** See
[[I Have Waited for Your Salvation - The First Yeshuah in Scripture]].

***"You shall never see them again"*** — ***lōʾ tōsîpû lirʾōtām ʿôd ʿad-ʿôlām***. **The idiom of Moses' last
word to Pharaoh, *"I will see your face again no more"*** (*lōʾ-ʾōsip ʿôd rəʾôt pānêkā*, 10:29; WLC).
**And at 14:30 *"Israel saw the Egyptians dead"*.** They do see them again, once, dead. The text records
both statements; whether 14:13 means *alive* is not said.

***"Yahweh will fight for you"*** — ***YHWH yillāḥēm lākem***. **The first time in Scripture Yahweh is said
to fight** (the niphal of *lāḥam* with Yahweh as subject first appears here in the Torah; WLC lemma search).
**Deuteronomy builds on it**: *"Yahweh your God who goes before you, he will fight for you, according to all
that he did for you in Egypt before your eyes"* (Deuteronomy 1:30, WEB; also 3:22; 20:4; WLC; *not yet
ingested*). See [[War]].

***"And you shall be still"*** — ***wəʾattem taḥărîšûn***, the hiphil of *ḥāraš*, *be silent*. **The verb of
Abraham's servant at the well, *"remaining silent"* (Genesis 24:21), and of Jacob, who *"held his peace"*
when Dinah was defiled (34:5) (WLC lemma search).** **The cry of 14:10 is answered with a command to be
silent.**

### Why do you cry to me? (14:15–18)

> **15** Yahweh said to Moses, "Why do you cry to me? Speak to the children of Israel, that they go forward.
> **16** Lift up your rod, and stretch out your hand over the sea, and divide it: and the children of
> Israel shall go into the middle of the sea on dry ground.
> **17** Behold, I myself will harden the hearts of the Egyptians, and they shall go in after them: and I
> will get myself honor over Pharaoh, and over all his armies, over his chariots, and over his horsemen.
> **18** The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh, when I have gotten myself honor over Pharaoh, over his
> chariots, and over his horsemen."

***"Why do you cry to me?"*** — ***mah-tiṣʿaq ʾēlāy***, singular. **Moses' cry is not narrated**; it was the
people who *"cried out to Yahweh"* (14:10), and Moses who told them to be still. **God addresses Moses as
if the cry were his.** The text does not say whether Moses cried unrecorded or is answered for the people
he leads. See [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

***"That they go forward"*** — ***wəyissāʿû***, *let them set out*: the verb of every stage of the itinerary
(*nāsaʿ*, 12:37; 13:20; WLC). **Moses said stand still; God says march.** Both are obeyed: the people do
nothing to save themselves, and they walk.

***"Lift up your rod, and stretch out your hand"*** — ***hārēm ʾet-maṭṭəkā ûnəṭēh ʾet-yādəkā***. **The rod of
4:2–4 and 7:9–20** and the stretched hand of the plagues (*nāṭâ yād*, 8:5 English; 9:22; 10:12, 21; WLC). **The rod is
commanded and then not mentioned again**: at 14:21 and 14:27 *"Moses stretched out his hand"*.

***"Divide it"*** — ***ûbəqāʿēhû***, from ***bāqaʿ***, *split*. **In the Torah the verb occurs at Genesis 7:11,
where *"all the fountains of the great deep burst open"* (*nibqəʿû*, the niphal) to begin the flood; at
Genesis 22:3, where Abraham *"split the wood"* for Isaac's burnt offering; here and at 14:21 (*wayyibbāqəʿû
hammāyim*, niphal again); and at Numbers 16:31, where *"the ground split apart"* under Korah** (WLC lemma
search). **The waters that split to drown the world split here to make a road.** See
[[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]].

***"On dry ground"*** — ***bayyabbāšâ***. ***Yabbāšâ*, *dry land*, is the word of the third day: *"let the dry
land appear … God called the dry land 'earth'"*** (Genesis 1:9–10). In the Torah it occurs only there, at
Exodus 4:9 (the Nile water poured *"on the dry land"* that becomes blood), here, at 14:22 and 29, and in the
Song's echo at 15:19 (WLC lemma search). **The sign that turned Egypt's water to blood on dry ground, and the
sea that turns to dry ground for Israel, share the noun of creation.** See [[Creation]].

***"I myself will harden the hearts of the Egyptians"*** — ***waʾănî hinənî məḥazzēq ʾet-lēb miṣrayim***:
an emphatic *"and I — behold me —"*, the participle *hardening*, and **the singular *lēb*, *heart*, of Egypt
as a whole** (WLC). **The hardening, which has been the king's (and at 9:34; 10:1 his servants'), now extends
to the army.** See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"Over his chariots, and over his horsemen"*** — ***bərikbô ûbəpārāšāyw***. ***Pārāš*, *horseman*, first
appears in Scripture at Genesis 50:9, in Joseph's funeral cortege: *"There went up with him both chariots and
horsemen"*** (*gam-rekeb gam-pārāšîm*; WLC). **The chariots and horsemen that escorted Jacob's body out of
Egypt with honour pursue his descendants to the sea.** Whether the echo is meant cannot be shown; the words
are the same.

### The angel and the cloud (14:19–20)

> **19** The angel of God, who went before the camp of Israel, moved and went behind them; and the pillar of
> cloud moved from before them, and stood behind them.
> **20** It came between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel; and there was the cloud and the darkness,
> yet gave it light by night: and one didn't come near the other all night.

***"The angel of God, who went before the camp of Israel"*** — ***malʾak hāʾĕlōhîm hahōlēk lipnê
maḥănēh yiśrāʾēl***. **The same participle as 13:21 — *hōlēk lipnê*, *going before* — given to a third
subject.** At 13:21 Yahweh went before them in the pillar; here the angel goes before them, and **the
pillar moves behind them in a separate clause** (WLC). **The text names three things going before Israel —
Yahweh, the angel of God, the pillar — and does not say how they relate.** It is the identity problem of
the angel at its sharpest: see [[The Angel of Yahweh]], which reports the readings and adopts none. The
angel sent before Israel is promised again at 23:20, 32:34, and 33:2 (*not yet ingested*).

***"The camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel"*** — ***maḥănēh***. **The word of Jacob's *"This is God's
army"* at Mahanaim, *"two camps"*** (Genesis 32:2, 7–10, English; WLC). See [[Mahanaim]].

***"There was the cloud and the darkness, yet gave it light by night"*** — ***wayhî heʿānān wəhaḥōšek
wayyāʾer ʾet-hallāylâ***. **The Hebrew is compressed and its sense debated**: it can be read as one cloud dark
to Egypt and lighting the night for Israel, or as darkness and light in some other division. The WEB's
*"yet gave it light"* reads the first way. **The wiki notes the difficulty and records only what the
words say: cloud, darkness, and light in one night.** *Ḥōšek* is the darkness of the ninth plague (10:21–22)
and of Genesis 1:2. See [[Light and Darkness]].

***"One didn't come near the other all night"*** — ***wəlōʾ-qārab zeh ʾel-zeh***. The verb *qārab* is the
one of *"When Pharaoh came near"* (*hiqrîb*, 14:10; WLC). **Pharaoh came near; the cloud made him come no
nearer.**

### The wall on the right and on the left (14:21–22)

> **21** Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and Yahweh caused the sea to go back by a strong east
> wind all night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided.
> **22** The children of Israel went into the middle of the sea on the dry ground, and the waters were a wall
> to them on their right hand, and on their left.

***"Moses stretched out his hand … and Yahweh caused the sea to go back"*** — **two subjects in one verse.**
Moses' hand and Yahweh's wind; the text gives the gesture to one and the act to the other, as with the
plagues (10:13). See [[Moses]].

***"By a strong east wind all night"*** — ***bərûaḥ qādîm ʿazzâ kol-hallaylâ***. **The wind of Pharaoh's
dream (Genesis 41:6, 23, 27) and of the locusts (10:13), and the last *rûaḥ qādîm* in the Torah** (WLC lemma
search). See [[The East Wind - Pharaoh's Dream, the Locusts, and the Sea]]. ***"All night"*** — the locusts'
wind blew *"all that day, and all night"* (10:13). **The text gives a wind and a duration, not a mechanism**:
whether the event has a natural explanation it does not discuss.

***"Made the sea dry land"*** — ***wayyāśem ʾet-hayyām leḥārābâ***. ***Ḥārābâ*, *dry ground*, occurs in the
Torah only here and at Genesis 7:22, in the flood: *"All on the dry land, in whose nostrils was the breath of
the spirit of life, died"*** (WLC lemma search). **In the flood the dry land was where everything died; in
the sea it is where Israel lives.** And Genesis 8:1 has God make *"a wind (rûaḥ) to pass over the earth"* to
end the flood. See [[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]] and
[[The Flood as Un-Creation]].

***"The waters were a wall to them"*** — ***wəhammayim lāhem ḥômâ***. ***Ḥômâ*, *wall*, occurs here for the
first time in Scripture** (WLC lemma search, Torah: Exodus 14:22, 29; Leviticus 25:29–31; Deuteronomy 3:5;
28:52) — **elsewhere in the Torah always a city wall.** **The first wall in the Bible is made of water, and
built to protect.** **14:29 repeats the clause word for word** (WLC), framing the drowning between two
identical statements of Israel's safety.

***"On their right hand, and on their left"*** — ***mîmînām ûmiśśəmōʾlām***. The pair of Abram's *"If you go
to the left hand, then I will go to the right"* (Genesis 13:9) and of Jacob's crossed hands (48:13–14)
(WLC). **Here there is no choice of side: the water is on both.**

### The morning watch (14:23–25)

> **23** The Egyptians pursued, and went in after them into the middle of the sea: all of Pharaoh's horses,
> his chariots, and his horsemen.
> **24** In the morning watch, Yahweh looked out on the Egyptian army through the pillar of fire and of
> cloud, and confused the Egyptian army.
> **25** He took off their chariot wheels, and they drove them heavily; so that the Egyptians said, "Let's
> flee from the face of Israel, for Yahweh fights for them against the Egyptians!"

***"In the morning watch"*** — ***bəʾašmōret habbōqer***, the last watch of the night. ***ʾAšmōret* occurs only
here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). **The night of 12:42 was a *"night of watching"* (*šimmurîm*, the same
root *šāmar*); this one ends in a watch.**

***"Yahweh looked out"*** — ***wayyašqēp***, the hiphil of *šāqap*, *look down*. **The verb of the visitors
who *"looked toward Sodom"* (Genesis 18:16), of Abraham who *"looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah"* the morning
after (19:28), and of Abimelech at the window (26:8)** (WLC lemma search). **In the Torah its only other use
with God as subject is the prayer of Deuteronomy 26:15, *"Look down from your holy habitation"* (WLC; *not
yet ingested*).** **The Egyptian army is seen the way Sodom was seen — from above, at dawn.**

***"Through the pillar of fire and of cloud"*** — **one pillar, both at once.** 13:21–22 kept the cloud for day
and the fire for night; at the turn of night to morning the narrator names both.

***"Confused the Egyptian army"*** — ***wayyāhom***, from ***hāmam***, *throw into panic*. In the Torah: here;
the promise *"I will send my terror before you, and will confuse (wəhammōtî) all the people"* (23:27); and
Deuteronomy 2:15; 7:23 (WLC lemma search; *not yet ingested*). **It is the verb of holy war, and this is its
first use.**

***"He took off their chariot wheels"*** — ***wayyāsar ʾēt ʾōpan markəbōtāyw***. **The verb is *sûr* in the
hiphil, *remove*.** Some ancient versions read a different verb (*bind* or *clog*), giving *"he clogged their
wheels"* (reported, not checked in this pass). ***Markābâ*, *chariot*, is the word of Joseph's *"second
chariot"*** (Genesis 41:43; WLC). See
[[The Signet Ring and the Second Chariot - Joseph, Mordecai, and Daniel]].

***"They drove them heavily"*** — ***waynahăgēhû bikbēdut***. ***Kəbēdut*, *heaviness*, occurs nowhere else in
the Torah** (WLC lemma search). **It is the root *kbd*: the heart that was heavy (7:14) is answered by wheels
that are heavy.** See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"Let's flee from the face of Israel"*** — ***ʾānûsâ mippənê yiśrāʾēl***. **Moses fled *"from the face of
Pharaoh"* (*mippənê parʿōh*, 2:15, *bāraḥ*); now Egypt flees *from the face of Israel*** (*nûs*; WLC).

***"For Yahweh fights for them"*** — ***kî YHWH nilḥām lāhem***. **Moses' promise of 14:14 — *"Yahweh will
fight for you"* — said by the enemy, in the same verb** (WLC). **The confession is the closest the chapter
comes to *"the Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"*.** It is made too late.

### Not one of them remained (14:26–28)

> **26** Yahweh said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, that the waters may come again on the
> Egyptians, on their chariots, and on their horsemen."
> **27** Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the sea returned to its strength when the morning
> appeared; and the Egyptians fled against it. Yahweh overthrew the Egyptians in the middle of the sea.
> **28** The waters returned, and covered the chariots and the horsemen, even all Pharaoh's army that went
> in after them into the sea. There remained not so much as one of them.

***"That the waters may come again"*** — ***wəyāšūbû hammayim***, the verb of 14:2's *"turn back"* (WLC).
**Israel turned back at God's word; now the sea does.**

***"The sea returned to its strength"*** — ***ləʾêtānô***, *to its steady flow*. ***ʾÊtān* is the word of
Jacob's blessing on Joseph — *"his bow remained strong"*** (Genesis 49:24) — and of Deuteronomy 21:4's
*"valley with running water"* (WLC; *not yet ingested*); in the Torah it occurs at only those, here, and
Numbers 24:21 (WLC lemma search).

***"When the morning appeared"*** — ***lipnôt bōqer***. **The plagues' mornings** (7:15; 8:20; 9:13) **and the
morning of 12:22 (*"until the morning"*) end here.**

***"The Egyptians fled against it"*** — ***nāsîm liqrāʾtô***, *fleeing to meet it*. **They run into the water
they are fleeing.**

***"Yahweh overthrew the Egyptians"*** — ***wayənaʿēr***, the piel of ***nāʿar***, *shake off*, *shake out*.
**The verb occurs only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). **Psalm 136:15 uses it again, with Pharaoh as
object**: *"But overthrew (wəniʿēr) Pharaoh and his army in the Red Sea"* (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).

***"All Pharaoh's army that went in after them … There remained not so much as one of them"*** — ***lōʾ-nišʾar
bāhem ʿad-ʾeḥād***. **The locusts' epitaph, *"There remained not one locust"* (10:19), applied to the army**
(WLC). See [[The East Wind - Pharaoh's Dream, the Locusts, and the Sea]].

**Whether Pharaoh himself died in the sea is not said in Exodus 14.** He *"prepared his chariot"* (14:6) and
*"came near"* (14:10); then the text speaks of *"all of Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen"*
(14:23) and *"all Pharaoh's army"* (14:28) going in, and does not narrate Pharaoh entering the sea or dying.
**Psalm 136:15 says God *"overthrew Pharaoh and his army in the Red Sea"*** (WEB; *not yet ingested*), and
the Song says *"the horses of Pharaoh went in with his chariots and with his horsemen into the sea"* (15:19).
**The wiki records both and does not decide**; the question is carried on
[[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]].

### Israel saw, feared, and believed (14:29–31)

> **29** But the children of Israel walked on dry land in the middle of the sea, and the waters were a wall
> to them on their right hand, and on their left.
> **30** Thus Yahweh saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians; and Israel saw the Egyptians
> dead on the seashore.
> **31** Israel saw the great work which Yahweh did to the Egyptians, and the people feared Yahweh; and they
> believed in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses.

***"Thus Yahweh saved Israel"*** — ***wayyôšaʿ***, the hiphil of ***yāšaʿ***. **The verb's first use in Exodus
was Moses at the well: *"Moses stood up and helped (wayyôšiʿān) them"*** (2:17; WLC lemma search). **The
deliverer's first act and God's last act at the sea share a verb.** It is the root of *yəšûʿâ* (14:13): the
salvation Moses told them to *see* is now narrated. See [[Moses]].

***"Out of the hand of the Egyptians"*** — ***miyyad miṣrayim***. **The hand again**: Egypt's hand (14:30),
Moses' hand (14:21, 26, 27), and ***"the great work"*** — ***hayyād haggədōlâ***, literally ***"the great
hand"*** (14:31; WLC), **which the WEB renders *"work"***. **Egypt's hand, Moses' hand, and at the last
Yahweh's.** See [[Signs and Wonders]].

***"Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore"*** — ***mēt ʿal-śəpat hayyām***. *Śəpat hayyām*, *the
lip of the sea*, is the phrase of the promise that Abraham's seed would be *"as the sand which is on the
seashore"* (Genesis 22:17; WLC). **Israel stands on the shore of the promise and looks at Egypt's dead on
it.** See [[Life and Death]].

***"The people feared Yahweh"*** — ***wayyîrəʾû hāʿām ʾet-YHWH***. **Fourteen verses after *"they were very
afraid"* (14:10) of Egypt.** See [[The Fear of God]].

***"They believed in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses"*** — ***wayyaʾămînû bYHWH ûbəmōšeh ʿabdô***. **The
construction of Genesis 15:6 — *"He believed in Yahweh"*, *wəheʾĕmin bYHWH*** — **is used here for the first
time since.** In the Torah the hiphil of *ʾāman* with *bə-* and Yahweh's name occurs at Genesis 15:6, here,
and in the negative at Deuteronomy 1:32; at Numbers 14:11 and 20:12 God says *"believe in me"* (*bî*) (WLC). **At 4:31 *"The people believed"*
with no object; here the object is named, and it is double.** **And this is the first time Moses is called
Yahweh's *servant*** — *ʿabdô*, *his servant*. In Exodus 1–14 every earlier *ʿebed* is Pharaoh's servant, an
Israelite officer to Pharaoh (5:15–16), a bought slave (12:44), the *"house of bondage"* (13:3, 14), or Moses
calling himself *"your servant"* to God (4:10) (WLC). **The book's root, *ʿābad*, is used of Moses' relation to God at the moment
Israel stops being Egypt's.** Exodus 19:9 will give the purpose of Sinai in the same terms: *"that the people
may hear when I speak with you, and may also believe you forever"* (*not yet ingested*). See [[Faith]] and
[[Slavery and Servitude]].

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 13:17 resumes the march; 14:31 closes the prose with Israel's
  belief, and 15:1 opens the Song with *"Then Moses and the children of Israel sang"*. The two belong
  together and are kept as separate pericopes because one is narrative and the other poetry.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** three subjects going before Israel (Yahweh in the pillar, 13:21; the
  angel of God and the pillar, 14:19); *"you shall never see them again"* (14:13) against *"Israel saw the
  Egyptians dead"* (14:30); the people's *"the word that we spoke to you in Egypt"* (14:12), which is not
  recorded; *"Why do you cry to me?"* to Moses, whose cry is not narrated; whether Pharaoh died (Exodus 14
  silent; Psalm 136:15 *"overthrew Pharaoh and his army"*); the people *"armed"* for war after being routed
  away from it.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** the sense of *ḥămušîm*; the sense of 14:20's cloud, darkness, and
  light; the variant *bind* for *took off* at 14:25; any natural mechanism for the wind and the dry ground;
  the location of the sea and of the three camp names; Egypt as the land of tombs.
- **New places: [[Etham]], [[Pihahiroth]], [[Migdol]], [[Baal Zephon]].** Each is named only to fix a stage
  and none is located. Migdol may recur in Jeremiah and Ezekiel as an Egyptian frontier town; whether it is
  the same place is not decided, and a later pass will qualify the page if a second Migdol needs one.
- **No new person pages.** The army, the captains (*šālīšīm*), and the people are collective.
