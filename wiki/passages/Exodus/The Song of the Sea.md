# The Song of the Sea

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 15:1–21

## Summary

**The prose of the crossing ended with Israel believing (14:31); the next word is *"Then"*, and Israel
sings.** *"Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to Yahweh"* (15:1). It is the first time in
Scripture anyone is said to sing — the verb *šîr* does not occur in Genesis (WLC lemma search) — and the
first long poem in Exodus. The pericope has three parts:

- **The Song (15:1b–18).** Eighteen verses addressed first *about* Yahweh and then *to* him. It retells
  the sea in images the prose did not use — the army *"went down into the depths like a stone"*, the waters
  *"piled up"* by *"the blast of your nostrils"*, *"the earth swallowed them"* — and then, without a
  break, moves forward to things that have not happened yet: the nations hearing and trembling, Israel
  *"planted"* on *"the mountain of your inheritance"*, and *"Yahweh shall reign forever and ever."*
- **The prose hinge (15:19).** One verse restates the crossing in the prose's own words — the horses,
  chariots, and horsemen *"into the sea"*, the waters brought back, Israel *"on dry land in the middle of
  the sea"*.
- **Miriam (15:20–21).** *"Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron"* — the first time the name is
  given — takes a tambourine, the women go out after her dancing, and she *"answered them"* with the
  Song's first line, turned into a command: *"Sing to Yahweh."*

**The Song is the people's first answer to God in Exodus, and it is praise.** Up to here Israel has groaned
(2:23), believed and bowed (4:31; 12:27), not listened (6:9), cried out, and accused Moses (14:10–12). **No
one in the book has yet spoken *to* God in the second person with anything but a complaint or a question**
— Moses' *"Why have you brought trouble on this people?"* (5:22) is the nearest. The Song's *"Your right
hand, Yahweh"* (15:6) is the first.

### The shape of it

| | Verses | Voice | What it sings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | 15:1b–3 | *"I"* — first person singular | *"I will sing … Yah is my strength and song … This is my God"* |
| The sea, told | 15:4–5 | about Yahweh, third person | chariots *"cast"* into the sea; *"like a stone"* |
| The sea, sung to him | 15:6–10 | *"you"*, second person | *"Your right hand"*; *"the blast of your nostrils"*; the enemy's boast; *"You blew with your wind"* |
| The incomparable | 15:11–12 | *"you"* | *"Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?"*; *"The earth swallowed them"* |
| The road ahead | 15:13–17 | *"you"* | *"led … guided … to your holy habitation"*; the nations still *"as a stone"*; *"plant them in the mountain"* |
| Close | 15:18 | about Yahweh | *"Yahweh shall reign forever and ever"* |
| Prose | 15:19 | the narrator | the crossing restated |
| Miriam | 15:20–21 | *"Sing"* — imperative plural | the opening line again |

**The Song turns at 15:12–13 from what was done to what will be.** Its verbs are almost all Hebrew
perfects and imperfects that the WEB renders in the past up to 15:12 and then in a mix of past and future
(WLC). **The habitation, the mountain, and the sanctuary of 15:13 and 15:17 are not yet reached in the
story** — the people are three days from a waterless wilderness (15:22). **The Song sings the guidance
*"to your holy habitation"* as done** (*nēhaltā*, a perfect, 15:13). Whether that is the poetry of
certainty, a later voice, or both, the text does not say. See the note at 15:13.

**Three things thread the poem** (all WLC):

- **Stone, twice.** Egypt *"went down into the depths like a stone"* (*kəmô-ʾāben*, 15:5); the nations
  *"are as still as a stone"* (*kāʾāben*, 15:16). **The enemy behind sinks like a stone; the enemies ahead
  freeze like one.**
- **The right hand.** *"Your right hand, Yahweh"* twice (15:6) and *"You stretched out your right hand"*
  (15:12) — ***yāmîn***. **In the prose it was Moses who stretched out his hand (14:21, 27); in the Song
  it is Yahweh's.**
- **Wind and breath.** *"The blast (rûaḥ) of your nostrils"* (15:8) and *"You blew with your wind
  (rûaḥ)"* (15:10). **The prose's *"strong east wind"* (14:21) becomes God's own breath.** See
  [[The East Wind - Pharaoh's Dream, the Locusts, and the Sea]].

## Key verses

### Then Moses sang (15:1)

> **1** Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to Yahweh, and said, "I will sing to Yahweh,
> for he has triumphed gloriously. The horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea.

***"Then … sang"*** — ***ʾāz yāšîr***, *then* with the imperfect. **Numbers 21:17 opens the only other song
the Torah introduces this way in identical words**: *ʾāz yāšîr yiśrāʾēl ʾet-haššîrâ hazzōʾt*, *"Then Israel
sang this song"* (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). Moses' second song is introduced differently, as words
he *"spoke"* (Deuteronomy 31:30; WEB; *not yet ingested*).

***"Sang"*** — ***šîr***. **The verb's first occurrence in Scripture.** In the Torah it appears only here, at
15:21, and at Numbers 21:17 (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The noun, *šîr*, *song*, occurs once
in Genesis, in Laban's complaint**: *"I might have sent you away with mirth and with songs, with tambourine
and with harp"* (Genesis 31:27). **The first songs in Scripture are a send-off a slave-master says he would
have given; the first song actually sung is Israel's own, after a master who would not send them away.**
See [[Served, Released, Pursued - The Laban Years as Proto-Exodus]] and [[Praise and Song]].

***"Moses and the children of Israel"*** — **Moses leads; the people sing with him.** The opening is *"I
will sing"* (*ʾāšîrâ*), singular — **a crowd singing in the first person singular**, as in *"Yah is my
strength … my God … my father's God"* (15:2).

***"He has triumphed gloriously"*** — ***gāʾōh gāʾâ***, the infinitive absolute with the finite verb, *he
has surely risen high*. **The doubled form of *"God will surely visit"* (*pāqōd yipqōd*, Genesis 50:24–25;
13:19) and of Moses' *"surely made swear"* (13:19).** The verb *gāʾâ* occurs in the Torah only here and in
Miriam's repetition (15:21); its noun, ***gāʾôn***, *"excellency"*, only at 15:7 (WLC lemma search). The
root is often used later of human *pride* (reported, not checked in this pass). **In the Song the height is
Yahweh's alone.** See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]].

***"The horse and his rider"*** — ***sûs wərōkəbô***. **The same pair, *horse* and *his rider*, closes Jacob's
blessing of Dan**: *"That bites the horse's heels, so that his rider falls backward"* (*sûs … rōkəbô*,
Genesis 49:17; WLC). **In Genesis a tribe brings down a horse and rider; here Yahweh does.** The text does
not connect them. See [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]].

***"He has thrown into the sea"*** — ***rāmâ bayyām***. ***Rāmâ*, *throw*, occurs in the Torah only in this
line and its repetition at 15:21** (WLC lemma search; the morphology also files a homonym, *deceive*, under
the same number at Genesis 29:25). **In form it is identical with the *rāmâ* of *"a high hand"*
(*bəyād rāmâ*, 14:8)**, which the WLC files under a different root, *rûm*, *be high*. Israel went out *with
a high hand*; the horse and rider were *thrown* — the same sound, and different words.

### Yah is my strength and song (15:2)

> **2** Yah is my strength and song. He has become my salvation. This is my God, and I will praise him; my
> father's God, and I will exalt him.

***"Yah"*** — ***yāh***, **the short form of the name, its first occurrence in Scripture.** In the Torah it
occurs only here and at 17:16 (WLC lemma search). See [[The Names of God]].

***"My strength and song"*** — ***ʿozzî wəzimrāt yāh***. ***Zimrāt***, *song*, occurs in the Torah only here
(WLC lemma search). **The whole line is repeated twice later in Scripture, word for word in the Hebrew**:
*"Yah is my strength and song. He has become my salvation"* (Psalm 118:14), and *"for Yah, Yahweh, is my
strength and song; and he has become my salvation"* (Isaiah 12:2) (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). See
[[Yah Is My Strength and Song - Exodus 15.2 in Psalm 118 and Isaiah 12]].

***"He has become my salvation"*** — ***wayhî-lî lîšûʿâ***. **The third *yəšûʿâ* in Scripture**: Jacob waited
for it without an object (*"I have waited for your salvation, Yahweh"*, Genesis 49:18); Moses promised it with
a date (*"see the salvation of Yahweh, which he will work for you today"*, 14:13); **the Song says it has
happened, and to *me*** (WLC lemma search, Torah). See
[[I Have Waited for Your Salvation - The First Yeshuah in Scripture]].

***"This is my God, and I will praise him"*** — ***zeh ʾēlî wəʾanwēhû***. *Wəʾanwēhû* occurs only here in the
Torah (WLC lemma search). **Its sense is disputed**: *praise* or *adorn* (the WEB), or — taking it from the
root of *nāweh*, *habitation*, which appears at 15:13 — *make him a dwelling*, the reading behind some older
English versions (reported, not checked in this pass). **If the second, the Song's first promise is the one it
ends on** — the dwelling of 15:13 and 15:17.

***"My father's God"*** — ***ʾĕlōhê ʾābî***. **The title God gave himself at the bush**: *"I am the God of your
father"* (*ʾĕlōhê ʾābîkā*, 3:6; WLC), and the title Jacob used of the God who kept him from Laban (*"the God
of my father"*, Genesis 31:5, 42). **Moses will name his second son for it** (18:4; *not yet ingested*).
**The people sing it in the singular — *my father's* — as if every singer were one son of one father.**

***"I will exalt him"*** — ***waʾărōməmenhû***, the polel of ***rûm***, *be high* — **the root of the *"high
hand"* (14:8) and of *"Lift up (hārēm) your rod"* (14:16)** (WLC). **The same verb recurs in Psalm 118,
in the psalm's second-to-last verse**: *"You are my God, I will exalt you"* (*ʾărôməmekkā*, 118:28; WEB; WLC;
*not yet ingested*).

### A man of war (15:3–5)

> **3** Yahweh is a man of war. Yahweh is his name.
> **4** He has cast Pharaoh's chariots and his army into the sea. His chosen captains are sunk in the Red
> Sea.
> **5** The deeps cover them. They went down into the depths like a stone.

***"Yahweh is a man of war"*** — ***YHWH ʾîš milḥāmâ***. **The noun of Pharaoh's fear (1:10), of God's
detour (13:17), and the verb of Moses' promise (*"Yahweh will fight for you"*, 14:14) become a title.**
***Milḥāmâ*** appears in Genesis only at 14:2 and 14:8, the war of the four kings against five (WLC lemma
search). **God avoided the road where Israel would *"see war"*; now Israel names him by it.** See [[War]].

***"Yahweh is his name"*** — ***YHWH šəmô***. **The name revealed at the bush (3:15) and expounded in 6:2–8
is sung back.** The line repeats the name it explains. See [[The Names of God]] and [[I Am Yahweh]].

***"He has cast"*** — ***yārâ***, *throw*, *shoot*. **The WLC files under the same lemma the hiphil *teach*,
*show***: *"I will … teach you what you shall speak"* (4:12, 15), and four verses after the Song, *"Yahweh
showed him a tree"* (15:25; WLC lemma search). **Whether the two senses are one root or two homonyms is
debated** (reported, not checked in this pass). **The Song's first strong verb and the next pericope's
first act of God use the same letters.**

***"Pharaoh's chariots"*** — ***markəbōt parʿōh***. **The *merkābâ* is the chariot of Joseph's honour** —
*"He made him to ride in the second chariot which he had"* (Genesis 41:43) — **and of Joseph's ride to meet
his father** (46:29); in Exodus, before this, it occurs only at 14:25 (WLC lemma search). See
[[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]].

***"His chosen captains"*** — ***mibḥar šālīšāyw***, the *šālīšīm* of 14:7. ***"Are sunk"*** — ***ṭubbəʿû***,
the pual of *ṭābaʿ*, **only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). ***"In the Red Sea"*** — ***bəyam-sûp***.
See [[The Red Sea]].

***"The deeps cover them"*** — ***təhōmōt yəkasyumû***. ***Təhôm*, *the deep*, is the word of the second verse
of the Bible**: *"Darkness was on the surface of the deep"* (Genesis 1:2). In the Torah it occurs there, at
the flood (*"all the fountains of the great deep burst open"*, 7:11; 8:2), in two blessings (Genesis 49:25;
Deuteronomy 33:13), at Deuteronomy 8:7, and in the Song twice (15:5, 8) — **the Song's are the first in the
plural** (WLC lemma search). ***Kāsâ*, *cover*, is the verb of the prose: *"The waters returned, and covered
the chariots"*** (14:28; WLC). See [[Creation]] and
[[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]].

***"Into the depths"*** — ***bimṣôlōt***, **only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). ***"Like a stone"*** —
***kəmô-ʾāben***. See the note on 15:16.

### Your right hand (15:6–8)

> **6** Your right hand, Yahweh, is glorious in power. Your right hand, Yahweh, dashes the enemy in pieces.
> **7** In the greatness of your excellency, you overthrow those who rise up against you. You send out your
> wrath. It consumes them as stubble.
> **8** With the blast of your nostrils, the waters were piled up. The floods stood upright as a heap. The
> deeps were congealed in the heart of the sea.

***"Your right hand, Yahweh … Your right hand, Yahweh"*** — **the first second-person praise of God in
Exodus**, and the line repeats itself before it finishes, a pattern the Song uses again at 15:11 and 15:16.

***"Glorious in power"*** — ***neʾdārî bakkōaḥ***, from ***ʾādar***. **The niphal occurs in the Torah only here
and at 15:11 (*"glorious in holiness"*); the adjective of the same root, *ʾaddîr*, only at 15:10, *"the mighty
waters"*** (WLC lemma search). **The waters are *ʾaddîrîm*; the God who sinks Egypt in them is *neʾdār*.**

***"The enemy"*** — ***ʾôyēb***. **In Genesis the word belongs to two blessings**: Abraham's seed will
*"possess the gate of his enemies"* (22:17), and Judah's *"hand will be on the neck of your enemies"*
(49:8) (WLC lemma search). **In the Song it is singular, and it speaks** (15:9).

***"You overthrow"*** — ***tahărōs***, *tear down*. ***"Those who rise up against you"*** — ***qāmêkā***.
**The Song names Egypt's army as God's opponents, not Israel's.**

***"You send out your wrath"*** — ***təšallaḥ ḥărōnəkā***. ***Šālaḥ***, the piel of *send away*, **is the verb
of every *"Let my people go"*** (5:1; 7:16; 13:17; WLC). **Pharaoh would not send Israel out; Yahweh sends out
his wrath.** ***Ḥārôn***, *burning anger*, occurs here for the first time in Scripture; its next is Moses'
plea after the calf, *"Turn from your fierce wrath"* (32:12; WLC lemma search; *not yet ingested*). See
[[Divine Grief and Relenting]].

***"It consumes them as stubble"*** — ***kaqqaš***. ***Qaš*, *stubble*, occurs in the Torah only here and at
5:12**: *"the people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw"*
(WLC lemma search). **The stubble Egypt made Israel gather is what Egypt becomes.** See
[[Bricks Without Straw]].

***"With the blast of your nostrils"*** — ***ûbərûaḥ ʾappêkā***. ***ʾAp***, *nostril*, is the noun of
*anger* — *"the anger (ʾap) of Yahweh burned against Moses"* (4:14; WLC). **The prose's east wind (14:21) is
here the breath of God's anger.** In Genesis the word is the nostrils into which God breathed life (2:7)
and those of everything that died in the flood (7:22) (WLC lemma search). See
[[The East Wind - Pharaoh's Dream, the Locusts, and the Sea]].

***"The waters were piled up"*** — ***neʿermû mayim***. *ʿĀram*, in the niphal, **only here in the Torah**
(WLC lemma search).

***"The floods stood upright as a heap"*** — ***niṣṣəbû kəmô-nēd nōzəlîm***. ***Niṣṣəbû***, *stood*, the
niphal of *nāṣab* — **the verb of Yahweh who *"stood above"* the ladder (Genesis 28:13) and of Moses told to
*"stand by the river's bank"* to meet Pharaoh (7:15)** (WLC lemma search). ***Nōzəlîm***, *flowing* things —
**the moving water stands.** ***Nēd***, *heap*, **occurs in the Torah only here; Joshua 3:16 uses it of the
Jordan, which *"rose up in one heap"*, and Psalm 78:13 of the sea: *"He split the sea, and caused them to
pass through. He made the waters stand as a heap"*** (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).

***"The deeps were congealed"*** — ***qāpəʾû təhōmōt***. *Qāpāʾ* **only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma search).
***"In the heart of the sea"*** — ***bəleb-yām***. ***Lēb*, *heart*, is the noun of every hardening** — *"I
will harden his heart"* (4:21; WLC). **Pharaoh's heart was made firm and he went in; the heart of the sea
was made firm and he went under.** The text does not draw the pair; the noun is the same.

### The enemy said (15:9–10)

> **9** The enemy said, 'I will pursue. I will overtake. I will divide the plunder. My desire shall be
> satisfied on them. I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them.'
> **10** You blew with your wind. The sea covered them. They sank like lead in the mighty waters.

**Six verbs in the first person, and one breath answers them.** The enemy's line is the longest speech in
the Song after the singers' own — ***ʾerdōp ʾaśśîg ʾăḥallēq šālāl … ʾārîq ḥarbî tôrîšēmô yādî*** (WLC) —
and 15:10 replies in three short clauses.

***"I will pursue. I will overtake"*** — ***rādap*** and ***nāśag***, **the verbs of the prose**: *"The
Egyptians pursued them … and overtook them"* (14:9; WLC). **The Song gives Pharaoh's army the words the prose
gave only to the narrator.**

***"I will divide the plunder"*** — ***ʾăḥallēq šālāl***. **Jacob's word for Benjamin**: *"At evening he will
divide the plunder"* (*yəḥallēq šālāl*, Genesis 49:27; WLC). **Egypt has just been plundered by Israel**
(*wayənaṣṣəlû*, 12:36), and in the Song it imagines plundering them back.

***"I will draw my sword"*** — ***ʾārîq ḥarbî***, the hiphil of *rîq*, *empty out*. **The verb of Abram, who
*"led out his trained men"* to rescue Lot** (*wayyāreq*, Genesis 14:14; WLC lemma search) **and of the
brothers *"emptying their sacks"*** (42:35). Leviticus 26:33 turns it on Israel: God will *draw out the sword*
after them (WLC; paraphrased, *not yet ingested*).

***"My hand shall destroy them"*** — ***tôrîšēmô yādî***, the hiphil of ***yāraš***, *possess*, *dispossess*.
**This is the verb of the promise of the land** — *"to give you this land to inherit it (lərištāh)"*
(Genesis 15:7) — and the Song's use of it is its first hiphil in Scripture (WLC lemma search). **The enemy
boasts of dispossessing Israel with the verb God will use for Israel dispossessing Canaan** (e.g. Numbers
33:52; WLC, *not yet ingested*). See [[Land and Sojourning]].

***"You blew with your wind"*** — ***nāšaptā bərûḥăkā***. *Nāšap* **only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma
search).

***"They sank like lead"*** — ***ṣālălû kaʿôperet***. *Ṣālal*, *sink*, **only here in the Torah**;
*ʿōperet*, *lead*, only here and in Numbers 31:22's list of metals (WLC lemma search). **Stone (15:5), lead
(15:10), stone (15:16)** — the Song's images for Egypt and the nations are heavy and inert.

***"In the mighty waters"*** — ***bəmayim ʾaddîrîm***. See the note on 15:6.

### Who is like you (15:11–12)

> **11** Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods? Who is like you, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises,
> doing wonders?
> **12** You stretched out your right hand. The earth swallowed them.

***"Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?"*** — ***mî-kāmōkâ bāʾēlim YHWH***. **The question answers
12:12**: *"Against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am Yahweh"* (WLC). **The Song does not
deny the gods exist; it denies they are like him.** Moses said to Pharaoh at the frogs *"that you may know
that there is no one like Yahweh our God"* (8:10, WEB; 8:6 Hebrew), and at the hail *"there is no one like
me in all the earth"* (9:14). See [[Idolatry]].

***"Glorious in holiness"*** — ***neʾdār baqqōdeš***. ***Qōdeš*** **first appears in Exodus at the bush,
*"the place where you are standing is holy ground"*** (*ʾadmat-qōdeš*, 3:5), **and then in the festival law
(*"a holy convocation"*, 12:16)**; the Song uses it twice (15:11, 13) (WLC). See [[Holiness]].

***"Fearful in praises"*** — ***nôrāʾ təhillōt***. ***Nôrāʾ*** **is Jacob's word at Bethel**: *"How dreadful
(mah-nnôrāʾ) is this place! This is none other than God's house"* (Genesis 28:17; WLC). ***Təhillâ***,
*praise*, **occurs here for the first time in Scripture**; its other Torah uses are both in Deuteronomy
(10:21; 26:19) (WLC lemma search). **God is to be feared *in* the act of praising him.** See
[[The Fear of God]] and [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]].

***"Doing wonders"*** — ***ʿōśēh peleʾ***. ***Peleʾ***, the noun, **occurs only here in the Torah** (WLC lemma
search). **Its verb asked Abraham's question**: *"Is anything too hard (hăyippālēʾ) for Yahweh?"* (Genesis
18:14), and gave God's promise at the bush, *"I will … strike Egypt with all my wonders (nipləʾōtay)"*
(3:20) (WLC). See [[Signs and Wonders]].

***"You stretched out your right hand"*** — ***nāṭîtā yəmînəkā***. **The *nāṭâ yād* of the plagues and the
sea was always Moses' or Aaron's** (7:19; 8:5–6, 16–17 WEB numbering; 9:22–23; 10:12–13, 21–22; 14:16, 21, 26–27; WLC) — **God had
spoken of stretching out *his* hand only in promise** (*"when I stretch out my hand on Egypt"*, 7:5).
**The Song says whose hand it was.**

***"The earth swallowed them"*** — ***tibləʿēmô ʾāreṣ***. **They drowned in the sea; the Song says the earth
swallowed them.** *Bālaʿ* is the verb of Pharaoh's dream, of Aaron's rod (*"Aaron's rod swallowed up their
rods"*, 7:12), and ahead of the earth that swallows Korah (Numbers 16:32) (WLC). The text does not explain
*earth* for *sea*; readers take it as the underworld, the grave, or the shore (reported, not decided). See
[[Swallowed Up - Pharaoh's Dream, Aaron's Staff, and the Sea]].

### Led and guided (15:13)

> **13** "You, in your loving kindness, have led the people that you have redeemed. You have guided them in
> your strength to your holy habitation.

***"In your loving kindness"*** — ***bəḥasdəkā***. **The first *ḥesed* in Exodus.** Genesis uses it at eleven
verses — Lot's *"you have magnified your loving kindness"* (19:19), Abraham's servant (24:12, 14, 27, 49),
Jacob's *"I am not worthy of the least of all the loving kindnesses"* (32:10 WEB; 32:11 Hebrew), Joseph in
prison (39:21; 40:14), Jacob's request for burial (47:29) (WLC lemma search). **Its next use in Exodus is in
the commandments** (20:6), and then in the name proclaimed at Sinai (34:6–7) (WLC; *not yet ingested*). See
[[Love]].

***"Have led"*** — ***nāḥîtā***, the verb of 13:17 and 13:21 (*"God didn't lead them"*; *"to lead them on
their way"*) and of Abraham's servant, *"Yahweh has led me"* (Genesis 24:27; WLC). See [[Providence]].

***"The people that you have redeemed"*** — ***ʿam-zû gāʾāltā***. ***Gāʾal*** **has been used twice before**:
by Jacob of *"the angel who has redeemed me from all evil"* (Genesis 48:16), and by God in the promise *"I
will redeem you with an outstretched arm"* (6:6) (WLC lemma search). **The Song says the promise of 6:6 is
kept.** See [[Redemption]] and [[I Am Yahweh]].

***"You have guided them"*** — ***nēhaltā***, the piel of ***nāhal***, *lead to water*, *lead gently*. **Its two
earlier uses are both shepherds'**: Jacob to Esau, *"I will lead on gently"* (Genesis 33:14), and Joseph,
who *"fed them with bread"* through the famine (47:17) (WLC lemma search). See
[[The Reconciliation with Esau]].

***"In your strength"*** — ***bəʿozzəkā***, the *ʿōz* of *"Yah is my strength"* (15:2) — **what the singer
called *my* strength is God's own.**

***"To your holy habitation"*** — ***ʾel-nəwēh qodšekā***. ***Nāweh***, *pasture*, *abode*, **occurs only here
in the Torah** (WLC lemma search). **The habitation is not named, and in the story it has not been reached**:
the verbs are perfects (WLC), the people are on the far shore of the sea, and Sinai, the tabernacle, and the
land are all ahead. **Readings vary — Sinai, the land as a whole, or the later sanctuary in Jerusalem** —
and the Song itself does not choose (reported, not decided).

### The peoples have heard (15:14–16)

> **14** The peoples have heard. They tremble. Pangs have taken hold on the inhabitants of Philistia.
> **15** Then the chiefs of Edom were dismayed. Trembling takes hold of the mighty men of Moab. All the
> inhabitants of Canaan have melted away.
> **16** Terror and dread falls on them. By the greatness of your arm they are as still as a stone— until
> your people pass over, Yahweh, until the people you have purchased pass over.

**Four nations, named in the order of a road that runs from the coast round to the land**: Philistia,
Edom, Moab, Canaan. **The first is the road God refused at 13:17** — *"the way of the land of the
Philistines"*.

***"Philistia"*** — ***pəlāšet***. **The first time the land is named in Scripture**; Genesis and Exodus 13:17
use the people's name, *Pəlištîm* (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). See
[[The Land of the Philistines]].

***"The chiefs of Edom"*** — ***ʾallûpê ʾĕdôm***. **The title of Esau's descendants in Genesis 36**, where
*ʾallûp*, *chief*, is used over forty times, and the list ends *"These are the chiefs of Edom"* (*ʾallûpê
ʾĕdôm*, 36:43) (WLC lemma search). **Outside Genesis 36 the word appears in the Torah only here.** **The
Song's *"chiefs of Edom"* are the line the wiki has already read by name.** See [[The Descendants of Esau]]
and [[Edom]].

***"Were dismayed"*** — ***nibhălû***. **The brothers' word**: *"His brothers couldn't answer him; for they were
terrified (nibhălû) at his presence"* (Genesis 45:3; the verb's only other Torah use; WLC lemma search). See
[[Joseph Reveals Himself]].

***"The mighty men of Moab"*** — ***ʾêlê môʾāb***, literally *the rams of Moab* (WLC; the sense *leaders*
is the WEB's). See [[Moab (place)]].

***"All the inhabitants of Canaan have melted away"*** — ***nāmōgû kōl yōšbê kənāʿan***. *Mûg*, in the niphal,
**only here in the Torah. Rahab uses the same words forty years later**: *"all the inhabitants of the land
melt away before you"* (*nāmōgû kol-yōšbê hāʾāreṣ*, Joshua 2:9; again 2:24; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).
See [[Terror Has Fallen on Us - The Song of the Sea in Rahab's Mouth]] and [[Canaan]].

***"Terror and dread falls on them"*** — ***tippōl ʿălêhem ʾêmātâ wāpaḥad***. ***ʾÊmâ*** **with *nāpal*, *fall*,
is the phrase of Abram's covenant night**: *"terror (ʾêmâ) and great darkness fell (nōpelet) on him"*
(Genesis 15:12; WLC). **There terror fell on the man to whom the land was promised; here it falls on those who
hold it.** Rahab says it a third time: *"the fear (ʾêmatkem) of you has fallen (nāpəlâ) upon us"*
(Joshua 2:9; WEB; WLC). ***Paḥad*** **is the word of Jacob's name for God, *"the fear of Isaac"*** (Genesis
31:42, 53; WLC). See [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] and [[The Fear of God]].

***"By the greatness of your arm"*** — ***bigdōl zərôăkā***. **The *"outstretched arm"* of the promise**
(*bizrôaʿ nəṭûyâ*, 6:6; WLC).

***"They are as still as a stone"*** — ***yiddəmû kāʾāben***. ***Dāmam***, *be silent, still*, **occurs in the
Torah only here and at Leviticus 10:3, *"Aaron held his peace"*** (WLC lemma search; *not yet ingested*);
Joshua 10:12–13 uses it of the sun that *stood still* (WLC; by reference, *not yet ingested*). **Egypt went
down like a stone; the nations stand like one.**

***"Until your people pass over"*** — ***ʿad-yaʿăbōr ʿamməkā***, said twice. ***ʿĀbar*** **is the verb of
Yahweh's own crossing of Egypt on Passover night**: *"I will go through (wəʿābartî) the land of Egypt"*
(12:12; 12:23; WLC). **God passed through Egypt to strike it; Israel will pass through the nations
untouched.** The crossing in view is not said; Joshua 3–4 uses the verb of the Jordan (by reference, *not yet
ingested*).

***"The people you have purchased"*** — ***ʿam-zû qānîtā***. ***Qānâ*, *acquire, buy*, has three telling uses
in Genesis**: Eve's *"I have gotten a man with Yahweh's help"* (4:1); Melchizedek's *"God Most High, possessor
(qōnēh) of heaven and earth"* (14:19, 22); and Joseph in the famine, ***"I have bought you and your land today
for Pharaoh"*** (47:23) (WLC lemma search). **Pharaoh's minister bought Egypt's people for Pharaoh; Yahweh
has bought Israel for himself.** **Moses' second song uses the same verb**: *"Isn't he your father who has
bought you? He has made you and established you"* (Deuteronomy 32:6; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). See
[[Joseph and the Famine]] and [[Redemption]].

### Plant them in the mountain (15:17–18)

> **17** You shall bring them in, and plant them in the mountain of your inheritance, the place, Yahweh,
> which you have made for yourself to dwell in; the sanctuary, Lord, which your hands have established.
> **18** Yahweh shall reign forever and ever."

***"Plant them"*** — ***wətiṭṭāʿēmô***. ***Nāṭaʿ*** **is the verb of the garden**: *"Yahweh God planted a garden
eastward, in Eden"* (Genesis 2:8), then of Noah's vineyard (9:20) and Abraham's tamarisk at Beersheba (21:33)
(WLC lemma search). **God planted a garden and put the man in it; the Song says he will plant the people
themselves.** See [[The Garden of Eden]] and [[Creation]].

***"The mountain of your inheritance"*** — ***bəhar naḥălātəkā***. ***Naḥălâ*** **first appears in Rachel and Leah's
complaint**, *"Is there yet any portion or inheritance for us in our father's house?"* (Genesis 31:14), and
once more in Genesis, in Jacob's ruling on Joseph's later sons (48:6) (WLC lemma search).
**The mountain is not named.** See the note on 15:13.

***"The place … to dwell in"*** — ***mākôn ləšibtəkā***. *Mākôn*, *fixed place*, **only here in the Torah**
(WLC lemma search).

***"The sanctuary, Lord"*** — ***miqdāš ʾădōnāy***. ***Miqdāš*** **occurs here for the first time in
Scripture**; its next is the command *"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them"* (25:8; WLC
lemma search; *not yet ingested*). **The Song names the sanctuary ten chapters before the instruction to build
one.** See [[Holiness]].

***"Which your hands have established"*** — ***kônənû yādêkā***, the polel of ***kûn***. **Deuteronomy 32:6 uses
the same form of the people**: *"He has made you and established you (wayəkōnənekā)"* (WEB; WLC; *not yet
ingested*). **In the one song God establishes a sanctuary; in the other, a people.**

***"Yahweh shall reign forever and ever"*** — ***YHWH yimlōk ləʿōlām wāʿed***. ***Mālak***, *reign*, **with
Yahweh as subject for the first time in Scripture.** **Its Genesis uses are human kings and a boy's
ambition**: *"These are the kings who reigned in the land of Edom, before any king reigned over the children
of Israel"* (Genesis 36:31), and Joseph's brothers, *"Will you indeed reign over us?"* (37:8) (WLC lemma
search). **The Song ends on Edom's verb with Yahweh as its subject, one verse after naming Edom's chiefs.**
**Pharaoh, *"the king of Egypt"* (14:5, 8), is not called king anywhere in the Song.** See
[[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Kings Will Come Out of You]].

### For the horses of Pharaoh (15:19)

> **19** For the horses of Pharaoh went in with his chariots and with his horsemen into the sea, and
> Yahweh brought back the waters of the sea on them; but the children of Israel walked on dry land in the
> middle of the sea.

**A prose verse inside the poetry, and it says nothing new.** *"Horses … chariots … horsemen"* is the list
of 14:9 and 14:23; *"brought back the waters"* (*wayyāšeb*) is 14:26–28; *"walked on dry land in the middle
of the sea"* (*bayyabbāšâ bətôk hayyām*) is 14:29 almost word for word (WLC). ***Yabbāšâ*** **is the dry land
of the third day** (Genesis 1:9–10). See
[[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]].

**It opens with *kî*, *for*, and so can be read as the close of Moses' song (its reason) or the opening of
Miriam's (its occasion).** The WEB closes the quotation marks at 15:18 and leaves 15:19 as narration. The
text does not decide; the wiki keeps the verse as the hinge between the two.

### Miriam the prophetess (15:20–21)

> **20** Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine in her hand; and all the women went
> out after her with tambourines and with dances.
> **21** Miriam answered them, "Sing to Yahweh, for he has triumphed gloriously. The horse and his rider he
> has thrown into the sea."

***"Miriam"*** — ***miryām***. **The name's first occurrence in Scripture.** The sister who *"stood far off"*
at the Nile (2:4) was unnamed; the identification of the two is an inference the wiki records on
[[Miriam]], supported by Numbers 26:59 — *"She bore to Amram Aaron and Moses, and Miriam their sister"* (WEB;
*not yet ingested*). **She is introduced by her calling and by one brother, not by both.**

***"The prophetess"*** — ***hannəbîʾâ***. **The first woman in Scripture called a prophet** — the feminine of
*nābîʾ* occurs nowhere else in the Torah (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Joshua). **The masculine has been used
twice**: of Abraham (*"he is a prophet, and he will pray for you"*, Genesis 20:7) and of Aaron (*"Aaron your
brother shall be your prophet"*, 7:1). **What makes her a prophet here is not said**; what she does is lead
the song. Micah later names her with her brothers among those God sent: *"I sent before you Moses, Aaron, and
Miriam"* (Micah 6:4; WEB; *not yet ingested*). See [[Prophecy and Vision]] and
[[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].

***"The sister of Aaron"*** — ***ʾăḥôt ʾahărōn***. **Not *"the sister of Moses"*.** Numbers 26:59 makes her
sister to both. **Why Exodus names only Aaron is not said**; readers suggest Aaron as the elder brother, or a
pairing of Miriam and Aaron that Numbers 12 will make against Moses (reported, not decided). See
[[Aaron]].

***"Took a tambourine in her hand"*** — ***wattiqqaḥ … ʾet-hattōp bəyādāh***. ***Tōp*** **occurs in the Torah
only here and in Laban's *"with tambourine and with harp"*** (Genesis 31:27; WLC lemma search). **The
send-off Laban said he would have given, Israel's women give themselves.** See
[[Served, Released, Pursued - The Laban Years as Proto-Exodus]].

***"All the women went out after her"*** — ***wattēṣeʾnā kol-hannāšîm ʾaḥărêhā***. ***Yāṣāʾ*, *go out*, the
book's verb of the exodus itself** (12:41; 13:3; 14:8; WLC). **The women's going out after Miriam is a second
exodus in miniature, led by a woman.**

***"With dances"*** — ***ûbimḥōlōt***. **The noun occurs in the Torah only here and at 32:19**, where Moses
*"saw the calf and the dancing"* (WLC lemma search; *not yet ingested*). **The dance of deliverance and the
dance of the calf share the word.**

***"Miriam answered them"*** — ***wattaʿan lāhem miryām***. ***ʿĀnâ***, *answer*, and **the suffix *-hem*,
*them*, is masculine plural** (WLC morphology). **It may mean she answered the men — Moses and *"the
children of Israel"* of 15:1 — in antiphon; or the masculine may cover the women as a group**, as Hebrew
often uses it (reported, not decided). **Numbers 21:17 uses the same verb for the song at the well**: *"Sing
to it"* (*ʿĕnû-lāh*, WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).

***"Sing to Yahweh"*** — ***šîrû laYHWH***. **Moses' *"I will sing"* (ʾāšîrâ) becomes Miriam's *"Sing"***
(šîrû), plural imperative (WLC); the rest of the line is 15:1 unchanged. **Whether Miriam's two lines are her
whole song or the refrain of a longer one, the text does not say.** **Psalm 106:12 summarises the sequence of
14:31 and 15:1**: *"Then they believed his words. They sang his praise"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*).

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 15:1 opens with *"Then"* after the prose's close at 14:31, and
  15:22 resumes the itinerary (*"Moses led Israel onward from the Red Sea"*). 15:19 is kept inside the
  pericope as the hinge to Miriam's song.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** *"the earth swallowed them"* (15:12) for a drowning; the habitation and
  sanctuary sung as reached (perfects, 15:13) before the story reaches them; the unnamed mountain of 15:17;
  15:19 as close of one song or opening of the other; Miriam *"the sister of Aaron"* and not of Moses; the
  masculine *"them"* she answers (15:21); whether 15:21 is her whole song; and whether Pharaoh died — the Song,
  like the prose, says only that his army went down.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** *wəʾanwēhû* as *praise* or *make a dwelling* (15:2); *yārâ* as one root
  or two (15:4, 25); *gāʾâ* and later *pride*; *ʾêlê môʾāb* as *rams* or *leaders*; the identity of the
  habitation and mountain.
- **New pages:** the themes [[Praise and Song]] and [[Redemption]], neither of which existed though the wiki
  had met *gāʾal* twice (Genesis 48:16; Exodus 6:6); the connections
  [[Yah Is My Strength and Song - Exodus 15.2 in Psalm 118 and Isaiah 12]],
  [[Terror Has Fallen on Us - The Song of the Sea in Rahab's Mouth]], and
  [[The Song of Moses and the Lamb - Exodus 15 in Revelation 15]]. **No new person or place pages**: Philistia
  is filed on [[The Land of the Philistines]], and the habitation/mountain is not located.
