# The Heads of the Fathers' Houses

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 6:14–27

## Summary

**The story stops for a genealogy, and the genealogy is there to answer one question: who are these
two men?** At 6:12 [[Moses]] told God that Pharaoh would not listen to a man *"of uncircumcised
lips"*, and at 6:13 God, without answering, commanded *"Moses and … Aaron"* together. Before the
narrative goes on, it stops to say who they are. **It starts at the top of Jacob's family, as if it
meant to list all twelve tribes**: [[Reuben]], *"the firstborn of Israel"*, with four sons (6:14), and
[[Simeon]] with six (6:15). Both lists are copied from the register of the seventy in
[[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] (Genesis 46:9–10). **Then it reaches the third son, [[Levi]], and
goes no further.** Levi gets ten verses where his elder brothers got one each (6:16–25): his three sons
[[Gershon]], [[Kohath]], and [[Merari]], with their sons; Kohath's line followed through [[Amram]] to
[[Aaron]] and Moses; Aaron's marriage and four sons; and one grandson, [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]].
**Then it names its subject twice, once in each order**: *"These are that Aaron and Moses"* (6:26),
and *"These are that Moses and Aaron"* (6:27).

The list is short and every addition to it is deliberate. **Only three men in it have their lifespans
given**: Levi, 137 years (6:16); Kohath, 133 (6:18); and Amram, 137 (6:20) — the three generations
from Jacob's son to Moses' father. **Only three women are named, and all three are wives in the
priestly line**: Jochebed, Amram's aunt (6:20); [[Elisheba]], daughter of Amminadab and sister of
[[Nahshon]] (6:23); and an unnamed *"one of the daughters of Putiel"* (6:25). **Moses' own wife and
sons are not in it**, and neither is [[Miriam]]. Aaron's line is followed for two generations past him,
Moses' line not at all.

### The shape of it

| | verses | who | what is given |

| 2 | 6:14b | **Reuben**, *"the firstborn of Israel"* | four sons; *"these are the families of Reuben"* |
| 3 | 6:15 | **Simeon** | six sons, the last *"the son of a Canaanite woman"* |
| 4 | 6:16 | **Levi** | three sons *"according to their generations"*; **137 years** |
| 5 | 6:17–19 | Gershon, Kohath, Merari | their sons; **Kohath 133 years** |
| 6 | 6:20 | **Amram** and **Jochebed** | Aaron and Moses; **Amram 137 years** |
| 7 | 6:21–22 | Izhar, Uzziel | their sons — **Korah**; Mishael and Elzaphan |
| 8 | 6:23 | **Aaron** and **Elisheba** | Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, Ithamar |
| 9 | 6:24 | **Korah** | his sons; *"the families of the Korahites"* |
| 10 | 6:25 | **Eleazar** and a daughter of Putiel | **Phinehas**; *"These are the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites"* |
| 11 | 6:26–27 | *"that Aaron and Moses … that Moses and Aaron"* | the point of the list |

**The heading comes back at the end of Levi's section.** *"The heads of their fathers' houses"* at
6:14 returns as *"the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites"* at 6:25. In Genesis, Exodus, and
Job the phrase *rāʾšê ʾābôt* occurs only at these two verses (WLC lemma search). **So the frame was
built for Israel and closes on Levi**: Reuben and Simeon are the way in, and the list was never going
to reach Judah.

## Key verses

### Reuben and Simeon, copied (6:14–15)

> **14** These are the heads of their fathers' houses. The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel:
> Hanoch, and Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi; these are the families of Reuben.
> **15** The sons of Simeon: Jemuel, and Jamin, and Ohad, and Jachin, and Zohar, and Shaul the son of
> a Canaanite woman; these are the families of Simeon.

***"The heads of their fathers' houses"*** — ***rāʾšê bêt-ʾăbōtām***. *Bêt ʾāb*, *a father's house*,
is the extended family under its living head, smaller than the tribe and larger than a household.
**A phrase for a family becomes an administrative term**: these are the heads by whom Israel will
be counted. Numbers runs its census and
its camp on the same unit.

***"Reuben the firstborn of Israel"*** — ***bəkōr yiśrāʾēl***. **Genesis 46:8 has *bəkōr yaʿăqōb*,
*"Reuben, Jacob's firstborn"*. Exodus changes the father's name to *Israel*.** It is the name of the
man and of the nation at once. Two chapters earlier, God told Moses to say to Pharaoh, *"Israel is my
son, my firstborn"* (4:22, *bənî bəkōrî yiśrāʾēl*). The genealogy uses the same two words for Reuben
— the firstborn of the man Israel, who is a son inside the people God calls his firstborn. Reuben
lost the firstborn's place at Jacob's deathbed (Genesis 49:3–4), and the list does not mention it; he
leads only because the order is the birth order. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].

**The four sons of Reuben and the six of Simeon are the names Genesis 46:9–10 gives, in the same
order, and in the Hebrew nearly letter for letter** (both checked in the WLC). The one difference in
wording is the ending: each brother here closes with *"these are the families of …"*, ***mišpəḥōt***,
which Genesis 46 does not have. **A list of the people who went down is reused as a list of clans.**
Reuben's Hezron has the same name as Perez's Hezron in Judah's line (Genesis 46:12); the wiki gives
the second of them a page, [[Hezron (son of Perez)]], and Reuben's none.

***"Shaul the son of a Canaanite woman"*** — ***wəšāʾûl ben-hakkənaʿănît***, the same words as Genesis
46:10. **The one ethnic note in the register of the seventy is copied with the list.** See
[[Shaul (son of Simeon)]].

### Levi, by generations (6:16–19)

> **16** These are the names of the sons of Levi according to their generations: Gershon, and Kohath,
> and Merari; and the years of the life of Levi were one hundred thirty-seven years.
> **17** The sons of Gershon: Libni and Shimei, according to their families.
> **18** The sons of Kohath: Amram, and Izhar, and Hebron, and Uzziel; and the years of the life of
> Kohath were one hundred thirty-three years.
> **19** The sons of Merari: Mahli and Mushi. These are the families of the Levites according to their
> generations.

***"According to their generations"*** — ***lətōlədōtām***. **This is Genesis's structuring word,
*tôlədôt*, back for the first time since Genesis 37:2** (*"This is the history of the generations of
Jacob"*). Exodus has it only three times — here, at 6:19, and at 28:10, where the names of the tribes
go on the high priest's shoulder stones *"in the order of their birth"*. (Lemma search of the WLC of
Genesis, Exodus, and Job.) **Genesis was built from ten *tôlədôt* headings; Exodus uses the word once,
for Levi, at both ends of his list.** The book of the nation picks up the book of the family's
genealogical form at the point where it introduces the priesthood. See
[[The Other Line First - The Order of the Toledot in Genesis]].

***"And the years of the life of Levi were one hundred thirty-seven years"*** — the lifespan formula
of Genesis, ***ûšənê ḥayyê lēwî***, used of Sarah (23:1), Abraham (25:7), Ishmael (25:17), and Jacob
(47:28). In Exodus it occurs only in this list, three times (6:16, 18, 20; WLC search). **Of
the twelve sons of Jacob only Joseph had his age at death given in Genesis (110, 50:26), and now
Levi.** The three lifespans in this list belong to the men between the descent and the exodus:
Levi went down to Egypt, Kohath went down with him (Genesis 46:11), Amram was born there, and his
sons led Israel out.

**The names of Levi's grandsons are the names of later Levite clans.** The same eight — Libni and
Shimei; Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel; Mahli and Mushi — stand in the same order at Numbers
3:18–20, where each group is *"according to their families"*, and Numbers 26:58 lists *"the family of
the Libnites, the family of the Hebronites, the family of the Mahlites, the family of the Mushites,
the family of the Korahites"* (Hebrew checked in the WLC; the English here is the wiki's own rendering,
because Numbers is not yet in `raw/`). Kohath's son ***Ḥebrôn*** bears the same name as the city
[[Hebron]], where the patriarchs are buried; the text makes nothing of it. See
[[The Sons of Levi - Genesis 46, Exodus 6, and Numbers 3 and 26]].

### Amram and Jochebed (6:20)

> **20** Amram took Jochebed his father's sister to himself as wife; and she bore him Aaron and Moses:
> and the years of the life of Amram were a hundred and thirty-seven years.

**This is where the parents of the birth story get their names.** At 2:1 they were *"a man of the
house of Levi"* and *"a daughter of Levi"*. Here they are [[Amram]] and [[Jochebed]], and the verse is
built on the marriage formula of Genesis, ***wayyiqqaḥ … lô ləʾiššâ***, *"took … to himself as
wife"*, the formula of Isaac's marriage (25:20) and Esau's (28:9). Exodus uses it three times, all in
this list (6:20, 23, 25; WLC search of Genesis and Exodus).

***"His father's sister"*** — ***dōdātô***, *his aunt*. **The Hebrew word is narrower than *aunt*
only by usage.** *Dōdâ* occurs nowhere else in Genesis, Exodus, and Job. In Leviticus its two uses
(18:14; 20:20) name the wife of one's father's brother, not one's father's sister; the father's sister
is ***ʾăḥôt-ʾābîkā*** (18:12; 20:19). (All four checked in the WLC.) WEB's *"his father's sister"*
reads *dōdâ* in light of Numbers 26:59, which calls Jochebed *"the daughter of Levi, who was born to
Levi in Egypt"* — Levi's daughter, and so Kohath's sister. **Either way, Leviticus forbids the
marriage**: the father's sister at 18:12, the uncle's wife at 18:14. **The parents of Moses and Aaron
married within a degree the law Moses brings will prohibit, and Exodus records it without
comment.** See [[Two Sisters - The Marriage Genesis Records and Leviticus Forbids]] and
[[Marriage]].

***"Aaron and Moses"*** — **the elder first.** Aaron is three years older than Moses: *"Moses was eighty years old, and Aaron eighty-three
years old, when they spoke to Pharaoh"* (7:7). This is
the first place Exodus names them in birth order. The verse names two sons, not three. Numbers 26:59
adds *"and Miriam their sister"*; Exodus 6 does not.

**Amram's 137 years are Levi's 137 exactly.** The text does not remark on it. Numbers of years in this
list are part of the argument over how long Israel was in Egypt: four generations, three lifespans
of 133–137 years, and an exodus *"at the end of four hundred thirty years"* (12:41). See
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

### Kohath's other sons, and Korah (6:21–22, 24)

> **21** The sons of Izhar: Korah, and Nepheg, and Zichri.
> **22** The sons of Uzziel: Mishael, and Elzaphan, and Sithri.

> **24** The sons of Korah: Assir, and Elkanah, and Abiasaph; these are the families of the
> Korahites.

**Two of Amram's three brothers get their sons listed, and Hebron's are passed over.** The two lines
that are given both matter later. ***Qōraḥ***, [[Korah (son of Izhar)]], is Moses' and Aaron's first
cousin; in Numbers 16 he leads a revolt of Levites against Aaron's priesthood and is swallowed by the
ground. *Mishael* and *Elzaphan* are the cousins Moses sends to carry the bodies of Nadab and Abihu
out of the camp (Leviticus 10:4; paraphrased, not yet ingested). **The genealogy names, in the same
breath as the priests, the cousin who will challenge them and the cousins who will bury two of
them.**

**Korah alone gets a second generation here.** Abiasaph and Elkanah are listed as *"the families of
the Korahites"*, ***mišpəḥōt haqqorḥî*** — **an unusual courtesy to a man whose revolt is still
ahead.** Numbers explains it after the fact: *"the sons of Korah did not die"* (Numbers 26:11,
*ûbənê-qōraḥ lōʾ-mētû*, WLC; the wiki's rendering). The *sons of Korah* are later the guild named in
the headings of eleven psalms (Psalms 42; 44–49; 84–85; 87–88 — cited from memory, not checked).
**This Korah is not the Korah of Genesis 36:5**, Esau's son by [[Oholibamah]]; the wiki qualifies his
page for that reason.

### Aaron and Elisheba (6:23)

> **23** Aaron took Elisheba, the daughter of Amminadab, the sister of Nahshon, as his wife; and she
> bore him Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.

**The fullest pedigree any woman in the list receives.** Elisheba is given her father and her brother.
Her brother [[Nahshon]] is the head of the tribe of Judah in the wilderness, the first to bring an
offering at the altar's dedication (Numbers 1:7; 2:3; 7:12 — WLC lemma search; the content is
paraphrased), and both he and Amminadab are in the line from Perez to David (Ruth 4:19–20 — cited by
reference, not yet ingested). **The first priestly house marries into the house that will be royal.** The text names her family and does not say why. See
[[Elisheba Daughter of Amminadab - Aaron's Marriage into Judah]] and
[[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]].

***ʾĔlîšebaʿ*** is usually read *my God is an oath*, or *my God is abundance*; the second element is
uncertain. **The Greek form of the name is *Elisabet*, the name of the priest Zechariah's wife in Luke
1:5, who is said there to be of the daughters of Aaron** (paraphrased; the equivalence of the two
names is the standard one, from memory, and was not checked against the Septuagint).

***"Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar"*** — **two pairs, and the first pair does not survive.**
[[Nadab (son of Aaron)]] and [[Abihu]] go up the mountain with Moses and the elders (24:1, 9) and die
for offering *"strange fire"* before Yahweh (Numbers 26:61, *ʾēš zārâ*, WLC; told in Leviticus 10).
[[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]] succeeds Aaron as high priest. [[Ithamar]] oversees the Levites' work at
the tabernacle. **The list names four sons; the priesthood descends through the second pair.** The
first pair has no sons (Numbers 3:4, from memory; not checked).

### Eleazar and Phinehas (6:25)

> **25** Eleazar Aaron's son took one of the daughters of Putiel as his wife; and she bore him
> Phinehas. These are the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites according to their families.

**The list goes to a fourth generation below Kohath only in this one line.** ***Pûṭîʾēl*** occurs only
here (WLC lemma search of Genesis, Exodus, and Job), and his daughter is not named. ***Pînəḥās*** is
commonly derived from Egyptian *pꜣ-nḥsy*, *the Nubian*; **the wiki records that etymology as the usual
one, not as certain**, beside *Moses*, the other Egyptian-sounding name in this family. See
[[Drawn Out of the Water - The Name of Moses]].

**Phinehas is the reason the list stops where it does.** He is the priest whose zeal at Peor wins a
*"covenant of peace"* and *"an everlasting priesthood"* for his descendants (Numbers 25:6–13,
paraphrased; not yet ingested). The genealogy ends one generation after Aaron's sons, with the heir
in whom the priestly line is confirmed. See [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]] and
[[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]].

### These are that Aaron and Moses (6:26–27)

> **26** These are that Aaron and Moses, to whom Yahweh said, "Bring out the children of Israel from
> the land of Egypt according to their armies."
> **27** These are those who spoke to Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring out the children of Israel from
> Egypt. These are that Moses and Aaron.

**The two verses form a chiasm around the commission.**

```
hûʾ ʾahărōn ûmōšeh          These are that Aaron and Moses        (6:26a)
   to whom Yahweh said, "Bring out …"                              (6:26b)
   who spoke to Pharaoh … to bring out                             (6:27a)
hûʾ mōšeh wəʾahărōn          These are that Moses and Aaron        (6:27b)
```

***Hûʾ***, *he*, singular, both times — literally *"he is Aaron and Moses"*, *"he is Moses and Aaron"*;
WEB's *"These are that"* keeps the oddity. **The genealogy's order, elder first, gives way to the
story's order, the one God called first.** In the genealogy Aaron leads; in the commission, to God and
to Pharaoh, Moses does. **The text uses both orders and does not rank them.**

***"According to their armies"*** — ***ʿal-ṣibʾōtām***. **It is the first time Israel is called
*ṣəbāʾôt*, *hosts*, *armies*.** In Genesis the word is used for the *"host"* of heaven and earth (2:1)
and for Phicol's army (21:22, 32; 26:26); in Job, for the *"warfare"* of human life (7:1; 14:14). In
Exodus it is used of Israel at 6:26; 7:4 (*"my armies, my people"*); 12:17, 41, 51 (lemma search of
the WLC of Genesis, Exodus, and Job). **The slaves of chapter 1 are listed as a muster.** The list of
heads of fathers' houses is the beginning of that muster. See [[War]].

***"These are those who spoke to Pharaoh"*** — ***hēm hamədabbərîm***, a participle. **The verse
describes as done what has hardly begun.** One audience has happened (5:1–5) and failed. The narrator
already calls them *the ones who speak to Pharaoh*.

### Why the list is here

**The genealogy answers Moses' objection without mentioning it.** Moses said Pharaoh would not
listen to him (6:12), and the list does not say he is eloquent, or that Pharaoh will listen. It
places him and his brother inside Israel: great-grandsons of Levi, grandsons of Kohath, sons of
Amram. God told Abram the return would come *"in the fourth generation"* (Genesis 15:16), and Levi,
Kohath, Amram, Moses are four names. **The text does not draw that connection, and the four can be
counted from more than one starting point**; the wiki notes the fit without building on it. It then resumes with the objection unchanged (6:28–30), so the
list answers *who* is being sent, not *whether* he can speak.

**It also does something the birth story could not.** At 2:1–10 Moses was the unnamed son of unnamed
parents, adopted into Pharaoh's house and given an Egyptian name. At 2:14 a Hebrew asked him *"Who made
you a prince and a judge over us?"*, and in Midian he was taken for *"an Egyptian"* (2:19). **The
genealogy is the answer to those questions: he is a Levite, in a named line, of a tribe with a
place in Israel's order.** See [[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].

**And it is a priestly list more than a Mosaic one.** Moses' own family stops at Moses. Aaron's
wife, sons, and grandson are named, as are the cousins who matter for the priesthood's future: Korah
who will contest it, Mishael and Elzaphan who will serve it. Moses' sons, [[Gershom]] and Eliezer,
are counted among the Levites only in 1 Chronicles 23:14–15 (cited from memory; not checked), and
never as priests. See [[Priesthood]].

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** 6:14 begins a new heading after 6:13's summary command, and 6:26–27
  close it by naming the two men; 6:28 (*"On the day when Yahweh spoke to Moses"*) resumes the
  narrative with a new temporal clause.
- **The *dōdâ* point is a nuance, not a contradiction, of what the wiki already says.** [[Amram]],
  [[Jochebed]], and [[Two Sisters - The Marriage Genesis Records and Leviticus Forbids]] say Amram
  married his *"father's sister"*, which Leviticus 18:12 forbids. That is the WEB's rendering, and it
  agrees with Numbers 26:59. The Hebrew of 6:20 says *his aunt*, and the word's two Leviticus uses
  mean an uncle's wife. The three pages now say both; the prohibition holds on either reading.
- **Cited from memory and not checked**: the eleven Korahite psalm headings; Numbers 3:4 (Nadab and
  Abihu had no sons); 1 Chronicles 23:14–15 (Moses' sons among the Levites); 1 Chronicles 6; the
  *Elisabet* form of Elisheba in the Septuagint and Luke. Each is hedged where it is used. No verse
  outside Genesis, Exodus, and Job is quoted in English on this page; the few short renderings of
  Numbers are the wiki's own, from the checked Hebrew, and are marked so.
- **Hebrew forms on this page were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Open Scriptures
  Hebrew Bible, `morphhb`): all of Exodus 6:14–27; Genesis 46:8–11; Leviticus 18:12, 14; 20:19–20;
  Numbers 3:17–20; 26:11, 57–61. Word counts and first occurrences (*rāʾšê ʾābôt*, *tôlədôt*, *dōdâ*,
  *ṣābāʾ*, *Pûṭîʾēl*, *Naḥšôn*) come from lemma searches of the WLC of Genesis, Exodus, and Job, with
  Leviticus searched for *dōdâ* and Numbers for *Naḥšôn*.
