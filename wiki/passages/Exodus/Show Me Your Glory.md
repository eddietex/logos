# Show Me Your Glory

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 33:1–23

## Summary

Yahweh orders Moses to lead Israel toward the land sworn to the fathers and promises an angel
and the removal of its inhabitants, but refuses to go among the people lest he consume them.
Israel mourns and strips off its jewelry (33:1–6). A notice about Moses' customary tent outside
the camp describes the descending cloud, the people's worship, direct speech with Yahweh, and
Joshua's continuing attendance (33:7–11).

Moses appeals to the favor God has shown him and insists that this nation is God's people. He
asks to know God's way, refuses departure without God's presence, and receives an affirmative
answer (33:12–17). Then he asks to see God's glory. God promises goodness, proclamation of his
name, and freely given mercy, while denying sight of his face. Moses will be sheltered in a
rock's cleft and allowed to see God's back after he passes (33:18–23). The chapter ends with
these instructions and promises, before the narrated passing in 34:5–7.

## Notes

### The dispute is about presence, not merely safe arrival

The oath, the angel, and the land survive the opening announcement (33:1–3). What is withheld
is Yahweh going among the people. Moses answers by calling them God's people (33:13), then
insisting that his own favor and the nation's distinctiveness depend on God going with them
(33:16). The singular conversation with Moses must therefore be read alongside his repeated
inclusion of the nation. Verse 17 grants the request; it is not another refusal.

### The tent outside the camp

Verses 7–11 describe customary action: Moses used to pitch the tent far outside the camp,
seekers went out to it, and the people watched and worshiped from their own tent doors. The
text does not narrate the construction prescribed in chapters 25–31. Joshua remains there,
while Moses returns to the camp. In the final narrative sequence this tent supplies a place
of encounter before the tabernacle is built. Calling it a provisional tent is a reading of
that sequence, not a label Exodus supplies. Its historical relationship to the prescribed
sanctuary is not settled; see [[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]].

### Face-to-face speech, but no sight of the face

The wording of 33:11 and 33:20 creates an explicit tension. Verse 11 compares the speech with
a friend's conversation; verse 20 denies sight of the face. Distinguishing direct conversation
from unrestricted vision makes sense of that comparison, but should not erase either sentence
or the report that the leaders saw God in [[The Blood of the Covenant]] (24:10–11). The wiki
records the alternatives in [[They Saw God - Exodus 24, Exodus 33, and John 1.18]].

### Goodness, mercy, and a protected glimpse

The request for glory receives an answer about goodness, the name, and freely given mercy
(33:18–19), followed by protection and a limit (33:20–23). The rock is not identified as the
rock struck in [[Water from the Rock]]; neither a precise location nor a direct prediction
about Christ is supplied here. The passage promises the encounter; 34:5–7 narrates the passing.
Paul later cites the speech about mercy in Romans 9:15, alongside the Pharaoh text in 9:17.
That later use is examined in the existing Romans connection rather than used to erase the
immediate setting of intercession for Israel.

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

The passing and proclamation promised in 33:19–23 are narrated in [[The Covenant Renewed]] (34:5–7). Moses
responds with worship and a renewed plea for presence, pardon, and belonging (34:8–9). The
description centers on divine character without depicting the face that 33:20 withholds.

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

Exodus 34:29–35 describes the effect of divine conversation on Moses' face and his repeated
entry before Yahweh. Israel sees Moses shining; that does not mean he saw God's face. The
setting of the later entries is not named, though the tent in 33:7–11 supplies nearby context.
