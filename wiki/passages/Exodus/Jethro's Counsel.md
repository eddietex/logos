# Jethro's Counsel

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 18:1–27

## Summary

**The last chapter before Sinai belongs to a foreign priest, and nobody murmurs in it.** *"Jethro, the priest
of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard of all that God had done for Moses, and for Israel his people"* (18:1).
He brings back Zipporah and Moses' two sons — the second named here for the first time, ***Eliezer***, *"My
father's God was my help and delivered me from Pharaoh's sword"* (18:4) — to the camp *"at the Mountain of
God"*. Moses goes out, bows, kisses him, and tells him everything; **Jethro rejoices, blesses Yahweh, confesses
*"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"*** (18:11), and **offers the first burnt offering actually
made in Exodus**, at which Aaron and all the elders eat bread with him *"before God"* (18:12).

**Then, the next day, he watches Moses judge.** The people stand around Moses *"from the morning to the
evening"*; Jethro asks why he sits *"alone"*; Moses says the people come *"to inquire of God"*; and Jethro
answers, ***"The thing that you do is not good … the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to perform it
yourself alone"*** (18:17–18). He gives a plan — Moses to stand before God for the people and teach them the
statutes, and *"able men which fear God: men of truth, hating unjust gain"* to judge the small cases in ranks
of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens — on one condition, ***"and God commands you so"*** (18:23). **Moses
listens to his father-in-law's voice and does all of it**, and Jethro goes home (18:24–27).

The pericope moves in two halves, each a day:

- **The visit (18:1–12).** The hearing (18:1); the family returned, and Eliezer's name (18:2–6); the meeting
  and the telling (18:7–8); Jethro's joy, blessing, and confession (18:9–11); the burnt offering and the meal
  before God (18:12).
- **The counsel (18:13–27).** Moses judging from morning to evening (18:13); Jethro's question and Moses'
  answer (18:14–16); *"not good"*, *"too heavy"*, *"alone"* (18:17–18); the plan (18:19–23); Moses' obedience
  and the judges installed (18:24–26); Jethro's departure (18:27).

**Jethro is named seven times in the first half and never in the second** (18:1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12; WLC lemma
search, Exodus). From 18:13 he is only ***"Moses' father-in-law"*** — *ḥōtēn*, which occurs thirteen times in the
chapter (WLC) — so the man who gives the counsel is named by his relation to the man who takes it.

### Where it sits

| | Verses | Who meets Israel | What they bring |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rephidim | 17:8–16 | Amalek | a sword; *"Then Amalek came and fought with Israel"* |
| **The mountain** | **18:1–27** | **Jethro of Midian** | **his family, a blessing, a sacrifice, and advice** |
| Next | 19:1–25 | — | Israel arrives in the wilderness of Sinai *"in the third month"* (19:1) |

**Two foreigners meet Israel between the sea and Sinai, one with war and one with peace.** The word for peace,
***šālôm***, **occurs three times in Exodus, and all three are Jethro's**: *"Go in peace"* (4:18), the two men
asking *"each other of their welfare"* (*ləšālôm*, 18:7), and the people going home *"in peace"* (18:23) (WLC
lemma search, Exodus). See [[The Battle with Amalek]] and [[The Nations]].

**The chapter breaks the pattern of the wilderness so far.** Marah, Sin, and Rephidim were each a need, a
complaint, a cry, and God's answer (see [[Murmuring]]); here the problem is not hunger or thirst but
administration, the one who names it is an outsider, and **the solution is a man's advice, which God is not
reported to confirm.**

**And it sits in an odd place.** 18:5 puts Israel already *"at the Mountain of God"*, while 19:1–2 has them
arrive at Sinai only afterwards; Moses speaks of making the people know *"the statutes of God, and his laws"*
(18:16) before the law has been given; and Jethro offers burnt offerings before any altar law. **Deuteronomy
places the appointment of the judges when Israel was leaving Horeb** (Deuteronomy 1:6–18, by reference for
1:6–8), and **Numbers has Moses' Midianite in-law leave at the departure from Sinai** (Numbers 10:29–32). Whether
Exodus 18 is told out of chronological order — gathered here by subject, the foreign visitor set beside the
foreign enemy — or in sequence is an old question; **the wiki records the tension and does not resolve it.**

## Key verses

### Jethro heard (18:1–6)

> **1** Now Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard of all that God had done for Moses, and
> for Israel his people, how that Yahweh had brought Israel out of Egypt.
>
> **2** Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, received Zipporah, Moses' wife, after he had sent her away,
>
> **3** and her two sons. The name of one son was Gershom, for Moses said, "I have lived as a foreigner in a
> foreign land".
>
> **4** The name of the other was Eliezer, for he said, "My father's God was my help and delivered me from
> Pharaoh's sword."

***"Heard"*** — ***wayyišmaʿ***. **The chapter opens and turns on the same verb**: Jethro *heard* (18:1), and
at the turn Jethro says *"Listen (šəmaʿ) now to my voice"* (18:19), and *"Moses listened (wayyišmaʿ) to the voice
of his father-in-law"* (18:24) (WLC). **News of the exodus reaches the nations as the Song said it would** —
*"The peoples have heard (šāməʿû)"* (15:14) — but the Song's nations *"tremble"*; Jethro comes and blesses.

***"After he had sent her away"*** — ***ʾaḥar šillûḥêhā***, literally *after her sendings-off*. ***Šillûḥîm***
**occurs nowhere else in the Torah** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Who sent her, when, and why are
not said.** The last time Zipporah was on the page she was travelling to Egypt with Moses (4:20) and circumcised
her son at the lodging place (4:24–26). **The Hebrew has no subject** — the suffix *-hā* is *her*, and the sender is
unstated; the WEB supplies *"he"*, and readers usually take the sender to be Moses. **Readings reported, not adopted**:
Moses sent his family back for safety before the plagues; or after the lodging place; or the noun means a
*divorce*, from *šālaḥ*, the verb Deuteronomy uses of sending away a wife (Deuteronomy 24:1, by reference). **The
text gives no ground for the last beyond the root**, and 18:5–6 has Jethro bring *"your wife"* back to him. See
[[Zipporah]].

***"Eliezer"*** — ***ʾĕlîʿezer***, *my God is help*. **The name is that of Abram's steward, *"Eliezer of
Damascus"* (Genesis 15:2), and those are the name's only two occurrences in the Torah** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). **This is the first time Moses' second son is named**; 4:20 said *"his sons"*, but only
Gershom had been born on the page (2:22). **Gershom's name had no God in it; Eliezer's has God twice** — *"My father's God"*, ***ʾĕlōhê ʾābî***, the title God
gave himself at the bush (*"I am the God of your father"*, 3:6) and the one the people sang at the sea, *"my
father's God"* (15:2) (WLC) — **and it remembers the flight**: *"Pharaoh's sword"* is the danger of 2:15, *"Pharaoh … sought to kill Moses"*.
***"My help"*** — ***bəʿezrî***, from ***ʿēzer***, **the noun of Genesis 2:18, *"I will make him a helper
(ʿēzer)"***; its only other Torah occurrences are in Moses' blessing (Deuteronomy 33:7, 26, 29) (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). See [[Eliezer (son of Moses)]] and [[Gershom]].

***"Delivered me"*** — ***wayyaṣṣilēnî***, from ***nāṣal***. **The verb occurs five times in this chapter**
(18:4, 8, 9, 10 twice; WLC) and is the chapter's word for the exodus. It is the verb of Jethro's daughters'
first report of Moses, *"An Egyptian delivered us (hiṣṣîlānû) out of the hand of the shepherds"* (2:19); of God at
the bush, *"I have come down to deliver (ləhaṣṣîlô) them out of the hand of the Egyptians"* (3:8); of Moses'
accusation, *"you have not rescued (lōʾ-hiṣṣaltā) your people at all"* (5:23); and of the promise, *"I will rescue
you"* (6:6) (WLC). **In 18:10 Jethro blesses Yahweh *"who has delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians"*
in the exact words of 3:8** (*hiṣṣîl … miyyad miṣrayim*; WLC). **The complaint of 5:23 is answered in a
Midianite's mouth.**

***"At the Mountain of God"*** (18:5) — ***har hāʾĕlōhîm***, the name of 3:1 and 4:27, and of 24:13 (WLC). **The
bush's sign — *"you shall serve God on this mountain"* (3:12) — is in sight**, and the first sacrifice offered
there is a foreigner's. See [[Horeb]].

### Moses went out to meet his father-in-law (18:7–8)

> **7** Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, and bowed and kissed him. They asked each other of their
> welfare, and they came into the tent.
>
> **8** Moses told his father-in-law all that Yahweh had done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians for Israel's sake,
> all the hardships that had come on them on the way, and how Yahweh delivered them.

**Moses bows** — ***wayyištaḥû***, the verb of worship — **and kisses**, as Aaron kissed him at the same mountain
(4:27). **No word is reported for Zipporah or the sons**; the greeting is between the two men. ***"Hardships"*** —
***hattəlāʾâ***; **its one other Torah use is Moses' message to the king of Edom**, *"You know all the travail that
has happened to us"* (Numbers 20:14; WEB; WLC). **Twice Moses recounts the road to a foreign ruler; Jethro
rejoices, Edom refuses.**

**The first time Moses tells the story, he tells it to a Midianite.** What Exodus told the reader, and what the
Song sang, is now told by its human agent, and the telling credits Yahweh at both ends: *"all that Yahweh had
done"* and *"how Yahweh delivered them"*.

### Now I know (18:9–12)

> **9** Jethro rejoiced for all the goodness which Yahweh had done to Israel, in that he had delivered them out of
> the hand of the Egyptians.
>
> **10** Jethro said, "Blessed be Yahweh, who has delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians, and out of the
> hand of Pharaoh; who has delivered the people from under the hand of the Egyptians.
>
> **11** Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods because of the thing in which they dealt arrogantly
> against them."
>
> **12** Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, took a burnt offering and sacrifices for God. Aaron came with all the
> elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God.

***"Rejoiced"*** — ***wayyiḥad***, from ***ḥādâ***, **which occurs nowhere else in the Torah** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy).

***"Blessed be Yahweh"*** — ***bārûk YHWH***, **the first such blessing of God in Exodus** (WLC lemma search,
Exodus). **In Genesis the formula is spoken by Noah** (*"Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Shem"*, Genesis 9:26), **by
Abraham's servant** (24:27), **and — of *ʾĒl ʿElyôn* — by Melchizedek**: *"blessed be God Most High, who has
delivered your enemies into your hand"* (Genesis 14:20). **In Genesis and Exodus the only priests outside Israel who bless God are Melchizedek and Jethro** — the
priests of Egypt (Genesis 41:45; 47:22) are named but bless no one (WLC lemma search for *kōhēn*,
Genesis–Exodus); Melchizedek blesses *ʾĒl ʿElyôn*, whom Abram then names as Yahweh (14:22). **Both bless God for a
deliverance from enemies, both meet the deliverer after a victory, and both share a meal** — bread and wine
brought out (14:18); bread eaten *"before God"* (18:12). **The verbs differ**: Melchizedek's *delivered* is
***miggēn***, Jethro's ***hiṣṣîl*** (WLC). See [[Blessed Be God Who Delivered - Melchizedek and Jethro]].

***"Now I know"*** — ***ʿattâ yādaʿtî kî*** (see
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]]). **The exact words of the angel at Moriah**, *"for now I know that you
fear God"* (Genesis 22:12; WLC). **And the answer to Pharaoh's first reply**, *"Who is Yahweh … ? I don't know
(lōʾ yādaʿtî) Yahweh"* (5:2). **The first blow was announced with *"In this you shall know that I am Yahweh"*** (7:17), and the
formula recurs through the plagues; **Pharaoh's *"I don't know Yahweh"* is never taken back in those words, and a
Midianite priest says *"Now I know"* from a report.**

***"Greater than all gods"*** — ***gādôl YHWH mikkol-hāʾĕlōhîm***. **The Passover promised *"Against all the gods
of Egypt I will execute judgments"*** (*ûbəkol-ʾĕlōhê miṣrayim*, 12:12), **and the Song asked *"Who is like you,
Yahweh, among the gods?"*** (15:11) (WLC). **Jethro's sentence is the Song's question answered by an outsider.** It
is comparative, not exclusive — *greater than*, not *the only* — and the text lets it stand. **Whether it is a
conversion** is not said: Jethro goes home to his own land (18:27), and Exodus never names any other god as his.
**Readings reported, not adopted**: a confession of faith by a convert (the rabbinic Jethro is commonly cited as
the model proselyte; sources not checked here); a polytheist's ranking of Yahweh as supreme; or a priest
acknowledging a god he already served under another name (*Reuel*, *friend of God*, 2:18).

***"Because of the thing in which they dealt arrogantly against them"*** — ***kî baddābār ʾăšer zādû
ʿălêhem***. **The clause is elliptical**, and the WEB supplies its sense. ***Zîd***, *to act presumptuously*, **is
the verb of Jacob's *"boiled stew"*** (Genesis 25:29, of *seething*) and in the law of the man who kills
*"presumptuously"* (21:14) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). The usual reading: **Egypt was paid in its own
coin** — the sons cast into the water (1:22) answered by the army drowned in it. That is a reading.

***"A burnt offering and sacrifices"*** — ***ʿōlâ ûzəbāḥîm***. **The first burnt offering actually offered in
Exodus**: Moses asked Pharaoh for *"sacrifices and burnt offerings"* (*zəbāḥîm wəʿōlōt*, 10:25), and the Passover is
called a *zebaḥ* (12:27), but **the first *ʿōlâ* the book reports being made is Jethro's** (WLC lemma search,
Exodus). **No altar is mentioned.** ***"Took"*** —
***wayyiqqaḥ*** — **the text does not say Jethro officiated**, only that he *took* or *provided*; the Samaritan
Pentateuch and some versions are reported to read *offered* instead (not checked here). See
[[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]].

***"Aaron came with all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God"*** — ***lipnê
hāʾĕlōhîm***. **Aaron's first act as named host is to eat with a foreign priest.** **Moses is not in the list**,
though the meal is in his camp and the text likely assumes him; *"before God"* recurs at 18:19 for Moses' role.
**The meal anticipates the covenant meal on the mountain**, where Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders
*"saw God, and ate and drank"* (24:11; *not yet ingested*). That is a reading; the vocabulary differs.

### What is this thing that you do? (18:13–16)

> **13** On the next day, Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood around Moses from the morning to the
> evening.
>
> **14** When Moses' father-in-law saw all that he did to the people, he said, "What is this thing that you do for
> the people? Why do you sit alone, and all the people stand around you from morning to evening?"
>
> **15** Moses said to his father-in-law, "Because the people come to me to inquire of God.
>
> **16** When they have a matter, they come to me, and I judge between a man and his neighbor, and I make them
> know the statutes of God, and his laws."

***"Moses sat to judge the people"*** — ***wayyēšeb mōšeh lišpōṭ ʾet-hāʿām***. ***Šāpaṭ*** **has occurred twice
before in Exodus**: the Hebrew's *"Who made you a prince and a judge (wəšōpēṭ) over us?"* (2:14) and the officers'
*"May Yahweh look at you, and judge (wəyišpōṭ)"* (5:21) (WLC lemma search, Exodus). **Here it occurs four times
(18:13, 16, 22, 26). The man refused as a judge is now the only judge, and has too much of it.** See
[[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].

***"Sat … stood"*** — **Moses *sits* (*yāšab*), the people *stand* (*ʿāmad*, *niṣṣāb*)** (WLC). **The day before he
sat on a stone because he could not hold his hands up** (17:12); **now he sits and they cannot sit.**

***"To inquire of God"*** — ***lidrōš ʾĕlōhîm***. **The first *dāraš* of God since Rebekah**, who *"went to inquire
of Yahweh"* (*lidrōš ʾet-YHWH*, Genesis 25:22) about the struggle in her womb; **those are the Torah's only two
uses of the phrase before Deuteronomy** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Rebekah went to God; Israel
goes to Moses.**

***"Between a man and his neighbor"*** — ***bên ʾîš ûbên rēʿēhû***. **The case is a dispute**, a *dābār* *"they
have"*. **And the second half is teaching**: *"I make them know (wəhôdaʿtî) the statutes of God, and his laws"* —
***ḥuqqê hāʾĕlōhîm wəʾet-tôrōtāyw***, the pair of Genesis 26:5 (*ḥuqqôtay wətôrōtāy*) and of 16:28's *"my
commandments and my laws"* (WLC). **Which statutes, before Sinai, the text does not say** — Marah's *"statute and
ordinance"* (15:25), the manna's Sabbath (16:23), or case-by-case rulings. See [[Law and Obedience]].

### Not good, too heavy, alone (18:17–18)

> **17** Moses' father-in-law said to him, "The thing that you do is not good.
>
> **18** You will surely wear away, both you, and this people that is with you; for the thing is too heavy for
> you. You are not able to perform it yourself alone.

***"Not good"*** — ***lōʾ-ṭôb***; ***"alone"*** — ***ləbaddekā***. **The two words stand together once before in
Scripture**: *"It is not good (lōʾ-ṭôb) for the man to be alone (ləbaddô). I will make him a helper (ʿēzer)"*
(Genesis 2:18; WLC). **Exodus 18 has all three — *not good*, *alone*, and, in Eliezer's name four verses
earlier, *my help*.** **Whether the narrator intends the echo is not demonstrable**; the shared vocabulary is a
fact, the allusion a reading. **What is clear in both places is that solitude is the problem, and that the
solution is other people.** See [[Creation]].

***"You will surely wear away"*** — ***nābōl tibbōl***, infinitive and finite verb, *withering you will wither*.
***Nābēl*** **is the verb of a leaf that fades**; **in this sense it occurs nowhere else in the Torah** (WLC lemma
search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **And it is the people who will wither too**: *"both you, and this people"*.

***"Too heavy for you"*** — ***kî-kābēd mimməkā haddābār***. ***Kābēd*** **is the word of Pharaoh's heart (7:14), of
Moses' mouth (4:10), and — one chapter ago — of his hands** (*"Moses' hands were heavy"*, *kəbēdîm*, 17:12) (WLC).
**Moses' insufficiency, which God answered at the bush with Aaron and on the hill with Aaron and Hur, is now
answered by Jethro with a whole hierarchy of other men.** **The plan's goal is the
opposite word**: *"So shall it be easier for you"* — ***wəhāqēl mēʿālêkā***, *make it light*, from ***qālal***
(18:22; WLC), the verb of the waters that *"abated"* after the flood (Genesis 8:8, 11).

***"You are not able to perform it yourself alone"*** — ***lōʾ-tûkal ʿăśōhû ləbaddekā***. **Moses will say it of
himself twice more, in almost the same Hebrew**: *"I am not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too
heavy for me"* (*lōʾ-ʾûkal ʾānōkî ləbaddî … kî kābēd mimmennî*, Numbers 11:14; WEB; WLC), and *"I am not able to
bear you myself alone"* (*lōʾ-ʾûkal ləbaddî*, Deuteronomy 1:9; WEB; WLC). See
[[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]].

### I will give you counsel (18:19–23)

> **19** Listen now to my voice. I will give you counsel, and God be with you. You represent the people before God,
> and bring the causes to God.
>
> **20** You shall teach them the statutes and the laws, and shall show them the way in which they must walk, and
> the work that they must do.
>
> **21** Moreover you shall provide out of all the people able men which fear God: men of truth, hating unjust
> gain; and place such over them, to be rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of
> tens.
>
> **22** Let them judge the people at all times. It shall be that every great matter they shall bring to you, but
> every small matter they shall judge themselves. So shall it be easier for you, and they shall share the load
> with you.
>
> **23** If you will do this thing, and God commands you so, then you will be able to endure, and all these people
> also will go to their place in peace."

***"I will give you counsel"*** — ***ʾîʿāṣəkā***. **The verb *yāʿaṣ* occurs only twice in the Torah, both times
in this identical form and both times from a foreigner**: here, and Balaam to Balak, *"Come, I will inform you
(ʾîʿāṣəkā) what this people shall do to your people"* (Numbers 24:14; WEB; WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). **A Midianite priest counsels Moses; a hired seer counsels Moab's king against Israel.** That
comparison is noted, not pressed.

***"And God be with you"*** — ***wîhî ʾĕlōhîm ʿimmāk***. **The promise of the bush, *"I will be with you"*
(*ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*, 3:12), turned into a wish in a foreigner's mouth** (WLC).

***"You represent the people before God"*** — ***hĕyēh ʾattâ lāʿām mûl hāʾĕlōhîm***, literally *be you for the
people in front of God*. ***Mûl***, *in front of*, is otherwise a word for position — of curtains, of the
breastpiece, of a valley *"opposite"* a town (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Jethro does not reduce
Moses' role; he narrows it to what only Moses can do**: stand before God, bring the cases to God, teach the
statutes (***wəhizhartâ***, *warn, enlighten*; the verb occurs nowhere else in the Torah; WLC), and show *"the
way"* and *"the work"*.

***"Provide"*** — ***teḥĕzeh***, literally ***you shall see***, from ***ḥāzâ***, **the verb of visions. This is its
first occurrence in Scripture**; its next is the elders who *"saw (wayyeḥĕzû) God"* on the mountain (24:11), and
then Balaam, *"who sees the vision of the Almighty"* (Numbers 24:4, 16; WEB) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy).
**Moses is to pick the judges by a seer's word for seeing.** In 18:25 the narrator reports what he did with the
ordinary word, *"Moses chose (wayyibḥar)"*.

***"Able men which fear God: men of truth, hating unjust gain"*** — **four qualifications**:

| Hebrew | WEB | Where the words have been |
|---|---|---|
| ***ʾanšê-ḥayil*** | *able men* | **Pharaoh to Joseph**: *"If you know any able men (ʾanšê-ḥayil) among them, then put them in charge (wəśamtām śārê) of my livestock"* (Genesis 47:6) — the same noun pair and the same verb *śîm* with *śārê* as 18:21 (WLC) |
| ***yirʾê ʾĕlōhîm*** | *which fear God* | the adjective *yārēʾ* with *God* only once before: *"now I know that you fear God (yərēʾ ʾĕlōhîm)"*, Genesis 22:12 (WLC); and the midwives *"feared God"* (1:17, verb) |
| ***ʾanšê ʾĕmet*** | *men of truth* | **the first *ʾĕmet* in Exodus**; its next is God's own name, *"abundant in loving kindness and truth"* (34:6) (WLC lemma search, Exodus) |
| ***śōnʾê bāṣaʿ*** | *hating unjust gain* | ***beṣaʿ*** **occurs once before in the Torah**: Judah's *"What profit (beṣaʿ) is it if we kill our brother?"* (Genesis 37:26) — the brother sold for silver (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy) |

**The list puts piety and incorruptibility beside competence**; Deuteronomy's retelling instead asks for men
*"wise … of understanding and well known"* (Deuteronomy 1:13; WEB), and its law of judges forbids the bribe
(Deuteronomy 16:18–19; WEB). See [[Integrity]] and [[The Fear of God]].

***"Rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens"*** — ***śārê ʾălāpîm …
ʿăśārōt***. ***Śar***, *prince, ruler*, **is the word the Hebrew threw at Moses**: *"Who made you a prince (śar)
and a judge over us?"* (2:14; WLC). **Now Moses makes *śārîm*.** **The ranks are military in form**; Numbers will
use *"captains of thousands"* and *"captains of hundreds"* for the army (Numbers 31:14, by reference). How the
decimal ranks map onto the tribes and clans the text does not say.

***"Every great matter … every small matter"*** — ***haddābār haggādōl … haddābār haqqāṭōn***; in the report
***haddābār haqqāšeh***, ***"the hard causes"*** (18:26). ***Qāšeh*** **is the *hard* of Egypt's *"hard service"***
(*ʿăbōdâ qāšâ*, 1:14) and *"cruel bondage"* (6:9) (WLC). **What was hard in Egypt was the work; what is hard in the
wilderness is the case.** **The word *dābār*, *thing, matter, case*, occurs eleven times in the chapter** (18:11,
14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 22 twice, 23, 26 twice; WLC) — **the chapter is about *the thing*.**

***"They shall share the load with you"*** — ***wənāśəʾû ʾittāk***, *they shall bear with you*. **Numbers 11
repeats it with God as the speaker**: the seventy elders *"shall bear (wənāśəʾû) the burden of the people with
you (ʾittāk), that you don't bear it yourself alone (ləbaddekā)"* (Numbers 11:17; WEB; WLC). **In Exodus the idea
is a Midianite's and the men are chosen for character; in Numbers it is God's and the men receive the Spirit.**

***"If you will do this thing, and God commands you so"*** — ***wəṣiwwəkā ʾĕlōhîm***. **Jethro makes his own advice
conditional on God's command.** **The text never reports the command**; 18:24 says only that Moses *"listened to
the voice of his father-in-law"*. **Readings reported, not adopted**: the condition is met silently (Moses would
not act otherwise); or it is met by Moses' own judgment; or Deuteronomy 1, where the plan is Moses' and the people
approve it (*"The thing which you have spoken is good to do"*, Deuteronomy 1:14; WEB), is the corrective. **The
wiki records that Exodus gives human counsel and no divine confirmation.** See [[Wisdom]].

***"You will be able to endure"*** — ***wəyākāltā ʿămōd***, literally *you will be able to stand*: **Moses, who
sat while the people stood, will be able to *stand*.** ***"Go to their place in peace"*** — ***bəšālôm***, the third
Jethro *šālôm*.

### Moses listened (18:24–27)

> **24** So Moses listened to the voice of his father-in-law, and did all that he had said.
>
> **25** Moses chose able men out of all Israel, and made them heads over the people, rulers of thousands, rulers of
> hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.
>
> **26** They judged the people at all times. They brought the hard causes to Moses, but every small matter they
> judged themselves.
>
> **27** Moses let his father-in-law depart, and he went his way into his own land.

***"Listened to the voice of"*** — ***wayyišmaʿ … ləqôl***. **Genesis uses the phrase for a man heeding another
human voice twice**: Abram *"listened to the voice of Sarai"* (16:2), and God to Adam, *"Because you have listened to
your wife's voice"* (3:17) (WLC). **Both ended badly; this one does not.** **Marah's condition was *"If you will
diligently listen to Yahweh your God's voice"*** (15:26). **The man who taught Israel to listen to God's voice
listens to a Midianite's.**

***"Did all that he had said"*** — ***wayyaʿaś kōl ʾăšer ʾāmār***. **The formula of obedience**, used of Moses toward
God (*"Moses did so"*, 17:6), **here used of Moses toward his father-in-law.**

**Only the first qualification survives the report**: 18:25 has *ʾanšê-ḥayil*, *"able men"*, and none of the other three.
**The narrator does not say the chosen men feared God, were truthful, or hated gain**; it says Moses *chose*
(*wayyibḥar*) them. Whether that is economy or a hint the text does not say.

***"Moses let his father-in-law depart"*** — ***wayšallaḥ***, ***the verb of "Let my people go"*** (*šallaḥ
ʾet-ʿammî*, 5:1), **and the root of *šillûḥîm* in 18:2** (WLC). **The chapter that began with Zipporah *sent
away* ends with Jethro *sent away*, in peace.** ***"Into his own land"*** — ***ʾel-ʾarṣô***. **Numbers 10:29–32
tells a parallel departure**: Moses begs *"Hobab, the son of Reuel the Midianite, Moses' father-in-law"* to stay —
*"you can be our eyes"* — and Hobab answers *"I will depart to my own land (ʾel-ʾarṣî)"* (Numbers 10:30; WEB; WLC).
**Numbers does not say whether he went**; Judges 1:16 and 4:11 put *"the children of the Kenite, Moses'
brother-in-law"* and *"the children of Hobab"* in the land (WEB). **Whether Hobab is Jethro, Jethro's son, or a
third tradition** is set out on [[Jethro]].

## People
- [[Jethro]] — **the priest of Midian**, named seven times in 18:1–12 and then only *"Moses' father-in-law"*;
  brings Zipporah and the sons, rejoices, blesses Yahweh, confesses *"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all
  gods"*, provides the first burnt offering in Exodus, and gives the counsel that reorganizes Israel's judging
- [[Moses]] — bows to and kisses his father-in-law; tells the story of the exodus for the first time; judges the
  people from morning to evening, *"alone"*; listens, chooses the judges, and keeps the hard cases; lets Jethro go
- [[Zipporah]] — *"sent away"* at some time not narrated, and brought back; silent
- [[Gershom]] — his name and its explanation repeated from 2:22
- [[Eliezer (son of Moses)]] — **first named**: *"My father's God was my help and delivered me from Pharaoh's
  sword"*
- [[Aaron]] — comes with the elders to eat bread with Jethro before God
- [[God]] — the subject of everything told, blessed, and confessed; the One the people come to inquire of; the
  condition of the plan (*"and God commands you so"*), whose command is not reported
- [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] — named twice, as the sword Moses escaped (18:4) and the hand Israel was delivered
  from (18:8, 10)
- The elders of Israel — eat with Jethro (18:12) — collective (no page)
- The judges — *"able men"*, the *śārîm* of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens — collective (no page)

- [[Midian]] — Jethro's priesthood and *"his own land"*, to which he returns (18:1, 27)

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 18:1 opens with a new subject (*"Now Jethro … heard"*) and 19:1 with a new
  date and place.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** Exodus 18's place before the arrival at Sinai (19:1) against its *"Mountain
  of God"* (18:5), its *"statutes of God, and his laws"* (18:16), and Deuteronomy 1's placing of the judges at the
  departure from Horeb; Exodus's Jethro-initiated, Moses-chosen judges against Deuteronomy 1's Moses-initiated,
  people-chosen ones, and Numbers 11's God-initiated, Spirit-endowed seventy; Jethro's departure (18:27) against
  Hobab's at Numbers 10:29–32.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** when and why Zipporah was *"sent away"*, including *šillûḥîm* as divorce;
  Jethro's confession as conversion, polytheist ranking, or prior faith; the sense of *zādû* in 18:11 as Egypt
  paid in its own coin; the meal of 18:12 as anticipating 24:11; Genesis 2:18 as an intended echo of 18:17–18;
  whether the condition *"and God commands you so"* was met.
- **Unchecked and not quoted:** the rabbinic Jethro as model proselyte and the Talmudic debate over the chapter's
  chronology; the Samaritan Pentateuch and versional readings of *"took"* in 18:12.
