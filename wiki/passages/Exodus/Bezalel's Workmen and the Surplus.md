# Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 35:30–36:7

## Summary

Moses tells Israel that Yahweh has called Bezalel by name and filled him with the Spirit of
God for wisdom, understanding, knowledge, and skilled work. His genealogy identifies him with
Judah. Oholiab, son of Ahisamach, belongs to Dan. The description now explicitly includes the
capacity to teach, alongside metalwork, stone setting, wood carving, engraving, embroidery,
and weaving (35:30–35). Named leaders belong to a wider company of gifted workers.

The commission requires the whole work to follow Yahweh's commands (36:1). Moses calls Bezalel,
Oholiab, and the workers whose God-given ability and willing hearts bring them to the task
(36:2). They receive the people's contributions from Moses, while freewill gifts continue to
arrive every morning (36:3).

The workers leave their tasks to tell Moses that the people are bringing more than the work
requires (36:4–5). He orders a proclamation throughout the camp: men and women are to make no
further contributions for the sanctuary. The people stop bringing, and the narrator confirms
that the material is sufficient, with a surplus (36:6–7). The collection ends because its
purpose is supplied; the building work continues in the following unit.

## Notes

### Boundary and the stages of work

The planned boundary is retained. Verse 35:30 starts Moses' announcement of the named makers;
36:7 concludes the report of sufficiency. Verse 36:8 starts the detailed making of the curtains,
which belongs to the next pericope. The workers are already engaged in work in 36:4; the surplus
report must not be turned into a claim that construction has not begun, or that it is finished.
The prohibition in 36:6 concerns making further offerings, not halting the sanctuary's construction.

### Gift, teaching, and willingness

Exodus 31:1–6 placed the appointment in God's speech to Moses. Here Moses announces it to Israel.
The explicit Spirit-filling statement concerns Bezalel (35:31); teaching is attributed to both
him and Oholiab (35:34), and wisdom and understanding to the wider company (36:1–2). These
statements need not be collapsed into identical descriptions of every worker. The description
of teaching provides no class size, curriculum, or account of previous training. Divine gifting
and teaching coexist; the text does not require a choice between them.

The skill and willingness of workers in 36:2 stand alongside the repeated willing gifts of the
preceding passage. Neither named leader is represented as the only capable person. The women's
spinning in 35:25–26 is already part of the material preparation; the present unit's final command
expressly addresses both men and women (36:6). No additional named donor or worker is supplied.

### Enough, and more than enough

The workers report the surplus before Moses orders the collection stopped; 36:7 confirms their
assessment. This permits a reading of responsible administration: those doing the work identify
its need, and the leader acts on their information. It is an interpretation of the narrated
sequence, not a claim that Exodus describes a formal audit. No quantity or disposal of the excess
is specified here. The cessation of this particular collection is not a ban on all later giving,
and no donor is promised personal enrichment in return for contributing.

### Genealogies without invented biographies

Uri, Hur, and Ahisamach identify the two makers; they are not said to join the workforce.
Judah and Dan identify tribes. As at 31:2, the genealogy does not settle whether Bezalel's
grandfather is [[Hur (companion of Moses)]]. The two Hur pages preserve that uncertainty.
