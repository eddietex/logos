# The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 12:29–51

## Summary

**The blow announced at 4:23 and dated at 11:4 falls in a single verse.** *"At midnight, Yahweh struck
all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the
firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon; and all the firstborn of livestock"* (12:29). **The
narrator spends no more words on it than that.** Ten chapters of negotiation end in one sentence, and
the rest of the pericope is about what happens afterwards.

**Egypt (12:29–36).** Pharaoh rises in the night with all his servants and all Egypt, and there is *"a
great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one dead"* (12:30) — the cry 11:6
foretold. **Pharaoh summons Moses and Aaron by night and concedes everything he has bargained over
since chapter 8**: *"Rise up, get out from among my people, both you and the children of Israel; and go,
serve Yahweh, as you have said! Take both your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone;
and bless me also!"* (12:31–32). The Egyptians urge the people out *"in haste, for they said, 'We are
all dead men'"* (12:33). Israel carries its dough unleavened in its kneading troughs, asks for silver,
gold, and clothing, and is given them: ***"They plundered the Egyptians"*** (12:34–36).

**The road (12:37–42).** From Rameses to Succoth, *"about six hundred thousand on foot who were men,
besides children"*, with *"a mixed multitude"* and *"very much livestock"* (12:37–38). They bake the
unleavened dough on the way, *"because they were thrust out of Egypt, and couldn't wait"* (12:39).
**Then the narrator stops the march to give a date**: *"the time that the children of Israel lived in
Egypt was four hundred thirty years"*, and they left *"to the day"* (12:40–41). The night becomes *"a
night to be much observed"* — kept by Yahweh, and to be kept by Israel *"throughout their
generations"* (12:42).

**The law again (12:43–49).** **Before the pericope can close on the exodus, God speaks a second
Passover law, and its subject is who may eat.** No foreigner, no settler, no hired servant; a bought
slave once circumcised; and a sojourner who wants to keep it, once all his males are circumcised, *"shall
be as one who is born in the land"*. ***"One law shall be to him who is born at home, and to the
stranger who lives as a foreigner among you"*** (12:49).

**And the close (12:50–51).** *"All the children of Israel did so"* — the obedience formula a second
time in a chapter — and *"That same day, Yahweh brought the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt
by their armies"* (12:51).

### The shape of it

| | 12:29–36 | 12:37–42 | 12:43–49 | 12:50–51 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mode | narrative | narrative, then a dated comment | law | narrative summary |
| Time | midnight, *"by night"* | the march; *"to the day"* | timeless — *"the ordinance"* | *"that same day"* |
| Who acts | Yahweh strikes; Pharaoh, the Egyptians, Israel | Israel travels and bakes | Yahweh to Moses and Aaron | Israel obeys; Yahweh brings out |
| Says why | *"We are all dead men"* (12:33) | *"they were thrust out"* (12:39) | *"one law"* (12:49) | — |

**The chapter is law, then story, then law, then story.** 12:1–28 gave the rite before the event; 12:29–42
tells the event; 12:43–49 returns to the rite; 12:50–51 closes the event. **Exodus will not tell the
night without the instructions for remembering it on either side.** The two closing verses repeat
12:28 and 12:41 almost word for word — the obedience formula and *bəʿeṣem hayyôm hazzeh*, *that same
day* — so the second law is framed by the same sentences that frame the departure.

## Key verses

### At midnight (12:29–30)

> **29** At midnight, Yahweh struck all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of
> Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon; and all the
> firstborn of livestock.
> **30** Pharaoh rose up in the night, he, and all his servants, and all the Egyptians; and there was a
> great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one dead.

***"At midnight"*** — ***bahăṣî hallaylâ***. Moses had said *"About midnight"* (*kaḥăṣōt hallaylâ*,
11:4; WLC); **the narrator drops the *about***. The event keeps the appointment.

***"Yahweh struck"*** — ***waYHWH hikkâ***. **The subject is Yahweh, placed first in the clause** (WLC).
At 12:23 Moses had spoken of *"the destroyer"* whom Yahweh would not let in; the narrator's account of
the night does not mention the destroyer and gives the act to Yahweh alone, as God's own forecast had
(*"I … will strike"*, 12:12). **This does not settle who the destroyer was** — the narrator also says God
destroyed Sodom (Genesis 19:24, 29) where the angels said *they* would (19:13) — but it is where the
book puts the weight. See [[The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night]].

***"From the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the
dungeon"*** — the range of 11:5, with its lower end changed. **Moses' forecast ended at *"the female
servant who is behind the mill"*; the narrator ends at *"the captive (haššəbî) who was in the dungeon
(bəbêt habbôr)"*.** *Šəbî*, *captive*, occurs here first in the Torah (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy). ***Bôr*, *pit* or *dungeon*, is Joseph's word** — the empty pit his brothers threw
him into (Genesis 37:24), and the dungeon he called his own in Egypt: *"they should put me into the
dungeon (babbôr)"* (40:15), *"they brought him hastily out of the dungeon"* (41:14) (WLC lemma search).
**Joseph came out of Egypt's *bôr* to save Egypt; now Egypt's *bôr* holds a firstborn who dies with
Pharaoh's.** Whether the writer meant the echo cannot be shown; the word and the land are the same. See
[[Joseph]].

***"And all the firstborn of livestock"*** — after the pestilence (9:6), the hail (9:25), and Pharaoh's
offer to keep Israel's herds (10:24), Egypt still has livestock to lose. The page on
[[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]] records the tension of *"all the livestock of Egypt died"* (9:6) with the
animals that survive it.

***"A great cry"*** — ***ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ***, as foretold at 11:6. **The book's first cry was Israel's under
Egypt** (3:7, 9); **its loudest is Egypt's.** The only other *ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ* in the Torah is Esau's for the
firstborn's blessing (Genesis 27:34; WLC). See
[[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]] and [[Mourning and Lament]].

***"There was not a house where there was not one dead"*** — the house again. **In 12:1–28 the house was
the unit of the rite and the place of safety; here every Egyptian house has its dead**, and the
distinction God promised at 11:7 is drawn at the door.

### Rise up, get out (12:31–32)

> **31** He called for Moses and Aaron by night, and said, "Rise up, get out from among my people, both
> you and the children of Israel; and go, serve Yahweh, as you have said!
> **32** Take both your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone; and bless me also!"

***"He called for Moses and Aaron by night"*** — **this sits awkwardly with 10:28–29.** Pharaoh had said
*"in the day you see my face you shall die!"*, and Moses *"I will see your face again no more."* The text
does not say the brothers came into Pharaoh's presence; *"called for"* (*wayyiqrāʾ lə-*) may mean he sent
for them, and some readers take the exchange as carried by messengers. Others read the seam as the same
one left visible at 11:4–8, where Moses is still before Pharaoh after 10:29. **The wiki records the
tension and adopts neither reading.**

***"Rise up, get out"*** — ***qûmû ṣəʾû***. **The same two imperatives Lot spoke to his sons-in-law on the
night Sodom fell**: *"Get up! Get out of this place, for Yahweh will destroy the city"* (Genesis 19:14,
*qûmû ṣəʾû min-hammāqôm hazzeh*; WLC). **There the warning came from inside the doomed house and was
laughed at; here it comes from the king of the doomed land, and is obeyed.** See
[[The Door and the Destroyer - Lot's House and the Passover Night]].

**Moses had forecast that Pharaoh's servants would come down, bow, and say *"Get out"*** (11:8, *ṣēʾ*).
**What is narrated is Pharaoh himself saying it**; the servants rise in the night (12:30) but their bowing
is not reported. See [[Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)]].

***"Both you and the children of Israel … both your flocks and your herds, as you have said"*** —
***gam … gam***, twice. **Every limit Pharaoh tried is withdrawn in one breath**: sacrifice *"in the land"*
(8:25), *"not very far away"* (8:28), the men only (10:11), everyone but the herds (10:24). He says
*"as you have said"* (*kədabberkem*, *kaʾăšer dibbartem*) twice: **the terms are Moses' to the
letter** — *"Not a hoof shall be left behind"* (10:26). See
[[Our Little Ones Stayed in Goshen - Genesis 50.8 and the Bargaining of Exodus 10]].

***"Go, serve Yahweh"*** — ***ʿibdû ʾet-YHWH***. The demand of 4:23, *"Let my son go, that he may serve
me"*, granted in its own verb. **The king who made Israel *serve* (1:13–14) now tells it whom to serve.**
See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"And bless me also!"*** — ***ûbēraktem gam-ʾōtî***. **The king who began the contest with *"Who is
Yahweh?"* (5:2) ends it asking to be blessed by Yahweh's servants.** The last time a Pharaoh and an
Israelite and blessing stood in one scene, *"Jacob blessed Pharaoh"* — twice, at his coming in and his
going out (Genesis 47:7, 10). **Then the blessing was given unasked; now it is asked for and no answer is
recorded.** The text does not say whether Moses blessed him. See
[[Jacob Blessed Pharaoh - The Lesser Blessed by the Greater]] and [[Blessing]].

### In haste (12:33–34)

> **33** The Egyptians were urgent with the people, to send them out of the land in haste, for they said,
> "We are all dead men."
> **34** The people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneading troughs being bound up in
> their clothes on their shoulders.

***"Were urgent"*** — ***wattehĕzaq miṣrayim***, from ***ḥāzaq***, *to be strong* — **the verb of Pharaoh's
hardened heart** (*wayyeḥĕzaq*, 7:13, 22; 8:19, 8:15 in Hebrew; 9:35; WLC). The strength that held Israel in now pushes
it out; God had said that *"by a strong hand"* Pharaoh would let them go and drive them out (6:1).

***"To send them out"*** — ***ləšalləḥām***, the *šālaḥ* of *"Let my people go"*, now the Egyptians'
own urgent wish.

***"We are all dead men"*** — ***kullānû mētîm***. **The servants had said *"Egypt is destroyed"* (10:7);
now all Egypt says it of itself.**

***"Their kneading troughs"*** — ***mišʾărōtām***. **The frogs had come *"into your ovens, and into your
kneading troughs"*** (8:3; 7:28 in Hebrew); the word occurs in the Torah only there, here, and in
Deuteronomy's blessing and curse on *"your basket and your kneading trough"* (28:5, 17) (WLC lemma
search). **The trough the plague filled is the trough Israel carries out.**

***"Before it was leavened"*** — ***ṭerem yeḥmāṣ***. **12:15–20 commanded seven days without leaven; here the
narrator gives a reason the law did not.** The rite came first, and the history caught up with it: *"for
it wasn't leavened, because they were thrust out"* (12:39).

### They plundered the Egyptians (12:35–36)

> **35** The children of Israel did according to the word of Moses; and they asked of the Egyptians
> jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and clothing.
> **36** Yahweh gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they let them have what
> they asked. They plundered the Egyptians.

**The third telling, and the one that reports it done.** The bush (3:21–22) said the women would ask and
*"You shall plunder the Egyptians"*; 11:2–3 had men and women ask and the favour already given; **here the
narrator closes the chain in the bush's own words**: *"They plundered the Egyptians"* —
***wayənaṣṣəlû ʾet-miṣrāyim*** (WLC).

***Wayənaṣṣəlû*** is the piel of ***nāṣal***, the root whose hiphil is God's *"I have come down to deliver
them"* (3:8), Moses' *"you have not rescued"* (5:23), God's *"I will rescue you"* (6:6), and, in this same
chapter, the children's *"he … spared our houses"* (12:27). **In Genesis–Deuteronomy the piel occurs only
at Exodus 3:22 and here** (WLC lemma search, read by stem); the root's only other use in Exodus outside
the hiphil is the hitpael of 33:6, after the calf: *"The children of Israel stripped themselves of their
jewelry"* (WLC; not yet ingested). **God rescues Israel from Egypt and Israel strips
Egypt, in one root, within ten verses.**

***"Yahweh gave the people favor"*** — ***nātan ʾet-ḥēn hāʿām***, the clause of 3:21 and 11:3. The goods are
asked for and given; the page on the plunder records the moral readings. See
[[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]], [[Favor and Grace]], and
[[Wealth and Possessions]].

**And Genesis 15:14 is paid out.** *"I will also judge that nation, whom they will serve. Afterward they
will come out with great wealth."* Judgment on the nation (12:29) and the wealth (12:35–36) arrive in the
same night. See [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

### From Rameses to Succoth (12:37–39)

> **37** The children of Israel traveled from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot who
> were men, besides children.
> **38** A mixed multitude went up also with them, with flocks, herds, and even very much livestock.
> **39** They baked unleavened cakes of the dough which they brought out of Egypt; for it wasn't
> leavened, because they were thrust out of Egypt, and couldn't wait, and they had not prepared any food
> for themselves.

***"From Rameses"*** — **the district Joseph gave his family as *"a possession … in the best of the
land"*** (Genesis 47:11), the store-city Israel built under the lash (1:11), and now the starting line.
Numbers 33:3–5 repeats it as the first stage of the wilderness itinerary. See [[Rameses]].

***"To Succoth"*** — ***sukkōtâ***, *booths*. **Not Jacob's Succoth** (Genesis 33:17), which lies east of the
Jordan; this one is in Egypt, the first camp of the march. The two sites share a name and nothing else,
as the Genesis page warned when it was written. See [[Succoth (Egypt)]] and
[[Succoth (Transjordan)]].

***"About six hundred thousand on foot who were men, besides children"*** — ***kəšēš-mēʾôt ʾelep raglî
haggəbārîm ləbad miṭṭāp***. *Raglî*, *on foot*, *foot-soldier*, occurs in the Torah only here and at
Numbers 11:21, where Moses repeats the figure (*"six hundred thousand men on foot"*, WEB) (WLC lemma
search). Numbers 1:46's census gives *"six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty"* (WEB). **With
women, children, and the mixed multitude, the figure implies a population of two million or more**, and
readers have long asked how such a number crossed the sea, lived in the wilderness, and gathered at one
tent. **Three standard answers**: the figure is to be taken as it stands; it is a later, schematic or
symbolic number; or *ʾelep*, *thousand*, here means a clan or military unit, which would make the count
far smaller. **The wiki records all three and adopts none.** See [[War]] — *raglî* and *"armies"*
(12:41, 51) give the column a military register.

***"A mixed multitude"*** — ***ʿēreb rab***. **The only *ʿēreb* in this sense in the Torah** (WLC lemma
search; the *ʿēreb*, *woof*, of Leviticus 13 is a different lemma). The text does not say who they were —
Egyptians, other slaves, or the households of intermarriage — and gives them no part in the Passover
law that follows a few verses later. **Numbers 11:4's *"mixed multitude"* (WEB) is a different word,
*ʾăsapsup*** (WLC). See [[The Nations]].

***"Unleavened cakes"*** — ***ʿugōt maṣṣôt***. *ʿUgâ* is the word of the cakes Sarah baked for the three
visitors (Genesis 18:6) and of the manna baked into cakes (Numbers 11:8) (WLC lemma search). **The first
bread of freedom is improvised from what was in the trough.**

***"They were thrust out"*** — ***gōršû***. **God said at 6:1 that Pharaoh would *"drive them out"*, and at
11:1 *"he will surely thrust you out altogether"*; here it is done, in the same verb.** *Gāraš* is the
verb of the man driven out of Eden (Genesis 3:24) and of Hagar cast out (21:10) (WLC lemma search).
**Expulsion, everywhere else in the Torah's story so far a loss, is here the rescue.**

***"And couldn't wait"*** — ***wəlōʾ yākəlû ləhitmahmēah***. **The verb of Lot**: *"But he lingered
(wayyitmahmāh)"*, and the angels took him by the hand (Genesis 19:16). In Genesis–Exodus *māhah* occurs
only there, at Genesis 43:10 (*"if we hadn't delayed"*), and here (WLC lemma search). **Lot lingered and
had to be dragged; Israel could not linger.**

***"They had not prepared any food for themselves"*** — ***ṣêdâ***, *provision for the way*. **Joseph twice
sent his brothers home with *ṣêdâ*** (Genesis 42:25; 45:21; WLC lemma search). The family that came down
supplied leaves without supplies. **The manna is two pericopes away** (16).

### Four hundred thirty years (12:40–42)

> **40** Now the time that the children of Israel lived in Egypt was four hundred thirty years.
> **41** At the end of four hundred thirty years, to the day, all of Yahweh's armies went out from the
> land of Egypt.
> **42** It is a night to be much observed to Yahweh for bringing them out from the land of Egypt. This
> is that night of Yahweh, to be much observed of all the children of Israel throughout their
> generations.

***"Four hundred thirty years"*** — **the Bible's own date for the sojourn, and it does not match Genesis
15:13's *"four hundred years"*.** The two figures, the Septuagint and Samaritan readings of this verse
(*"in Egypt and in Canaan"*, as reported there; not checked in this pass), Galatians 3:17's *"four
hundred thirty years after"* the promise, and the genealogy of Exodus 6 are laid out at
[[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]]. **The wiki records the spread there and does not
choose.**

***"To the day"*** — ***bəʿeṣem hayyôm hazzeh***, the phrase of 12:17 and 12:51 (WLC). **The prophecy given
in the dark to a childless man (Genesis 15:12–14) is kept to the day.** ***ʿEṣem***, the noun in the phrase,
is literally *bone*: the same lemma stands at 12:46, *"Do not break any of its bones (wəʿeṣem)"*, and at
13:19 for Joseph's bones (WLC lemma search). *"The bone of this day"* is an idiom for *this very day*, and
the English cannot show that the chapter's date and the lamb's bones are one word.

***"All of Yahweh's armies"*** — ***kol-ṣibʾôt YHWH***. **God had called them *"my armies"* (7:4); this is the
first time the narrator does.** *Ṣābāʾ* in Genesis is the *"host"* of heaven and earth (2:1) and the
captain of Abimelech's army (21:22, 32; 26:26); in Exodus it is Israel, *"according to their armies"*
(6:26; 7:4; 12:17, 41, 51) (WLC lemma search). **A slave column that carries no weapon in the story is
named as an army.** See [[War]].

***"A night to be much observed"*** — ***lêl šimmurîm***, literally *a night of watchings*, from ***šāmar***,
*to keep, guard, watch*. **The phrase is doubled and turned around in a single verse**: it is *"to
Yahweh"* a night of watching *"for bringing them out"*, and it is *"to all the children of Israel"* a night
of watching *"throughout their generations"*. **Readers have taken the first as God keeping vigil over
Israel that night, and the second as Israel's vigil in return**; the Hebrew allows that reading and does
not spell it out. *Šimmurîm* occurs in the Torah only in this verse (WLC lemma search). See
[[Remembrance]].

### The ordinance of the Passover (12:43–49)

> **43** Yahweh said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the ordinance of the Passover. No foreigner shall eat
> of it,
> **44** but every man's servant who is bought for money, when you have circumcised him, then shall he
> eat of it.
> **45** A foreigner and a hired servant shall not eat of it.
> **46** It must be eaten in one house. You shall not carry any of the meat outside of the house. Do not
> break any of its bones.
> **47** All the congregation of Israel shall keep it.
> **48** When a stranger shall live as a foreigner with you, and will keep the Passover to Yahweh, let all
> his males be circumcised, and then let him come near and keep it; and he shall be as one who is born in
> the land: but no uncircumcised person shall eat of it.
> **49** One law shall be to him who is born at home, and to the stranger who lives as a foreigner among
> you."

**The law is placed after the departure, though it governs the meal eaten before it.** The pericope plan
kept it here because the text does: the narrative stops a second time, and the subject is no longer how
to eat but who.

**The WEB's *foreigner* translates three different Hebrew words in five verses**, and the law turns on
the difference (WLC):

| Hebrew | WEB | Who | Passover |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ben-nēkār* (12:43) | *"foreigner"* | an outsider, not of Israel | **excluded** |
| *ʿebed … miqnat-kāsep* (12:44) | *"servant who is bought for money"* | a slave owned by an Israelite | **eats, once circumcised** |
| *tôšāb* (12:45) | *"foreigner"* | a settler, resident but not attached | **excluded** |
| *śākîr* (12:45) | *"hired servant"* | a wage labourer | **excluded** |
| *gēr* (12:48–49) | *"stranger who … live[s] as a foreigner"* | a resident alien | **eats, once all his males are circumcised — and is then *"as one who is born in the land"*** |

***"No foreigner"*** — ***kol-ben-nēkār***, and ***"bought for money"*** — ***miqnat kāsep***. **Both phrases are
from the circumcision law of Genesis 17**: every male *"born in the house, or bought with money (miqnat
kesep) from any foreigner (ben-nēkār) who is not of your offspring"* must be circumcised (17:12–13), and
Abraham circumcised *"all the men of his house … bought with money from a foreigner"* (17:27) (WLC; WEB).
**The Passover's admission rule is Abraham's household rule**: the bought slave belongs to the house, and
the house is circumcised. See [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]] and [[Circumcision]].

***"A foreigner"*** (12:45) — ***tôšāb***. **Abraham's word for himself**: *"I am a stranger and a foreigner
(gēr wətôšāb) living with you"* (Genesis 23:4; WLC). In the Torah *tôšāb* occurs at Genesis 23:4, here,
and then in Leviticus and Numbers (Leviticus 22:10; 25; Numbers 35:15) (WLC lemma search). **The two
words Abraham used together are separated here: the *gēr* may be admitted, the *tôšāb* may not.** The law
does not say why; the usual explanation is that the *gēr* has thrown in his lot with Israel and the
*tôšāb* and *śākîr* are attached only by residence or wage. See
[[A Stranger and a Foreigner - The Sojourner's Confession]].

***"It must be eaten in one house. You shall not carry any of the meat outside"*** — **the house is the
boundary again**, as in 12:22. Nothing crosses the door.

***"Do not break any of its bones"*** — ***wəʿeṣem lōʾ tišbərû-bô***. **No reason is given.** Numbers 9:12
repeats it for the second Passover: *"They shall leave none of it until the morning, nor break a bone of
it"* (WEB; Hebrew *wəʿeṣem lōʾ yišbərû-bô*, WLC). **John 19:36 cites a Scripture that says *"A bone of
him will not be broken"*** (WEB; *Ὀστοῦν οὐ συντριβήσεται αὐτοῦ*, SBLGNT) without naming it; readers
hear this law, Numbers 9:12, or Psalm 34:20 — *"He protects all of his bones. Not one of them is broken"*
(WEB), where the Hebrew (34:21) uses *šōmēr*, *keeps*, and *nišbārâ*, *is broken* (WLC). **The Psalm's
*šōmēr* is the root of 12:42's night of *watching*.** Whether John means the lamb or the righteous
sufferer, or both, is not said. See
[[Christ Our Passover - Exodus 12 in the New Testament]].

***"All the congregation of Israel shall keep it"*** — ***kol-ʿădat yiśrāʾēl***, the *ʿēdâ* first named at
12:3.

***"Let all his males be circumcised"*** — ***himmôl lô kol-zākār***. **The exact words of Genesis 17:10,
*himmôl lākem kol-zākār*, *every male among you shall be circumcised*, with *lô*, *for him*, for
*lākem*, *for you*** (WLC). ***"No uncircumcised person (ʿārēl)"*** — *ʿārēl* occurs in Genesis–Deuteronomy only
at Genesis 17:14, Exodus 6:12, 30 (Moses' *"uncircumcised lips"*), here, and in Leviticus 19:23 and 26:41
(WLC lemma search). **The rite that marks Israel's birth is closed to the uncircumcised**, and the man who
called his own lips uncircumcised is the one who gives the rule. Moses' own son was circumcised only on the
road, by Zipporah (4:24–26). See [[Uncircumcised Lips, Ears, and Heart]].

***"He shall be as one who is born in the land"*** — ***kəʾezraḥ hāʾāreṣ***. *ʾEzraḥ* appeared first at
12:19, where the stranger and the native were put under the same penalty. **Here the stranger is put
under the same privilege.** The land is still Egypt, and neither group owns any of it.

***"One law"*** — ***tôrâ ʾaḥat***. **The first *tôrâ* in the singular in Scripture**; Genesis has it once,
in the plural, of Abraham's keeping *"my laws"* (26:5) (WLC lemma search). The next is 13:9, *"that
Yahweh's law may be in your mouth"*. **The word that will name the whole Torah is first used to say that
one law covers the native and the stranger.** The principle recurs in the same or nearly the same words at
Leviticus 24:22, Numbers 9:14, and Numbers 15:15–16, 29. See
[[One Law for the Native and the Stranger]] and
[[A Foreigner in a Foreign Land - Gershom and the Law of the Stranger]].

### That same day (12:50–51)

> **50** All the children of Israel did so. As Yahweh commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.
> **51** That same day, Yahweh brought the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their armies.

***"As Yahweh commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did"*** — the obedience formula of 12:28, **its second use
of the whole people in one chapter.** See [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

***"Yahweh brought … out"*** — ***hôṣîʾ YHWH***. **The verb of the book's title, at last in the narrator's
voice as a completed act.** God said *hôṣēʾtî*, *I have brought out*, in the festival law before it
happened (12:17); here it has happened. **12:41 said the armies *went out* (yāṣəʾû); 12:51 says Yahweh
*brought them out* (hôṣîʾ)** (WLC). The same departure is told once as Israel's act and once as God's.

### Night or day?

**The pericope dates the departure two ways.** Pharaoh summons Moses *"by night"* (12:31); 12:42 calls it
*"a night … for bringing them out"*; and Deuteronomy 16:1 says *"Yahweh your God brought you out of Egypt
by night"* (WEB, quoted on [[The Passover]]). But 12:41 and 12:51 say *"that same day"*, and Numbers 33:3
gives the date and the hour: *"on the fifteenth day of the first month; on the next day after the
Passover, the children of Israel went out with a high hand in the sight of all the Egyptians, while the
Egyptians were burying all their firstborn"* (33:3–4, WEB). **The usual harmonization** is that a day
runs from evening, so that the night of the fourteenth–fifteenth and the following daylight are one day,
and the people were released by night and marched by day. **The text does not state it**, and some readers
take the two datings as different traditions. The wiki records both.

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 12:29–51 runs from the blow to the summary of the departure;
  13:1–16, the consecration of the firstborn, is the next pericope. 12:43–49 is law inside the narrative
  and was left where the text puts it.
- **Places renamed.** Genesis's [[Succoth (Transjordan)]] was created as `Succoth` with a note that Exodus
  12:37 would collide with it; this pass renamed it, fixed its inbound links, and created
  [[Succoth (Egypt)]], using the qualifiers the Genesis page proposed.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** Pharaoh's *"called for Moses"* (12:31) against *"see my face no
  more"* (10:28–29); the servants' bowing foretold (11:8) and not narrated; *"by night"* (12:31, 42;
  Deuteronomy 16:1) against *"that same day"* (12:41, 51) and *"the next day after the Passover"*
  (Numbers 33:3); four hundred thirty years (12:40) against Genesis 15:13's four hundred; the size of the
  six hundred thousand.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** who struck (Yahweh, the destroyer of 12:23, or both); Joseph's
  *bôr* as a deliberate echo; the *šimmurîm* as God's vigil and Israel's; the meaning of *ʾelep*; who the
  mixed multitude were; why bones are not to be broken; which Scripture John 19:36 cites.
- **No new person pages.** The Egyptians are a people, recorded on [[Egypt]]; the mixed multitude is
  unidentified.
