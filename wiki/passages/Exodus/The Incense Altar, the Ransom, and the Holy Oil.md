# The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 30:1–38

## Summary

The tabernacle instructions return to furniture after the ordination of chapter 29. They are given
in five speeches. The first has no new introduction and continues the speech of chapter 29. The
other four each open with *"Yahweh spoke to Moses"* or *"Yahweh said to Moses"* (30:11, 17, 22, 34).

**The incense altar** (30:1–10). A small acacia-wood altar, one cubit square and two cubits high,
with horns of one piece with it, overlaid with pure gold, with a gold molding and rings and poles
for carrying. It stands *"before the veil that is by the ark of the testimony, before the mercy
seat … where I will meet with you"* (30:6). Aaron burns *"incense of sweet spices"* on it every
morning when he tends the lamps and every evening when he lights them, *"a perpetual incense before
Yahweh"* (30:7–8). No *"strange incense"*, burnt offering, meal offering, or drink offering may go
on it (30:9). Once a year Aaron makes atonement on its horns *"with the blood of the sin offering of
atonement"*. *"It is most holy to Yahweh"* (30:10).

**The ransom at the census** (30:11–16). Whenever Israel is counted, each man counted pays
*"a ransom for his soul to Yahweh … that there be no plague among them"* (30:12). The amount is
half a shekel by the sanctuary shekel, from everyone twenty years old and upward. *"The rich shall
not give more, and the poor shall not give less"* (30:15). The *"atonement money"* goes to *"the
service of the Tent of Meeting"*, as *"a memorial for the children of Israel before Yahweh, to make
atonement for your souls"* (30:16).

**The basin** (30:17–21). A brass basin on a brass base stands between the tent and the altar, with
water in it. Aaron and his sons wash their hands and feet before entering the tent or approaching
the altar, *"that they not die"*. The phrase is said twice, and the washing is *"a statute forever"*
(30:20–21).

**The holy anointing oil** (30:22–33). Moses takes liquid myrrh, fragrant cinnamon, fragrant cane,
and cassia, by weight in sanctuary shekels, and a hin of olive oil, and makes *"a holy anointing
oil, a perfume compounded after the art of the perfumer"* (30:23–25). With it he anoints the tent,
the ark, the table, the lampstand, the incense altar, the altar of burnt offering, and the basin,
so that they become *"most holy. Whatever touches them shall be holy"* (30:26–29). He anoints Aaron
and his sons (30:30). Israel is told that the oil is not to be poured *"on man's flesh"* or copied.
Whoever makes any like it, or puts it *"on a stranger"*, *"shall be cut off from his people"*
(30:31–33).

**The incense** (30:34–38). Equal weights of sweet spices, gum resin, onycha, galbanum, and pure
frankincense, *"seasoned with salt, pure and holy"*. Some is beaten very small and put *"before the
testimony in the Tent of Meeting, where I will meet with you"*. It too is *"most holy"* and may not
be made for private use. Whoever makes it *"to smell of it"* is cut off (30:34–38).

As with chapters 25–29, everything is commanded and nothing is yet done. The making is reported in
chapters 37–38 and the anointing commanded again at 40:9–15.

## People

- [[God]] — gives all five instructions; twice names the place *"where I will meet with you"*
  (30:6, 36), and sets the census ransom *"that there be no plague among them"* (30:12).
- [[Moses]] — is told to make the altar, take the atonement money, set up the basin, and compound
  the oil and the incense himself (*"take to yourself"*, 30:23, 34); he anoints the tent, its
  furniture, and the priests (30:26–30).
- [[Aaron]] — burns the incense morning and evening, makes atonement on the altar's horns once a
  year, washes at the basin, and is anointed with the holy oil (30:7–10, 19, 30).
- [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]], [[Abihu]], [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]], and [[Ithamar]] — included as
  *"his sons"*, not by name: they wash at the basin *"that they not die"* and are anointed with the
  holy oil (30:19–21, 30).

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — Sinai, by the continuing setting of chapters 24–31. The instructions locate everything
  relative to the tent: the incense altar *"before the veil"* (30:6), the basin *"between the Tent of
  Meeting and the altar"* (30:18).

## Themes

- [[Atonement]] — the root *kpr* six times as verb or *kippurîm*, besides *kōper*: on the altar's
  horns, and in the census money that atones *"for your souls"* (30:10, 12, 15–16, MAM).
- [[Prayer]] — incense morning and evening at the place of meeting; the later scriptural reading of
  incense as prayer (Psalm 141:2; Revelation 8:3–4).
- [[Redemption]] — *kōper napšô*, *the ransom of his soul*, paid for every man counted (30:12).
- [[Holiness]] — *"most holy"* three times (30:10, 29, 36); holiness passed by touch (30:29); oil and
  incense that may not be copied.
- [[Priesthood]] — the incense service, the yearly atonement on the incense altar, washing before
  service, and the anointing of the priests.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — an altar on which no burnt, meal, or drink offering may be made; the
  half shekel called *"an offering to Yahweh"* (30:13).
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — hands and feet washed at the basin before every approach (30:19–21).
- [[Life and Death]] — *"that they not die"* (30:20, 21); *"cut off from his people"* (30:33, 38);
  *"no plague"* at the census (30:12).
- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — *"The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give

- [[Remembrance]] — the atonement money as *"a memorial for the children of Israel before
  Yahweh"* (30:16).
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — *"where I will meet with you"* (30:6, 36).
- [[Blood]] — the blood of the sin offering on the incense altar's horns once a year (30:10).

## Notes

### Five speeches, and the first one is long

The speech that began *"Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying"* at 25:1 runs without a new introduction
through the ark, the tent, the altar, the garments, the ordination, and the incense altar, and
ends at 30:10. Only then does the formula return, four times in quick succession (30:11, 17, 22,
34), and twice more in chapter 31 (31:1, 12). The paragraph markers in MAM (Sefaria) fall at the
same places, after 30:10, 16, 21, 33, and 38. This is a description of how the text is divided,
not a theory of how it was composed.

### Why is the incense altar here?

The table and the lampstand are described in chapter 25 and placed in chapter 26 (26:35), but the
third piece of holy-place furniture is left out of both and appears only now, after the priests are
dressed and ordained. The text gives no reason. Readers have suggested that the incense altar is
held back because its use belongs with the priestly service just established, or that it closes
the instructions on the centre of daily worship; critical scholars often take its placement as a
sign that the passage was added to an earlier sequence. None of these is stated by Exodus, and the
wiki records the fact of the placement only.

### Where the altar stands

30:6 puts the altar *"before the veil that is by the ark of the testimony, before the mercy seat"*,
and 40:26 (local WEB) has it *"in the Tent of Meeting before the veil"*. That is outside the most
holy place, with the table and lampstand. Hebrews 9:3–4 (verified WEB) lists *"a golden altar of
incense"* in the description of the Holy of Holies. **These two statements are recorded as they
stand.** Common resolutions are that Hebrews describes the altar's liturgical association with the
inner room — its incense entered there on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:12–13, verified WEB) —
or that the Greek word in Hebrews means a censer, which is how some older English versions render
it. The Greek of Hebrews 9:4 was not checked against an edition in this pass, so the second
resolution is reported, not endorsed.

### A perpetual incense beside a continual offering

The incense is *"a perpetual incense"* (30:8). In MAM the phrase is *qəṭōret tāmîd*, the same
*tāmîd* as the *ʿōlat tāmîd*, *continual burnt offering*, of 29:42. The lamb on the outer altar and
the incense on the inner altar are both offered morning and evening. The incense is tied to the
lamps: burned *"when he tends the lamps"* and *"when Aaron lights the lamps at evening"* (30:7–8).
40:26–27 (local WEB) has Moses himself burn the first incense, as Moses performs the ordination in
chapter 29.

### Strange incense

*"You shall offer no strange incense on it"* (30:9): *qəṭōret zārâ*. The adjective *zār* recurs at
30:33, where the oil may not be put *"on a stranger"*. In Leviticus 10:1 (MAM; verified WEB), Nadab
and Abihu put incense on fire in their censers and offer *ʾēš zārâ*, *"strange fire"*, *"which he
had not commanded them"*, and are consumed. Exodus does not say what makes incense *strange*; the
verse names only what is excluded. See
[[Strange Incense - Exodus 30.9, Leviticus 10, Numbers 16, and Uzziah]].

### Atonement for the altar, once a year

*"Aaron shall make atonement on its horns once in the year; with the blood of the sin offering of
atonement"* (30:10). In MAM: *wəkipper … ʾaḥat baššānâ middam ḥaṭṭaʾt hakkippurîm*. *Hakkippurîm*,
*the atonements*, is the form in *yôm hakkippurîm* of Leviticus 23:27 (MAM), which
the WEB renders *"the day of atonement"* (23:27–28, verified WEB). Leviticus 16:18 (verified WEB) has the high priest *"go out to the
altar that is before Yahweh"* and put blood on its horns. Whether that altar is the incense altar or
the altar of burnt offering is debated, and this pass does not decide it. Exodus 30:10 itself is the
first annual rite in the tabernacle instructions.

### A ransom at the census

The census paragraph uses three words already in the wiki. *"When you take a census"* is
*kî tiśśāʾ ʾet-rōʾš*, *when you lift up the head* (30:12, MAM), the idiom of
[[Lift Up Your Head - One Idiom and Two Verdicts]]. *"A ransom for his soul"* is *kōper napšô*, the
*kōper* of 21:30 and of [[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]]. And
*"atonement money"* is *kesep hakkippurîm* (30:16), from the same root as *kōper*. The paragraph
calls the payment ransom, offering, and atonement in turn (30:12, 13, 15–16).

The text says the payment prevents *"plague"* (*negep*, MAM) *"when you count them"*. It does not
say why counting is dangerous. 2 Samuel 24 (verified WEB) narrates a census of David followed by
*"a pestilence on Israel"* (24:15), and many readers connect the two passages. 2 Samuel 24 does not
mention the half shekel or Exodus 30, so the connection is an inference. See
[[The Half Shekel - Census, Ransom, and the Temple Tax]].

### The rich and the poor pay the same

*"The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less"* (30:15). Every life counted is
ransomed at the same price. That contrasts with the sanctuary offering of 25:2, which was
*"from everyone whose heart makes him willing"* and had no amount. The equal sum is a statement
about souls, not about means. Exodus 38:25–28 (local WEB) shows what the silver became: the sockets
under the frames and the veil, and the hooks and fillets of the court. The house of God stands, in
the most literal sense, on the ransom of every man in Israel.

The money is given to *"the service of the Tent of Meeting"* (30:16). In MAM the noun is *ʿăbōdat*,
the *ʿăbōdâ* of 1:14 (MAM), where Egypt made Israel's lives bitter *baʿăbōdâ qāšâ*, *"in hard
service"*. The [[Exodus]] overview reads the book through this word. Here Israel pays for a
*service* of a different master.

### Washing that they not die

The priests wash *hands and feet* at the basin (30:19). The ordination washing of 29:4 was of the
whole person, once; this is a washing repeated at each approach. *"That they not die"* is said twice
(30:20–21), as death was warned of for the bells and the breeches in chapter 28 (28:35, 43). The basin is made from
*"the mirrors of the ministering women"* (38:8, local WEB), a detail chapter 30 does not anticipate.
40:31 (local WEB) includes Moses among those who wash.

### The anointing oil and the anointed

The oil's verb is *māšaḥ* (*ûmāšaḥtā*, 30:26; *timšāḥ*, 30:30, MAM). The adjective *māšîaḥ*,
*anointed*, is used of the priest in Leviticus 4:3, *hakkōhēn hammāšîaḥ*, *"the anointed priest"*
(MAM; verified WEB). This is the word later used of kings, and the one behind *Messiah*. In Exodus
30 the anointing is of furniture and priests. The text does not extend it to a king or a coming
figure, and the wiki notes the word history without reading it back into this chapter.

The oil makes things *"most holy"*, and *"whatever touches them shall be holy"* (30:29), the same
language used of the altar at 29:37. But it may not be put *"on man's flesh"* (30:32) or on
*"a stranger"* (30:33). Holiness here is not a quality that anyone may acquire by using the right
materials: the same recipe, made privately, is a capital offence (*"cut off from his people"*,
30:33, 38). Genesis 17:14 (local WEB) used the same penalty for the uncircumcised male.

### The offering brought — Exodus 35:1–29

The incense altar, anointing oil, incense, basin, and base enter the public work list
(35:15–16); rulers supply spice and oil (35:28). The willing gifts of 35:21–29 are distinct
from the fixed half-shekel requirement of 30:11–16, although both support sanctuary service.

See [[The Freewill Offering]]. Comparison checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:25–29

The incense altar's dimensions, integral horns, gold overlay, rings, and poles are made as
specified in 30:1–5. The oil and incense recipes of 30:22–38 become the concise making report
of 37:29, without repeating their ingredients or prohibitions. No anointing or incense burning
is narrated here; those are distinct from manufacture. The basin and the use of census silver
remain in chapter 38's later unit. See [[The Furnishings Made]].
