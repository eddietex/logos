# I Am Yahweh

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 6:2–13

## Summary

**God answers Moses' complaint with his name.** At the end of [[Bricks Without Straw]], [[Moses]]
accused God of sending him for nothing: *"you have not rescued your people at all"* (5:23). God's
reply, begun at 6:1, continues here as a single speech that opens and closes with the same three
words — *"I am Yahweh"* (6:2, 8) — and says them four times in seven verses (6:2, 6, 7, 8). It looks
back: God *appeared* to [[Abraham]], [[Isaac]], and [[Jacob]] *"as God Almighty"*, and *"by my name
Yahweh I was not known to them"* (6:3). He *established* his covenant to give them [[Canaan]] (6:4).
He *has heard* the groaning and *has remembered* the covenant (6:5). Then it looks forward, in seven
first-person verbs: *I will bring you out*, *I will rid you*, *I will redeem you*, *I will take you*,
*I will be to you a God*, *I will bring you into the land*, and *I will give it to you* (6:6–8). The
whole thing is to end in Israel's knowing: *"you shall know that I am Yahweh your God"* (6:7).

**Moses delivers it, and nobody listens.** *"They didn't listen to Moses for anguish of spirit, and
for cruel bondage"* (6:9). The people who *"believed"* at 4:31 do not hear the fullest promise in
the book so far. Yahweh then sends Moses back to Pharaoh (6:10–11), and Moses objects by arguing
from the lesser case to the greater: *"Behold, the children of Israel haven't listened to me. How
then shall Pharaoh listen to me, who am of uncircumcised lips?"* (6:12). **God does not answer the
objection.** He gives a command to Moses *and Aaron* together, to the people and to Pharaoh, *"to
bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt"* (6:13). Then the narrative stops for a
genealogy (6:14–27), and when it resumes (6:28–30) Moses is raising the same objection in nearly the
same words.

### The shape of it

| | verses | what is said | turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6:2 | *"I am Yahweh"* | the name |
| 2 | 6:3–5 | appeared, established, heard, remembered | **the past**: what God has done |
| 3 | 6:6a | *"I am Yahweh"* | the name |
| 4 | 6:6b–8a | bring out, rid, redeem, take, be, bring in, give | **the future**: what God will do |
| 5 | 6:8b | *"I am Yahweh"* | the name |
| 6 | 6:9 | *"they didn't listen to Moses"* | Israel refuses |
| 7 | 6:10–11 | *"Go in, speak to Pharaoh"* | the commission repeated |
| 8 | 6:12 | *"How then shall Pharaoh listen to me?"* | Moses objects |
| 9 | 6:13 | *"gave them a command … to bring the children of Israel out"* | the command, unexplained |

**The speech is built around the name.** The name stands first, in the middle, and last, and
everything between is something God has done or will do. At 6:7 the name and the promise become one
sentence, *"I am Yahweh your God, who brings you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians"*. That
is the knowledge the exodus is meant to produce. **The narrative that follows is built around
listening.** The verb *šāmaʿ* runs through it: God *has heard* (6:5); Israel *did not hear* (6:9);
Israel *has not heard me* and Pharaoh *will not hear me* (6:12).

## Key verses

### The name, and the patriarchs (6:2–5)

> **2** God spoke to Moses, and said to him, "I am Yahweh;
> **3** and I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, as God Almighty; but by my name Yahweh I
> was not known to them.

***"God spoke … 'I am Yahweh'"*** — the narrator says ***ʾĕlōhîm***, *God*, and the first word God
speaks is the name. ***ʾĂnî YHWH*** is a complete clause, with no verb and nothing after it. It is the
self-introduction of Genesis 15:7 (*"I am Yahweh who brought you out of Ur of the Chaldees"*) and
Genesis 28:13 (*"I am Yahweh, the God of Abraham your father"*). From this chapter on, it is the
signature of the law.

***"I appeared"*** — ***wāʾērāʾ***, the niphal of *rāʾâ*, *was seen*. **It answers the fear Moses
voiced at the bush.** He said the people would say ***"Yahweh has not appeared to you"*** (4:1,
*lōʾ-nirʾâ*, the same verb and stem). Here God lists the men he did appear to. It is also the verb
Genesis uses for God's appearing to the patriarchs (Genesis 12:7; 17:1; 26:2, 24; 35:9). Jacob
quoted it at 48:3: *"God Almighty appeared to me at Luz"*.

***"As God Almighty"*** — ***bəʾēl šadday***. In Genesis the name occurs six times (17:1; 28:3;
35:11; 43:14; 48:3; and *šadday* alone at 49:25). The occurrences cluster in the speeches of promise:
fruitfulness, a nation, kings, and the land. Exodus 6:3 is its only occurrence in Exodus.
(Occurrences counted in the Westminster Leningrad Codex.) See
[[God Almighty - El Shaddai in Genesis and Exodus]].

***"By my name Yahweh I was not known to them"*** — ***ûšəmî YHWH lōʾ nôdaʿtî lāhem***.
**Genesis, as the wiki has ingested it, does not obviously agree.** In Genesis God introduces himself
to Abram as *"I am Yahweh"* (15:7) and to Jacob in the same words (28:13). Abram swears by the name
(14:22, *"I have lifted up my hand to Yahweh"*). And Genesis 4:26 puts the calling on *"Yahweh's
name"* ten generations before Abraham. **The wiki records the tension instead of resolving it.** There
are three standing readings:

1. **Experiential.** *Yādaʿ* means to know by experience. The patriarchs had the word but had not
   seen the name *done*, meaning God's keeping of covenant by delivering a nation. The verse itself
   points this way, because its whole second half (6:6–8) is about what Israel is about to *know*.
2. **Source-critical.** This is the Priestly writer's account of the revelation of the name, which
   is consistent with that source's own usage in Genesis (*ʾĕlōhîm* and *ʾĒl Šadday*). It is
   inconsistent with the strand that uses *Yahweh* from Genesis 2 onward. Read this way, the
   chapter is a second telling of the call in Exodus 3.
3. **Interrogative.** The verse can be read as a question, *"and by my name Yahweh was I not known
   to them?"* That reading has defenders but no interrogative particle in the text.

See [[Calling on the Name of Yahweh]] and [[The Names of God]].

> **4** I have also established my covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land of
> their travels, in which they lived as aliens.
> **5** Moreover I have heard the groaning of the children of Israel, whom the Egyptians keep in
> bondage, and I have remembered my covenant.

***"I have also established my covenant"*** — ***hăqimōtî ʾet-bərîtî***, the hiphil of *qûm*. It is
the verb of Genesis 17:7 (*"I will establish my covenant"*, *wahăqimōtî*), and before that of the
covenant with Noah. **The covenant in view is the one made at Genesis 17, the chapter where God named
himself *ʾĒl Šadday*.**

***"The land of their travels, in which they lived as aliens"*** — ***ʾereṣ məgurêhem ʾăšer-gārû
bāh***. *Məgûrîm*, *sojournings*, comes from *gûr*, *to sojourn as an alien*. **It is a Genesis word
for the patriarchs' life in the land**: *"the land where you are traveling"* (17:8, God to Abraham);
28:4 (Isaac's blessing on Jacob); 36:7; 37:1 (*"the land of his father's travels"*); and Jacob before
Pharaoh, *"the days of the years of my pilgrimage"* (47:9, twice). **The land is described by the
fact that the fathers never owned it.** See [[Land and Sojourning]].

***"I have heard the groaning … and I have remembered my covenant"*** — this repeats 2:24 almost
exactly: *"God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant"*. ***Naʾăqâ***, *groaning*,
occurs only at these two verses in the books the wiki has reached (WLC lemma search of Genesis,
Exodus, and Job). At 2:24 the narrator reports what God heard. **At 6:5 God says it himself, to
Moses, three chapters and one failed audience later.** See
[[God Remembered - Noah, Abraham, Rachel, and Israel]] and [[Remembrance]].

*"Whom the Egyptians keep in bondage"* — ***maʿăbidîm***, the causative of *ʿābad*, *serve*. It is
the stem used at 1:13, where the Egyptians *"ruthlessly made [them] serve"*. **Every *ʿābad* in the
speech belongs to Egypt.** The promised verb *serve*, with God as its object (3:12; 4:23), is not in
it.

### Seven verbs (6:6–8)

> **6** Therefore tell the children of Israel, 'I am Yahweh, and I will bring you out from under the
> burdens of the Egyptians, and I will rid you out of their bondage, and I will redeem you with an
> outstretched arm, and with great judgments:
> **7** and I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God; and you shall know that I
> am Yahweh your God, who brings you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians.
> **8** I will bring you into the land which I swore to give to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob; and I
> will give it to you for a heritage: I am Yahweh.'"

**Seven first-person promises, each a *wəqāṭal* verb with God as subject:**

| | Hebrew | WEB | what it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *wəhôṣēʾtî* | *I will bring you out* | 3:10, *"that you may bring my people … out"*, the task Moses doubted (3:11) |
| 2 | *wəhiṣṣaltî* | *I will rid you* | 5:23, *"you have not rescued (hiṣṣaltā) your people at all"* |
| 3 | *wəgāʾaltî* | *I will redeem you* | new |
| 4 | *wəlāqaḥtî* | *I will take you to me for a people* | new |
| 5 | *wəhāyîtî* | *I will be to you a God* | Genesis 17:7–8 |
| 6 | *wəhēbēʾtî* | *I will bring you into the land* | 3:8, 17 |
| 7 | *wənātattî* | *I will give it to you* | 6:4, *"to give them the land of Canaan"* |

**The second verb answers Moses' accusation in its own word.** Moses said *wəhaṣṣēl lōʾ-hiṣṣaltā*,
*"you have not rescued at all"* (5:23). God answers *wəhiṣṣaltî*, *"I will rescue"*. The WEB's *"rid
you out"* hides the echo. It is the verb God used at the bush, *ləhaṣṣîlô*, *"to deliver them"*
(3:8). (Forms checked in the WLC.)

***"From under the burdens of the Egyptians"*** — ***mittaḥat siblōt miṣrayim***, twice (6:6, 7).
**This answers Pharaoh.** He sent Moses and Aaron back with *"Get back to your burdens!"*,
*ləsiblōtêkem* (5:4). The same noun named the oppression at 1:11 and what Moses *"looked at"* at 2:11.

***"I will redeem you"*** — ***gāʾal***. The verb belongs to family law: the next of kin who buys back
a relative's land or freedom and takes up his cause. **Among the books the wiki has reached it occurs
once before this verse**, in Jacob's blessing of Joseph's sons: *"the angel who has redeemed me from
all evil"* (Genesis 48:16, *haggōʾēl*). Its next occurrence is the Song of the Sea: *"the people that
you have redeemed"* (15:13). (WLC lemma search of Genesis, Exodus, and Job.) **The verb puts God in
the place of Israel's kinsman.** See [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]].

***"With an outstretched arm"*** — ***bizrôaʿ nəṭûyâ***. This is the first occurrence of the phrase
in canonical order. Deuteronomy uses it often, usually paired with *"a mighty hand"*, as its
shorthand for the exodus; that later usage is cited from memory and not checked here. In Exodus the
*arm* comes back once more, in the song: *"By the greatness of your arm they are as still as a
stone"* (15:16).

***"With great judgments"*** — ***uḇišpāṭîm gədōlîm***. *Šəpāṭîm*, *acts of judgment*, occurs three
times in Exodus (6:6; 7:4; 12:12; WLC). 7:4 repeats *"by great judgments"*, and the third occurrence
names the defendant: ***"Against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am Yahweh"***
(12:12). **The officers asked Yahweh to *judge* Moses** (5:21, *wəyišpōṭ*). **He will judge, but
Egypt and its gods.** See [[Judgment]].

***"I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God"*** — ***wəlāqaḥtî ʾetkem lî
ləʿām wəhāyîtî lākem lēʾlōhîm***. **This is the covenant formula, said for the first time to a
people rather than a family.** Its first half is new. Its second half is Genesis 17:7–8, *"to be a God
to you and to your offspring after you"* and *"I will be their God"*, where *wəhāyîtî lāhem
lēʾlōhîm* (17:8) differs from 6:7 only in its pronoun. *Lāqaḥ*, *take*, is the ordinary verb for
taking a wife, as in Genesis 4:19, 11:29, and 24:67. Some readers hear a marriage here. The text does
not draw that image, and the wiki only notes that the verb permits it. See
[[I Will Take You as My People - The Covenant Formula]] and [[Covenant]].

***"And you shall know that I am Yahweh your God"*** — ***wîdaʿtem kî ʾănî YHWH ʾĕlōhêkem***. **This
is the answer to Pharaoh's *"I don't know Yahweh"* (5:2), and the first time the refrain is said to
Israel.** From 7:5 on it is said of Egypt: *"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"*. 6:3 said
the fathers did not *know* the name. 6:7 says the children will. See
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

***"Which I swore"*** — ***nāśāʾtî ʾet-yādî***, literally *"I lifted my hand"*. **It is the first
use of *nāśāʾ yād* as an oath formula in the books the wiki has reached.** Abram's oath at Genesis
14:22 uses a different verb for the same gesture, *hărîmōtî yādî*, *"I have lifted up my hand to
Yahweh"*. **At 14:22 a man lifted his hand to God. At 6:8 God says he lifted his own.** (Lemma
search of the WLC of Genesis, Exodus, and Job.) See [[Oaths and Vows]].

***"For a heritage"*** — ***môrāšâ***, *a possession*, from *yāraš*, *to inherit* or *to dispossess*.
It occurs only here in Genesis, Exodus, and Job (WLC lemma search). Genesis 15:7 uses the same root
for the same land: *"to give you this land to inherit it"*, *lərištāh*.

### No one listens (6:9)

> **9** Moses spoke so to the children of Israel, but they didn't listen to Moses for anguish of
> spirit, and for cruel bondage.

**This is the worst news in the pericope, and it takes one verse.** The promise of 6:6–8 is the
fullest in the book so far, and it goes unheard. The reason given is not unbelief but exhaustion.
***Miqqōṣer rûaḥ***, *"for anguish of spirit"*, is literally *from shortness of spirit*: breath, or
patience, cut short. It is the only occurrence of the noun *qōṣer* in Genesis, Exodus, and Job (WLC
lemma search). ***Uméʿăbōdâ qāšâ***, *"and for cruel bondage"*, is the *ʿăbōdâ qāšâ*, *"hard
service"*, of 1:14. **The oppression of chapter 1 is now what stops Israel's ears.**

**The order of the last two chapters is reversed.** At the bush God said, *"They will listen to your
voice"* (3:18). Moses feared, *"they will not believe me, nor listen to my voice"* (4:1). At 4:31 God
was proved right and Moses wrong: *"The people believed"*. **After the bricks, Moses' fear comes
true.** In 5:2 it was Pharaoh who would not *listen to his voice*. Now Israel will not listen either.
The text does not blame them. It gives their reason, and the reason is Pharaoh's work. See
[[Faith]] and [[Suffering]].

### Uncircumcised lips (6:10–13)

> **10** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,
> **11** "Go in, speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt, that he let the children of Israel go out of his land."
> **12** Moses spoke before Yahweh, saying, "Behold, the children of Israel haven't listened to me. How
> then shall Pharaoh listen to me, who am of uncircumcised lips?"

**God's reply to a people who will not listen is to send Moses back to the king who will not either.**
The commission of 6:11 uses ***wîšallaḥ***, *let go*, the *šālaḥ* that 5:1–6:1 turned on.

**Moses' objection is a lesser-to-greater argument.** If his own people, to whom he brought good
news, have not listened, Pharaoh, to whom he brings a demand, will not either. **Its opening, *hēn*,
*"behold"*, is the *wəhēn* of 4:1.** The fear he voiced there now looks proved.

***"Who am of uncircumcised lips"*** — ***waʾănî ʿăral śəpātāyim***. This is a new image for the
complaint of 4:10, *"I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue"*, literally *heavy of mouth and heavy
of tongue*. ***ʿĀrēl***, *uncircumcised*, occurs in Genesis, Exodus, and Job only at Genesis 17:14,
Exodus 6:12, 6:30, and 12:48 (WLC lemma search). Both legal uses are about exclusion: the
uncircumcised male is *"cut off from his people. He has broken my covenant"* (Genesis 17:14), and
*"no uncircumcised person shall eat of"* the Passover (12:48). **Moses describes his lips with the
covenant's word for what is shut out.** This comes one chapter after Zipporah's flint, where a son of
his was circumcised to save his life (4:24–26). It is the first time Scripture applies *uncircumcised*
to a part of the body other than the flesh. Later books apply it to ears and hearts. See
[[Uncircumcised Lips, Ears, and Heart]] and [[Circumcision]].

> **13** Yahweh spoke to Moses and to Aaron, and gave them a command to the children of Israel, and to
> Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt.

**God does not answer the objection.** At the bush he answered every one, angrily at the last
(4:14). Here he restates the command. The difference is that it now has two addressees: ***"Yahweh
spoke to Moses and to Aaron"***, *ʾel-mōšeh wəʾel-ʾahărōn*. **It is the first time in Scripture that
Yahweh is said to speak to the two of them together** (WLC search of Exodus for the pair). Aaron has
been beside Moses since 4:27 and has spoken for him, but God had spoken to him only once, alone
(4:27). From here on, *"Moses and Aaron"* receive the word as a pair.

***"Gave them a command"*** — ***wayəṣawwēm***, from *ṣāwâ*. The commission becomes an order to be
carried *to* two parties, *to the children of Israel* and *to Pharaoh*. Both have just refused to
listen. The verse is a summary, not a speech: the narrator says that a command was given and what it
was for, *"to bring the children of Israel out"*, but quotes nothing.

### Where the pericope stops

**The story halts at 6:13 and does not resume until 6:28.** Between them stands the genealogy of
6:14–27, which begins as a list of *"the heads of their fathers'
houses"* for Reuben and Simeon, settles on Levi, and ends on Aaron and Moses: *"These are that Aaron
and Moses"* (6:26). 6:28–30 then repeats 6:10–12 almost word for word: *"I am Yahweh. Speak to
Pharaoh … Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh listen to me?"* (6:29–30).
**The repeated objection works like a bracket.** The narrative pauses at Moses' doubt, answers *who
is this man?* with a family tree, and picks up the doubt where it left off. The wiki ingests 6:14–27
and 6:28–7:13 as their own pericopes, as the book plan already draws them.

### Two commissions

**Exodus 6:2–13 and Exodus 3:1–4:17 tell the same thing twice**: a revelation of the name, a recital
of the patriarchs, God hearing and knowing the affliction, the promise to bring Israel out and into
Canaan, a commission to Pharaoh, and Moses' objection that he cannot speak. The differences matter
as much. Ch. 3 has a bush, a mountain, signs, and five objections answered. Ch. 6 has none of
these: no place, no sign, no answer. It has more about the covenant, and the verbs *redeem*, *take*,
and *know*. Source criticism reads the two as parallel accounts of one call. Read in sequence, ch.
6 is a renewal after failure: the same promise said again to a man and a people for whom the first
telling has gone wrong. The wiki keeps both readings. See
[[Two Commissions - Exodus 3 and Exodus 6]].

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** 6:2 begins with a new introduction (*"God spoke to Moses"*) after
  6:1 closes the complaint of 5:22–23. 6:13 is a summary, and 6:14 begins the genealogy. The repeat
  of 6:10–12 at 6:28–30 confirms that the narrative pauses at 6:13.
- **The tension between 6:3 and Genesis is recorded, not resolved.** Genesis 15:7 and 28:13 have God
  say *"I am Yahweh"* to Abram and to Jacob, against *"by my name Yahweh I was not known to them"*.
  Three readings are listed above. The wiki adopts none, consistent with
  [[God Almighty - El Shaddai in Genesis and Exodus]] and [[Calling on the Name of Yahweh]].
- **The marriage reading of *lāqaḥ* (6:7)** is noted as something the verb permits, not something
  the text asserts.
- **Rabbinic tradition** connects the four cups of the Passover meal with the verbs of 6:6–7
