# Bitter Water at Marah

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 15:22–27

## Summary

**The Song ended on *"Yahweh shall reign forever and ever"* (15:18); three days later there is nothing to
drink.** Six verses take Israel from the shore of the sea to an oasis, and in between they hold the first
crisis of the wilderness, the first complaint, the first wilderness miracle, the first statute given to
Israel as a people, the first test of Israel, and the first time God names himself by what he does for
Israel's body: ***"I am Yahweh who heals you"*** (15:26).

The pericope moves in four steps:

- **The march and the thirst (15:22).** *"Moses led Israel onward from the Red Sea"* — the first itinerary
  verb of the wilderness — into *"the wilderness of Shur"*, and *"they went three days in the wilderness, and
  found no water."*
- **Marah (15:23–25a).** Water, but bitter; the place named for it; the people *"murmured against Moses"*;
  Moses *"cried to Yahweh"*; Yahweh *"showed him a tree"*; Moses *"threw it into the waters, and the waters
  were made sweet."*
- **The statute and the test (15:25b–26).** *"There he made a statute and an ordinance for them, and there
  he tested them"*, and a conditional promise: listen, do what is right, heed, keep — and *"I will put none
  of the diseases on you, which I have put on the Egyptians."*
- **Elim (15:27).** *"Twelve springs of water, and seventy palm trees: and they encamped there by the
  waters."*

**It is the first of a pattern the rest of the Torah repeats**: need, complaint against Moses, Moses' cry,
God's provision, and a lesson attached. Manna and quail (16), water from the rock (17:1–7), and Numbers 11,
14, 16–17, 20, and 21 all run some version of it. **Marah is the mildest of them.** No anger of God is
reported, no one is punished, Moses does not rebuke the people, and the complaint is a question —
*"What shall we drink?"* — not yet the wish to have died in Egypt that 16:3 will voice. See [[Murmuring]].

**And the place is named for the water, not for the miracle.** *"Therefore its name was called Marah"*
(15:23), *bitter*. The water was made sweet, and the name stayed bitter. Numbers 33:8 keeps the name in the
itinerary — *"encamped in Marah"* — with no word of the sweetening (WEB; *not yet ingested*).

**The Song sang *"You have guided them (nēhaltā)"* (15:13), the piel of *nāhal*, *lead to water*** (see the
note there on [[The Song of the Sea]]). **The first guidance after it is three days with no water at
all.** The pericope does not comment on the distance between the Song's perfects and the people's thirst;
it reports both.

**Three days is the distance Moses asked for.** *"Please let us go three days' journey into the wilderness,
that we may sacrifice to Yahweh, our God"* (3:18; again 5:3; 8:27). **The three days arrive, and there is no
sacrifice in them — only thirst.** The text does not draw the line; the number is the same.

## Key verses

### Three days, and no water (15:22)

> **22** Moses led Israel onward from the Red Sea, and they went out into the wilderness of Shur; and they
> went three days in the wilderness, and found no water.

***"Moses led … onward"*** — ***wayyassaʿ***, the hiphil of ***nāsaʿ***, *pull up stakes*, *set out*. **The
qal is the verb of every stage of the march** — *"They took their journey from Succoth"* (13:20), *"They
took their journey from Elim"* (16:1) — and of Abram's journeying in Genesis (12:9) (WLC). **Here it is
causative, with Moses as subject**: he makes Israel set out. It is the first thing Moses does after the
Song.

***"From the Red Sea"*** — ***miyyam-sûp***. See [[The Red Sea]].

***"They went out"*** — ***wayyēṣəʾû***, *yāṣāʾ*, **the exodus verb** (12:41; 13:3; 14:8; WLC), and the verb of
the women going out after Miriam four verses before (15:20).

***"The wilderness of Shur"*** — ***midbar-šûr***. **Shur has appeared three times in Genesis, every time on
the edge of Egypt**: Hagar is found *"by a fountain of water in the wilderness, by the fountain on the way to
Shur"* (Genesis 16:7); Abraham lives *"between Kadesh and Shur"* (20:1); Ishmael's sons range *"from Havilah
to Shur"* (25:18) (WLC lemma search, Torah: these four verses are the only ones). **An Egyptian slave woman
was found at a spring on the road to Shur on her way back to Egypt; Israel, Egypt's slaves, comes out of
Egypt into Shur and finds no water.** See [[Shur]] and [[Affliction and Flight - Hagar and the Exodus]].

**Numbers 33:8 calls the same stretch *"the wilderness of Etham"***: *"They went three days' journey in the
wilderness of Etham, and encamped in Marah"* (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). **The two names stand in two
books; the wiki records both without harmonizing.** See [[Etham]].

***"They went three days"*** — ***šəlōšet yāmîm***. **The number of Abraham's journey to Moriah** — *"On the
third day Abraham lifted up his eyes"* (Genesis 22:4) — **and of the journey Moses asked for** (3:18; 5:3;
8:27). **Both three-day journeys end at a test.** See [[Testing]] and the note at 15:25.

***"And found no water"*** — ***wəlōʾ-māṣəʾû mayim***. **Hagar's water ran out in a wilderness too** — *"The
water in the bottle was spent"* (Genesis 21:15) — and *"God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water"*
(21:19). **There a mother is shown water that was already there; here a man is shown a tree that makes bad
water good.** See [[Hagar]].

### They could not drink (15:23)

> **23** When they came to Marah, they couldn't drink from the waters of Marah, for they were bitter.
> Therefore its name was called Marah.

***"Marah"*** — ***mārâ***, the place-name, **said three times in one verse**: *"to Marah … the waters of
Marah … its name was called Marah"* (WLC). **The adjective between them is *mārîm*, *bitter* (plural, of the
water).** The name is the adjective, made feminine and made a place.

***"Bitter"*** — ***mar***. **The adjective's one use in Genesis is Esau's**: *"he cried with an exceeding
great and bitter cry"* (*ṣəʿāqâ gədōlâ ûmārâ*, Genesis 27:34; WLC lemma search, Torah). **That verse also has
the verb of 15:25 — *ṣāʿaq*, *cry*.** Esau's cry was bitter and was not answered with a blessing; Moses'
cry over bitter water is answered at once. See [[The Stolen Blessing]].

**The root runs from Egypt to here.** The Egyptians *"made their lives bitter (wayəmārərû) with hard
service"* (1:14); Israel ate the Passover *"with bitter herbs (mərōrîm)"* (12:8); and the first water after
the sea is *mārîm* (WLC). **They tasted Egypt's bitterness as a rite the night they left, and meet it again
as water three days out.** The text does not draw the line; the root is the same. See
[[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[The Passover]].

**Numbers 5 is the Torah's other bitter water**: the suspected wife is given *"the water of bitterness that
brings a curse"* (*mê hammārîm*, Numbers 5:18; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*), as a test of what cannot
otherwise be known. **The Torah's two bitter waters are both connected with a test** — this one with the
people's, that one with a woman's. The connection is the wiki's observation, not the text's.

***"Therefore its name was called"*** — ***ʿal-kēn qārāʾ šəmāh***, the impersonal *"one called"*, **the
naming formula of Genesis** — *"Therefore its name was called Babel"* (*ʿal-kēn qārāʾ šəmāh bābel*, 11:9;
WLC). **Who named it is not said.** See [[Names and Naming]].

**Ruth's Naomi will take the name for herself**: *"Don't call me Naomi. Call me Mara; for the Almighty has
dealt very bitterly with me"* (Ruth 1:20; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **The WLC spells her name with a final
ʾālep (*mārāʾ*) and files it under a separate number from the place**; the root of her verb, *hēmar*, is the
*mārar* of 1:14 (WLC). See [[Marah]].

### What shall we drink? (15:24)

> **24** The people murmured against Moses, saying, "What shall we drink?"

***"Murmured"*** — ***wayyillōnû***, the niphal of ***lûn***. **The first murmuring in Scripture.** In the
Torah the verb in this sense appears only in the wilderness — Exodus 15:24; 16:2, 7, 8; 17:3; Numbers 14:2,
27, 29, 36; 16:11; 17:6, 20 (Hebrew numbering) — and **in the narrator's reports its object is always Moses
(or Moses and Aaron), never God; it is Moses and God who name God as the one finally accused** (*"Your
murmurings are not against us, but against Yahweh"*, 16:8; *"this evil congregation, that murmur against
me"*, Numbers 14:27) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The WLC files under the same lemma the
verb *lodge*, *stay the night*** — Lot's *"stay all night"* (Genesis 19:2), Jacob at Bethel, *"he stayed there all night"* (28:11) —
with a sense letter separating the two. **Whether they are one root or two is debated** (reported, not
checked in this pass). See [[Murmuring]].

***"Against Moses"*** — ***ʿal-mōšeh***. **Not yet against Aaron, and not against God by name.** At 16:2 it
will be *"against Moses and against Aaron"*; at 17:3 *"against Moses"* again. **The people believed *"in his
servant Moses"* three days before** (14:31).

***"What shall we drink?"*** — ***mah-nništeh***, two words. **The shortest complaint in the wilderness
cycle**, and the only one that is a plain question of need. At the sea they had asked *"Because there were no
graves in Egypt, have you taken us away to die in the wilderness?"* (14:11); at the manna they will say
*"We wish that we had died by Yahweh's hand in the land of Egypt"* (16:3); at Massah, *"Why have you brought us
up out of Egypt, to kill us, our children, and our livestock with thirst?"* (17:3). **Marah's question has no
Egypt in it.** See [[The Crossing of the Sea]].

### He cried, and Yahweh showed him a tree (15:25a)

> **25** Then he cried to Yahweh. Yahweh showed him a tree, and he threw it into the waters, and the waters
> were made sweet. …

***"He cried to Yahweh"*** — ***wayyiṣʿaq ʾel-YHWH***. **The verb of Moses' first prayer in Exodus**: *"Moses
cried to Yahweh concerning the frogs"* (8:12 WEB; 8:8 Hebrew), and of the people at the sea, *"the children of
Israel cried out to Yahweh"* (14:10), answered with *"Why do you cry to me?"* (14:15) (WLC lemma search). **The
people murmur to Moses; Moses cries to God.** **He does it again at Massah** (17:4) **and for Miriam** — *"Moses
cried to Yahweh, saying, 'Heal her, God, I beg you!'"* (Numbers 12:13; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*): **the
same pair, *ṣāʿaq* and *rāpāʾ*, cry and heal, as Marah.** See [[Prayer]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

***"Yahweh showed him"*** — ***wayyôrēhû***, the hiphil of ***yārâ***. **The WLC files this under the lemma of
*"I will … teach you what you shall speak"* (4:12, 15), of Judah sent *"to show the way"* to Goshen (Genesis
46:28), and of the tablets given *"that you may teach them"* (24:12; [[The Blood of the Covenant]])** — and also of the
Song's *"He has cast (yārâ) Pharaoh's chariots"* (15:4) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **The noun *tôrâ*,
*law*, *instruction*, is usually derived from this verb** (reported, not checked in this pass; the WLC files
*tôrâ* under its own number). **So the first thing God does after the sea is *show*, with the verb of
teaching, in the pericope where he gives the first statute.** See [[Law and Obedience]].

***"A tree"*** — ***ʿēṣ***, *tree* or *wood* (the WEB has *tree*; many English versions *a piece of wood* or
*a log*; the Hebrew does not distinguish). **The word of the trees of the garden** — *"the tree of life … and
the tree of the knowledge of good and evil"* (Genesis 2:9) — **and of the wood Abraham laid on Isaac**
(22:6, 9; WLC). **What kind of tree, and how it worked, is not said.** Readings range from a natural
sweetening agent to a pure sign; the text gives no mechanism (reported, not decided). See
[[The Trees of the Patriarchs]].

***"He threw it into the waters"*** — ***wayyašlēk***, the hiphil of ***šālak***, *throw*, *cast*. **The verb
has been a verb of rejection and of signs**: Hagar *"put"* the child under a shrub to die (*wattašlēk*,
Genesis 21:15), the brothers *"threw"* Joseph into the pit (37:24), Pharaoh's *"You shall cast every son …
into the river"* (1:22), and Moses' rod *"Throw it on the ground"* (4:3; 7:9–12) (WLC lemma search). **Egypt
cast Israel's sons into the water to kill them; Moses casts a tree into the water to make it drinkable.**
**Elisha will do the same thing with the same verb**: *"He went out to the spring of the waters, and threw
salt into it (wayyašlek)"*, and Yahweh says *"I have healed (rippîtî) these waters"* (2 Kings 2:21; WEB; WLC;
*not yet ingested*). See
[[Bitter Water Made Sweet - Marah and Elisha's Spring]].

***"And the waters were made sweet"*** — ***wayyimtəqû hammāyim***. ***Mātaq*, *be sweet*, occurs only here
in the Torah** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The first plague turned the water of Egypt to
blood, so that *"the Egyptians couldn't drink water from the river"*** (7:21; WLC *wəlōʾ-yākəlû*); **at Marah
Israel *"couldn't drink"* (lōʾ yākəlû, 15:23) — the same phrase — and the water is made good.** The first
sign in Egypt spoils water; the first sign in the wilderness heals it. See [[Water Turned to Blood]] and
[[Signs and Wonders]].

### A statute and an ordinance (15:25b)

> **25** … There he made a statute and an ordinance for them, and there he tested them;

***"There … and there"*** — ***šām … wəšām***. **The pericope says *there* three times** (15:25 twice, 15:27
once). Marah is where the statute is made and the test is set; Elim is where the people camp. **The
statute is not given at Sinai first; it is given at the bitter water.**

***"He made"*** — ***śām***, from ***śîm***, *put*, *set*. **Who *he* is is not said.** The nearest subject is
Yahweh (*"Yahweh showed him"*), but Moses threw the tree; most readers take Yahweh, and 15:26 is his speech.
***"For them"*** is ***lô***, *for him*, singular — the people as one (WLC). **The verb recurs twice in the
next verse**: *"the diseases … which I have put (śamtî) on the Egyptians"* and *"I will put (ʾāśîm) none … on
you"* (WLC). **God sets a statute for Israel and sets diseases on Egypt, with the same verb.**

***"A statute"*** — ***ḥōq***. **Its first statute in Scripture is Joseph's, in Egypt, for Pharaoh**:
*"Joseph made it a statute (wayyāśem … ləḥōq) concerning the land of Egypt to this day, that Pharaoh should
have the fifth"* (Genesis 47:26; also 47:22) — **the same verb, *śîm*, with the same noun.** **And its first
use in Exodus is the brick quota**: *"Why haven't you fulfilled your quota (ḥoqkem) … in making brick?"*
(5:14) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **Israel's first *ḥōq* was a quota of bricks under Pharaoh; the
first after the sea is set by Yahweh.** The content of this one is not given. See [[Joseph and the Famine]],
[[Bricks Without Straw]], and [[Law and Obedience]].

***"And an ordinance"*** — ***ûmišpāṭ***, *judgment*, *justice*, *ordinance*. **Its Genesis uses are Abraham's
charge** — *"to do righteousness and justice (mišpāṭ)"* (Genesis 18:19) — **his question to God** — *"Shouldn't
the Judge of all the earth do right (mišpāṭ)?"* (18:25) — and the cupbearer restored to serve *"the way
you did when you were his cup bearer"* (*kammišpāṭ*, 40:13) (WLC lemma search). **The pair *ḥōq ûmišpāṭ* is
its first occurrence**; the plurals,
*statutes and ordinances*, become the refrain of Deuteronomy (4:1, 5; 5:1; WLC lemma search; *not yet
ingested*). **The *mišpāṭîm* of 21:1 are the next ordinances in Exodus** (*not yet ingested*).

**What the statute and ordinance were is not said.** Readers have taken 15:26 as their content, or the
Sabbath (which 16:23–30 introduces), or some instruction not recorded (reported, not decided). The text
gives a name and no content, and then a condition.

***"There he tested them"*** — ***wəšām nissāhû***, the piel of ***nāsâ***, again with a singular *him*.
**The verb's first occurrence is *"God tested Abraham"*** (Genesis 22:1); **this is its second in Scripture,
and the first time Israel is tested** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). It then comes four times in
the next five chapters: manna *"that I may test them, whether they will walk in my law, or not"* (16:4);
Israel testing God at Massah (17:2, 7); and Sinai, *"God has come to test you"* (20:20). **Abraham was tested
with a son on the third day; Israel with water on the third day.** **What the test measured is stated in the
next verse, in the form of a condition.** See [[Testing]] and [[The Binding of Isaac]].

### I am Yahweh who heals you (15:26)

> **26** and he said, "If you will diligently listen to Yahweh your God's voice, and will do that which is
> right in his eyes, and will pay attention to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, I will put none
> of the diseases on you, which I have put on the Egyptians; for I am Yahweh who heals you."

**Four conditions, one promise, and a name.** The conditions are *listen*, *do*, *pay attention*, *keep*;
their objects are *voice*, *what is right*, *commandments*, *statutes* (WLC). **The first law speech to Israel
after the sea is not a list of laws; it is a list of ways to hear.**

***"If you will diligently listen to … voice"*** — ***ʾim-šāmôaʿ tišmaʿ ləqôl***, the infinitive absolute with
the finite verb. **The doubled form of *"God will surely visit you"* (Genesis 50:24–25) and of the Song's *"he
has triumphed gloriously"* (15:1)** (WLC). **The same construction opens the covenant offer at Sinai**: *"if
you will indeed obey my voice (šāmôaʿ tišməʿû bəqōlî), and keep my covenant"* (19:5), and again in the
angel's promise (*šāmōaʿ tišmaʿ bəqōlô*, 23:22) (WLC; [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]). **Marah is Sinai's condition in
advance.**

**The vocabulary is Genesis 26:5's.** God told Isaac the blessing stood *"because Abraham obeyed my voice
(šāmaʿ … bəqōlî), and kept (wayyišmōr) my requirements, my commandments (miṣwōtay), my statutes (ḥuqqôtay),
and my laws"*. **Marah has *šāmaʿ … qôl*, *šāmar*, *miṣwōt*, and *ḥuqqîm* in one verse.** ***Miṣwâ***,
*commandment*, has occurred only once before, in that verse (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **Isaac was
told his father had kept commandments no narrative had given; Israel is told to keep commandments not yet
given either.** (Genesis 26:5 has *ḥuqqôt*, the feminine plural, where 15:26 has the masculine *ḥuqqîm*;
the WLC files them under separate numbers.) See
[[Because Abraham Obeyed My Voice - Law Before Sinai]].

***"That which is right in his eyes"*** — ***wəhayyāšār bəʿênāyw***. ***Yāšār*, *upright*, *right*, occurs here
for the first time in Scripture**; its other Torah uses are Balaam's *"the death of the righteous"*
(*yəšārîm*, Numbers 23:10) and Deuteronomy's *"You shall do that which is right and good in Yahweh's
sight"* (6:18; also 12:25, 28; 13:18 WEB; 21:9) and *"just and right is he"* (32:4) (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy; *not yet
ingested*). **The standard is not *what is right* but *what is right in his eyes*** — the formula that
Deuteronomy repeats and Judges inverts, when *"everyone did that which was right in his own eyes"* (Judges
17:6; 21:25; by reference, *not yet ingested*). See [[Righteousness]].

***"In his eyes"*** — ***bəʿênāyw***, from ***ʿayin***, *eye*. **The next verse uses the same word for
*springs*** — *"twelve springs (ʿênōt) of water"* (15:27). The WLC files them as two senses of one lemma
(5869 a and b). **Doing what is right in God's eyes, and then twelve *eyes* of water**: the wordplay is in the
Hebrew and not drawn by the text.

***"Pay attention"*** — ***wəhaʾăzantā***, the hiphil of ***ʾāzan***, *give ear*. **Its one use in Genesis is
Lamech's**: *"You wives of Lamech, listen to my speech"* (Genesis 4:23); in the rest of the Torah only Balaam
(Numbers 23:18), Deuteronomy 1:45, and Moses' last song, *"Give ear, you heavens"* (Deuteronomy 32:1) (WLC
lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **The first man in Scripture to demand an ear demanded it for a boast of
killing; the next ear asked for is for God's commandments.** See [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]].

***"I will put none of the diseases on you, which I have put on the Egyptians"*** — ***kol-hammaḥălâ ʾăšer-śamtî
bəmiṣrayim lōʾ-ʾāśîm ʿālêkā***. ***Maḥălâ*** is singular, *all the sickness* (WLC); the WEB renders *diseases*.
**It occurs in the Torah only here and at 23:25**: *"he will bless your bread and your water, and I will take
sickness (maḥălâ) away from among you"* (WLC lemma search; [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]) — **a promise that also joins
water and the end of sickness.** Deuteronomy's *"the evil diseases of Egypt"* (7:15) and *"all the diseases of
Egypt"* (28:60) use a different noun, *madweh* (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*); **28:60 is the threat that
reverses the promise**: *"He will bring on you again all the diseases of Egypt."* **What *"the diseases … on
the Egyptians"* were is not specified**; the plagues — boils (9:8–12) and the pestilence (9:1–7) in
particular — are the obvious referent, but the text does not name them (reported, not decided). See
[[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]] and [[Judgment]].

***"For I am Yahweh who heals you"*** — ***kî ʾănî YHWH rōpəʾekā***, the qal participle of ***rāpāʾ*** with a
suffix. **The verb's first occurrence in Scripture is *"God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his female
servants"***, after Abraham prayed (Genesis 20:17). **Its only other Genesis use is a participle, the same
form as here: *"the physicians (hārōpəʾîm)"* who embalmed Jacob in Egypt** (50:2) (WLC lemma search). **Egypt's
healers were embalmers; Israel's healer is Yahweh.** The text does not draw the contrast; the word is the
same.

**It is the first *"I am Yahweh"* in Exodus with a participle of what he does *for Israel*.** The
self-naming of 6:2–8 framed promises; the formula *"you shall know that I am Yahweh"* has run through the
plagues (7:5, 17; 10:2). **Here the name is completed with a role**: *Yahweh rōpəʾekā*. **Later piety made
it a title — *Yahweh Rophe*** (reported, not checked in this pass). **Deuteronomy 32:39 gives the same
verb in God's own mouth with its opposite**: *"I wound and I heal"* (WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). See
[[Healing]], [[The Names of God]], and [[I Am Yahweh]].

### Elim (15:27)

> **27** They came to Elim, where there were twelve springs of water, and seventy palm trees: and they
> encamped there by the waters.

***"Elim"*** — ***ʾêlim***. **The name is usually taken as *trees* — terebinths or large trees** (reported,
not checked in this pass); the WLC files it as a proper name. **Its only other Torah uses are the itinerary**
— *"They took their journey from Elim"* (16:1) and Numbers 33:9–10 (WLC lemma search). **The Song had asked
*"Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods (bāʾēlim)?"*** (15:11) — **nearly the same letters — the noun
*ʾēlim* spelled without the *yod* that the place-name has** (WLC). The wiki records the resemblance and
draws nothing from it. See [[Elim]].

***"Twelve springs of water"*** — ***šətêm ʿeśrēh ʿênōt mayim***. ***ʿAyin*, *spring*, is the word of Hagar's
fountain on the way to Shur** (Genesis 16:7), **of the spring where Abraham's servant waited for Rebekah**
(24:13, 16, 29–30, 42–45), and of *"Joseph is a fruitful vine, a fruitful vine by a spring"* (49:22) (WLC
lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). See [[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]].

***"Seventy palm trees"*** — ***wəšibʿîm təmārîm***. ***Tāmār*, *palm*, occurs here for the first time in
Scripture**; in the rest of the Torah only Leviticus 23:40 (the branches of the booths), Numbers 33:9, and
Jericho *"the city of palm trees"* (Deuteronomy 34:3) (WLC lemma search; *not yet ingested*). **The same
letters are the name of Tamar (Genesis 38)**, which the WLC files under a separate number (reported as the
usual gloss of her name, not checked in this pass). See [[Tamar]].

**Twelve and seventy are the book's numbers for Israel.** *"The twelve tribes of Israel"* (Genesis 49:28);
*"All the souls who came out of Jacob's body were seventy souls"* (1:5); and ahead, *"seventy of the elders of
Israel"* (24:1, 9; [[The Blood of the Covenant]]). **Many readers take Elim's springs and palms as a picture of the
people, or of the tribes and elders** (reported, not decided). **The text gives only the count.**
Numbers 33:9 repeats it exactly (WEB).

***"They encamped there by the waters"*** — ***wayyaḥănû-šām ʿal-hammāyim***. ***Ḥānâ*, the camp verb of
13:20 and 14:2, 9** (WLC). **After three days without water and one bitter well, the pericope ends *by the
waters*, plural, with no complaint and no miracle.** How long they stayed is not said; 16:1 dates the next
departure to *"the fifteenth day of the second month"*, a month after the Passover (12:6).

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 15:22 resumes the itinerary after the Song, and 16:1 opens a new
  stage (*"They took their journey from Elim"*). Elim (15:27) is kept with Marah as its resolution rather
  than with the manna.
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** *"the wilderness of Shur"* (15:22) against Numbers 33:8's *"wilderness
  of Etham"*; the unnamed subject of *"he made … he tested"* (15:25), and its singular *"him"*; the content of
  the statute and ordinance, which is not given; the referent of *"the diseases … on the Egyptians"*; the
  place named for its bitterness after the bitterness was removed.
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** the tree as natural agent or sign; *lûn* (murmur) and *lûn* (lodge) as
  one root or two; *tôrâ* as derived from *yārâ*; *Elim* as *trees*; the springs and palms as the tribes and
  the elders; *Yahweh Rophe* as a later title; Tamar's name as *palm*. The early Christian reading of
  Marah's tree as a figure of the cross is likewise reported, not checked in this pass, and not adopted.
- **New pages:** the themes [[Murmuring]], [[Healing]], and [[Law and Obedience]], none of which existed —
  the wiki had met *rāpāʾ* twice (Genesis 20:17; 50:2), and the material on obedience had been parked on
  [[Testing]] and [[Covenant]] for want of a page; the places [[Marah]] and [[Elim]]; the connection
  [[Bitter Water Made Sweet - Marah and Elisha's Spring]].
