# Locusts and Darkness

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 10:1–29

## Summary

**The eighth and ninth plagues, and the last audience but one.** The locusts eat what the hail left;
the darkness stops Egypt where it stands. Between and around them, Pharaoh bargains twice more,
confesses a second time, and ends by threatening Moses with death if he ever sees his face again.
**This is also the chapter where Pharaoh's own servants first speak to him, and where God first says
why the story must be told afterward.**

**God's word to Moses (10:1–2).** No warning to Pharaoh comes first; a word to Moses does. *"Go in to
Pharaoh, for I have hardened his heart, and the heart of his servants, that I may show these my signs
among them"* (10:1). **For the first time the purpose reaches past Egypt to Israel's children**: *"that
you may tell in the hearing of your son, and of your son's son, what things I have done to Egypt … that
you may know that I am Yahweh"* (10:2).

**The locusts threatened (10:3–11).** [[Moses]] and [[Aaron]] ask, in Yahweh's name, ***"How long will
you refuse to humble yourself before me?"*** (10:3), and threaten locusts that will cover the ground,
eat *"the residue of that which has escaped"* the hail, and fill every house in Egypt (10:4–6). Moses
turns and walks out (10:6). **Then Pharaoh's servants speak, for the first time in the story**: *"How
long will this man be a snare to us? … Don't you yet know that Egypt is destroyed?"* (10:7). Moses and
Aaron are brought back. Pharaoh asks *"who are those who will go?"*; Moses answers *"our young and …
our old … our sons and … our daughters … our flocks and … our herds"*, *"for we must hold a feast to
Yahweh"* (10:8–9). Pharaoh's reply is a curse dressed as a blessing — *"Yahweh be with you if I will let
you go with your little ones!"* — and an offer: the men only. **Then *"they were driven out from
Pharaoh's presence"*** (10:10–11).

**The locusts (10:12–20).** Moses stretches out his rod; Yahweh drives an east wind *"all that day, and
all night"*, and in the morning *"the east wind brought the locusts"* (10:13). They cover *"the surface
of the whole earth, so that the land was darkened"*; *"There remained nothing green"* (10:14–15).
Pharaoh calls them *"in haste"*: ***"I have sinned against Yahweh your God, and against you. Now
therefore please forgive my sin again"***, and asks them to take away *"this death"* (10:16–17). Moses
prays. Yahweh turns *"an exceeding strong west wind"* which drives the locusts *"into the Red Sea.
There remained not one locust"* (10:18–19). ***"But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart"*** (10:20).

**The darkness (10:21–29).** No warning, as at the lice and the boils. Moses stretches out his hand
toward the sky, and there is ***"darkness which may be felt"***, *"thick darkness in all the land of
Egypt for three days"* (10:21–22). *"They didn't see one another, and nobody rose from his place for
three days; ***but all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings***"* (10:23). Pharaoh's
last offer: everyone may go, but *"let your flocks and your herds stay behind"* (10:24). Moses: *"Not a
hoof shall be left behind … we don't know with what we must serve Yahweh, until we come there"*
(10:25–26). ***"But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart"*** (10:27). **Pharaoh: *"Get away from me! Be
careful to see my face no more; for in the day you see my face you shall die!"* Moses: *"You have
spoken well. I will see your face again no more"*** (10:28–29).

### The shape of it

| | Locusts (10:1–20) | Darkness (10:21–29) |
|---|---|---|
| Warning | yes, in the palace | none |
| Agent | Moses' rod; Yahweh's east wind | Moses' hand toward the sky |
| Israel spared | not stated | *"light in their dwellings"* (10:23) |
| Pharaoh's offer | the men only (10:11) | everyone, but not the herds (10:24) |
| Pharaoh | confesses, asks forgiveness and prayer | threatens Moses with death |
| The heart | Yahweh *ḥāzaq* (piel) (10:20) | Yahweh *ḥāzaq* (piel) (10:27) |

**The third set closes as the first two did.** The hail and the locusts come with warnings; the
darkness, like the lice and the boils, comes with none. **And for the first time in Exodus, every
hardening in the chapter has Yahweh as its subject**: he says *"I have hardened"* (10:1), and the
narrator says it twice (10:20, 27). Pharaoh no longer hardens himself in this chapter, and no
hardening is left without an agent.

## Key verses

### Why the signs are told (10:1–2)

> **1** Yahweh said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh, for I have hardened his heart, and the heart of his
> servants, that I may show these my signs among them,
> **2** and that you may tell in the hearing of your son, and of your son's son, what things I have
> done to Egypt, and my signs which I have done among them; that you may know that I am Yahweh."

***"I have hardened his heart"*** — ***hikbadtî ʾet-libbô***, the hiphil of ***kābēd***, *I have made
heavy*. **Pharaoh was the subject of this exact stem at 8:15, 32 and 9:34; now God is.** In Exodus the
hiphil of *kābēd* takes Pharaoh as subject three times and God once, here (WLC lemma search). **The
same root turns up four chapters on in another stem**: *"I will get honor over Pharaoh"*, *wəʾikkābdâ*,
the niphal (14:4, 17, 18). **The heart God made heavy is the place where God is made heavy, that is,
glorified.** The two are the same root; whether the writer meant the play cannot be shown. ***"And the
heart of his servants"*** — **God takes up 9:34's *"he and his servants"* in his own voice.** See
[[Hardening of the Heart]] and [[Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)]].

***"That I may show these my signs among them"*** — ***ləmaʿan šitî ʾōtōtay ʾēlleh bəqirbô***,
literally *that I may set these my signs in his midst*. ***Šît***, *to set*, is the verb of *"he didn't
even take this to heart"*, *lōʾ-šāt libbô* (7:23). **Pharaoh would not set his heart to the sign; God
sets the signs in him.** See [[Signs and Wonders]] and [[Water Turned to Blood]].

***"That you may tell in the hearing of your son"*** — ***ûləmaʿan təsappēr bəʾoznê binkā ûben-binkā***.
***Sāpar*** in the piel is the verb of 9:16, *"that my name may be declared (səppēr) throughout all
the earth"*. **The purpose of 9:16 was the earth; the purpose of 10:2 is the family.** In Exodus the
piel of *sāpar* occurs only at 9:16, 10:2, 18:8, and 24:3 (WLC lemma search), and the first fulfilment
is a telling: *"Moses told his father-in-law all that Yahweh had done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians
for Israel's sake"* (18:8). **This is the first form of what becomes the Passover question**: *"when
your children ask you, 'What do you mean by this service?'"* (12:26); *"You shall tell your son in
that day"* (13:8); *"when your son asks you in time to come"* (13:14); and Deuteronomy's *"When your son
asks you in time to come … then you shall tell your son, 'We were Pharaoh's slaves in Egypt'"*
(Deuteronomy 6:20–21, WEB). See [[Remembrance]].

***"What things I have done to Egypt"*** — ***ʾēt ʾăšer hitʿallaltî bəmiṣrayim***. ***Hitʿallēl*** is a
hard verb: *to deal ruthlessly with*, *to make a fool of*. **In the Torah it occurs only here and at
Numbers 22:29, where Balaam says to his donkey, *"Because you have mocked me"*** (WLC lemma search; the
poel at Leviticus 19:10 and Deuteronomy 24:21, *gleaning*, is a different sense). **The Philistine
priests use both of this passage's verbs when they remember Egypt**: *"Why then do you harden your
hearts (təkabbədû), as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? When he had worked wonderfully
among them (hitʿallēl bāhem), didn't they let the people go …?"* (1 Samuel 6:6, WEB; Hebrew checked in
the WLC). **The telling 10:2 asks for reached even Israel's enemies.**

***"That you may know that I am Yahweh"*** — ***wîdaʿtem kî-ʾănî YHWH***, the plural. **For the first
time since 6:7 the knowledge formula is addressed to Israel, not Egypt** (7:5, 17; 8:10, 22; 9:14,
29 address Pharaoh or the Egyptians). See
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

### The locusts threatened (10:3–11)

> **3** Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh, and said to him, "This is what Yahweh, the God of the
> Hebrews, says: 'How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me? Let my people go, that they
> may serve me.
> ...
> **5** and they shall cover the surface of the earth, so that one won't be able to see the earth. They
> shall eat the residue of that which has escaped, which remains to you from the hail, and shall eat
> every tree which grows for you out of the field.
> **6** Your houses shall be filled, and the houses of all your servants, and the houses of all the
> Egyptians; as neither your fathers nor your fathers' fathers have seen, since the day that they were
> on the earth to this day.'" He turned, and went out from Pharaoh.

***"How long will you refuse"*** — ***ʿad-mātay mēʾantā***. ***Mēʾēn***, *refuse*, is the verb of the
first message: *"you have refused to let him go"* (4:23); *"he refuses to let the people go"* (7:14).
**Six chapters later God asks Israel the same question**: *"How long do you refuse to keep my
commandments and my laws?"* (16:28, *ʿad-ʾānâ mēʾantem*, WLC). **The complaint God makes of Egypt's
king he will make of his own people.** ***ʿAd-mātay***, *how long*, comes twice in this chapter — God's
here, and the servants' at 10:7 — and in the Torah otherwise only at Numbers 14:27, God again, of
Israel: *"How long shall I bear with this evil congregation?"* (WEB) (WLC lemma search; it also means
*when* at Genesis 30:30 and Exodus 8:9 [Hebrew 8:5]).

***"To humble yourself before me"*** — ***lēʿānōt mippānay***, the niphal of ***ʿānâ***. **This is the
verb of Israel's affliction.** God told Abram that Egypt would *"afflict them four hundred years"*
(Genesis 15:13, *wəʿinnû*); Egypt set taskmasters *"to afflict them with their burdens"* (1:11–12,
*ʿannōtô*, *yəʿannû*) (WLC). **The king who afflicted Israel is asked to be humbled himself — the same
root, turned back.** See [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] and
[[Affliction and Flight - Hagar and the Exodus]].

***"Let my people go, that they may serve me"*** — ***wəyaʿabdunî***. **The demand has not changed
since 7:16.** See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"They shall cover the surface of the earth"*** — ***wəkissâ ʾet-ʿên hāʾāreṣ***, literally *the eye
of the land*. **The same phrase describes Israel itself, seen from Moab**: *"they cover the surface of
the earth"* (Numbers 22:5, WEB; the same Hebrew phrase at 22:11, WLC; not yet ingested). **The residue that escaped** — ***yeter
happəlēṭâ*** — is the hail's *"leftover"*, and ***pəlēṭâ***, *what escapes*, is the word of Jacob's
*"the company which is left will escape"* (Genesis 32:8, Hebrew 32:9) and Joseph's *"to save you alive by
a great deliverance"* (Genesis 45:7). Those are its only other occurrences in Genesis and Exodus (WLC
lemma search). **Twice in Genesis God keeps a remnant alive; here the remnant of Egypt's harvest is
taken.**

***"As neither your fathers nor your fathers' fathers have seen"*** — **the *"such as has not been"* of
the hail (9:18, 24)**, now measured in generations. See
[[The Day of the Locust - Exodus 10 and Joel 1-2]].

***"He turned, and went out from Pharaoh"*** — ***wayyipen wayyēṣēʾ***. **Moses does not wait for an
answer.** At the frogs and the flies he went out after Pharaoh had asked for prayer (8:12, 30); this
time he leaves with nothing asked and nothing promised.

> **7** Pharaoh's servants said to him, "How long will this man be a snare to us? Let the men go, that
> they may serve Yahweh, their God. Don't you yet know that Egypt is destroyed?"

**The first words of Pharaoh's court to Pharaoh.** At 9:20 some of them *"feared Yahweh's word"*; at
9:34 they hardened their hearts with him; at 10:1 God says he has hardened theirs. **Now they speak,
and what they say is God's own question back to their king: *"How long"*.**

***"A snare"*** — ***ləmôqēš***. **This is its first occurrence in the Bible.** In the Torah its other
uses are all warnings to Israel about the Canaanites and their gods: *"it will surely be a snare to
you"* (23:33; also 34:12; Deuteronomy 7:16) (WLC lemma search). **Egypt's court calls Moses a snare; the
law will call the gods of Canaan one.**

***"Let the men go"*** — ***hāʾănāšîm***, the men. **The servants already concede what Pharaoh will
concede next**: men, not families (10:11). ***"Don't you yet know"*** — ***hăṭerem tēdaʿ***. *Ṭerem*,
*not yet*, is Moses' word to Pharaoh at the hail: *"you don't yet fear Yahweh God"* (9:30). **Moses
said he did not yet fear; his servants say he does not yet know.** ***"That Egypt is destroyed"*** —
***ʾābədâ miṣrāyim***, the perfect, *has perished*. See [[The Fear of God]] and
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

> **8** Moses and Aaron were brought again to Pharaoh, and he said to them, "Go, serve Yahweh your God;
> but who are those who will go?"
> **9** Moses said, "We will go with our young and with our old; with our sons and with our daughters,
> with our flocks and with our herds will we go; for we must hold a feast to Yahweh."
> **10** He said to them, "Yahweh be with you if I will let you go with your little ones! See, evil is
> clearly before your faces.
> **11** Not so! Go now you who are men, and serve Yahweh; for that is what you desire!" They were
> driven out from Pharaoh's presence.

***"Who are those who will go?"*** — ***mî wāmî hahōləkîm***, literally *who and who*. **Pharaoh asks
for a list.** Moses gives one in four pairs — young and old, sons and daughters, flocks and herds —
that leaves nothing out.

***"We must hold a feast to Yahweh"*** — ***kî ḥag-YHWH lānû***. **This is the first time the Bible
uses the noun *ḥag*, *feast*.** The verb came in the first request: *"that they may hold a feast to me
in the wilderness"* (5:1). The next *ḥag* is the Passover: *"you shall keep it a feast to Yahweh"*
(12:14) (WLC lemma search). See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].

***"Yahweh be with you"*** — ***yəhî kēn YHWH ʿimmākem***. **Pharaoh uses the words of a blessing as a
taunt**: *may Yahweh be with you as surely as I let your little ones go*. The same God told Moses at
the bush *"I will be with you"* (3:12). ***"See, evil is clearly before your faces"*** — ***rəʾû kî
rāʿâ neged pənêkem***. **Pharaoh means that he sees through their plan: a feast that takes the
children is an escape.** Some readers hear in *rāʿâ*, *evil*, a pun on Ra, the Egyptian sun-god, as if
Pharaoh said *Ra is against you* — with the darkness of 10:21–23 as God's answer. **That reading is
reported here, not adopted; the Hebrew is the ordinary noun *rāʿâ*.**

***"Go now you who are men"*** — ***haggəbārîm***, *the grown men*, a different word from the
servants' *ʾănāšîm*. ***"They were driven out"*** — ***wayəgāreš***, the piel of ***gāraš***. **This is
the verb of the garden**: *"So he drove out the man"* (Genesis 3:24). It is Cain's complaint, *"you have
driven me out"* (Genesis 4:14), and Sarah's demand about Hagar, *"Cast out this servant"* (Genesis
21:10). **And it is God's promise about Pharaoh**: *"by a strong hand he shall drive them out of his
land"* (6:1), repeated at 11:1, *"he will surely thrust you out altogether"*. At 12:39 Israel is
*"thrust out of Egypt"* (*gōrəšû*) (WLC lemma search). **Pharaoh drives out the two men now; he will
drive out the whole people later, as God said he would.** See
[[Our Little Ones Stayed in Goshen - Genesis 50.8 and the Bargaining of Exodus 10]].

### The locusts (10:12–20)

> **13** Moses stretched out his rod over the land of Egypt, and Yahweh brought an east wind on the
> land all that day, and all night; and when it was morning, the east wind brought the locusts.
> **14** The locusts went up over all the land of Egypt, and rested in all the borders of Egypt. They
> were very grievous. Before them there were no such locusts as they, nor will there ever be again.
> **15** For they covered the surface of the whole earth, so that the land was darkened, and they ate
> every herb of the land, and all the fruit of the trees which the hail had left. There remained
> nothing green, either tree or herb of the field, through all the land of Egypt.

***"An east wind"*** — ***rûaḥ qādîm***. **The wind of Pharaoh's dream**, where the seven heads of
grain were *"thin and blasted with the east wind"* (Genesis 41:6, 23, 27). **And the wind of the sea**:
*"Yahweh caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all night"* (14:21). In the Torah *qādîm*
occurs only in those three places (WLC lemma search). **The wind that once warned a Pharaoh of famine
now brings it, and next will open the sea.** ***"Brought"*** — ***nihag***, *drove*, as a herdsman drives
a flock. See [[The East Wind - Pharaoh's Dream, the Locusts, and the Sea]].

***"The locusts"*** — ***hāʾarbeh***. **In the Torah *ʾarbeh* occurs only in this chapter, in the
food law (Leviticus 11:22), and in the curses**: *"You will carry much seed out into the field, and
will gather little in; for the locust will consume it"* (Deuteronomy 28:38, WEB) (WLC lemma search). See
[[The Diseases of Egypt - The Plagues in Deuteronomy 28]].

***"Very grievous"*** — ***kābēd məʾōd***. **The adjective of Pharaoh's heart (7:14), again describing
the plague**, as with the flies (8:24), the pestilence (9:3), and the hail (9:18, 24). ***"Before them
there were no such locusts as they, nor will there ever be again"*** — ***ləpānāyw lōʾ-hāyâ kēn ʾarbeh
kāmōhû wəʾaḥărāyw lōʾ yihyeh-kēn***. **Joel's locust army is described in almost the same words**:
*"there has never been the like, neither will there be any more after them"* (Joel 2:2, WEB; *kāmōhû
lōʾ nihyâ … wəʾaḥărāyw lōʾ yôsēp*, WLC). The two claims sit side by side in the canon without
comment. See [[The Day of the Locust - Exodus 10 and Joel 1-2]].

***"So that the land was darkened"*** — ***wattehšak hāʾāreṣ***. **This is the only occurrence of the
verb *ḥāšak* in the Torah** (WLC lemma search), **and it comes one plague before the darkness.** The
locusts darken the land before the darkness does.

***"There remained nothing green"*** — ***wəlōʾ-nôtar kol-yereq***. ***Yereq***, *green*, is the word
of the creation gift: *"I have given every green herb (yereq ʿēśeb) for food"* (Genesis 1:30), renewed
to Noah, *"As the green herb, I have given everything to you"* (Genesis 9:3) (WLC). **The green God
gave all living things to eat is the thing that is gone.** ***"Every herb of the land"*** is the hail's
*ʿēśeb* (9:22, 25). See [[Creation]] and [[The Flood as Un-Creation]].

> **16** Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in haste, and he said, "I have sinned against Yahweh
> your God, and against you.
> **17** Now therefore please forgive my sin again, and pray to Yahweh your God, that he may also take
> away from me this death."
> **18** He went out from Pharaoh, and prayed to Yahweh.
> **19** Yahweh turned an exceeding strong west wind, which took up the locusts, and drove them into
> the Red Sea. There remained not one locust in all the borders of Egypt.
> **20** But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he didn't let the children of Israel go.

***"I have sinned against Yahweh your God, and against you"*** — ***ḥāṭāʾtî laYHWH ʾĕlōhêkem
wəlākem***. **The second confession, and more specific than the first** (*"I have sinned this time"*,
9:27): it names whom he sinned against, and includes Moses and Aaron. See [[Sin]].

***"Please forgive my sin"*** — ***śāʾ nāʾ ḥaṭṭāʾtî***, literally *lift, please, my sin*. **These are
the words of Joseph's brothers**: *"Now please forgive the disobedience of your brothers, and their
sin"* — *śāʾ nāʾ pešaʿ ʾaḥêkā wəḥaṭṭāʾtām* (Genesis 50:17, WLC). **Both times the request is made to a
man, not to God**: the brothers ask Joseph, and Pharaoh asks Moses and Aaron — *"please forgive my
sin"* is spoken to them, and only then *"pray to Yahweh"*. ***"Only this once"*** — ***ʾak happaʿam***,
the WEB's *"again"*: **the *paʿam*, *time*, of 8:32 and 9:14, 27 once more.** See [[Forgiveness]].

***"This death"*** — ***hammāwet hazzeh***. **Pharaoh calls the locusts death.** See [[Life and Death]].

***"He went out from Pharaoh, and prayed to Yahweh"*** — ***wayyeʿtar***, the *ʿātar* of 8:30. **This
is the fourth time Moses prays for Pharaoh** (8:12, 30; 9:33; 10:18). See [[Prayer]],
[[Mediation and Advocacy]], and
[[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]].

***"An exceeding strong west wind"*** — ***rûaḥ-yām ḥāzāq məʾōd***, literally *a sea wind*, from the
Mediterranean, which lies west. ***Ḥāzāq***, *strong*, is the root of the next verse's *wayḥazzēq*,
*"hardened"*. **The strong wind takes away the plague; the strengthened heart keeps the people.**

***"Into the Red Sea"*** — ***yāmmâ sûp***, *toward the Sea of Reeds*. **This is the first time
Scripture names the *yam sûp*.** The *sûp* first appeared as the *"reeds"* where Moses' basket was laid
(2:3, 5); after this the sea is named at 13:18 and 15:4, 22 (WLC lemma search). **The locusts go where
Pharaoh's army will go.** ***"Drove them"*** — ***wayyitqāʿēhû***, *thrust, drove in*, the verb of
Jacob *pitching* his tent (Genesis 31:25) and of blowing the trumpets (Numbers 10:3–10). See
[[The Red Sea]].

***"There remained not one locust"*** — ***lōʾ nišʾar ʾarbeh ʾeḥād***. **The third *not one* of the
plagues**: *"There remained not one"* of the flies (8:31, *lōʾ nišʾar ʾeḥād*), *"not one"* of Israel's
herds (9:6–7), and here. **At the sea the same words are said of Pharaoh's army**: *"There remained not
so much as one of them"* (14:28, *lōʾ-nišʾar bāhem ʿad-ʾeḥād*) (WLC).

***"But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart"*** — ***wayḥazzēq YHWH ʾet-lēb parʿōh***, the piel of 9:12.
See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

### The darkness (10:21–29)

> **21** Yahweh said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, that there may be darkness over
> the land of Egypt, even darkness which may be felt."
> **22** Moses stretched out his hand toward the sky, and there was a thick darkness in all the land of
> Egypt for three days.
> **23** They didn't see one another, and nobody rose from his place for three days; but all the
> children of Israel had light in their dwellings.

***"Darkness"*** — ***ḥōšek***. **The first word of the world before the first word of God**: *"Darkness
was on the surface of the deep"* (Genesis 1:2), and then *"Let there be light"* (Genesis 1:3). In the
Torah *ḥōšek* occurs in Genesis 1 (1:2, 4, 5, 18), here (10:21 twice, 22), and at the sea, where
*"there was the cloud and the darkness"* between the camps (14:20) (WLC lemma search). **The ninth
plague returns Egypt to the first verse of Genesis, except in Israel's houses, where the light of the
first day stays on.** That reading — the plagues as an undoing of creation — is a standing one; the
text does not name it. See [[Light and Darkness]] and [[Creation]].

***"Darkness which may be felt"*** — ***wəyāmēš ḥōšek***, from ***māšaš***, *to feel, to grope*. **It
is the verb of blind Isaac feeling Jacob**: *"perhaps my father will touch me"* (Genesis 27:12, 22),
and of Laban searching Rachel's tent (31:34, 37). **And it is the verb of the curse**: *"You will grope
at noonday, as the blind gropes in darkness (bāʾăpēlâ)"* (Deuteronomy 28:29, WEB). **The two words
of Exodus 10:21–22, *māšaš* and *ʾăpēlâ*, occur together again only there in the Torah** (WLC lemma
search). See [[The Diseases of Egypt - The Plagues in Deuteronomy 28]].

***"Thick darkness"*** — ***ḥōšek-ʾăpēlâ***. **Joel's day of Yahweh is *"a day of darkness and
gloominess"***, *yôm ḥōšek waʾăpēlâ* (Joel 2:2, WEB and WLC) — the same two nouns, in the same verse as
the locusts' *"there has never been the like"*. See
[[The Day of the Locust - Exodus 10 and Joel 1-2]].

***"For three days"*** — ***šəlōšet yāmîm***. **The three days Moses has been asking for, from the
first audience**: *"let us go three days' journey into the wilderness"* (3:18; 5:3; 8:27). **Egypt sits
still for exactly the time Israel asked to go.** The link is in the numbers; the text does not draw it.

***"They didn't see one another"*** — ***lōʾ-rāʾû ʾîš ʾet-ʾāḥîw***, *a man his brother*. ***"Nobody
rose from his place"*** — **the whole country stops.** ***"But all the children of Israel had light in
their dwellings"*** — ***ûləkol-bənê yiśrāʾēl hāyâ ʾôr bəmôšəbōtām***. **The fourth time the text says
Israel was spared** (8:22–23; 9:4–7, 26; 10:23). **[[Goshen]] is not named**; the light is *"in their
dwellings"*, wherever they were. *Môšāb*, *dwelling*, is used again of Israel's whole stay in Egypt,
*"the time that the children of Israel lived in Egypt"* (12:40) (WLC). See [[Call and Election]].

**Later retellings differ on order and detail.** Psalm 105 puts the darkness first: *"He sent
darkness, and made it dark"* (Psalm 105:28, WEB), then the blood, frogs, flies and lice, hail, locusts,
and firstborn (105:29–36). The Wisdom of Solomon, in the WEB's deuterocanon, turns the scene into a
meditation: the Egyptians *"prisoners of darkness, and bound in the fetters of a long night"*, while
*"for your holy ones there was great light"* (Wisdom 17:2; 18:1, WEB). Revelation's fifth bowl darkens
the beast's kingdom (Revelation 16:10, WEB).

> **24** Pharaoh called to Moses, and said, "Go, serve Yahweh. Only let your flocks and your herds stay
> behind. Let your little ones also go with you."
> **25** Moses said, "You must also give into our hand sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may
> sacrifice to Yahweh our God.
> **26** Our livestock also shall go with us. Not a hoof shall be left behind, for of it we must take to
> serve Yahweh our God; and we don't know with what we must serve Yahweh, until we come there."
> **27** But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he wouldn't let them go.

***"Let your flocks and your herds stay behind"*** — ***yuṣṣāg***, the hophal of ***yāṣag***, *be set
down, be left*. **Pharaoh's last offer keeps back the herds**, exactly what Joseph's household left in
Goshen when it went up to bury Jacob (Genesis 50:8). See
[[Our Little Ones Stayed in Goshen - Genesis 50.8 and the Bargaining of Exodus 10]].

***"You must also give into our hand sacrifices and burnt offerings"*** — ***zəbāḥîm wəʿōlôt***.
**Moses now asks Pharaoh to supply the animals.** The *ʿōlâ* is the burnt offering of Noah (Genesis
8:20) and of Moriah (Genesis 22:2). See [[Sacrifice and Offering]].

***"Not a hoof shall be left behind"*** — ***lōʾ tiššāʾēr parsâ***. **This is the only *parsâ* in the
Torah outside the food laws**, where the split hoof is what makes an animal clean (Leviticus 11:3–7,
26; Deuteronomy 14:6–8) (WLC lemma search). ***Tiššāʾēr***, *be left*, is the verb of the
locusts' *"There remained not one"* (10:19). **Not one locust stays in Egypt, and not one hoof will.**

***"We don't know with what we must serve Yahweh, until we come there"*** — ***waʾănaḥnû lōʾ-nēdaʿ
mah-naʿăbōd ʾet-YHWH ʿad-bōʾēnû šāmmâ***. **Israel does not yet know how to worship.** The instructions
are given only at the mountain. *ʿĀbad*, *serve*, appears three times in 10:24–26; it is both the work
of a slave and the worship of God. See [[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he wouldn't let them go"*** — ***wəlōʾ ʾābâ
ləšalləḥām***. ***ʾĀbâ***, *be willing*: **he was not willing.** Yahweh hardens; Pharaoh is
unwilling. **The verse names both.**

> **28** Pharaoh said to him, "Get away from me! Be careful to see my face no more; for in the day you
> see my face you shall die!"
> **29** Moses said, "You have spoken well. I will see your face again no more."

***"Be careful to see my face no more; for in the day you see my face you shall die"*** — ***bəyôm
rəʾōtəkā pānay tāmût***. **Pharaoh claims for his own face what God will say of his**: *"You cannot see
my face, for man may not see me and live"* (33:20). And Jacob, after wrestling, named the place Peniel:
*"I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved"* (Genesis 32:30). ***"In the day … you shall
die"*** is the form of the garden warning, *"in the day that you eat of it, you will surely die"*
(Genesis 2:17). **The king who made himself like a god in Egypt (7:1 made Moses *"as God to Pharaoh"*)
speaks like one.** The echoes are verbal; whether they are deliberate cannot be shown. See
[[Seeing and Being Seen]].

***"You have spoken well. I will see your face again no more"*** — ***kēn dibbartā lōʾ-ʾōsip ʿôd
rəʾôt pāneykā***. **The text does not end the audience here.** In 11:4–8 Moses is still speaking to
Pharaoh, announces the last plague, and *"went out from Pharaoh in hot anger"* (11:8). **At 12:31
Pharaoh *"called for Moses and Aaron by night"***, and whether they met face to face is not said.
Readers have taken 11:4–8 as the end of this same audience, or 10:29 as Moses' last word on a meeting
that ends at 11:8. **The text leaves the seam visible, and so does this page.**

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** Chapter 10 holds the last two plagues of the third set and
  the end of the bargaining. 10:28–29 reads like the end of the audiences, but 11:4–8 continues to
  address Pharaoh; that seam is noted above and left to the next pericope,
  [[The Last Plague Announced]] (11:1–10).
- **The *rāʿâ*/Ra pun at 10:10** is reported as a reading held by some interpreters. It was not
  verified against any named commentary, and the wiki does not adopt it.
- **The plagues as un-creation** (darkness and Genesis 1:2; *yereq* and Genesis 1:30) is offered as a
  reading built on verified word links. The text of Exodus does not name it.
- **The *kābēd* hiphil/niphal play** (10:1; 14:4, 17–18) is lexical, from the WLC; whether it was
  meant is not claimed.
- **Psalm 105:28b** (*"They didn't rebel against his words"*) has a well-known textual difficulty
  between the Hebrew and the Greek. It is quoted here only in the WEB, and no claim is made about it.
- **Verse quotations outside `raw/`** (Joel 1:4; 2:2, 10, 25; Deuteronomy 6:20–21; 28:29, 38; 1 Samuel
  6:6; Numbers 14:27; 22:29; Psalm 78:46; 105:28–36; Wisdom 17:2; 18:1; Revelation 9:3; 16:10) were
  fetched from bible-api.com (`translation=web`), one reference at a time. Genesis and Exodus are quoted
  from `raw/bible/`.
- **Hebrew forms on this page were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Open Scriptures
  Hebrew Bible, `morphhb`): all of Exodus 10; Exodus 6:1; 8:27 (Hebrew); 14:21, 28; 16:28; 33:20;
  Genesis 50:17; Deuteronomy 28:29, 38; 1 Samuel 6:6; Joel 1:4; 2:2, 10, 25. Occurrence counts come
  from lemma searches of the WLC Torah (Genesis–Deuteronomy), with 1 Samuel where stated. The
  translation glosses of Hebrew words are standard ones and were not checked against a lexicon.
