# The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 40:1–38

## Summary

Yahweh commands Moses to raise the tabernacle on the first day of the first month, place
its furniture and boundaries, anoint the sanctuary and its equipment, and wash, clothe,
and anoint Aaron and his sons for priestly service (40:1–15). A general statement affirms
Moses’ obedience (40:16). The erection is dated to that day in the second year (40:17).

Moses raises the structure and coverings, places the testimony in the ark and screens it,
arranges the table and bread, lights the lamps, burns incense, and offers burnt and meal
offerings. He installs the basin, where he, Aaron, and the sons wash hands and feet, and
raises the court and its entrance screen. His work is finished (40:18–33).

The cloud then covers the Tent of Meeting and Yahweh’s glory fills the tabernacle. Moses
cannot enter because of the cloud and glory (40:34–35). Israel’s travel follows the cloud’s
lifting; when it stays, they wait. Cloud by day and fire in it by night remain visible to
all Israel throughout their journeys (40:36–38). Exodus ends with God dwelling among and
guiding a people who have not yet reached the promised land.

## Notes

### One closing unit with three movements

The planned 40:1–38 boundary is retained: commands (1–15), obedience and erection (16–33),
then filling and guidance (34–38). The date in verse 17 answers the command in verse 2.
The first-month calendar recalls Exodus 12:2, but the erection is on the first day, not
on the Passover date, and the chapter supplies no number of days spent manufacturing.

### From inventory to service

The detailed report goes beyond arranging unused objects. Bread, lamps, incense, offerings,
and washing appear as actions (40:23–32). Moses is the named agent through this erection
account; Aaron and his sons are expressly included in the washing. Bezalel and Oholiab’s
craftsmanship belongs to the preceding making accounts; neither is assigned an act here.
Israel means the people, not an appearance of Jacob. The sons are identified through 28:1,
without inventing an individual act for any of them.

The ark’s poles retain a question of sequence: 25:15 forbids taking them out, 37:5 reports
insertion, and 40:20 reports setting them on the ark. No removal is expressly narrated.
A recap or adjustment is possible, but the WEB alone does not establish the precise sequence.
The veil screens the ark, while the table stands north and the lampstand south outside it
(40:21–24; compare 26:33–35). The golden incense altar and the outer offering altar remain
distinct. The chapter does not supply a full ritual inventory for the offerings of 40:29.

### Consecration commanded, obedience affirmed

Verses 9–15 prescribe anointing the sanctuary and equipment and washing, clothing, and
anointing the priests. Verse 16 broadly affirms compliance. The detailed account in 17–33
then emphasizes erection and service, without separately recounting all the prescribed
anointings or the full seven-day ordination of Exodus 29. It would be inaccurate either
to claim that no obedience is reported or to supply an unreported step-by-step ceremony.
The hand-and-foot washing of 40:31–32 is explicitly narrated and includes Moses.
See [[Ordination Commanded and Performed - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 8]].

Seven notices in the stored WEB repeat “as Yahweh commanded Moses” (19, 21, 23, 25, 27,
29, 32), after the general notice in verse 16. This is an English-text observation, not a
claim that each contains an independently verified Hebrew formula. The work’s completion
in verse 33 follows manufacture’s completion in 39:32: the tasks are different.

### Presence comes near without becoming controllable

The filling answers the dwelling purpose of 25:8 and 29:45–46 after the calf, the threatened
absence of 33:3, Moses’ intercession, and covenant renewal. The outcome does not erase that
crisis or represent human workmanship as purchasing divine presence.

**The access reports differ.** Moses enters the cloud at Sinai in 24:18 and enters the
outside tent before the cloud descends in 33:9. In 40:35 he cannot enter the filled tent.
The text gives the cloud and glory as the reason, not a new sin or permanent rejection.
These are different moments and settings; they must not become a claim that Moses always
could, or never could, enter God’s presence. The obstruction here is not attributed merely
to the veil. The historical relationship between the outside tent and the newly erected
sanctuary remains unexplicated.

### A dwelling at the end of a book about departure

The closing verses describe a rule across the journeys, not a newly dated departure from
Sinai. When the cloud remains, the people remain. The night fire is visible to the whole
house of Israel (40:38), while the interior lamps have their own place and service.
The guidance of 13:21–22 returns at the conclusion: God’s dwelling accompanies the people
on the way. Presence is the book’s achieved ending; possession of the land remains beyond it.
