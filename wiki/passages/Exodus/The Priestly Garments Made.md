# The Priestly Garments Made

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 39:1–31

## Summary

The makers turn the prescribed priestly clothing into finished articles. Gold, blue, purple,
scarlet, and fine linen form the ephod; gold is beaten into plates and cut into wires for the
textile work. Engraved onyx stones are mounted on its shoulders as memorials for Israel (39:1–7).

The doubled, square breastplate receives four rows of stones, twelve in all, engraved for the
twelve tribes. Gold chains, settings, and rings, together with blue lace, secure it to the ephod
so it cannot come loose (39:8–21). The blue robe has a reinforced opening and a hem of alternating
pomegranates and gold bells (39:22–26).

Coats for Aaron and his sons, the turban, headbands, linen breeches, and embroidered sash follow.
The pure gold plate of the holy crown is inscribed “HOLY TO YAHWEH” and fastened with blue lace
to the turban (39:27–31). Seven notices identify the making as obedience to Yahweh’s command to
Moses. No priest is dressed or ordained in this unit; the final inspection and blessing begin
with the next pericope, and erection and service remain ahead.

## Key verses

> **39:1** Of the blue, purple, and scarlet, they made finely worked garments, for ministering in the holy place, and made the holy garments for Aaron; as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **39:2** He made the ephod of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen.

> **39:3** They beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires, to work it in with the blue, the purple, the scarlet, and the fine linen, the work of the skillful workman.

> **39:4** They made shoulder straps for it, joined together. At the two ends it was joined together.

> **39:5** The skillfully woven band that was on it, with which to fasten it on, was of the same piece, like its work; of gold, of blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen; as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **39:6** They worked the onyx stones, enclosed in settings of gold, engraved with the engravings of a signet, according to the names of the children of Israel.

> **39:7** He put them on the shoulder straps of the ephod, to be stones of memorial for the children of Israel, as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **39:8** He made the breastplate, the work of a skillful workman, like the work of the ephod; of gold, of blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen.

> **39:9** It was square. They made the breastplate double. Its length was a span, and its width a span, being double.

> **39:10** They set in it four rows of stones. A row of ruby, topaz, and beryl was the first row;

> **39:11** and the second row, a turquoise, a sapphire, and an emerald;

> **39:12** and the third row, a jacinth, an agate, and an amethyst;

> **39:13** and the fourth row, a chrysolite, an onyx, and a jasper. They were enclosed in gold settings.

> **39:14** The stones were according to the names of the children of Israel, twelve, according to their names; like the engravings of a signet, everyone according to his name, for the twelve tribes.

> **39:15** They made on the breastplate chains like cords, of braided work of pure gold.

> **39:16** They made two settings of gold, and two gold rings, and put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate.

> **39:17** They put the two braided chains of gold in the two rings at the ends of the breastplate.

> **39:18** The other two ends of the two braided chains they put on the two settings, and put them on the shoulder straps of the ephod, in its front.

> **39:19** They made two rings of gold, and put them on the two ends of the breastplate, on its edge, which was toward the side of the ephod inward.

> **39:20** They made two more rings of gold, and put them on the two shoulder straps of the ephod underneath, in its front, close by its coupling, above the skillfully woven band of the ephod.

> **39:21** They bound the breastplate by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a lace of blue, that it might be on the skillfully woven band of the ephod, and that the breastplate might not come loose from the ephod, as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **39:22** He made the robe of the ephod of woven work, all of blue.

> **39:23** The opening of the robe in the middle of it was like the opening of a coat of mail, with a binding around its opening, that it should not be torn.

> **39:24** They made on the skirts of the robe pomegranates of blue, purple, scarlet, and twined linen.

> **39:25** They made bells of pure gold, and put the bells between the pomegranates around the skirts of the robe, between the pomegranates;

> **39:26** a bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, around the skirts of the robe, to minister in, as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **39:27** They made the coats of fine linen of woven work for Aaron, and for his sons,

> **39:28** and the turban of fine linen, and the linen headbands of fine linen, and the linen breeches of fine twined linen,

> **39:29** and the sash of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, the work of the embroiderer, as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **39:30** They made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold, and wrote on it a writing, like the engravings of a signet: “HOLY TO YAHWEH”.

> **39:31** They tied to it a lace of blue, to fasten it on the turban above, as Yahweh commanded Moses.

## Notes

### The same design, with a different task in view

[[The Priestly Garments]] commands the clothing; this unit reports its making. Both follow the
ephod with the breastplate and blue robe, but chapter 39 places the linen clothing before the
gold crown. Exodus 39:3 adds the method of integrating gold into the fabric. The stored WEB
also includes twined linen among the pomegranate materials in 39:24, where 28:33 lists the three
colors, and gives the sash’s materials in 39:29 more fully than 28:39. These are differences
of description, not grounds for rewriting either source or claiming that obedience meant a
word-for-word duplicate. See [[The Garments Commanded and Made - Exodus 28 and 39]].

### Names remembered, functions not repeated

The shoulder stones retain the description “stones of memorial” (39:7). The breastplate has
twelve named stones (39:14), but the chapter does not enumerate the names, assign each tribe
a stone, or repeat the shoulder-name birth order from 28:10. The gem labels in 39:10–13 match
28:17–20 in the stored WEB; this is an English comparison, not an identification of ancient
minerals or a claim about exact Hebrew vocabulary.

The Urim and Thummim are not mentioned in this unit. Chapter 28:30 commands putting them in
the breastplate without commanding their manufacture. Their absence from the making report
therefore supplies no evidence that they were rejected, lost, or themselves made of the twelve
stones. Likewise, 39:26 records the robe’s bells for ministry without repeating 28:35’s warning
about death. Chapter 39 makes what the earlier chapter explained; the explanatory omissions
are not cancellations.

### The crown’s attachment and the stored translation

**There is a discrepancy in the stored WEB:** 28:37 places the plate’s blue lace on the sash,
whereas 39:31 explicitly fastens it on the turban above. The forehead placement in 28:38 and
the crown-on-turban direction in 29:6 support the latter picture. The earlier passage’s notes
record a source-language check of this issue. This ingest compares the stored English only,
leaves the raw wording intact, and does not introduce a new Hebrew claim. The inscription’s
holiness is clear even where the translation’s attachment terminology differs.

### Whose work, and which stage?

Neither Bezalel nor Oholiab is named in these thirty-one verses. Their shared commission in
36:1–2 and credit in 38:22–23 identify the surrounding workforce, while the WEB alternates
singular and plural making descriptions here. Assigning a specific garment to Oholiab or every
individual operation to Bezalel would exceed that evidence.

Aaron and his sons receive clothing after the calf and covenant renewal, but no washing,
anointing, dressing, or ordination sacrifice takes place here. The paragraph prepares priestly
service rather than narrating it. The seven obedience notices occur at 39:1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29,
and 31. Their number is an observation in the stored WEB; no claim about a seven-day making
schedule or exact Hebrew formula is inferred.
