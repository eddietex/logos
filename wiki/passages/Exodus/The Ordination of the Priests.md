# The Ordination of the Priests

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 29:1–46

## Summary

God now tells Moses what he must do to Aaron and his sons *"to make them holy, to minister to me in
the priest's office"* (29:1). Chapter 28 described their clothing. Chapter 29 describes the rite in
which they are put into it. Moses gathers one young bull, two rams without defect, and a basket of
unleavened bread, cakes, and wafers (29:1–3). He brings the priests to the door of the Tent of
Meeting and washes them with water. He dresses Aaron in the coat, robe, ephod, breastplate, and
woven band, sets the turban on his head and *"the holy crown on the turban"*, and pours the
anointing oil on his head. Then he clothes the sons (29:4–9).

Three animals follow, each after Aaron and his sons lay their hands on its head. The bull is a
**sin offering**: its blood goes on the altar's horns and at its base, its fat is burned on the
altar, and its meat, skin, and dung are burned *"outside of the camp"* (29:10–14). The first ram is
a **burnt offering**, burned whole, *"a pleasant aroma"* (29:15–18). The second ram is the **ram of
consecration**. Its blood is put on the right ear, right thumb, and right big toe of each priest
and sprinkled on the altar. Blood from the altar and anointing oil are then sprinkled on the
priests and on their garments, *"and he shall be made holy, and his garments"* (29:19–21). Its fat,
right thigh, and one of each kind of bread are put in the priests' hands, waved before Yahweh, and
burned (29:22–25). Its breast is waved and given to Moses as *"your portion"*. The breast and thigh
of later peace offerings become the priests' portion forever (29:26–28).

Aaron's garments are to pass to his successors, who will wear them for seven days at their own
ordination (29:29–30). The priests boil and eat the ram's meat and the remaining bread at the
tent's door. A *"stranger"* may not eat it, and leftovers are burned by morning (29:31–34). The
rite lasts seven days, with a bull of sin offering every day. Over those seven days the altar
itself is atoned for and anointed, until it is *"most holy"* and *"whatever touches the altar shall
be holy"* (29:35–37).

The chapter ends with what will happen at that altar every day afterward. A lamb is offered each
morning and each evening, with flour, oil, and wine, as *"a continual burnt offering throughout
your generations"* (29:38–42). At the tent's door, God says, *"I will meet with you, to speak there
to you."* He will sanctify tent, altar, and priests. *"I will dwell among the children of Israel,
and will be their God."* Israel will know that he brought them out of Egypt *"that I might dwell
among them"* (29:42–46). As in chapters 25–28, all of this is commanded, not yet done. Leviticus 8
narrates the performance.

## People

- [[God]] — prescribes the rite, and promises to meet Israel at the tent's door, sanctify tent,
  altar, and priests, and dwell among the people (29:42–46).
- [[Moses]] — performs every act of the rite: he washes, clothes, anoints, kills, applies blood,
  waves, and burns, and receives the breast of the ram as *"your portion"* (29:26).
- [[Aaron]] — washed, clothed in the garments of chapter 28, anointed on the head, and given the
  priesthood *"by a perpetual statute"* (29:7, 9); his garments pass to *"the son who is priest in
  his place"* (29:30).
- [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]], [[Abihu]], [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]], and [[Ithamar]] — included as
  *"his sons"* throughout, not named individually in this chapter; clothed, sprinkled with blood
  and oil, and given the priests' share of the offerings.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — Sinai, by the continuing setting of chapters 24–31; Numbers 28:6 later calls the
  daily offering one *"ordained in Mount Sinai"*. The rite itself is set at *"the door of the
  Tent of Meeting"* (29:4, 11, 32, 42).
- [[Egypt]] — *"who brought them out of the land of Egypt, that I might dwell among them"* (29:46).

## Themes

- [[Priesthood]] — the ordination rite, the priests' portions, succession in the garments, and the
  first daily duty at the altar.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — sin offering, burnt offering, ram of consecration, wave offering, and
  the twice-daily lamb, in one chapter.
- [[Blood]] — on the altar's horns, at its base, on ear, thumb, and toe, and sprinkled with oil on
  the priests and their garments.
- [[Holiness]] — priests, garments, altar, tent, and food made holy; the altar becomes *"most
  holy"* and makes holy what touches it.
- [[Sin]] — the bull is *"a sin offering"*; atonement is made for the priests and for the altar.
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — washing at the door, and the altar cleansed for seven days.
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — Moses mediates the priests' installation; the priests eat what
  made atonement for them.
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — *"I will dwell among the children of Israel"* (29:45).
- [[The Glory of God]] — *"the place shall be sanctified by my glory"* (29:43).
- [[Covenant]] — *"and will be their God"*, the covenant formula joined to the dwelling.
- [[Atonement]] — *kuppar* for the priests (29:33) and atonement for the altar (29:36–37).

## Notes

### What Moses does

Moses is the only actor in the chapter besides God. Every verb of the rite is *you*: Moses brings,
washes, clothes, anoints, kills, takes the blood, burns, waves, and boils. Aaron and his sons only
lay their hands on the animals, hold the portions, and eat (29:10, 15, 19, 24, 32). Moses therefore
does priestly acts before any priest exists. He is given the breast of the ordination ram as his
portion (29:26), which Leviticus 8:29 confirms as *"Moses' portion of the ram of consecration"*.
The text does not explain by what office Moses acts, and it does not call him a priest here.

### *Filling the hand*

The WEB says *"you shall consecrate Aaron and his sons"* (29:9), and calls the second ram *"a ram
of consecration"* (29:22). The Hebrew, read in *Miqra according to the Masorah* (MAM, via
Sefaria), has *ûmillēʾtā yad-ʾahărōn wəyad-bānāyw*, literally *you shall fill the hand of Aaron and
the hand of his sons* (29:9). The ram is *ʾêl millūʾîm*, *a ram of fillings* (29:22), and 29:33 uses
*ləmallēʾ ʾet-yādām*, *to fill their hand*. The chapter itself puts the ram's fat, its thigh, and the
bread *"in Aaron's hands, and in his sons' hands"* (29:24). The text does not say that the idiom
comes from this gesture; that link is a common inference, not a statement of the verse.

### The plate and the turban: the chapter 28 discrepancy resolved

[[The Priestly Garments]] flagged that the stored WEB of 28:37 fastens the gold plate *"on the
sash"*, while 28:38, 39:31, and Leviticus 8:9 put it on the forehead or turban. Chapter 29 sets
*"the holy crown on the turban"* (29:6). In MAM, 28:37 reads *wəhāyâ ʿal-hammiṣnāpet*, and
*miṣnepet* is the word the WEB renders *"turban"* at 28:4, 28:39, and 29:6. The Hebrew of 28:37
therefore says turban. The WEB's *"sash"* there is a rendering of this edition, not the Hebrew.
The reverse also happens here: the WEB's *"belts"* at 29:9 renders *ʾabnēṭ*, the word it translates
*"sash"* at 28:4 and 28:39. The raw text is not altered.

### Three offerings in a fixed order

The bull is a sin offering (29:14), the first ram a burnt offering (29:18), and the second ram the
ram of consecration, partly burned, partly waved, and partly eaten (29:22–34). Leviticus 8 performs
them in the same order. Many readers see a sequence of dealing with sin, then a whole gift, then a
shared meal. The chapter gives the order but not the reason, so this remains an interpretation.

The sin offering's meat, skin, and dung are burned *"outside of the camp"* (29:14), the first time
Exodus uses the phrase for an offering. The priests eat *"those things with which atonement was
made"* (29:33); in the Hebrew this is *kuppar* (MAM). The atonement is made for the people who will
later make atonement for others. In the stored WEB text, 29:14 is the first *"sin offering"* and
29:33 the first *"atonement"* in Genesis and Exodus (search of `raw/bible/`). This is a fact about
the English text; earlier Hebrew uses of the same root were not checked in this pass.

### Ear, thumb, and toe

The blood of the ordination ram goes on the right ear, right thumb, and right big toe of each
priest (29:20). The chapter does not explain this. Readings that associate the three places with
hearing, doing, and walking are interpretations. The same three places receive blood and then oil
in the cleansing of a person in Leviticus 14:14–17 (verified WEB). There a cleansed person, not a
priest, is marked in the same way. The verse records the rite, not its meaning.

### Garments made holy with their wearers

Blood from the altar and anointing oil are sprinkled on Aaron *"and on his garments"*, and on the
sons and their garments, *"and he shall be made holy, and his garments"* (29:21). The garments of
chapter 28 are consecrated with their wearers. The oil is poured on Aaron's head (29:7), while the
sons receive it only in the sprinkling of 29:21. Exodus 40:15 (local WEB) later says the sons are to
be anointed *"as you anointed their father"*. The difference is noted, not resolved; chapter 29 does
not describe a separate pouring on the sons.

### A rite with a successor built in

The garments *"shall be for his sons after him"*, and the son who succeeds wears them for seven
days (29:29–30). The office is designed to outlast its first holder in the same chapter that
creates it. The breast and thigh given to Aaron's family are their portion *"forever from the
children of Israel"* (29:28). At the ordination itself, the thigh is burned (29:22, 25) and the
breast goes to Moses (29:26). The permanent rule and the one-time rite are distinguished in the
text itself; this is not a contradiction.

### An altar that needs atonement

For seven days a bull of sin offering is offered *"for atonement"*, and Moses must *"cleanse the
altar"* and anoint it (29:36–37). The altar is not a sinner, yet it is atoned for. In MAM the verb
of 29:36 is *wəḥiṭṭēʾtā*, written with the same consonants as the noun *ḥaṭṭāʾt*, *sin offering*,
in the same verse. The text gives no reason the newly made altar needs cleansing. The result is
stated: the altar becomes *"most holy"* (*qōdeš qodāšîm*, MAM), and *"whatever touches the altar
shall be holy"* (29:37). Exodus 30:29 (local WEB) and Leviticus 6:18 (verified WEB) use the same
language of touch.

### The daily lambs at the place of meeting

The chapter does not end with the ordination. It ends with the duty the priests are ordained to:
two lambs a day, morning and evening, as *"a continual burnt offering"* (29:38–42). In MAM this is
*ʿōlat tāmîd*. The site is *"the door of the Tent of Meeting before Yahweh, where I will meet with
you"* (29:42). In the Hebrew of 29:42–43 the verb *meet* (*ʾiwwāʿēd*, *nōʿadtî*) stands beside the
*môʿēd* of *ʾōhel-môʿēd*, Tent of Meeting. Standard lexicons relate the two to one root (*yʿd*);
that derivation was not checked against a lexicon in this pass. See
[[The Continual Burnt Offering - Exodus 29, Numbers 28, and Daniel 8]].

### *Sanctified by my glory*

God says *"the place shall be sanctified by my glory"* (29:43). In MAM the clause is
*wəniqdaš bikḇōdî*, with no separate noun for *place*. The subject is implied, and the WEB
supplies *"the place"*.
Leviticus 9:23–24 (verified WEB) narrates Yahweh's glory appearing and fire consuming the offering
at the end of the priests' installation. Exodus 40:34–35 (local WEB) has the glory fill the
tabernacle.

### What the exodus was for

29:45–46 is the most explicit purpose statement in the book so far: God brought Israel out of Egypt
*"that I might dwell among them"*. In MAM the verb is *wəšākantî* and the purpose clause
*ləšokənî bətôkām*. The deliverance of chapters 1–15 is now given its goal in a place of worship,
and the covenant formula of 6:7 is repeated over the tent. See
[[That I May Dwell Among Them - Tabernacle, Temple, Flesh, and New Creation]] and
[[I Will Take You as My People - The Covenant Formula]].
