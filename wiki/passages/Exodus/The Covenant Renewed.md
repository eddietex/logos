# The Covenant Renewed

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 34:1–28

## Summary

Moses cuts replacement tablets and ascends Sinai alone under renewed restrictions. Yahweh descends
in the cloud, passes before him, and proclaims his name and character: mercy, grace, patience,
loyal kindness, forgiveness, and judgment reaching successive generations (34:1–7). Moses bows
and asks for accompaniment, pardon, and acceptance of the people as God's inheritance (34:8–9).

God announces a covenant and unprecedented marvels. He forbids treaties that draw Israel into
the inhabitants' worship, orders their cult objects destroyed, and prohibits cast idols
(34:10–17). Unleavened Bread, firstborn redemption, weekly rest even during plowing and harvest,
the annual feasts, and offering regulations follow (34:18–26). Moses is commanded to write;
he remains with Yahweh forty days and nights without food or water, and the unit closes with
the tablets and the ten commandments (34:27–28). His descent and shining face begin the next unit.

## Notes

### The name proclaimed after the breach

[[Show Me Your Glory]] ends with a promise of goodness passing and the name proclaimed (33:19–23).
Here the descent, passing, and proclamation occur (34:5–7). The account emphasizes what God says
about himself; it does not describe the face previously denied to Moses or restate every detail
of the promised protected glimpse. The name already disclosed at the bush now receives an
extended account of divine character in the aftermath of [[The Golden Calf]].

**Forgiveness and judgment are both in the proclamation.** Verse 7 neither makes pardon impossible
nor says that every consequence disappears. It places forgiveness beside refusal to clear the
guilty, without explaining the relation exhaustively. The generational warning also remains in
explicit tension with Job 21:19–21 and Ezekiel 18:19–23, which resist charging a son for his
father's wrongdoing. The qualification about those who hate God in Exodus 20:5 is not repeated
in the WEB of 34:7. It should not be silently inserted here. Nor does the WEB add *generations*
after *thousands*. See
[[Visiting the Iniquity of the Fathers - Job 21.19, Jeremiah 31, and Ezekiel 18]].

### The people still need presence and pardon

Moses responds to revelation by bowing, then turns personal favor into a request for the people
(34:8–9). He includes himself in the appeal concerning our iniquity and sin; that solidarity does
not narrate him making the calf. Stiff-neckedness remains acknowledged. The WEB renders the plea
concessively, *although*; this page makes no claim to resolve the underlying Hebrew syntax.
The request that God take the people as his inheritance concerns belonging, alongside the land
promised to them. Verse 10's covenant announcement answers in action and obligation, without
using the explicit pardon declaration found in Numbers 14:20.

### Renewed allegiance after the calf

The prohibition of cast idols (34:17) now follows the destruction of a cast calf (32:4, 8).
The danger of treaties is traced through shared sacrifices and marriages into worship of other
gods (34:12–16). Jealous is applied to God in the demand for exclusive worship (34:14).
The prostitution language describes religious infidelity here; it does not document a particular
sexual ritual. Asherah poles are named among objects to destroy; the verse does not describe an
individual goddess acting in this scene. The six nations remain on [[The Nations]] and [[Canaan]].

### A calendar for the restored relationship

Many instructions repeat [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] and
[[The Consecration of the Firstborn]]. The additions and differences matter: Sabbath applies in
plowing and harvest (34:21); the WEB calls the feasts in 34:22 Weeks and harvest, with wheat
first fruits and year's end distinguishing their seasons; land is promised protection during
pilgrimage (34:24); and 34:25 names the Passover sacrifice where 23:18 names festival fat.
The firstborn son must be redeemed; only the donkey receives the neck-breaking alternative
(34:20). The goat-and-milk rule is repeated without a rationale (34:26). No location for the
house of Yahweh is identified, and none of these laws reports that a feast has already occurred.

### Whose writing, and which ten commandments?

Verse 1 promises God's writing of the former words. Verse 27 commands Moses to write the covenant
words; verse 28 follows Moses' forty-day stay with *He wrote* (WEB), without naming the subject
again. Reading God as the tablet writer while Moses records the instructions distinguishes two
acts of writing and fits 34:1; reading Moses as the continuing subject of 34:28 follows its
immediate narrative sequence. Deuteronomy 10:1–5 explicitly attributes the replacement writing
to God and identifies it with the words spoken at the assembly. The texts should remain visible
rather than making the pronoun alone settle the question.

The proximity of 34:28 to the worship laws in 34:10–26 raises a further question about their
relation to Exodus 20's commandments. Exodus 34 does not enumerate ten items in the preceding
laws, and 34:1 expressly points back to the original tablets. The wiki records both the
continuity claim and the different immediate law collection in
[[The Replacement Tablets - Exodus 34 and Deuteronomy 10]]. No particular reconstruction of
literary sources is treated as established by this passage.

### Forty days, and the stopping point

The forty days repeat 24:18's duration, but 34:28 explicitly adds abstention from bread and
water. It is a narrative report about Moses' encounter, not an instruction for Israel to fast
this way. The planned 34:1–28 boundary is retained; the descent is now ingested separately as
[[The Shining Face of Moses]], Exodus 34:29–35.
