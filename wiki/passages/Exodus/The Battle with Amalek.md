# The Battle with Amalek

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 17:8–16

## Summary

**Israel's first battle as a people comes before Sinai and before the law, in the same camp as the water from
the rock, and it is won on a hill by a man who cannot keep his hands up.** *"Then Amalek came and fought with
Israel in Rephidim"* (17:8). Moses sends a man the text has never mentioned — ***Joshua*** — to choose men and
fight, and himself goes up *"the top of the hill"* with *"God's rod"* in his hand, with Aaron and a second man
the text has never mentioned, ***Hur***. **The battle goes whichever way Moses' hand goes**; his hands grow
*heavy*; Aaron and Hur sit him on a stone and hold his hands up, one on each side, *"until sunset"*; and Joshua
defeats Amalek. Then three things are done so that the day will not be forgotten: **God tells Moses to write
it in a book** — the first *write* in Scripture — and that he will ***"utterly blot out the memory of Amalek
from under the sky"***; **Moses builds an altar and names it *Yahweh our Banner*** (*YHWH nissî*); and **Moses
says that Yahweh will have war with Amalek *"from generation to generation"***.

The pericope moves in four steps:

- **The attack (17:8).** *"Then Amalek came and fought with Israel in Rephidim."* No cause is given.
- **The orders (17:9–10).** Moses to Joshua: *"Choose men for us, and go out, fight with Amalek. Tomorrow I
  will stand on the top of the hill with God's rod in my hand."* Joshua obeys; *"Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up
  to the top of the hill."*
- **The hands (17:11–13).** ***"When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed. When he let down his hand,
  Amalek prevailed."*** The stone, Aaron and Hur, *"His hands were steady until sunset"*; *"Joshua defeated
  Amalek and his people with the edge of the sword."*
- **The memorial (17:14–16).** The book, *"rehearse it in the ears of Joshua"*, the blotting out; the altar
  *Yahweh our Banner*; *"Yahweh will have war with Amalek from generation to generation."*

**It answers the question the last pericope ended on.** At the rock the people asked ***"Is Yahweh among us, or
not?"*** (17:7). The next verse is *"Then Amalek came"*. **The text does not connect the two, and it does not
need to: the answer is given on the hill**, where Israel prevails exactly as long as God's rod is lifted. See
[[Water from the Rock]].

**And it breaks the pattern of the wilderness so far.** Marah, Sin, and Rephidim were each a need, a complaint,
Moses' cry, and God's provision (see [[Murmuring]]). **Here there is no complaint and no cry.** The threat comes
from outside, Israel does the fighting, and **the only words anyone speaks to God are the name of an altar**.
**And it is the first time since the sea that Israel fights at all** — at the sea Moses said *"Yahweh will
fight for you, and you shall be still"* (14:14); at Rephidim Israel fights and God's part is a raised rod.
See [[War]].

### Where it sits

| | Verses | What happens | Who fights |
|---|---|---|---|
| The sea | 14:13–31 | *"Yahweh will fight for you, and you shall be still"* | God alone |
| The song | 15:3 | *"Yahweh is a man of war"* | God, sung |
| **Rephidim** | **17:8–16** | ***"Choose men for us, and go out, fight"***; the raised hand | **Israel, with the rod lifted** |
| Next | 18:1–27 | Jethro hears of everything and comes to the camp | — |

**God was going to keep Israel from war.** He led them the long way *"Lest perhaps the people change their minds
when they see war, and they return to Egypt"* (13:17). **Rephidim is the war he did not take them round.**
**It is followed by the visit of a different foreign people's priest** — Jethro of Midian, who comes in peace
and blesses Yahweh (18:1–12; [[Jethro's Counsel]]). **Two foreign peoples meet Israel between the sea and Sinai,
one with a sword and one with a sacrifice.**

## Key verses

### Then Amalek came (17:8)

> **8** Then Amalek came and fought with Israel in Rephidim.

***"Amalek"*** — ***ʿămālēq***, **the name of a man used for a people**, as *Israel* is in the same sentence.
**Genesis has introduced both uses already**: *"all the country of the Amalekites"* in Abram's war (Genesis
14:7), and Amalek the grandson of [[Esau]], son of Eliphaz by the concubine [[Timna]] (Genesis 36:12, 16).
**Exodus does not say which it means**, and says nothing about the people's origin at all. **Exodus gives no
reason for the attack**; Deuteronomy supplies one forty years on: *"how he met you by the way, and struck the
rearmost of you, all who were feeble behind you, when you were faint and weary; and he didn't fear God"*
(Deuteronomy 25:18; WEB; *not yet ingested*). **That detail — an attack on the stragglers — is not in Exodus**,
which describes a pitched battle with Joshua's chosen men. **The wiki records both accounts and does not
harmonize them.** See [[Amalek]] and
[[Amalek - From a Concubine's Son to Israel's First Enemy]].

***"Fought"*** — ***wayyillāḥem***, from ***lāḥam***. **The first time in Exodus that a human enemy fights
Israel.** Pharaoh's first fear was that Israel would *"join our enemies, and fight against us"* if *"war"*
came (1:10), and at the sea it was Yahweh who *"fights"* for Israel against Egypt (14:14, 25). **Now someone
fights Israel, and Israel fights back.** The noun ***milḥāmâ***, *war*, has occurred in Exodus at 1:10, 13:17,
and 15:3 before its last word in this pericope (17:16) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus).

***"In Rephidim"*** — ***birpîdim***. **The same camp as the water from the rock, one verse later.** The rod
that struck the rock (17:5–6) is in Moses' hand again by 17:9. See [[Rephidim]].

### Choose men for us (17:9–10)

> **9** Moses said to Joshua, "Choose men for us, and go out, fight with Amalek. Tomorrow I will stand on the
> top of the hill with God's rod in my hand."
>
> **10** So Joshua did as Moses had told him, and fought with Amalek; and Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to
> the top of the hill.

***"Joshua"*** — ***yəhôšuaʿ***. **The first time the name appears in Scripture** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy), **and Joshua is not introduced**: no father, no tribe, no explanation of why Moses has a
commander. **Exodus will give him a role before it gives him a family** — *"Joshua, his servant"*
(*məšārətô*, 24:13) — and the patronymic only at 33:11, *"his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man"*.
**Numbers says this is not even his first name**: *"Moses called Hoshea the son of Nun Joshua"* (Numbers 13:16;
WEB; *not yet ingested*) — Hebrew *hôšēaʿ* to *yəhôšuaʿ*, the divine name added to the front (WLC). **Exodus
uses the later name from his first line**: the narrator writes from after the renaming. The name is commonly derived from
*Yahweh* and the root *yāšaʿ*, *to save* — the verb of *"Thus Yahweh saved Israel that day"* (14:30; WLC) — a
standard etymology the text itself does not spell out. See [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] and
[[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]].

***"Choose men for us"*** — ***bəḥar-lānû ʾănāšîm***. **Not the whole people: chosen men.** **And *lānû*, *for
us*** — Moses includes himself. **The first act of Joshua's career is to obey** — *"So Joshua did as Moses had
told him"* (*wayyaʿaś yəhôšuaʿ kaʾăšer ʾāmar-lô mōšeh*) — **the same report the narrator gave of Moses at the
rock one verse earlier**, *"Moses did so"* (*wayyaʿaś kēn mōšeh*, 17:6; WLC).

***"Tomorrow I will stand on the top of the hill"*** — ***māḥār ʾānōkî niṣṣāb ʿal-rōʾš haggibʿâ***. **At the
rock God said *"I will stand before you"* (*ʿōmēd*, 17:6); here Moses says *"I will stand"* (*niṣṣāb*)**, a
different verb (WLC). ***Niṣṣāb*** is the word for taking a position: God sent Moses to *"stand by the river's
bank"* to meet Pharaoh with the rod (7:15), and will tell him *"you shall stand on the rock"* when his glory
passes (33:21) (WLC). ***Gibʿâ***, *hill*, occurs once before this in Scripture, in Jacob's blessing of
Joseph, *"the ancient hills"* (Genesis 49:26; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **The hill is not named,
and nothing is built on it.**

***"With God's rod in my hand"*** — ***ûmaṭṭēh hāʾĕlōhîm bəyādî***. **The second time Exodus calls it *God's*
rod**, after Moses took it from Midian, *"Moses took God's rod in his hand"* (4:20; WLC). In between it has been
*"the rod which was turned to a serpent"* (7:15) and, one paragraph ago, *"the rod … with which you struck the
Nile"* (17:5). **The same object is named by its owner here, and the rest of the scene will be about the hand
that holds it.**

***"Moses, Aaron, and Hur"*** — ***mōšeh ʾahărōn wəḥûr***. **Hur appears without introduction, as Joshua did.**
He is named again only once in the narrative — *"Behold, Aaron and Hur are with you. Whoever is involved in a
dispute can go to them"* (24:14) — when Moses leaves the camp for the mountain. **A *Hur* is also Bezalel's
grandfather**, *"Bezalel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah"* (31:2), and Chronicles makes
that Hur a son of Caleb and Ephrath (1 Chronicles 2:19–20; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). **Whether the Hur of
the hill is Bezalel's grandfather the text does not say**; the wiki records the possibility and does not
adopt it. Later Jewish tradition supplies more about him (including that he was Miriam's husband); **those
sources have not been checked here and are not quoted.** See [[Hur (companion of Moses)]].

### When Moses held up his hand (17:11)

> **11** When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed. When he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed.

***"Held up his hand"*** — ***yārîm mōšeh yādô***. ***Rûm***, *to raise*, is the verb of Aaron's rod lifted over
the Nile — *"he lifted up the rod"* (7:20) — and of God's command at the sea, *"Lift up your rod, and stretch
out your hand over the sea"* (14:16); **and Israel left Egypt *"with a high hand"*, *bəyād rāmâ*, the same verb
as an adjective** (14:8) (WLC lemma search, Exodus). **The hand is singular** (*yādô*) **in 17:11 and becomes
plural in 17:12** (*yədê*, *yādāyw*) (WLC) — **one hand holding the rod becomes two hands that need holding.**

**The text does not say what the raised hand means.** It does not say Moses prayed, does not say the rod was
pointed at the enemy, and does not say the soldiers could see it. **Readings reported, not adopted**: the
raised hand as **prayer** (the gesture of spreading the hands to God elsewhere in Scripture); as a **signal**
or standard visible to the troops, which the altar's name *Yahweh our Banner* might support; or as the **rod
itself**, God's instrument in Moses' hand as at the sea. **The rabbinic question whether Moses' hands could make
or break a war** is commonly cited from the Mishnah (Rosh Hashanah 3:8, by reference); **that source has not
been checked here.** What the text does say is only the correlation, stated twice in parallel.

***"Prevailed"*** — ***wəgābar***. ***Gābar*** is the verb of the flood: four times *"the waters prevailed"*
in the Hebrew of Genesis 7:18–24, which the WEB renders *"rose"*, *"flooded"*, and the like, and once in
Jacob's blessing (Genesis 49:26) (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **This is its first use for a people.**

***"When he let down his hand"*** — ***wəkaʾăšer yānîaḥ yādô***. **The verb is *nûaḥ*, *to rest*** — the
locusts *"rested"* on Egypt (10:14), and God *"rested the seventh day"* (20:11) (WLC lemma search, Exodus).
**When Moses' hand *rests*, Amalek prevails.** **The same verb comes back in Deuteronomy's command**: *"when
Yahweh your God has given you rest (bəhānîaḥ) from all your enemies … you shall blot out the memory of
Amalek"* (Deuteronomy 25:19; WEB; WLC). **That echo is a reading — the shared lemma is a fact, the link
between the two uses is not drawn by either text.**

### His hands were steady (17:12–13)

> **12** But Moses' hands were heavy; and they took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat on it. Aaron and
> Hur held up his hands, the one on the one side, and the other on the other side. His hands were steady until
> sunset.
>
> **13** Joshua defeated Amalek and his people with the edge of the sword.

***"Moses' hands were heavy"*** — ***wîdê mōšeh kəbēdîm***. ***Kābēd*** **is the word Exodus has used for
Pharaoh's heart** — *"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn (kābēd)"* (7:14) — **and the word Moses used of himself at
the bush**: *"I am slow (kəbad) of speech, and of a slow (ûkəbad) tongue"* (4:10, literally *heavy of mouth and
heavy of tongue*) (WLC). **Moses' weakness has moved from his mouth to his hands, and again someone else
supplies what he lacks**: at the bush God gave Aaron to be his mouth (4:14–16); on the hill Aaron and Hur become
his hands. **The same adjective returns at once**: in the next chapter Jethro tells him *"the thing is too heavy
(kābēd) for you. You are not able to perform it yourself alone"* (18:18; WLC). See [[Jethro's Counsel]] and
[[Mediation and Advocacy]].

***"They took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat on it"*** — ***wayyiqḥû ʾeben wayyāśîmû taḥtāyw
wayyēšeb ʿālêhā***. **The stone is not an altar, a pillar, or a monument** — Jacob set up stones and anointed them
(Genesis 28:18; 35:14) — **it is a seat.** **The man who could not keep his arms up is not made to stand.**

***"Aaron and Hur held up his hands"*** — ***wəʾahărōn wəḥûr tāməkû bəyādāyw***. ***Tāmak***, *to grasp, hold
up*, **occurs once before this in Scripture**: when Jacob crossed his hands over Joseph's sons, *"He held up
(wayyitmōk) his father's hand, to remove it from Ephraim's head to Manasseh's head"* (Genesis 48:17; WLC lemma
search, Genesis–Deuteronomy, where these are its only two occurrences). **Joseph held up a hand to move it;
Aaron and Hur hold up hands to keep them where they are.** See [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]].

***"The one on the one side, and the other on the other side"*** — ***mizzeh ʾeḥād ûmizzeh ʾeḥād***. **Moses'
hands are held from both sides**, as the waters stood *"a wall to them on their right hand, and on their left"*
(14:22). That is a visual likeness only; the vocabulary is different.

***"His hands were steady"*** — ***wayhî yādāyw ʾĕmûnâ***, literally *his hands were steadiness* —
***ʾĕmûnâ***, **the word later translated *faithfulness* and *faith*. This is its first occurrence in
Scripture** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Its only other use in the Torah is in the Song of Moses,
in the same verse that calls God *the Rock***: *"The Rock, his work is perfect … A God of faithfulness (ʾēl
ʾĕmûnâ) who does no wrong"* (Deuteronomy 32:4; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). **The root is *ʾ-m-n*, the root of
*believe*** — *"they believed (wayyaʾămînû) in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses"* (14:31) and Abram's *"He
believed in Yahweh"* (Genesis 15:6). **And Habakkuk's *"the righteous will live by his faith"* is *beʾĕmûnātô***
(Habakkuk 2:4; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*). **In Exodus 17 the word describes arms that do not drop, held up by
other people.** Whatever later uses make of it, here it is physical steadiness. See [[Faith]].

***"Until sunset"*** — ***ʿad-bōʾ haššāmeš***. **The battle lasts a day**, *"tomorrow"* (17:9) to the evening.

***"Joshua defeated Amalek and his people"*** — ***wayyaḥălōš yəhôšuaʿ ʾet-ʿămālēq wəʾet-ʿammô***. ***Ḥālaš*** occurs
nowhere else in the Torah (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy); its sense is *to weaken, overpower*. **The
text says *defeated*, not *destroyed***: Amalek survives the day, which is why the next three verses are
about remembering and about a war that does not end.

***"With the edge of the sword"*** — ***ləpî-ḥāreb***, literally *by the mouth of the sword*. **The phrase's
first occurrence in Scripture is Simeon and Levi's slaughter at Shechem**: *"They killed Hamor and Shechem
his son with the edge of the sword"* (Genesis 34:26; WLC). **There it was a massacre the patriarch cursed**
(Genesis 49:5–7); **here it is a defence, commanded and won.** See [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]].

### Write this for a memorial in a book (17:14)

> **14** Yahweh said to Moses, "Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua: that
> I will utterly blot out the memory of Amalek from under the sky."

***"Write"*** — ***kətōb***. **The first time anyone in Scripture is told to write, and the first occurrence
of the verb *kātab* at all** (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **Genesis has a *book*** — *"This is the
book (sēper) of the generations of Adam"* (Genesis 5:1) — **but no one in Genesis writes.** **The next things
written in Exodus are the covenant** (*"Moses wrote all Yahweh's words"*, 24:4) and **the tablets, *"written
with God's finger"*** (31:18) (WLC; *not yet ingested*). **The first thing God orders written down is a war, not a
law.** See [[Remembrance]].

***"For a memorial"*** — ***zikkārôn***. **The Passover was *"a memorial"*** (12:14) and the unleavened bread
*"a memorial between your eyes"* (13:9) (WLC). **Those were rites to be kept; this is a text to be kept.**
***"In a book"*** — ***bassēper***, with the article: *in the book*. **The text does not say which book**, and
whether it means a particular scroll or *"a book"* in general — the WEB's choice — is debated; the wiki records
the article and does not adopt a reading.

***"Rehearse it in the ears of Joshua"*** — ***wəśîm bəʾoznê yəhôšuaʿ***, literally *put it in Joshua's ears*.
**The memorial has two forms, one written and one spoken, and the spoken one has a named hearer.** **Why Joshua
is singled out the text does not say**; he is the man who fought, and in Deuteronomy and Joshua he becomes the
man who will lead Israel into the land where Deuteronomy says the blotting out is to be done (Deuteronomy
25:19; 31:7; *not yet ingested*). That is a reading.

***"I will utterly blot out the memory of Amalek"*** — ***kî-māḥōh ʾemḥeh ʾet-zēker ʿămālēq***. **The infinitive
before the finite verb** — *blotting I will blot* — is the emphatic form the WEB renders *"utterly"* (WLC).
***Māḥâ***, *to wipe out*, **is the verb of the flood**: *"I will destroy (ʾemḥeh) man"* (Genesis 6:7), *"I will
destroy every living thing"* (7:4), *"Every living thing was destroyed (wayyimaḥ)"* (7:23) — **the WEB renders
each *destroy*, but the Hebrew word is the one translated *blot out* here** (WLC lemma search,
Genesis–Deuteronomy, where these three verses are Genesis's only uses; 7:23 has it twice). **Its next use in Exodus is Moses' own offer
after the golden calf**: *"please blot me out (məḥēnî) of your book (missiprəkā) which you have written
(kātābtā)"*, and God's reply, *"Whoever has sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book"* (32:32–33;
WLC). **Book, write, and blot out occur together twice in Exodus: here, of an enemy's memory, and at the calf,
of a name in God's book.** See
[[Blot Out - The Flood, Amalek, and God's Book]].

***"The memory of Amalek"*** — ***zēker ʿămālēq***. ***Zēker*** **has occurred once before in Scripture: God's
own name at the bush**, *"This is my name forever, and this is my memorial (zikrî) to all generations (lədōr
dōr)"* (3:15; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy). **God's *zēker* lasts *to all generations*; Amalek's is to
be blotted out, by a war that lasts *"from generation to generation"* (17:16).** **And the command is written
down so that the blotting out will be remembered** — the paradox Deuteronomy puts into two clauses: *"you shall
blot out the memory of Amalek from under the sky. You shall not forget"* (Deuteronomy 25:19; WEB; WLC; *not yet
ingested*).

**Exodus 17:14 and Deuteronomy 25:19 differ on who blots out.** Here God says ***"I will"*** blot out
(*ʾemḥeh*, first person); in Deuteronomy Israel is told ***"you shall"*** (*timḥeh*, second person) (WLC). **The
wiki records both and does not harmonize them**; the later history in 1 Samuel 15, where Saul is sent to *"utterly
destroy"* Amalek (1 Samuel 15:3; WEB; *ḥāram* in the Hebrew, a different verb; WLC; *not yet ingested*), treats
it as Israel's task.

***"From under the sky"*** — ***mittaḥat haššāmāyim***. **The flood's range**: God would destroy all flesh
*"from under the sky"* (Genesis 6:17, by reference to `raw/`). **Deuteronomy uses the same phrase with *māḥâ*
for Israel's own name when God threatens it after the calf** — *"blot out their name from under the sky"*
(Deuteronomy 9:14; WEB; WLC) — and for the apostate in 29:20 (WEB; 29:19 Hebrew).

### Yahweh our Banner (17:15–16)

> **15** Moses built an altar, and called its name Yahweh our Banner.
>
> **16** He said, "Yah has sworn: 'Yahweh will have war with Amalek from generation to generation.'"

***"Moses built an altar"*** — ***wayyiben mōšeh mizbēaḥ***. **The first altar in Exodus** (WLC lemma search,
Exodus), **and the first built by Moses.** **No sacrifice is recorded on it.** **Altars that are named have
precedents in Genesis**: Jacob called his altar at Shechem *El Elohe Israel* (Genesis 33:20) and his altar at
Bethel *El Beth El* (35:7); Abraham named the place on Moriah *Yahweh Will Provide* (22:14). **Like those, this
altar is named for what God did, and its name is a sentence about him.** **The next altar in Exodus is God's
own instruction**: *"You shall make an altar of earth for me … In every place where I record (ʾazkîr) my name I
will come to you"* (20:24; WLC) — *zākar*, the root of *memorial*. See [[Sacrifice and Offering]],
[[The Names of God]], and [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

***"Yahweh our Banner"*** — ***YHWH nissî***, literally ***Yahweh is my banner*** (WLC: *nissî*, first-person
singular). **The WEB's *"our"* is a translation choice; the Hebrew suffix is *my*.** ***Nēs*** **is a pole or
standard raised high where it can be seen.** **In the Torah the noun occurs only here (in the name) and in
Numbers**, most notably for the pole on which Moses sets the bronze serpent: *"Make a venomous snake, and set it
on a pole (nēs)"* (Numbers 21:8–9; WEB; WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy; *not yet ingested*). **The altar's
name may explain the raised hand of 17:11 — Yahweh, not the rod, is what was lifted up over the battle.** That
is a reading the name invites; the text does not draw it.

***"Yah has sworn"*** — ***kî-yād ʿal-kēs yāh***. **The WEB's rendering is an interpretation of four obscure Hebrew
words**, literally ***"for a hand upon the kēs of Yah"***. ***Kēs*** **occurs nowhere else in the WLC books
searched** (Genesis–Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, Samuel, 1 Chronicles, Esther, Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah,
Habakkuk), and is usually taken as a short form of ***kissēʾ***, *throne*. **Readings reported, not adopted**:

| Reading | Sense | Whose hand |
|---|---|---|
| *kēs* = *kissēʾ*, throne; the hand raised in an oath | *"A hand upon the throne of Yah!"* — an oath sworn by God's throne, hence the WEB's *"Yah has sworn"* | God's, or Moses' |
| the same, the hand raised against the throne | *"a hand against the throne of Yah"* — Amalek's attack as an assault on God himself, which is why the war is perpetual | Amalek's |
| *kēs* a copying error for *nēs* (one letter, *k* for *n*) | *"a hand upon the banner of Yah"* — tying 17:16 to the altar's name in 17:15 | Moses' |

**None of these is certain, and the wiki adopts none.** **The verse's second half is clear**: ***milḥāmâ
laYHWH baʿămālēq middōr dōr***, *war for Yahweh with Amalek from generation to generation*. ***Middōr dōr*** **echoes
the bush, *lədōr dōr*** (3:15; WLC) — **the name that lasts and the war that lasts are measured the same way.**

**The war the verse foresees is carried through the rest of the Hebrew Bible.** Balaam: *"Amalek was the first
of the nations, but his latter end shall come to destruction"* (Numbers 24:20; WEB); Deuteronomy 25:17–19;
Saul's war and its failure (1 Samuel 15:2–3; WEB, *"I remember what Amalek did to Israel"* — Hebrew
*pāqadtî*, *I have attended to*, a different verb from *zākar*; WLC); and Haman *"the Agagite"* (Esther 3:1;
WEB), whom later readers connect to Agag the Amalekite king of 1 Samuel 15. **The Esther link is an inference
from the name *Agag*; Esther never mentions Amalek.** All *not yet ingested*. See
[[Amalek - From a Concubine's Son to Israel's First Enemy]].

## People
- [[God]] — gives the victory while the rod is lifted; commands the first writing in Scripture; ***"I will
  utterly blot out the memory of Amalek from under the sky"***; named in the altar, *Yahweh our Banner*; swears
  (on the WEB's reading) a war *"from generation to generation"*

## Notes

- **The boundary was kept as planned.** 17:8 opens a new event in the same camp, and 18:1 opens a new one
  (*"Now Jethro … heard"*).
- **Tensions recorded, not resolved:** Exodus's pitched battle against Deuteronomy 25:18's attack on the
  stragglers; God's *"I will blot out"* (17:14) against Israel's *"you shall blot out"* (Deuteronomy 25:19);
  Amalek the Edomite grandson of Genesis 36 against the Amalekite country already in Genesis 14:7 (see
  [[Amalek]]).
- **Readings reported, not adopted:** the meaning of the raised hand (prayer, signal, or rod); the altar's name
  as the explanation of the raised hand; the three readings of *kēs* in 17:16; whether *bassēper* is a
  particular book; why the memorial is put in Joshua's ears; *nûaḥ* in 17:11 and Deuteronomy 25:19 as a
  deliberate echo; the Hur of 17:10 as Bezalel's grandfather. **Unchecked and not quoted:** the Mishnah on
  Moses' hands (Rosh Hashanah 3:8), and the later Jewish traditions about Hur.
- **WEB *"our Banner"*** renders a Hebrew first-person singular, *nissî*, *my banner*; **WEB *"Yah has sworn"***
  is an interpretation of an obscure clause. Both are flagged at the point of the quotation.
- **New pages:** the people [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] and [[Hur (companion of Moses)]]; the connections
  [[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]] and [[Blot Out - The Flood, Amalek, and God's Book]]. **Joshua's page is
  qualified by his patronymic**, which disambiguates him both from the book [[Joshua]] and from the later
  Joshuas of Scripture (the high priest of Haggai and Zechariah); **Hur's page is qualified by his role**,
  because Exodus gives him no father and the Hur of 31:2 and Numbers 31:8 may be other men.
