# Frogs, Lice, and Flies

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 8:1–32

## Summary

**Three plagues, and in each one something new is conceded.** The frogs bring the first request
from Pharaoh, the lice the first confession from his magicians, and the flies the first line drawn
between Israel and Egypt. **And all three end the same way: Pharaoh's heart, and no release.**

**The frogs (8:1–15).** [[Moses]] carries the demand of 5:1 into the palace: *"Let my people go, that
they may serve me"*, with a threat — frogs from the river in the house, the bedroom, the bed, the
ovens and the kneading troughs (8:1–4). [[Aaron]] stretches out the rod and the frogs cover the land;
**the magicians do the same, and so there are more frogs** (8:5–7). **Then, for the first time,
Pharaoh asks**: *"Entreat Yahweh, that he take away the frogs … and I will let the people go"* (8:8).
Moses lets him choose the hour — *"Tomorrow"* — so *"that you may know that there is no one like
Yahweh our God"* (8:9–10). Moses cries to Yahweh, and ***"Yahweh did according to the word of
Moses"*** (8:13). The frogs die where they are, are piled in heaps, and *"the land stank"* (8:14).
**When Pharaoh sees *"a respite"*, he hardens his heart** (8:15).

**The lice (8:16–19).** No warning this time. Aaron strikes *"the dust of the earth"*, and it becomes
lice on man and animal *"throughout all the land of Egypt"* (8:16–17). **The magicians try, and
can't**, and they say to Pharaoh: ***"This is God's finger"*** (8:18–19). He does not listen.

**The flies (8:20–32).** Moses is sent to meet Pharaoh *"early in the morning"* at the water again,
and this time God adds a promise of separation: ***"I will set apart in that day the land of
Goshen"***, *"to the end you may know that I am Yahweh on the earth"*; *"I will put a division
between my people and your people"* (8:20–23). The swarms come and *"the land was corrupted"* (8:24).
**Then Pharaoh bargains**: *"Go, sacrifice to your God in the land!"* (8:25). Moses refuses — Israel's
sacrifice would be *"the abomination of the Egyptians"*, and they would be stoned — and holds to
*"three days' journey into the wilderness"* (8:26–27). Pharaoh agrees, with a limit: *"only you shall
not go very far away. Pray for me"* (8:28). Moses warns him not to *"deal deceitfully any more"*, prays,
and the flies go, ***"There remained not one"*** (8:29–31). **Pharaoh hardens his heart *"this time
also"*** (8:32).

### The shape of it

| | verses | warning? | who strikes | magicians | Pharaoh | the heart |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **frogs** | 8:1–15 | yes, in the palace | Aaron's hand over the waters | **match it** | **asks for prayer; promises release** | *"he hardened his heart"* (*kābēd*, Pharaoh the subject) |
| **lice** | 8:16–19 | **none** | Aaron strikes the dust | **fail; *"God's finger"*** | says nothing | *"was hardened"* (*ḥāzaq*, no agent) |
| **flies** | 8:20–32 | yes, at the water, in the morning | **Yahweh himself** (8:24) | absent | **bargains twice; asks for prayer** | *"hardened his heart this time also"* (*kābēd*, Pharaoh the subject) |

**Three things move across the chapter.** **The magicians** go from match, to failure, to silence.
**The striking hand** goes from Aaron's to God's: in the frogs and lice Aaron stretches out the rod;
in the flies no rod is mentioned and *"Yahweh did so"* (8:24). **And the land** goes from all Egypt
alike to Egypt with Goshen cut out of it. With [[Water Turned to Blood]], blood–frogs–lice form the
first of three cycles of three, each opening with a morning meeting at the water or before Pharaoh
(7:15; 8:20; 9:13), and each cycle's third plague arriving unannounced (8:16; 9:8; 10:21).

## Key verses

### The frogs threatened (8:1–4)

> **2** If you refuse to let them go, behold, I will plague all your borders with frogs:
> **3** and the river shall swarm with frogs, which shall go up and come into your house, and into
> your bedroom, and on your bed, and into the house of your servants, and on your people, and into
> your ovens, and into your kneading troughs:

**A note on numbering first.** The Hebrew text puts 8:1–4 at the end of chapter 7 (7:26–29), so
Hebrew 8:1–28 is English 8:5–32. **This page uses English numbers throughout**, as the WEB does, and
gives Hebrew numbers only where a Hebrew form is cited.

***"I will plague all your borders"*** — ***nōgēp ʾet-kol-gəbûləkā***. *Nāgap*, *to strike* or *to
smite*, is not the *nākâ* of the river (7:17, 20). **Its next uses in Exodus are at the Passover**:
Yahweh will pass over *"to strike"* the Egyptians, and the destroyer will not *"strike"* Israel's
houses (12:23, 27) (WLC lemma search). **The word of the frogs is the word of the last night.**

***"The river shall swarm with frogs"*** — ***wəšāraṣ hayəʾōr ṣəpardəʿîm***. ***Šāraṣ*** is the verb of
creation's fifth day — *"Let the waters abound with living creatures"* (Genesis 1:20) — of the blessing
renewed after the flood, *"Increase abundantly in the earth"* (9:7), and of Israel in Egypt: *"The
children of Israel were fruitful, and increased abundantly"* (Exodus 1:7). **In Genesis and Exodus it
occurs only at Genesis 1:20, 21; 7:21; 8:17; 9:7; Exodus 1:7; and here** (WLC lemma search). **Israel
*swarmed* in Egypt and Pharaoh tried to stop it; now Egypt's river *swarms*, and he cannot.** The
creation verb is turned against the king who fought it. See [[Creation]] and
[[The Flood as Un-Creation]].

**The list goes into the house, room by room**: house, bedroom (*ḥădar miškābəkā*), bed, servants'
houses, people, ovens, kneading troughs. **The blood stayed outside, in the river and the jars; the
frogs come to bed and into the bread.** The kneading troughs (*mišʾărôt*) will appear once more in
Exodus, when Israel carries its unleavened dough out of Egypt, *"their kneading troughs being bound
up in their clothes on their shoulders"* (12:34).

### The frogs come, and Pharaoh asks (8:5–10)

> **7** The magicians did the same thing with their enchantments, and brought up frogs on the land of
> Egypt.
> **8** Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron, and said, "Entreat Yahweh, that he take away the frogs
> from me, and from my people; and I will let the people go, that they may sacrifice to Yahweh."
> **9** Moses said to Pharaoh, "I give you the honor of setting the time that I should pray for you,
> and for your servants, and for your people, that the frogs be destroyed from you and your houses,
> and remain in the river only."
> **10** He said, "Tomorrow." He said, "Be it according to your word, that you may know that there is
> no one like Yahweh our God.

***"The frogs came up, and covered the land of Egypt"*** (8:6) — ***wattəkas***, from *kāsâ*, *to
cover*. It is the verb of the sea at 14:28: *"The waters returned, and covered the chariots and the
horsemen"* (*wayəkassû*) (WLC). **The magicians' match** (*wayyaʿăśû-kēn*, 8:7) **again adds to the
plague instead of lifting it**, exactly as at 7:22. **What they cannot do is make frogs go away**, and
that is the thing Pharaoh needs. See [[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]].

***"Entreat Yahweh"*** — ***haʿtîrû ʾel-YHWH***. **The man who said *"I don't know Yahweh"* (5:2) now
names him, and asks for him.** ***ʿĀtar*** occurs in Genesis only at 25:21 — *"Isaac entreated Yahweh
for his wife, because she was barren. Yahweh was entreated by him"* — and in Exodus only in the
plagues: here five times (8:8, 9, 28, 29, 30) and then at 9:28 and 10:17–18 (WLC lemma search). **It is
the verb of a petition that is granted**, and every time Pharaoh uses it he is the one asking for it.
See [[Prayer]].

***"And I will let the people go, that they may sacrifice to Yahweh"*** — ***waʾăšalləḥâ ʾet-hāʿām***.
**The first promise of release.** Pharaoh concedes in the frogs what he refused in the rods and the
blood: the God, the request, and the people's going.

***"I give you the honor"*** — ***hitpāʾēr ʿālay***, literally *glorify yourself over me*, or *have the
advantage of me*. **The verb occurs nowhere else in Genesis or Exodus** (WLC lemma search). Moses hands
the initiative to Pharaoh — *you name the time* — **so that the timing itself is the proof**: whatever
hour Pharaoh picks, the frogs will die on it.

***"Tomorrow"*** — ***ləmāḥār***. **Pharaoh, offered relief now, picks tomorrow.** The text gives no
reason. One night more with the frogs is a strange choice for a man who has just begged; the narrative
lets it stand.

***"That you may know that there is no one like Yahweh our God"*** — ***ləmaʿan tēdaʿ kî-ʾên kaYHWH
ʾĕlōhênû***. **The knowledge refrain of 7:17, sharpened**: not only *that I am Yahweh* but *that there
is none like him*. It will come again at 9:14 — *"that you may know that there is no one like me in all
the earth"* — and Israel will sing it at the sea: *"Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?"* (15:11).
See [[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]] and [[The Names of God]].

### The frogs end, and the heart (8:12–15)

> **12** Moses and Aaron went out from Pharaoh, and Moses cried to Yahweh concerning the frogs which
> he had brought on Pharaoh.
> **13** Yahweh did according to the word of Moses, and the frogs died out of the houses, out of the
> courts, and out of the fields.
> **14** They gathered them together in heaps, and the land stank.
> **15** But when Pharaoh saw that there was a respite, he hardened his heart, and didn't listen to
> them, as Yahweh had spoken.

***"Moses cried to Yahweh"*** — ***wayyiṣʿaq mōšeh ʾel-YHWH***. ***Ṣāʿaq*** has a history in Exodus
already (WLC lemma search). Pharaoh mocked Israel with it — *"they are idle; therefore they cry,
saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God'"* (5:8) — and the foremen *"cried to Pharaoh"* (5:15).
**Israel cried to Pharaoh and was refused; Moses now cries to Yahweh for Pharaoh, and is heard.** The
cry to sacrifice that Pharaoh called idleness is the request he now grants, for a day. See
[[Bricks Without Straw]].

***"Yahweh did according to the word of Moses"*** — ***wayyaʿaś YHWH kidbar mōšeh***. **The formula of
obedience turned round.** Through 7:6, 10, 20 it was Moses and Aaron who *"did so, as Yahweh
commanded"*; here **Yahweh does as Moses said**, to the hour Moses set and Pharaoh chose. It recurs at
8:31. **The prophet who is *"as God to Pharaoh"* (7:1) is answered as an intercessor.** See
[[Mediation and Advocacy]] and [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

***"The frogs died"*** — not *went back to the river*, as Moses had said they would *"remain in the river
only"* (8:9, 11). **The frogs already in the land die where they are**; only those in the river remain.
**Relief comes as a heap of carcasses.**

***"They gathered them together in heaps"*** — ***ḥŏmārim ḥŏmārim***, *heaps, heaps*, the noun doubled.
**The WLC tags it with the same lemma as *ḥōmer*, *mortar* or *clay*** — Babel's *"tar for mortar"*
(Genesis 11:3) and Israel's labour *"in mortar and in brick"* (Exodus 1:14); in Genesis and Exodus
those are its only other occurrences (WLC lemma search). **The homonym is real; whether the narrator
meant the play is not something the text says.** But the picture it allows is exact: **the Egyptians
who worked Israel in *ḥōmer* now shovel their own *ḥŏmārîm* — of dead frogs.** See
[[Slavery and Servitude]].

***"And the land stank"*** — ***wattibʾaš hāʾāreṣ***, the verb of the foremen's curse (*"you have made
us a stench"*, 5:21) and of the Nile (7:18, 21). **Now it is the whole land.**

***"When Pharaoh saw that there was a respite"*** — ***kî hāytâ hārəwāḥâ***. *Rəwāḥâ*, *breathing
room*, occurs nowhere else in Genesis or Exodus (WLC lemma search). ***"He hardened his heart"*** —
***wəhakbēd ʾet-libbô***, the hiphil of *kābēd*, *he made his heart heavy*. **This is the first time
in Exodus that Pharaoh is the grammatical subject of his own hardening.** God forecast it (4:21, *"I
will harden"*; 7:3, *"I will harden"*); the narrator reported it without an agent (7:13, 22); God
described it (7:14, *"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn"*). **Now the narrator names Pharaoh as the one who
does it**, at the precise moment pressure is taken off — and still adds *"as Yahweh had spoken"*. See
[[Hardening of the Heart]].

### The lice, and God's finger (8:16–19)

> **17** They did so; and Aaron stretched out his hand with his rod, and struck the dust of the earth,
> and there were lice on man, and on animal; all the dust of the earth became lice throughout all the
> land of Egypt.
> **18** The magicians tried with their enchantments to produce lice, but they couldn't. There were lice
> on man, and on animal.
> **19** Then the magicians said to Pharaoh, "This is God's finger:" and Pharaoh's heart was hardened,
> and he didn't listen to them; as Yahweh had spoken.

**No warning, no audience, no bargain.** The third plague of the cycle goes straight from command to
blow.

***"Strike the dust of the earth"*** — ***wəhak ʾet-ʿăpar hāʾāreṣ***. **The rod returns to *nākâ*, the
verb of the river** (7:17, 20), **and now it strikes the ground.** *ʿĂpar hāʾāreṣ*, *"the dust of the
earth"*, is the phrase of the promise to Abram — *"I will make your offspring as the dust of the
earth, so that if a man can count the dust of the earth, then your offspring may also be counted"*
(Genesis 13:16; again to Jacob, 28:14). **The promise made Abraham's seed as countless as the dust;
the plague makes the dust of Egypt as countless as lice.** Exodus draws no line between the two, and
the wiki records the shared phrase only.

***"Lice"*** — ***kinnîm*** (also *kinnām*). **The word occurs in the Pentateuch only in this pericope**
(WLC lemma search of Genesis and Exodus; it does not occur in Leviticus or Numbers either). Whether
the creatures are lice, gnats, mosquitoes, or ticks cannot be settled from the word; the WEB has
*"lice"*, other translations *gnats*. The wiki uses the WEB's word.

***"On man, and on animal"*** — ***bāʾādām ûbabbəhēmâ***. **The plague touches bodies for the first
time.** The blood and the frogs were in the water and the house; the lice are on the skin.

***"The magicians tried … but they couldn't"*** — ***wayyaʿăśû-kēn haḥarṭummîm … ləhôṣîʾ … wəlōʾ
yākōlû***. **The text uses the same *wayyaʿăśû-kēn*, *"they did so"*, it used for their success at 7:22
and 8:7**, and then adds *to bring out the lice, and they could not*. **The formula of their success
becomes the formula of their failure.**

***"This is God's finger"*** — ***ʾeṣbaʿ ʾĕlōhîm hîʾ***. **Three things are notable in four words**
(WLC):

- **The noun is *ʾĕlōhîm*, not *YHWH*.** The magicians do not use the name Moses has been preaching;
  they say *God*, or possibly *a god*. **Their confession is real, and limited.**
- ***ʾEṣbaʿ*, *finger***, occurs in Exodus only three times: here; at 29:12, the priest's finger putting
  blood on the altar's horns; and at 31:18, the tablets *"written with God's finger"* (WLC lemma search).
  **God's finger strikes the dust in Egypt and writes on stone at Sinai.**
- **A finger is less than a hand.** Exodus has spoken of a *"mighty hand"* (3:19), a *"strong hand"*
  (6:1), and an *"outstretched arm"* (6:6). **The magicians' word for what is beating them is the smallest part of
  it.**

**The confession is spoken to Pharaoh, and Pharaoh's heart *"was hardened"*** — ***wayyeḥĕzaq***,
qal of *ḥāzaq*, no agent, as at 7:13 and 7:22. **Here, as in the blood, Pharaoh asks for nothing.**
See [[The Finger of God - Exodus 8.19, Exodus 31.18, and Luke 11.20]] and
[[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]].

### The flies, and the line through Egypt (8:20–24)

> **20** Yahweh said to Moses, "Rise up early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh; behold, he comes
> out to the water; and tell him, 'This is what Yahweh says, "Let my people go, that they may serve me.
> **21** Else, if you will not let my people go, behold, I will send swarms of flies on you, and on your
> servants, and on your people, and into your houses: and the houses of the Egyptians shall be full of
> swarms of flies, and also the ground whereon they are.
> **22** I will set apart in that day the land of Goshen, in which my people dwell, that no swarms of
> flies shall be there; to the end you may know that I am Yahweh on the earth.
> **23** I will put a division between my people and your people: by tomorrow shall this sign be."'"
> **24** Yahweh did so; and there came grievous swarms of flies into the house of Pharaoh, and into his
> servants' houses: and in all the land of Egypt the land was corrupted by reason of the swarms of
> flies.

***"Rise up early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh"*** — ***haškēm babbōqer wəhityaṣṣēb lipnê
parʿōh***. *Hityaṣṣēb*, *take your stand*, is the verb of [[Miriam]] at the river: *"His sister stood
far off, to see what would be done to him"* (2:4, *wattētaṣṣab*). Its later uses in Exodus are 9:13;
Israel at the sea, *"Stand still, and see the salvation of Yahweh"* (14:13); the people at the foot of
Sinai (19:17); and Yahweh himself, who *"stood with him there"* on the mountain (34:5) (WLC lemma
search). **A girl stood at the river to watch over Moses; now Moses stands at the river against
Pharaoh.**

***"If you will not let my people go, behold, I will send"*** — ***kî ʾim-ʾênəkā məšallēaḥ ʾet-ʿammî
hinənî mašlîaḥ bəkā … ʾet-heʿārōb***. **The verb is *šālaḥ* on both sides**: *if you are not sending my
people, I am sending on you the swarm.* (WLC; the two participles are piel and hiphil of the same
root.) **The plague is the request in reverse.** Pharaoh will not send; God sends.

***"Swarms of flies"*** — ***heʿārōb***, a single noun, *the swarm*. **The word does not name an insect.**
It occurs only in this plague in the Pentateuch (seven times, 8:21–31; WLC lemma search) and is
related by most lexicons to *ʿārab*, *to mix*: hence old renderings like *mixed swarms*. The WEB's
*"flies"* follows the Psalms' retellings (78:45; 105:31), where the WEB also has *"swarms of flies"*.
Whether the Hebrew of those verses has the same word is not checked here.

***"The houses of the Egyptians shall be full"*** — ***ûmāləʾû***, *mālēʾ*, the last verb of 1:7 (*"the
land was filled with them"*) and of 7:25 (*"seven days were fulfilled"*). **Egypt was filled with
Israelites; now its houses are filled with swarms.**

***"I will set apart in that day the land of Goshen"*** — ***wəhiplêtî … ʾet-ʾereṣ gōšen***. ***Pālâ***,
*to distinguish*, *to treat as separate*, occurs in Exodus only four times, all in this thread (WLC
lemma search): here; 9:4 — *"Yahweh will make a distinction between the livestock of Israel and the
livestock of Egypt"*; 11:7 — *"that you may know that Yahweh makes a distinction between the Egyptians
and Israel"*; and 33:16, where Moses asks God to go with them *"so that we are separated, I and your
people, from all the people who are on the surface of the earth"*. **The distinction begins as
geography in the plagues and ends as Israel's identity at Sinai.** See [[Goshen]] and
[[Call and Election]].

**Exodus has not said, before this, where Israel lives.** Goshen was Joseph's grant (Genesis 45:10;
46:34; 47:6); Exodus 1–7 never names it. **It reappears at the moment it is exempted.**

***"To the end you may know that I am Yahweh on the earth"*** — ***kî ʾănî YHWH bəqereb hāʾāreṣ***, literally
*in the midst of the land* (or *earth*). **The WEB's *"on the earth"* is one reading; *in the middle
of the land* — of Egypt, where his people live — is another.** Either way, **the refrain adds a
location**: Yahweh is not only God over Israel somewhere else; he is at work *here*.

***"I will put a division"*** — ***wəśamtî pədut***. **The Hebrew noun is *pədût*, which elsewhere means
*redemption*** (Psalm 111:9; 130:7 — cited from standard lexicons, not checked in the WLC here); in
Genesis and Exodus this is its only occurrence (WLC lemma search). **The WEB's *"division"* follows the
sense of the context and is the usual translation; many scholars regard the consonants as a variant
or error for *pəlut*, *separation*, from the *pālâ* of 8:22.** Read as it stands, God says *I will put
redemption between my people and your people*. **The wiki does not decide between the readings**, and
notes that both say the same thing about the plague: from here on it falls on one people and not the
other.

***"By tomorrow shall this sign be"*** — ***ləmāḥār yihyeh hāʾōt hazzeh***. **Tomorrow again**, as at
8:10, and now God sets it, not Pharaoh. *ʾŌt*, *sign*, is the word of the bush (3:12; 4:8–9, 17, 28,
30) and of the coming forecast (7:3). See [[Signs and Wonders]].

***"Yahweh did so"*** — ***wayyaʿaś YHWH kēn***. **No rod, no Aaron, no outstretched hand.** For the
first time in the plagues the narrator gives the act to Yahweh alone.

***"Grievous swarms"*** — ***ʿārōb kābēd***. **The *kābēd* adjective of Pharaoh's heart (7:14), now
of the swarm.** It will describe the pestilence (9:3), the hail (9:18, 24), and the locusts (10:14)
(WLC lemma search). **The heavy heart meets a heavy plague.**

***"The land was corrupted"*** — ***tiššāḥēt hāʾāreṣ***. **The phrase is the flood's.** *"The earth was
corrupt before God"* (Genesis 6:11) is *wattiššāḥēt hāʾāreṣ*: the same verb, the same stem, the same
subject (WLC). *Šāḥat* in Genesis is the verb of the flood (6:11–17; 9:11, 15) and of Sodom (18:28–32;
19:13–14, 29); in Exodus, after this, it is *"the destroyer"* of the Passover night (12:23) and the
people who *"have corrupted themselves"* at the calf (32:7) (WLC lemma search). **In Genesis the
earth was corrupt and God destroyed it; here God's plague corrupts the land.** See
[[The Flood as Un-Creation]].

### The bargaining (8:25–29)

> **25** Pharaoh called for Moses and for Aaron, and said, "Go, sacrifice to your God in the land!"
> **26** Moses said, "It isn't appropriate to do so; for we shall sacrifice the abomination of the
> Egyptians to Yahweh our God. Behold, shall we sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians before their
> eyes, and won't they stone us?
> **27** We will go three days' journey into the wilderness, and sacrifice to Yahweh our God, as he shall
> command us."
> **28** Pharaoh said, "I will let you go, that you may sacrifice to Yahweh your God in the wilderness,
> only you shall not go very far away. Pray for me."
> **29** Moses said, "Behold, I go out from you, and I will pray to Yahweh that the swarms of flies may
> depart from Pharaoh, from his servants, and from his people, tomorrow; only don't let Pharaoh deal
> deceitfully any more in not letting the people go to sacrifice to Yahweh."

**The first of Pharaoh's offers, and the first of the conditions.** **Offer one**: sacrifice, but *"in
the land"* — ***bāʾāreṣ*** (8:25). **Offer two**: the wilderness, but *"you shall not go very far
away"* — ***harḥēq lōʾ-tarḥîqû***, the verb doubled for emphasis (8:28). **Exodus 10 will add two more**:
the men without the little ones (10:8–11), and everyone without the flocks (10:24–26). **Every offer
keeps something of Israel inside Pharaoh's reach.** See
[[Our Little Ones Stayed in Goshen - Genesis 50.8 and the Bargaining of Exodus 10]].

***"Your God"*** — ***lēʾlōhêkem***. **Pharaoh, who said *"Entreat Yahweh"* at 8:8, now says *"your
God"* at 8:25** — then *"Yahweh your God"* at 8:28. He is still talking about someone else's deity.

***"The abomination of the Egyptians"*** — ***tôʿăbat miṣrayim***. **The word's only earlier
occurrences are both in Genesis, both about Egypt**: the Egyptians *"don't eat bread with the Hebrews,
for that is an abomination to the Egyptians"* (43:32), and *"every shepherd is an abomination to the
Egyptians"* (46:34) (WLC lemma search). **Moses argues from the separation Genesis described.** What
exactly would offend — the animals sacrificed, the manner, or the act — the text does not say, and
the wiki does not guess. See
[[An Abomination to the Egyptians - Separate Tables in Genesis and Exodus]].

***"Won't they stone us?"*** — ***wəlōʾ yisqəlūnû***. **The first mention of stoning in Scripture**
(*sāqal* does not occur in Genesis; WLC lemma search). The threat comes from the Egyptians, not from
the law; Exodus's own stonings come later (17:4; 19:13; 21:28–32).

***"As he shall command us"*** — ***kaʾăšer yōʾmar ʾēlênû***. **Moses cannot say what the sacrifice
will be, because God has not said.** It is the same reason he will give at 10:26: *"we don't know with
what we must serve Yahweh, until we come there"*.

***"Pray for me"*** — ***haʿtîrû baʿădî***. **Pharaoh asks for intercession in the same breath as he
sets the limit.**

***"Don't let Pharaoh deal deceitfully any more"*** — ***ʾal-yōsēp parʿōh hātēl***. ***Tālal***, *to
deceive*, *to trifle with*, occurs elsewhere in Genesis and Exodus only at Genesis 31:7 — Jacob's
complaint, *"Your father has deceived me, and changed my wages ten times"* (WLC lemma search). **Laban
changed the terms ten times; Pharaoh will change them through ten plagues.** Moses says to Pharaoh's
face what he did at 8:15. See [[Deception]].

### The flies end, and the heart (8:30–32)

> **31** Yahweh did according to the word of Moses, and he removed the swarms of flies from Pharaoh,
> from his servants, and from his people. There remained not one.
> **32** Pharaoh hardened his heart this time also, and he didn't let the people go.

***"There remained not one"*** — ***lōʾ nišʾar ʾeḥād***. **The same words close the sea**: *"There
remained not so much as one of them"* (14:28, *lōʾ nišʾar bāhem ʿad-ʾeḥād*) (WLC). **Not one fly is
left in Egypt; at the sea, not one Egyptian is left.** And the frogs, by contrast, *"remain in the river
only"* (8:9, 11), with the same verb *šāʾar*.

***"Pharaoh hardened his heart this time also"*** — ***wayyakbēd parʿōh ʾet-libbô gam bappaʿam
hazzōʾt***. **The hiphil of *kābēd* again, and now the subject is named outright: *Pharaoh*.** No *"as
Yahweh had spoken"* this time. And ***bappaʿam hazzōʾt***, *this time*, recurs at 9:14 in God's
mouth: *"For this time I will send all my plagues against your heart"* (*bappaʿam hazzōʾt*) (WLC).
**Pharaoh hardens his heart *this time*; God answers that *this time* the plagues go to his heart.**

## People

- [[God]] — sends frogs, lice, and swarms; does *"according to the word of Moses"* twice; sets Goshen
  apart; strikes with the flies directly (8:24)
- [[Moses]] — carries the demands; lets Pharaoh set the hour; cries and prays to Yahweh for Pharaoh;
  refuses to sacrifice in the land; warns Pharaoh not to deal deceitfully
- [[Aaron]] — stretches the rod over the waters (frogs) and strikes the dust (lice); absent from the
  flies except as a name Pharaoh calls (8:25)

  twice, and hardens his own heart twice
- [[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]] — bring up frogs; fail at the lice; say *"This is God's
  finger"*
- *Pharaoh's servants*, *the Egyptians* — in every list of the afflicted

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** The plan takes chapter 8 whole; the three plagues share one shape and
  the cycle's first plague (blood) is its own page. The next unit (9:1–35) takes the second cycle's
  first two plagues and the third cycle's first.
- **Verse numbering.** English 8:1–4 = Hebrew 7:26–29; English 8:5–32 = Hebrew 8:1–28. All references
  on this page are English unless marked Hebrew.
- **Correction carried to [[Water Turned to Blood]]:** that page listed the later *kābēd* adjectives of
  the plagues as 9:3, 9:18, 24, 10:14, and missed this pericope's *ʿārōb kābēd* (8:24; Hebrew 8:20).
  It now includes it.
- **The *pədut* / *pəlut* question at 8:23** is left open. The meaning *redemption* for *pədût* outside
  Exodus (Psalm 111:9; 130:7) is reported from standard lexicons and was not checked in the Hebrew
  here. No claim is made about the Septuagint's reading.
- **The *ḥōmer* wordplay** (mortar / heaps) rests on the WLC's lemma tagging: the homonym is real, the
  play is a reading.
- **What the abomination is** (8:26) — the animal sacrificed, the rite, or the act — is not said, and
  the explanations sometimes offered from Egyptian religion are not pursued.
- **Natural explanations** — frogs driven from a polluted Nile, insects breeding on their carcasses —
  are a standing line of interpretation and are not pursued here.
- **Hebrew forms on this page were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Open Scriptures
  Hebrew Bible, `morphhb`): all of Hebrew Exodus 7:26–8:28, and Exodus 9:14; 14:28; 15:11. Occurrence
  counts (*šāraṣ*, *ḥōmer*, *ʿātar*, *pāʾar*, *hityaṣṣēb*, *pālâ*, *pədût*, *kābēd*, *šāḥat*, *tôʿēbâ*,
  *tālal*, *šāʾar*, *ṣāʿaq*, *ʿārōb*, *kinnîm*, *ʾeṣbaʿ*, *rəwāḥâ*, *nāgap*, *sāqal*) come from lemma
  searches of the WLC of Genesis and Exodus, with Leviticus and Numbers where stated. Psalms,
  Deuteronomy, Luke, and Revelation are quoted only from the WEB at bible-api.com; no claim is made
  about their Hebrew or Greek.
