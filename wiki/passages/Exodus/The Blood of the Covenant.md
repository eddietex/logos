# The Blood of the Covenant

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 24:1–18

## Summary

The covenant offered at the foot of the mountain in chapter 19 is now made. God summons Moses,
Aaron, Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of Israel's elders to come up and *"worship from a
distance"*; Moses alone is to come near, and the people are not to go up at all (24:1–2). The order
is not carried out at once. First Moses comes down and tells the people *"all Yahweh's words, and all
the ordinances"*, and they answer *"with one voice"*: *"All the words which Yahweh has spoken will we
do"* (24:3).

**Then the ceremony.** Moses writes the words down, rises early, and builds an altar at the foot of
the mountain with twelve pillars, one for each tribe (24:4). Young men of Israel, not priests, offer
burnt offerings and peace offerings of cattle (24:5). Moses divides the blood: half into basins, half
thrown on the altar (24:6). He reads *"the book of the covenant"* aloud, and the people say it a
second time, now with an added verb — *"All that Yahweh has spoken will we do, and be obedient"*
(24:7). Then he throws the other half on the people: ***"Look, this is the blood of the covenant,
which Yahweh has made with you concerning all these words"*** (24:8). One blood touches both the
altar, which stands for God, and the people.

**Then the ascent that 24:1 ordered.** The seventy-four go up, and the narrator says plainly what
nobody in the book has yet been allowed: ***"They saw the God of Israel."*** What is described is
only what is under his feet, *"like a paved work of sapphire stone, like the skies for clearness"*
(24:10). *"He didn't lay his hand on the nobles of the children of Israel. They saw God, and ate and
drank"* (24:11). The covenant ends in a meal in God's presence, and no one dies.

**Then Moses goes higher alone.** God calls him up to receive *"the stone tablets with the law and the
commands that I have written"* (24:12). Moses takes Joshua, *"his servant"*, leaves the elders with
Aaron and Hur to settle disputes (24:13–14), and climbs into a mountain covered by cloud. *"Yahweh's
glory settled on Mount Sinai"* for six days; on the seventh God calls to Moses from the cloud
(24:16). To Israel below, the glory looks *"like devouring fire on the top of the mountain"* (24:17).
Moses walks into the middle of the cloud and stays *"forty days and forty nights"* (24:18). What God
says to him there fills the next seven chapters.

## People

- [[God]] — who summons, makes the covenant (*"which Yahweh has made with you"*, 24:8), is seen, does
  not lay his hand on those who see him, writes the tablets, and calls from the cloud.
- [[Moses]] — the only one who comes near: he relays, writes, builds, divides the blood, reads,
  sprinkles, speaks the formula, and enters the cloud for forty days.
- [[Aaron]] — called up with the elders, sees God, and is left in charge below with Hur (24:1, 9,
  14). The next time the people come to him in Moses' absence, it is for the calf (32:1).
- [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]] and [[Abihu]] — Aaron's two eldest sons, named among those who see God and
  eat (24:1, 9). Leviticus 10:1–2 records their death by fire *"from before Yahweh"*.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — *"his servant"* (*məšārətô*, 24:13; MAM), who goes up part of the way with
  Moses.
- [[Hur (companion of Moses)]] — who held up Moses' hand against Amalek (17:12), left with Aaron to
  hear disputes (24:14).
- The seventy elders and the *"young men"* who offer the sacrifices (24:1, 5, 9) are groups, not
  named individuals; no person page is created. See [[Priesthood]] and
  [[Seventy Nations - Genesis 10 in Deuteronomy 32 and Luke 10]].

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — *"Mount Sinai"* (24:16) and *"God's Mountain"* (24:13), the altar *"under the

## Notes

### The boundary

The planned boundary is kept. 24:1 turns from the speech that began at 20:22 to a new summons, and
24:18 puts Moses inside the cloud, where 25:1 begins the tabernacle instructions. The order of
events is not straightforward. **The summons of 24:1–2 is given before the ceremony of 24:3–8 and
carried out only after it (24:9).** The Hebrew of 24:1 opens with its object first, *wəʾel-mōšeh
ʾāmar*, *and to Moses he said* (MAM), which some readers take as a sign that 24:1–2 resumes an
earlier moment, perhaps the end of 20:21, before the ordinances were given. The wiki reads the
chapter in its present order and does not reconstruct a different one.

### Two answers, one added verb

The people answer twice, and the second time is longer. At 24:3, after hearing the words spoken,
they say *naʿăśeh*, *we will do* — the same single verb as 19:8 (MAM). At 24:7, after hearing them
read from the book, they say *naʿăśeh wənišmāʿ*, *we will do and we will hear* (MAM). The WEB gives
the second verb as *"be obedient"*; the Hebrew verb is *šāmaʿ*, the ordinary word for *hear*, which
also means *obey*. The Septuagint keeps both verbs as *poiēsomen kai akousometha* (Rahlfs). Jewish
interpretation has long noticed that *do* comes before *hear*; the wiki records the word order and
leaves the reading to the reader.

### One blood, two parties

The blood is the center of the ceremony, and the text spends three verses on it (24:6–8). Moses
divides it *before* the reading and the second assent, and throws the second half only *after*
them. The first half goes on the altar, the second on the people. The verb for both is *zāraq*,
*to throw* or *dash* (24:6, 8; MAM), the verb Leviticus uses for the priests dashing blood around
the altar (Leviticus 1:5; MAM); the WEB's *"sprinkled"* softens it.

Exodus does not explain the rite. Three readings are common, and the text supports parts of each:

- **A self-curse.** As in [[Passing Between the Pieces]], the party bound to the covenant identifies
  itself with the slaughtered animals. The text does not say this.
- **A bond of kinship.** One blood on both partners makes them one family. The text does not say this
  either, but the division of a single blood between the two is its most visible feature.
- **Consecration.** The same blood that makes the altar holy is applied to the people, as it will be
  applied to the priests at their ordination (29:20–21, *not yet ingested*). Israel was offered the
  status of *"a kingdom of priests"* at 19:6.

The wiki records all three as readings and does not choose among them.

### "The book of the covenant"

24:7 is the first place in Scripture where something written is called a *sēper*, a *book*, of the
covenant (MAM). The wiki has used the name since [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]] for the laws of
20:22–23:33, following this verse. The text does not say exactly what Moses wrote at 24:4: *"all
Yahweh's words"* most naturally means the speech just given, but whether the Ten Commandments were
included is not stated. The stone tablets of 24:12, written by God, are a separate document.

### "They saw the God of Israel"

Verse 10 says *wayyirʾû*, *they saw* (MAM), with no qualification. Verse 11 repeats it with a
different verb, *wayyeḥĕzû*, from *ḥāzâ*, a word often used for prophetic vision (MAM). Neither verse
describes God himself. The description stops at his feet and at what is beneath them: a pavement
*libnat hassappîr*, like *brickwork of sapphire* (MAM) — *ləbēnâ* is the word for the bricks Israel
made in Egypt (1:14; 5:7). Whether *sappîr* is the modern sapphire or lapis lazuli is debated, and
the wiki does not decide it.

**The Septuagint softens both verses.** In Rahlfs' text, 24:10 reads *they saw the place where the
God of Israel stood*, and 24:11 reads *of the chosen ones of Israel not even one perished, and they
appeared in the place of God*. The Hebrew has neither *place* nor *appeared*. The translators seem
to have found the plain statement difficult.

**The difficulty is real inside Exodus.** At 33:20 God tells Moses *"you cannot see my face, for man
may not see me and live"*. 24:11 seems written with that rule already in mind: *"He didn't lay his
hand on"* them is the narrator's way of saying that what might have killed them did not. The wiki
sets the two side by side in [[They Saw God - Exodus 24, Exodus 33, and John 1.18]], and does not
reconcile them.

### The meal

*"They saw God, and ate and drank"* (24:11). The peace offerings of 24:5 (*zəbāḥîm šəlāmîm*, MAM) are
the sacrifice whose meat is eaten — *"The flesh of the sacrifice of his peace offerings … shall be
eaten"* (Leviticus 7:15, *not yet ingested*) — so the meal is most likely that meat, though 24:11
does not say so. The covenant partners eat together. Jethro's meal
*"before God"* at 18:12 had the same guests — Aaron and the elders — and no vision. Isaiah 25:6
places a feast for all peoples *"in this mountain"*; the wiki notes the image without linking it to
Sinai.

### The glory settles

*"Yahweh's glory settled on Mount Sinai"* (24:16). The verb is *šākan*, *to dwell* or *settle* (MAM).
God's first instruction from the cloud will use the same verb — *"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I
may dwell among them"* (25:8, *wəšākantî bətôkām*; MAM) — and its noun *miškān*, *dwelling*, names the
tabernacle (25:9); see [[The Offering for the Sanctuary]]. At the end of the book the cloud *šākan* on the tent and the glory fills the
*miškān*, and Moses cannot enter (40:35; MAM). Here he walks into the middle of the cloud. **The
rest of Exodus moves the glory from the mountain into the camp.**

Six days of cloud and a call on the seventh (24:16) repeat the shape of the creation week. The wiki
notes the pattern; the text does not draw the connection.

### Who saw, and what became of them

Of the named leaders who saw God and ate, Aaron will make the golden calf while Moses is on the
mountain (32:1–6), and Nadab and Abihu will die offering *"strange fire"* (Leviticus 10:1–2). The
elders left in Aaron and Hur's charge are not named when the people gather to Aaron. Seeing God did
not protect them from what followed; Exodus makes no comment on this here.
