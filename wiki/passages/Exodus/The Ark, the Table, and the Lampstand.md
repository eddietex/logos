# The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 25:10–40

## Summary

**The instructions start at the centre.** Before any tent, wall, altar, or priest, God describes
three objects. They are the furniture of his presence, and each is made of acacia wood or gold.

**The ark** (25:10–22) is a chest of acacia wood, *"two and a half cubits"* long and *"a cubit and a
half"* wide and high, overlaid with pure gold *"inside and outside"*. It is carried on gold-covered
poles through four gold rings, and the poles *"shall not be taken from it"* (25:15). Inside it goes
*"the testimony which I shall give you"* (25:16). On top of it goes a slab of pure gold, the
***"mercy seat"*** (25:17). Two cherubim of hammered gold are made of one piece with it. They face each
other, with their wings spread upward over it (25:18–20). **Then God says what the ark is for:**
***"There I will meet with you, and I will tell you from above the mercy seat, from between the two
cherubim … all that I command you for the children of Israel"*** (25:22).

**The table** (25:23–30) is smaller, also acacia and gold, with its own rings and poles. It has gold
dishes, spoons, ladles, and *"bowls to pour out offerings with"*. On it goes ***"bread of the presence
… before me always"*** (25:30).

**The lampstand** (25:31–40) is one piece of hammered pure gold. A central shaft has six branches, three
on each side, and its cups are shaped *"like almond blossoms"*, each with a bud and a flower. It carries
seven lamps that *"give light to the space in front of it"* (25:37). No size is given, only a weight:
*"a talent of pure gold"* (25:39). **The section ends with a warning:** ***"See that you make them after
their pattern, which has been shown to you on the mountain"*** (25:40).

The ark will stand alone behind the veil. The table and lampstand will stand across from each other
outside it (26:33–35; 40:20–24). **The first thing God designs for the place where he will live is a
place to speak from.**

## People

- [[God]] — the designer, who promises to *"meet with you"* and to speak *"from above the mercy seat,
  from between the two cherubim"* (25:22). The bread is set *"before me"* (25:30).
- [[Moses]] — addressed throughout in the singular (*"you shall make"*), and the one *"shown … on the
  mountain"* (25:40). He is also the one God will meet at the ark (25:22). He does not make the objects
  himself; Bezalel does that at 37:1–24 *(not yet ingested)*.
- [[Cherubim]] — two figures of hammered gold over the mercy seat (25:18–20). They are the only figures
  in the furniture, and this is their first appearance since they guarded Eden (Genesis 3:24). **The
  page is created with this pericope.**
- The makers of the ark are plural, *"They shall make"* (25:10), and unnamed. The lamps are lit by an
  unnamed *"they"* (25:37); 27:20–21 assigns the lamp to Aaron and his sons.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — *"on the mountain"* (25:40), where Moses is shown the pattern. The mountain is not named in
  the pericope; Moses has been in the cloud there since 24:18.

## Themes

- [[The Dwelling of God]] — the ark is the place where God meets and speaks (25:22). The dwelling's first
  furniture is for speaking, feeding, and light.
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — *"There I will meet with you"*: one man, at one place, receiving
  commands *"for the children of Israel"* (25:22).
- [[Covenant]] — *"the testimony"* (*ʿēdut*) goes into the ark (25:16, 21); the ark is the *"ark of the
  testimony"* (25:22). These are the tablets of 31:18.
- [[Holiness]] — pure gold, inner and outer, and an ark that is never handled directly (25:14–15).
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — the table's bowls *"to pour out offerings"* (25:29), the bread set out
  *"always"* (25:30), and the *kappōret*, the place of atonement in Leviticus 16.
- [[Light and Darkness]] — seven lamps burning in a room with no window (25:37; 27:21).
- [[Work]] — hammered work (*miqšâ*), *"of one piece"* (25:18, 31, 36), and a design to be followed
  exactly (25:40).
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — *"pure gold"* eight times in thirty-one verses, and a talent of it for the
  lampstand.

## Notes

### The boundary

The planned boundary is kept. The MAM text marks a paragraph break (*{פ}*) after 25:22 and after 25:30,
and a closed break (*{ס}*) after 25:40. So the Hebrew divides the chapter into the same three objects
this page names. 26:1 starts the tent itself. The three are kept together because 25:40 closes all of
them (*"make them after their pattern"*) and because they are the furniture of the inner rooms.

### The order: from the inside out

The instructions start with the object deepest inside and work outward: the ark (behind the veil),
then the table and lampstand (the outer room), then the tent (26), then the altar and court (27), and
only then the priests (28–29). **God's design starts where God will speak.** The order is the text's;
the reason is the wiki's reading. The building narrative later uses a different order. Bezalel makes the
tent first (36:8–38) and the ark after it (37:1).

### *ʾĀrôn*: a chest, and a coffin

***ʾĀrôn*** (25:10; MAM) is a box or chest. **In Genesis and Exodus 1–24 it occurs once**, at Genesis
50:26, where Joseph is *"put in a coffin in Egypt"* (*bāʾārôn*; MAM search, Genesis–Exodus 25). The
next *ʾārôn* is this one. See
[[Embalmed and Put in a Coffin - The Only Two Mummies in Scripture]].

It is a different word from Noah's *tēbâ*. **But the instruction borrows Noah's phrase.** The ark is
overlaid *"inside and outside"*, ***mibbayit ûmiḥûṣ*** (25:11), the same two words used when Noah's ship
is sealed *"inside and outside with pitch"* (Genesis 6:14; both MAM). The pitch in Genesis is *kōper*,
and the verb is *kāpar*. They have the same consonants as the ark's lid, the *kappōret* (25:17). The
wiki records the overlaps and does not claim the writer intended them. See
[[The Two Arks - Noah's Ship and Moses' Basket]].

**"They shall make."** The ark is the only object here whose instruction opens with a plural verb,
***wəʿāśû***, *they shall make* (25:10). The table and lampstand open with the singular ***wəʿāśîtā***,
*you shall make* (25:23, 31; all MAM). The rest of the ark section is singular too, except 25:19's
*taʿăśû*. Jewish interpreters have long read the plural to mean that all Israel has a share in the ark;
that tradition is reported here, not checked against a named source. The Septuagint has the singular,
*kai poiēseis*, in 25:10 (Rahlfs).

**The size.** Two and a half by one and a half by one and a half cubits. The cubit is usually reckoned
at roughly 45 cm, so the ark would be about 1.1 m long and 0.7 m wide and high. That is the standard
estimate; Exodus does not define the cubit. **It is small**: a chest a person could carry, not a
room.

### The poles never come out

*"The poles shall be in the rings of the ark. They shall not be taken from it"* (25:15). No such rule is
given for the table's poles. **The ark is built to be carried and never touched.** Later texts take
this seriously. When the ark comes into Solomon's temple, *"The poles were so long that the ends of the
poles were seen from the holy place … They are there to this day"* (1 Kings 8:8). When Uzzah *"reached
for God's ark, and took hold of it; for the cattle stumbled"*, *"God struck him there for his error"*
(2 Samuel 6:6–7). Both are *not yet ingested*. Numbers 4 gives the Kohathites the job of carrying it;
see [[Kohath]].

### "The testimony which I shall give you"

***Hāʿēdut*** (25:16, 21; MAM), *the testimony*. **It has not been given yet.** God promised the tablets
at 24:12 and gives them at 31:18: *"the two tablets of the testimony, stone tablets, written with God's
finger"*. The ark is designed as their container before they exist. The first use of the word in
Scripture is earlier still: the manna is laid up *"before the Testimony"* at 16:34 (MAM). That verse is
told from after this one; see [[Manna and Quail]].

From now on the ark is named for what it holds: *ʾărôn hāʿēdut*, *"the ark of the testimony"* (25:22).
The name *"ark of the covenant"* (*ʾărôn bərît*) comes later, for example at 1 Samuel 4:4 (MAM). The
Septuagint calls it the *kibōtos martyriou*, *ark of testimony*, already at 25:10, where the Hebrew has
only *ark* (Rahlfs).

**What else was in it?** Exodus says the testimony. Hebrews 9:4 lists *"a golden pot holding the manna,
Aaron's rod that budded, and the tablets of the covenant"*. 1 Kings 8:9 says *"There was nothing in the
ark except the two stone tablets"*. The three are recorded side by side in [[Manna and Quail]].

### The mercy seat — *kappōret*

***Kappōret*** (25:17; MAM) is a slab of pure gold the size of the ark's top. It has no wood in it. It
appears here for the first time in Scripture (MAM search, Genesis–Exodus 25; the only earlier match for
the consonants is the verb in Genesis 6:14). **Its meaning is disputed.**

- One view relates it to *kāpar* in its sense *cover*: the lid.
- Another relates it to the piel *kipper*, *to make atonement*: the place of atonement. That is where
  the high priest puts the blood once a year (Leviticus 16:14–15).

Both are standard proposals; the wiki does not decide. The English *"mercy seat"* is usually credited to
William Tyndale, following Luther's *Gnadenstuhl*; that is the common report, not checked here.

**The Septuagint chose atonement.** It translates *kappōret* as ***hilastērion epithema***, a
*propitiatory covering* (25:17, Rahlfs), and after that as *hilastērion* alone (25:18–22). That word
is the one Hebrews uses for the mercy seat (9:5). Paul uses it of Christ: *"whom God sent to be an
atoning sacrifice"* (Romans 3:25; *hilastērion*, SBLGNT; WEB). See
[[The Mercy Seat - Kapporet and Hilasterion from Leviticus 16 to Romans 3]].

### The cherubim

Two ***kərubîm*** (25:18; MAM), of ***miqšâ***, *hammered* or *beaten work*, made *"of one piece with
the mercy seat"* (25:19). **Exodus says almost nothing about what they look like.** They have wings and
faces. The wings are *"spread out … upward, covering"* (*sōkəkîm*, *screening*) the mercy seat. The faces
are turned ***ʾîš ʾel-ʾāḥîw***, *each toward his brother*, and down toward the mercy seat (25:20; MAM).
How many faces, what kind of bodies, how tall — the text does not say. Ezekiel's later visions describe
cherubim in detail *(not yet ingested)*; the wiki does not read those details back into Exodus.

**They are the second cherubim in Scripture**, and the first were guards. *"He placed cherubim at the
east of the garden of Eden … to guard the way to the tree of life"* (Genesis 3:24). **The verb in
Genesis is *wayyaškēn***, the hiphil of ***šākan*** (MAM), the verb of *"that I may dwell among them"*
(25:8). God *"caused the cherubim to dwell"* at the entrance of the garden. The overlap is in the Hebrew;
what it means is a reading. Many readers see the cherubim on the ark, and woven into the curtains
(26:1), as marking the tabernacle as a way back toward the presence that Eden lost. See [[Cherubim]],
[[The Fall]], and [[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]].

**They do not break the second commandment, in the text's own terms.** The same God who said *"You shall
not make for yourselves an idol, nor any image"* (20:4) commands these figures. The wiki's discussion of
the tension is in [[The Ten Commandments]] and [[Idolatry]]. Here it notes only that the cherubim are
not to be worshipped, are not set in public view, and are part of the furniture of the God who has no
image.

### "There I will meet with you"

***Wənôʿadtî ləkā šām*** (25:22; MAM), *and I will meet with you there*. The verb is the niphal of
***yāʿad***, *to appoint*, *to meet by appointment*. The same root gives *môʿēd*, the *"meeting"* of the
*"Tent of Meeting"*, *ʾōhel môʿēd* (27:21, MAM; *not yet ingested*). **The verse is a promise of
regular, arranged speech**: God will tell Moses *"all that I command you for the children of
Israel"*. It is kept at Numbers 7:89: Moses *"heard his voice speaking to him from above the mercy seat
that was on the ark of the Testimony, from between the two cherubim"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*).

*Môʿēd* and *ʿēdut*, *testimony*, sound alike, and the ark of the *ʿēdut* is where God *nôʿad*. They come
from different roots (*y-ʿ-d* and *ʿ-w-d*, as usually analysed). The wiki notes the sound and does not
rest anything on it.

**The Septuagint reads *gnōsthēsomai soi ekeithen***, *I will be made known to you from there* (25:22,
Rahlfs), not *I will meet you*. **The ark is a place of speech, not of sight.** Nothing in Exodus 25
says God is seen there. At Leviticus 16:2 God says *"I will appear in the cloud on the mercy seat"*
(*ʾērāʾeh*, MAM; *not yet ingested*), but that is an appearance in a cloud, to the high priest, once a
year.

**Later Israel calls God *"Yahweh of Armies, who sits above the cherubim"*** (1 Samuel 4:4; compare
Psalm 80:1). The Hebrew is ***yōšēb hakkərubîm*** (MAM), *who sits* or *dwells* on or among the cherubim;
the WEB supplies *above*. On that reading the ark is a throne, or its footstool, and the empty space
over the mercy seat is where the invisible king sits. That is the later tradition's picture, not
Exodus 25's wording; Exodus says only *"from between the two cherubim"*.

### The table and the bread of the presence

***Šulḥān*** (25:23; MAM), *table*. It has a rim *"of a hand width"* (*ṭōpaḥ*) with a gold moulding.
Its gold vessels are ***qəʿārōt***, ***kappōt***, ***qəśôt***, and ***mənaqqiyyōt***, *"to pour out
offerings with"* (*ʾăšer yussak bāhēn*; 25:29; MAM). They are vessels for libations. The exact shape of
each is uncertain, and the English names vary from version to version.

***"You shall set bread of the presence on the table before me always"*** — ***leḥem pānîm ləpānay
tāmîd*** (25:30; MAM). **The Hebrew says *bread of face, before my face*.** *Pānîm* is used twice, once
for the bread and once for God. The Septuagint has *artous enōpious*, *loaves set before* (Rahlfs).
Older English has *showbread*, as the WEB does in 1 Samuel 21:6 and Matthew 12:4.

**Exodus does not say what the bread is, how much, or who eats it.** Leviticus 24:5–9 does: *"twelve
cakes"* in *"two rows, six on a row, on the pure gold table before Yahweh"*, renewed *"Every Sabbath
day"*, and eaten by *"Aaron and his sons … in a holy place"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*). Twelve loaves for
twelve tribes is the usual reading; Leviticus does not say so. **David eats it** (1 Samuel 21:6), and
Jesus cites that to the Pharisees (Matthew 12:3–4). See
[[The Bread of the Presence - David's Hunger and Matthew 12]].

**Bread on a table in front of God.** In ancient temples food was set before a god as a meal, and many
readers have asked whether this is that. Exodus does not say God eats it, and Leviticus gives it to the
priests. The wiki leaves the bread's meaning open beyond what the two texts say: it is always there,
before God, and it is renewed.

### The lampstand

***Mənôrâ*** (25:31; MAM), *lampstand*. It occurs here for the first time in Scripture; so do
*šulḥān* (25:23) and *kərub* in the singular (25:19). The plural *kərubîm* is at Genesis 3:24 (MAM
search, Genesis–Exodus 25).

**It is a tree in gold.** A central stem, six branches, and on each branch three cups
***məšuqqādîm***, *made like almonds* (25:33), each with a ***kaptōr***, *bud* or *knob*, and a
***peraḥ***, *flower* (MAM). The stem itself has four almond cups (25:34). It is all *"one beaten work
of pure gold"* (25:36), hammered from one piece, not assembled. **The words are botanical**: shaft,
branches, cups, buds, flowers. The Septuagint has *karyiskous*, *nut-shaped* (25:33, Rahlfs).

**The almond.** *Šāqēd* has occurred once before, in the *"nuts, and almonds"* Jacob sends down to Egypt
(Genesis 43:11; *šəqēdîm*, MAM). It recurs twice in later books (*not yet ingested*):

- **Numbers 17:8.** Aaron's rod, left *"in the Tent of the Testimony"*, *"sprouted, budded, produced
  blossoms, and bore ripe almonds"*. The Hebrew uses *peraḥ*, the lampstand's *flower*, and
  *šəqēdîm* (Numbers 17:23 MAM).
- **Jeremiah 1:11–12.** Jeremiah sees *"a branch of an almond tree"* (*šāqēd*), and God says *"I watch
  (šōqēd) over my word to perform it"* (MAM; WEB). The pun is in the Hebrew.

The almond is usually said to be the first tree to blossom in the year in Israel's climate. That is a
standard botanical report, not checked here. Whether Exodus means anything by the almond is not
stated.

**Many readers see a tree of life in it** — a flowering tree, inside the holy place, guarded by
cherubim on the curtains around it (26:1). That reading is widespread, and the text does not make it.
See [[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]] and
[[Seven Lamps - The Lampstand in Zechariah 4 and Revelation 1]].

**Seven lamps** (*nērōteyhā šibʿâ*, 25:37; MAM) to *"give light to the space in front of it"*, that is,
across the room toward the table. The verb *wəheʿĕlâ*, *and he shall raise up* (the lamps), is singular
in the Hebrew (MAM); the WEB's *"they shall light"* takes it as impersonal. **The holy place has no
window.** The lamp is to *"burn continually"*, kept by Aaron and his sons *"from evening to morning"*
(27:20–21).

**The weight.** *"A talent of pure gold"* (*kikkār*; 25:39), lampstand and accessories together. A talent
is usually reckoned at around 30–35 kg; that is the common estimate, and the Bible does not define it.
No height or width is given. **The lampstand is the only one of the three described by weight
instead of by size.**

### "See, and make"

***Ûrəʾēh waʿăśēh bətabnîtām ʾăšer-ʾattâ morʾeh bāhār*** (25:40; MAM): *see, and make according to their
pattern, which you are being shown on the mountain*. Three points:

- **Two imperatives.** *See*, then *make*. The WEB's *"See that you make"* is a fair English rendering,
  but the Hebrew also lets *see* stand as a separate command: look at what is being shown.
- ***Morʾeh*** is a hophal participle, *being made to see* or *being shown*. It is a present participle,
  like 25:9's *marʾeh*, *I am showing*. The showing is happening during the speech.
- ***Tabnît*** is the word of 25:9. This is its second occurrence in Scripture (MAM search,
  Genesis–Exodus 25). The pattern is now located: *"on the mountain"*.

**The Septuagint is the text the New Testament quotes.** Rahlfs reads *hora poiēseis kata ton typon ton
dedeigmenon soi en tō orei* — *see, you shall make according to the type shown to you on the mountain*.
Hebrews 8:5 quotes it as *Hora gar, phēsin, poiēseis **panta** kata ton typon ton deichthenta soi en tō
orei* (SBLGNT): it adds *panta*, *everything*, and changes the participle to the aorist. The WEB:
*"See, you shall make everything according to the pattern that was shown to you on the mountain"*.
Hebrews draws from it that the priests *"serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things"*. Stephen in
Acts 7:44 recalls the tabernacle made *"according to the pattern that he had seen"* (*kata ton typon hon
heōrakei*, SBLGNT). **Both use *typos***, the Septuagint's word, not the Hebrew *tabnît*. See
[[After Their Pattern - Exodus 25.40 in Acts 7 and Hebrews 8]].

### What is not here

No use is given for the mercy seat (that waits for Leviticus 16). There is no rite for the bread (that
waits for Leviticus 24) and no oil for the lamp (that waits for 27:20). **There are no priests yet.**
The furniture is described before anyone is appointed to serve at it. And nothing is said to be
sacrificed here: the altar of burnt offering is in chapter 27, outside, in the court.
