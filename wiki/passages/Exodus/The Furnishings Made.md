# The Furnishings Made

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 37:1–29

## Summary

Bezalel is named as the maker of the ark, and the account continues through the sanctuary's
furnishings. The acacia chest is covered inside and outside with pure gold, fitted with rings
and carrying poles, and accompanied by a gold mercy seat. Two cherubim are made of one piece
with that seat, their wings spread over it and their faces directed toward it and one another
(37:1–9). These are crafted figures, not a new appearance of heavenly beings.

The table is made of acacia and gold with a hand-width border, rings, poles, and gold vessels
(37:10–16). The lampstand is beaten from pure gold, with a central shaft, six branches,
almond-blossom cups, buds, and flowers. Its seven lamps and accessories belong to the stated
talent of gold (37:17–24). The incense altar is square, one cubit by one cubit and two cubits
high, with integral horns, gold overlay, rings, and poles. Holy anointing oil and pure incense
are compounded by the perfumer's art (37:25–29).

The report brings together furnishings prescribed in Exodus 25 and 30 after the enclosure
made in chapter 36. Detailed correspondence makes the response to the instructions visible.
The testimony is not yet reported placed in the ark, bread is not yet set out, and lamps and
incense are not yet lit. Making the equipment, installing it, and beginning its service are
distinct stages.

## Notes

### Boundary and attribution

The planned 37:1–29 boundary is retained: it moves from the ark through the indoor furnishings
to the oil and incense, before 38:1 turns to the altar of burnt offering. It combines the
instructions of 25:10–40 and selected portions of chapter 30 rather than following the command
chapters one for one. The change in order is observable; it is not evidence by itself for a
particular compositional history.

Bezalel is explicitly named in 37:1, unlike the enclosure unit of 36:8–38. The following
singular descriptions continue that attribution. They do not say that he worked without the
company summoned in 36:1–2. Oholiab is not named here, and no division of labor between the
leaders can be recovered from this chapter. No extra person page for an unnamed perfumer is
warranted: 37:29 describes a craft, not a separately introduced character.

### Command and making, with omissions preserved

Exodus 25:10–14 corresponds to 37:1–5; 25:17–20 to 37:6–9; 25:23–29 to 37:10–16;
25:31–39 to 37:17–24; and 30:1–5 to 37:25–28. The measurements, materials, carrying
arrangements, and forms closely agree. Verse 37:29 compresses the oil and incense instructions
of 30:22–38 into one statement without repeating the ingredients, quantities, or prohibitions.

Several directions concern use rather than manufacture. The permanent-pole rule of 25:15 is
not restated, nor are the insertion of the testimony (25:16, 21), the promise of speech
(25:22), bread placement (25:30), or lighting the lamps (25:37). Their absence from this
report is not a cancellation. Exodus 40:20–27 separately narrates the testimony, placement,
bread, lamps, and burning incense. The repeated instruction to anoint is in 40:9–15; making
the oil here does not itself report that anointing.

**The pole descriptions retain a sequencing question.** Exodus 37:5 reports putting the poles
into the ark's rings; 25:15 prohibits their removal; 40:20 again describes setting the poles
on the ark. The later wording does not say they were removed and reinserted. It might recap
or describe arranging them, but the WEB alone does not establish the precise sequence of
handling. All three statements are kept rather than inventing an unreported removal.

The table-vessel descriptions use different English terms and order: 25:29 lists dishes,
spoons, ladles, and bowls, while 37:16 lists dishes, spoons, bowls, and pitchers. Both describe
pure-gold vessels for pouring. Without checking a source-language edition, this comparison
does not claim different underlying Hebrew words or a changed inventory.

### Objects made for service

The ark is two and a half by one and a half by one and a half cubits; the mercy seat shares
its length and width (37:1, 6). The table is two by one by one and a half cubits (37:10).
The incense altar is one cubit square and two high (37:25). The text gives a talent for the
lampstand with its vessels (37:24), not a total weight for the gold used throughout this
chapter. No modern conversion or symbolic meaning of these measurements is required.

The lampstand has six branches and seven lamps, not seven separate stands. Its floral
ornament is described, but it is not identified here as the tree of life, the Spirit, or a
community. The incense is compounded rather than burned, and the holy oil made rather than
applied. The later connections should retain these distinctions between preparation, use,
and interpretation.

### Gold after the calf

The gold cherubim of 37:7–9 carry out 25:18–20 after the calf's condemnation in 32:4–8.
The tension with the image prohibition of 20:4–5 remains explicit: the same narrative forbids
images and their worship, condemns the calf, and commissions these representations. Chapter
37 does not report worship of them or explain a general rule covering all religious art.
Their wings and faces are described, but a full visual reconstruction is not supplied.

The work follows covenant renewal, willing giving, and the stopping of excess contributions.
This is the narrative setting for renewed obedience, not a statement that gold buys pardon
or that skilled work compels God's presence. No individual object's gold is traced from the
calf, Egyptian gifts, or a particular donor to one of these furnishings.
