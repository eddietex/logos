# Bricks Without Straw

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 5:1–6:1

## Summary

**The first audience with Pharaoh, and everything in it goes wrong.** [[Moses]] and [[Aaron]] deliver
the word as commanded — *"This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says, 'Let my people go'"* (5:1) —
and [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] answers with the question the rest of the plague narrative exists to
answer: ***"Who is Yahweh, that I should listen to his voice to let Israel go? I don't know
Yahweh"*** (5:2). They try again, more humbly, with three days' journey and a sacrifice (5:3); he
sends them back to their burdens (5:4–5).

**The same day, he makes the work worse.** The straw that went into the bricks will no longer be
supplied, and the quota will not be reduced: *"for they are idle; therefore they cry, saying, 'Let us
go and sacrifice to our God'"* (5:6–9). The people scatter over all Egypt to gather stubble (5:12);
the Israelite officers set over them are beaten for the shortfall (5:14). They go to Pharaoh and cry
to him as *"your servants"*, and are told *"You are idle! You are idle!"* (5:15–18).

**Coming out, they meet Moses and Aaron waiting for them, and curse them.** *"May Yahweh look at you,
and judge, because you have made us a stench … to put a sword in their hand to kill us"* (5:20–21).
**The people who *"believed"* and *"worshiped"* at 4:31 have, one audience later, handed the deliverer
over to God's judgment.** Moses takes it straight to Yahweh: *"Lord, why have you brought trouble on
this people? Why is it that you have sent me? … you have not rescued your people at all"* (5:22–23).
**Yahweh does not rebuke him.** He answers with a promise: *"Now you shall see what I will do to
Pharaoh, for by a strong hand he shall let them go, and by a strong hand he shall drive them out of
his land"* (6:1).

### The shape of it

**The pericope is a chain of command run downward and then back up**, and every link of it speaks in
the form of a message or a complaint.

| | verses | who speaks | to whom | what is said |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5:1–5 | Moses and Aaron | Pharaoh | *"Let my people go"* — *"Who is Yahweh?"* — *"Get back to your burdens!"* |
| 2 | 5:6–9 | Pharaoh | taskmasters and officers | no straw; the same number of bricks; *"they are idle"* |
| 3 | 5:10–14 | taskmasters | the people | *"This is what Pharaoh says"*; the people scattered; the officers beaten |
| 4 | 5:15–18 | the officers | Pharaoh | *"Why do you deal this way with your servants?"* — *"You are idle!"* |
| 5 | 5:19–21 | the officers | Moses and Aaron | *"May Yahweh look at you, and judge"* |
| 6 | 5:22–23 | Moses | Yahweh | *"Why have you brought trouble on this people? Why … have you sent me?"* |
| 7 | 6:1 | Yahweh | Moses | *"Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh"* |

**Two kings send words down the chain**, and the chapter uses the same formula for both: *kōh ʾāmar
YHWH*, *"This is what Yahweh … says"* (5:1), and ***kōh ʾāmar parʿōh***, *"This is what Pharaoh says"*
(5:10). **And the complaints come back up it as questions** — *lāmmâ*, *why*, four times (5:4, 15, 22
twice), and *maddûaʿ*, *why*, once (5:14). Pharaoh asks why Moses and Aaron take the people from their
work; the taskmasters ask the officers why the quota is short; the officers ask Pharaoh why he deals
so with his servants; and Moses asks God, twice, why. **The only line in the chain that is not a
question or a command is the last one, and it is God's.**

## Key verses

### The first audience (5:1–5)

> **1** Afterward Moses and Aaron came, and said to Pharaoh, "This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel,
> says, 'Let my people go, that they may hold a feast to me in the wilderness.'"
> **2** Pharaoh said, "Who is Yahweh, that I should listen to his voice to let Israel go? I don't know
> Yahweh, and moreover I will not let Israel go."

***"Moses and Aaron came"*** — **and the elders do not.** The plan at the bush was *"you and the elders
of Israel"* (3:18), and the elders were gathered at 4:29. At 5:1 only the two brothers go in. The text
does not say why, and the wiki does not supply a reason.

***"Let my people go"*** — ***šallaḥ ʾet-ʿammî*** — **is the demand of 4:23 with *my son* become *my
people*.** It is the first time Pharaoh hears it. The verb is ***šālaḥ***, *send*, *send away*, *let
go*, and **it is the verb the whole pericope turns on**: Yahweh demands that Pharaoh *šallaḥ* (5:1);
Pharaoh says twice that he will not *šallaḥ* (5:2); Moses asks Yahweh why he *šālaḥ*-ed *him* (5:22);
and Yahweh answers that by a strong hand Pharaoh *will* *šallaḥ* them (6:1). **The one sent is asking
why he was sent, because the one he was sent to will not send.** See [[The Burning Bush]] — *"I AM has
sent me"* (3:14), *šəlāḥanî*. Jacob asked the same of Laban — *šalləḥēnî*, *"send me away"*
(Genesis 30:25); see [[Served, Released, Pursued - The Laban Years as Proto-Exodus]].

***"That they may hold a feast to me"*** — ***wəyāḥōggû lî***, from *ḥāgag*, *keep a pilgrim feast*.
3:18 said *sacrifice*; 5:1 says *feast*; 5:3 goes back to *sacrifice*. The request is religious from
the first word: **Israel is asking to go and worship, and Pharaoh hears a labour stoppage** (5:4–5).

***"Who is Yahweh?"*** — ***mî YHWH*** — **is the question Exodus 5–14 answers.** Pharaoh does not deny
that Yahweh exists. He says he does not know him, and that *not knowing* is the ground of the refusal:
***lōʾ yādaʿtî ʾet-YHWH***, *I don't know Yahweh*. **Three verbs of knowing have already set this up.**
The new king of 1:8 *"didn't know Joseph"* (*lōʾ-yādaʿ*); God, at 2:25, *"knew"*; and at the bush,
*"I know their sorrows"* (3:7). Now Pharaoh does not know Yahweh — and from 6:7 on, the refrain of the
plagues is *"you shall know that I am Yahweh"* (6:7; 7:5, 17; 8:22; 10:2; 14:4, 18). **The plagues are
an answer to 5:2.** See [[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]].

*"That I should listen to his voice"* — ***ʾešmaʿ bəqōlô***. At the bush Yahweh promised that the
elders *"will listen to your voice"* (3:18, *ləqōlekā*), and Moses objected that they would not (4:1,
*bəqōlî*). The elders did. **Pharaoh will not — and by 6:9, neither will Israel** (*"they didn't listen
to Moses for anguish of spirit"*).

> **3** They said, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us go three days' journey into
> the wilderness, and sacrifice to Yahweh, our God, lest he fall on us with pestilence, or with the
> sword."

**The second request is the script of 3:18, word for word in places, with two changes.** The script
read *"Yahweh, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us"*; at 5:3 the first word is gone, and they say
only ***ʾĕlōhê hāʿibrîm***, *"The God of the Hebrews"*. **To a king who has just said he does not know
the name, they name God by the people.** And they add a reason the script did not have: ***pen-
yipgāʿēnû baddeber ʾô beḥāreb***, *"lest he fall on us with pestilence, or with the sword"*.

**Both halves of that reason come back on the speakers' heads within the chapter — and the first
comes back on Egypt's later.** *Pāgaʿ*, *fall on*, *meet*, is the verb of 5:20, when the beaten
officers *"met"* Moses and Aaron (*wayyipgəʿû*) — to curse them. The *sword* is the sword the officers
say Moses has put *"in their hand to kill us"* (5:21). **Moses asked leave so that God would not bring
the sword on Israel; the officers say Moses has brought Pharaoh's sword on Israel instead.** And the
*pestilence*, *deber*, is used in Exodus only here and in the fifth plague and its sequel — on Egypt's
livestock (9:3), and in God's word to Pharaoh, *"I would have … struck you and your people with
pestilence"* (9:15). (Occurrences of *deber* counted in the Westminster Leningrad Codex of Exodus.)

> **4** The king of Egypt said to them, "Why do you, Moses and Aaron, take the people from their work?
> Get back to your burdens!"
> **5** Pharaoh said, "Behold, the people of the land are now many, and you make them rest from their
> burdens."

***"Your burdens"*** — ***siblōtêkem*** — **is the word of the oppression.** Egypt set taskmasters over
Israel *"to afflict them with their burdens"* (1:11, *bəsiblōtām*); Moses went out and *"looked at
their burdens"* (2:11, *bəsiblōtām*); and in the next pericope Yahweh will promise, twice, to bring
them out *"from under the burdens of the Egyptians"* (6:6, 7, *mittaḥat siblōt*). **Pharaoh sends
Moses back to the very thing God has promised to take him out from under.**

***"You make them rest"*** — ***wəhišbattem***, the causative of ***šābat***, *cease*, *rest* — **is the
Sabbath verb.** In canonical order it has been used only of God resting on the seventh day (Genesis
2:2–3) and of day and night that *"will not cease"* (8:22); **Exodus 5:5 is the first time it is used
of people being made to rest, and the speaker is Pharaoh, and he means it as an accusation.** The next
time the people *šābat*, it is by command, with manna to make it possible (16:30), and Exodus later
grounds the rest in mercy to servants: *"that your ox and your donkey may have rest, and the son of
your servant woman … may be refreshed"* (23:12). **The first rest in Israel's history is the one
Pharaoh forbids.** (Occurrences of *šābat* checked in the WLC of Genesis, Exodus, and Job.) See
[[Sabbath]].

*"The people of the land are now many"* — ***rabbîm***, the *many* of 1:9 (*"more and mightier than
we"*, *rab*). The multiplication that frightened the first Pharaoh into making them work is now
Pharaoh's reason they must not stop.

### No more straw (5:6–14)

> **6** The same day Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters of the people, and their officers, saying,
> **7** "You shall no longer give the people straw to make brick, as before. Let them go and gather
> straw for themselves.
> **8** The number of the bricks, which they made before, you require from them. You shall not
> diminish anything of it, for they are idle; therefore they cry, saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to
> our God.'
> **9** Let heavier work be laid on the men, that they may labor in it; and don't let them pay any
> attention to lying words."

**Two ranks of overseer, and the second are Israelites.** The ***nōgəśîm***, *taskmasters*, are
Pharaoh's — the word is the one Yahweh used at the bush, *"their cry because of their taskmasters"*
(3:7). The ***šōṭərîm***, *officers*, are *"the officers of the children of Israel, whom Pharaoh's
taskmasters had set over them"* (5:14). **The *šōṭərîm* appear here for the first time in Scripture**
(in canonical order; checked in the WLC of Genesis and Exodus, and not found in Job) — and the system
they belong to is the one Egypt runs on: a foreign overseer above, a native foreman below, and the
foreman beaten when the quota fails.

***"Straw"*** — ***teben*** — **the chopped straw mixed into mud bricks to bind them.** In Genesis it
appears only as fodder, at Laban's house, when Rebekah tells Abraham's servant *"We have both straw and
feed enough"* (Genesis 24:25, 32). ***"Brick"*** — ***ləbēnîm*** — **is Babel's word**: *"Come, let's
make bricks"* (Genesis 11:3). Exodus 1:14 used it once, *"in mortar and in brick"*; here it comes five
times. See [[Come, Let Us - Babel's Bricks and Pharaoh's]].

***"They are idle"*** — ***nirpîm***, *slack*, *sinking*. **Pharaoh's diagnosis of a religious
request is laziness**, and he says it twice (5:8, 17). ***"Therefore they cry"*** — ***ṣōʿăqîm*** —
uses the verb Genesis first used of Abel's blood: *"The voice of your brother's blood cries to me from
the ground"* (Genesis 4:10, *ṣōʿăqîm*, the same form). God heard that cry; Pharaoh hears this one as
proof of idleness.

***"Let heavier work be laid on the men"*** — ***tikbad hāʿăbōdâ***, *let the service be heavy*.
*ʿĂbōdâ* is the *service* of 1:14 and 2:23 — the word of the book's central contest (see the section
*Serve whom* below). The root ***kbd***, *be heavy*, is one of the three Exodus uses for the hardening
of Pharaoh's heart — *"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn"* (7:14) is *kābēd lēb*, the adjective of the same
root — **so the king who makes the service *heavy* here will have his own heart called *heavy* in the
plagues.** The link is lexical, and the wiki records it at that strength. See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"Lying words"*** — ***diḇrê šāqer*** — **is what Pharaoh calls the word of Yahweh.** He has just
said he does not know the speaker; now he says the speech is false.

> **10** The taskmasters of the people went out, and their officers, and they spoke to the people,
> saying, "This is what Pharaoh says: 'I will not give you straw.
> **11** Go yourselves, get straw where you can find it, for nothing of your work shall be
> diminished.'"
> **12** So the people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble for
> straw.

***"This is what Pharaoh says"*** — ***kōh ʾāmar parʿōh*** — **is the messenger formula, and it is the
formula Moses has just used for Yahweh** (5:1; first at 4:22). Pharaoh's word goes out through his
taskmasters exactly as Yahweh's goes out through Moses and Aaron. **The chapter has two kings with two
sets of messengers, issuing contradictory decrees in the same form**, and the people under both of
them have to decide which *"This is what … says"* is real.

***"Scattered abroad throughout all the land"*** — ***wayyāpeṣ hāʿām bəkol-ʾereṣ miṣrāyim*** — **is
Babel again.** Babel built with bricks *"lest we be scattered abroad"* (Genesis 11:4, *pen-nāpûṣ*), and
*"Yahweh scattered them abroad"* (11:8, *wayyāpeṣ*, the same form). **In Egypt the brickmakers are
scattered — not by God to fill the earth, but by Pharaoh to find stubble.** See
[[Come, Let Us - Babel's Bricks and Pharaoh's]].

***"Stubble"*** — ***qaš***, the dry stalks left in a harvested field — appears in Exodus only here and
in the Song of the Sea, where Yahweh's wrath *"consumes them as stubble"* (15:7). **The stuff Israel is
sent to gather becomes the image of what happens to Egypt's army.** (Checked in the WLC of Exodus; the
wiki does not claim the Song alludes to chapter 5.)

> **13** The taskmasters were urgent saying, "Fulfill your work quota daily, as when there was
> straw!"
> **14** The officers of the children of Israel, whom Pharaoh's taskmasters had set over them, were
> beaten, and demanded, "Why haven't you fulfilled your quota both yesterday and today, in making brick
> as before?"

***"Were beaten"*** — ***wayyukkû***, from ***nākâ***, *strike*. **It is the verb of Moses' first act
as a man**: he saw *"an Egyptian striking a Hebrew"* (2:11, *makkeh*), and he *"killed the Egyptian"*
(2:12, *wayyak*). And it is the verb of God's promise at the bush: *"I will … strike Egypt with all my
wonders"* (3:20, *wəhikkêtî*). **Moses killed a man in secret to stop one Hebrew being struck; his
first open act on Israel's behalf has got all their foremen struck.** God's striking is still ahead.

### The officers' appeal (5:15–19)

> **15** Then the officers of the children of Israel came and cried to Pharaoh, saying, "Why do you
> deal this way with your servants?
> **16** No straw is given to your servants, and they tell us, 'Make brick!' and behold, your
> servants are beaten; but the fault is in your own people."

***"Cried to Pharaoh"*** — ***wayyiṣʿăqû ʾel-parʿōh***. **Israel's cry went up *to God* at 2:23**, and
God heard it at 3:7, 9 (*ṣaʿăqātām*, *their cry*). **Here the foremen cry *to Pharaoh*.** In Exodus the
verb *ṣāʿaq* is next used when Moses *"cried to Yahweh concerning the frogs"* (8:12), and when the people
at the sea *"cried out to Yahweh"* (14:10). **The first cry Israel addresses to anyone by name in this
book, with this verb, is to the king.** (Occurrences of *ṣāʿaq* and *ṣəʿāqâ* checked in the WLC of
Exodus, whose chapter 8 runs four verses ahead of the English; the cry of 2:23 is a different verb,
*zāʿaq*.)

***"Your servants"*** — ***ʿăbādêkā***, three times in two verses. **The officers of the people whom
Yahweh has demanded as *his* servants — *"that he may serve me"* (4:23) — call themselves Pharaoh's.**
It is the posture Genesis recorded the Egyptians adopting during the famine: *"we and our land will be
servants to Pharaoh"* (Genesis 47:19), *"we will be Pharaoh's servants"* (47:25). See
[[Buy Us and Our Land - Egypt Sold into Servitude and Israel After It]].

***"The fault is in your own people"*** — ***wəḥāṭāʾt ʿammekā*** — **two words, and the second half of
an appeal to justice.** The officers do what the people of 2:13 would not let Moses do: they name *the
one in the wrong*. *Ḥāṭāʾt* is from the root *ḥāṭāʾ*, *sin*, *miss the mark*. The WEB reads it as *the
fault is in your own people*; how the first word parses is not certain, and the wiki has not checked it
against a lexicon, so the rendering is recorded as the WEB's and not pressed.

> **17** But he said, "You are idle! You are idle! Therefore you say, 'Let us go and sacrifice to
> Yahweh.'
> **18** Go therefore now, and work, for no straw shall be given to you, yet you shall deliver the same
> number of bricks!"
> **19** The officers of the children of Israel saw that they were in trouble, when it was said, "You
> shall not diminish anything from your daily quota of bricks!"

***"Go … and work"*** — ***ləkû ʿibdû***, *go, serve*. See *Serve whom* below.

***"Let us go and sacrifice to Yahweh"*** — **Pharaoh puts the name in their mouths.** The officers
never said it. Pharaoh, who does not know Yahweh, now quotes him as the cause of the trouble — and the
officers, in the next verse, will lay the trouble at Moses' feet.

***"They were in trouble"*** — ***bərāʿ***, *in evil*, *in a bad case*. The root ***rāʿaʿ*** runs
through the end of the chapter: the officers see themselves *bərāʿ* (5:19), and Moses says God has
*"brought trouble"* — ***hărēʿōtâ***, *done evil* — to the people (5:22), and that Pharaoh *"has
brought trouble"* — ***hēraʿ*** — on them (5:23). **The causative of *rāʿaʿ* is used in Exodus only in
these two verses** (WLC), and it comes back once more in the Torah on Moses' lips, in the same words:
*"Why have you treated your servant so badly?"* — ***lāmâ hărēʿōtā*** (Numbers 11:11; checked in the
WLC of Numbers).

### The curse (5:20–21)

> **20** They met Moses and Aaron, who stood in the way, as they came out from Pharaoh:
> **21** and they said to them, "May Yahweh look at you, and judge, because you have made us a stench
> to be abhorred in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in the eyes of his servants, to put a sword in their hand
> to kill us."

***"Who stood in the way"*** — ***niṣṣābîm liqrāʾtām***, *standing to meet them*. **Moses and Aaron
are waiting outside the audience to hear how it went.** They are not in the room; the officers'
appeal to Pharaoh was made without them.

***"May Yahweh look at you, and judge"*** — ***yēreʾ YHWH ʿălêkem wəyišpōṭ*** — **is the second time
Israel has refused Moses, and this time it is in God's name.** The first Hebrew asked *"Who made you a
prince and a judge over us?"* (2:14, *šōpēṭ*); these ones hand him over to the Judge. **The verbs are
the two God has already been doing.** He *saw* (2:25; 3:7, 9; 4:31) and he will *judge* — at 6:6 he
promises to redeem Israel *"with great judgments"*, *šəpāṭîm*, on Egypt. The officers invoke the right
God for the right things against the wrong man. Genesis has the same appeal, in its own disputes:
Sarai's *"Yahweh judge between me and you"* (Genesis 16:5) and Laban's *"the God of Abraham … judge
between us"* (31:53). See
[[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].

***"You have made us a stench"*** — ***hiḇʾaštem ʾet-rêḥēnû***, *you have made our smell stink*. **It is
Jacob's complaint to Simeon and Levi after Shechem**, in the same causative of the same verb: *"You
have troubled me, to make me odious to the inhabitants of the land"* (Genesis 34:30,
*ləhaḇʾîšēnî*). Jacob's sons had made him a stench by killing; Moses is accused of making Israel a
stench by asking. **And the verb is about to change sides**: in the first plague *"the river became
foul"* (7:21, *wayyiḇʾaš*; 7:18), and the frogs are heaped up until *"the land stank"* (8:14). **The
stench the officers fear for themselves ends up in Egypt's river and on Egypt's land.**

***"To put a sword in their hand to kill us"*** — ***ləhārgēnû***, *to kill us*. *Hārag* is the verb of
2:14 — *"Do you plan to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian?"* — and of 4:23, *"I will kill your son,
your firstborn"*. **The officers accuse Moses of arming Egypt to do to Israel what God has said he will
do to Egypt.**

### Moses' complaint (5:22–23)

> **22** Moses returned to Yahweh, and said, "Lord, why have you brought trouble on this people? Why
> is it that you have sent me?
> **23** For since I came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has brought trouble on this people; and
> you have not rescued your people at all."

***"Moses returned to Yahweh"*** — ***wayyāšāḇ mōšeh ʾel-YHWH***. Where he went, the text does not say.
**He does not answer the officers.** He takes the curse to the one it was made in the name of.

***"Lord"*** — ***ʾădōnāy***, the title Moses used at the bush in his last two objections (4:10, 13).
See [[The Names of God]].

***"Why is it that you have sent me?"*** — ***lāmmâ zeh šəlaḥtānî***. **It is the fifth objection of
3:1–4:17 come back as a complaint** — *"send someone else"* (4:13) — **except that this time Moses went,
and is asking what the going was for.** *Šālaḥ*: see the section on 5:1 above.

***"To speak in your name"*** — ***ləḏabbēr bišmekā*** — **the name he asked for at the bush and was
given** (3:13–15). He spoke in it, and the name's bearer was asked *"Who is Yahweh?"*, and the people
were beaten.

***"You have not rescued your people at all"*** — ***wəhaṣṣēl lōʾ-hiṣṣaltā***, the infinitive
absolute doubling the verb: *and rescuing you have not rescued*. ***Nāṣal*** **is the verb of the
promise**: *"I have come down to deliver them"* (3:8, *ləhaṣṣîlô*). **Moses quotes God's own word back
to him as unkept.** It is the first complaint Moses makes to God, and it is a lament in the form the
Psalms will use — *"Why do you sleep, Lord? … Why do you hide your face, and forget our affliction and
our oppression?"* (Psalm 44:23–24). See [[Prayer]] and [[Mourning and Lament]].

**Note who is called whose.** The officers called themselves Pharaoh's servants and the Egyptians
*"your own people"* (5:16). Moses calls Israel ***ʿammekā***, *your people* — God's — and **he says it of
the people who have just cursed him** (5:23).

### Now you shall see (6:1)

> **1** Yahweh said to Moses, "Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh, for by a strong hand he
> shall let them go, and by a strong hand he shall drive them out of his land."

**There is no rebuke.** At the bush, when Moses asked God to send someone else, *"Yahweh's anger burned
against Moses"* (4:14). Here Moses accuses God of doing evil and failing to deliver, and the reply is a
promise. **The text records God angry at the refusal to go, and not at the complaint of the one who
went.** It does not explain the difference.

***"Now you shall see"*** — ***ʿattâ tirʾeh***. **The officers asked Yahweh to *see* Moses (5:21); Yahweh
tells Moses that *he* will see.**

***"By a strong hand"*** — ***bəyāḏ ḥăzāqâ***, twice. **At the bush God said the king would not let
them go *"no, not by a mighty hand"*** — ***wəlōʾ bəyāḏ ḥăzāqâ***, the same phrase (3:19; the WEB has
*mighty* there and *strong* here) — and went on, *"I will reach out my hand and strike Egypt … and after
that he will let you go"* (3:20). **6:1 is 3:19–20 restated after its first test.** And ***ḥāzāq***,
*strong*, is the root of ***ʾăḥazzēq***, *"I will harden"* (4:21): **Pharaoh's heart will be made strong,
and a strong hand will overcome it.** See [[Hardening of the Heart]].

**Whose strong hand is not stated.** It may be God's, compelling Pharaoh — the reading 3:19–20 supports,
and the one most translations imply; or it may be Pharaoh's own, in the haste with which he will at last
expel them (12:31–33). Both are recorded; neither is adopted.

***"He shall drive them out"*** — ***yəḡārəšēm***, from ***gāraš***. **It is the verb of every expulsion
in Genesis**: God *"drove out the man"* from Eden (Genesis 3:24); Cain, *"you have driven me out"* (4:14);
and Sarah's *"Cast out this servant and her son!"* (21:10) — **the slave woman driven out of the master's
house.** **Here the slave people will be driven out of the master's land** — and it happens in those
words: *"he will surely thrust you out altogether"* (11:1, *gārēš yəḡārēš*), *"because they were thrust
out of Egypt"* (12:39, *ḡōrəšû*). In Exodus the verb was last used of the shepherds who *"drove away"*
Jethro's daughters from the well (2:17). See [[Moses Flees to Midian]].

### Serve whom

**The verb *ʿābad*, *serve*, is the spine of Exodus**, and this pericope puts both masters' commands in
it a few verses apart.

| | the word | what is commanded |
|---|---|---|
| Yahweh, through Moses (4:23) | *"Let my son go, that he may* ***serve*** *me"* — *wəyaʿabdēnî* | service to God, in the wilderness |
| Pharaoh, to the officers (5:18) | *"Go therefore now, and* ***work****"* — ***ʿibdû*** | service to Pharaoh, in the brickfields |
| Pharaoh, to the taskmasters (5:9) | *"Let heavier* ***work*** *be laid on the men"* — ***hāʿăbōdâ*** | the service made heavy |
| the officers, to Pharaoh (5:15–16) | *"your* ***servants****"* — ***ʿăbādêkā***, three times | the servants naming their master |

**The WEB's *work* in 5:9 and 5:18 is the Hebrew *serve* and *service*.** The question of the chapter
is not whether Israel will work but whom it will serve — and the officers' answer, at least for now, is
*Pharaoh*. Job's wicked ask the same thing of God: *"What is the Almighty, that we should serve him?"*
(Job 21:15, *naʿabdennû*). See [[Slavery and Servitude]] and [[Work]].

### What the pericope undoes, and what it sets up

**It undoes 4:31.** The people who *"believed"* and *"bowed their heads and worshiped"* have, in one
chapter, been beaten, called idle, and turned against the deliverer. In the next pericope they will not
listen *"for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage"* (6:9). **The first plague has not yet fallen,
and the faith that was given at the first telling is already gone.** See [[Faith]].

**It sets up the plagues.** Pharaoh has said three things — *I don't know Yahweh*, *I will not let
Israel go*, *their words are lies* — and every one is a claim the next nine chapters test. **And it
sets up the next pericope**: Moses asked *why*, and the answer in 6:2–8 begins *"I am Yahweh"* — the
answer, at last, to Pharaoh's question, given to Moses first.

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** 5:1 opens the first audience; 6:1 is Yahweh's answer to 5:22–23 and
  belongs with the complaint. 6:2 starts again, *"God spoke to Moses, and said to him, 'I am
  Yahweh'"*, and is the next pericope.
- **Why the elders are absent at 5:1** — not stated; recorded, not explained.
- **Whose *strong hand* (6:1)** — God's, or Pharaoh's in the expulsion; both recorded, neither adopted.
- ***Wəḥāṭāʾt ʿammekā* (5:16)** — the WEB's rendering is recorded; the parse of *ḥāṭāʾt* was not
  checked against a lexicon, and the page does not press it.
- **The absence of rebuke at 6:1** is an observation about the text, set beside 4:14; the wiki does not
  supply a reason for it.
- **Hebrew forms on this page were checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Open Scriptures
  Hebrew Bible, `morphhb`): all of Exodus 5:1–6:1; and Exodus 1:8, 9, 11; 2:11, 12, 14, 17, 23, 25;
  3:7, 8, 18, 19, 20; 6:3, 6, 7; 7:18; 11:1; 12:39; 16:30; 23:12; Genesis 2:2–3; 3:24; 4:10; 11:4, 8,
  9; 16:5; 21:10; 31:53; 34:30; 47:19, 25; Job 21:15; Numbers 11:11; Proverbs 30:9. Word counts and
  first occurrences (*šōṭərîm*, *teben*, *ləbēnîm*, *šābat*, *gāraš*, *pûṣ*, *bāʾaš*, *deber*, *qaš*,
  *ṣāʿaq*, the causative of *rāʿaʿ*, *ʾădōnāy*) were taken from lemma searches of the WLC of Genesis,
  Exodus, and Job, which are the books the wiki has reached; a claim of *first in Scripture* means
  first in canonical order among them.
