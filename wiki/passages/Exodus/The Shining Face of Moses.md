# The Shining Face of Moses

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 34:29–35

## Summary

Moses descends Mount Sinai carrying the two tablets of the testimony. His face shines because
of his conversation with Yahweh, but he does not know it. Aaron and the Israelites are afraid
to approach. Moses calls them back: Aaron and the rulers return first, then all Israel draws
near and hears the commandments spoken to him on the mountain (34:29–32).

Only after speaking does Moses veil his face. The closing verses describe a recurring pattern:
he removes the veil when going before Yahweh, comes out and relays the commands, and covers his
face again after Israel has seen it shining (34:33–35). The renewed covenant reaches the people
through a mediator whose appearance bears the effect of the encounter.

## Key verses

> **29** When Moses came down from Mount Sinai with the two tablets of the testimony in Moses’ hand, when he came down from the mountain, Moses didn’t know that the skin of his face shone by reason of his speaking with him.

> **30** When Aaron and all the children of Israel saw Moses, behold, the skin of his face shone; and they were afraid to come near him.

> **31** Moses called to them, and Aaron and all the rulers of the congregation returned to him; and Moses spoke to them.

> **32** Afterward all the children of Israel came near, and he gave them all the commandments that Yahweh had spoken with him on Mount Sinai.

> **33** When Moses was done speaking with them, he put a veil on his face.

> **34** But when Moses went in before Yahweh to speak with him, he took the veil off, until he came out; and he came out, and spoke to the children of Israel that which he was commanded.

> **35** The children of Israel saw Moses’ face, that the skin of Moses’ face shone: and Moses put the veil on his face again, until he went in to speak with him.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh speaks with Moses and gives the commands conveyed to Israel.
- [[Moses]] — returns with the tablets, calls the people near, and speaks with his face unveiled.
- [[Aaron]] — initially afraid, then returns with the rulers at Moses' call.

The rulers and the children of Israel are collective participants. No individual ruler is
named, and Israel here is the people, not Jacob personally.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — Mount Sinai, explicitly named in 34:29, 32; the existing page covers both names.

The setting of Moses' later entries before Yahweh (34:34–35) is not named. The tent of
Exodus 33 supplies nearby context, but these verses do not identify a building.

## Notes

### A changed descent after the broken covenant

Moses again descends carrying two tablets, as in 32:15. This time the story describes neither
an idol before him nor his destruction of the stones. The people draw near and receive the
commands. The change gives visible form to the renewal without asserting that their earlier
sin never happened or that every announced consequence has been canceled.

Moses does not know that he shines (34:29). The narrator attributes the change to his speaking
with Yahweh, not to an action Moses undertakes to enhance his authority. Aaron, who made the
calf, is named among those afraid and among those who return (34:30–31). The unit does not
report a separate acquittal or ordination for him. The rulers are not identified by name or
number, so they should not automatically be equated individually with the seventy in chapter 24.

### The order of speaking and covering

**The veil follows the speech** (34:33). Moses removes it before Yahweh, comes out, speaks,
and is seen shining before covering his face again (34:34–35). A paraphrase in which he veils
himself so that Israel can approach and hear him reverses the narrated sequence. The passage
reports their initial fear, but does not expressly state his motive for adopting the veil.
The repeated entries and exits carry the narrative beyond a single descent; their duration
and location are not specified here.

**Paul gives a further interpretation.** In 2 Corinthians 3:7–18 the radiance belongs to a
ministry whose glory passes away, and the veil prevents a steadfast look at the end of what
is passing away. Exodus does not narrate the brightness fading or give that reason for the
veil. These are distinct presentations, retained in the connection page rather than silently
harmonized. Paul's later contrast also does not mean that the Israelites in Exodus never saw
the face: 34:35 explicitly says that they did.

### Whose face is seen

Israel sees Moses' changed face. That is not a report that Moses saw God's face, and it does
not settle the tension between the sight of God in 24:10–11 and the prohibition in 33:20.
The request for glory, the proclamation of the name, and the shining mediator belong to the
same narrative sequence, but its movement should not be converted into a claim that all
three passages describe the same visual experience.

The Transfiguration accounts provide a later comparison of mountain, changed appearance,
Moses, divine speech, and fear. Matthew expressly describes Jesus' face shining; Mark describes
the transformed appearance and clothing; Luke describes the altered face during prayer.
The connection is a literary and theological comparison, not a fulfillment claim stated in
Exodus.
