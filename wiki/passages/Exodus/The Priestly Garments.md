# The Priestly Garments

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 28:1–43

## Summary

God tells Moses to bring Aaron and his four sons near, by name, *"that he may minister to me in
the priest's office"* (28:1). This is the first command in Exodus that names who will serve in the
sanctuary being designed. Holy garments are to be made for Aaron *"for glory and for beauty"*,
by the wise-hearted whom God says he has *"filled with the spirit of wisdom"* (28:2–3). Six items
are listed — breastplate, ephod, robe, checkered coat, turban, and sash — from gold, blue, purple,
scarlet, and fine linen (28:4–5).

The chapter then works outward from what Aaron carries. The ephod has two onyx stones on its
shoulder straps, engraved with the names of the children of Israel, six on each, *"in the order of
their birth"*; Aaron bears the names *"before Yahweh on his two shoulders for a memorial"*
(28:6–14). The breastplate of judgment, a span square and folded double, holds twelve stones in
four rows, each engraved with a tribe's name; it is chained and laced to the ephod so it cannot
swing loose. Aaron bears the names *"on his heart"* when he enters the holy place, and the Urim and
the Thummim are put in it, so that he bears *"the judgment of the children of Israel on his heart
before Yahweh continually"* (28:15–30).

The robe of the ephod is all blue, with a reinforced neck opening and alternating golden bells and
yarn pomegranates at the hem; its sound must be heard as Aaron goes in and out, *"that he not
die"* (28:31–35). A pure gold plate engraved *"HOLY TO YAHWEH"* is set on Aaron's forehead, where he
*"shall bear the iniquity of the holy things"* Israel consecrates, *"that they may be accepted
before Yahweh"* (28:36–38). Coats, sashes, and headbands are made for his sons; Moses is to clothe,
anoint, consecrate, and sanctify them all (28:39–41). Linen breeches complete the clothing so
that they do not *"bear iniquity, and die"* when they approach the altar or enter the Tent of
Meeting (28:42–43). As in chapters 25–27, everything here is commanded; the garments are made in
chapter 39, and the ordination itself is prescribed in chapter 29.

## People

- [[God]] — names the priests, designs their clothing, and has filled the makers with wisdom.
- [[Moses]] — receives the command to bring Aaron near, instruct the craftsmen, and clothe, anoint,
  and consecrate the priests (28:1, 3, 41).
- [[Aaron]] — the one whose garments are described; he bears Israel's names, judgment, and the
  iniquity of the holy things before Yahweh.
- [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]], [[Abihu]], [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]], and [[Ithamar]] — named
  individually at 28:1, the first time Exodus names them for the priest's office; their own
  garments follow at 28:40–43.
- [[Jacob]] — not named, but *"the children of Israel"* whose names are engraved *"in the order of
  their birth"* (28:10) are his sons; the chapter does not list the names or their order on the
  breastplate.
- The wise-hearted makers are unnamed here; Bezalel and Oholiab are named in Exodus 31.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — Sinai, by the continuing setting of chapters 24–31; no place is named in the chapter.
  The *"holy place"* and *"Tent of Meeting"* of 28:29, 35, 43 are the prescribed sanctuary spaces.

## Themes

- [[Priesthood]] — Aaron and his four sons are named for the priest's office for the first time.
- [[Holiness]] — holy garments, a plate reading *"HOLY TO YAHWEH"*, and the holy gifts Aaron
  carries.
- [[Mediation and Advocacy]] — Aaron bears Israel's names on shoulders and heart, their judgment,
  and the iniquity of their holy gifts.
- [[Remembrance]] — the onyx stones and breastplate are *"for a memorial before Yahweh"*.
- [[Names and Naming]] — twelve names engraved twice, and a divine name on the forehead.
- [[Nakedness and Covering]] — linen breeches cover the priests' nakedness at the altar.
- [[The Glory of God]] — the garments are *"for glory and for beauty"*, a glory given to the
  priest; the chapter does not connect it to the glory on the mountain.
- [[Judgment]] — the *"breastplate of judgment"* and the Urim and Thummim.
- [[Sin]] — Aaron bears *"the iniquity of the holy things"*; the priests must not *"bear
  iniquity, and die"*.
- [[Wisdom]] — the makers are *"wise-hearted"*, filled with *"the spirit of wisdom"*.
- [[Work]] — weaving, engraving, setting, and embroidery for priestly service.
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — gold, dyed yarns, linen, and precious stones become vestments.
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — the priest's clothing is designed for entering the holy place.

## Notes

### The order of the chapter

The opening list of 28:4 gives six garments in the order breastplate, ephod, robe, coat, turban,
sash. The instructions then follow a different order: ephod (6–14), breastplate (15–30), robe
(31–35), plate (36–38), and coat, turban, and sash in a single verse (39). The plate and the
breeches are not in the opening list. The detail is concentrated on the items that carry names,
judgment, sound, and iniquity — what Aaron *bears* — rather than on the plain linen garments.

### Names on the shoulders and on the heart

The children of Israel's names appear twice: two onyx stones with six names each on the
shoulders (28:9–12), and twelve stones each with one name on the breastplate (28:17–21). Both are
called a *"memorial before Yahweh"* (28:12, 29). The shoulder names are ordered *"in the order of
their birth"* (28:10). The breastplate stones are *"according to their names"* and *"for the
twelve tribes"* (28:21), but the chapter does not say which name goes on which stone or whether
Joseph's two sons are counted in place of Joseph and Levi. That question is left open here; the
existing page [[Two Tribes in Place of One - The Adoption of Ephraim and Manasseh]] tracks the
later tribal counts.

### What the stones were

The WEB names ruby, topaz, beryl; turquoise, sapphire, emerald; jacinth, agate, amethyst;
chrysolite, onyx, jasper (28:17–20). These are a translation's English labels. The identification
of ancient gem names is uncertain, and this page makes no claim about the minerals or the Hebrew
terms behind them. The onyx of 28:9 and 28:20 shares its English label with the onyx found in
Havilah at Genesis 2:12; whether the Hebrew word is the same was not checked in this pass.

### Urim and Thummim

The Urim and the Thummim are *put in* the breastplate (28:30). They are not described,
manufactured, or explained: no material, number, or use is given here, and no making of them is
commanded alongside the other items. Their role in inquiry appears only in later texts
(Numbers 27:21; 1 Samuel 28:6). Whether they were lots, stones, or something else is not
answered by Exodus. See
[[Urim and Thummim - The Breastplate of Judgment and Later Inquiry]].

### Bells *"that he not die"*

The bells' sound is required *"when he goes in to the holy place before Yahweh, and when he comes
out, that he not die"* (28:35). The text gives the purpose of the sound — that it be heard — but
not who must hear it or why the sound protects him. Readings that the bells reassured those
outside, or announced the priest before God, are interpretations, not statements of the verse.
The same warning returns with the breeches (28:43), so the chapter frames the vestments as a
condition of survival in the holy place as well as of honour.

### The plate: forehead, turban, and an English discrepancy

The gold plate reading *"HOLY TO YAHWEH"* is to be *"on Aaron's forehead"* (28:38). The stored WEB
text at 28:37 says it is fastened with blue lace *"on the sash; on the front of the sash"*. The
parallel construction notice in the same WEB file (Exodus 39:31) fastens it *"on the turban
above"*, and the WEB of Leviticus 8:9 sets the plate *"on the turban, in front"*. The placement on
the forehead favors the turban. **Resolved at the next ingest:** the Hebrew of 28:37, read in
*Miqra according to the Masorah* via Sefaria, has *ʿal-hammiṣnāpet*, the word the WEB renders
*"turban"* at 28:4, 28:39, and 29:6. The discrepancy belongs to the WEB's English, not the Hebrew.
See [[The Ordination of the Priests]], where 29:6 sets *"the holy crown on the turban"*. The raw
text is not altered.

Aaron *"shall bear the iniquity of the holy things"* that Israel consecrates, *"that they may be
accepted before Yahweh"* (28:38). The holy gifts themselves can carry iniquity, and the priest
carries it for the people. The chapter does not explain how this bearing works. Leviticus 10:17
later speaks of the sin offering given to the priests *"to bear the iniquity of the congregation,
to make atonement for them"* (verified WEB, not ingested); that development is not read back here.

### Breeches and the steps

Exodus 20:26 forbids going up to the altar by steps, *"that your nakedness may not be exposed to
it"*. Here the priests receive linen breeches *"to cover the flesh of their nakedness"* when they
approach the altar or enter the tent (28:42–43). Exodus places these two provisions far apart and
does not relate them. It is a natural inference that both guard the same exposure, but it remains
an inference; how the large altar of 27:1–8 was reached is not described in either chapter.

### A family appointed within a priestly people

Exodus 19:6 promised that all Israel would be *"a kingdom of priests"*, and 24:5 had young men
offer the covenant sacrifices. Chapter 28 now gives *"the priest's office"* to one family, by
name. The text does not present this as the cancellation of 19:6. It also does not yet record
any act by these priests; the ordination ceremony belongs to chapter 29, and its performance to
Leviticus 8.
