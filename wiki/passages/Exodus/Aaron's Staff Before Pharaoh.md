# Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh

**Book:** [[Exodus]]
**Reference:** Exodus 6:28–7:13

## Summary

**The narrative picks up where the genealogy interrupted it, with Moses' objection, and this time
God answers.** 6:28–30 repeats 6:10–12 almost word for word: *"I am Yahweh. Speak to Pharaoh king of
Egypt all that I speak to you"*, and [[Moses]]' *"Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall
Pharaoh listen to me?"* At 6:13 God gave no answer. **At 7:1 he gives one, and it is the arrangement
already made at the bush, now aimed at the king**: *"I have made you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron
your brother shall be your prophet"*. Moses speaks what God commands; [[Aaron]] speaks it to
[[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] (7:2).

**Then God tells them how it will go, before it goes.** He will harden Pharaoh's heart and multiply
his signs (7:3). **Pharaoh will not listen** — so Moses' fear at 6:30 is granted, and it does not
matter — and God will bring out *"my armies, my people"* *"by great judgments"* (7:4). The point of
all of it is stated: ***"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"*** (7:5). Moses and Aaron obey
(7:6). Their ages are given — eighty and eighty-three (7:7).

**Then the first sign before Pharaoh, which is not a plague.** God tells them Pharaoh will demand a
wonder (7:9). Aaron casts down his rod and it becomes a ***tannîn*** — a serpent, or something
larger (7:10). Pharaoh calls his own wise men, sorcerers, and magicians, and *"they also … did the
same thing"* (7:11). **Then Aaron's rod swallows their rods** (7:12). Nobody is hurt and nothing in
Egypt is damaged; the contest ends in a tie broken by a swallowing. And *"Pharaoh's heart was
hardened, and he didn't listen to them; as Yahweh had spoken"* (7:13). **Everything foretold in 7:3–4
happens at once, in one verse.**

### The shape of it

| | verses | what happens | turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6:28–30 | *"I am Yahweh. Speak to Pharaoh"*; *"I am of uncircumcised lips"* | the objection of 6:12, resumed |
| 2 | 7:1–2 | *"as God to Pharaoh"*; *"Aaron … your prophet"* | **the answer**: the chain of speech |
| 3 | 7:3–5 | harden, multiply, not listen, lay my hand, bring out, know | **the forecast** |
| 4 | 7:6–7 | *"As Yahweh commanded them, so they did"*; 80 and 83 | obedience, and the speakers' ages |
| 5 | 7:8–9 | *"When Pharaoh … says, 'Perform a miracle!'"* | the sign prescribed |
| 6 | 7:10 | Aaron's rod becomes a *tannîn* | the sign done |
| 7 | 7:11 | wise men, sorcerers, magicians: *"they also … did the same"* | **the sign matched** |
| 8 | 7:12 | *"Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods"* | the tie broken |
| 9 | 7:13 | *"Pharaoh's heart was hardened … as Yahweh had spoken"* | the forecast fulfilled |

**The pericope is a hinge.** Its first half (6:28–7:7) closes the second commission that began at
6:2; its second half (7:8–13) opens the contest that runs to chapter 11. **It is told twice: once as
prediction, once as event**, and the event confirms the prediction in its own words — *"not
listen"*, *"harden"*, *"as Yahweh had spoken"*.

## Key verses

### The objection, resumed (6:28–30)

> **28** On the day when Yahweh spoke to Moses in the land of Egypt,
> **29** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying, "I am Yahweh. Speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I speak
> to you."
> **30** Moses said before Yahweh, "Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh listen
> to me?"

***"On the day when Yahweh spoke"*** — ***bəyôm dibber***. **This is a resumption, not a new event.**
The narrator goes back to the scene the genealogy interrupted. 6:29 condenses 6:2 and 6:11 (*"I am
Yahweh"*; *"speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt"*). 6:30 condenses 6:12, keeping *ʿăral śəpātayim*,
*"uncircumcised lips"*, and the question *wəʾêk yišmaʿ … parʿōh*, *"how shall Pharaoh listen?"* It
drops the lesser-to-greater half — *"the children of Israel haven't listened to me"*. (Wording
compared in the WLC.) See [[I Am Yahweh]] and [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]].

***"All that I speak to you"*** — ***ʾēt kol-ʾăšer ʾănî dōbēr ʾēlêkā***, a participle: what God *is
speaking*. **The commission is not a single message but an open channel.** That is why the answer in
7:1–2 is about who speaks to whom, not about what is said.

### As God, and his prophet (7:1–2)

> **1** Yahweh said to Moses, "Behold, I have made you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother
> shall be your prophet.
> **2** You shall speak all that I command you; and Aaron your brother shall speak to Pharaoh, that he
> let the children of Israel go out of his land.

***"Behold, I have made you as God to Pharaoh"*** — ***rəʾēh nətattîkā ʾĕlōhîm ləparʿōh***.
Literally *"See, I have given you, God, to Pharaoh"*. **This is the bush's arrangement turned
outward.** At 4:16 God told Moses that Aaron would be *"to you a mouth, and you will be to him as
God"* — there *lēʾlōhîm*, with the preposition *lə-*, *as* or *for God*. **At 7:1 *ʾĕlōhîm* has no
preposition**, and the one Moses is *God* to is no longer his brother but the king. (Both verses
checked in the WLC.) The WEB's *"as God"* in both places is a fair rendering of the sense, and it
smooths the difference. **The text does not make Moses divine.** It gives him the place in the chain
of speech that God holds toward a prophet: the one whose words the prophet carries. The opening
***rəʾēh***, *"see"*, is the word God used at 4:21: *"See that you do before Pharaoh all the
wonders"*.

***"Aaron your brother shall be your prophet"*** — ***nəbîʾekā***. **This is the second occurrence of
*nābîʾ* in Scripture, and the first that says what a prophet is.** The first was Genesis 20:7, where
God tells Abimelech that Abraham *"is a prophet, and he will pray for you"* (*nābîʾ* occurs only at
these two verses in Genesis, Exodus, and Job; WLC lemma search). Genesis defined the prophet by
intercession. **Exodus defines him by speech on another's behalf**: 4:16 said *mouth*, and 7:1 says
*prophet*, in the same position. **Read together, the verses give the Bible's working definition: a
prophet is the mouth of someone else.** See
[[A Prophet Who Prays - The First Nabi in Scripture]] and [[Prophecy and Vision]].

**It is God's first answer to *"uncircumcised lips"*.** At 6:12–13 the objection got only a command.
Here it gets the arrangement 4:14–16 already made in anger: the man who cannot speak will not have to.
**The objection is not refuted — it is routed around.**

***"You shall speak … and Aaron your brother shall speak to Pharaoh"*** — two *dibbēr* clauses, one
for each brother. **The chain has four links**: God commands Moses, Moses speaks to Aaron, Aaron
speaks to Pharaoh, and Pharaoh is to *let go* (*wəšillaḥ*, the *šālaḥ* of the whole demand). See
[[Language and Speech]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

### The forecast (7:3–5)

> **3** I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt.
> **4** But Pharaoh will not listen to you, and I will lay my hand on Egypt, and bring out my armies,
> my people the children of Israel, out of the land of Egypt by great judgments.
> **5** The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh, when I stretch out my hand on Egypt, and bring out
> the children of Israel from among them."

***"I will harden"*** — ***waʾănî ʾaqšeh***, the hiphil of ***qāšâ***, *to make hard*. **It is not
the verb of 4:21**, which was *ʾăḥazzēq*, *I will make strong*. Of Exodus's three hardening verbs this
is the rarest. In Genesis, Exodus, and Job the root as a verb occurs only at Genesis 35:16–17 (Rachel's
*hard* labour), Genesis 49:7 (the *cruel* wrath of Simeon and Levi), here, Exodus 13:15 (*"when
Pharaoh stubbornly refused"*, *hiqšâ*), and Job 9:4 (*"who has hardened himself against him?"*) (WLC
lemma search). **7:3 is the only one of these with *heart* as its object.** See
[[Hardening of the Heart]].

***"And multiply my signs and my wonders"*** — ***wəhirbêtî ʾet-ʾōtōtay wəʾet-môpətay***. **The verb
is *rābâ*, the verb of Israel's multiplying in chapter 1** (1:7, 10, 12, 20). Pharaoh's whole policy
was *"lest they multiply"* (1:10, *pen-yirbeh*). **Now God says he will multiply too** — signs, in
Egypt. This is the first *hiphil* of the root in Exodus, with God as subject (WLC lemma search). The
pair *ʾōtōt* and *môpətîm* names what the plagues are. *Môpēt*, *wonder*, occurs in Exodus only at
4:21; 7:3, 9; 11:9, 10 (WLC). **11:9 repeats this verse's claim at the far end of the plagues**:
*"Pharaoh won't listen to you, that my wonders may be multiplied (rəbôt môpətay) in the land of
Egypt"*. The two verses bracket the contest. See [[Signs and Wonders]].

***"But Pharaoh will not listen to you"*** — ***wəlōʾ-yišmaʿ ʾălēkem parʿōh***. **God agrees with
Moses.** Moses asked *"how shall Pharaoh listen to me?"* (6:30). God's answer is: he won't. **The
objection was correct and was never the point.** Pharaoh's not listening is part of the plan, because
it is what lets the signs be multiplied. *Šāmaʿ* was the thread of [[I Am Yahweh]]; here it runs into
the forecast, and at 7:13 into the fulfilment.

***"I will lay my hand on Egypt"*** — ***wənātattî ʾet-yādî bəmiṣrāyim***, literally *"I will give my
hand against Egypt"*. ***"My armies, my people"*** — ***ṣibʾōtay ʾet-ʿammî***. **It is the first time
God calls Israel *my armies*.** 6:26 had *ʿal-ṣibʾōtām*, *"according to their armies"*. The word
returns when the people leave: *"all of Yahweh's armies went out from the land of Egypt"* (12:41;
also 12:17, 51). In Exodus *ṣābāʾ* occurs only at these five verses (WLC). **A slave population is
named as a host on the march before it has left.** See [[War]].

***"By great judgments"*** — ***bišpāṭîm gədōlîm***, repeating 6:6 exactly. See [[Judgment]].

***"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"*** — ***wəyādəʿû miṣrayim kî-ʾănî YHWH***. **The
refrain of 6:7, *"you shall know"*, said to Israel, now turns to Egypt for the first time.** Pharaoh
said *"I don't know Yahweh"* (5:2). **The plagues are God's answer to that sentence**, and it is
stated as their purpose before the first one falls. ***"When I stretch out my hand"*** —
*binṭōtî ʾet-yādî*, the verb *nāṭâ* of the *"outstretched arm"* of 6:6. See
[[Who Is Yahweh - Pharaoh's Question and the Knowledge of Yahweh]] and [[The Names of God]].

### Obedience, and two old men (7:6–7)

> **6** Moses and Aaron did so. As Yahweh commanded them, so they did.
> **7** Moses was eighty years old, and Aaron eighty-three years old, when they spoke to Pharaoh.

***"As Yahweh commanded them, so they did"*** — ***kaʾăšer ṣiwwâ YHWH ʾōtām kēn ʿāśû***. **This is
the first time Exodus reports Moses' obedience in the formula of obedience.** Since 3:11 he has
objected, asked, delayed, and complained; here he does what he is told and says nothing. The
formula is old: **before this verse, Genesis uses its full form only of Noah** — *"According to all
that God commanded him, so he did"* (6:22, *kēn ʿāśâ*). After it, Exodus uses it for the Passover
(12:28, 50) and for the finished tabernacle (39:32, 43; 40:16). (WLC search of Genesis and Exodus
for *ṣiwwâ* with *kēn ʿāśâ*.) See [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

***"Moses was eighty years old, and Aaron eighty-three"*** — **the only ages given for either brother
in Exodus.** Deuteronomy 34:7 gives Moses *"one hundred twenty years old when he died"*. Stephen, in
Acts 7:23 and 30, divides the hundred and twenty into three forties — *"when he was forty years
old"* he visited his brothers, and *"when forty years were fulfilled"* the angel appeared in the bush.
**The three forties are Stephen's arithmetic; Exodus gives only the eighty.** (Deuteronomy and Acts
quoted from the WEB at bible-api.com; neither book is ingested yet.) **Aaron is the elder by three
years**, which [[The Birth of Moses]] has already noted sits uneasily with 2:1–2, where Moses reads
like a first child. The text does not say how Aaron survived the decree of 1:22, or whether he was born
before it.

**The speakers are old men.** Abraham was seventy-five when he left Haran (Genesis 12:4). The
deliverers of Egypt are older still, and the verse fixes the date by speech: *bədabbərām*, *"when
they spoke to Pharaoh"*.

### The sign Pharaoh asks for (7:8–10)

> **8** Yahweh spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,
> **9** "When Pharaoh speaks to you, saying, 'Perform a miracle!' then you shall tell Aaron, 'Take
> your rod, and cast it down before Pharaoh, that it become a serpent.'"
> **10** Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh, and they did so, as Yahweh had commanded: and Aaron cast
> down his rod before Pharaoh and before his servants, and it became a serpent.

***"Perform a miracle!"*** — ***tənû lākem môpēt***, literally *"Give for yourselves a wonder"*.
**Pharaoh is expected to ask for proof**, as a king would of envoys claiming to speak for a god. **It
is the only sign in the contest that Pharaoh requests.** Every later one is imposed.

***"Take your rod"*** — ***maṭṭəkā***, said to Aaron. **At the bush the rod was Moses'**: *"What is
that in your hand?" He said, "A rod"* (4:2). Here it is Aaron's hand and Aaron's rod (7:10, 12,
*maṭṭēhû*, *maṭṭēh ʾahărōn*). **The text does not say whether it is the same staff.** It does not
need to: the mouth now also carries the sign.

***"That it become a serpent"*** — ***yəhî lətannîn***. **It is not the word of the bush.** At 4:3 the
rod became a ***nāḥāš***, *a snake*, the word of Genesis 3, and Moses ran from it. **Here it becomes a
*tannîn***. And at 7:15 God refers back to *"the rod which was turned to a serpent"* — and there the
word is *nāḥāš* again. (All three checked in the WLC.) **So the text uses both words of the rod, and
switches to *tannîn* for the scene in Pharaoh's court.**

**What a *tannîn* is matters.** In Genesis, Exodus, and Job the word occurs only six times (WLC lemma
search):

| verse | WEB | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 1:21 | *"the large sea creatures"* | **the first creatures said to be *created*, *bārāʾ*, after the heavens and the earth** |
| Exodus 7:9, 10, 12 | *"a serpent"*, *"serpents"* | the rods |
| Job 7:12 | *"Am I a sea, or a sea monster"* | **a power God sets a guard over** |
| Job 30:29 | *"a brother to jackals"* | the creatures of the ruins (same lemma in the WLC tagging) |

**In later Scripture the *tannîn* becomes Egypt's own emblem.** Ezekiel 29:3 addresses *"Pharaoh
king of Egypt, the great monster that lies in the middle of his rivers"*, and Isaiah 51:9 asks the
arm of Yahweh, *"Isn't it you who cut Rahab in pieces, who pierced the monster?"*, in a chapter
that goes on to the drying of the sea. Psalm 74:13 has *"You divided the sea by your strength. You
broke the heads of the sea monsters in the waters."* (WEB, quoted from bible-api.com. **The Hebrew
of those three verses was not checked here**; they are usually read as forms of *tannîn*, and
whether Ezekiel 29:3 spells the word the same way is not verified.) **If the later reading is in
view, Aaron throws down before Pharaoh the creature that stands for Pharaoh.** Exodus itself does
not say so, and the wiki records the reading as a resonance, not a claim. See
[[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]] and [[Creation]].

***"Before Pharaoh and before his servants"*** — the court is the audience. **The sign is public.**

### The magicians match it (7:11)

> **11** Then Pharaoh also called for the wise men and the sorcerers. They also, the magicians of
> Egypt, did the same thing with their enchantments.

**Three words for the court's professionals, and *gam*, *also*, twice.** *"Pharaoh also called"*;
*"they also … did"*. **The verse is built on matching**: the king answers a sign with his own
experts, and they answer it with the same act.

- ***Ḥăkāmîm***, *"wise men"* — the ordinary word for the wise, used of Joseph himself at Genesis
  41:33, 39.
- ***Məkaššəpîm***, *"sorcerers"* — a participle of *kāšap*. In Genesis, Exodus, and Job the root
  occurs only here and in the law of 22:18, *"You shall not allow a sorceress to live"* (Hebrew
  22:17; WLC lemma search).
- ***Ḥarṭummê miṣrayim***, *"the magicians of Egypt"* — **the exact phrase of Genesis 41:8**, where the
  same guild, with the *ḥăkāmîm*, could not interpret Pharaoh's dream. **In Genesis they failed. Here
  they succeed.** See [[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]].

***"With their enchantments"*** — ***bəlahăṭêhem***. The Open Scriptures morphology tags this word as
***lahaṭ***, *flame* — the noun of Genesis 3:24, *"a flaming sword"* (*lahaṭ haḥereb*), its only other
occurrence in Genesis, Exodus, and Job. **That tagging is a lexical judgment, not a settled fact.**
7:22 tells the same act with *bəlāṭêhem*, from *lāṭ*, *secrecy*, and many lexicons read 7:11 the same
way, as *secret arts*. The wiki notes the Genesis 3:24 link as possible and does not lean on it.

**The magicians' success is not denied or explained away.** The text says they *"did the same
thing"*. **It does not say how**, and it does not call it trickery. That restraint is the point of
the next verse.

### The swallowing (7:12)

> **12** For they each cast down their rods, and they became serpents: but Aaron's rod swallowed up
> their rods.

***"Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods"*** — ***wayyiblaʿ maṭṭēh-ʾahărōn ʾet-maṭṭōtām***. **The
subject is the *rod*, not the serpent**, and the objects are *rods*, not serpents. The text has turned
the creatures back into staffs in the telling, and the contest is one of staffs — of authority.

***Bālaʿ***, *to swallow*, has a short and pointed history in the books the wiki has reached (WLC
lemma search of Genesis and Exodus):

- **Genesis 41:7, 24** — *"The thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven healthy and full ears"*.
  **It is the verb of a Pharaoh's dream**, the dream the *ḥarṭummîm* could not read.
- **Exodus 7:12** — Aaron's rod swallows the magicians' rods.
- **Exodus 15:12** — *"You stretched out your right hand. The earth swallowed them."* — the end of
  Pharaoh's army.

**The first sign before Pharaoh ends with the verb that will end his chariots.** Beyond Exodus,
Numbers 16:32 uses it of the earth that *"opened its mouth and swallowed them up"* — Korah's men
and their households, the Korah named in [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]] (WLC; Numbers quoted
from the WEB at bible-api.com). See [[Swallowed Up - Pharaoh's Dream, Aaron's Staff, and the Sea]].

**Nothing else happens.** No Egyptian is struck, no water turns, no animal dies. **The sign proves a
claim and harms no one.** It is credentials, not judgment — and it is the last sign of which that is
true.

### Hardened, as Yahweh had spoken (7:13)

> **13** Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he didn't listen to them; as Yahweh had spoken.

***"Pharaoh's heart was hardened"*** — ***wayyeḥĕzaq lēb parʿōh***. **The verb is the qal of *ḥāzaq*,
*to be strong* — intransitive, not passive.** Literally *"Pharaoh's heart was strong"*, or *"grew
firm"*. **No agent is named**: the WEB's *"was hardened"* is a fair rendering that may suggest a
hardener the Hebrew does not mention. (Form checked in the WLC.) God said *qāšâ* at 7:3; the
narrator says *ḥāzaq*, the verb of 4:21. **The fulfilment uses the first prediction's verb, not the
second's.**

***"He didn't listen to them"*** — *wəlōʾ šāmaʿ ʾălēhem*: 7:4's *"will not listen"*, now done.
***"As Yahweh had spoken"*** — ***kaʾăšer dibber YHWH***. **The phrase will close plague after plague**
(7:22; 8:15, 19; 9:12, 35, English numbering, per [[Hardening of the Heart]]). **It makes the refusal
itself a fulfilled word.** Pharaoh, by not listening, does exactly what God said he would.

**The pericope stops before the first blow.** 7:14 begins a new speech, *"Pharaoh's heart is
stubborn"* (*kābēd*, the third hardening verb), and sends Moses to the river with the rod in the
morning.

## Notes

- **Boundary kept as planned.** 6:28 resumes the scene of 6:10–12 after the genealogy, and 7:14
  opens a new speech (*"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn"*) and the first plague. 6:28–7:7 closes the
  commission; 7:8–13 is its first execution. The plan's single unit keeps the prediction and its
  first fulfilment together.
- **Moses *"as God"* (7:1)** is read as a role in the chain of speech, as 4:16 frames it. The
  difference between *lēʾlōhîm* (4:16) and bare *ʾĕlōhîm* (7:1) is recorded, not interpreted
  further.
- **The rod's two names** — *nāḥāš* at 4:3 and 7:15, *tannîn* at 7:9–12 — are recorded as the text
  gives them. The wiki does not decide whether the *tannîn* is a large snake or a sea creature.
- **Aaron's three years' seniority** against 2:1–2 is recorded on [[The Birth of Moses]] and not
  resolved.
- **The names Jannes and Jambres** come from 2 Timothy 3:8, which presupposes a tradition Exodus does
  not contain. Their attestation in the Targums and the Damascus Document is cited from memory and was
  not checked; see [[Jannes and Jambres - The Magicians Who Opposed Moses]].
