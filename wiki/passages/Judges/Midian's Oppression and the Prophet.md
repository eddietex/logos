# Midian's Oppression and the Prophet

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 6:1–10

## Summary

The Song of Deborah ended with *the land had rest forty years* (5:31). The next verse opens the cycle
again with the formula of 3:12 and 4:1, *did that which was evil in Yahweh's sight*, but without their
*again*, and with the verb of giving over that 2:14 used, *delivered them into the hand*:

> **6:1** The children of Israel did that which was evil in Yahweh’s sight: and Yahweh delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years.

This time the oppressor is not a king with a capital. It is a people on the move, and the passage
spends five verses on what that means for the land.

**Hiding in the hills (6:2).** *The hand of Midian prevailed against Israel*, and Israel makes *the
dens which are in the mountains, and the caves, and the strongholds*. The first deliverer stories had
Israel paying tribute (3:15) or being *mightily oppressed* (4:3). Here Israel goes underground. The
caves of the hill country are where the five kings hid from Joshua at Makkedah (Joshua 10:16); now
Israel hides in them.

**The harvest raid (6:3–5).** The raids come *when Israel had sown*: the Midianites, the Amalekites,
and *the children of the east* came up, camped on the fields, and *destroyed the increase of the earth,
until you come to Gaza*. From the eastern edge of the land to the Philistine coast, nothing is left:
*no sustenance in Israel, and no sheep, ox, or donkey*. They bring *their livestock and their tents*,
so this is not a battle but a migration onto Israel's crops. The image the narrator chooses is the
eighth plague on Egypt: *They came in as locusts for multitude. Both they and their camels were without
number; and they came into the land to destroy it.* Deuteronomy 28 had threatened exactly this: *A
nation which you don't know will eat the fruit of your ground and all of your work* (28:33), and *the
locust will consume it* (28:38). See
[[Like Locusts for Multitude - Exodus 10, Deuteronomy 28, and the Midianite Raids]].

The camels are new. They are the first camels in Judges, and *without number* is said of them as well
as of the men. Judges 7:12 repeats the picture just before the battle, adding *as the sand which is on
the seashore for multitude*.

**The cry (6:6–7).** *Israel was brought very low because of Midian; and the children of Israel cried
to Yahweh.* The cry is told twice, at the end of verse 6 and again at the start of verse 7, *When the
children of Israel cried to Yahweh because of Midian*. In every earlier cycle the cry was followed by a
savior (3:9, 15) or by a judge already in place (4:3–4). This time, the first answer is not a savior.

**A prophet instead of a savior (6:8–10).** *Yahweh sent a prophet to the children of Israel.* He is
unnamed, he appears nowhere else, and he speaks in the full prophetic form, *Yahweh says, the God of
Israel*. His speech has three parts:

1. **What Yahweh did.** *I brought you up from Egypt, and brought you out of the house of bondage. I
   delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians, and out of the hand of all who oppressed you, and
   drove them out from before you, and gave you their land.* The opening is the preface of the Ten
   Words, *who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage* (Exodus 20:2).
2. **What Yahweh said.** *I am Yahweh your God. You shall not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose
   land you dwell.* The phrase *the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you dwell* is Joshua's, word for
   word, from the choice he put to Israel at Shechem (Joshua 24:15).
3. **The charge.** *But you have not listened to my voice.*

Then he stops. He announces no punishment, offers no promise, and calls for no response. This is the
same charge the angel gave at Bochim (2:2) and Yahweh gave in 2:20, but with the sentence left off;
see [[I Brought You Up from Egypt - The Indictments at Bochim and in Gideon's Day]]. The next verse
brings *Yahweh's angel* to Ophrah, to a man threshing wheat in a winepress *to hide it from the
Midianites* (6:11). The cry is answered twice: first with a reminder of why Israel is suffering, then
with a deliverer.

**How the cycle has changed.** In the formula of 2:16–18, Yahweh raised up judges because *it grieved
Yahweh because of their groaning*. Under Othniel and Ehud the cry led straight to a savior. Under
Deborah it led to a judge already at work. Here it leads first to a rebuke. The cry is still heard, but
it no longer produces rescue on its own. Judges 10:10–14 takes the next step: Israel cries, and Yahweh
answers *Go and cry to the gods which you have chosen*. The prophet of chapter 6 stands halfway between
3:9 and 10:14.

**The first oppression told as hunger.** Seven years is shorter than Eglon's eighteen (3:14) or
Jabin's twenty (4:3), but it is the first oppression described as hunger. The land Yahweh gave, whose
*increase* (*yəbûl*) was the promised reward of obedience (Leviticus 26:4), is stripped every year at
harvest. The covenant curse withholds that increase (Leviticus 26:20; Deuteronomy 11:17); here Midian
takes it.

## Key verses

> **6:1** The children of Israel did that which was evil in Yahweh’s sight: and Yahweh delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years.

> **6:2** The hand of Midian prevailed against Israel; and because of Midian the children of Israel made themselves the dens which are in the mountains, and the caves, and the strongholds.

> **6:5** For they came up with their livestock and their tents. They came in as locusts for multitude. Both they and their camels were without number; and they came into the land to destroy it.

> **6:6** Israel was brought very low because of Midian; and the children of Israel cried to Yahweh.

> **6:8** Yahweh sent a prophet to the children of Israel; and he said to them, “Yahweh says, the God of Israel, ‘I brought you up from Egypt, and brought you out of the house of bondage.

> **6:10** I said to you, “I am Yahweh your God. You shall not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you dwell.” But you have not listened to my voice.’”

## Notes

**The verbs of the raid.** *Wattāʿāz yad-midyān ʿal-yiśrāʾēl* (2, WLC), *the hand of Midian was strong
over Israel*. The raiders *wayyašḥîtû ʾet-yəbûl hāʾāreṣ*, *destroyed the increase of the land* (4), and
come *ləšaḥătāh*, *to destroy it* (5). Both are forms of *šāḥat* (lemma 7843, WLC morphology: hiphil
and piel). The verse begins and ends the description with the same verb.

**Dens, caves, strongholds.** *Hamminhārôt … hamməʿārôt … hamməṣādôt* (2, WLC). In the WLC files read
for this ingest (Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges), *minhārâ* (lemma 4492) and
*məṣādâ* (4679) occur only in this verse; *məʿārâ* (4631) is the cave of Lot (Genesis 19:30), of
Machpelah (Genesis 23; 25:9; 49–50), and of Makkedah (Joshua 10:16–27). *Minhārâ* is often explained as
a shelter cut into rock, sometimes connected with *nāhār*, *river*, or with a word for *light*; that
is reported, not checked.

**"As locusts for multitude."** *Kədê-ʾarbeh lārōb* (5, WLC): *as enough of locusts for abundance*.
*ʾArbeh* (lemma 697) is the locust of Exodus 10 and of Deuteronomy 28:38. Judges 7:12 has *kāʾarbeh
lārōb*. *Their camels were without number*, *ʾên mispār* (5), is said again at 7:12, where the camels
are also *as the sand which is on the seashore*.

**"Until you come to Gaza."** *ʿAd-bôʾăkā ʿazzâ* (4, WLC): an address to the reader, *until your
coming to Gaza*, the idiom Genesis 10:19 uses for the Canaanite border, *as you go toward Gerar*
(*bōʾăkâ*, WLC), which also runs to Gaza. The raids cross from the
eastern desert to the coast. How far the raiders actually reached, and whether *until Gaza* is a literal
route or a way of saying *the whole land*, the text does not say.

**"Brought very low."** *Wayyiddal yiśrāʾēl məʾōd* (6, WLC; lemma 1809, *dālal*). In the WLC files read,
the verb occurs only here. In 6:15 Gideon calls his clan *haddal*, *the poorest* (lemma 1800, *dal*).
The WLC lists the two lemmas separately; lexicons treat them as the same root, which is reported, not
checked. If they are, the word for what happened to Israel is the word Gideon uses for his own clan.

**The cry, twice.** *Wayyizʿăqû* (6) and *kî-zāʿăqû* (7), both *zāʿaq* (lemma 2199, WLC), the verb of
Othniel's and Ehud's cycles (3:9, 15) and of Exodus 2:23. Judges 4:3 uses the other spelling of the
same idea, *wayyiṣʿăqû* (*ṣāʿaq*). The repetition joins verse 6 to verse 7 and makes the cry the hinge
between the oppression and the prophet.

**"Again" is missing.** Judges 3:12 and 4:1 open with *wayyōsipû … laʿăśôt hāraʿ*, *again did evil*
(WLC). Judges 6:1 has only *wayyaʿăśû … hāraʿ*, *did evil*, as 3:7 has (WLC). Nothing in the text says
why; the difference is recorded, not interpreted.

**"A man, a prophet."** *ʾÎš nābîʾ* (8, WLC). Deborah was *ʾiššâ nəbîʾâ*, *a woman, a prophetess* (4:4,
WLC). The pairing is exact in form. The prophet is the first figure in Judges whose only act is to
speak.

**"The house of bondage."** *Mibbêt ʿăbādîm* (8, WLC), the phrase of Exodus 20:2 and Deuteronomy 5:6
(WLC). The verb *wāʾōṣîʾ*, *I brought out*, is the verb of those verses too (*yāṣāʾ*, hiphil); the
first verb, *heʿĕlêtî*, *I brought up*, is the verb of Bochim (*ʿālâ*, 2:1).

**"All who oppressed you."** *Kol-lōḥăṣêkem* (9, WLC; lemma 3905, *lāḥaṣ*). It is the verb of Exodus 3:9,
*the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them*, of Judges 2:18, *those who oppressed them*, and
of Jabin, *he mightily oppressed* (4:3; all WLC). The prophet's history ends with *drove them out*,
*wāʾăgārēš* (*gāraš*, parsed piel), the verb of Exodus 23 and of Bochim's *I will not drive them out*
(2:3).

**"You shall not fear the gods."** *Lōʾ tîrəʾû ʾet-ʾĕlōhê hāʾĕmōrî ʾăšer ʾattem yôšəbîm bəʾarṣām* (10,
WLC). Joshua 24:15 has *ʾĕlōhê hāʾĕmōrî ʾăšer ʾattem yōšəbîm bəʾarṣām* (WLC), the same clause except for
the spelling of the participle. Joshua offered the gods of the Amorites as one choice Israel might make.
The prophet treats that choice as already made. The command not to *fear* other gods, rather than not
to *serve* them, appears later in 2 Kings 17:35–38; that is from memory, and the book is not stored, so
it is cited by reference only.

**Where the speech ends.** No *therefore* follows the charge. A common reading is that the speech was
cut off, or left open so that 6:11 can supply the answer. Some scholars also note that a Dead Sea Scroll
fragment of Judges (4QJudg^a) passes from 6:6 to 6:11 without verses 7–10, and take the prophet's speech
as a later addition. Both points are reported, not checked; the wiki has not seen the fragment.

**Midian after Numbers 31.** [[The War Against Midian]] has Israel kill every Midianite male and burn
their cities (Numbers 31:7, 10). Here Midian is strong enough to rule Israel for seven years. The wiki
records both, as on [[Midian]], and does not harmonize them. Readings that the Midianites of Numbers 31
were one group of a larger people are reported, not checked.

**Judgment calls.** The unnamed prophet gets a page, as a speaking figure, qualified by period under the
title rule: [[The Prophet (of Gideon's time)]]. *The children of the east* get a page as a people:
[[The Children of the East]]. The Midianites stay on the place page [[Midian]], as in every earlier
ingest. Joash, Gideon, and Ophrah are left for the next pericope.
