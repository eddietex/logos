# Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 15:1–20

## Summary

*After a while, in the time of wheat harvest*, [[Samson]] comes back to Timnah with a young goat to visit
his wife: *I will go in to my wife's room*. Her father will not let him in. *I most certainly thought that
you utterly hated her; therefore I gave her to your companion*, he says, and offers the younger sister:
*Isn't her younger sister more beautiful than she? Please take her, instead* (1–2). Samson answers, *This
time I will be blameless in the case of the Philistines, when I harm them* (3).

He catches three hundred foxes, ties them tail to tail with a torch between each pair, lights the torches,
and lets them go into the Philistines' standing grain. The shocks, the standing grain, and the olive groves
burn (4–5). The Philistines ask who did it and are told: *Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite, because he
has taken his wife, and given her to his companion*. *The Philistines came up, and burned her and her
father with fire* (6). Samson answers that too: *If you behave like this, surely I will take revenge on
you, and after that I will cease*. He strikes them *hip and thigh with a great slaughter*, goes down, and
lives *in the cave in Etam's rock* (7–8).

The Philistines go up, camp in [[Judah]], and spread out in [[Lehi]]. The men of Judah ask why, and are
told: *We have come up to bind Samson, to do to him as he has done to us* (9–10). Three thousand men of
Judah go down to the rock. *Don't you know that the Philistines are rulers over us? What then is this that
you have done to us?* Samson answers in the Philistines' own words: *As they did to me, so I have done to
them* (11). They have come to bind him and hand him over. He makes them swear not to attack him themselves;
they promise to bind him and not kill him, tie him with two new ropes, and bring him up from the rock
(12–13).

At Lehi the Philistines shout as they meet him. *Then Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him*, and the ropes
on his arms become *as flax that was burned with fire*, and the bands drop from his hands (14). He finds *a
fresh jawbone of a donkey* and strikes a thousand men with it (15). He sings:

> With the jawbone of a donkey, heaps on heaps;
> with the jawbone of a donkey I have struck a thousand men. (16)

He throws the jawbone away, and the place is called *Ramath Lehi* (17). Then he is very thirsty, and for the
first time in the story he speaks to Yahweh: *You have given this great deliverance by the hand of your
servant; and now shall I die of thirst, and fall into the hands of the uncircumcised?* (18). *God split the
hollow place that is in Lehi, and water came out of it. When he had drunk, his spirit came again, and he
revived*. The spring is named [[En Hakkore]], *which is in Lehi, to this day* (19). The chapter closes with
a sentence that sounds like the end of a judge's story: *He judged Israel twenty years in the days of the
Philistines* (20).

**What is going on.** Chapter 14 ended with a wife given away; chapter 15 is what follows from it, an
exchange of blows that grows at each turn. A wife given to another man leads to fields burned. The burned
fields lead to the wife and her father burned. Their deaths lead to *a great slaughter*. The slaughter brings
a Philistine army into Judah, and the army brings three thousand Israelites to hand over their own
deliverer. Each side says it is only paying back: the Philistines *to do to him as he has done to us* (10),
and Samson *As they did to me, so I have done to them* (11). Samson twice says he will stop: *this time*
(3), *after that I will cease* (7). He does not stop, and neither do they.

Then the story turns. Bound and handed over, Samson is given the Spirit, a weapon, a victory, and a song,
and the song is about himself: *I have struck a thousand men*. Only when he is about to die does he speak
to God, and he calls the victory God's: *You have given this great deliverance*. God answers with water.
The chapter that began with a private grievance ends with a prayer answered and a judge's twenty years.

**What is notable.**

- **Blameless, then avenged.** *Niqqêtî happaʿam*, *This time I will be blameless* (3, WLC, *nāqâ*, lemma
  5352), and *niqqamtî bākem*, *surely I will take revenge on you* (7, WLC, *nāqam*, lemma 5358). The two
  verbs differ by one letter in these forms. The first claims the right to retaliate; the second names it.
  *Nāqam* returns in Samson's last prayer, *that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two
  eyes* (16:28, WLC *wəʾinnāqəmâ*), and *happaʿam*, *this time*, returns there too, *only this once*. See
  [[Vengeance]].
- **The formula of retaliation, from both sides.** The Philistines: *laʿăśôt lô kaʾăšer ʿāśâ lānû*, *to do to
  him as he has done to us* (10, WLC). Samson: *kaʾăšer ʿāśû lî kēn ʿāśîtî lāhem*, *As they did to me, so I
  have done to them* (11, WLC). It is the frame of Adoni-Bezek's *As I have done, so God has done to me*
  (1:7), and of the private resolve Proverbs forbids: *Don't say, "I will do to him as he has done to me"*
  (Proverbs 24:29, stored WEB). Here both sides say it, and the killing does not end. See
  [[As He Has Done to Me - Proverbs 24.29 and the Law of Retaliation]].
- **The burned fields and the law of fire.** *Gādîš*, *shocks*, and *qāmâ*, *standing grain*, burned by
  fire set with the verb *bāʿar* (5, WLC), are the words of Exodus 22:6: *If fire breaks out … so that the
  shocks of grain, or the standing grain, or the field are consumed; he who kindled the fire shall surely
  make restitution* (stored WEB; Exodus 22:5 in the WLC numbering). *Gādîš* (lemma 1430) occurs in the WLC
  files of Genesis through 2 Samuel checked only in those two verses. The law asks for restitution; Samson
  sets the fire on purpose, and no one pays. See [[Laws of Property and Restitution]].
- **The threat carried out.** *Lest we burn you and your father's house with fire* (14:15); *burned her and
  her father with fire* (15:6, *wayyiśrəpû … bāʾēš*, WLC). She told the riddle to save herself and her
  father, and it did not save them. See [[We Will Burn Your House - Judges 12.1, 14.15, and 15.6]] and
  [[Samson's Timnite Wife]].
- **Judah hands over its deliverer.** *Don't you know that the Philistines are rulers over us?* (11). The
  verb is *māšal* (lemma 4910), the verb of the narrator's *the Philistines ruled over Israel* (14:4) and of
  Gideon's *Yahweh shall rule over you* (8:23, WLC). Judah, the tribe that went up first against the
  Canaanites (1:1–2), now sends three thousand men to tie up the one man fighting the Philistines and give
  him to them. Their question to Samson, *What then is this that you have done to us?*, is the complaint of
  a people that has accepted its masters. See
  [[Who Made You a Prince and a Judge - The Rejected Deliverer]].
- **Bound.** *Leʾĕsôr*, *to bind* (10, 12, 13, WLC *ʾāsar*, lemma 631), and *two new ropes*, *ʿăbōtîm
  ḥădāšîm* (13). In Judges *ʾāsar* occurs only in chapters 15 and 16 (WLC), and *new ropes* returns with
  Delilah: *If they only bind me with new ropes … then shall I become weak* (16:11–12). The ropes melt here
  *as flax that was burned with fire* (14); there, *He broke them off his arms like a thread*. The binding
  that fails twice works the last time, at Gaza, *with fetters of brass* (16:21). See
  [[Bound to Be Handed Over - The Men of Judah and Delilah]].
- **The Spirit's third rush.** *Wattiṣlaḥ ʿālāyw rûaḥ YHWH* (14, WLC), the verb of 14:6 and 19. It is the
  last time the verb is used of Samson. See [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].
- **A shout.** *The Philistines shouted as they met him*, *hērîʿû* (14, WLC, *rûaʿ*), the verb of the battle
  shout at Jericho. Here it is the enemy who shouts, and the enemy who falls. See
  [[Horns, a Shout, and a Fallen Enemy - Jericho and the Camp of Midian]].
- **Donkey, heaps.** *Bilḥî haḥămôr ḥămôr ḥămōrātāyim* (16, WLC). *Ḥămôr*, *donkey* (lemma 2543), and
  *ḥămôr*, *heap* (lemma 2565), are written with the same consonants; the second occurs only here in the WLC
  files checked. The WEB's *heaps on heaps* keeps the sense and loses the pun. The couplet is the only song
  of victory Samson sings, and it names no one but himself.
- **The great deliverance.** *Hattəšûʿâ haggədōlâ hazzōʾt*, *this great deliverance* (18, WLC, lemma 8668, a
  noun usually derived from the root of *yāšaʿ*, *save*; the derivation is reported, not checked). It is the
  first saving-word in the Samson story since the promise *he shall begin to save* (13:5), and Samson
  speaks it, not the narrator. The phrase *təšûʿâ gədôlâ* recurs of the Philistine victories of David and
  his men: *Yahweh worked a great victory for all Israel* (1 Samuel 19:5) and *Yahweh worked a great
  victory that day* (2 Samuel 23:10, 12; WEB at bible-api.com; WLC). There Yahweh *works* it; here
  Samson says Yahweh *gave* it *by the hand of your servant*. See
  [[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].
- **Thirst.** *Wayyiṣmāʾ*, *he was thirsty*, and *bāṣṣāmāʾ*, *of thirst* (18, WLC, lemmas 6770 and 6772).
  Exodus 17:3 has the same pair: *The people were thirsty for water there … to kill us … with thirst*
  (stored WEB; *wayyiṣmāʾ … baṣṣāmāʾ*, WLC). The verb occurs otherwise in the WLC files of Genesis through
  2 Samuel checked only at Judges 4:19, Sisera's *I am thirsty*. At Massah the people quarrel; Samson
  prays. See [[Shall I Die of Thirst - Massah, Hagar's Well, and En Hakkore]].
- **"Uncircumcised."** *Hāʿărēlîm* (18, WLC), the word of his parents' objection to the marriage (14:3).
  Now it is Samson who uses it. See [[Uncircumcised Lips, Ears, and Heart]].
- **The spring of the one who calls.** *Wayyiqrāʾ ʾel-YHWH*, *called on Yahweh* (18), and the spring is
  named *ʿên haqqôrēʾ* (19, WLC). The second word has the consonants of *qārāʾ*, *call*, the verb also used
  in 17 (*that place was called*) and 19 (*its name was called*). The WLC tags the spring's name as a
  single proper noun (lemma 5875). It is usually rendered *the spring of the one who calls*; the rendering
  is reported, not checked. The place is named for the prayer, not for the battle.
- **The jaw and Lehi.** *Leḥî* is the word for *jaw* (15–17, lemma 3895) and the name of the place (9, 14,
  19, lemma 3896, WLC). In 19 the WLC data tags the first *balleḥî*, *the hollow place that is in Lehi*, as
  the common noun *jaw* and the second, *which is in Lehi*, as the place. The WEB reads both as the place.
  The wiki notes the tagging and does not decide between *in the jaw* and *in Lehi*.
- **His spirit came again.** *Wattāšāb rûḥô wayyeḥî* (19, WLC), *his spirit came again, and he revived*.
  1 Samuel 30:12 has the same *wattāšāb rûḥô* of a starving Egyptian slave (WEB at bible-api.com; WLC).
  Genesis 45:27, *the spirit of Jacob … revived*, uses the verb *ḥāyâ* with *rûaḥ* as its subject; here
  *ḥāyâ* has Samson as its subject and *rûaḥ* takes *šûb*. See
  [[The Spirit of Jacob Revived - Ruach Returning]].
- **The closing formula, early.** *He judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines* (20) stands
  where the notices of Tola, Jair, and the others stand, at the end of a judge's story. It is repeated at
  16:31 after his death. The wiki records the repetition and does not draw a theory of composition from it.

**How it connects.** The book's first sentence of retribution was a Canaanite king's, *As I have done, so
God has done to me* (1:7). Here the formula is said by the Philistines and by Samson, and God is in neither
sentence; the chapter puts God back in only when Samson prays.

The water is given as it was at Massah and to Hagar. At Massah the people *murmured* and asked *Is Yahweh
among us, or not?* (Exodus 17:3, 7). Hagar, with the water gone, put the boy under a shrub so as not to see
him die, and *God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water* (Genesis 21:15–19). Samson's prayer is
closer to a complaint than a hymn, but it is addressed to Yahweh and it names him as the giver of the
victory. Of the three it is the only one in which the thirsty person asks God for water himself: at
Massah it is Moses who cries to Yahweh (Exodus 17:4), and in Genesis 21 it is the boy's voice that God
hears. Massah and En Hakkore are named afterward, as Hagar earlier named [[Beer Lahai Roi]] for the God who
saw her (Genesis 16:13–14).

The jawbone completes the parallel with Shamgar's ox goad (3:31): one man, a tool that is not a weapon, and
a round number of the dead. See
[[One Man Against the Philistines - Shamgar's Ox Goad and Samson's Jawbone]].

## Notes

**The father's reasoning.** *I most certainly thought that you utterly hated her* (2) is *ʾāmōr ʾāmartî kî
śānōʾ śənēʾtāh* (WLC): both verbs doubled, *I surely said … you surely hated*. Samson left in anger
(14:19), and the father read that as divorce. *Hate* is the wife's own word from 14:16, *You just hate
me*. The father gives her *to your companion*, *ləmērēʿekā* (2, WLC), the word of 14:20. Whether he was
within his rights the text does not say; the Philistines blame Samson's grievance on him (6), and kill
him for it.

**The younger sister.** *Isn't her younger sister more beautiful than she?* (2). The offer of the younger
for the older reverses Laban's exchange of Leah for Rachel (Genesis 29:26, *to give the younger before
the firstborn*). The likeness is the wiki's observation; the text does not draw it. Samson does not
answer the offer.

**The young goat.** *Bigdî ʿizzîm* (1, WLC), *with a young goat*. *Gədî* (lemma 1423) is the payment
Judah promised Tamar (Genesis 38:17, *a young goat from the flock*), the animal Gideon and Manoah prepared
for the angel (6:19; 13:15, 19), and the animal Samson tore the lion like (14:6). Its meaning as a
marriage-visit gift is commonly suggested; that is reported, not checked.

**Three hundred foxes.** *Šûʿālîm* (4, WLC, lemma 7776), only here in the WLC files checked. Some
translations and commentators suggest *jackals*, which run in packs; that is reported, not checked. The
torches, *lappîdîm* (lemma 3940), are the word of Gideon's torches in the jars (7:16, 20) and of the
*flaming torch* of Genesis 15:17 (WLC). The number three hundred is also Gideon's (7:6–8); the wiki notes
it and builds nothing on it.

**Hip and thigh.** *Šôq ʿal-yārēk* (8, WLC). The idiom occurs only here, and its exact sense, beyond a
thorough slaughter, is not known to the wiki.

**Etam's rock.** *Səʿîp selaʿ ʿêṭām* (8, 11, WLC), *the cleft of the rock of Etam*; *səʿîp* (lemma 5585)
occurs only in these two verses in the files checked. The WEB's *cave* interprets it. See
[[The Rock of Etam]].

**Three thousand and one.** Three thousand men of Judah go down to bind one man (11), and he then kills a
thousand Philistines alone (15). The numbers are the narrator's; the chapter does not comment on them.

**The oath.** *Swear to me that you will not attack me yourselves* (12). Samson asks only that his own
people not kill him; he does not ask them not to hand him over. They keep the oath exactly: *surely we will
not kill you* (13).

**Ramath Lehi.** *Rāmat leḥî* (17, WLC, tagged as one proper noun, lemma 7437). It is usually rendered
*height of the jaw*; reported, not checked. The place was already called Lehi in 9 and 14, before the
jawbone; the chapter gives a second name, not the first. See [[Lehi]].

**Shammah's field.** At 2 Samuel 23:11 the Philistines gather *into a troop*, *laḥayyâ* (WLC, tagged
lemma 2416, *company*). Some propose reading *at Lehi*; that is reported, not checked, and the wiki keeps
the WLC tagging. See
[[One Man Against the Philistines - Shamgar's Ox Goad and Samson's Jawbone]].

**Is it a Nazirite breach?** Samson handles *a fresh jawbone of a donkey*, a part of a dead animal, as he
took honey from the lion (14:9). The chapter does not raise the question; see the note on [[Samson's Riddle]]
for why the wiki states no breach.

**Judgment calls.** One pericope, 15:1–20, as planned. Two new place pages, [[Lehi]] (with Ramath Lehi as
an alias) and [[En Hakkore]], and a third, [[The Rock of Etam]], following [[The Rock of Oreb]]. Ramath Lehi
gets no page of its own because the text presents it as a second name for Lehi. The wife's father and the
younger sister get no pages; the father is covered on [[Samson's Timnite Wife]]. *The men of Judah* are
filed on [[Judah]], as that page already holds the tribe's history in Judges. Two new connections: the
binding thread of chapters 15–16, and the three thirsts.
