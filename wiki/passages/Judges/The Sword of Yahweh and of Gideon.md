# The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 7:16–25

## Summary

Gideon has come back from the edge of the camp with Yahweh's sentence in his mouth: *Arise; for Yahweh
has delivered the army of Midian into your hand!* (7:15). This pericope is the battle, and then the
pursuit. Three hundred men carry no weapon the text names. Israel's men do not strike the enemy in the
valley at all. The killing in the camp is done by Midian's own swords.

**The equipment (7:16).**

> **7:16** He divided the three hundred men into three companies, and he put into the hands of all them trumpets, and empty pitchers, with torches within the pitchers.

*Three companies* is *three heads* in Hebrew, *šəlōšâ rāʾšîm* (see the Notes). The word *head* comes
back three times in this pericope: the *beginning* (head) of the middle watch (19), *the three
companies* (20), and *the heads of Oreb and Zeeb* (25). Saul will divide his army into three companies
and come into the camp *in the morning watch* (1 Samuel 11:11, WEB checked; not yet ingested).

The men have trumpets because the departing army left theirs. *The people took food in their hand, and
their trumpets* (7:8). The *trumpet* is the *šôpār*, the ram's horn Gideon blew to muster Abiezer
(6:34), and the instrument of Jericho (see the Notes and
[[Trumpet and Ram's Horn - Two Instruments, One English Word]]). The *pitchers* are *kaddîm*, the
ordinary water jar. In Genesis–Judges the word is otherwise used only of Rebekah's jar at the well
(Genesis 24). The pitchers are *empty*, and they hide *torches*, *lappîdîm*. The same word is the
*flaming torch* that passed between the pieces when Yahweh made his covenant with Abram (Genesis 15:17),
and the *lightnings* at Sinai (Exodus 20:18) (see the Notes). Judges does not refer to either. No sword
is handed out.

**The plan (7:17–18).**

> **7:17** He said to them, “Watch me, and do likewise. Behold, when I come to the outermost part of the camp, it shall be that, as I do, so you shall do.

> **7:18** When I blow the trumpet, I and all who are with me, then blow the trumpets also on every side of all the camp, and shout, ‘For Yahweh and for Gideon!’”

*Watch me, and do likewise*. The man who needed sign after sign is now the sign his men follow.
*The outermost part of the camp* is where he stood with Purah the same night (7:11). The plan has only
one action: surround the camp and blow. And it supplies the words to shout. *For Yahweh and for Gideon!*
is the cry Gideon writes for himself, and it puts his own name beside Yahweh's. Yahweh had cut the army
down *lest Israel brag against me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me'* (7:2). The text does not comment
on the shout. It is the first time in the story that Gideon speaks of himself with any confidence, and
the last chapter of his story will ask what that confidence becomes (8:22–27, not yet ingested).

**The attack (7:19–21).**

> **7:19** So Gideon, and the hundred men who were with him, came to the outermost part of the camp in the beginning of the middle watch, when they had but newly set the watch. Then they blew the trumpets, and broke in pieces the pitchers that were in their hands.

The time is exact: *the beginning of the middle watch*, just after the guard has changed. New sentries
are awake but have not yet learned the dark. The noun *watch*, *ʾašmōret*, occurs in Genesis–Judges only
here and in Exodus 14:24, *In the morning watch, Yahweh looked out on the Egyptian army … and confused
the Egyptian army* (see the Notes). The sea was crossed at night, and the enemy was thrown into
confusion at a named watch. So is Midian.

> **7:20** The three companies blew the trumpets, broke the pitchers, and held the torches in their left hands, and the trumpets in their right hands with which to blow; and they shouted, “The sword of Yahweh and of Gideon!”

Every hand is full. A torch in the left, a horn in the right. A man holding both cannot hold a sword.
The *held* is *ḥāzaq*, the verb of *your hands will be strengthened* (7:11) and of the three hundred
*retained* (7:8). Gideon's hands were strengthened, and what they hold is a torch and a horn.

Then the shout, and it has grown. Gideon gave them *For Yahweh and for Gideon!* (18). They cry *The sword
of Yahweh and of Gideon!* The word *sword* came from the Midianite in the dark: *This is nothing other
than the sword of Gideon the son of Joash* (7:14). The enemy said *the sword of Gideon* and said *God*
had given the victory. The army says *the sword of Yahweh* first. There is still no sword in any
Israelite hand. The only swords in the next two verses are Midian's.

The pitchers are *broken* twice, with two verbs: *nāpaṣ*, *broke in pieces* (19), and *šābar*,
*broke* (20). The noun *šeber* of 7:15, *its interpretation*, is filed by the WLC lemma data with
*šābar*; whether the narrator means the reader to hear it is not settled (see the Notes on
[[The Dream of the Barley Loaf]]).

> **7:21** They each stood in his place around the camp; and all the army ran; and they shouted, and put them to flight.

*They each stood in his place.* Israel does not move. It is the opposite of Jericho, where *the people
went up into the city, every man straight in front of him* (Joshua 6:20). Here no one goes in.
*All the army ran*. Who *shouted* is not certain. The verb is *rûaʿ*, the battle shout of Jericho
(Joshua 6:5, 10, 16, 20). But the sentence's subject is *all the army*, Midian's camp, so the cry can be
the enemy's own outcry as it runs. The last verb has a written form and a read form (ketiv and qere).
The written form means *made them flee*, and WEB follows it with *put them to flight*. The read form
means *they fled* (see the Notes). On the read form, the whole verse after *stood in his place* is
about Midian: it ran, cried out, and fled.

**Yahweh's act (7:22).**

> **7:22** They blew the three hundred trumpets, and Yahweh set every man’s sword against his fellow, and against all the army; and the army fled as far as Beth Shittah toward Zererah, as far as the border of Abel Meholah, by Tabbath.

*Yahweh set every man's sword against his fellow*. This is the sentence the pericope exists for, and
its subject is Yahweh. The *fellow* is *rēaʿ*, the word of the night before: *a man telling a dream to
his fellow*, and *His fellow answered* (7:13–14) (see the Notes). The two men who talked in the dark
are now any two men in the camp, and each one's sword is against the other. The dream said a barley
loaf would knock the tent flat. It is done by the tent's own people.

Judges 4:15 said *Yahweh confused Sisera* with the verb *hāmam*. This verse does not use that verb. It
says what the confusion was, and the effect is the same: the victory is Yahweh's, and Israel's army
does not strike first. See [[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]]. Later wars repeat
the picture of an enemy turning its swords on itself: Saul's men find the Philistines *all striking
each other with their swords in very great confusion* (1 Samuel 14:20), and in Jehoshaphat's day
*everyone helped to destroy another* (2 Chronicles 20:23) (both WEB checked; not yet ingested).

The flight goes south-east, down the valley toward the Jordan. [[Beth Shittah]], *house of the acacia*,
[[Zererah]], *the border of* [[Abel Meholah]], and [[Tabbath]] are named only here in Judges. Their
sites are not fixed. Abel Meholah will be the home of Elisha son of Shaphat (1 Kings 19:16, WEB
checked; not yet ingested).

**The pursuit (7:23–25).**

> **7:23** The men of Israel were gathered together out of Naphtali, and out of Asher, and out of all Manasseh, and pursued Midian.

The tribes Gideon mustered come back for the chase. *Were gathered together* is a passive of *ṣāʿaq*,
*cry out*, the summons-verb. The muster of 6:34–35 used its twin *zāʿaq* in the same form (see the
Notes). The list is 6:35 without Zebulun. The text does not say whether these are the men sent home
in 7:3 and 7:7, called back, or others. Either way, the three hundred are no longer alone, and the
victory is already won. Israel's arms enter the story only after Yahweh's act.

> **7:24** Gideon sent messengers throughout all the hill country of Ephraim, saying, “Come down against Midian, and take before them the waters, as far as Beth Barah, even the Jordan!” So all the men of Ephraim were gathered together, and took the waters as far as Beth Barah, even the Jordan.

Ephraim was not summoned in 6:35, and is summoned now, late. The order is to cut off the crossing
places before the fleeing army reaches them: *take … the waters … even the Jordan*. Ehud did the same
against Moab, from the same hill country. He *blew a trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim*, and
Israel *took the fords of the Jordan against the Moabites, and didn't allow any man to pass over*
(3:27–28). Jephthah's Gileadites will take *the fords of the Jordan* against Ephraim itself (12:5). That
pursuit starts when *The men of Ephraim were gathered together* (12:1) with the same verb form as 7:24
(see the Notes). See [[The Fords of the Jordan - Joshua 2, Judges 3, 7, and 12]].
[[Beth Barah]] is named only in this verse.

> **7:25** They took the two princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb. They killed Oreb at Oreb’s rock, and Zeeb they killed at Zeeb’s wine press; and pursued Midian. Then they brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon beyond the Jordan.

Ephraim takes the waters, and then takes the two *princes*, *śārîm*. [[Oreb]] is *raven* and [[Zeeb]] is
*wolf* (see the Notes). Each dies at a place that afterwards bears his name: [[The Rock of Oreb]] and
[[The Winepress of Zeeb]]. The text does not say whether the places were named after the deaths or
already had the names. Gideon's story began in *the wine press*, threshing wheat *to hide it from the
Midianites* (6:11). It is a Midianite prince who dies at a wine press. In English the match is exact; in
Hebrew the two nouns differ (see the Notes), and the text does not point it out.

*They brought the heads … to Gideon beyond the Jordan*. This runs ahead. Gideon crosses the Jordan only
in 8:4 (not yet ingested). The narrator finishes Ephraim's part before turning back to Gideon's. The
heads are the last of the four *heads* in this pericope. And they are trouble: the next verse is
Ephraim's complaint, *Why have you treated us this way, that you didn't call us …?* (8:1).

The battle was later remembered by these two names. *Make their nobles like Oreb and Zeeb* (Psalm 83:11),
*as in the day of Midian* (Isaiah 9:4), *as in the slaughter of Midian at the rock of Oreb*
(Isaiah 10:26) (all WEB checked; not yet ingested). See
[[The Day of Midian - Oreb and Zeeb in Psalm 83 and Isaiah 9-10]].

**How the pericope sits in Gideon's story.** It is the battle the whole cycle has prepared for, and it
is short. The comparison is Jericho. Horns blown, a shout, an enemy that falls with no Israelite blow,
and Israel standing still. See
[[Horns, a Shout, and a Fallen Enemy - Jericho and the Camp of Midian]]. At Jericho the priests blew and
the people shouted, and Yahweh's ark was carried around the city. In the valley there is no priest and no ark. A
judge leads, and his name is in the shout. The night that began with Gideon afraid ends with his name
called out beside Yahweh's, and with princes' heads brought to him. The two questions Judges 8 will ask
are already here: what Ephraim is owed, and what Gideon will make of the name.

## Notes

**"Three companies."** *Šəlōšâ rāʾšîm* (16, WLC), *three heads*. *Rōʾš* (7218) recurs as *rōʾš
hāʾašmōret*, *the beginning of the watch* (19), *šəlōšet hārāʾšîm*, *the three companies* (20), and
*rōʾš ʿōrēb ûzəʾēb*, *the head of Oreb and Zeeb* (25, singular in WLC; WEB *heads*).

**"Pitchers."** *Kaddîm* (3537, WLC). In the WLC of Genesis–Judges the noun occurs only in Genesis 24
(Rebekah's jar, nine verses) and Judges 7:16, 19, 20. *Rēqîm*, *empty* (7386), is used of persons in
Judges 9:4 and 11:3 (WEB *vain fellows*, stored WEB).

**"Torches."** *Lappîdîm* (3940, WLC). In Genesis–Judges it occurs at Genesis 15:17 (*a flaming torch*),
Exodus 20:18 (*the lightnings*), Judges 7:16, 20, and Judges 15:4–5 (Samson's foxes) (WLC). The
Genesis 15:17 link is already noted in
[[Smoking Furnace and Flaming Torch - Fire as Theophany]]. In Judges 7 the torch is a weapon of
surprise, and the text implies no theophany.

**"The middle watch."** *Rōʾš hāʾašmōret hattîkônâ* (19, WLC). *ʾAšmōret* (821) occurs in the WLC of
Genesis–Judges only here and at Exodus 14:24, *bəʾašmōret habbōqer*, *in the morning watch*. The usual
explanation that Israel divided the night into three watches, making *the middle* the second, is
reported, not checked. *ʾAk hāqēm hēqîmû ʾet-haššōmərîm*, *they had but newly set the watch* (WLC).

**"Broke in pieces … broke."** *Wənāpôṣ hakkaddîm* (19, WLC), an infinitive absolute of *nāpaṣ* (5310).
In Genesis–Judges *nāpaṣ* occurs elsewhere only at Genesis 9:19 (WEB *populated*), with a sense of
spreading or scattering. *Wayyišbərû hakkaddîm* (20, WLC), *šābar* (7665).

**"Held."** *Wayyaḥăzîqû* (20, WLC), hiphil of *ḥāzaq* (2388), as at 7:8 (*heḥĕzîq*) and the qal
*teḥĕzaqnâ* of 7:11.

**The two shouts.** *LaYHWH ûləgidʿôn* (18, WLC) and *ḥereb laYHWH ûləgidʿôn* (20, WLC). The *lə-* is
*for* or *belonging to*: *a sword for Yahweh and for Gideon*. 7:14 had *ḥereb gidʿôn*, *the sword of
Gideon*, in construct, with no *lə-* (WLC). 7:18 has *waʾămartem*, *and say*; 7:20 has *wayyiqrəʾû*,
*and they called out*, not *rûaʿ* (WLC).

**"They shouted, and put them to flight."** *Wayyārîʿû* (21, WLC), hiphil of *rûaʿ* (7321). In the WLC
of Genesis–Judges *rûaʿ* occurs at Numbers 10:7, 9 (the trumpet alarm), Joshua 6:5, 10 (three times),
16, 20 (twice), Judges 7:21, and Judges 15:14. The final verb is written *wynysw*, a hiphil, *made
flee*, and read *wayyānûsû*, qal, *fled* (ketiv/qere, WLC). WEB follows the ketiv. The subject of the
verbs after *all the army ran* is grammatically open; the wiki reports both readings and adopts
neither.

**"Every man's sword against his fellow."** *Wayyāśem YHWH ʾēt ḥereb ʾîš bərēʿēhû* (22, WLC). *Rēaʿ*
(7453) in Judges 7 occurs at 7:13 (*ləreʿēhû*), 7:14 (*rēʿēhû*), and 7:22 (WLC). *Hāmam* does not occur
in Judges 7 (WLC lemma search). The Hebrew of 1 Samuel 14:20 and 2 Chronicles 20:23 was not checked.

**The flight.** *Bêt haššiṭṭâ ṣərērātâ … ʿad śəpat-ʾābēl məḥôlâ ʿal-ṭabbāt* (22, WLC). *Beth Shittah*
(1029), *Zererah* (6888), and *Tabbath* (2888) occur in Genesis–Judges only here. *Šiṭṭâ* is
*acacia*, the wood of the ark and tabernacle; *Abel Meholah* is usually glossed *meadow of dancing*.
The glosses are reported, not checked. *Śəpat*, WEB *border*, is the same noun as *śəpat hayyām*, *the
seashore*, in 7:12 (WLC). Identifications of the sites are not adopted.

**"Were gathered together."** *Wayyiṣṣāʿēq* (23, 24, WLC), niphal of *ṣāʿaq* (6817). 6:34–35 has
*wayyizzāʿēq*, niphal of *zāʿaq* (2199), the related root (WLC). In the WLC of Judges the niphal
*wayyiṣṣāʿēq* is at 7:23, 7:24, and 12:1; *wayyiṣṣāʿăqû* is at 10:17.

**"Take before them the waters."** *Wəliḵdû lāhem ʾet-hammayim ʿad bêt bārâ wəʾet-hayyardēn* (24, WLC).
Judges 3:28 has *wayyilkədû ʾet-maʿbərôt hayyardēn ləmôʾāb*, and 12:5 *wayyilkōd gilʿād ʾet-maʿbərôt
hayyardēn ləʾeprāyim* (WLC). The verb *lākad* is shared; 7:24 has *the waters*, not *the fords*.
*Maʿbārâ* (4569) occurs in Genesis–Judges at Genesis 32:22 (WEB numbering), Joshua 2:7, and Judges 3:28;
12:5, 6 (WLC). *Beth Barah* (1012) occurs only in 7:24 (WLC, Genesis–Judges).

**"Oreb … Zeeb."** *ʿŌrēb* (6159) and *Zəʾēb* (2062), WLC, occur in Genesis–Judges only at 7:25 and
8:3. As common nouns *ʿōrēb* is the raven of Genesis 8:7 and *zəʾēb* the wolf of Genesis 49:27; the
wiki did not run a lemma count on the common nouns. *Bəṣûr-ʿôrēb*, *at the rock of Oreb*, and
*bəyeqeb-zəʾēb*, *at the wine press of Zeeb* (WLC). *Yeqeb* (3342) is a different noun from the *gat*
(1660) of 6:11, *baggat* (WLC); WEB renders both *wine press*. So the match of 6:11 and 7:25 is in
English, and in subject matter, but not in the Hebrew word. In Genesis–Judges *yeqeb* otherwise occurs
at Numbers 18:27, 30 and Deuteronomy 15:14; 16:13 (WLC).

**"To hide it" and "fled."** 6:11 has *ləhānîs mippənê midyān* (WLC), a hiphil of *nûs* (5127), *to make
it flee*, *to get it away* — WEB *to hide it*. 7:21 has the same verb, hiphil in the ketiv and qal in
the qere, and 7:22 *wayyānos hammaḥăneh*, *the army fled* (WLC). In 6:11 Gideon makes the wheat escape
from Midian; in 7:21–22 Midian flees. Recorded as an observation on the Hebrew; the narrator does not
mark it.

**"Beyond the Jordan."** *Mēʿēber layyardēn* (25, WLC). Gideon crosses in 8:4 (stored WEB). Read as the
narrator's summary running ahead; some read it as *from beyond*, which would place Ephraim, not Gideon,
across the river. Reported, not adopted.

**Judgment calls.** The boundary was kept as planned: 7:16 opens the battle plan and 7:25 closes
Ephraim's part with the heads, before Ephraim speaks in 8:1. [[Oreb]] and [[Zeeb]] get person pages;
the four places of the flight, [[Beth Barah]], and the two death-places get place pages, following the
wiki's practice for every named place. Psalm 83, Isaiah 9–10, 1 Samuel, 2 Chronicles, and 1 Kings were
verified at bible-api.com (WEB) and not stored in `raw/`. The ketiv/qere of 7:21 and the subject of the
shout are left open. The *torch*, *pitcher*, and *rûaʿ* word links are recorded here; only *rûaʿ*, with
the horns, the standing still, and the fallen enemy, enters a connection (Jericho). The *wine press* of
6:11 and 7:25 is treated as an English-level echo only, since the Hebrew nouns differ.
