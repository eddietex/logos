# The Call of Gideon

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 6:11–24

## Summary

The prophet of 6:8–10 ended on a charge and no sentence, *But you have not listened to my voice*. The
next verse answers the cry a second time, and this time with a deliverer:

> **6:11** Yahweh’s angel came, and sat under the oak which was in Ophrah, that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite. His son Gideon was beating out wheat in the wine press, to hide it from the Midianites.

**The man in the winepress (6:11).** The scene is set in a single image of the oppression just
described. Midian takes the harvest every year (6:3–4), so Gideon threshes his wheat where no one
threshes, in the vat where grapes are trodden, *to hide it from the Midianites*. The angel *sat* under
the oak and watched before he spoke. The tree belongs to Joash, Gideon's father, of the clan of
Abiezer, one of the families of Manasseh (Joshua 17:2). See [[The Trees of the Patriarchs]].

**The greeting and the complaint (6:12–13).** *Yahweh is with you, you mighty man of valor!* The title
is said to a man in hiding. Gideon takes up the first half and not the second. He answers with the
plural, *if Yahweh is with us*, and with three questions the prophet had just answered. *Why then has
all this happened to us?* The prophet had said why: *you have not listened to my voice* (6:10).
*Where are all his wondrous works which our fathers told us of, saying, "Didn't Yahweh bring us up
from Egypt?"* The prophet had just recited them: *I brought you up from Egypt* (6:8). *But now Yahweh
has cast us off, and delivered us into the hand of Midian.* The narrator had used that last phrase in
6:1. Gideon repeats the history and draws the opposite conclusion: the exodus is something his fathers
*told*, and his own generation has been abandoned. The text does not say whether Gideon heard the
prophet. It places his words beside the prophet's without comment. See
[[I Brought You Up from Egypt - The Indictments at Bochim and in Gideon's Day]].

**The commission (6:14).** *Yahweh looked at him, and said, "Go in this your might, and save Israel
from the hand of Midian. Haven't I sent you?"* Here the narrator stops calling the speaker *Yahweh's
angel* and calls him *Yahweh*, and does so again in 6:16 and 6:23. The angel returns as *the angel of
God* in 6:20 and *Yahweh's angel* in 6:21–22. The passage moves between the two names and does not
explain the move; see [[The Angel of Yahweh]]. The commission answers none of Gideon's three questions.
It gives him a task, *save Israel*, and a reason, *Haven't I sent you?*. Yahweh *sent* a prophet in
6:8 and now *sends* Gideon, with the same verb.

**The objection and the promise (6:15–16).** *O Lord, how shall I save Israel? Behold, my family is
the poorest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house.* The answer is the answer given to
Moses at the bush: *Surely I will be with you*, with the added promise *you shall strike the
Midianites as one man*. Moses' call at Exodus 3:11–12 has the same parts in the same order: a
sending (*I have sent you*), an objection about the one sent, *I will be with you*, and a sign. See
[[Who Am I - The Objections of the Called]] and [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].

**The sign and the offering (6:17–21).** Gideon asks for a sign, *that it is you who talk with me*,
and asks the visitor to wait while he brings *my present*. The visitor agrees: *I will wait until you
come back.* Gideon prepares *a young goat and unleavened cakes of an ephah of meal*. An ephah is a
large quantity of flour in a year when Midian has left *no sustenance in Israel* (6:4). He brings the
meat in a basket and the broth in a pot, as hospitality. The angel turns the meal into an offering.
He tells Gideon to put the meat and cakes on the rock and pour out the broth, then touches them with
the tip of his staff, and *fire went up out of the rock, and consumed the meat and the unleavened
cakes*. Then he is gone. Abraham's guests under the oaks of Mamre ate the meal (Genesis 18:8). This
guest burns it. See [[Entertaining Angels Unawares]] and
[[Smoking Furnace and Flaming Torch - Fire as Theophany]].

**Face to face (6:22–23).** The sign answers the question Gideon asked, and he is terrified by the
answer: *Alas, Lord Yahweh! Because I have seen Yahweh's angel face to face!* He assumes, as Hagar and
Jacob assumed before him, that seeing God should kill him (Genesis 16:13; 32:30), and Exodus 33:20 had
said so: *man may not see me and live*. Yahweh, now unseen, answers: *Peace be to you! Don't be afraid.
You shall not die.* See [[They Saw God - Exodus 24, Exodus 33, and John 1.18]].

**The altar (6:24).** *Gideon built an altar there to Yahweh, and called it "Yahweh is Peace." To this
day it is still in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.* The altar takes its name from the word Gideon was just
given. It is the third altar in Scripture to be given a name, after Jacob's *El Elohe Israel* (Genesis
33:20) and Moses' *Yahweh our Banner* (Exodus 17:15); see
[[An Altar Called by a Name - El Elohe Israel, Yahweh Our Banner, and Yahweh Is Peace]]. *To this
day* places the narrator later than the event. The next verse, *That same night* (6:25), sends
Gideon to tear down another altar in the same town, his father's altar to Baal. The first altar Gideon
builds stands in the town of the next pericope's Baal altar.

**How the call is told.** Earlier deliverers in Judges are *raised up* in a single clause (3:9, 15)
or are already judging when the story begins (4:4). Gideon is the first whose call is narrated. He
argues, asks for proof, and receives it. The story keeps returning to the same doubt. In this
pericope he asks for a sign. In 6:36–40 he asks for a sign twice more with the fleece. In 7:10–15 he
is given a further sign he did not ask for, the dream of the barley loaf.

## Key verses

> **6:12** Yahweh’s angel appeared to him, and said to him, “Yahweh is with you, you mighty man of valor!”

> **6:13** Gideon said to him, “Oh, my lord, if Yahweh is with us, why then has all this happened to us? Where are all his wondrous works which our fathers told us of, saying, ‘Didn’t Yahweh bring us up from Egypt?’ But now Yahweh has cast us off, and delivered us into the hand of Midian.”

> **6:14** Yahweh looked at him, and said, “Go in this your might, and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Haven’t I sent you?”

> **6:15** He said to him, “O Lord, how shall I save Israel? Behold, my family is the poorest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father’s house.”

> **6:16** Yahweh said to him, “Surely I will be with you, and you shall strike the Midianites as one man.”

> **6:21** Then Yahweh’s angel stretched out the end of the staff that was in his hand, and touched the meat and the unleavened cakes; and fire went up out of the rock, and consumed the meat and the unleavened cakes. Then Yahweh’s angel departed out of his sight.

> **6:22** Gideon saw that he was Yahweh’s angel; and Gideon said, “Alas, Lord Yahweh! Because I have seen Yahweh’s angel face to face!”

> **6:23** Yahweh said to him, “Peace be to you! Don’t be afraid. You shall not die.”

> **6:24** Then Gideon built an altar there to Yahweh, and called it “Yahweh is Peace.” To this day it is still in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

## Notes

**"Beating out wheat in the wine press."** *Ḥōbēṭ ḥiṭṭîm baggat* (11, WLC). *Ḥābaṭ* (lemma 2251) is to
beat out, as with a stick, and *gat* (1660) is the winepress. Wheat is normally threshed on an open
floor where the wind carries the chaff away. A winepress is a vat, often cut into rock, so it would
hide the work. That is the usual explanation of the scene and is reported, not checked. *Ləhānîs*,
*to hide it*, is parsed by the WLC as a hiphil infinitive of *nûs* (5127), *to flee*: literally *to
cause it to flee*, *to get it away*, from the Midianites.

**"Mighty man of valor."** *Gibbôr heḥāyil* (12, WLC). The phrase is a title for a warrior, or for a
man of standing and means. Jephthah is later called the same (11:1; stored WEB *a mighty man of
valor*). Whether the angel is describing what Gideon is, naming what he will become, or speaking
ironically to a man hiding his grain, the text does not say. All three readings are common.

**"My lord" and "Lord."** Gideon's first address is *bî ʾădōnî* (13, WLC lemma 113), the ordinary
courtesy *my lord* used to a man. His second is *bî ʾădōnāy* (15, lemma 136), the form reserved for
God. WEB marks the difference with *my lord* and *Lord*. Between the two, the narrator has said that
*Yahweh* turned to him (14). After the fire Gideon says *ʾăhāh ʾădōnāy YHWH*, *Alas, Lord Yahweh*
(22). The vowel pointing that makes 6:13 and 6:15 differ is the Masoretes'. The consonants are the same
(*ʾdny*), so the change of address is in the vocalized text, not in the consonants.

**"Yahweh looked at him."** *Wayyipen ʾēlāyw YHWH* (14, WLC; *pānâ*, lemma 6437), more exactly *turned
to him*. The verb shares its root letters with *pānîm*, *face*, which returns in 6:22. That echo is
from the shared letters; it is not a claim about derivation.

**"Wondrous works" and "cast us off."** *Niplәʾōtāyw* (13, *pālāʾ*, lemma 6381) is the word for God's
wonders in Egypt. In the WLC of Judges, *pālāʾ* occurs here and at 13:19, where the angel *did an
amazing thing* (*maplîʾ*) in Manoah's flame. *Nәṭāšānû*, *he has cast us off* (*nāṭaš*, 5203), occurs
in Judges only here and at 15:9 (WLC).

**"Haven't I sent you?" and "I will be with you."** *Hălōʾ šәlaḥtîkā* (14), *kî ʾehyeh ʿimmāk* (16),
and *ʾôt*, *a sign* (17), all WLC. Exodus 3:12 has *kî ʾehyeh ʿimmāk … hāʾôt kî ʾānōkî šәlaḥtîkā* (WLC):
the same promise, the same word for sign, and the same verb and form for *I have sent you*. The match
is exact in the Hebrew, and the three items appear within four verses here. The verb *šālaḥ* is also
the verb of 6:8, *Yahweh sent a prophet* (*wayyišlaḥ*, WLC).

**"My family is the poorest."** *ʾAlpî haddal bimnaššeh* (15, WLC). *ʾElep* (504) is the word for
*thousand*, used here, as WEB renders it, for a clan or family unit. *Dal* (1800) is *poor*, *weak*,
*thin*. The previous pericope's *Israel was brought very low* is *wayyiddal* (6:6, *dālal*, 1809);
the link between the two words is noted on [[Midian's Oppression and the Prophet]] and is not
repeated here. *Haṣṣāʿîr*, *the least* (6810), can also mean *the youngest*.

**"My present."** *Minḥātî* (18, WLC; *minḥâ*, 4503). The word means a gift, a tribute, and the grain
offering. It is the word for the tribute Ehud carried to Eglon (3:15, 17, WLC) and for the *meal
offering* Manoah brings in 13:19 (WLC). Gideon's word allows either a meal or an offering. He serves
it as a meal, and the angel treats it as an offering.

**Two rocks.** The angel tells Gideon to put the food on *this rock*, *hassela ʿ hallāz* (20, WLC,
*selaʿ*, 5553). The fire goes up *from the rock*, *min-haṣṣûr* (21, *ṣûr*, 6697). Manoah offers *on the
rock*, *ʿal-haṣṣûr* (13:19, WLC). The two words are near-synonyms; the switch is recorded, not
interpreted.

**"Peace be to you."** *Šālôm lәkā* (23) and the altar's name *YHWH šālôm* (24), both WLC. The greeting
is the ordinary one, and the altar turns it into a name for God. *Wayyiqrāʾ lô*, *called it*, is the
formula of Genesis 33:20, Jacob's altar, *wayyiqrāʾ lô ʾēl ʾĕlōhê yiśrāʾēl* (WLC).

**Ophrah.** The Ophrah of this passage is *Ophrah of the Abiezrites* (24), in Manasseh. It is a
different town from the Ophrah of Benjamin's allotment (Joshua 18:23). The wiki now has two pages,
[[Ophrah (Manasseh)]] and [[Ophrah (Benjamin)]]. Modern identifications of Gideon's Ophrah vary and are
not checked here.

**Judgment calls.** Gideon gets his page now, with his other name, *Jerub-Baal* (6:32), recorded as an
alias for the next pericope to explain. Joash gets a page as [[Joash (father of Gideon)]], since later
books have other men named Joash, kings among them. Abiezer stays on the collective
[[The Families of Manasseh]]. The angel's meal is not given its own connection page; it is added to
[[Entertaining Angels Unawares]], which already sets Judges 6 beside Genesis 18 and Judges 13.
