# The Nations Left to Test Israel

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 3:1–6

## Summary

The book's double introduction ends here, in six verses that do three things.

First comes the **reason** (1–2, 4). These are the nations Yahweh *left, to test Israel by them* (1).
The test falls on those *who had not known all the wars of Canaan*, the generation that came after
Joshua. A second purpose is added: *that the generations of the children of Israel might know, to
teach them war, at least those who knew nothing of it before* (2). Verse 4 restates the test and
names what it measures: *to know whether they would listen to Yahweh's commandments, which he
commanded their fathers by Moses*.

Second comes the **list** (3). *The five lords of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians,
and the Hivites who lived on Mount Lebanon, from Mount Baal Hermon to the entrance of Hamath.* These
are the peoples of the coast and the far north, the edges of the land that Joshua 13:2–6 had already
called *the land that still remains*.

Third comes the **result** (5–6). Israel *lived among* six nations: the Canaanites, the Hittites, the
Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites (5). *They took their daughters to be their
wives, and gave their own daughters to their sons and served their gods* (6). The test was set in
verse 4. Verse 6 reports how Israel answered it, before the first judge appears in the next verse.

## Key verses

> **3:1** Now these are the nations which Yahweh left, to test Israel by them, even as many as had not known all the wars of Canaan;

> **3:2** only that the generations of the children of Israel might know, to teach them war, at least those who knew nothing of it before:

> **3:4** They were left to test Israel by them, to know whether they would listen to Yahweh’s commandments, which he commanded their fathers by Moses.

> **3:5** The children of Israel lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites.

> **3:6** They took their daughters to be their wives, and gave their own daughters to their sons and served their gods.

## Notes

**The end of the introduction.** Judges 1:1–2:5 told the time after Joshua as a list of cities not
taken. Judges 2:6–23 told it as a cycle of sin and rescue. These six verses close both. They name the
nations, as chapter 1 did, and give the reason for them, as chapter 2 did. The next verse, 3:7, begins
the first story of a judge, [[Othniel]].

**"Left."** *Hinnîaḥ YHWH* (1, WLC), the hiphil of *nûaḥ*, *to leave, let remain*. This is the verb of
2:23, *Yahweh left those nations* (*wayyannaḥ*, WLC), not the *ʿāzab* of 2:21, where *Joshua left*
them when he died. So 3:1 picks up where 2:23 stopped, with Yahweh as the one who left them.

**The test, again.** *Lənassôt bām ʾet-yiśrāʾēl* (1, 4, WLC), *to test Israel by them*. The words are
the same as 2:22. Verse 4 adds the purpose clause *lādaʿat*, *to know*, and the question *hăyišməʿû*,
*whether they would listen*. Deuteronomy 8:2 had the same pattern for the wilderness: *lənassōtəkā
lādaʿat … hăṯišmōr miṣwōtāw ʾim-lōʾ*, *to prove you, to know … whether you would keep his commandments,
or not* (WLC; stored WEB). Judges 2:22 asks whether they will *keep* (*šāmar*) Yahweh's way. Judges 3:4
asks whether they will *listen* (*šāmaʿ*) to his commandments. *Listen* is the verb of the charge at
Bochim, *you have not listened to my voice* (2:2), and of 2:17 and 2:20.

**Knowing, four times.** The verb *yādaʿ*, *to know*, comes four times in four verses (WLC): those who
*had not known* the wars (1); *that the generations … might know* (2, the infinitive *daʿat*); those
who *knew nothing of it* (2); and *to know whether they would listen* (4). In 2:10 the new generation
*didn't know Yahweh*. Here they have not known war either. Yahweh means them to learn it, and he means
himself *to know* what they will do.

**"To teach them war."** *Ləlammədām milḥāmâ* (2, WLC), the piel of *lāmad*. The phrase is the
pericope's one surprise. Every other reason the wiki has recorded for the nations who stayed is about
the land or about Israel's sin: the land kept from becoming desolate (Exodus 23:29–30; Deuteronomy
7:22), a punishment (2:3, 21), and a test (2:22). This one is about Israel's skill. The generation that
missed Joshua's wars will learn war from the nations Joshua did not finish. The text does not say
whether this is a fifth reason or another side of the test, and the wiki does not decide. See
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].

**"Only."** Verse 2 begins *raq*, *only*, and uses it again in the middle of the verse (WLC). The WEB
gives *only* and *at least*. The word narrows the purpose: the nations were left *only* for this. How
that narrowing fits beside verse 4, which gives a different purpose, is not explained in the text.

**The five lords.** *Ḥămēšet sarnê pəlištîm* (3, WLC). The phrase is the same, word for word, in Joshua
13:3, where the five are named by city: Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron. *Seren*, *lord*, is a
title the OpenScriptures lemma data files under its own entry (`5633 a`). It is said to be used only
for Philistine rulers; that is reported here, not checked against every occurrence. Judges 1:18 said
Judah *took* Gaza, Ashkelon, and Ekron. Here the Philistines are still there, five lords strong. The
wiki records the two statements side by side; how much *took* means in 1:18 is not settled by the text.
Samson's story will be set among these lords (16:5).

**Mount Baal Hermon and the entrance of Hamath.** Joshua 13:5 gave the same reach as *from Baal Gad
under Mount Hermon to the entrance of Hamath*. Judges 3:3 has *mēhar baʿal ḥermôn*, *from Mount Baal
Hermon* (WLC). The lemma data gives *Baal Hermon* its own entry (`1179`), apart from *Baal Gad*
(`1171`) and from *Hermon* (`2768`). The wiki files *Mount Baal Hermon* as a name on [[Hermon]] and
keeps [[Baal Gad]] separate, because the text does not say whether the two names mark the same spot.
*The entrance of Hamath*, *ləbôʾ ḥămāt*, is the same phrase in both verses (WLC). The morphology data
tags *ləbôʾ* there as part of a proper name, which is why some translations write *Lebo-hamath*; the
WEB keeps *the entrance of Hamath*.

**Two lists, not one.** Verse 3 names the nations *left*: the Philistines, the Canaanites, the
Sidonians, and the Hivites, all on the coast or in the north. Verse 5 names the nations Israel *lived
among*: six peoples, most of them in the hill country. Only the Canaanites and the Hivites are in both.
The first list is the land still to be taken. The second is the people Israel already shares the land
with.

**The six nations, in order.** Verse 5 lists *the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the
Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites*. Exodus 3:8 and 3:17 list the same six in the same order,
in the promise at the burning bush, *a land flowing with milk and honey* (WEB; the Hebrew order of 3:8
also matches, WLC). Other lists use the same names in other orders (Exodus 23:23; 33:2; 34:11;
Deuteronomy 20:17; Joshua 9:1; 12:8, stored WEB), and some add the Girgashites as a seventh
(Deuteronomy 7:1; Joshua 3:10; 24:11). The list at the bush named the peoples whose land Israel would
receive. The same list here names the peoples Israel now lives among.

**"Lived among."** *Yāšəbû bəqereb hakkənaʿănî* (5, WLC). Judges 1:32 used the same phrase for one
tribe: *the Asherites lived among the Canaanites* (*bəqereb hakkənaʿănî*, WLC). In 1:32 it was said of
Asher alone. In 3:5 it is said of all *the children of Israel*.

**Daughters, both ways.** Deuteronomy 7:3 forbade it in two clauses: *You shall not give your daughter
to his son, nor shall you take his daughter for your son*. In Hebrew, *bittəkā lōʾ-tittēn libnô
ûbittô lōʾ-tiqqaḥ libnekā* (WLC). Judges 3:6 uses the same two verbs and the same two nouns, in the
opposite order: *wayyiqḥû ʾet-bənôtêhem … wəʾet-bənôtêhem nātənû libnêhem*, *they took their
daughters … and gave their own daughters to their sons* (WLC). Then Deuteronomy 7:4 gave the reason:
*he will turn away your son from following me, that they may serve other gods* (*wəʿābədû ʾĕlōhîm
ʾăḥērîm*). Judges 3:6 ends *and served their gods* (*wayyaʿabdû ʾet-ʾĕlōhêhem*, WLC). The verse
reports the prohibition broken and the warned result fulfilled, with the law's own verbs. See
[[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]].

The first time the wiki met the two clauses together, they were an offer. Hamor the Hivite said to
Jacob's family, *Make marriages with us. Give your daughters to us, and take our daughters for
yourselves* (Genesis 34:9, stored WEB), and promised in 34:16 that *we will become one people*. Jacob's
sons answered with deceit and a massacre. Judges 3:6 does what Hamor asked, and the Hivites are among
the six peoples in verse 5. See [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] and [[The Hivites]].

Joshua had warned of this too: *if you … make marriages with them* (Joshua 23:12, stored WEB). His word
for *make marriages*, *hitḥattēn*, is the same verb Deuteronomy 7:3 uses (WLC, lemma `2859 b`). Judges
3:6 does not use it; it spells the marriages out as taking and giving.

**Served their gods.** Which gods is not said. The Baals and the Ashtaroth were named in 2:11–13, and
the next verse names *the Baals and the Asheroth* (3:7, stored WEB). Exodus 34:16 had joined the two
things in one sentence: *and you take of their daughters to your sons, and their daughters play the
prostitute after their gods, and make your sons play the prostitute after their gods* (stored WEB).

**Looking ahead.** The book will return to this. Samson's parents ask him, *Isn't there a woman among
your brothers' daughters … that you go to take a wife of the uncircumcised Philistines?* (14:3, stored
WEB). And the book ends with Israel's own daughters: an oath that *None of us will give his daughter to
Benjamin as a wife*, and the daughters of Shiloh *carried off* as wives (21:1, 21–23, stored WEB).
Those chapters are not yet ingested; this note only points to them.
