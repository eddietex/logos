# Samson and Delilah

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 16:4–22

## Summary

*It came to pass afterward, that he loved a woman in the valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah* (4). The
lords of the Philistines come up to her: *Entice him, and see in which his great strength lies, and by
what means we may prevail against him, that we may bind him to afflict him; and we will each give you
eleven hundred pieces of silver* (5).

She asks him plainly, *Please tell me where your great strength lies, and what you might be bound to
afflict you* (6), and he answers three times with a lie:

1. *seven green cords that were never dried* (7). The lords bring them, she binds him, an ambush waits *in
   the inner room*, and at *The Philistines are on you, Samson!* he breaks them *as a string of tow is
   broken when it touches the fire. So his strength was not known* (8–9).
2. *new ropes with which no work has been done* (11). She binds him, the ambush waits, and *He broke them
   off his arms like a thread* (12).
3. *If you weave the seven locks of my head with the web* (13). *She fastened it with the pin*, and he
   *awakened out of his sleep, and plucked away the pin of the beam, and the web* (14).

After each failure she says *you have mocked me, and told me lies* (10, 13). Then: *How can you say, 'I
love you,' when your heart is not with me? You have mocked me these three times* (15). *When she pressed
him daily with her words, and urged him, his soul was troubled to death* (16).

*He told her all his heart*: *No razor has ever come on my head; for I have been a Nazirite to God from my
mother's womb. If I am shaved, then my strength will go from me, and I will become weak, and be like any
other man* (17). She sees that this time it is true and sends for the lords: *Come up this once, for he
has told me all his heart*, and they come *and brought the money in their hand* (18). *She made him sleep
on her knees*, calls a man who shaves off *the seven locks of his head*, *and she began to afflict him, and
his strength went from him* (19).

*The Philistines are upon you, Samson!* He wakes and says, *I will go out as at other times, and shake
myself free.* *But he didn't know that Yahweh had departed from him* (20). The Philistines seize him, put
out his eyes, bring him down to [[Gaza]], bind him *with fetters of brass*, *and he ground at the mill in
the prison* (21). *However the hair of his head began to grow again after he was shaved* (22).

**What is going on.** The pericope is the long fall the whole Samson story has been leaning toward. Every
element of it has been rehearsed: a woman told to *entice* him by Philistines (14:15), a secret pressed
out of him day after day (14:17), Judah's *new ropes* (15:13), a Philistine city lying in wait for him
(16:2). What is new is the secret itself. The riddle was a game; this is the sign of what he is, the
uncut hair of the Nazirite *from the womb* (13:5). And what is new is the end: for the first time a
binding holds.

The narrator tells it almost entirely in dialogue, and the dialogue is a refrain. Delilah asks four
times; Samson answers four times; *The Philistines are on you, Samson!* is cried four times. Three times
the strength is still there, and the narrator does not say why. The fourth time it is gone, and the
narrator says why in a clause Samson does not hear: *he didn't know that Yahweh had departed from him*.
The strength was never in the hair as a charm; when the hair goes, Yahweh goes, and the strength goes
with him. The text puts the two together without explaining the link.

The last verse looks forward. The cut hair *began to grow again*. Nothing else in the verse is said, and
the next pericope, Dagon's feast and the fallen house, [[The Death of Samson]] (16:23–31), is where it
matters.

**What is notable.**

- **He loved.** *Wayyeʾĕhab ʾiššâ* (4, WLC, *ʾāhab*, lemma 157). It is the first time the book says
  Samson loved anyone; the Timnite wife was *right in my eyes* (14:3). In the WLC of Judges, *ʾāhab*
  occurs only at 5:31, *those who love him*, Deborah's last line of the song; 14:16, *You just hate me,
  and don't love me*; and here twice, 16:4 and 16:15, *How can you say, 'I love you'*. The text says
  Samson loved Delilah. It never says she loved him.
- **Her name, and only hers.** Delilah is the only woman in the Samson story who is named. His mother,
  the Timnite wife, her sister, and the prostitute at Gaza are not. Whether she is Philistine or
  Israelite the text does not say. The valley of Sorek lies between Samson's home country and the
  Philistine plain; the location is reported, not checked against a source here. See [[Delilah]].
- **Sorek.** *Naḥal śōrēq* (4, WLC, lemma 7796). The name is commonly connected with *śōrēq*, *choice
  vine*; in the WLC data the vine of Genesis 49:11, *his donkey's colt to the choice vine*
  (*laśśōrēqâ*), carries a different lemma number (8321), so the data does not settle whether they are
  one word. If the connection holds, the Nazirite who may not touch the fruit of the vine (Numbers
  6:3–4) loses his hair in the Valley of the Vine. The wiki records the suggestion and builds nothing on
  it. See [[The Valley of Sorek]].
- **Entice him.** *Pattî ʾôtô* (5, WLC, *pātâ*, lemma 6601), the order the thirty companions gave the
  Timnite wife: *pattî ʾet-ʾîšēk*, *Entice your husband* (14:15). See
  [[Entice Him - The Timnite Wife and Delilah]].
- **To afflict him.** *Ləʿannōtô* (5, 6, 19, WLC, *ʿānâ*, lemma 6031): the lords' purpose (5), Delilah's
  question (6), and what she *began* to do (19). The verb is the verb of Egypt, *they will afflict them
  four hundred years* (Genesis 15:13, stored WEB), and of Israel's oppressors. Here the Philistines' word
  is put in a lover's mouth.
- **Eleven hundred pieces of silver, each.** *ʾÎš ʾelep ûmēʾâ kāsep* (5, WLC). The next chapter opens on the
  same sum: *The eleven hundred pieces of silver that were taken from you* (17:2), stolen by Micah from
  his mother and made into an idol (17:3). The phrase *ʾelep ûmēʾâ* with *kesep* occurs in Judges only in
  these verses (16:5; 17:2, 3; WLC). See
  [[Eleven Hundred Pieces of Silver - Delilah and Micah's Mother]].
- **The inner room.** *Wəhāʾōrēb yōšēb lāh baḥeder*, *the ambush sitting for her in the inner room* (9,
  12, WLC). *ʾŌrēb* is the verb of the Gazites who *laid wait for him all night* (16:2, *ʾārab*, lemma
  693). *Ḥeder* (lemma 2315) occurs in Judges otherwise only at 3:24, Eglon's *upper room*, the room he
  was killed in, and 15:1, *my wife's room*, which Samson was refused.
- **Tow and thread.** *As a string of tow is broken when it touches the fire*, *kaʾăšer yinnātēq
  pətîl-hannəʿōret bahărîḥô ʾēš*, literally *when it smells fire* (9, WLC); *like a thread*, *kaḥûṭ* (12).
  At Lehi the ropes *became as flax that was burned with fire* (15:14). The images grow smaller: flax
  burned, tow singed, a thread. See [[Bound to Be Handed Over - The Men of Judah and Delilah]].
- **The pin.** *Wattitqaʿ bayyātēd* (14, WLC), *she fastened it with the pin*: *tāqaʿ* (8628) with *yātēd*
  (3489), the verb and noun of Jael, who *took a tent peg … and struck the pin into his temples* while
  Sisera slept, *wattitqaʿ ʾet-hayyātēd* (4:21, stored WEB; WLC). In the WLC files of Genesis through
  Judges, *yātēd* occurs otherwise only as tent pegs of the tabernacle (Exodus, Numbers) and the digging
  implement the WEB calls a *trowel* in Deuteronomy 23:13 (23:14 WLC). A woman, a pin, and a sleeping man: once it kills Israel's enemy,
  and here it is the beginning of the capture of Israel's judge. See [[Jael and Sisera]] and
  [[Fallen Down Dead - Eglon's Upper Room and Jael's Tent]].
- **The seven locks.** *Šebaʿ maḥləpôt rōʾšî* (13, 19, WLC). *Maḥlāpâ* (lemma 4253) occurs in the WLC files
  of Genesis through Judges only in these two verses. The weaving, *with the web* and *the pin of the
  beam* (13–14), is usually explained as a loom; the WEB's wording follows the Hebrew closely, and the
  details of the loom are not reconstructed here.
- **Three lies and the truth, in the same words.** *I shall become weak, and be as another man*,
  *wəḥālîtî wəhāyîtî kəʾaḥad hāʾādām* (7, 11, WLC); then *be like any other man*, *kəkol-hāʾādām* (17).
  The truth is told in the formula of the lies, with one word changed.
- **He told her all his heart.** *Wayyaggēd-lāh ʾet-kol-libbô* (17, WLC); the narrator repeats it (18) and
  Delilah says it to the lords (18). It answers her charge, *your heart is not with me*, *wəlibbəkā ʾên
  ʾittî* (15). At Timnah he said *I haven't told my father or my mother, so why should I tell you?*
  (14:16); here he tells her what he never told anyone in the text.
- **His soul was short.** *Wattiqṣar napšô lāmût* (16, WLC), *his soul was troubled to death*; the idiom of
  Israel on the road (Numbers 21:4) and of Yahweh in Judges 10:16. *Hēṣîqâ*, *she pressed him* (16), is
  the verb of 14:17. See [[His Soul Was Short - Numbers 21.4, Judges 10.16, and Judges 16.16]].
- **The razor.** *Môrâ lōʾ-ʿālâ ʿal-rōʾšî* (17, WLC), the angel's words of 13:5, *ûmôrâ lōʾ-yaʿăleh
  ʿal-rōʾšô*, turned from promise to report; and *nəzîr ʾĕlōhîm … mibbeṭen ʾimmî*, *a Nazirite to God
  from my mother's womb*, the angel's *nəzîr ʾĕlōhîm … min-habbeṭen* (13:5). Samson quotes his own
  annunciation, which the text never showed him hearing. See
  [[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]].
- **On her knees.** *Wattəyaššənēhû ʿal-birkêhā* (19, WLC), *she made him sleep on her knees*: Sisera *was
  in a deep sleep* when Jael struck (4:21). The man who shaves him is not named, and the text does not say
  who he was.
- **She began; it began.** *Wattāḥel ləʿannôtô*, *she began to afflict him* (19), and *wayyāḥel
  śəʿar-rōʾšô ləṣammēaḥ*, *the hair of his head began to grow* (22): the same verb, *ḥālal* (lemma 2490),
  that promised *he shall begin to save Israel* (13:5, WLC). Two beginnings, one hers and one God-given,
  three verses apart. See [[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].
- **His strength turned aside; Yahweh turned aside.** *Wayyāsar kōḥô mēʿālāyw* (19) and *kî YHWH sār
  mēʿālāyw* (20, WLC): the same verb (*sûr*, lemma 5493) and preposition, first of the strength and then
  of Yahweh. Samson had predicted the first, *wəsār mimmennî kōḥî*, *my strength will go from me* (17). He
  did not predict the second. Caleb and Joshua said the Canaanites' *defense is removed from over them*,
  *sār ṣillām mēʿălêhem* (Numbers 14:9, stored WEB; WLC); 1 Samuel 16:14 will say *Now Yahweh's Spirit
  departed from Saul*, *sārâ mēʿim šāʾûl* (WEB at bible-api.com; WLC `1Sam.xml`). See
  [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].
- **As at other times.** *Kəpaʿam bəpaʿam* (20, WLC). The phrase occurs in Judges again only at 20:30–31,
  where Benjamin goes out against Israel *as at other times*, and in Numbers 24:1, where Balaam *didn't go,
  as at the other times, to meet with enchantments* (stored WEB). *Paʿam*, *time*, threads the scene:
  *these three times* (15), *this once* (18), and, in the next pericope, *only this once* (16:28).
- **His eyes.** *Wayənaqqərû ʾet-ʿênāyw* (21, WLC, *nāqar*, lemma 5365). In the WLC files of Genesis
  through Judges the verb occurs otherwise only in Numbers 16:14, Dathan and Abiram's *Will you put out
  the eyes of these men?* (stored WEB). Samson's story began with his eyes: *she pleases me well*, *hîʾ
  yāšrâ bəʿênāy*, *she is right in my eyes* (14:3, WLC), and he *saw* the Timnite woman and the prostitute
  at Gaza (14:1; 16:1). The wiki notes the arc; the text does not draw it.
- **The mill.** *Wayəhî ṭôḥēn bəbêt hāʾăsûrîm* (21, WLC), *he ground at the mill in the prison*, literally
  *he was grinding in the house of the bound*. The WLC's consonantal text reads *hāʾăsîrîm*, *the
  prisoners*, and the margin *hāʾăsûrîm*, *the bound ones*, from *ʾāsar*, the binding verb of 16:5–13.
  *Ṭāḥan* (lemma 2912) occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges otherwise only of the golden calf,
  *ground it to powder* (Exodus 32:20; Deuteronomy 9:21), and of the manna, *ground it in mills* (Numbers
  11:8, all stored WEB). That grinding at a hand mill was slaves' and women's work is commonly said; Exodus
  11:5 names *the female servant who is behind the mill* (stored WEB). The wiki does not claim more than
  that.
- **Gaza.** He is *brought down to Gaza* (21), the city whose gate he carried off (16:3), in fetters,
  *bannəḥuštayim*, *with bronze* (WLC). See [[Samson at Gaza]].

**How it connects.** Delilah is the third woman of the story, and the scene is built from the first two.
The Timnite wife was *enticed* and pressed out the riddle; the prostitute at Gaza was the occasion of an
ambush that failed. Delilah does both, four times, and the fourth time succeeds. See
[[Entice Him - The Timnite Wife and Delilah]] and [[Samson at Gaza]].

The bindings close a thread begun when Judah bound him with *two new ropes* for the Philistines (15:13).
At Lehi, *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him* and the ropes melted (15:14). Here no Spirit is named at
the first three breaks, and at the fourth the narrator names its absence. See
[[Bound to Be Handed Over - The Men of Judah and Delilah]] and
[[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]].

The story of Jael is the mirror image: a woman, a sleeping man, a pin. Jael's pin kills the oppressor
and Deborah blesses her *above women* (5:24, stored WEB). Delilah's pin is the first move against
Israel's judge, and she is paid. See [[Jael and Sisera]].

And the next chapter begins with eleven hundred pieces of silver, the same sum, now stolen inside an
Israelite family and made into an idol. See
[[Eleven Hundred Pieces of Silver - Delilah and Micah's Mother]].

## Notes

**Where was the strength?** Samson says it is in the uncut hair (17), and when the hair is cut, *his
strength went from him* (19). The narrator then gives a second account in the next verse: *Yahweh had
departed from him* (20). The text holds both, and does not explain how they relate. The angel's charge
in 13:5 tied the razor to the Nazirite status, not to strength; the strength was given by the Spirit at
Timnah and Lehi (14:6, 19; 15:14). The wiki reads the hair as the sign of the vow and Yahweh as the
source of the strength, and records that this is a reading of two verses the text leaves side by side.

**Did Samson know?** After three betrayals in the same form, he tells her the truth. The text does not
say whether he believed the hair mattered, whether he thought she would not act, or whether he no longer
cared, *his soul was short to death* (16). The wiki does not choose.

**Was Delilah a Philistine?** The text does not say. She lives in the valley of Sorek, and the lords come
*up* to her (5, 18). Interpreters divide; the wiki leaves it open on [[Delilah]].

**The Nazirite vow before this.** Whether Samson had already broken the Nazirite rules, by touching the
lion's carcass (14:8–9) or by the wedding *feast* (14:10), is discussed on
[[Samson's Riddle]] and not settled there. Here the one rule the angel named in 13:5, the razor, is
broken, and not by Samson's own hand.

**The money.** Five lords, if all five are meant, at eleven hundred each, would be fifty-five hundred
pieces of silver. The text says *each*, *ʾîš*, but does not say how many lords came; Judges 3:3 and
Joshua 13:3 name *the five lords of the Philistines*. The total is arithmetic on an assumption the text
does not make.

**Judgment calls.** Kept 16:4–22 as planned, ending at the regrowth of the hair, which the WEB and the
plan both treat as the hinge into Dagon's feast. New pages: [[Delilah]], [[The Valley of Sorek]], and the
connection on the eleven hundred pieces of silver. The man who shaves Samson and the ambushers get no page;
*the lords of the Philistines* are filed on [[The Philistines]]. The Sorek–vine link is recorded with its
lemma caveat and given no connection page.
