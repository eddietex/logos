# Samson at Gaza

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 16:1–3

## Summary

*Samson went to Gaza, and saw there a prostitute, and went in to her* (1). The Gazites hear that he is
there: *Samson is here!* They surround the place and *laid wait for him all night in the gate of the
city*, keeping quiet all night and saying, *Wait until morning light, then we will kill him* (2).

*Samson lay until midnight, and arose at midnight*. He takes hold of *the doors of the gate of the city,
and the two posts*, pulls them up *bar and all*, puts them on his shoulders, and carries them *up to the
top of the mountain that is before Hebron* (3).

**What is going on.** Three verses, and no one speaks to Samson or he to anyone. The chapter that ended
with a judge's formula, *He judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines* (15:20), goes on
as if it had not: Samson is alone, in a Philistine city, for a prostitute.
The Gazites plan for the morning; he leaves at midnight, and takes their gate with him.

There is no Spirit in this episode. At Timnah, at Ashkelon, and at Lehi the narrator says *Yahweh's Spirit
came mightily on him* before each act of strength (14:6, 19; 15:14). Here the strength is there and the
Spirit is not named. Nor is God. Nothing in the three verses says whether the escape is Yahweh's doing,
and the wiki does not supply it.

The episode is also a hinge. Gaza is where Samson will be brought back blind, *bound … with fetters of
brass*, to grind at the mill (16:21), and the city whose gate he carried away is the city where he dies
(16:23–30). The next verse opens the Delilah story: *It came to pass afterward, that he loved a woman in
the valley of Sorek* (16:4).

**What is notable.**

- **He saw, and went in.** *Wayyarʾ-šām ʾiššâ zônâ wayyābōʾ ʾēlêhā* (1, WLC). The order is the order of
  14:1–2, *saw a woman in Timnah* and *get her for me*, without the parents, the wedding, or the
  negotiation. *Zônâ* is a participle of *zānâ* (WLC lemma 2181), the word the narrator of Genesis 38 uses
  for what Judah took Tamar to be (38:15), and the word of Rahab in Joshua 2:1. *Went to* is *wayyēlek*
  (1, *hālak*); at Timnah it was *wayyēred*, *went down* (14:1, WLC).
- **Who told the Gazites?** The WEB has *The Gazites were told*. The WLC has no verb: *laʿazzātîm lēʾmōr*,
  *to the Gazites, saying* (2). Joshua 2:2, the report to Jericho's king, has one: *wayyēʾāmar*, *it was
  told* (WLC, niphal). The translation supplies what the Hebrew leaves out; who carried the news is not
  said, and nothing in the text accuses the woman.
- **"Samson is here."** *Bāʾ šimšôn hēnnâ* (2, WLC). Jericho's king heard *hinnê ʾănāšîm bāʾû hēnnâ
  hallaylâ*, *Behold, men … came in here tonight* (Joshua 2:2, WLC). Same verb, same adverb, at night, in
  a prostitute's house, in an enemy city. See
  [[The Prostitute and the City Gate - Rahab at Jericho and Samson at Gaza]].
- **They lay in wait.** *Wayyeʾerbû-lô kol-hallaylâ* (2, WLC, *ʾārab*, lemma 693). The verb comes back in
  the next pericope, where Delilah has *an ambush waiting in the inner room* (16:9, 12). In the WLC of
  Joshua and Judges it is the verb of the ambush at Ai (Joshua 8), Abimelech's ambushes (Judges 9), and
  the ambush at Gibeah (Judges 20). In Proverbs, *ʾārab* is what the prostitute does: *she lies in wait
  like a robber* (Proverbs 23:28; also 7:12, stored WEB; WLC). At Gaza it is the city that lies in wait
  while Samson is with her.
- **They kept quiet.** *Wayyitḥārəšû kol-hallaylâ* (2, WLC, hithpael). The WLC data tags it with the same
  lemma number (2790) as *ḥăraštem*, *If you hadn't plowed with my heifer* (14:18); whether the two
  senses, *be silent* and *plow*, are one root or two is not settled here, and the wiki builds nothing on
  the tag.
- **Morning light, and midnight.** The Gazites say *ʿad-ʾôr habbōqer*, *until morning light* (2). Samson
  *lay until midnight, and arose at midnight*, *ʿad-ḥăṣî hallaylâ … baḥăṣî hallaylâ* (3, WLC). The second
  phrase is the exact phrase of Exodus 12:29, *At midnight, Yahweh struck all the firstborn*,
  *baḥăṣî hallaylâ* (stored WEB; WLC). In the WLC files of Genesis, Exodus, Joshua, and Judges checked it
  occurs only in those two verses. The match is noted; the text draws nothing from it.
- **Doors, posts, bar.** *Daltôt šaʿar-hāʿîr*, *the doors of the gate of the city*; *šətê hammǝzûzôt*,
  *the two posts*; *ʿim-habbərîaḥ*, *bar and all* (3, WLC). *Məzûzâ* (lemma 4201) occurs in the files
  checked only here and in Exodus 12:7, 22, 23 (the Passover blood on *the two door posts*) and 21:6 (the
  slave's ear at the doorpost). *Bərîaḥ* (lemma 1280) occurs otherwise in those files only as the bars of
  the tabernacle frames (Exodus 26; 35–36; 39–40). The Passover pairing of midnight and doorposts is a
  coincidence of vocabulary the wiki records and does not interpret.
- **The gate of his enemies.** Genesis promised Abraham: *Your offspring will possess the gate of his
  enemies* (Genesis 22:17, stored WEB; *šaʿar ʾōyəbāyw*, WLC). Samson takes the gate of an enemy city with
  his own hands, and does not possess the city. He carries the gate away from it, into Judah, and Gaza is
  still Philistine when he is brought back to it in chains (16:21). Judah had *taken Gaza with its border*
  (1:18); the city is not Israel's now. See [[Possess the Gate of Those Who Hate Them]].
- **On his shoulders, up to Hebron.** *Wayyāśem ʿal-kətēpāyw wayyaʿălēm* (3, WLC, hiphil of *ʿālâ*),
  *put them on his shoulders, and carried them up*. *ʿAl-pənê ḥebrôn*, *before Hebron*, can mean *facing*
  or *east of*; which is meant is not settled here. Hebron is Caleb's city in Judah (1:20), the tribe whose
  men bound Samson and handed him over (15:9–13). The distance from Gaza to Hebron, usually given as about
  sixty kilometres and a steady climb into the hill country, is reported, not checked. The text gives no
  reason for Hebron.

**How it connects.** Rahab's house at Jericho is the closest parallel in the stored text: Israelite men
come at night to a prostitute's house in an enemy city; the king is told *men … came in here tonight*;
the city gate is the point of control, *shut* behind the pursuers (Joshua 2:1–7). There the prostitute
hides the Israelites and asks for an oath, and the city falls. Here the prostitute does nothing at all in
the text, the Israelite hides from no one, and the city does not fall; only its gate is moved. See
[[The Prostitute and the City Gate - Rahab at Jericho and Samson at Gaza]] and [[Rahab and the Spies]].

Proverbs 7 is the other frame: a young man *in the twilight, in the evening of the day, in the middle of the night and in the
darkness* (7:9), a woman *lurking at every corner* (7:12), and a man who goes *as an ox goes to the slaughter*
(7:22). Samson walks into a city of men lying in wait, and walks out. The wiki notes the vocabulary
(*ʾārab*, night) and does not read Proverbs as a comment on Judges. See
[[The Young Man Void of Understanding]].

## Notes

**Is it a Nazirite breach, or a sin the text names?** The Nazirite rules of Numbers 6 say nothing about a
prostitute, and the narrator passes no judgment. The law of Deuteronomy 23:17 forbids *a prostitute of the
daughters of Israel*; the woman at Gaza is not said to be Israelite or not, though she lives in a
Philistine city. The wiki files the episode under [[Sexual Sin]] because the act is one the Torah and
Proverbs treat as such, and records that Judges itself does not say so.

**The prostitute.** She is unnamed, does not speak, and does not act after verse 1. She gets no page of her
own; she is covered here and on [[Samson]]. Unlike the Timnite wife and Delilah, she is never pressed for
anything.

**Midnight.** *Samson lay until midnight* uses *šākab*, *lie down*, the verb also used of the spies at
Rahab's house, *and slept there* (Joshua 2:1, *wayyiškəbû*, WLC). The WEB's *lay* does not say whether he
slept.

**The gate as the city.** The gate was where the city's men gathered and where its defence was held. That
it was also where they lay in wait is why taking it is more than a feat of strength: Samson takes away the
place they were waiting in. This is the wiki's reading; the text only reports the act.

**Why Hebron?** The text does not say. Suggestions that it was meant as a display to Judah, or a token
that Gaza belonged to Judah's allotment (Joshua 15:47), are possible readings; neither is in the text, and
the wiki does not adopt one.

**Judgment calls.** One pericope, 16:1–3, as planned, though it is the shortest in the Samson story; it
ends where the WEB's verse 4 begins the Delilah story with *It came to pass afterward*. No new person or
place pages: the prostitute is covered here, *the Gazites* are filed on [[The Philistines]], and *the
mountain that is before Hebron* is filed on [[Hebron]]. One new connection, on Rahab and Gaza. The
Genesis 22:17 echo was added to the existing [[Possess the Gate of Those Who Hate Them]] rather than given
its own page.
