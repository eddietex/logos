# Jotham's Fable

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 9:7–21

## Summary

The son who hid comes out to speak. When Jotham hears that Abimelech has been made king, he climbs to
the top of Mount Gerizim above Shechem and calls down to the town (7). He tells a fable. The trees go
out to anoint a king. The olive, the fig, and the vine each refuse, because each would have to give up
its fruit to *wave back and forth over the trees* (8–13). Then the trees ask the bramble, and the
bramble accepts. Its terms are *come and take refuge in my shade*, or else fire will come out of it
and *devour the cedars of Lebanon* (14–15).

Then Jotham applies the fable (16–20). He sets one condition twice: *if you have dealt truly and
righteously* in making Abimelech king. Between the two halves he puts the charge. His father fought for
Shechem and *risked his life*, and Shechem has *slain his sons, seventy persons, on one stone*, and
made king *the son of his female servant … because he is your brother*. If Shechem dealt truly, then
let it rejoice in Abimelech. If not, let fire come out of each and devour the other. Then he runs to
Beer and stays there, *for fear of Abimelech his brother* (21).

This is the first fable in the Bible. It is the only speech in Judges 9 that names God, and the only
word of judgment on what Shechem did. Nobody answers it. The narrator does not say whether it came true
until the last verse of the chapter: *the curse of Jotham the son of Jerubbaal came on them* (9:57,
stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**From the mountain of blessing (9:7).**

> **9:7** When they told it to Jotham, he went and stood on the top of Mount Gerizim, and lifted up his voice, cried out, and said to them, "Listen to me, you men of Shechem, that God may listen to you.

[[Mount Gerizim]] is the mountain where Israel was to *set the blessing* (Deuteronomy 11:29). At
Shechem, between it and Ebal, Joshua read *the blessing and the curse* (Joshua 8:33–34). In the WLC
of Genesis–Judges the name *Gerizim* occurs only in those passages (Deuteronomy 11:29; 27:12; Joshua
8:33) and here (see the Notes). Jotham stands on the mountain of the blessing to speak what the
chapter's last verse calls *the curse of Jotham*. The text does not draw that contrast. The place
draws it. See
[[Blessing on Gerizim and Curse on Ebal - Deuteronomy 11, 27, and Joshua 8]].

*Listen to me … that God may listen to you*. The verb *šāmaʿ* is used twice, *šimʿû … wəyišmaʿ*. God
appears here for the first time in Judges 9, and Jotham makes his hearing depend on theirs. He
addresses *baʿălê šəkem*, *the lords of Shechem*, the phrase of 9:2–3, 6 (WLC). Gideon's one surviving
son speaks from a height. Gideon himself went *down* to listen at the camp of Midian (7:11). Jotham
goes *up* to be heard by Shechem.

**The three good trees (9:8–13).**

> **9:8** The trees set out to anoint a king over themselves. They said to the olive tree, 'Reign over us.'

> **9:9** "But the olive tree said to them, 'Should I stop producing my oil, with which they honor God and man by me, and go to wave back and forth over the trees?'

> **9:10** "The trees said to the fig tree, 'Come and reign over us.'

> **9:11** "But the fig tree said to them, 'Should I leave my sweetness, and my good fruit, and go to wave back and forth over the trees?'

> **9:12** "The trees said to the vine, 'Come and reign over us.'

> **9:13** "The vine said to them, 'Should I leave my new wine, which cheers God and man, and go to wave back and forth over the trees?'

*To anoint*, *limšōaḥ*, is *māšaḥ*. Up to this verse every anointing in Genesis–Judges is of a priest,
the tabernacle and its things, offerings made with oil, or Jacob's pillar at Bethel (Genesis 31:13).
None is of a king (WLC; see the Notes). The first anointing of a king in the Bible is in a fable,
and the ones doing it are trees. The second will be Saul, *Hasn't Yahweh anointed you to be prince
over his inheritance?* (1 Samuel 10:1, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested).

*Reign over us* is *mālak*, which is not the *māšal*, *rule*, of 8:22–23 and 9:2. The fable uses
the king word. Each tree is asked to *be king*, which is what Shechem has just made Abimelech.

The three trees are the land's three great crops. Deuteronomy describes the land as *a land of wheat
and barley, and vines and fig trees and pomegranates; a land of olive trees and honey* (8:8). In the
WLC of Genesis–Judges that is the only verse that names olive, fig, and vine together (see the
Notes). Each tree answers with a question that expects *no*. Each says first what it gives:

- The olive gives *my oil*, *dišnî*, literally *my fatness*. *With which they honor God and man by me*.
  Elsewhere in Genesis–Judges *dešen* is only the *ashes* of the altar, the fat-soaked ash of Leviticus
  1:16; 4:12; 6:3–4 (WLC). Olive oil served lamp, offering, and anointing. The tree asked to be anointed
  is the tree whose oil does the anointing.
- The fig gives *my sweetness, and my good fruit*. *Mōteq*, *sweetness*, occurs only here in
  Genesis–Judges. *Tənûbâ*, *fruit* or *produce*, is elsewhere only in Deuteronomy 32:13, *the
  increase of the field* (WLC).
- The vine gives *my new wine*, *tîrôšî*, *which cheers God and man*. *Tîrôš* is the word of Isaac's
  blessing, *plenty of grain and new wine* (Genesis 27:28), and of Deuteronomy's list of what Yahweh
  gives, *your grain, your new wine, and your oil* (Deuteronomy 7:13, WLC).

The olive's oil *honors God and man*, and the vine's wine *cheers God and man*. Both serve God and
people at once. Kingship, in the fable, would serve neither.

*Should I stop*, *heḥŏdaltî*, is *ḥādal*, *to cease*. It is the verb of Babel, *They stopped building
the city* (Genesis 11:8, WLC). To become king, each tree would have to stop being fruitful.

*To wave back and forth over the trees*, *lānûaʿ ʿal-hāʿēṣîm*, three times. *Nûaʿ* is to sway, totter,
or wander. In Genesis–Judges the WLC has it only of Cain, *a fugitive and a wanderer*, *nāʿ wānād*
(Genesis 4:12, 14), of the people who *trembled* at Sinai (Exodus 20:18), of Israel made to *wander* in
the wilderness forty years (Numbers 32:13), and in these three verses. The good trees describe kingship
not as rule or service but as swaying above the others, rootless. Whether *nûaʿ* here carries the
sense of Cain's wandering, or only of a tree swaying, the wording does not settle. Jotham's fable was
told the day after a brother killed his brothers, and Cain is the Bible's first.

**The bramble (9:14–15).**

> **9:14** "Then all the trees said to the bramble, 'Come and reign over us.'

> **9:15** "The bramble said to the trees, 'If in truth you anoint me king over you, then come and take refuge in my shade; and if not, let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of Lebanon.'

*All the trees*. Only now do they speak together. *The bramble*, *hāʾāṭād*, a thorn bush. In the WLC of
Genesis–Judges *ʾāṭād* is otherwise only the place-name of Jacob's mourning, *the threshing floor of
Atad, which is beyond the Jordan* (Genesis 50:10–11; see the Notes). The kind of plant is not
certain; *buckthorn* and *boxthorn* are suggested (reported, not checked). The bramble has no fruit to
leave. It is the only plant in the fable that does not refuse, and the only one that names no gift.

Its answer begins with a condition. *If in truth you anoint me*, *ʾim beʾĕmet*. Jotham will take over
those words, *ʾim beʾĕmet* (9:16, 19). It offers *take refuge in my shade*, *ḥăsû bəṣillî*. A
bramble's shade is a joke: it is low and thorny, and it is not shade at all. *Ḥāsâ*, *take refuge*,
occurs in Genesis–Judges only here and at Deuteronomy 32:37, of the false gods: *Where are their gods,
the rock in which they took refuge?* (WLC). *Ṣēl*, *shade*, is the *shadow of my roof* under which Lot
protected his guests (Genesis 19:8). It is also Caleb's word for the Canaanites' protection, *Their
defense is removed from over them* (Numbers 14:9) (WLC). The bramble offers what a king was supposed
to offer, protection. It has nothing to give it with.

*And if not, let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of Lebanon*. Dry thorn burns fast,
and a bramble fire can run to the big trees. The cedars of [[Lebanon]] are the greatest trees the
reader knows of. The bramble cannot shade them, but it can burn them. In Genesis–Judges the cedar
(*ʾerez*) is otherwise the wood of the cleansing rites (Leviticus 14; Numbers 19) and Balaam's *cedar
trees beside the waters* (Numbers 24:6). Only here is it named with Lebanon (WLC).

*Fire … come out … devour*, *tēṣēʾ ʾēš … wətōʾkal*. In Genesis–Judges the three words appear
together at Exodus 22:6, where a fire *breaks out, and catches in thorns* and the
grain is consumed, at Leviticus 9:24 and
10:2, where *fire came out from before Yahweh*, at Numbers 16:35, and in the song of Heshbon, *a fire
has gone out of Heshbon … It has devoured Ar of Moab* (Numbers 21:28; WEB versification of Exodus
22:6 is 22:5 in the WLC; see the Notes). In Leviticus and Numbers the fire comes from Yahweh. In the
fable it comes from the bramble.

**"If you have dealt truly" (9:16–20).**

> **9:16** "Now therefore, if you have dealt truly and righteously, in that you have made Abimelech king, and if you have dealt well with Jerubbaal and his house, and have done to him according to the deserving of his hands

> **9:17** (for my father fought for you, and risked his life, and delivered you out of the hand of Midian;

> **9:18** and you have risen up against my father's house today, and have slain his sons, seventy persons, on one stone, and have made Abimelech, the son of his female servant, king over the men of Shechem, because he is your brother);

> **9:19** if you then have dealt truly and righteously with Jerubbaal and with his house today, then rejoice in Abimelech, and let him also rejoice in you;

> **9:20** but if not, let fire come out from Abimelech, and devour the men of Shechem, and the house of Millo; and let fire come out from the men of Shechem, and from the house of Millo, and devour Abimelech."

The application does not decode the fable tree by tree. It never says that Gideon was the olive, or
that the seventy were the fig and the vine. It names only the bramble, and only by the bramble's own
sentence: *if … in truth*, and *if not, let fire come out*. The reader is left to see that Abimelech
is the bramble.

*Truly and righteously*, *beʾĕmet ûbətāmîm*, twice (16, 19). WEB's *righteously* renders *tāmîm*,
*whole* or *blameless*, the word of [[Integrity]]. Joshua had used the same pair at Shechem, the other
way round: *Now therefore fear Yahweh, and serve him in sincerity and in truth* (Joshua 24:14),
*bətāmîm ûbeʾĕmet*. In the WLC of Genesis–Judges the two words appear together only in those verses.
Joshua asked Shechem to serve Yahweh *in sincerity and in truth*. Jotham asks the same town whether it
made Abimelech king *in truth and sincerity*. Both speeches open *Now therefore*, *wəʿattâ* (Joshua
24:14; Judges 9:16). See
[[In Truth and Sincerity - Joshua 24.14 and Judges 9.16-19]].

*According to the deserving of his hands*, *kigmûl yādāyw*. *Gəmûl*, *what is dealt out*, occurs only
here in Genesis–Judges (WLC). The chapter's last verses take it up with another verb, *Thus God repaid
the wickedness of Abimelech* (9:56, stored WEB).

The parenthesis (17–18) is the only place Gideon's story is told back in Judges 9. *My father fought
for you, and risked his life*, *wayyašlēk ʾet-napšô minneged*, literally *threw his life away in front*
(WLC). Gideon at Ophrah was afraid, asked for signs, and went with three hundred. His son remembers
the risk. *And delivered you out of the hand of Midian*, *wayyaṣṣēl*. Then the charge: *you have risen
up against my father's house today*. *Risen up* is *qûm*, the verb of Cain, who *rose up against Abel,
his brother* (Genesis 4:8). *And have slain his sons, seventy persons, on one stone*. In 9:5 the killer
was Abimelech. Here Jotham says *you*. The men who paid for it share the guilt. 9:24 will say the same
(stored WEB; not yet ingested).

*The son of his female servant*, *ben-ʾămātô*. Gideon's own word for her was *pîlegeš*, *concubine*
(8:31). The narrator lets the two stand. Jotham's is a brother's insult. *Because he is your brother*,
*kî ʾăḥîkem hûʾ*, gives back Shechem's own reason, *He is our brother* (9:3), word for word but in the
second person.

*Then rejoice in Abimelech, and let him also rejoice in you*. *Śāmaḥ* is the verb of the vine's *new
wine, which cheers God and man* (13). If Shechem dealt truly, let it have its joy. Jotham does not
think it did.

*But if not*. The fire this time comes out of both sides. Abimelech burns the men of Shechem and *the
house of Millo*, and they burn him. It is a curse of mutual destruction. *Beth Millo* is named twice,
the only other verse in Genesis–Judges that names it (see [[Beth Millo (Shechem)]]). The chapter's
second half (9:22–57) carries it out in that order: Shechem turns against Abimelech; he burns the
tower of Shechem with fire, and dies at Thebez (stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**Flight to Beer (9:21).**

> **9:21** Jotham ran away, and fled, and went to Beer, and lived there, for fear of Abimelech his brother.

*Ran away*, *wayyānās*, *nûs*, and *fled*, *wayyibraḥ*, *bāraḥ*, two verbs of flight. *Bāraḥ* is the
verb of Hagar fleeing Sarai (Genesis 16:6), of Jacob fleeing Laban (Genesis 31:20–22), and of Moses
fleeing Pharaoh (Exodus 2:15). It returns at once in Judges for Jephthah, who *fled from his brothers*
(11:3) (WLC; stored WEB). [[Beer (Jotham's refuge)]] is not located. *For fear of Abimelech his
brother*, *mippənê ʾăbîmelek ʾāḥîw*. Twelve verses before, Shechem said *He is our brother*. Now
Jotham calls him *his brother*. Jotham never appears again. His curse is fulfilled in 9:57 without
him.

**How the pericope sits in Judges.** The scene is a turn in the story. Judges 9:1–6 had no word from
God and no word against Abimelech. Jotham's speech names God (7), states the charge (16–18), and sets
the terms of judgment (20). The second half of the chapter carries them out. Gideon's *Yahweh shall
rule over you* (8:23) stands behind the fable. The trees ask for a king, and the good trees refuse in
words close to Gideon's. Whether the fable is against kingship itself, or only against this king and
the way Shechem made him, is read both ways. The wiki notes both. The fable mocks the office
(*to wave back and forth over the trees*). The application judges the choice (*if you have dealt
truly … if not*). See [[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]] and
[[The Bramble and the Thistle - Plant Fables in Judges 9 and 2 Kings 14]].

## Notes

**"Mount Gerizim."** *Har gərizzîm* (9:7), *Gərizzîm* (1630). In the WLC of Genesis–Judges: Deuteronomy
11:29; 27:12; Joshua 8:33; Judges 9:7, found by a lemma search.

**"To anoint a king."** *Limšōaḥ … melek* (9:8), *mōšḥîm ʾōtî ləmelek* (9:15), *māšaḥ* (4886). The
lemma's other occurrences in Genesis–Judges are Genesis 31:13 (the pillar); Exodus 28:41; 29:2, 7, 36;
30:26, 30; 40:9–15; Leviticus 2:4; 6:13; 7:12, 36; 8:10–12; 16:32; Numbers 3:3; 6:15; 7:1, 10, 84, 88;
35:25 (WLC). No other has a king as its object. 1 Samuel 10:1 was checked in WEB only, not in Hebrew.

**"Reign over us."** *Molkâ … mālkâ ʿālênû* (9:8, written and read forms), *mālkî* (9:10, 12),
*məlāk* (9:14), *mālak* (4427) in the qal imperative (WLC).

**Olive, fig, vine.** *Zayit* (2132), *təʾēnâ* (8384), *gepen* (1612). Only Deuteronomy 8:8 and Judges
9 have all three in one verse-set in Genesis–Judges, by a search for verses containing all three
lemmas; in Judges 9 they fall in separate verses, so the search matched Deuteronomy 8:8 only, and the
comparison is the wiki's (WLC).

**"My oil."** *Dišnî* (9:9), *dešen* (1880): otherwise Leviticus 1:16; 4:12 (twice); 6:3, 4 (WLC
versification; WEB 6:10–11). WEB renders those *ashes*. *Mōteq* (4987), only Judges 9:11; *tənûbâ*
(8570), Deuteronomy 32:13 and Judges 9:11; *tîrôš* (8492), Genesis 27:28, 37; Numbers 18:12;
Deuteronomy 7:13; 11:14; 12:17; 14:23; 18:4; 28:51; 33:28; Judges 9:13 (WLC).

**"Should I stop."** *Heḥŏdaltî* (9:9, 11, 13), *ḥādal* (2308), with the interrogative *he*. Other
occurrences include Genesis 11:8; 18:11; 41:49; Exodus 9:29–34; 14:12; 23:5 (WLC).

**"Wave back and forth."** *Lānûaʿ* (9:9, 11, 13), *nûaʿ* (5128). In Genesis–Judges: Genesis 4:12, 14;
Exodus 20:18; Numbers 32:13 (hiphil); Judges 9:9, 11, 13 (WLC). Whether the word's Cain sense is in
play is the wiki's question, not a claim.

**"The bramble."** *Hāʾāṭād* (9:14, 15 twice), *ʾāṭād* (329). In Genesis–Judges otherwise only Genesis
50:10–11, *gōren hāʾāṭād*, which the WLC morphology tags as a proper noun at 50:11. The botanical
identification is reported, not checked.

**"Take refuge in my shade."** *Ḥăsû bəṣillî* (9:15). *Ḥāsâ* (2620): Deuteronomy 32:37; Judges 9:15
only. *Ṣēl* (6738): Genesis 19:8; Numbers 14:9; Judges 9:15, 36 (WLC). Judges 9:36 is Zebul's *the
shadow of the mountains* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**"Fire … devour."** *Yāṣāʾ* (3318) with *ʾēš* (784) and *ʾākal* (398) in one verse: Exodus 22:5 (WLC;
WEB 22:6); Leviticus 9:24; 10:2; Numbers 16:35; 21:28; Judges 9:15, 20, by a lemma search. *ʾErez*
(730): Leviticus 14:4, 6, 49, 51, 52; Numbers 19:6; 24:6; Judges 9:15 (WLC). 2 Kings 14:9 has *hāʾerez
ʾăšer ballǝbānôn* and *haḥôaḥ*, *the thistle* (2336), not *ʾāṭād* (WLC, OpenScriptures morphhb
`2Kgs.xml`).

**"Truly and righteously."** *Beʾĕmet ûbətāmîm* (9:16, 19), *ʾĕmet* (571) and *tāmîm* (8549). *Tāmîm*
with the preposition *bə-* in Genesis–Judges: Joshua 24:14 (*bətāmîm ûbeʾĕmet*) and Judges 9:16, 19
only (WLC). *Wəʿattâ* opens both Joshua 24:14 and Judges 9:16. *Gəmûl* (1576), Judges 9:16 only.

**"Risked his life."** *Wayyašlēk ʾet-napšô minneged* (9:17), hiphil of *šālak* (7993) with *nepeš*
(5315); the only verse in Genesis–Judges with both lemmas (WLC). *Qamtem* (9:18), *qûm* (6965), as in
Genesis 4:8. *Ben-ʾămātô* (9:18), *ʾāmâ* (519); 8:31 has *pîlagšô*.

**"Ran away, and fled."** *Wayyānās … wayyibraḥ* (9:21), *nûs* (5127) and *bāraḥ* (1272). *Bāraḥ* in
Genesis–Judges: Genesis 16:6, 8; 27:43; 31:20–22, 27; 35:1, 7; Exodus 2:15; 14:5; 26:28; 36:33
(the *middle bar* that *shall pass through*, a separate sense); Numbers 24:11; Judges 9:21; 11:3 (WLC).
*Bəʾērâ*, *to Beer*, with directional *he* (876).

**Judgment calls.** The planned boundary was kept: 9:21 ends with Jotham's flight, and 9:22 starts
Abimelech's three years. *Beer* gets its own page, [[Beer (Jotham's refuge)]], qualified by the person
it appears with (the rule `AGENTS.md` gives for unnamed figures, applied here to an unlocated place).
The wiki already had a bare `Beer` for the well of Numbers 21:16, which is renamed
[[Beer (well in Moab)]] in this pass. Nothing identifies the two. A new theme, [[Parables]], is
opened for the Bible's first fable, ahead of Nathan's parable and the Gospels. The bramble, the olive,
the fig, and the vine get no pages. Whether the fable condemns kingship or this king is left open.
