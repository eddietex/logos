# Samson's Riddle

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 14:1–20

## Summary

The first thing [[Samson]] does in the story is look. *Samson went down to Timnah, and saw a woman in
Timnah of the daughters of the Philistines* (1). He comes back up and tells his parents: *I have seen a
woman … Now therefore get her for me as my wife* (2). They object, in the words of the patriarchs: *Isn't
there a woman among your brothers' daughters, or among all my people, that you go to take a wife of the
uncircumcised Philistines?* His answer is the whole of his argument: *Get her for me, for she pleases me
well* (3). The narrator then tells the reader what the parents do not know: *it was of Yahweh; for he
sought an occasion against the Philistines. Now at that time the Philistines ruled over Israel* (4).

He goes down to Timnah with his father and mother. At the vineyards of Timnah *a young lion roared
against him*. *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him*, and he tears the lion apart *as he would have torn a
young goat*, with *nothing in his hand*. He does not tell his parents (5–6). He talks with the woman, and
she pleases him (7). *After a while* he returns to take her, turns aside to see the lion's carcass, and
finds a swarm of bees in it, and honey. He scoops it out into his hands and eats as he walks, gives some
to his father and mother, and does not tell them where it came from (8–9).

His father goes down to the woman, and Samson holds a feast there, *for the young men used to do so*.
*When they saw him, they brought thirty companions to be with him* (10–11). Samson puts a riddle to them
with a wager: if they solve it within the seven days of the feast, he gives them thirty linen garments
and thirty changes of clothing; if not, they give him the same (12–13). *Out of the eater came out food.
Out of the strong came out sweetness.* For three days they cannot solve it (14).

On the seventh day they threaten his wife: *Entice your husband, that he may declare to us the riddle,
lest we burn you and your father's house with fire. Have you called us to impoverish us?* (15). She
weeps before him: *You just hate me, and don't love me*. He answers that he has not told his own father
or mother (16). *She wept before him the seven days, while their feast lasted*. On the seventh day,
*because she pressed him severely*, he tells her, and she tells her people (17). Before sunset on the
seventh day the men of the city give the answer: *What is sweeter than honey? What is stronger than a
lion?* Samson replies, *If you hadn't plowed with my heifer, you wouldn't have found out my riddle* (18).

*Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him*, and he goes down to [[Ashkelon]], strikes thirty men, takes their
plunder, and pays the riddle's solvers with it. *His anger burned, and he went up to his father's house*
(19). *But Samson's wife was given to his companion who had been his friend* (20).

**What is going on.** The chapter is a marriage that fails within a week, and the narrator says it was
meant to. Every human motive in it is small: Samson wants a woman he has seen, his parents want him to
marry within his people, the companions want to win a bet, the wife wants to save her father's house.
Verse 4 sets all of it inside a purpose no character knows: Yahweh *sought an occasion against the
Philistines*. The chapter does not say that Samson's desire was good, or that his parents were wrong.
It says that Yahweh was working through a marriage they opposed and he chose for no better reason than
his eyes.

The chapter is also built on secrets. Samson keeps two from his parents, the lion (6) and the honey from
its body (9), and makes a riddle out of them for strangers. The strangers get the answer by threatening
a woman, and the woman by weeping. A secret kept from father and mother is given up to a wife. The man
whose birth was announced in secret to his mother (13:3) begins his public life with a secret that leaks.

**What is notable.**

- **Right in his eyes.** *She pleases me well* is *hîʾ yāšərâ bəʿênāy*, literally *she is right in my
  eyes* (3, WLC), and verse 7 repeats it: *wattîšar bəʿênê šimšôn*, *she was right in Samson's eyes*. The
  verb is *yāšar* (lemma 3474); the closing refrain of the book uses the adjective of the same root,
  *ʾîš hayyāšār bəʿênāyw yaʿăśeh*, *Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes* (17:6; 21:25,
  WLC, lemma 3477). The first deliverer to be ruled by his own eyes is the one the book says Yahweh is
  using. See
  [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].
- **"It was of Yahweh."** *Mēyhwh hîʾ* (4, WLC). Two close phrasings stand earlier in the stored text.
  Laban and Bethuel, of Rebekah's marriage: *The thing proceeds from Yahweh*, *mēyhwh yāṣāʾ haddābār*
  (Genesis 24:50, WLC). And Joshua 11:20: *For it was of Yahweh to harden their hearts*, *mēʾēt yhwh
  hāyətâ* (WLC). Here the marriage is of Yahweh in the way the hardening was: as a means to a war. The
  *occasion*, *tōʾănâ* (lemma 8385), occurs nowhere else in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges that
  were checked. *He sought* is *hûʾ məbaqqēš*, and the pronoun can be read as Yahweh or as Samson; the
  WEB's lowercase *he* leaves it open, and so does the wiki.
- **A Nazirite at a carcass and a feast.** The rules given at his birth bound his mother: no wine, no
  strong drink, nothing unclean (13:4, 7, 14). Samson takes honey *out of the lion's body* (9). Numbers
  6:6 forbids a Nazirite to *go near a dead body*, and Leviticus 11:27 makes *Whatever goes on its paws*
  unclean, with *Whoever touches their carcass shall be unclean until the evening* (both stored WEB).
  The Nazirite verse speaks of a human dead body (*nepeš mēt*, Numbers 6:6, WLC) and the chapter speaks
  of a lion, so the text does not state that a vow was broken; it tells the reader he did not tell his
  parents where the honey came from, and lets the reader decide why. The feast is a *mišteh* (10, 12,
  17, WLC, lemma 4960), the word used for Laban's wedding feast (Genesis 29:22). It is commonly derived
  from *šātâ*, *to drink*; that derivation is reported, not checked. The chapter does not say Samson
  drank. See
  [[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]].
- **The Spirit rushes.** *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him* (6, 19) is *wattiṣlaḥ ʿālāyw rûaḥ
  YHWH* (WLC). The verb is *ṣālaḥ* (lemma 6743), elsewhere in the stored books the verb of *prosper*
  (Genesis 24:21; 39:2–3; Joshua 1:8). With the Spirit as subject it occurs in Judges only of Samson
  (14:6, 19; 15:14), and in 1 Samuel of Saul and David (10:6, 10; 11:6; 16:13, WLC), and of *an evil
  spirit from God* on Saul (18:10). At 14:19
  and 1 Samuel 11:6 the Spirit's coming is followed in both by *wayyiḥar ʾappô*, *his anger burned*
  (WLC). See [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].
- **Entice.** *Entice your husband*, *pattî ʾet-ʾîšēk* (15, WLC, *pātâ*, lemma 6601). The Philistine
  lords will say *Entice him*, *pattî ʾôtô*, to Delilah (16:5, WLC). *She pressed him*, *hĕṣîqathû* (17,
  WLC, *ṣûq*, lemma 6693), returns at 16:16, *she pressed him daily*. In the WLC files of Genesis
  through Judges checked, *ṣûq* occurs otherwise only in Deuteronomy 28:53, 55, 57, of the siege. The
  wife's *you hate me, and don't love me* (16) is answered in chapter 16 by *How can you say, 'I love
  you,' when your heart is not with me?* (16:15). The Timnah week is a first draft of Delilah. See
  [[Entice Him - The Timnite Wife and Delilah]].
- **"To impoverish us."** *Hălə-yārəšēnû qərāʾtem lānû* (15, WLC). The verb is *yāraš* (lemma 3423),
  which the WEB renders *impoverish*. It is the verb of Judges 1 for taking possession and driving out
  (*hôrîš*, 1:19–33, WLC), the verb the tribes failed to complete. The Philistine guests accuse an
  Israelite bridegroom of dispossessing them by a riddle. The wiki notes the shared lemma; what the
  narrator intended by it is not stated.
- **"Before the sun went down."** *Bəṭerem yābōʾ haḥarsâ* (18, WLC). The word for *sun* is not the
  common *šemeš* but *ḥeres* (lemma 2775), which in Judges occurs otherwise only in *the ascent of
  Heres* (8:13); *Mount Heres* (1:35) is tagged in the WLC data as a proper name, lemma 2776. The
  common word, *šemeš*, is the one usually connected with Samson's name (see [[Samson]]); the narrator
  does not use it here. Whether that is deliberate is not stated.
- **The heifer.** *If you hadn't plowed with my heifer* (18). The answer is itself a riddle, in the
  form of the one it answers. The wiki takes it as an accusation that they used his wife; whether it
  insults her or them, or both, is left open.

**How it connects.** The marriage question was opened in Genesis and closed by law. Abraham made his
servant swear not to take a wife for Isaac *of the daughters of the Canaanites* (Genesis 24:3); Isaac
gave Jacob the same command (28:1). Deuteronomy 7:3 forbade intermarriage with the seven peoples of the
land, and Judges 3:6 reported Israel doing it anyway. Samson's parents do not cite the law; they speak
as Abraham did, of *your brothers' daughters* and *all my people*, and they name the Philistines by the
word Dinah's brothers used of Shechem: *uncircumcised* (Genesis 34:14). The Philistines are not among
Deuteronomy 7's seven nations. See [[Not from the Daughters of the Canaanites]] and
[[Uncircumcised Lips, Ears, and Heart]].

The seven-day feast is Laban's: *Fulfill the week of this one* (Genesis 29:27), a wedding week in which
the bride is not who she should be. Here she is lost before it ends.

The lion belongs to Samson's tribe. Moses' blessing called *Dan … a lion's cub that leaps out of Bashan*
(Deuteronomy 33:22, stored WEB); the Danite meets *a young lion*, *kəpîr ʾărāyôt* (5, WLC), and tears
it. David later tells Saul he killed a lion with his hands (1 Samuel 17:34–35, WEB at bible-api.com).
The Spirit's verb links them too: the *ṣālaḥ* that came on Samson at the vineyards comes on David *from
that day forward* (1 Samuel 16:13).

The chapter opens the cycle that 13:5 promised would only *begin*. Samson's first blow at the
Philistines is thirty men at Ashkelon, struck to pay a debt, not to save Israel. See
[[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]] and
[[One Man Against the Philistines - Shamgar's Ox Goad and Samson's Jawbone]].

## Notes

**Who the thirty are.** *When they saw him, they brought thirty companions to be with him* (11). The
text does not say who *they* are (the bride's family, the townsmen), or why the companions were
brought: as an honour, as wedding attendants, or as a guard on a dangerous stranger. *Mērēʿîm*,
*companions* (11, WLC, lemma 4828), is the word for the man the wife is given to in verse 20,
*ləmērēʿēhû ʾăšer rēʿâ lô*, *to his companion who had been his friend*. One of the thirty gets the bride.

**The arithmetic of days.** *They couldn't in three days declare the riddle* (14); *on the seventh day*
they threaten the wife (15); *She wept before him the seven days* (17); and *on the seventh day* he
tells her (17). The days do not resolve neatly: she weeps all seven days, but the threat is on the
seventh. Some ancient versions are said to read *fourth* for *seventh* in verse 15; that is reported,
not checked. The WLC has *bayyôm haššəbîʿî*, *on the seventh day*, in 15, 17, and 18. The wiki records
the tension and does not resolve it.

**The riddle itself.** *Mēhāʾōkēl yāṣāʾ maʾăkāl ûmēʿaz yāṣāʾ mātôq* (14, WLC). Two lines of three words,
*eater / food* from one root (*ʾākal*) and *strong / sweet*. It is unanswerable by design: it depends on
an event only Samson saw. The counter-riddle in 18, *What is sweeter than honey? What is stronger than a
lion?*, answers it with two questions. *Ḥîdâ*, *riddle* (lemma 2420), and its verb *ḥûd* (2330) occur
in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges checked only here and, for the noun, Numbers 12:8, where
Yahweh speaks to Moses *not in riddles*.

**"Linen garments and changes of clothing."** *Sədînîm* (12–13, WLC, lemma 5466) and *ḥălipôt bəgādîm*.
What he takes at Ashkelon is *ḥălîṣôtām* (19, WLC, lemma 2488), *their stripped garments*, a different
word: the price of the riddle is paid from the dead.

**Where the woman is from, and Timnah.** Joshua 19:43 lists Timnah in Dan's lot, and Joshua 15:10 on
Judah's border by Beth Shemesh. Here it is in Philistine hands: *the daughters of the Philistines* live
there (1). The [[Timnah]] page has the identifications; the wiki does not add one.

**Going down.** Samson *went down* (*yārad*) to Timnah (1, 5, 7), his father *went down* (10), and he
*went down* to Ashkelon (19); he *came up* (*ʿālâ*) to his parents (2) and *went up to his father's
house* (19). The direction marks Israel's hill country against the Philistine lowland; the book will use
the same verbs at Lehi and Gaza. The pattern is noted, not built on.

**The father is not named.** In chapter 14 Manoah is only *his father*. His name returns at 16:31.

**Judgment calls.** One pericope, 14:1–20, as planned; 14:20 closes it, though the wife's story
continues in 15:1–6. The woman gets a page, [[Samson's Timnite Wife]], following [[Manoah's Wife]] and
[[Jephthah's Daughter]]: she is the chapter's second actor, and her death at 15:6 is ahead. The
companion of 14:20 and the thirty companions get no page. No page for the lion. Two new connections: the
Timnah week and Delilah, which is the clearest intra-book parallel the chapter opens, and the Spirit's
*ṣālaḥ*, which the existing [[The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah]] already named in
passing.
