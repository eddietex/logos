# Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 12:8–15

## Summary

Eight verses, three judges, twenty-five years. *After him*, after Jephthah, Ibzan of Bethlehem
judged Israel. He had thirty sons and sixty daughters by marriage, thirty sent *outside* and thirty
brought *in from outside* for his sons. He judged seven years and was buried at Bethlehem (8–10).
*After him* Elon the Zebulunite judged ten years and was buried in Aijalon in the land of Zebulun
(11–12). *After him* Abdon the son of Hillel the Pirathonite judged eight years. He had forty sons
and thirty grandsons who rode on seventy donkey colts. He was buried in Pirathon, *in the land of
Ephraim, in the hill country of the Amalekites* (13–15).

As with Tola and Jair (10:1–5), nothing else happens. No sin is reported, no enemy named, no one
cries out. The Spirit comes on no one, no battle is fought, and the land is not said to rest. God is
not mentioned. These are the last three of the five short notices scholars call the *minor judges*,
a modern label, not a word of the text. The next verse begins the last cycle of the book: *The
children of Israel again did that which was evil in Yahweh's sight; and Yahweh delivered them into
the hand of the Philistines forty years* (13:1). See
[[The Minor Judges - Judges 10.1-5 and 12.8-15]].

**Ibzan (12:8–10).**

> **12:8** After him Ibzan of Bethlehem judged Israel.

> **12:9** He had thirty sons; and thirty daughters he sent outside his clan, and thirty daughters he brought in from outside his clan for his sons. He judged Israel seven years.

> **12:10** Ibzan died, and was buried at Bethlehem.

*After him Ibzan … judged Israel*, *wayyišpōṭ ʾaḥărāyw ʾet-yiśrāʾēl ʾibṣān* (WLC). Tola and Jair
*arose* (10:1, 3). The three in this chapter do not. Each notice opens with the judging verb itself,
and the verb comes before the man's name. What is said of Ibzan first is that he judged.

*Ibzan*, *ʾibṣān* (lemma 78). In the WLC books read for this ingest (Genesis, Numbers, Joshua,
Judges) the name occurs only here, at 12:8 and 12:10. Its meaning is not given in the text and the
wiki does not supply one.

*Of Bethlehem*, *mibbêt leḥem*. Which Bethlehem the text does not say. Every other time Judges names
the town, it is *Bethlehem Judah*: the Levite of Micah's house (17:7–9) and the concubine of
chapter 19 (19:1–2, 18). In the WLC, *bêt leḥem* in Judges is followed by *yəhûdâ* at 17:7, 8, 9;
19:1, 2, and twice at 19:18. Only Ibzan's two verses (12:8, 10) leave it bare. Joshua 19:15 lists a
second Bethlehem among Zebulun's twelve cities, and the next judge is a Zebulunite. Either town fits
the text. The wiki records the notice on both place pages and decides neither. See [[Bethlehem]] and
[[Bethlehem (Zebulun)]]. Later Jewish tradition identifying Ibzan with Boaz of Bethlehem Judah is
reported, not checked (recalled, not verified, from the Talmudic tractate Bava Batra 91a); the text of
Judges makes no such link.

*Thirty daughters he sent outside his clan, and thirty daughters he brought in from outside his clan
for his sons.* The Hebrew has no word for *clan*. It reads *šillaḥ haḥûṣâ*, *he sent outside*, and
*hēbîʾ ləbānāyw min-haḥûṣ*, *he brought for his sons from outside* (WLC). *His clan* is the WEB's
interpretation of *outside*. Outside of what, the family, the clan, the tribe, the text does not
say. The marriages run both ways, out and in, in equal number. The book's first comment on marriage
ran the other way: Israel took the daughters of the land's peoples *to be their wives, and gave
their own daughters to their sons and served their gods* (3:6). Ibzan's sixty marriages are told
with no comment and no gods. Whether *outside* reaches beyond Israel, the text does not say, and the
wiki does not read 3:6 into it. In the WLC, *ḥûṣ* (lemma 2351) occurs in Judges only here and at
19:25, where the concubine from Bethlehem Judah is brought *out* to the men of Gibeah (*haḥûṣ*, WLC). The
verbal link is the wiki's observation and a slight one. See [[Marriage]].

*Thirty sons.* Jair also had thirty sons (10:4). The number matches. Ibzan's sons are given wives
where Jair's are given donkeys and cities.

*Buried at Bethlehem.* He is buried where he is from, as Tola was (10:1–2).

**Elon (12:11–12).**

> **12:11** After him Elon the Zebulunite judged Israel; and he judged Israel ten years.

> **12:12** Elon the Zebulunite died, and was buried in Aijalon in the land of Zebulun.

*Elon the Zebulunite*, *ʾêlôn hazzəbûlōnî* (lemma 356). The shortest notice of the five: a name, a
tribe, ten years, a grave. No family is mentioned.

**The judge bears his tribe's clan name.** *Elon* is the name of Zebulun's second son: *The sons of
Zebulun: Sered, Elon, and Jahleel* (Genesis 46:14), and the census counts *of Elon, the family of the
Elonites* (Numbers 26:26). The WLC gives all three the same lemma, 356. Tola the son of Puah, the
first minor judge, likewise bore the names of Issachar's clans (Genesis 46:13; Numbers 26:23; see
[[Tola and Jair]]). Two of the five minor judges, then, carry the clan names of their own tribes.
Whether that reflects a naming custom, a clan remembered as a person, or simple recurrence of family
names, the text does not say. See
[[Clan Names Among the Minor Judges - Genesis 46, Numbers 26, and Judges 10 and 12]].

The same lemma names two other figures: Esau's father-in-law [[Elon the Hittite]] (Genesis 26:34;
36:2) and a town in Dan's list, *Elon* (Joshua 19:43). None of these is the judge.

*Buried in Aijalon in the land of Zebulun*, *bəʾayyālôn bəʾereṣ zəbûlun* (WLC). The burial place
looks like the judge's name. *Elon* at 12:11 is written *ʾylwn*, *Aijalon* at 12:12 *ʾylwn*: the same
consonants, pointed differently (*ʾêlôn*, *ʾayyālôn*). At 12:12 the name of the man is written without
its *y*, *ʾlwn*. The morphhb lemma data keeps them apart, 356 for the man and 357 for the place. The
better-known Aijalon is in Dan (Joshua 19:42; 21:24; Judges 1:35), and 12:12 specifies *in the land of
Zebulun*, as if to keep the two apart. The Danite town *Elon* stands next to *Aijalon* in Joshua's
list (19:42–43). That a Zebulunite Elon was buried in an otherwise unknown Zebulunite *Aijalon* may
be a name the place took from him, a variant spelling of one word, or a coincidence. The text does
not explain it. Some ancient translations are reported to read the place name as *Elon*; that is
recalled, not verified, and no Greek text was consulted. See [[Aijalon (Zebulun)]].

**Abdon (12:13–15).**

> **12:13** After him Abdon the son of Hillel the Pirathonite judged Israel.

> **12:14** He had forty sons and thirty sons' sons, who rode on seventy donkey colts. He judged Israel eight years.

> **12:15** Abdon the son of Hillel the Pirathonite died, and was buried in Pirathon in the land of Ephraim, in the hill country of the Amalekites.

*Abdon the son of Hillel the Pirathonite*, *ʿabdôn ben-hillēl happirʿātônî* (WLC). Given a father and
a town, and both repeated in full at his death (12:15). *Abdon* is also the name of a Levitical city of
Asher (Joshua 21:30), the same word and lemma (5658). The two are unrelated in the text. See
[[Abdon (city)]]. The name is often connected with *ʿebed*, *servant*, and *Hillel* with *hll*, *to
praise*. Both derivations are reported, not checked.

*Forty sons and thirty sons' sons, who rode on seventy donkey colts.* The numbers repeat the book.
Seventy riders recall Gideon's seventy sons (8:30) and Abimelech's *seventy persons, on one stone*
(9:5). The donkey colts, *ʿăyārim* (lemma 5895, spelled here without the second *y*), are the animals
of Jair's thirty sons (10:4). In the WLC books of Genesis through Judges, *ʿayir* occurs in only five
verses: Genesis 32:16 (WLC numbering; 32:15 in WEB), Genesis 49:11, Judges 10:4 (twice), and here.
Riding donkeys is a mark of standing in Judges: *you who ride on white donkeys, you who sit on rich
carpets* (5:10). Abdon's notice is the only one of the five to count grandsons. The household is
three generations deep.

*Pirathon in the land of Ephraim.* The first named town of an Ephraimite judge since the hill country
where Deborah sat (4:5) and Ehud blew the trumpet (3:27). Ephraim has just lost forty-two thousand at
the fords (12:6). The next judge of Israel after that slaughter is buried in Ephraim's land. The text
does not comment, and the sequence is the wiki's observation. In the WLC books read here, *Pirathon*
(lemma 6552) and *Pirathonite* (6553) occur only in this notice. The site is not given. See
[[Pirathon]].

*In the hill country of the Amalekites*, *bəhar hāʿămālēqî* (WLC), literally *the hill country of the
Amalekite*, a singular gentilic as at Genesis 14:7 and Numbers 14:25, 43, 45. An Amalekite name inside
Ephraim's hill country. The Song of Deborah already said *Those whose root is in Amalek came out of
Ephraim* (5:14). Whether the phrase preserves an older Amalekite presence, a clan name, or an old
place name, the text does not say. Amalek is elsewhere in Judges an enemy, allied with Moab (3:13) and
with Midian (6:3, 33; 7:12). Here it is only a name on the map. See [[Amalek]] and
[[The Hill Country of Ephraim]].

**The three together.** Each notice ends *and he died … and was buried in* a named place,
*wayyāmot … wayyiqqābēr* (WLC), the formula that closed Jephthah's notice the verse before (12:7).
Every minor judge is buried, and every grave is named: Shamir, Kamon, Bethlehem, Aijalon, Pirathon.
Read in order, the three come from the south or north (Bethlehem, the text does not say which), from
Zebulun in the north, and from Ephraim in the centre. Their years are short, seven, ten, and eight,
twenty-five in all, after Tola's twenty-three and Jair's twenty-two. Two of the three have large
households. The text gives no verdict on them. After them it moves to the Philistines and Samson
(13:1), whose notice will also close *He judged Israel twenty years* and with a burial in his
father's tomb (16:31).

## People
- [[Ibzan]] — of Bethlehem; thirty sons, sixty daughters by marriage; judges seven years (8–10).
- [[Elon the Zebulunite]] — judges ten years; buried in Aijalon in Zebulun (11–12).
- [[Abdon (son of Hillel)]] — the Pirathonite; forty sons and thirty grandsons on seventy donkey
  colts; judges eight years (13–15).
- [[Jephthah]] — *after him*, the judge before Ibzan (8; cf. 12:7).
- [[Zebulun]] — Elon's tribe; *Elon* is the name of Zebulun's son and clan (Genesis 46:14; Numbers
  26:26).
- [[Ephraim]] — Abdon is buried *in the land of Ephraim* (15), after Ephraim's defeat at the fords.
- [[Amalek]] — *the hill country of the Amalekites* (15).
- [[Elon the Hittite]] — a different Elon, Esau's father-in-law; same name, unrelated.
- Hillel, Abdon's father, is named only in his son's patronymic and gets no page; see the judgment
  calls in the log.

## Notes

**One pericope or three?** The plan kept the three notices together, and this ingest keeps them so.
They share one form and are joined by *after him* (8, 11, 13), and 13:1 begins a new cycle with
*again did that which was evil*. Three pages of two or three verses each would split one literary
unit, as with Tola and Jair.

**Which Bethlehem.** The arguments both ways are recorded above. The wiki's only firm observation
is the WLC fact that Judges qualifies Bethlehem as *Judah* everywhere else and not here. Whether the
bare name means the narrator thought no qualification was needed, or that a different town is meant,
the text does not say.

**Seven, ten, eight.** With Tola's twenty-three and Jair's twenty-two, the minor judges total seventy
years. Interpreters have noticed the total; whether it is meant is not something the text says, and
the wiki records the sum as arithmetic only. Jephthah's six years lie between the two groups (12:7).
