# Gideon's Ephod and Death

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 8:22–35

## Summary

The war is over and the kings are dead. This pericope is the end of Gideon's story, and it is told in
three movements. Israel offers him rule, and he refuses it in Yahweh's name (22–23). He asks for gold
instead, and makes an ephod that becomes Israel's idol and his own house's snare (24–27). Then the
narrator closes the account the way the book closes a judge's account, with rest, a family, a death,
and a burial (28–32), and adds the relapse the book has taught the reader to expect (33–35). The
last two verses carry a charge the book has not made before: Israel forgot Yahweh, and it also failed
to show *kindness* to Gideon's house. The next chapter shows what that failure looks like. The son
named in verse 31 is Abimelech.

**The offer of rule (8:22–23).**

> **8:22** Then the men of Israel said to Gideon, "Rule over us, both you, and your son, and your son's son also; for you have saved us out of the hand of Midian."

The offer is for a dynasty: *you, and your son, and your son's son*. The word is *rule*, *məšol*, from
*māšal*. Neither *king*, *melek*, nor the verb *reign*, *mālak*, occurs in 8:22–23 (see the Notes).
The reason given is *for you have saved us*, *hôšaʿtānû*. That is the verb of Gideon's commission, *save
Israel from the hand of Midian* (6:14), and of his own fleece prayer, *you will save Israel by my hand*
(6:37). It is also the verb of Yahweh's warning before the battle. Yahweh cut the army to three hundred
*lest Israel brag against me, saying, "My own hand has saved me"* (7:2). The men of Israel do not say
*my own hand*. They say *you*. But the credit Yahweh would not let them take for themselves they now
give to a man.

> **8:23** Gideon said to them, "I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you. Yahweh shall rule over you."

The answer uses *māšal* three times, once for each of the three refused rulers: not I, not my son,
Yahweh. It is the clearest statement in Genesis–Judges that Yahweh *rules* Israel; in the WLC of those
books Yahweh is not elsewhere the subject of *māšal* (see the Notes). Samuel will hear the same idea
from the other side when Israel asks for a king: *they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me
as the king over them* (1 Samuel 8:7, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested). See
[[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]].

Joseph's brothers asked the same question in the same verb: *Will you indeed reign over us? Or will
you indeed have dominion over us?* (Genesis 37:8), *māšôl timšōl bānû* (WLC). There it was a sneer at a
dream. Here it is an offer, and the man refuses it. The refusal is real. Whether Gideon keeps to it is
what the rest of the pericope tests.

**The ephod (8:24–27).**

> **8:24** Gideon said to them, "I do have a request, that you would each give me the earrings of his plunder." (For they had golden earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.)

> **8:25** They answered, "We will willingly give them." They spread a garment, and every man threw the earrings of his plunder into it.

The refusal is followed at once by a request. *I do have a request* is *ʾešʾălâ mikkem šəʾēlâ*, *let me
ask of you an asking*. What he asks for is gold, *the earrings of his plunder*, one from each man. The
narrator explains why there was so much of it: *because they were Ishmaelites*. The enemy has been
called Midian throughout. Calling them Ishmaelites here is what Genesis 37 does in the sale of Joseph,
where the same caravan is *Ishmaelites* (37:25, 27, 28) and *Midianites* (37:28, 36). Judges 8:24 is the
verse most often cited to harmonize that problem. See
[[Ishmaelites or Midianites - The Caravan That Bought Joseph]].

*We will willingly give them* is *nātôn nittēn*, an emphatic doubling, *we will surely give*. They
spread a *garment*, *śimlâ*, and *threw* in the earrings. *Threw* is *wayyašlîkû*, from *šālak*. Aaron
used that verb in his account of the calf: *they gave it to me; and I threw it into the fire, and out
came this calf* (Exodus 32:24) (see the Notes).

> **8:26** The weight of the golden earrings that he requested was one thousand and seven hundred shekels of gold, besides the crescents, and the pendants, and the purple clothing that was on the kings of Midian, and besides the chains that were about their camels' necks.

The narrator weighs the gold. *Shekels* is WEB's word; the Hebrew says only *a thousand and seven
hundred of gold* (see the Notes). For scale, the servant's gift to Rebekah was *a golden ring of half a
shekel weight* (Genesis 24:22). *Golden earrings*, *nizmê hazzāhāb*, is the phrase of Aaron's command:
*Take off the golden rings, which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters,
and bring them to me* (Exodus 32:2). In the WLC of Genesis–Judges *nezem* with *zāhāb* occurs only in
Genesis 24:22, Exodus 32:2, 3, and here in 8:24, 26 (see the Notes). See
[[Golden Earrings - Shechem's Oak, Aaron's Calf, and Gideon's Ephod]].

The list goes on: the *crescents* Gideon took from the camels in 8:21, *pendants*, *purple clothing*
from the kings, and the camels' *chains*. *Pendants* and *chains* occur nowhere else in Genesis–Judges
(see the Notes). *Purple*, *ʾargāmān*, is otherwise in those books a tabernacle word, the purple of
the curtains and of the high priest's ephod (Exodus 28:6). The verse says these things were *besides*
the weight. It does not say they went into the ephod.

> **8:27** Gideon made an ephod out of it, and put it in Ophrah, his city. Then all Israel played the prostitute with it there; and it became a snare to Gideon, and to his house.

*Gideon made an ephod out of it*, *wayyaʿaś ʾôtô gidʿôn ləʾēpôd*. An *ephod*, *ʾēpôd*, in Genesis–Judges
is first the high priest's garment of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet (Exodus 28:6), worn for inquiry,
with the breastplate (28:15–30). Every occurrence of the word in the WLC of Exodus and Leviticus is the
high priest's ephod. After this verse it occurs again only in Micah's shrine, *an ephod, and teraphim*
(17:5, see [[Micah's Idol and Levite]]; 18:14–20, stored WEB, not yet ingested) (see the Notes). What Gideon's ephod looked like, and
whether it was worn or set up as an object, the text does not say. Seventeen hundred of gold is a great
deal for a garment. The wiki does not decide between the readings.

He *put it in Ophrah, his city*. *Put*, *wayyaṣṣēg*, is the verb he used of the fleece, *I will put a
fleece of wool on the threshing floor* (6:37), and the verb of Yahweh's *you shall set him by himself*
at the water (7:5). Ophrah is where he threw down his father's altar to Baal and built the altar he
named *Yahweh is Peace*, still standing *to this day* (6:24–28). Now the same town holds an ephod, and
*all Israel played the prostitute with it there*. *Played the prostitute … with it* is *wayyiznû …
ʾaḥărāyw*, *went whoring after it*. The phrase is the book's word for Israel's apostasy: *they
prostituted themselves to other gods* (2:17). It returns six verses later for the Baals (8:33).

*It became a snare*, *môqēš*. That word is the Torah's warning about the land's gods: *if you serve
their gods, it will surely be a snare to you* (Exodus 23:33); *you shall not serve their gods; for that
would be a snare to you* (Deuteronomy 7:16). The angel at Bochim said it in Judges: *their gods will be
a snare to you* (2:3). In Genesis–Judges *môqēš* occurs seven times, and this is the only one where the
snare is an object an Israelite made (see the Notes). The snare is *to Gideon, and to his
house*. The house is named because the next chapter destroys it. See
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].

The ephod is the answer to 8:23. Gideon refused to rule, and then made an object that drew *all
Israel* to his city. The narrator does not say he meant that. It says what happened. See
[[They Turned Aside Quickly - The Golden Calf and the Cycle of Judges]] and
[[Gold for the Calf and Gold for the Sanctuary]].

**The closing formula (8:28–32).**

> **8:28** So Midian was subdued before the children of Israel, and they lifted up their heads no more. The land had rest forty years in the days of Gideon.

This is the book's closing formula for a deliverance. *Subdued*, *wayyikkānaʿ*, is the word for Moab
after Ehud (3:30) and for Jabin after Barak (4:23). *The land had rest*, *wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ*, is the
verb of Othniel's forty years (3:11), Ehud's eighty (3:30), and the forty after the Song (5:31). It is
the last time in Judges that the land has rest. *Šāqaṭ* returns in the book only for Laish, *quiet and
secure* before Dan destroys it (18:7, 27, stored WEB) (see the Notes). *They lifted up their heads no
more* is new; it is said of no earlier enemy.

> **8:29** Jerubbaal the son of Joash went and lived in his own house.

> **8:30** Gideon had seventy sons conceived from his body, for he had many wives.

> **8:31** His concubine who was in Shechem also bore him a son, and he named him Abimelech.

The name changes. The man who *lived in his own house* is *Jerubbaal*, the name Joash gave him for the
altar, *Let Baal contend against him* (6:32). In 8:29–35 the narrator uses both names. The next
chapter never calls him Gideon; it says *Jerubbaal* eight times (see the Notes).

*Seventy sons conceived from his body* is *yōṣəʾê yərēkô*, *going out of his thigh*. The phrase is used
of Jacob's household going down to Egypt (Genesis 46:26) and of *All the souls who came out of Jacob's
body were seventy souls* (Exodus 1:5). In the WLC of Genesis–Judges it occurs only in those two verses
and here (see the Notes). Gideon's household is the size of Jacob's. *For he had many wives*, *nāšîm
rabbôt*. Deuteronomy's law for the king says *He shall not multiply wives to himself … He shall not
greatly multiply to himself silver and gold* (17:17). Judges does not cite it. Gideon, who would not be
king, has the wives and the gold the law withholds from a king. See [[The Law for the King]] and
[[A King Under Instruction - Proverbs 31.1-5 and Deuteronomy 17.17-20]].

Then one more son, from *his concubine who was in Shechem*. *Concubine*, *pîlegeš*, is the word for
Abraham's concubines and for Bilhah in Genesis (25:6; 35:22) and for the Levite's concubine whose death ends the book
(19:1–30, stored WEB; not yet ingested). She lives in [[Shechem (city)]], not in Ophrah. *He named him
Abimelech*, *wayyāśem ʾet-šəmô*, *he set his name*. *Abimelech* is the name of the Philistine kings of
Gerar in Genesis 20–26 (see [[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]] and [[Abimelech (of Isaac's time)]]). It is
usually glossed *my father is king* (reported, not checked). A man who has just said *I will not rule
over you* gives his son that name. The text does not explain it. The kings of Midian had called
Gideon's brothers *the children of a king* (8:18). See [[Abimelech (son of Gideon)]].

> **8:32** Gideon the son of Joash died in a good old age, and was buried in the tomb of Joash his father, in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

*In a good old age*, *bəśêbâ ṭôbâ*. In the WLC of Genesis–Judges the phrase occurs only three times:
the promise to Abram, *You will be buried at a good old age* (Genesis 15:15), Abraham's death (25:8), and
Gideon's (see the Notes). No other judge is given it. *Ophrah of the Abiezrites* is the phrase of the
altar *Yahweh is Peace* (6:24). Gideon is buried in the town where he was called, near the altar he
built and the ephod he made. See
[[Buried in a Good Old Age - The Promise at Genesis 15.15 and Its Fulfilment]].

**After Gideon (8:33–35).**

> **8:33** As soon as Gideon was dead, the children of Israel turned again, and played the prostitute following the Baals, and made Baal Berith their god.

*As soon as Gideon was dead* is the pattern of 2:19: *when the judge was dead, they turned back*. *Turned
again* is *wayyāšûbû*, the verb of 2:19. *Played the prostitute following the Baals* is the phrase of
8:27 with a new object. Gideon's first act was to throw down Baal's altar (6:25–32). After his death
Israel goes back to the Baals.

*And made Baal Berith their god*. *Made* is *wayyāśîmû*, the verb of *he named* in 8:31. Gideon *set*
the name Abimelech on his son, and Israel *sets* Baal Berith as its god. *Baal Berith* means *lord of
the covenant* (the meaning of *bərît* is not in doubt; whether the deity's title was understood that
way is the usual explanation, not checked). It occurs only here and in 9:4, where the money that pays
Abimelech's men comes *out of the house of Baal Berith*, at Shechem (stored WEB; not yet ingested). The
book will also call it *the house of Elberith* (9:46). Shechem is where Joshua *made a covenant with the
people* (Joshua 24:25). See [[Baal Berith]] and
[[The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem]].

> **8:34** The children of Israel didn't remember Yahweh their God, who had delivered them out of the hand of all their enemies on every side;

> **8:35** neither did they show kindness to the house of Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon, according to all the goodness which he had shown to Israel.

Two failures to remember. The first is the one Deuteronomy warned of: *But you shall remember Yahweh
your God, for it is he who gives you power to get wealth* (8:18). *Didn't remember*, *lōʾ zākərû*, is
the first use of *zākar* in Judges (see the Notes). *Who had delivered them* is *hammaṣṣîl*, from
*nāṣal*, a different verb from the *you have saved us* of 8:22. Israel had given the saving to Gideon.
The narrator gives it back to Yahweh.

The second failure is new. *Neither did they show kindness*, *ḥesed*, *to the house of Jerubbaal
Gideon*. WEB supplies *that is*; the Hebrew sets the two names side by side (see the Notes). *Ḥesed*
occurs in Judges only twice, here and in the spies' promise to the man of Bethel, *we will deal kindly
with you* (1:24). Rahab asked for the same: *you also will deal kindly with my father's house* (Joshua
2:12). Rahab's house was spared for the kindness she had shown. Gideon's house will not be. Jotham will put the
same question to Shechem: *if you have dealt well with Jerubbaal and his house* (9:16, stored WEB; not
yet ingested).

**How the pericope sits in Gideon's story.** Gideon's account began with Israel crying to Yahweh and a
prophet reminding them who brought them out of Egypt (6:7–10). It ends with Israel not remembering
Yahweh at all (8:34). In between, Gideon threw down one altar and set up an ephod. He refused a crown in
words that state Yahweh's kingship more plainly than any earlier verse, then took the gold, the wives,
and the household of a king, and named a son *my father is king*. The narrator gives no verdict on him.
It gives him the patriarch's *good old age* and the judge's forty years, and it also records the snare.
Both are on the page, and the wiki keeps both. Gideon is the last judge in Judges after whom the land
has rest.

## Notes

**"Rule over us."** *Məšol-bānû* (8:22, WLC), *māšal* (4910). The lemma occurs three times in 8:23,
*lōʾ-ʾemšōl … wəlōʾ-yimšōl … YHWH yimšōl* (WLC). Neither *melek* (4428) nor *mālak* (4427) occurs in 8:22–23
(WLC). *Māšal* in the WLC of Genesis–Judges: Genesis 1:18; 3:16; 4:7; 24:2; 37:8 (twice); 45:8, 26;
Exodus 21:8; Deuteronomy 15:6 (twice); Joshua 12:2, 5; Judges 8:22, 23 (three times); 9:2 (twice); 14:4;
15:11. In none of those but 8:23 is Yahweh the subject. Genesis 37:8 has both verbs, *hămālōk timlōk …
ʾim-māšôl timšōl bānû* (WLC). The search covered Genesis through Judges, Leviticus included.

**"You have saved us."** *Hôšaʿtānû* (8:22, WLC), *yāšaʿ* (3467). In Judges 6–8 the verb occurs at 6:14,
15, 31, 36, 37; 7:2, 7; and 8:22 (WLC).

**"I do have a request."** *ʾEšʾălâ mikkem šəʾēlâ* (8:24, WLC), *šāʾal* (7592) with its noun *šəʾēlâ*
(7596). *Ishmaelites*, *yišməʿēʾlîm* (3459), occurs in Genesis–Judges only at Genesis 37:25, 27, 28;
39:1; and Judges 8:24 (WLC).

**"We will willingly give them."** *Nātôn nittēn* (8:25, WLC), infinitive absolute with the finite
verb. *Śimlâ* (8071) with *pāraś* (6566) within three words occurs in Genesis–Judges only at Deuteronomy
22:17 and Judges 8:25 (WLC). *Wayyašlîkû* (8:25) and *wāʾašlikēhû* (Exodus 32:24) are both hiphil of
*šālak* (7993) (WLC).

**"One thousand and seven hundred."** *ʾElep ûšəbaʿ mēʾôt zāhāb* (8:26, WLC). No word for *shekel*
(8255) occurs in the verse; WEB supplies it, as most translations do. *Nizmê hazzāhāb*, *nezem* (5141)
immediately followed by *zāhāb* (2091), occurs in Genesis–Judges only at Genesis 24:22 (singular,
*nezem zāhāb*), Exodus 32:2, 3, and Judges 8:24 (*nizmê zāhāb*), 26 (WLC). *Nezem* alone also occurs at
Genesis 24:30, 47; 35:4; Exodus 35:22; Judges 8:25. *Nəṭîpôt* (5188), *pendants*, and *ʿănāqôt* (6060),
*chains*, occur in Genesis–Judges only in 8:26 (WLC). *ʾArgāmān* (713), *purple*, occurs in Exodus
25–39 (the tabernacle and priestly garments), Numbers 4:13, and Judges 8:26 (WLC). *ləbad min*,
*besides*, governs both lists.

**"An ephod."** *Ləʾēpôd* (8:27, WLC), lemma 646. In Genesis–Judges: Exodus 25:7; 28:4, 6, 12, 15, 25–28,
31; 29:5; 35:9, 27; 39:2, 7, 8, 18–22; Leviticus 8:7; Judges 8:27; 17:5; 18:14, 17, 18, 20 (WLC). All the
Exodus and Leviticus occurrences belong to the high priest's vestment. *Wayyaṣṣēg*, hiphil of *yāṣag*
(3322); the same lemma is at 6:37 (*maṣṣîg*) and 7:5 (WLC).

**"Played the prostitute."** *Zānâ* (2181) followed within two words by *ʾaḥărê* (310) occurs in
Genesis–Judges at Exodus 34:15, 16; Leviticus 17:7; 20:5, 6; Numbers 15:39; Deuteronomy 31:16; Judges
2:17; 8:33 (WLC). In 8:27 the form is *wayyiznû kol-yiśrāʾēl ʾaḥărāyw*, with *kol-yiśrāʾēl* between
the verb and the preposition, so the two-word search does not find it; it is the same construction.

**"A snare."** *Ləmôqēš* (8:27, WLC). *Môqēš* (4170) in Genesis–Judges: Exodus 10:7; 23:33; 34:12;
Deuteronomy 7:16; Joshua 23:13; Judges 2:3; 8:27 (WLC). Exodus 10:7 is Pharaoh's servants calling Moses
a snare; the other five concern the nations or their gods.

**"Subdued … the land had rest."** *Wayyikkānaʿ* (8:28), *kānaʿ* (3665); in Genesis–Judges at Leviticus
26:41; Deuteronomy 9:3; Judges 3:30; 4:23; 8:28; 11:33 (WLC). *Wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ*, *šāqaṭ* (8252); in
Genesis–Judges at Joshua 11:23; 14:15; Judges 3:11, 30; 5:31; 8:28; 18:7, 27 (WLC). Judges 11:33 (Ammon
*subdued* under Jephthah) has *kānaʿ* without *šāqaṭ*.

**"Conceived from his body."** *Yōṣəʾê yərēkô* (8:30, WLC), *yāṣāʾ* (3318) immediately followed by
*yārēk* (3409). Only Genesis 46:26; Exodus 1:5; and Judges 8:30 in Genesis–Judges (WLC). *Nāšîm rabbôt*
(8:30) uses the adjective *rab* (7227); Deuteronomy 17:17's *lōʾ yarbeh-lô nāšîm* uses the verb *rābâ*
(7235) (WLC). The two passages share the nouns *nāšîm* and *zāhāb*, not the verb.

**"Concubine."** *Pîlegeš* (6370) in Genesis–Judges: Genesis 22:24; 25:6; 35:22; 36:12; Judges 8:31; 19:1–
29 (eight times); 20:4, 5, 6 (WLC).

**"He named him Abimelech."** *Wayyāśem ʾet-šəmô ʾăbîmelek* (8:31, WLC), *śîm* (7760). The same lemma is
*wayyāśîmû lāhem baʿal bərît lēʾlōhîm* in 8:33 (WLC). *ʾĂbîmelek* (40) occurs in Genesis 20; 21; 26
(the Philistine kings) and from Judges 8:31 to 10:1 (WLC). The gloss *my father is king* is the standard
one, not checked against a lexicon.

**"A good old age."** *Bəśêbâ ṭôbâ* (8:32, WLC). *Śêbâ* (7872) immediately followed by *ṭôb* (2896)
occurs in Genesis–Judges only at Genesis 15:15; 25:8; and Judges 8:32 (WLC).

**"Baal Berith."** *Baʿal bərît* (8:33, WLC), filed in the morphhb data as a single proper name (1170);
only Judges 8:33 and 9:4 in Genesis–Judges. *ʾĒl bərît* at 9:46 is not counted in that search.

**"Didn't remember."** *Wəlōʾ zākərû* (8:34, WLC), *zākar* (2142). In the WLC of Judges *zākar* occurs
only at 8:34; 9:2; and 16:28. *Hammaṣṣîl*, hiphil participle of *nāṣal* (5337). In Judges 9 *Gideon* (1439) does not occur;
*Jerubbaal* (3378) occurs in eight verses (WLC).

**"The house of Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon."** *ʿIm-bêt yərubbaʿal gidʿôn* (8:35, WLC); no word for
*that is*. *Ḥesed* (2617) in the WLC of Judges: 1:24 and 8:35 only. *Ḥesed* immediately followed by *ʿim*
occurs in Genesis–Judges at Genesis 24:12, 14 and Judges 8:35; Joshua 2:12 has *ʿim-bêt ʾābî ḥesed*, the
same words in another order (WLC).

**Judgment calls.** The boundary was kept as planned: 8:22 opens on a new subject, and 8:35 closes the
Gideon account before Abimelech's own story begins at 9:1. 8:28–35 could have been split off as a
separate *Death of Gideon* unit, but the ephod's *snare to … his house* (27) and the failed *kindness to
the house* (35) frame the same house, so the pericope stays whole. [[Abimelech (son of Gideon)]] and
[[Baal Berith]] get pages now, because both are named here and both carry chapter 9. Abimelech's page is
qualified from the start under the title-figure convention already applied to the Abimelechs of
Genesis. Gideon's concubine, the seventy sons as a group, and the Ishmaelites as a people get no pages;
the Ishmaelites are tracked on [[Ishmael]] and the existing connection. The form and use of the ephod
(worn garment or set-up object) are left open, as are the meanings of *Abimelech* and *Baal Berith*,
which are reported, not checked.
