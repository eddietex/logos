# Jael and Sisera

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 4:17–24

## Summary

The battle is over and its general is still alive. The pericope follows him on foot to a tent, and
there the word Deborah spoke at 4:9, *Yahweh will sell Sisera into a woman's hand*, is fulfilled by a
woman nobody has mentioned until now.

**The tent and the treaty (17).** *Sisera fled away on his feet to the tent of Jael the wife of Heber
the Kenite; for there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite*
(17). The fact set down at 4:11, that Heber had *pitched his tent* by Kedesh, now has its use. The
*for* gives Sisera's reason: he goes where his king has peace. The tent is called Jael's, not
Heber's. Heber never appears in person.

**The welcome (18–19).** *Jael went out to meet Sisera*, and invites him twice: *Turn in, my lord, turn
in to me; don't be afraid* (18). He comes in, *and she covered him with a rug*. He asks for *a little
water*; *she opened a bottle of milk, and gave him a drink, and covered him* (19). She gives more than
he asks and covers him a second time. Everything she does is what a host does. The narrator says
nothing about what she intends.

**His instruction (20).** *Stand in the door of the tent, and if any man comes and inquires of you, and
says, "Is there any man here?" you shall say, "No."* The general who gave orders to nine hundred
chariots now gives one to a woman at a tent door, and it is a lie for her to tell. The answer he
scripts, *No*, Hebrew *ʾāyin*, *there is none* (WLC), turns out to be true: by the time Barak comes,
there is no man in the tent, only a body.

**The killing (21).** *Then Jael Heber's wife took a tent peg, and took a hammer in her hand, and went
softly to him, and struck the pin into his temples, and it pierced through into the ground; for he was
in a deep sleep; so he fainted and died.* The tools are a tent-dweller's tools; in the Bedouin practice
often cited, pitching the tent is women's work, a point reported here and not verified. The verse
gives her four verbs in a row (*took … took … went … struck*), then his three (*was in a deep sleep …
fainted … died*). Her name and her husband's are repeated in full, *Jael Heber's wife*, at the moment
she breaks the house's peace with Jabin.

**Barak arrives (22).** *Behold, as Barak pursued Sisera, Jael came out to meet him* (22), *went out to
meet*, the same Hebrew as in 18. She says *Come, and I will show you the man whom you seek*. He goes in,
*and behold, Sisera lay dead, and the tent peg was in his temples*. Barak arrives second, as Deborah
said he would (4:9). The scene is built like the end of [[Ehud and Eglon]]: a man killed alone in a
closed room by someone he trusted, and others coming in to find him, *behold*, fallen dead. See
[[Fallen Down Dead - Eglon's Upper Room and Jael's Tent]].

**God subdued Jabin (23–24).** *So God subdued Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel
on that day* (23). The narrator credits God, not Jael and not Barak. The divine name changes: verses
1–15 used *Yahweh*; this verse uses *ʾĕlōhîm*, *God* (WLC). The verb *wayyaknaʿ*, *subdued*, is the
root of Moab's subjection at 3:30, there passive (*wattikkānaʿ*), here active with God as subject
(WLC). It sounds like *Kənaʿan*, *Canaan*, the word that ends the same clause; whether the narrator
intends the sound play is a reading, not something the text says. Then *the hand of the children of
Israel prevailed more and more against Jabin the king of Canaan, until they had destroyed Jabin king of
Canaan* (24). Jabin is named three times in the verse and does not act once. The story that began with
Israel *sold into the hand of Jabin* (4:2) ends with Jabin destroyed by Israel's *hand*. No rest-formula
follows here; *the land had rest forty years* is held back to the end of the Song (5:31).

**What the narrator does not say.** Judges 4 never judges Jael. It does not call her deceitful,
brave, faithful, or treacherous, and it does not say whether the peace of verse 17 bound her. The Song
calls her *blessed above women* (5:24, next pericope). Readers since have divided over a host who kills
her guest. The wiki records the facts the text gives on both sides: she offered the protection a host
offers (*turn in … don't be afraid*), and Sisera was the general of a king who had *mightily oppressed*
Israel for twenty years (4:3), under Yahweh's word that he would be sold into a woman's hand. See
[[Into a Woman's Hand - Judges 4.9, 4.21, and 9.53-54]] and [[Hospitality]].

## Themes

- [[Hospitality]] — the full forms of a host's welcome, and the guest killed under them (18–21).
- [[Deception]] — the welcome, the covering, and the milk; Sisera's own scripted lie (18–20).
- [[War]] — the general's death after the battle, and the war against Jabin carried to its end (21–24).
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — *a woman's hand* (4:9) fulfilled (21–22).
- [[Providence]] — the tent placed at 4:11, the peace of 4:17, and the narrator's *God subdued* (23).

## Notes

**"Turn in."** *Sûrâ ʾădōnî sûrâ ʾēlay* (18, WLC): the imperative twice, then *wayyāsar*, *he turned
in*. *ʾĂdōnî*, *my lord*, is the ordinary courtesy title. *Don't be afraid* (*ʾal-tîrāʾ*) is a phrase
that elsewhere in the books read is often on God's lips (Genesis 15:1; 26:24); here it is a host's.

**The rug.** *Śəmîkâ* (18, WLC). In the WLC files for Genesis, Exodus, Numbers, Joshua, and Judges,
lemma 8063 occurs only here. What kind of covering it was is not recoverable from those files; the
WEB's *rug* is a translation choice.

**The milk.** *Nōʾd heḥālāb*, *the skin-bottle of milk* (19, WLC). *Nōʾd* is the word for the
Gibeonites' *wineskins* (Joshua 9:4, 13) in the same files. The Song says *she gave him milk. She
brought him butter in a lordly dish* (5:25). Whether milk was meant to make him sleep, as is often
suggested, the text does not say; it says only that he asked for water and got more.

**"Stand."** *ʿĂmōd* (20, WLC). The OpenScriptures morphology parses it as a masculine singular
imperative (`HVqv2ms`), though Sisera is speaking to a woman; the following *wəʾāmart*, *you shall
say*, is feminine (`HVqq2fs`). Whether the masculine form is significant, an accepted variant, or
should be read as an infinitive absolute used as a command, the morphology alone does not settle;
not verified against a grammar.

**"Is there any man here?"** *Hăyēš-pōh ʾîš … ʾāyin* (20, WLC). Sisera calls himself *ʾîš*, *a man*,
and tells her to say there is none. Barak in 22 is shown *hāʾîš ʾăšer ʾattâ məbaqqēš*, *the man whom
you seek*, dead.

**The tools.** *Yətad hāʾōhel*, *the peg of the tent*, and *hammaqqebet*, *the hammer* (21, WLC).
*Yātēd* is the word for the tabernacle's pins (Exodus 27:19; 35:18; 38:20, 31; 39:40) and the court's
pins carried by the Merarites (Numbers 3:37; 4:32) in the WLC files read. *Maqqebet* occurs only here
in those files (lemma 4718); the Song uses a different phrase, *the workmen's hammer* (5:26).

**"Struck."** *Wattitqaʿ* (21, WLC), from *tāqaʿ*, the verb for driving in or thrusting and for
blowing a trumpet. In the WLC files read, Ehud *thrust* his sword into Eglon with it (*wayyitqāʿehā*,
3:21) and then *blew* the trumpet with it (3:27). And in Judges 16:14 Delilah *wattitqaʿ bayyātēd*,
*fastened it with the pin* (stored WEB; WLC): the same verb, the same noun, a woman, a sleeping enemy of
Israel's, inside a house. Here the pin kills; there Samson wakes and pulls it out.

**"Pierced through into the ground."** *Wattiṣnaḥ bāʾāreṣ* (21, WLC). In the WLC files read, *ṣānaḥ*
occurs only three times: here, and of Achsah *getting off* her donkey (Joshua 15:18; Judges 1:14). The
subject here is grammatically feminine (`HC/Vqw3fs`); WEB takes it as the peg, *it pierced through*.
What the verb means exactly is not clear from three occurrences; the WEB sense is followed.

**"Deep sleep … fainted."** *Wəhûʾ nirdām wayyāʿap wayyāmōt* (21, WLC). *Nirdām* is a niphal participle
of *rādam*, *to be in heavy sleep*; the related noun *tardēmâ* is the *deep sleep* Yahweh caused on Adam
(Genesis 2:21) and on Abram (15:12), a relation reported here and not checked against a lexicon.
*Wayyāʿap* is assigned by OpenScriptures to lemma 5774, the root whose only other occurrence in the
files read is *let birds fly* (Genesis 1:20). Whether it means *he fainted*, *he was exhausted*, or
something else here is disputed; WEB's *fainted* is followed and the alternatives are not verified.

**The Song's version.** Judges 5:26–27 tells the killing as poetry: *With the hammer she struck Sisera.
She struck through his head … At her feet he bowed, he fell, he lay* (stored WEB). Read alone, the
Song does not mention sleep or the tent, and its bowing and falling *at her feet* (*bên raglêhā*, WLC)
have been read as a man struck while standing. Judges 4:21 says he was in a deep sleep. **Recorded, not
resolved:** whether the two accounts differ or the poetry compresses the same scene is for the next
pericope's ingest to weigh. Note also *on his feet* (*bəraglāyw*, 17, WLC) and *at her feet*
(*bên raglêhā*, 5:27): the same noun.

**"God subdued."** *Wayyaknaʿ ʾĕlōhîm* (23, WLC). The root *kānaʿ* in Judges, in the files read:
3:30 (Moab), 8:28 (Midian), 11:33 (Ammon), each a niphal, *was subdued*; 4:23 is the only hiphil, *made
to be subdued*, and the only one with God as subject. The niphal pattern (3:30; 8:28) is followed by a
land-rest formula; 4:23 is not, until 5:31.

**Jael's name.** *Yāʿēl* is also the ordinary word for *mountain goat*; that is reported, not
verified, and nothing in the text plays on it.
