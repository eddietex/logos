# Deborah and Barak

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 4:1–16

## Summary

The third cycle opens on the formula and then hands the story to a woman who is already judging
before the crisis begins. Othniel and Ehud were raised up after Israel cried. Deborah is not raised
up at all; she is introduced sitting under a tree, and Israel comes to her.

**Sin and oppression (1–3).** *The children of Israel again did that which was evil in Yahweh's
sight, when Ehud was dead* (1). The clock runs from Ehud, not from Shamgar (3:31). *Yahweh sold them
into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor* (2), and his captain is Sisera, *who
lived in Harosheth of the Gentiles*. The name *Jabin king of Hazor* is the name of the king Joshua
killed when he burned Hazor (Joshua 11:1, 10–13); the text does not explain the second Jabin (see
Notes). Israel cries, *for he had nine hundred chariots of iron; and he mightily oppressed the
children of Israel for twenty years* (3). The chariots that stopped Judah in the valley (1:19) are
now named as the reason for the cry. Twenty years is longer than Cushan's eight or Eglon's eighteen.

**Deborah (4–5).** *Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, judged Israel at that time*
(4). Every earlier judge was *raised up* in answer to a cry; Deborah is already judging. She sits
*under Deborah's palm tree between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim*, and *the
children of Israel came up to her for judgment* (5). This is the only place in Judges where *judge*
means what Moses and the elders did at Exodus 18 and Deuteronomy 1: people bring cases to her. She
is also the first prophet in the book, and the first woman called *prophetess* since Miriam (Exodus
15:20). See [[The Prophetess - Miriam and Deborah]].

**The command to Barak (6–7).** She summons *Barak the son of Abinoam out of Kedesh Naphtali* and
puts Yahweh's command to him as a question: *Hasn't Yahweh, the God of Israel, commanded, "Go and
lead the way to Mount Tabor, and take with you ten thousand men of the children of Naphtali and of
the children of Zebulun?"* (6). The quoted command goes on in Yahweh's voice: *I will draw to you,
to the river Kishon, Sisera, … and I will deliver him into your hand* (7). The Hebrew uses one verb
for both halves (*māšak*, WLC): Barak is to *draw* toward Tabor, and Yahweh will *draw* Sisera to
the Kishon. The *Hasn't …* suggests the command had already been given and not obeyed.

**Barak's condition and the woman's hand (8–9).** *If you will go with me, then I will go; but if you
will not go with me, I will not go* (8). Deborah agrees: *I will surely go with you. Nevertheless,
the journey that you take won't be for your honor; for Yahweh will sell Sisera into a woman's hand*
(9). *Sell* is the verb of verse 2, where Yahweh sold Israel to Jabin. The sentence does not name
the woman. A reader at this point will think of Deborah; the next pericope gives the honor to Jael
(4:17–22).

**The muster and Heber (10–11).** Barak calls Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh, and *ten thousand men
followed him; and Deborah went up with him* (10). Then the story stops for a fact: *Heber the Kenite
had separated himself from the Kenites, even from the children of Hobab, Moses' brother-in-law, and
had pitched his tent as far as the oak in Zaanannim, which is by Kedesh* (11). Nothing happens with
it yet. It puts a tent near the battlefield before the battle starts; 4:17 adds that this house was
at peace with Jabin.

**Sisera's muster (12–13).** Told that Barak has gone up Tabor, Sisera gathers *all his chariots,
even nine hundred chariots of iron*, from Harosheth to the Kishon (13). The Hebrew uses the same verb
for both musters, *wayyazʿēq*, *called out* (10, 13, WLC). Yahweh's *I will draw* (7) is happening,
and Sisera thinks it is his own decision.

**The battle (14–16).** Deborah speaks once more: *Go; for this is the day in which Yahweh has
delivered Sisera into your hand. Hasn't Yahweh gone out before you?* (14). Barak comes down with his
ten thousand. *Yahweh confused Sisera, all his chariots, and all his army, with the edge of the sword
before Barak* (15). The verb is *hāmam* (WLC), the verb of the Egyptian army at the sea (Exodus
14:24) and of the kings at Gibeon (Joshua 10:10); see
[[Yahweh Confused Them - Hamam in the Wars of Yahweh]]. *Sisera abandoned his chariot and fled away on his feet* (15). Barak chases the chariots to
Harosheth, and *all the army of Sisera fell by the edge of the sword. There was not a man left* (16).
The Hebrew of that last clause, *lōʾ nišʾar ʿad-ʾeḥād* (WLC), is the clause of Exodus 14:28 at the
sea, *There remained not so much as one of them*, minus *bāhem*. The chariot army ends the way
Pharaoh's did. The general does not: he leaves on foot, and the pericope ends with him running.

**Who acts.** Yahweh sells (2), commands (6), draws (7), will sell again (9), delivers (14), goes out
(14), and confuses (15). Barak goes up, goes down, and pursues. Deborah judges, summons, speaks for
Yahweh, and goes. No one in this pericope is said to *save*, and no Spirit comes on anyone. The
chariots of iron, the obstacle of 1:19 and Joshua 17:16–18, are defeated without the story explaining
how, except by the verb *confused*. The Song in chapter 5 will add rain and a river (5:4, 21).

## Key verses

> **4:2** Yahweh sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor; the captain of whose army was Sisera, who lived in Harosheth of the Gentiles.

> **4:3** The children of Israel cried to Yahweh, for he had nine hundred chariots of iron; and he mightily oppressed the children of Israel for twenty years.

> **4:4** Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, judged Israel at that time.

> **4:5** She lived under Deborah’s palm tree between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim; and the children of Israel came up to her for judgment.

> **4:8** Barak said to her, “If you will go with me, then I will go; but if you will not go with me, I will not go.”

> **4:9** She said, “I will surely go with you. Nevertheless, the journey that you take won’t be for your honor; for Yahweh will sell Sisera into a woman’s hand.” Deborah arose, and went with Barak to Kedesh.

> **4:14** Deborah said to Barak, “Go; for this is the day in which Yahweh has delivered Sisera into your hand. Hasn’t Yahweh gone out before you?” So Barak went down from Mount Tabor, and ten thousand men after him.

> **4:15** Yahweh confused Sisera, all his chariots, and all his army, with the edge of the sword before Barak. Sisera abandoned his chariot and fled away on his feet.

## Notes

**"A prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth."** *ʾIššâ nəbîʾâ ʾēšet lappîdôt* (4, WLC). The phrase
begins *a woman*, which the WEB leaves out because *prophetess* already says it. *Nəbîʾâ* occurs in
the WLC files for Genesis through Judges and 1 Samuel only here and at Exodus 15:20 (Miriam), by lemma
search. *Lappîdôt* looks like the plural of *lappîd*, *torch*; it is often read that way, either as
a name or as a description (*woman of torches*). That reading is reported, not checked against any
lexicon, and the WEB takes it as a husband's name. The lemma occurs only here in those files.

**"Judged Israel."** *Hîʾ šōpṭâ ʾet-yiśrāʾēl bāʿēt hahîʾ* (4, WLC), a participle: she *was judging*.
Othniel's *wayyišpōṭ* (3:10) is a narrative verb after the Spirit comes. Verse 5 says *the children
of Israel came up to her lammišpāṭ*, *for judgment*. The other judges in the book are deliverers in
war; Deborah is the one who hears cases, and she does not fight.

**The palm.** *Tōmer dəbôrâ* (5, WLC). *Tōmer* is a rare form; the lemma search finds it only here in
the WLC files read, where the ordinary word for palm is *tāmār*. The tree is named after her, as the
oak below Bethel was named for the other Deborah's burial (Genesis 35:8). The two trees stand near the
same town. The text does not connect the two women; see [[Deborah's Palm Tree]].

**Barak.** *Bārāq* is also the ordinary noun *lightning*; the name is usually read that way. That is
reported, not verified from a lexicon. In 1 Samuel 7:10, the other place the WLC files read pair
*hāmam* with Yahweh's victory over a named enemy in narrative, the confusion comes with thunder,
*wayyarʿēm YHWH bəqôl-gādôl*. Judges 4 does not mention weather; 5:4 and 5:20–21 do.

**"Draw."** *Ûmāšaktā bəhar tābôr … ûmāšaktî ʾēleykā* (6–7, WLC). WEB renders the first *lead the
way* and the second *draw*. The same verb is used of drawing Joseph out of the pit (Genesis 37:28) and
of the long blast on the ram's horn (Joshua 6:5) in the WLC files read. The wiki takes the repetition
as deliberate: Barak's action and Yahweh's are one verb.

**"Your honor."** *Lōʾ tihyeh tipʾartəkā* (9, WLC). In the WLC files read, *tipʾeret* is
the *beauty* of Aaron's garments, made *for glory and for beauty* (Exodus 28:2, 40), and the *honor*
in which Yahweh will set Israel *high above all nations* (Deuteronomy 26:19). The WEB's *honor* is a
fair sense.

**"Called."** Israel *cried* in 4:3 with *ṣāʿaq*. Barak and Sisera *called out* their troops with the
hiphil of *zāʿaq* (10, 13). The two roots are close in sound and sense; the WLC lemma data gives them
different numbers.

**The second Jabin.** Joshua 11 tells that Joshua *took Hazor, and struck its king with the sword*
(11:10) and *burned* it (11:13), and the king summoning the coalition is *Jabin king of Hazor* (11:1).
Judges 4:2 has *Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor*. Read in sequence, Hazor was rebuilt and
had a king again, and either the name recurred, or *Jabin* was a dynastic name, or the two traditions
tell of one king. The text supplies none of these. The wiki keeps two pages under the narrative-keyed
rule, [[Jabin (of Joshua's time)]] and [[Jabin (of Deborah's time)]], and does not identify them.
1 Samuel 12:9 (WEB, checked) remembers *Sisera, captain of the army of Hazor*, without naming Jabin.

**Harosheth.** *Ḥărōšet haggôyim* (2, 13, 16, WLC), *Harosheth of the nations*. *Gôyim* is the word
for *nations*. The place occurs only in this chapter in the WLC files read and is not located by the
text.

**Kedesh.** Verse 6 calls it *qedeš naptālî*, *Kedesh of Naphtali*. The wiki files it on [[Kedesh]],
whose page already gathers Naphtali's Kedesh (Joshua 19:37; 20:7). Verse 11 places the oak in
Zaanannim *by Kedesh*. Joshua 19:33 put the same oak, *ʾēlôn bəṣaʿănannîm* in both texts (WLC), on
Naphtali's border.

**Hobab.** *Mibbənê ḥōbāb ḥōtēn mōšeh* (11, WLC). WEB gives *brother-in-law*. The same *ḥōtēn* is
*father-in-law* in WEB's Numbers 10:29 and Exodus 3:1. The wiki has the discussion on [[Hobab]] and
on [[Hobab and the Kenites - Numbers 10, Judges 1 and 4, and 1 Samuel 15]].

**"Hasn't Yahweh gone out before you?"** *Hălōʾ YHWH yāṣāʾ ləpāneykā* (14, WLC). Deuteronomy 20:4 had
told Israel *Yahweh your God is he who goes with you, to fight for you* (stored WEB). Deborah's
question uses a different verb, *yāṣāʾ*, the verb of a king or army *going out* to war.

**Lappidoth and Abinoam get no pages.** Each is named only as a relation, as Kenaz, Gera, and Anath
were. Recorded in the log.
