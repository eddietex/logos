# The Dream of the Barley Loaf

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 7:9–15

## Summary

The army is down to three hundred, and *the camp of Midian was beneath him in the valley* (7:8). The
next scene happens the same night.

> **7:9** That same night, Yahweh said to him, “Arise, go down into the camp; for I have delivered it into your hand.

**The command, and a sign offered before it is asked for (7:9–11).** *That same night* is how the altar
scene began (6:25), and the fleece ended *that night* (6:40). Gideon's story keeps happening in the dark.
The command is plain: *Arise, go down*. The promise is in the perfect tense, *I have delivered it*. The
battle has not started, and the matter is already settled. Then Yahweh adds a condition:

> **7:10** But if you are afraid to go down, go with Purah your servant down to the camp.

> **7:11** You will hear what they say; and afterward your hands will be strengthened to go down into the camp.” Then went he down with Purah his servant to the outermost part of the armed men who were in the camp.

This time Gideon does not ask for a sign. Yahweh offers one first. *If you are afraid* uses the same
Hebrew word as the proclamation of 7:3, *whoever is fearful* (see the Notes). Twenty-two thousand men
went home under that word. Their commander is not sent home. He is given a way to go down afraid. The
text then says *Then went he down with Purah his servant*. It never says Gideon was afraid. But he takes
the option Yahweh gave only to a man who was afraid, so the reader can work it out. The narrator leaves
it there.

The sign is something to hear, not something to see. *You will hear what they say*. The fire from the
rock (6:21) and the fleece (6:36–40) were things done in front of Gideon. This one is a conversation
among the enemy. And the promised result is about Gideon's hands: *your hands will be strengthened*. The
verb is *ḥāzaq*, the *Be strong* Yahweh said to Joshua (Joshua 1:9). It is also the verb of 7:8, where
Gideon *retained* the three hundred. *Hand* runs through the whole pericope. The camp is given *into
your hand* (9). *Your hands* will be strengthened (11). The Midianite says *into his hand* (14). Gideon
tells his men *into your hand* (15). That is the word Yahweh had said Israel must not boast in: *My own
hand has saved me* (7:2).

Gideon and Purah reach *the outermost part of the armed men* (11). The word is used of Israel's tribes
going *armed* across the Jordan in Joshua (see the Notes). Here it describes the enemy's front line.

**What Gideon sees (7:12).**

> **7:12** The Midianites and the Amalekites and all the children of the east lay along in the valley like locusts for multitude; and their camels were without number, as the sand which is on the seashore for multitude.

This is what three hundred men are up against. The narrator uses two comparisons. *Like locusts for
multitude* and *without number* repeat the raids of 6:5. *As the sand which is on the seashore for
multitude* is almost word for word the Canaanite coalition at the waters of Merom: *many people, even as
the sand that is on the seashore in multitude* (Joshua 11:4). Joshua defeated that army. Yahweh used the
same image for Abraham's offspring (Genesis 22:17). In Judges it describes Israel's enemy and its
camels. See [[Like Locusts for Multitude - Exodus 10, Deuteronomy 28, and the Midianite Raids]] and
[[The Northern Kings at the Waters of Merom]]. The army *lay along* in the valley. The Hebrew is
*nōpəlîm*, *falling* or *lying*, the verb the dream will use twice in the next verse for the tent that
*fell*.

**The dream (7:13).**

> **7:13** When Gideon had come, behold, there was a man telling a dream to his fellow. He said, “Behold, I dreamed a dream; and behold, a cake of barley bread tumbled into the camp of Midian, and came to the tent, and struck it so that it fell, and turned it upside down, so that the tent lay flat.”

Gideon arrives at exactly the right moment. The narrator's *behold* puts the reader at the tent beside
him. The timing is not explained, and it is not called a coincidence. It is what Yahweh said would happen:
*You will hear what they say*.

This is the first dream told in Scripture since Pharaoh's dreams in Genesis 41. Between them the
noun *dream* comes up only where Yahweh says he speaks to a prophet *in a dream* (Numbers 12:6) and where
the law warns against a *dreamer of dreams* who leads Israel to other gods (Deuteronomy 13:1–5, WEB
numbering) (see the Notes). In Genesis the dreamers were often outsiders, Abimelech, Laban, Pharaoh's
officers, and Pharaoh. Joseph the Israelite interpreted their dreams. Here an outsider dreams, and
another outsider interprets. Israel's general only listens. The man's words, *Behold, I dreamed a
dream*, are the words of Joseph's second dream, *Behold, I have dreamed yet another dream* (Genesis 37:9),
also introduced by the verb *told*. Judges does not point to Genesis. See
[[This Dreamer Comes - Joseph's Dreams and Their Fulfilment]].

The dream itself is a small, strange picture. A *cake of barley bread*, a round loaf, comes *tumbling*
into the camp. It hits *the tent*, and the tent falls, turns over, and lies flat. The word for *cake*
appears nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible, and the written and read forms of it differ (see the Notes).
The dream does not say it is a battle. It describes the most ordinary food knocking over the largest
thing in sight. One common reading takes the barley loaf as the settled farmer, Israel, whose harvest
Midian came to eat (6:3–4), and the tent as the nomad, Midian, who came *with their livestock and their
tents* (6:5). That fits the story, but it is a reading. The dream does not explain itself.

**The interpretation (7:14).**

> **7:14** His fellow answered, “This is nothing other than the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a man of Israel. God has delivered Midian into his hand, with all the army.”

The second man interprets at once, with no hesitation. A barley loaf becomes *the sword of Gideon*. The
enemy knows Gideon's name and his father's name. He knows them before any battle, and he says the
outcome in the same perfect tense Yahweh used: *God has delivered*. He calls Gideon only *a man of
Israel*. The commander who called his family *the poorest in Manasseh* (6:15) is a famous name in
Midian's camp. The interpreter says *God*, *hāʾĕlōhîm*, not Yahweh. When Gideon repeats it in the next
verse he says *Yahweh*. The interpreter's phrase *the sword of Gideon* is heard again in 7:20, in
the shout *The sword of Yahweh and of Gideon!* ([[The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon]]). The army will add the
name the Midianite left out.

The scene is like the spies at Jericho. Joshua's two men heard the enemy's fear from Rahab: *the fear of
you has fallen upon us, and … all the inhabitants of the land melt away before you* (Joshua 2:9). They
went back and said *Truly Yahweh has delivered all the land into our hands* (Joshua 2:24). Gideon hears
the same kind of thing from the enemy, and goes back and says nearly the same sentence. See
[[Spies Who Hear the Enemy's Fear - Joshua 2 and Judges 7]].

**Worship, and the order (7:15).**

> **7:15** It was so, when Gideon heard the telling of the dream, and its interpretation, that he worshiped. Then he returned into the camp of Israel, and said, “Arise; for Yahweh has delivered the army of Midian into your hand!”

Before he does anything else, Gideon worships. Apart from this verse, every use of the verb *worship* in
Judges is Israel bowing down to other gods (2:12, 17, 19). This is the first time in the book that
anyone worships Yahweh with it (see the Notes). The text does not say where he bowed or what he said. It
was at the edge of the enemy camp, in the dark. The man who built an altar and named it *Yahweh is Peace*
(6:24) now responds to an answered sign, as Abraham's servant did when his sign was answered: *The man
bowed his head, and worshiped Yahweh* (Genesis 24:26).

Then he goes back and repeats Yahweh's command to his men. *Arise; for Yahweh has delivered … into your
hand* is Yahweh's own sentence from verse 9, now said by Gideon and made plural, *your* hand meaning the
three hundred. It is also Deborah's sentence to Barak: *Go; for this is the day in which Yahweh has
delivered Sisera into your hand* (4:14). WEB renders her *qûm* as *Go*, and Judges 7:9 and 15 as
*Arise*. The Hebrew is the same imperative in all three (see the Notes). Barak needed a prophetess to say
it to him. Gideon says it himself, once he has been strengthened. His hands *were strengthened*, as
Yahweh promised. He does not ask for anything more.

**How the pericope sits in Gideon's story.** This is the last of Gideon's signs before the battle, and
the only one Yahweh offers without being asked. The pattern of his call — a frightened man reassured
again and again — reaches its end here: Gideon worships and gives the order. The scene also turns the
fear around. In 7:3 the fearful in Israel went home. In 7:14 it is Midian's camp that is afraid, though
the text never uses the word fear for the Midianites. Their dreams and their words show it. The three
hundred have not yet done anything, and the enemy already says the matter is settled.

## People

- [[Gideon]] — told *Arise, go down*, offered a sign *if you are afraid* (9–10); goes down with Purah
  (11); hears the dream, worships, and gives the order (15).
- [[Purah]] — *your servant*, Gideon's companion on the night reconnaissance (10–11). Named only here.
- [[God]] — *Yahweh*, who says *I have delivered it into your hand* and offers the sign (9–11); *God* in
  the interpreter's mouth (14).
- [[Joash (father of Gideon)]] — named by the Midianite: *Gideon the son of Joash* (14).
- [[Amalek]] — in the valley with Midian (12).
- [[The Children of the East]] — the third partner, *all the children of the east* (12).
- The dreamer and his fellow — two unnamed men of Midian's camp (13–14). No page; see the Notes.

## Places

- [[Midian]] — *the camp of Midian* (13), and its army (14–15).
- [[The Valley of Jezreel]] — *in the valley* (12), the valley of 6:33 and 7:1, 8.

## Themes

- [[The Fear of God]] — *if you are afraid to go down* (10), the same *yārēʾ* as 7:3; and the fear of
  Midian's camp, shown but not named.
- [[Signs and Wonders]] — a sign offered unasked, heard rather than seen (10–11).
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — a dream and its interpretation, the first told since Genesis 41, both of
  them from the enemy (13–14).
- [[Praise and Song]] — *he worshiped* (15), the only use of *hištaḥăwâ* toward Yahweh in Judges.
- [[War]] — the reconnaissance before the battle, and the order *Arise* (9, 15).
- [[Providence]] — Gideon arrives as the dream is told (13), as Yahweh said he would hear (11).

## Notes

**"That same night."** *Wayhî ballaylâ hahûʾ* (9, WLC), the same phrase that opens 6:25. *Qûm rēd
bammaḥăneh kî nətattîw bəyādekā* (9, WLC): *nətattîw*, *I have given it*, is a perfect.

**"If you are afraid."** *Wəʾim-yārēʾ ʾattâ lāreḏet* (10, WLC). *Yārēʾ* (3373) is the word of 7:3,
*mî-yārēʾ wəḥārēd*. In the WLC of Judges it occurs only in these two verses. The morphology tags it as an
adjective in 7:3 and as a participle here. The form is the same.

**"Purah your servant."** *Pūrâ naʿarkā* (10, WLC). *Purah* (6513) occurs in Genesis–Judges only in 7:10
and 11. *Naʿar* (5288) is a young man or an attendant. Judges uses it later for Gideon's son Jether (8:20)
and for Abimelech's armor-bearer (9:54). What Purah's name means is not settled here.

**"Your hands will be strengthened."** *Wəʾaḥar teḥĕzaqnâ yādêkā* (11, WLC). *Ḥāzaq* (2388) is the verb
of *heḥĕzîq*, *retained* (7:8), and of *ḥăzaq*, *Be strong*, in Joshua 1:9 (WLC). It returns in 7:20, where
the three hundred *held* the torches (*wayyaḥăzîqû*, WLC).

**"The armed men."** *Ḥămušîm* (2571, WLC), passive participle. In Genesis–Judges it occurs four times:
Israel going up *armed* out of Egypt (Exodus 13:18), the eastern tribes crossing *armed* (Joshua 1:14;
4:12), and here, of the enemy. Its exact sense is disputed, with *in battle array* and *in fifties* among
the suggestions. These are reported, not checked.

**"Lay along in the valley."** *Nōpəlîm bāʿēmeq* (12, WLC), the participle of *nāpal* (5307). The dream
uses *nāpal* twice in 7:13: *wayyippōl*, *it fell*, and *wənāpal hāʾōhel*, *the tent lay flat* (WLC).

**"As the sand which is on the seashore for multitude."** *Kaḥôl šeʿal-śəpat hayyām lārōb* (12, WLC).
Joshua 11:4 has *kaḥôl ʾăšer ʿal-śəpat hayyām lārōb* (WLC). The only difference is the relative particle,
*še-* for *ʾăšer*. Genesis 22:17 has *wəkaḥôl ʾăšer ʿal-śəpat hayyām* without *lārōb* (WLC). *Kāʾarbeh
lārōb*, *like locusts for multitude* (12), matches 6:5's *kədê-ʾarbeh lārōb*, and *ʾên mispār*, *without
number*, is in both verses (WLC).

**"Telling a dream."** *ʾÎš məsappēr ləreʿēhû ḥălôm … hinnēh ḥălôm ḥālamtî* (13, WLC). Genesis 37:9 has
*wayəsappēr … hinnēh ḥālamtî ḥălôm ʿôd* (WLC). The verb *sippēr*, *tell* (5608), the noun *ḥălôm* (2472),
and the verb *ḥālam* (2492) are the same, and the order of noun and verb is reversed. The noun *ḥălôm*
occurs in the WLC of Genesis–Judges in Genesis 20, 31, 37, 40–42, at Numbers 12:6, at Deuteronomy 13:2,
4, 6 (WEB 13:1, 3, 5), and in Judges only at 7:13 (twice) and 7:15. No dream is narrated in
Exodus–Joshua.

**"A cake of barley bread."** *Ṣəlîl leḥem śəʿōrîm* (13, WLC). The written text (ketiv) is *ṣlwl*, and the
reading text (qere) is *ṣəlîl*. The word (6742) occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible. *Cake* or *round
loaf* is the usual rendering, drawn from context. Proposed derivations, such as one from a root meaning
*to roll* or *to clatter*, are reported, not checked. *Śəʿōrîm* (8184) is barley.

**"Tumbled."** *Mithappēk* (13, WLC), hitpael participle of *hāpak* (2015), *turn*. In the WLC of
Genesis–Judges the hitpael of *hāpak* occurs only here and at Genesis 3:24, *the flaming sword which turned
every way*, *lahaṭ haḥereb hammithappeket*. In Genesis a sword turns. In Judges a loaf turns, and the
interpreter says it is *the sword of Gideon*. The two verses share a rare verb form and the word
*sword*. The wiki records this and does not make it a connection. Judges does not refer to Eden, and
two uses of one form are not enough to show an allusion. *Wayyahapkēhû ləmaʿlâ*, *turned it upside
down*, is the qal of the same verb (WLC).

**"The tent."** *Hāʾōhel* (13, WLC), with the article: *the* tent. The text does not say whose tent it is.
Readings that make it the commander's tent are reported, not checked.

**"Nothing other than the sword of Gideon."** *ʾÊn zōʾt biltî ʾim-ḥereb gidʿôn ben-yôʾāš ʾîš yiśrāʾēl nātan
hāʾĕlōhîm bəyādô* (14, WLC). *Hāʾĕlōhîm*, *God*, with the article. The interpreter does not use the name
Yahweh. Gideon uses it in 15. The shout of 7:20 is *ḥereb laYHWH ûləgidʿôn*, *a sword for Yahweh and for
Gideon* (WLC).

**"Its interpretation."** *Wəʾet-šibrô* (15, WLC). *Šeber* (7667) in the morphhb lemma data. Its other
occurrences in Genesis–Judges, Leviticus 21:19 (twice) and 24:20 (twice), mean *break* or *fracture*
(WLC). The sense *interpretation* occurs only here. Genesis 40–41 uses *pātar* (6622) and *pitrôn* (6623)
for interpreting dreams. The morphhb lemma puts the noun with *šābar*, *break* (7665), the verb used in
7:20 for breaking the pitchers, *wayyišbərû hakkaddîm* (WLC). Whether the *interpretation* sense is the
same word as *break* or a homonym, and whether the narrator means a pun on the pitchers, is a lexicon
question. It was not checked here, and the wiki does not draw the pun.

**"He worshiped."** *Wayyištāḥû* (15, WLC), the *hištaḥăwâ* form of the root *ḥwh* (7812). In the WLC of
Judges it occurs four times. Three are Israel bowing to other gods (2:12, 17, 19). This is the fourth, and
the only one directed at Yahweh. Genesis 24:26 has *wayyištaḥû laYHWH* (WLC).

**"Arise."** *Qûm* in 7:9 (singular) and *qûmû* in 7:15 (plural) (WLC). Deborah's sentence in 4:14 is *qûm
kî zeh hayyôm ʾăšer nātan YHWH ʾet-sîsrāʾ bəyādekā* (WLC). WEB renders *qûm* there as *Go*. Joshua 2:24
has *kî-nātan YHWH bəyādênû ʾet-kol-hāʾāreṣ* (WLC). In 7:15, *kî-nātan YHWH bəyedkem ʾet-maḥănēh midyān*
(WLC). All four use *nātan … bəyad-*, *give into the hand*, the standard formula of holy war in these
books. It is not unique to these passages.

**Midian's numbers.** The text still gives no count here. 8:10 will speak of *one hundred twenty thousand
men who drew sword* who fell (stored WEB; not yet ingested).

**Judgment calls.** The boundary was kept as planned. 7:9 opens a new scene, *That same night*, and 7:16
turns to the battle plan. The verses fit together: a command, a sign, a dream, worship, and the order.
[[Purah]] gets a page. He is named twice and is the only named companion in Gideon's story, so he
meets the threshold used for minor named figures. The dreamer and his fellow are unnamed and act only
in these two verses, so they get no page. The barley-loaf-as-farmer reading is reported, not adopted. The
Genesis 3:24 *mithappēk* and the possible *šeber*/*šābar* pun are recorded in these Notes only. The
reconnaissance is made a connection with Joshua 2 because the formula *Yahweh has delivered … into
your/our hand* and the enemy's own words were checked in both. A new connection was not made for the sand
image. The existing locust connection already compares Joshua 11:4, so it was revised instead.
