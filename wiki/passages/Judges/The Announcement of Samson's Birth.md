# The Announcement of Samson's Birth

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 13:1–25

## Summary

The last cycle of the book opens without a cry. *The children of Israel again did that which was evil
in Yahweh's sight; and Yahweh delivered them into the hand of the Philistines forty years* (1). Every
earlier oppression had Israel cry out to Yahweh before a deliverer came: under Cushan-Rishathaim (3:9),
Eglon (3:15), Jabin (4:3), Midian (6:6–7), and Ammon (10:10). Here no one cries. The deliverer is
announced anyway, before he is conceived, and he is promised only to *begin* the work (5).

A man of Zorah, of the family of the Danites, is named Manoah. His wife is *barren, and childless*, and
she is never named (2). Yahweh's angel appears to her, not to him, and tells her she will conceive and
bear a son. She is to drink no wine or strong drink and eat nothing unclean, because *No razor shall
come on his head; for the child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb. He shall begin to save Israel
out of the hand of the Philistines* (3–5).

She tells her husband. She calls the visitor *a man of God* whose face was *like the face of the angel
of God, very awesome*, and says she did not ask his name and he did not give it. Her report repeats the
promise and the rules but changes the ending: she leaves out *he shall begin to save Israel* and adds
*to the day of his death* (6–7).

Manoah *entreated Yahweh* that the man of God come again *and teach us what we should do to the child*
(8). God listens. The angel comes again, again to the woman, *as she sat in the field*, and again
Manoah is not with her (9). She runs to fetch him. He asks, *Are you the man who spoke to my wife?* and
the visitor says, *I am* (10–11). Manoah asks what the child's *way of life and mission* will be (12).
The angel answers with no new information. He repeats the rules for the woman and does not say a word
about the child (13–14).

Manoah offers a meal of a young goat. The angel refuses to eat, and tells him that a burnt offering, if
he makes one, must go to Yahweh, *For Manoah didn't know that he was Yahweh's angel* (15–16). Manoah
asks his name, *that when your words happen, we may honor you*. The answer: *Why do you ask about my
name, since it is incomprehensible?* (17–18). Manoah offers the goat and the meal offering *on the rock
to Yahweh*. As the flame goes up from the altar, *Yahweh's angel ascended in the flame of the altar*,
and Manoah and his wife fall on their faces (19–20). He does not appear to them again, *Then Manoah
knew that he was Yahweh's angel* (21).

*We shall surely die, because we have seen God*, Manoah says. His wife answers that if Yahweh meant to
kill them he would not have accepted their offerings, shown them these things, or told them this
(22–23). She bears a son and names him Samson. *The child grew, and Yahweh blessed him*. And *Yahweh's
Spirit began to move him in Mahaneh Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol* (24–25).

**What is going on.** The chapter is an annunciation, the same scene Genesis gave Hagar (16:7–12) and
Sarah (18:9–15): a barren or desperate woman, the angel of Yahweh, a son promised, his destiny named.
It is also the second time in Judges that the angel of Yahweh appears to a person and is offered a goat
and a meal (6:11–24), and the second time a visitor at a meal turns out to be divine, after Abraham's
three guests (Genesis 18:1–8). The chapter tells the familiar scene with an unusual distribution of
understanding: the woman sees first, sees most, and reasons best, and the husband, though he prays and
is answered, never gets past her. Every visit goes to her. Every message he receives is a repetition of
hers. His question about the child gets no answer. His fear of death is corrected by her. The narrator
leaves her nameless and gives him the name, and then gives her every line that turns out to be right.

**What is notable.**

- **No outcry.** The sequence of sin, oppression, outcry, and deliverer, set out at 2:11–19 and run five
  times, loses its third step here. The text does not explain the silence. Israel's last cry had been
  answered with *I will save you no more* (10:13) and then relented over (10:16). Here the deliverer
  comes unasked.
- **Only a beginning.** *He shall begin to save*, *wəhûʾ yāḥēl ləhôšîaʿ* (5, WLC). The angel promises a
  start, not a rest. The land had rest after Othniel, Ehud, Deborah, and Gideon (3:11, 30; 5:31; 8:28),
  and after Gideon it is never said to rest again. The work of saving *out of the
  hand of the Philistines* is next promised for Saul, in the same words (1 Samuel 9:16), and the
  Philistines are said to be *subdued* only under Samuel (1 Samuel 7:13) and David (2 Samuel 8:1). See
  [[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]].
- **The same verb closes the chapter.** *Yahweh's Spirit began to move him*, *wattāḥel rûaḥ YHWH
  ləpaʿămô* (25, WLC). The verb of *begin*, *ḥālal* (lemma 2490 in its sense *to begin*), frames the
  chapter: the promise of a beginning (5) and the beginning of the Spirit's work (25). It returns at the
  end of Samson's life, when Delilah *began to afflict him* (16:19) and *the hair of his head began to
  grow again* (16:22). In the WLC of Judges the verb in this sense occurs at 10:18; 13:5, 25; 16:19, 22;
  20:31, 39, 40.
- **A Nazirite before he is born.** Numbers 6 describes a vow a person chooses, for a set time, ending in
  a shaving at the Tent. Samson has none of this. God imposes it, from the womb, *to the day of his
  death* (7). The rules bind his mother before they bind him. See
  [[Nazirite by Vow, Nazirite from the Womb - Numbers 6, Judges 13, and 1 Samuel 1]].
- **A wonder that will not be named.** The angel calls his name *incomprehensible*, *pelʾî* (18, WLC,
  lemma 6383). The next verse says he *did an amazing thing*, *maplîʾ laʿăśôt* (19, WLC, *pālāʾ*, lemma
  6381). Both words are from the root *p-l-ʾ*, the root of the *wondrous works* Gideon asked after
  (6:13), of Sarah's *Is anything too hard for Yahweh?* (Genesis 18:14), and of the verb with which
  Numbers opens the Nazirite law, *When either man or woman shall make a special vow*, *kî yaplīʾ*
  (Numbers 6:2, WLC). The refused name is acted out the next moment as a wonder.
- **The angel goes up.** The verb *ʿālâ*, *go up*, runs through the chapter: *No razor shall come*
  (literally *go up*) *on his head* (5); a burnt offering *ʿōlâ* (16, 23), *you must offer it*, *taʿălennâ*
  (16); Manoah *offered it*, *wayyaʿal* (19); *when the flame went up*, *baʿălôt* (20); *the angel
  ascended*, *wayyaʿal* (20). All WLC. The visitor leaves by the same road as the offering. That chain
  is the wiki's reading of a common verb.

**How it connects.** [[The Call of Gideon]] is the closest parallel in the book: the angel of Yahweh, a
young goat and a meal offered, a rock, fire, the angel's departure, and a man who fears death for having
seen. Gideon's fire came *out of the rock* at the touch of the staff (6:21); here the angel himself goes
up in it. Gideon was answered *Peace be to you … You shall not die* by Yahweh (6:23); Manoah is answered
by his wife. [[Why Do You Ask My Name - Genesis 32, Exodus 3, and Judges 13]] already sets the name
refusal beside Jacob's at the Jabbok, and [[Entertaining Angels Unawares]] the refused meal beside
Abraham's. [[The Barren Matriarchs]] places Manoah's wife in the line of Sarah, Rebekah, and Rachel, and
Manoah's prayer uses the verb of Isaac's for Rebekah, *wayyeʿtar*, *entreated* (Genesis 25:21; Judges
13:8, WLC). In the WLC books of Genesis through Numbers and Judges, *ʿātar* is otherwise Moses entreating
for Pharaoh (Exodus 8–10).

Samson's tribe matters. [[Dan (person)]] was pressed into the hill country by the Amorites (1:34), and
Zorah and Eshtaol stand at the head of Dan's border list (Joshua 19:41) and in Judah's lowland list
(Joshua 15:33). Samson's story begins between Zorah and Eshtaol (13:25) and ends there, with his burial
*between Zorah and Eshtaol in the burial site of Manoah his father* (16:31). In chapter 18 the
Danites set out *from Zorah and Eshtaol* to find another home (18:2, 11).

## Notes

**The woman is never named.** Later Jewish tradition is reported to give her a name; that is recalled,
not verified, and the wiki does not use it. Her page is [[Manoah's Wife]], following
[[Jephthah's Daughter]].

**What she adds and leaves out.** The angel's words to her (3–5) and her report of them (7) differ.
She calls the unclean thing *ṭumʾâ*, *uncleanness* (7, WLC, lemma 2932), where the angel had *ṭāmēʾ*,
*unclean* (4, lemma 2931); the angel himself uses *ṭumʾâ* to Manoah (14). She leaves out the razor and
*he shall begin to save Israel*, and adds *to the day of his death*. Whether the narrator means her
report as a foreshadowing, an error, or a mother's emphasis, the text does not say. *To the day of his
death* is where the story ends: before he dies, the hair cut at 16:19 *began to grow again* (16:22,
30). Whether he is still a Nazirite then, the text does not say.

**"A man of God."** *ʾÎš hāʾĕlōhîm* (6, 8, WLC). The phrase is used of Moses (Deuteronomy 33:1; Joshua
14:6) and later of prophets. She takes the visitor for a prophet with a frightening face. *Nôrāʾ
məʾōd*, *very awesome* (6), is the word of Jacob at Bethel, *How dreadful is this place!* (Genesis 28:17,
*mah-nnôrāʾ*, WLC lemma 3372 in both). That echo is a common word and is noted only.

**"I am."** Manoah asks *Are you the man …?* and he answers *ʾānî* (11, WLC), the plain Hebrew of *I
(am)*. It is the ordinary answer to a question of identity and is not the *ʾehyeh* of Exodus 3:14.

**"The child's way of life and mission."** *Mišpaṭ hannaʿar ûmaʿăśēhû* (12, WLC). *Mišpāṭ* (lemma
4941) is the noun of the root *š-p-ṭ*, the root of *judge* (*šōpēṭ*). The WEB's *way of life* is one
of its senses, *rule* or *custom*. Manoah asks, in the word of the book's title, what the child's
*judgment* will be. The angel does not answer. The pun is the wiki's observation; the word is ordinary.

**"Incomprehensible."** *Pelʾî* (18, WLC; lemma 6383; written *pelîʾ* in the consonants, *plʾy*).
In the WLC books of Genesis through Numbers and Judges, lemma 6383 occurs only here; the noun *peleʾ*
(6382) occurs at Exodus 15:11, *doing wonders*. Isaiah 9:6 is often cited for *peleʾ* as a name, but
Isaiah was not checked for this ingest. The WEB's *incomprehensible* is one rendering; *wonderful* is
another.

**"Begin to move him."** *Ləpaʿămô* (25, WLC; *pāʿam*, lemma 6470). In the WLC books of Genesis,
Exodus, Numbers, and Judges the verb occurs only here and at Genesis 41:8, where Pharaoh's *spirit was
troubled* (*wattippāʿem rûḥô*). The noun *paʿam*, *a beat, a time, a footstep*, is common. *Move* may
mean *stir*, *impel*, or *trouble*; the text does not say what form the moving took.

**Mahaneh Dan.** *Maḥănēh-dān*, *the camp of Dan* (25, WLC). Here it lies *between Zorah and Eshtaol*.
Judges 18:12 explains the name from the six hundred Danites who *encamped in Kiriath Jearim, in Judah.
Therefore they called that place Mahaneh Dan, to this day. Behold, it is behind Kiriath Jearim.* So the
name is used in chapter 13 before chapter 18 tells how it arose, and the two verses locate it
differently. Whether they describe one site, two camps of the same name, or a narrator using the later
name in an earlier story, the text does not say. Both are recorded on [[Mahaneh Dan]]; the wiki chooses
neither.

**Samson's name.** *Šimšôn* (24, WLC, lemma 8123). The name is usually connected with *šemeš*, *sun*,
and Beth Shemesh lies near Zorah. Both are reported, not checked. The text gives no reason for the
name. *Manoah*, *mānôaḥ* (lemma 4495), is usually connected with *nûaḥ*, *rest*; that too is reported,
not checked, and if it is right it is a name of rest in a cycle that has none.

**Judgment calls.** One pericope, 13:1–25. The plan's boundary is kept. 13:1 could be read as a heading
for chapters 13–16 rather than part of the annunciation, but it gives the oppression the announcement
answers, so it stays here. [[Manoah's Wife]] gets her own page: she is the central human actor of the
chapter. [[Mahaneh Dan]] gets a page because it is named twice with different locations. No page for
the meal offering or for the rock.
