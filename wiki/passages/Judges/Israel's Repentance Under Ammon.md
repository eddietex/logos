# Israel's Repentance Under Ammon

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 10:6–18

## Summary

The cycle begins again, and this time it is told at length. Israel *again did that which was evil*
and served the gods of seven peoples (6). Yahweh's anger burned and he sold them to the Philistines
and to Ammon. For eighteen years Ammon oppressed the Israelites beyond the Jordan, in Gilead, and
then crossed the river against Judah, Benjamin, and Ephraim (7–9). Israel cried out and, for the
first time in the book, the cry has words: *We have sinned against you* (10). Yahweh answers in his
own voice, with no angel or prophet between. He lists seven peoples he saved them from, and ends,
*Therefore I will save you no more. Go and cry to the gods which you have chosen* (11–14). Israel
confesses again and gives itself up to him: *Do you to us whatever seems good to you; only deliver
us, please, today*. They put away the foreign gods and serve Yahweh, *and his soul was grieved for
the misery of Israel* (15–16). Then the armies gather, Ammon in Gilead and Israel at Mizpah. The
princes of Gilead ask one another who will begin the fight, and promise that man the headship of
Gilead (17–18). No name is given. Yahweh raises up no one.

The passage is the long preface to the Jephthah story (11:1–12:7), and it also stands over the
Samson story. The Philistines named in 10:7 are the oppressors of chapters 13–16.

**The relapse (10:6–9).**

> **10:6** The children of Israel again did that which was evil in Yahweh's sight, and served the Baals, the Ashtaroth, the gods of Syria, the gods of Sidon, the gods of Moab, the gods of the children of Ammon, and the gods of the Philistines. They abandoned Yahweh, and didn't serve him.

*Again*, *wayyōsipû* (WLC, lemma 3254, *yāsap*, *to add*). Israel *added* to do evil. It is the
formula of 4:1 and 13:1. The same verb returns in Yahweh's answer: *lōʾ-ʾôsîp ləhôšîaʿ ʾetkem*,
*I will not add to save you*, rendered *I will save you no more* (10:13, WLC). Israel adds evil;
Yahweh will not add salvation. The repetition is in the Hebrew. Whether it is meant as a play on
the word is the wiki's reading.

*The Baals, the Ashtaroth, the gods of Syria, the gods of Sidon, the gods of Moab, the gods of the
children of Ammon, and the gods of the Philistines.* The longest list of gods in the book. Earlier
the charge was *Baal and the Ashtaroth* (2:13) or *the Baals and the Asheroth* (3:7). Here two kinds
of god are followed by five peoples' gods. That makes seven. Syria is *ʾărām* (WLC), Aram. The gods
are named by their peoples, and two of those peoples are the oppressors of the next verse. Israel
serves the gods of Ammon and the Philistines and is sold into the hand of Ammon and the Philistines.
See [[Baal (deity)]], [[Ashtoreth (deity)]], [[Aram (place)]], [[Sidon (city)]], [[Moab (place)]],
[[Ammon]], [[The Philistines]], and [[Idolatry]].

*They abandoned Yahweh, and didn't serve him.* The verse says it twice, once as an act and once as
an absence. *Abandoned*, *ʿāzab* (lemma 5800), is the verb of 2:12–13, and it is the verb of both
confessions and of Yahweh's charge: *we have forsaken our God* (10), *you have forsaken me* (13; all
WLC).

> **10:7** Yahweh's anger burned against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of the Philistines, and into the hand of the children of Ammon.

> **10:8** They troubled and oppressed the children of Israel that year. For eighteen years, they oppressed all the children of Israel that were beyond the Jordan in the land of the Amorites, which is in Gilead.

> **10:9** The children of Ammon passed over the Jordan to fight also against Judah, and against Benjamin, and against the house of Ephraim, so that Israel was very distressed.

*Anger burned … sold them*, *wayyiḥar ʾap … wayyimkərēm* (WLC). The two verbs of 2:14 and 3:8.

*Two oppressors.* The Philistines and Ammon are sold-to together, but the story that follows tells
only of Ammon. The Philistines return at 13:1: *Yahweh delivered them into the hand of the
Philistines forty years*. So 10:7 opens two stories at once. The book does not say whether the
oppressions overlapped in time. See [[The Philistines]].

*Troubled and oppressed*, *wayyirʿăṣû wayrōṣəṣû* (WLC). Two verbs that sound alike. The first,
*rāʿaṣ* (lemma 7492), occurs only here in the WLC files read for this ingest, Genesis, Numbers,
Deuteronomy, Joshua, and Judges. Its sense, *to shatter*, is reported from lexica, not checked. The
second, *rāṣaṣ* (lemma 7533), *to crush*, is the verb of the covenant curse: *You will only be
oppressed and crushed always* (Deuteronomy 28:33, *wərāṣûṣ*, WLC).

*That year. For eighteen years.* The Hebrew has both, *baššānâ hahîʾ šəmōneh ʿeśrēh šānâ* (WLC). The
text does not say which year *that year* is. It may mean the year the oppression began, or the year
Jair died. The wiki leaves it unresolved.

*Beyond the Jordan in the land of the Amorites, which is in Gilead.* The land Israel took from
Sihon king of the Amorites, *from the Arnon to the Jabbok, even to the children of Ammon*
(Numbers 21:24). Whose land it is will be the subject of Jephthah's letter to Ammon's king
(11:12–28). See [[Gilead]], [[The Amorites]], and [[Sihon and Og]].

*Against Judah, and against Benjamin, and against the house of Ephraim.* The oppression crosses
west of the Jordan, into the heart of the land. It reaches the three tribes of the south and centre.
See [[Judah]], [[Benjamin]], and [[Ephraim]].

**The cry and the refusal (10:10–14).**

> **10:10** The children of Israel cried to Yahweh, saying, "We have sinned against you, even because we have forsaken our God, and have served the Baals."

*Cried … saying.* Israel has cried out before (3:9, 15; 4:3; 6:6–7), but the narrator never gave
the cry any words. Here it has a confession: *ḥāṭāʾnû*, *we have sinned* (WLC, lemma 2398). The
reason is given too: *we have forsaken our God, and have served the Baals*. See [[Sin]] and
[[Prayer]].

The verb of the cry is *zāʿaq* (lemma 2199), as at 3:9, 15 and 6:6–7. At 4:3 it was *ṣāʿaq*
(lemma 6817). Both verbs appear in this passage (10, 12, 14, 17; WLC).

> **10:11** Yahweh said to the children of Israel, "Didn't I save you from the Egyptians, and from the Amorites, from the children of Ammon, and from the Philistines?

> **10:12** The Sidonians also, and the Amalekites, and the Maonites, oppressed you; and you cried to me, and I saved you out of their hand.

> **10:13** Yet you have forsaken me, and served other gods. Therefore I will save you no more.

> **10:14** Go and cry to the gods which you have chosen. Let them save you in the time of your distress!"

*Yahweh said.* There is no messenger. At Bochim an angel spoke (2:1), in Gideon's day a prophet
(6:8), and at 2:20 Yahweh spoke but no hearer was named. This time the speech is addressed *to the
children of Israel*. It is the fourth speech of its kind in the book. The others ended *you have not
listened to my voice*. This one ends with a refusal. See
[[I Brought You Up from Egypt - The Indictments at Bochim and in Gideon's Day]].

*Seven peoples.* The list matches the seven kinds of god in 10:6, seven against seven. The match
is the wiki's observation; the text does not draw it. The peoples are the Egyptians, the Amorites,
Ammon, the Philistines, the Sidonians, the Amalekites, and the Maonites. Some of these rescues have
been narrated: Egypt in Exodus; the Amorites Sihon and Og (Numbers 21:21–35); Ammon and Amalek as
Eglon's allies (Judges 3:13); the Philistines in Shamgar's verse (3:31); Amalek again with Midian
(6:3). The Sidonians and the Maonites have no oppression narrated anywhere in the books stored so
far. The Sidonians were among the nations left to test Israel (3:3). The Maonites appear only here.
The lemma for *Maon* (4584) is the same one used for the town of Maon in Judah's hill country
(Joshua 15:55, WLC), but the text does not link a people to that town. Some Greek manuscripts are
reported to read *Midian* here, which would put the book's last great oppressor in the list. That
reading is reported, not checked; no Greek text was consulted. See [[The Maonites]], [[Egypt]],
[[The Sidonians]], and [[Amalek]].

*You cried to me, and I saved you*, *wattiṣʿăqû … wāʾôšîʿâ* (WLC). The whole cycle of 2:16–18 in four
words.

*I will save you no more*, *lōʾ-ʾôsîp ləhôšîaʿ ʾetkem* (WLC). The sentence has the shape of 2:21,
*I also will no longer drive out*, *lōʾ ʾôsîp ləhôrîš* (WLC). There Yahweh stopped driving out the
nations. Here he says he will stop saving Israel from them. The next chapter shows a man saving
Gilead, so the sentence is not kept to the letter. The text does not say whether Yahweh changed his
mind or whether the refusal was meant to be tested. See [[Redemption]].

*Go and cry to the gods which you have chosen.* *Which you have chosen*, *ʾăšer bəḥartem bām* (WLC,
*bāḥar*, lemma 977). At Shechem Joshua said, *choose today whom you will serve* (Joshua 24:15), and
then, *You are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen Yahweh* (24:22, *bəḥartem*, WLC).
Israel chose; now it is told to call on what it chose. The taunt is close to the Song of Moses:
*Where are their gods … Let them rise up and help you!* (Deuteronomy 32:37–38). See
[[I Will Save You No More - Deuteronomy 32.37-38, Joshua 24, and Judges 10.13-14]],
[[The Covenant at Shechem]], and [[The Song of Moses]].

**The second confession (10:15–16).**

> **10:15** The children of Israel said to Yahweh, "We have sinned! Do you to us whatever seems good to you; only deliver us, please, today."

> **10:16** They put away the foreign gods from among them, and served Yahweh; and his soul was grieved for the misery of Israel.

*Whatever seems good to you*, *kəkol-haṭṭôb bəʿênêkā*, *all that is good in your eyes* (WLC). The
passage opened with Israel doing *that which was evil in Yahweh's sight*, *hāraʿ bəʿênê YHWH* (10:6).
Now Israel gives the judgment of what is good to Yahweh's eyes. The book's closing refrain puts the
eyes elsewhere: *Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes* (17:6; 21:25). See
[[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].

*Only deliver us*, *haṣṣîlēnû* (WLC, *nāṣal*, lemma 5337). Israel does not ask to be *saved*,
*yāšaʿ*, the verb Yahweh has just refused. It asks with another verb. Whether that is a deliberate
change is not stated.

*They put away the foreign gods from among them*, *wayyāsîrû ʾet-ʾĕlōhê hannēkār miqqirbām* (WLC). In
the WLC files read for this ingest, the phrase *put away* (*sûr*, hiphil) with *gods of the foreign*
(*ʾĕlōhê hannēkār*) occurs in only three verses: Jacob's order at Shechem (Genesis 35:2), Joshua's
order at Shechem (Joshua 24:23), and here. Both earlier verses are commands. Jacob's household obeyed by
*giving* him its gods (Genesis 35:4, *wayyittənû*, WLC). This is the first time the nation is said to
put them away. The verse is the first action of Israel's in the passage, not a word. See
[[Put Away the Foreign Gods - Genesis 35.2-4 and Joshua 24.14-23]].

*His soul was grieved for the misery of Israel*, *wattiqṣar napšô baʿămal yiśrāʾēl* (WLC). Literally,
*his soul became short*. The idiom *qāṣar* with *nepeš* occurs in only three verses of the WLC files
read for this ingest. In Numbers 21:4 it is the people on the road around Edom, *The soul of the
people was very discouraged*. In Judges 16:16 it is Samson under Delilah's pressing, *his soul was
troubled to death*. In both it describes a person who can bear no more. Here the subject is
Yahweh. See [[His Soul Was Short - Numbers 21.4, Judges 10.16, and Judges 16.16]].

*The misery*, *ʿāmāl* (lemma 5999), is *toil* or *trouble*. In the same WLC files it is Joseph's
*all my toil* (Genesis 41:51), Balaam's *perverseness* not seen in Israel (Numbers 23:21), and the
*toil* Yahweh saw in Egypt in the first-fruits confession (Deuteronomy 26:7). The WEB reads Judges
10:16 as Yahweh grieving over Israel's suffering, which matches 2:18, *it grieved Yahweh because of
their groaning* (there the verb is *nāḥam*, WLC). Some interpreters read the clause the other way:
Yahweh grew impatient with Israel's misery, or with its *trouble*, meaning its efforts to win him
back. That reading is reported, not checked. The text does not say what Yahweh then does. See
[[Divine Grief and Relenting]] and [[Compassion and Mercy]].

**The armies gather (10:17–18).**

> **10:17** Then the children of Ammon were gathered together and encamped in Gilead. The children of Israel assembled themselves together and encamped in Mizpah.

> **10:18** The people, the princes of Gilead, said to one another, "Who is the man who will begin to fight against the children of Ammon? He shall be head over all the inhabitants of Gilead."

*Were gathered together*, *wayyiṣṣāʿăqû* (WLC). This is the niphal of *ṣāʿaq*, the verb of the cry in
10:12 and 4:3. Ammon is *cried up*, called to arms, with the verb Israel used for crying to Yahweh. The
WEB rightly translates it *gathered*. The shared root is visible only in the Hebrew. Israel's verb is
the ordinary *ʾāsap* (lemma 622).

*Mizpah.* *Bammiṣpâ* (WLC), *the watchpost*. Israel camps there; Jephthah will speak *all his words
before Yahweh in Mizpah* (11:11), and the place is called *Mizpah of Gilead* at 11:29. Genesis gives
a Mizpah in Gilead too: the heap of witness Jacob and Laban raised (Genesis 31:49). The text does not
say whether the two are the same place. See [[Mizpah (Gilead)]] and [[Galeed]].

*Who is the man who will begin to fight*, *mî hāʾîš ʾăšer yāḥēl ləhillāḥēm* (WLC). The book's first
verse asked a question of the same shape: *Who should go up for us first against the Canaanites, to
fight against them?* (1:1, *mî yaʿăleh … ləhillāḥem*, WLC). There *the children of Israel asked of
Yahweh*. Here the princes of Gilead ask *one another*. They do not inquire of Yahweh, and no answer
is given. See [[Who Shall Go Up First - Judges 1.1-2 and 20.18]].

*He shall be head*, *lərōʾš* (WLC). They offer a reward: the man who fights will rule. In every
earlier cycle Yahweh *raised up* the savior (3:9, 15) or called him (6:11–14). Here the leader is to
be found by a contract. The elders make the same offer to Jephthah, *You will be our head over all the
inhabitants of Gilead* (11:8), and *the people made him head and chief over them* (11:11). See
[[Kingship and Empire]].

**What the passage does.** It is the book's fullest account of the cycle's middle. Before, the
narrator moved from cry to deliverer in a verse. Here the cry is spoken twice, Yahweh refuses, Israel
acts, Yahweh's *soul* is moved, and still no deliverer is raised. The passage ends with Israel
looking for one among themselves. Gideon refused to rule (8:23), and Abimelech took rule (9:1–6). Now
the princes of Gilead offer to give it.

## Notes

**Boundaries.** The plan's 10:6–18 is kept. 10:6 opens a new cycle with *again did that which was
evil*. 10:17–18 could be read as the start of the Jephthah story, but it ends on a question that
11:1 answers by introducing a man. The question belongs with the crisis, and 11:1 begins with a new
subject, *Now Jephthah the Gileadite*.

**Is this repentance?** The title says so, and the text supports it: two confessions, and the gods
put away (10, 15–16). But Yahweh's speech comes between the first and second confession, and it
calls the first one insufficient, or at least does not accept it. The second confession adds an
action. Whether Israel's turning is genuine or a bargain for help, the text does not say. What it
reports is that Yahweh's *soul* was moved, not that he forgave.

**Two speeches, one with a result.** The prophet in Gideon's day (6:8–10) also listed Yahweh's acts
and charged Israel, and no result was stated. Here Yahweh states a result, *I will save you no more*,
and then is not shown keeping it. Both speeches leave the deliverance to the narrative that follows.
