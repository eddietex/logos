# Wives for Benjamin

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 21:1–25

## Summary

The chapter opens with an oath the war chapter did not report: *Now the men of Israel had sworn in
Mizpah, saying, "None of us will give his daughter to Benjamin as a wife"* (1). The people come to Bethel,
sit *until evening before God*, and weep *severely*: *Yahweh, the God of Israel, why has this happened in
Israel, that there should be one tribe lacking in Israel today?* (2–3). The next morning they build an
altar and offer burnt offerings and peace offerings (4). No answer from Yahweh is recorded.

A second oath surfaces. *Who is there among all the tribes of Israel who didn't come up in the assembly to
Yahweh?* — *for they had made a great oath concerning him who didn't come up to Yahweh to Mizpah, saying,
"He shall surely be put to death"* (5). *The children of Israel grieved for Benjamin their brother, and
said, "There is one tribe cut off from Israel today. How shall we provide wives for those who remain, since
we have sworn by Yahweh that we will not give them of our daughters to wives?"* (6–7). The count turns up
no one from Jabesh Gilead (8–9). The congregation sends twelve thousand men: *strike the inhabitants of
Jabesh Gilead with the edge of the sword, with the women and the little ones. This is the thing that you
shall do: you shall utterly destroy every male, and every woman who has lain with a man* (10–11). They find
*four hundred young virgins* and bring them *to the camp to Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan* (12).

The congregation sends to the six hundred *in the rock of Rimmon, and proclaimed peace to them* (13).
Benjamin returns and receives the women of Jabesh Gilead. *There still weren't enough for them* (14). *The
people grieved for Benjamin, because Yahweh had made a breach in the tribes of Israel* (15).

The elders take up the question again: *How shall we provide wives for those who remain, since the women
are destroyed out of Benjamin?* (16). *There must be an inheritance for those who are escaped of Benjamin,
that a tribe not be blotted out from Israel* (17). The oath is quoted a third time, now as a curse: *Cursed
is he who gives a wife to Benjamin* (18). So they point to *a feast of Yahweh from year to year in
Shiloh*, located with a route description — *on the north of Bethel, on the east side of the highway that
goes up from Bethel to Shechem, and on the south of Lebonah* (19) — and command Benjamin: *Go and lie in
wait in the vineyards, and see, and behold, if the daughters of Shiloh come out to dance in the dances,
then come out of the vineyards, and each man catch his wife of the daughters of Shiloh, and go to the land
of Benjamin* (20–21). When fathers or brothers complain, the elders will answer for them: *Grant them
graciously to us, because we didn't take for each man his wife in battle, neither did you give them to
them, otherwise you would now be guilty* (22).

*The children of Benjamin did so, and took wives for themselves, according to their number, of those who
danced, whom they carried off. They went and returned to their inheritance, built the cities, and lived in
them* (23). Israel disperses, *every man to his tribe and to his family … to his own inheritance* (24). The
book ends: *In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes*
(25).

**What is going on.** The war is over and the problem it made remains. The assembly that wept before the
battle (20:23, 26) now weeps over its result, and the question it asks is Yahweh's to answer: *why has
this happened … ?* (3). In chapter 20 Yahweh answered three inquiries. Here nothing is asked of him about
what to do, and nothing is reported from him; every plan in the chapter comes from the people, the
congregation, or the elders. The narrator's only theological sentence is in the people's mouth: *Yahweh
had made a breach* (15).

The chapter is built from oaths. An oath not to give daughters (1, 7, 18) closes the obvious solution. An
oath to kill whoever stayed away (5) supplies a second solution, and the oath is carried out on a city, not
on its men alone. When that falls two hundred short (600 survivors, 400 women; the figure *two hundred* is
not in the text), the first oath is kept by its letter: the fathers of Shiloh do not *give* their
daughters, so they are not guilty; Benjamin *catches* them. The narrator does not comment on any of it. He
closes with the refrain.

**What is notable.**

- **The oath, told late.** *Wəʾîš yiśrāʾēl nišbaʿ bammiṣpâ* (1, WLC). Chapter 20 never mentions this oath or
  the oath of verse 5; both are reported only now, in the pluperfect sense the WEB gives with *had sworn*.
  The chapter quotes the first oath three ways: *None of us will give his daughter* (1), *we have sworn by
  Yahweh* (7), and *Cursed is he who gives a wife to Benjamin*, *ʾārûr nōṯēn ʾiššâ ləḇinyāmin* (18). The
  form *wayyiššāḇəʿû … lēʾmōr ʾārûr*, swearing with a curse, is Joshua's at Jericho, *Joshua commanded them
  with an oath at that time, saying, "Cursed is the man …"* (Joshua 6:26, WLC). See [[Oaths and Vows]].
- **One tribe lacking.** *Ləhippāqēḏ … šēḇeṭ ʾeḥāḏ* (3, WLC): *lacking* is *pāqaḏ*, the verb of mustering
  and counting. Six verses later *the people were counted*, *wayyiṯpāqēḏ hāʿām* (9), and the count finds
  Jabesh Gilead missing. The same root asks for the missing tribe and finds a missing city.
- **Grieved.** *Wayyinnāḥămû … ʾel-binyāmin ʾāḥîw* (6; *niḥām*, 15, WLC). *Nāḥam* in the niphal occurs in the
  WLC of Judges only here and at 2:18, *it grieved Yahweh because of their groaning*. There Yahweh's grief
  raised up judges; here Israel's grief for the brother it struck raises a raid. See
  [[Divine Grief and Relenting]].
- **Cut off.** *Nigdaʿ hayyôm šēḇeṭ ʾeḥāḏ* (6, WLC). *Gādaʿ* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through 1
  Samuel only at Deuteronomy 7:5 and 12:3, the Asherah poles *cut down*; here; and 1 Samuel 2:31, *I will
  cut off your arm* to Eli's house (lemma 1438; 1 Samuel WEB checked at bible-api.com). It is a word for
  felling sacred poles, used of a tribe. The wiki notes it and builds nothing on it.
- **He shall surely be put to death.** *Môṯ yûmāṯ* (5, WLC), the sentence formula of the law (Exodus 21:12;
  Leviticus 20:2–27; Numbers 35:16–18; WLC). It is not found elsewhere in Joshua or Judges. Here it is an oath, and
  its object is whoever *didn't come up*. See
  [[Those Who Did Not Come - Meroz, Succoth and Penuel, and Jabesh Gilead]].
- **Every woman who has lain with a man.** *Wəḵol-ʾiššâ yōḏaʿaṯ miškaḇ-zāḵār taḥărîmû* (11, WLC);
  *ʾăšer lōʾ-yāḏəʿâ ʾîš ləmiškaḇ zāḵār* (12). *Miškaḇ zāḵār* with *yāḏaʿ* occurs in the files searched only
  in Numbers 31:17, 18, 35, the women of Midian, and here (lemma 4904). In Numbers Moses commands it after a
  war Yahweh ordered; here the congregation commands it against an Israelite town. The verb is *ḥāram*,
  *utterly destroy*, the ban. See [[Every Woman Who Has Known a Man - Numbers 31 and Jabesh Gilead]] and
  [[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]].
- **Young virgins.** *Naʿărâ ḇəṯûlâ* (12, WLC), the phrase of Deuteronomy 22:23, 28 and in the files
  searched found only there and here. Deuteronomy 22:28–29 legislates for a man who *grabs* an unbetrothed
  virgin. See [[The Unbetrothed Virgin - Exodus 22.16-17 and Deuteronomy 22.28-29]].
- **Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan.** *Šilōh ʾăšer bəʾereṣ kənāʿan* (12, WLC), the wording of
  Joshua 22:9, when the eastern tribes left Shiloh *to go to the land of Gilead* (compare Joshua 21:2,
  *at Shiloh in the land of Canaan*). The camp has moved from Mizpah and Bethel to Shiloh without the text
  saying when. The women of Gilead are brought west across the Jordan to the place the Gileadite tribes
  once left with a blessing. See [[Shiloh]].
- **Proclaimed peace.** *Wayyiqrəʾû lāhem šālôm* (13, WLC). Deuteronomy 20:10, *When you draw near to a city
  to fight against it, then proclaim peace to it*, *wəqārāʾṯā ʾēleyhā ləšālôm*, uses the same verb and noun
  of a city about to be besieged. The offer of peace the law makes before a war comes here after one, to
  the six hundred. See [[Laws for Going to War]] and [[The Rock of Rimmon]].
- **A breach.** *Kî-ʿāśâ YHWH pereṣ bəšiḇṭê yiśrāʾēl* (15, WLC). The noun *pereṣ* occurs in the files
  searched only here and at Genesis 38:29, the midwife's *Why have you made a breach for yourself?*,
  which gave Perez his name (lemma 6556). The people say Yahweh made it; nothing in chapter 20 says he did
  more than answer and *strike Benjamin* (20:35).
- **Not blotted out.** *Yəruššaṯ pəlêṭâ ləḇinyāmin wəlōʾ-yimmāḥeh šēḇeṭ miyyiśrāʾēl* (17, WLC). *Lōʾ
  yimmāḥeh … miyyiśrāʾēl* is the purpose clause of the levirate law, *that his name not be blotted out of
  Israel*, *wəlōʾ-yimmāḥeh šəmô miyyiśrāʾēl* (Deuteronomy 25:6). The law preserves a dead man's name by a
  wife; the elders preserve a tribe by wives. *Pəlêṭâ*, *those who are escaped*, is Joseph's *a great
  deliverance* (Genesis 45:7) and Jacob's *the company which is left will escape* (Genesis 32:8; Hebrew
  32:9) (lemma 6413). See [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]] and
  [[Blot Out - The Flood, Amalek, and God's Book]].
- **From year to year.** *Ḥaḡ-YHWH bəšilô miyyāmîm yāmîmâ* (19, WLC). *Miyyāmîm yāmîmâ* occurs in the files
  searched at Exodus 13:10 (Passover, *from year to year*); Judges 11:40, the daughters of Israel who go
  yearly for Jephthah's daughter; here; and 1 Samuel 1:3 and 2:19, Elkanah going up *from year to year … in
  Shiloh*. *Ḥaḡ YHWH* is the name Leviticus 23:39 gives the autumn feast of ingathering, *the feast of
  Yahweh*; Exodus 10:9 uses it of the feast Moses asks to hold in the wilderness. Judges does not say which
  feast this is; readers who name Booths point to the vineyards — reported, not decided. See
  [[From Year to Year - Jephthah's Daughter, the Feast at Shiloh, and Elkanah]] and
  [[Festivals and First Fruits]].
- **Directions to Shiloh.** Verse 19 locates Shiloh as if the reader might not know where it is: north of
  Bethel, east of the highway, *mêsillâ*, from Bethel to Shechem, south of [[Lebonah]]. *Ləḇônâ* occurs
  only here in the files searched (lemma 3829). The book that called Shiloh the place of *God's house*
  (18:31) now needs a map to find it. The wiki notes the contrast; some read it as a narrator distant from
  the time, some as a gloss for readers after Shiloh's fall — reported, not checked.
- **Lie in wait.** *Waʾăraḇtem bakkərāmîm* (20, WLC). *ʾĀraḇ* is the verb of the ambush at Gibeah
  (20:29–38), at Shechem (9:25–43), and against Samson (16:2–12). The last ambush in Judges is set for
  dancing girls. In 9:25 the men in ambush *robbed*, *wayyiḡzəlû*, all who passed; here Benjamin *carried
  off*, *gāzālû* (23), the dancers. *Gāzal* is the verb of robbery in the law (Leviticus 19:13, *nor rob
  him*). See [[Theft and Restitution]].
- **Catch.** *Waḥăṭap̄tem lāḵem ʾîš ʾištô* (21, WLC). *Ḥāṭap̄*, *seize*, occurs in the files searched only
  here (lemma 2414).
- **To dance in the dances.** *Lāḥûl bamməḥōlôṯ* (21; *hamməḥōləlôṯ*, 23, WLC). *Məḥōlâ* is the dance of
  Miriam (Exodus 15:20), of the calf (Exodus 32:19), of Jephthah's daughter (Judges 11:34), and of the women
  who meet Saul and David (1 Samuel 18:6). The book that began with a daughter given as a prize (1:12–13)
  and passed through a daughter who came out *with tambourines and with dances* (11:34) ends with daughters
  seized while they dance. See
  [[With Tambourines and with Dances - Miriam, Jephthah's Daughter, and the Daughters of Shiloh]].
- **Not in battle.** *Kî lōʾ lāqaḥnû ʾîš ʾištô bammilḥāmâ* (22, WLC). The verse is difficult. The WEB has
  the elders say *we didn't take for each man his wife in battle*: on one reading, the war at Jabesh did not
  supply enough, so the fathers should consent. Deuteronomy 21:10–11 allows a soldier to take *a beautiful
  woman* among the captives *as your wife*; whether verse 22 alludes to it the text does not say. Then
  *neither did you give them to them, otherwise you would now be guilty*, *kāʿēṯ teʾšāmû*: the fathers
  keep the oath because they did not *give*. *ʾĀšam*, *be guilty*, is otherwise in the files searched a
  word of Leviticus 4–5 and Numbers 5 (lemma 816). See [[Guilt and Conscience]].
- **Grant them graciously.** *Ḥānnûnû ʾôṯām* (22, WLC), *be gracious to us with them*. *Ḥānan* is the verb
  of Exodus 33:19, *I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious*, and of the blessing, *Yahweh … be
  gracious to you* (Numbers 6:25). The elders ask grace of the fathers for an abduction they arranged.
- **To their inheritance.** *Wayyāšûḇû ʾel-naḥălāṯām wayyiḇnû ʾeṯ-heʿārîm* (23); *wayyēṣəʾû miššām ʾîš
  lənaḥălāṯô* (24, WLC). *ʾÎš lənaḥălāṯô*, *each to his inheritance*, is the phrase of Joshua 24:28 and
  Judges 2:6, when Joshua sent the people away. The book's second introduction opened with the tribes
  going home to their inheritance under Joshua (2:6); its last chapter closes with them going home again,
  with no one sending them. Benjamin *built the cities*: the towns burned in 20:48.
- **The fourth refrain.** *Bayyāmîm hāhēm ʾên meleḵ bəyiśrāʾēl ʾîš hayyāšār bəʿênāyw yaʿăśeh* (25, WLC), the
  full form of 17:6, word for word. The epilogue opened and closes with it. See
  [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].

**How it connects.** Three threads end here. The *did not come* sequence of the Song (5:23) and of
Succoth and Penuel (8:5–17) ends in a city destroyed for staying home. Benjamin, reduced to six hundred at
[[The Rock of Rimmon]], is given back an inheritance by two massacres' worth of wives. And the refrain
that began with Micah's shrine closes the book. Two threads run on into 1 Samuel. Shiloh's yearly feast is
where Elkanah goes *from year to year* (1 Samuel 1:3, WEB checked at bible-api.com). And Jabesh Gilead,
whose women became Benjamin's wives, is the city a Benjamite from Gibeah rescues, *Saul* (1 Samuel 11:1–11),
and the city whose men take his body from the wall of Beth Shan and bury it (31:11–13). The kinship many
readers infer from Judges 21 is not stated in 1 Samuel. See
[[Jabesh Gilead and Benjamin - Judges 21, 1 Samuel 11, and 1 Samuel 31]].

## Notes

**Did the assembly do right?** The text gives no verdict inside the chapter and one at its end. In
favour of the elders: they grieve for a brother tribe, they offer peace, and they find a way to keep a tribe
from being *blotted out* (17). Against: the oaths that make the problem were sworn without inquiry of
Yahweh; the solution to the first oath is a second oath carried out on a city's women and children; and the
solution to what remains is the seizure of daughters at Yahweh's own feast, defended by a reading of the
oath that keeps its words and not its purpose. The closing refrain, *everyone did that which was right in
his own eyes*, follows directly. Readers take it as the narrator's judgment on the chapter; the wiki records
that reading as the one the placement invites and leaves the verdict there.

**Two oaths, and which one gives.** The oath of verse 5, *he shall surely be put to death*, is executed on
the whole town, beyond its terms, which named *him who didn't come up*. The oath of verse 1 is kept so
closely that the fathers of Shiloh are excused by it. The chapter keeps both oaths, one more harshly and one
more loosely than it was spoken. Compare Jephthah, who said *I can't go back* (11:35); see
[[Oaths and Vows]].

**Why no inquiry?** In chapter 20 the assembly asked at Bethel three times and was answered. In chapter 21
it weeps at Bethel and asks *why*, but asks nothing about what to do, and Yahweh says nothing. Some read the
silence as judgment, some as the narrator's way of showing a people that no longer asks. Neither is stated.

**The numbers.** Six hundred survive (20:47); four hundred women are taken from Jabesh (12); *there still
weren't enough* (14). The text does not say how many are taken at Shiloh; *two hundred* is arithmetic, not
the text. The women of Jabesh are *every woman who has lain with a man* destroyed and the rest kept, so
Jabesh's men, married women, and children are all killed (10–11).

**Where is the camp?** The people are at Bethel (2) and the virgins are brought *to the camp to Shiloh*
(12). The text does not narrate the move. The wiki files the weeping and the offerings under [[Bethel]] and
the camp under [[Shiloh]].

**Judgment calls.** Kept 21:1–25 as one pericope as planned; Jabesh (1–14) and Shiloh (15–24) answer one
question twice, and verse 25 closes the book. Created place pages for [[Jabesh Gilead]], which recurs in 1
Samuel, and [[Lebonah]], named only here. No person pages for the women of Jabesh or the daughters of
Shiloh, unnamed groups; they are listed under People above and are the subject of the connection page on
dances. The Jabesh–Saul kinship reading is reported on its connection page, not adopted.
