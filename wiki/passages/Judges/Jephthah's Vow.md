# Jephthah's Vow

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 11:29–40

## Summary

The king of Ammon *didn't listen* (11:28). In the next verse Yahweh acts: *Then Yahweh's Spirit came on
Jephthah* (29). He passes through Gilead and Manasseh to Mizpah of Gilead, and from there toward Ammon.
Before the battle he makes a vow. If Yahweh gives Ammon into his hand, *whatever comes out of the doors
of my house to meet me, when I return in peace … shall be Yahweh's, and I will offer it up for a burnt
offering* (30–31).

Yahweh gives them into his hand (32). Jephthah strikes twenty cities, from Aroer to Minnith and on to
Abel Keramim, and *the children of Ammon were subdued before the children of Israel* (33). The verse
gives no rest formula; see the notes.

He comes home to Mizpah. *Behold, his daughter came out to meet him with tambourines and with dances. She
was his only child* (34). He tears his clothes. *Alas, my daughter! You have brought me very low … for I
have opened my mouth to Yahweh, and I can't go back* (35). She answers that he must do what he said,
*because Yahweh has taken vengeance for you on your enemies* (36). She asks for two months on the
mountains with her companions, to *bewail my virginity* (37). He lets her go (38). She comes back, and
*he did with her according to his vow which he had vowed. She was a virgin* (39). Every year after,
*the daughters of Israel went yearly to celebrate the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite four days in a
year* (40).

She is never named. She speaks more in the passage than anyone but her father, and her words are the
ones that hold him to the vow.

**The Spirit and the march (11:29).**

> **11:29** Then Yahweh's Spirit came on Jephthah, and he passed over Gilead and Manasseh, and passed over Mizpah of Gilead, and from Mizpah of Gilead he passed over to the children of Ammon.

*Yahweh's Spirit came on Jephthah*, *wattəhî ʿal-yiptāḥ rûaḥ YHWH* (WLC). It is the clause used of
Othniel at 3:10 and of Balaam at Numbers 24:2. Gideon's verb was *clothed* (6:34). See
[[The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah]].

*Passed over*, *ʿābar* (lemma 5674, WLC), three times in the verse and again in 11:32. Jephthah crosses
his own region on the way to the enemy. The verse names the route but no muster, no horn and no
messengers, unlike Gideon's at 6:34–35.

The Spirit comes before the vow. The order matters for how the vow is read. Jephthah already has the
gift that marks a judge in this book, and he still bargains for the victory. The text does not say the
vow was needed, or that Yahweh asked for it. It does not say Yahweh accepted it either.

**The vow (11:30–31).**

> **11:30** Jephthah vowed a vow to Yahweh, and said, "If you will indeed deliver the children of Ammon into my hand,

> **11:31** then it shall be, that whatever comes out of the doors of my house to meet me, when I return in peace from the children of Ammon, it shall be Yahweh's, and I will offer it up for a burnt offering."

*Vowed a vow*, *wayyiddar … neder* (WLC), the phrase of Jacob at Bethel (Genesis 28:20). *If you will
indeed deliver*, *ʾim-nātôn tittēn* (WLC), the verb doubled for force. Jephthah's last speech was an
argument made to a king. This one is a bargain made with God. He offered the elders a condition, *if …
Yahweh delivers them before me* (11:9), and now he offers Yahweh one.

*Whatever comes out*, *hayyôṣēʾ ʾăšer yēṣēʾ* (WLC), *the one going out who goes out*. The participle is
masculine, the form Hebrew uses when it does not specify. The WEB's *whatever* keeps the question open,
and so does the Hebrew. It could be an animal or a person. *To meet me*, *liqrāʾtî*, is a phrase used of
people going out to greet someone, and it returns in verse 34. The text does not say what Jephthah
expected.

*I will offer it up for a burnt offering*, *wəhaʿălîtihû ʿôlâ* (WLC). The verb is *ʿālâ* in the causative
with a masculine suffix, *offer him* or *offer it*. God told Abraham *Offer him there as a burnt offering*,
*wəhaʿălēhû šām ləʿōlâ* (Genesis 22:2, WLC), with the same verb, the same suffix and the same noun. See
[[Your Only Son - Genesis 22 and Judges 11]].

*It shall be Yahweh's, and I will offer it up*. The two clauses are joined by *wə-*, *and*. Some
interpreters read that *wə-* as *or*: it shall be Yahweh's (if a person), *or* I will offer it up (if
an animal). The wiki recalls this reading from the commentary tradition (often credited to David Kimchi)
without verifying it. The Hebrew allows *wə-* to mean *or* in some contexts. Nothing in the verse marks
it as having that sense here.

*When I return in peace*, *bəšûbî bəšālôm* (WLC). Jacob said the same words in his vow (Genesis 28:21),
and Gideon said them in a threat (Judges 8:9). See
[[When I Return in Peace - Jacob's Vow, Gideon's Threat, and Jephthah's Vow]].

**The victory (11:32–33).**

> **11:32** So Jephthah passed over to the children of Ammon to fight against them; and Yahweh delivered them into his hand.

> **11:33** He struck them from Aroer until you come to Minnith, even twenty cities, and to Abelcheramim, with a very great slaughter. So the children of Ammon were subdued before the children of Israel.

*Yahweh delivered them into his hand*, *wayyittənēm YHWH bəyādô* (WLC). These are the words of the vow,
*nātôn tittēn … bəyādî* (30), turned into narration. The condition is met in one verse, and the
battle itself takes one more.

*From Aroer until you come to Minnith, even twenty cities, and to Abel Keramim*. It is the only place in
the WLC of Genesis–Judges where Minnith and Abel Keramim are named. The Aroer could be the Arnon-valley
town of 11:26 or the Aroer *near Rabbah* of Joshua 13:25. The wiki infers that a pursuit into Ammon's own land
would suit the second better. The text does not say which. See [[Aroer]], [[Minnith]] and [[Abel Keramim]].

*Subdued*, *wayyikkānəʿû* (*kānaʿ*, lemma 3665, WLC), the word for Moab under Ehud (3:30), Jabin
(4:23), and Midian (8:28). Ehud's and Gideon's are followed in the same verse by *the land had rest*,
eighty years and forty (3:30; 8:28), and Jabin's by forty after the song (5:31). Jephthah's is not. The
next thing the book reports is Ephraim's threat (12:1). See
[[The Land Had Rest - Joshua 11.23, 14.15, and the Judges Formula]].

**The daughter comes out (11:34–35).**

> **11:34** Jephthah came to Mizpah to his house; and behold, his daughter came out to meet him with tambourines and with dances. She was his only child. Besides her he had neither son nor daughter.

*Came out to meet him*, *yōṣēʾt liqrāʾtô* (WLC). The vow's words, *yēṣēʾ … liqrāʾtî* (31), now have a
subject. The narrator gives no pause between the vow's condition and its answer: *behold*, *wəhinnēh*.

*With tambourines and with dances*, *bətuppîm ûbimḥōlôt* (WLC). The same two words in the same form
describe the women who went out after Miriam at the sea: *with tambourines and with dances* (Exodus
15:20). In the WLC of Genesis–Judges the pair occurs only there and here. She does what Israel's women
do to greet a victory. See
[[With Tambourines and with Dances - Miriam, Jephthah's Daughter, and the Daughters of Shiloh]].

*She was his only child*, *wəraq hîʾ yəḥîdâ* (WLC). *Yāḥîd*, *only*, occurs in the WLC of Genesis–Judges
four times: three in Genesis 22 of Isaac, *your son, your only son* (22:2, 12, 16), and this once. The
narrator then says it twice more: *besides her he had neither son nor daughter*. Jephthah, driven out
from his father's house without an inheritance (11:2), has no heir but her.

> **11:35** When he saw her, he tore his clothes, and said, "Alas, my daughter! You have brought me very low, and you are one of those who trouble me; for I have opened my mouth to Yahweh, and I can't go back."

*He tore his clothes*, *wayyiqraʿ ʾet-bəgādāyw* (WLC), the act of mourning Reuben began at Genesis 37:29.
See [[Mourning and Lament]].

*You have brought me very low*, *hakrēaʿ hikraʿtinî* (WLC), the verb doubled again. *Kāraʿ* means *to bow,
kneel, sink down*. In Judges it was used of Sisera at Jael's feet, *he bowed, he fell* (5:27), and of the
men who *bow down on his knees to drink* (7:5–6). Those are all in the simple stem. Here it is
causative, *you have made me bow*, the only causative use in the WLC of Genesis–Judges.

*You are one of those who trouble me*, *wəʾatt hāyît bəʿōkərāy* (WLC), *you have become among my
troublers*. The verb is *ʿākar*, Jacob's word to Simeon and Levi, *You have troubled me* (Genesis 34:30),
and Joshua's to Achan, *Why have you troubled us? Yahweh will trouble you today* (Joshua 7:25). In those
verses the troubler had done something. Here the daughter has done nothing but come out of the door. The
blame Jephthah puts on her in this sentence belongs to his vow, and the next clause says so. See
[[The Valley of Achor - Trouble Turned to a Door of Hope]].

*I have opened my mouth to Yahweh*, *pāṣîtî-pî ʾel-YHWH* (WLC). Elsewhere in the WLC of Genesis–Judges
the verb *pāṣâ* is used only of the ground opening its mouth. It *opened its mouth to receive your
brother's blood* (Genesis 4:11), and it *opened its mouth, and swallowed* Korah's company (Numbers 16:30;
Deuteronomy 11:6). This is the first time a person is said to open his mouth with it. The echo is the
wiki's observation; Judges does not point to it.

*I can't go back*, *wəlōʾ ʾûkal lāšûb* (WLC). *Šûb* is the verb of the vow's condition, *when I return*,
*bəšûbî* (31). He has returned, and he cannot return from his word. It is also the verb the king of
Ammon used, *restore … peaceably* (11:13). See
[[Yes, He Will Be Blessed - The Irrevocable Word]].

The law on vows agrees with him. *When a man vows a vow to Yahweh … he shall not break his word. He shall
do according to all that proceeds out of his mouth* (Numbers 30:2). Numbers 30 lets a father cancel his
daughter's vow, but it gives no one the power to cancel a man's own vow. Leviticus 27:2–8 sets a price in
silver when *a man consecrates a person to Yahweh in a vow*. Jephthah's vow did not vow a person to be valued in silver. It vowed a
burnt offering, and the text never mentions the price. See
[[A Person Vowed to Yahweh - Leviticus 27.1-8, Judges 11, and 1 Samuel 1]] and
[[Vows of Men and Women]].

**The daughter's answer (11:36–38).**

> **11:36** She said to him, "My father, you have opened your mouth to Yahweh; do to me according to that which has proceeded out of your mouth, because Yahweh has taken vengeance for you on your enemies, even on the children of Ammon."

*Do to me according to that which has proceeded out of your mouth*, *ʿăśēh lî kaʾăšer yāṣāʾ mippîkā*
(WLC). Numbers 30:2 has *according to all that proceeds out of his mouth he shall do*, *kəkol-hayyōṣēʾ
mippîw yaʿăśeh* (WLC 30:3), with the same two verbs, *yāṣāʾ* and *ʿāśâ*. The daughter states the law of
Numbers 30 to her father, though she does not cite it. The vow was about *whatever comes out*,
*hayyôṣēʾ*, of his door. She speaks of what *has come out*, *yāṣāʾ*, of his mouth.

*Because Yahweh has taken vengeance for you*, *ʿāśâ ləkā YHWH nəqāmôt* (WLC). *Nəqāmâ*, *vengeance*, is
otherwise in the WLC of Genesis–Judges only at Numbers 31:2–3, *Avenge the children of Israel for the
Midianites … to execute Yahweh's vengeance on Midian*. She does not say the vow won the battle. She says
Yahweh won it, and that the vow is owed because Yahweh did what was asked. See [[Vengeance]].

> **11:37** She said to her father, "Let this thing be done for me. Leave me alone two months, that I may depart and go down on the mountains, and bewail my virginity, I and my companions."

> **11:38** He said, "Go." He sent her away for two months: and she departed, she and her companions, and mourned her virginity on the mountains.

*Leave me alone*, *harpēh mimmennî* (WLC), from *rāpâ*, *let go, let drop*. *Bewail my virginity*,
*ʾebkeh ʿal-bətûlay* (WLC). *Bətûlîm* occurs otherwise in the WLC of Genesis–Judges only in the laws of
Leviticus 21:13 and Deuteronomy 22:14–20, where it means proof that a woman is a virgin. She does not
mourn her death by name. She mourns that she will die, or live, without marriage or children. In a house
where she was the only child, that ends her father's line as well.

*My companions*, *rēʿôtay* (WLC; the written text has a variant spelling and the margin corrects it).
They go with her and stay with her for the two months. They are the first of the *daughters of Israel*
in verse 40.

**The vow kept, and the custom (11:39–40).**

> **11:39** At the end of two months, she returned to her father, who did with her according to his vow which he had vowed. She was a virgin. It was a custom in Israel

> **11:40** that the daughters of Israel went yearly to celebrate the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite four days in a year.

*She returned*, *wattāšob* (WLC). The verb *šûb* again. He could not go back on his word, and she comes
back to him.

*He did with her according to his vow which he had vowed*, *wayyaʿaś lāh ʾet-nidrô ʾăšer nādār* (WLC). The
narrator does not name the act. The vow named a burnt offering (31). The narrator states only that the
vow was done.

**What did he do?** There are two readings, and the wiki keeps both.

- **He offered her as a burnt offering.** The vow said *I will offer it up for a burnt offering* (31),
  and 11:39 says he did what he vowed. The daughter asks for two months to mourn before it, and the
  annual lament makes most sense after a death. This is the plain sense, and it is how
  [[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]] has read the passage.
- **He dedicated her to Yahweh unmarried for life.** This reading rests on *she was a virgin*, *wəhîʾ
  lōʾ-yādəʿâ ʾîš*, *she had not known a man* (39). It is placed right after the vow is done, as though
  it said what was done. It also rests on her mourning her *virginity* rather than her life, and on the
  *or* reading of verse 31. The wiki recalls this reading from medieval Jewish commentators and later
  Christian ones without verifying the attributions. It has no other support in the text.

Neither is stated. The narrator, who names the burnt offering in the vow, does not name it in the
fulfillment. Against the first reading stand the laws: *you shall not give any of your children as a
sacrifice to Molech* (Leviticus 18:21), and Deuteronomy's *they even burn their sons and their daughters
in the fire to their gods* (12:31), said of the nations. Judges reports what happened and does not
condemn it. For Gideon's ephod it gave a verdict in the same verse, *a snare* (8:27); here it gives none. The passage
does not say Yahweh accepted the vow, and it does not say he rejected it.

*It was a custom in Israel*, *wattəhî ḥōq bəyiśrāʾēl* (WLC). *Ḥōq* is *statute*, the word of the laws in
Exodus through Deuteronomy, and of *a statute and an ordinance* that Joshua made at Shechem (Joshua
24:25). It occurs only here in Judges. The verb *wattəhî* is feminine, though *ḥōq* is masculine. The
subject may be *it*, the matter, with the feminine as the neuter. Or it may be *she*: *she became a
statute in Israel*. The Hebrew allows both. The WEB joins the phrase to verse 40.

*To celebrate*, *lətannôt* (*tānâ*, lemma 8567, WLC). The verb occurs otherwise in the WLC of
Genesis–Judges only in the Song of Deborah, *there they will rehearse Yahweh's righteous acts* (5:11).
It means to recount, to tell over again. The women do not go to celebrate in the sense of a feast. They
go to tell her story, four days every year.

**What the passage does.** Each of the passage's movements uses a word from the one before it. The vow's
*whatever comes out … to meet me* is answered by *his daughter came out to meet him*. His *when I
return* is answered by *I can't go back*, and then by *she returned*. What comes out of his door answers
what came out of his mouth. The Spirit gave Jephthah his victory, and his words cost him his only child.
Genesis 22 told of a father told to offer his *only son* and stopped by a voice. Judges 11 tells of a
father who offered his *only* child himself, and no one stops him. Yahweh does not speak in this passage.

The book does not comment on it here. But the vow comes from the man whose call was a contract with the
elders (11:4–11), and whose speech to Ammon was a careful legal case (11:12–28). He brings the same terms
to God. In the next pericope Ephraim threatens to burn his house with fire (12:1), and the man who
argued so carefully with Ammon goes to war with Ephraim. *Forty-two thousand of Ephraim fell* at the
fords (12:6).

## Notes

**Boundaries.** The plan's 11:29–40 is kept. 11:29 starts the action that 11:28 left waiting for, and
11:40 ends the story with the custom. 12:1 begins a new episode with Ephraim.

**Readings recorded, not resolved.** (1) Whether Jephthah burned his daughter or dedicated her to
lifelong virginity (11:39). Both readings and their grounds are written out above. The wiki's earlier
page [[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]] lists the outcome as *carried out*. That is
the plain reading, and that page now notes the dispute rather than being changed to the other reading.
(2) Whether *wə-* in 11:31 means *and* or *or*. (3) Whether the subject of *wattəhî ḥōq* is *it* or
*she*. (4) Which Aroer is meant at 11:33. The names of the commentators behind the second reading of (1)
and the reading in (2) are recalled, not verified.

**No rest formula.** *Subdued* (11:33) appears without *the land had rest* for any span of years. Gideon's
*subdued* at 8:28 was followed by *forty years*. Jephthah's years are given instead as a judgeship, *six
years* (12:7; see [[Shibboleth]]).
