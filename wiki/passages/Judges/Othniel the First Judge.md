# Othniel the First Judge

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 3:7–11

## Summary

The first judge's story is five verses long, and it is the cycle of Judges 2:11–19 told once with
names in it.

**Sin (7).** *The children of Israel did that which was evil in Yahweh's sight, and forgot Yahweh
their God, and served the Baals and the Asheroth.*

**Anger and sale (8).** *Yahweh's anger burned against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of
Cushan Rishathaim king of Mesopotamia.* Israel *served* Cushan Rishathaim eight years. In verse 7
they served the Baals; in verse 8 they serve a foreign king.

**Cry and savior (9).** *When the children of Israel cried to Yahweh, Yahweh raised up a savior to the
children of Israel, who saved them, even Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother.* The
deliverer is a man the book has already met: he took Kiriath Sepher and married Achsah in 1:13.

**Spirit, judging, and war (10).** *Yahweh's Spirit came on him, and he judged Israel; and he went out
to war, and Yahweh delivered Cushan Rishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand.* No battle is
described. No place is named for it.

**Rest and death (11).** *The land had rest forty years, then Othniel the son of Kenaz died.*

Nobody speaks in the pericope. Othniel has no words, no weakness, and no story beyond the formula.
The judges after him will each add something to it, and most of what they add is trouble. Othniel is
the pattern before it frays.

## Key verses

> **3:7** The children of Israel did that which was evil in Yahweh’s sight, and forgot Yahweh their God, and served the Baals and the Asheroth.

> **3:8** Therefore Yahweh’s anger burned against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of Cushan Rishathaim king of Mesopotamia; and the children of Israel served Cushan Rishathaim eight years.

> **3:9** When the children of Israel cried to Yahweh, Yahweh raised up a savior to the children of Israel, who saved them, even Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother.

> **3:10** Yahweh’s Spirit came on him, and he judged Israel; and he went out to war, and Yahweh delivered Cushan Rishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand. His hand prevailed against Cushan Rishathaim.

> **3:11** The land had rest forty years, then Othniel the son of Kenaz died.

## Notes

**The cycle, enacted.** Judges 2:11–19 described the pattern without names. Here it happens once, in
the same words (all WLC):

| Step | Judges 2 | Judges 3:7–11 |
|---|---|---|
| Sin | *wayyaʿăśû … ʾet-hāraʿ bəʿênê YHWH* (2:11) | the same words (3:7) |
| Anger | *wayyiḥar ʾap YHWH bəyiśrāʾēl* (2:14) | the same words (3:8) |
| Sale | *wayyimkərēm bəyad ʾôyəbêhem* (2:14) | *wayyimkərēm bəyad kûšan rišʿātayim* (3:8) |
| Raised up | *wayyāqem YHWH šōpəṭîm* (2:16) | *wayyāqem YHWH môšîaʿ* (3:9) |
| Saved | *wayyôšîʿûm* (2:16) | *wayyôšîʿēm* (3:9) |

Where Judges 2 had *their enemies*, Judges 3 has a name. Where Judges 2 had *judges*, Judges 3 has *a
savior*, *môšîaʿ*, and then says *he judged Israel* (*wayyišpōṭ*, 10). Othniel carries both titles.

**What chapter 2 did not have.** Two steps are new.

The first is the **cry**: *wayyizʿăqû … ʾel-YHWH* (9, WLC). Judges 2:18 spoke of Israel's *groaning*,
*naʾăqātām*, and said Yahweh was *grieved* by it. It did not say Israel called on him. Here they do.
The verb *zāʿaq* is the one used of Israel in Egypt: *they cried, and their cry came up to God*
(Exodus 2:23, *wayyizʿāqû*, WLC; stored WEB). The book will use it again at 3:15, for Ehud, and at
10:10, where the cry for the first time has words: *We have sinned against you* (stored WEB). Here the
cry has no recorded words. The text does not say whether it was repentance.

The second is the **Spirit**, below.

**"Forgot."** Judges 2:12–13 said Israel *forsook* Yahweh (*ʿāzab*). Judges 3:7 says they *forgot*
him, *wayyiškəḥû ʾet-YHWH ʾĕlōhêhem* (WLC). That is the verb of Moses' warning in Deuteronomy 8:
*Beware lest you forget Yahweh your God* (8:11, *pen-tiškaḥ*, WLC), and *if you shall forget Yahweh
your God, and walk after other gods, and serve them … you shall surely perish* (8:19, stored WEB).
Judges 3:7 has the same three things in the same order: forgot Yahweh, served other gods. In 2:10 the
new generation *didn't know Yahweh*. Now they have forgotten him.

**Served, twice.** *Wayyaʿabdû ʾet-habbəʿālîm* (7) and *wayyaʿabdû … ʾet-kûšan rišʿātayim* (8, WLC).
The same verb, *ʿābad*, two verses apart. Israel chose whom to serve, and then served someone it did
not choose. Joshua 24:15 had asked the people to *choose today whom you will serve* (stored WEB). The
narrator makes no comment on the repetition.

**The Asheroth.** *Hāʾăšērôt* (7, WLC). The law named *Asherah poles* as objects to cut down or burn
(Exodus 34:13; Deuteronomy 7:5; 12:3) and forbade planting *an Asherah of any kind of tree* beside
Yahweh's altar (Deuteronomy 16:21, stored WEB). Here the word stands beside *the Baals* as something
Israel *served*. Whether it means a goddess, her cult objects, or both cannot be settled from this
verse; see [[Asherah]]. The form is also unusual. In the WLC files read for this ingest (Exodus,
Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges), the plural elsewhere ends in *-îm* (Deuteronomy 7:5; 12:3;
Exodus 34:13 has a suffixed form), and Judges 3:7 alone has *-ôt*, the ending of *the Ashtaroth*,
*ʿaštārôt*, at 2:13. Some read 3:7's form as a mix of the two names. That is reported, not checked
here. Gideon will cut down an Asherah in 6:25 (stored WEB).

**Cushan Rishathaim.** The name is given in full four times in three verses (8 twice, 10 twice; WLC).
*Rišʿātayim* ends in *-ayim*, the same ending as *nahărayim*, *two rivers*, in his title: *kûšan
rišʿātayim melek ʾăram nahărāyim* (8). The noun *rišʿâ* means *wickedness*, and the name is commonly
read as *Cushan of double wickedness*, a mocking name rather than the king's own. That reading is
reported, not checked. What his real name was, where he ruled, and how a king from upper Mesopotamia
came to rule Israel are not said. See [[Cushan Rishathaim]].

**"Mesopotamia," twice, in two forms.** The WEB says *king of Mesopotamia* in both 3:8 and 3:10. The
Hebrew differs. Verse 8 has *melek ʾăram nahărāyim*, *king of Aram of the two rivers*. Verse 10 has
only *melek ʾărām*, *king of Aram* (WLC). The WEB has evened the two out. *Aram Naharaim* is Rebekah's
country (Genesis 24:10) and Balaam's (Deuteronomy 23:4). See [[Aram Naharaim]].

**Caleb's younger brother.** *ʾĂḥî kālēb haqqāṭōn mimmennû* (9, WLC), the same four words as 1:13.
The question noted on [[Othniel]] stands: the Hebrew could make *younger* describe Kenaz, which would
make Othniel Caleb's nephew. The WEB attaches it to Othniel, and the wiki does not decide.

Caleb is called *the Kenizzite* (Numbers 32:12; Joshua 14:6, 14; stored WEB), and Othniel is *the son
of Kenaz*. The book's first judge comes from Caleb's house, which was of Judah by allotment and
Kenizzite by name. Judah *went up first* in 1:2, and the first judge comes from a house settled in
Judah's lot (Joshua 15:13).

**The first judge is a man already proved.** Othniel took a city in 1:13 and was given a wife for it.
When he is raised up in 3:9, the reader already knows what he can do. No later judge in the book is
shown in action before his or her own story begins.

**"Yahweh's Spirit came on him."** *Wattəhî ʿālāyw rûaḥ YHWH* (10, WLC). This is the first time in
Judges the Spirit is named. The same clause, with *ʾĕlōhîm*, describes Balaam: *the Spirit of God
came on him* (Numbers 24:2, *wattəhî ʿālāyw rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm*, WLC; stored WEB). It will return for
Jephthah, *Yahweh's Spirit came on Jephthah* (11:29, stored WEB). For Gideon (6:34) and Samson (14:6)
the verbs are different (WLC). On Balaam the Spirit produced an oracle; on Othniel it produces
judging and war. See [[The Spirit Came on Him - Balaam, Othniel, and Jephthah]].

**"His hand prevailed."** *Wattāʿoz yādô* (10, WLC), from *ʿāzaz*, *to be strong*. The verse turns on
*hand*. Yahweh *sold them into the hand* of Cushan (8, *bəyad*). Yahweh *delivered Cushan … into his
hand* (10, *bəyādô*). Then *his hand prevailed*. The hand that held Israel is replaced by the hand of
Israel's savior. The whole victory is told in that verse.

**"The land had rest forty years."** *Wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ ʾarbāʿîm šānâ* (11, WLC). The verb *šāqaṭ*
first came in Joshua: *The land had rest from war* (Joshua 11:23; 14:15, stored WEB). Judges repeats
the formula after Ehud (3:30, eighty years), after Deborah (5:31, forty), and after Gideon (8:28,
forty). It does not use it again after Gideon. The *forty* may be a round number for a generation.
That is a common reading, not something the text says. See
[[The Land Had Rest - Joshua 11.23, 14.15, and the Judges Formula]].

**Framed by his father's name.** *Othniel the son of Kenaz* comes in verse 9 and again in verse 11,
the last words of the pericope. The man is named, and then named again at his death. Between the two
lies everything he does.

**What happens after.** The next verse begins *The children of Israel again did that which was evil*
(3:12, stored WEB), *wayyōsipû* (WLC). Judges 2:19 said that *when the judge was dead … they turned
back*. Othniel dies in 3:11 and Israel turns back in 3:12, as 2:19 said.

**Kenaz gets no page.** He is named four times now (Joshua 15:17; Judges 1:13; 3:9, 11) and only as
Othniel's father. Genesis 36 has an Edomite chief of the same name, so a page would need a qualifier
from the start. The decision is recorded in the log.
