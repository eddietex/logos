# The Tribes That Did Not Drive Out

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 1:22–36

## Summary

The house of Joseph goes up against Bethel, and *Yahweh was with them* (22). They send men to spy out
the city, whose name before was Luz (23). The watchers see a man coming out and ask him to show them
the way in, promising, *we will deal kindly with you* (24). He shows them; they strike the city with
the sword but let the man and his whole family go (25). He goes to the land of the Hittites, builds a
city, and calls it Luz, *which is its name to this day* (26).

Then the chapter becomes a list, tribe by tribe, of what was not done. Manasseh does not drive out
Beth Shean, Taanach, Dor, Ibleam, or Megiddo and their towns, *but the Canaanites would dwell in that
land* (27). When Israel grows strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor *and did not utterly
drive them out* (28). Ephraim does not drive out the Canaanites of Gezer, who live *among them* (29).
Zebulun does not drive out Kitron or Nahalol; the Canaanites live among them and become forced
laborers (30). Asher does not drive out Acco, Sidon, Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphik, or Rehob, and *the
Asherites lived among the Canaanites* (31–32). Naphtali does not drive out Beth Shemesh or Beth Anath,
and *lived among the Canaanites*, though those two cities become forced laborers (33).

Last, Dan is not the subject of a *did not* at all. The Amorites force the children of Dan into the hill
country and will not let them come down to the valley (34). The Amorites *would dwell* in Mount Heres,
Aijalon, and Shaalbim, until the hand of the house of Joseph prevails and they too become forced
laborers (35). The chapter closes on the Amorite border, *from the ascent of Akrabbim, from the rock,
and upward* (36).

## Notes

**One victory, then the list.** The pericope has two halves of very different kinds. Verses 22–26 are
a story, the only narrated campaign in the chapter outside Judah's. Verses 27–36 are a register, and
nearly every line in it has the same verb. The house of Joseph opens with the same assurance Judah had
in 1:19: *Yahweh was with them* (22). Then the chapter reports what six tribes did not do.

**The Hebrew of the refrain.** Every tribal line from 27 to 33 has *lōʾ hôrîš*, *did not drive out*, the
hiphil of *yāraš* without any verb of ability. Verses 28 and 32 add *hôrîšô*, *drive him (them)
out*. Verse 28 has the emphatic double form *wəhôrêš lōʾ hôrîšô*, literally *and driving out, he did not
drive them out*, which the WEB renders *did not utterly drive them out*. Joshua 17:13 has the same
phrase word for word. Joshua 17:12, on the same five cities of Manasseh, has *wəlōʾ yāḵəlû … ləhôrîš*,
*they were not able to drive out*. Judges 1:27 has no *yāḵōl*. All read in the WLC (OpenScriptures
morphhb), 2026-11-13. So where Joshua says Manasseh *could not*, Judges says Manasseh *did not*, about
the same cities. The text does not say which statement is more exact, or whether Judges intends a
charge Joshua did not make. Judges 2:2–3 will give Yahweh's answer: *you have not listened to my
voice … I will not drive them out from before you* (stored WEB).

**"The Canaanites would dwell."** The WEB's *would* in verse 27 (and *would dwell* of the Amorites in
35) translates *wayyôʾel*, from *yāʾal*, *to be willing, to be determined, to undertake* (WLC; the
gloss is the usual lexical one). It is the Canaanites' resolve, not a future tense. Joshua 17:12 has
the same verb in the same clause. Two wills are set against each other in the verse: the Canaanites
determined to stay, and Manasseh not driving them out.

**Who lives among whom.** The register shifts in a way the WEB keeps and the Hebrew sharpens. In
verses 29–30 the Canaanites live *bəqirbô*, *in his midst*, inside Ephraim and Zebulun. In verses 32–33
the subject flips: *the Asherites lived among the Canaanites* and Naphtali *lived among the
Canaanites, the inhabitants of the land*, *bəqereb hakkənaʿănî* (WLC). For Asher and Naphtali, Israel
is the minority in the land. Then in 34 Dan is not even *among*; it is pushed out. The list runs from
partial possession to displacement, and it runs north and west as it goes. Whether that ordering is
deliberate the text does not say, but the movement is in the verses.

**Forced labor.** *Mas* (forced labor) is the outcome named four times (28, 30, 33, 35). Joshua 16:10
and 17:13 had already recorded it at Gezer and in Manasseh. Deuteronomy 20:11 provides forced labor
for a far city that answers in peace, but 20:16–17 excludes *the cities of these peoples, that Yahweh
your God gives you for an inheritance*, naming the Hittite, Amorite, Canaanite, Perizzite, Hivite, and
Jebusite (stored WEB). Deuteronomy 7:2 forbids covenant with them. The register therefore records an
arrangement the law had made for other cities. Judges 1 itself does not comment; the next pericope
does.

**Gezer and the other Joshua parallels.** Judges 1:27–28 is close to Joshua 17:11–13, though Joshua's
list also names Endor. Judges 1:29 is close to Joshua 16:10, though Joshua adds *to this day* and
forced labor, and Judges has neither. So in Judges, Gezer is the one city in the list whose Canaanites
are not said to become forced laborers. That is an observation from the stored WEB and the WLC; it may
mean nothing.

**Zebulun's and Asher's names.** Several of the cities appear under different spellings in Joshua 19.
The WLC's lemma tagging treats three pairs as the same word: Judges' *Nahalol* (*nahălōl*) and Joshua
19:15's *Nahalal*; Judges' *Aphik* (*ʾăpîq*) and Joshua 19:30's *Aphek*; and Judges' *Shaalbim* and
Joshua 19:42's *Shaalabbin*. A shared lexical tag shows the same name, not necessarily the same
site, but the setting in each case is the same tribe, so the wiki files them on the existing pages.
*Kitron* (*qiṭrôn*) is tagged differently from Joshua 19:15's *Kattath*, and it is often said to be
the same town. That identification is not made here. *Acco*, *Ahlab*, and *Helbah* are not in Asher's
list in Joshua 19 as the WEB reads it, though Sidon (19:28), Achzib (29), Aphek, and Rehob (30) are.
Whether any of the three stands behind another name in that list is not tested here.

**The man of Luz and Rahab.** The Bethel story repeats the shape of Jericho in Joshua 2: spies sent, a
Canaanite who helps them, a promise, a city struck, and a household spared. The promise even uses the
same words. Judges 1:24 has *wəʿāśînû ʿimməḵā ḥāsed*, *we will deal kindly with you*; Joshua 2:14 has
*wəʿāśînû ʿimmāḵ ḥesed weʾĕmet*, *we will deal kindly and truly with you* (WLC). The differences are as
telling. Rahab acts first and asks for an oath by Yahweh (Joshua 2:12); the man of Luz is asked, and
no oath is reported. Rahab *lives in the middle of Israel to this day* (Joshua 6:25,
*bəqereb yiśrāʾēl*); the man leaves for the land of the Hittites and rebuilds what was destroyed,
under its old name. Even the verb for spying differs: Joshua 2:1 has *məraggəlîm*, from *rāgal*; Judges
1:23 has *wayyātîrû*, from *tûr*, the verb of Numbers 13:2's twelve spies (WLC). See
[[The Spies at Jericho and at Bethel - Joshua 2 and Judges 1]].

**Luz, twice.** Genesis 28:19 says Jacob *called the name of that place Bethel, but the name of the
city was Luz at the first*, and Genesis 35:6 and 48:3 keep the old name. Joshua 16:2 runs a border
*from Bethel to Luz*, as if they were two places, and Joshua 18:13 identifies them. Judges 1:23
identifies them again. And verse 26 then puts the name Luz on a different city altogether, *in the
land of the Hittites*, *to this day*. The wiki does not locate that land. The text gives no other
notice of the second Luz.

**Dan.** Joshua 19:47 says Dan's border *went out beyond them*, and that Dan took Leshem and named it
Dan. Judges 1:34 gives the reason Dan needed to go: the Amorites *would not allow them to come down to
the valley*. Judges 18:1 will say *their inheritance had not fallen to them among the tribes of
Israel* (stored WEB). The verb in 1:34 is *wayyilḥăṣû*, *they pressed, oppressed* (WLC, *lāḥaṣ*), the
verb of Exodus 3:9, *the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them*. That echo is noted from the
lemma, not compared verse by verse here. See
[[Dan Pressed from the Valley - Joshua 19, Judges 1, and Judges 18]].

**The valley again.** Judah could not take *the valley, because they had chariots of iron* (1:19). Dan
is kept from *the valley* (*hāʿēmeq*, 1:34). The chapter's two failures in narrative form both happen
at the valley's edge, and the hill country is what Israel holds.

**"The hand of the house of Joseph prevailed."** Literally *was heavy* (*wattiḵbad yad*, WLC). The
house of Joseph that opened the pericope by taking Bethel closes it by subjecting the Amorites near Dan.
But what it achieves is forced labor, not removal.

**The Amorite border.** The last verse gives the border *from the ascent of Akrabbim, from the rock,
and upward* (36). The ascent of Akrabbim is a landmark of the southern border in Numbers 34:4 and
Joshua 15:3 (stored WEB). *The rock* is *hasselaʿ* (WLC); some read it as a place name, Sela. The
verse's sense, and why an Amorite border in the far south closes a chapter that ended in Dan and the
north, are not explained. Some manuscripts and commentators read *Edomite* for *Amorite* here; that is
reported as a claim and has not been checked.
