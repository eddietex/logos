# The Angel at Bochim

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 2:1–5

## Summary

Yahweh's angel comes up from Gilgal to Bochim (1). He speaks in Yahweh's first person. *I* brought
you out of Egypt, *I* brought you to the land sworn to your fathers, and *I* said, *I will never break
my covenant with you* (1). In return Israel was to make no covenant with the inhabitants of the land
and to break down their altars. *But you have not listened to my voice. Why have you done this?* (2).
So, he says, *I also said*: Yahweh will not drive the peoples out, *but they shall be in your sides,
and their gods will be a snare to you* (3).

When the angel has spoken *to all the children of Israel*, the people lift up their voice and weep
(4). They name the place Bochim, *weepers*, and they sacrifice there to Yahweh (5). The pericope ends
there. Nothing says whether the weeping changed anything, and the next verse goes back to Joshua
sending the people away (2:6).

## Key verses

> **2:1** Yahweh’s angel came up from Gilgal to Bochim. He said, “I brought you out of Egypt, and have brought you to the land which I swore to give your fathers. I said, ‘I will never break my covenant with you.

> **2:2** You shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land. You shall break down their altars.’ But you have not listened to my voice. Why have you done this?

> **2:3** Therefore I also said, ‘I will not drive them out from before you; but they shall be in your sides, and their gods will be a snare to you.’”

> **2:4** When Yahweh’s angel spoke these words to all the children of Israel, the people lifted up their voice, and wept.

> **2:5** They called the name of that place Bochim, and they sacrificed there to Yahweh.

## Notes

**Where this sits in time.** Judges 1:1 began *after the death of Joshua*. Judges 2:6 then goes back
to Joshua sending the people away, nearly word for word from Joshua 24:28, and 2:8 tells his death
again. So the angel at Bochim is placed between a chapter set after Joshua and a retelling that
begins before his death. The text gives Bochim no date. It reads as the verdict on chapter 1, set
before the book's second introduction (2:6–3:6). Whether it happened after Joshua died, the book
does not say.

**The angel speaks as Yahweh.** The speaker is *malʾak YHWH*, *Yahweh's messenger*. Every verb in
the speech is first person: *I brought you up*, *I swore*, *I said*, *my covenant*, *my voice*, *I
will not drive them out* (WLC). There is no *thus says Yahweh*. Judges 6:8 does use that formula, for
a prophet who is plainly sent with Yahweh's words. At Bochim the messenger simply says *I*. This is
the same alternation [[The Angel of Yahweh]] records from Genesis 16 on, and it is one of the
plainest cases. The wiki records it and does not resolve it.

**Up from Gilgal.** Gilgal was Israel's camp in Joshua 4–14. The circumcision, the first Passover in
the land, and the end of the manna happened there. The commander of Yahweh's army stood before
Joshua *by Jericho* (Joshua 5:13), near that camp. The angel now *came up* (*wayyaʿal*) *from Gilgal*,
as though leaving the place where the conquest began. The text gives no reason for the route. One
detail in the Hebrew is worth noting. The angel *went up* (*wayyaʿal*, qal of *ʿālâ*), and his first
words are *I brought you up* (*ʾaʿăleh*, hiphil of the same verb; WLC). The WEB's *I brought you
out* hides the repeated verb.

**An unusual verb form.** *ʾaʿăleh* is parsed in the OpenScriptures morphology as a hiphil imperfect,
first person (`HVhi1cs`). A past *I brought you up* would usually be a perfect. Judges 6:8 has the
perfect, *heʿĕlêtî* (`HVhp1cs`; both WLC). How to read the imperfect at 2:1 is a question for the
grammars, and the wiki does not settle it. The WEB and most English versions translate it as past.

**"I will never break my covenant."** *Lōʾ ʾāpēr bərîtî ʾittəkem ləʿôlām* (WLC), hiphil of *pārar*,
*to break, annul*. Leviticus 26:44 has the same verb in the same kind of promise: even in exile,
Yahweh will not *break my covenant with them*, *ləhāpēr bərîtî ʾittām* (stored WEB; WLC). The speech sets that promise first. Then
comes Israel's part, stated as two commands, and the charge that Israel did not listen. The
sentence that follows withdraws one promise: the driving out. It does not withdraw the covenant.
Judges 2:20 will say the reverse of Israel: *this nation transgressed my covenant* (stored WEB).

**Three echoes of the law, nearly word for word.** Every clause of verse 2 and the end of verse 3
come from the Torah's own wording (all WLC):

| Judges 2 | Earlier text |
|---|---|
| *lōʾ tikrətû bərît ləyôšəbê hāʾāreṣ*, *make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land* (2) | *pen tikrōt bərît ləyôšēb hāʾāreṣ*, *lest you make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land* (Exodus 34:12); *lōʾ tikrōt lāhem bərît* (Deuteronomy 7:2) |
| *mizbəḥôtêhem tittōṣûn*, *you shall break down their altars* (2) | the same noun and the same verb form, *tittōṣûn* (Exodus 34:13; Deuteronomy 7:5) |
| *ʾĕlōhêhem yihyû lāḵem ləmôqēš*, *their gods will be a snare to you* (3) | *ʾet ʾĕlōhêhem kî yihyeh ləḵā ləmôqēš* (Exodus 23:33); *môqēš* also at Exodus 34:12, Deuteronomy 7:16, and Joshua 23:13 |

The angel is not adding a new law. He is quoting the old one back.

**Whose verb is "drive out."** Judges 1 used one verb for what the tribes *did not* do: *yāraš*,
hiphil, *drive out, dispossess*. Verse 3 uses a different verb for what Yahweh will not do:
*lōʾ ʾăgārēš ʾôtām mippənêkem*, piel of *gāraš* (WLC). That is the verb of Yahweh's own promise in
Exodus 23. There he said *wəgērəšâ*, the hornet *will drive out* the peoples (23:28), and
*ʾăgārəšennû*, *I will drive them out*, *little by little* (23:30). He also said
*lōʾ ʾăgārəšennû mippāneyḵā bəšānâ ʾeḥāt*, *I will not drive them out from before you in one year*
(23:29). Judges 2:3 says *lōʾ ʾăgārēš ʾôtām mippənêkem*, the same negated first-person piel, but
without *in one year*. Exodus 34:11 has *hinənî gōrēš*, *behold, I am driving out*, again *gāraš*
(all WLC). So the angel's sentence withdraws, in the same words, the promise Exodus made. It does
not use the verb of Israel's own failure. Judges 2:21 then returns to *yāraš*, *ləhôrîš* (WLC). How
much the change of verb matters is a matter of reading; that the verbs differ is in the text.

**"In your sides."** The Hebrew of verse 3 has *wəhāyû lāḵem ləṣiddîm*, literally *and they will be
to you for sides* (WLC; `ṣiddîm` is parsed as plural absolute, with no *your*). The WEB supplies
*in your sides*. It is reading the verse by Numbers 33:55, *as thorns in your sides*
(*liṣnînim bəṣiddêḵem*), and Joshua 23:13, *a scourge in your sides* (*ləšōṭēṭ bəṣiddêḵem*). Both
of those name the thing that will be in the side; Judges 2:3 names only the sides. Some interpreters
supply *thorns* from Numbers, and some propose a different Hebrew word altogether, *ṣārîm*,
*adversaries*. That proposal is reported here as a claim; it has not been checked against any ancient
version.

**"I also said."** *Wəgam ʾāmartî* (WLC), a perfect. It can be read as a reference back to an earlier
warning (Numbers 33:55 and Joshua 23:13 both made the threat) or as a sentence pronounced now, *so I
now say*. The WEB's *Therefore I also said* keeps both readings open. Either way, the sentence matches
the earlier warnings. Joshua 23:13 said *Yahweh your God will no longer drive these nations from out
of your sight* if Israel clung to them (stored WEB). Judges 2:3 says it has come to that.

**A different reason from Exodus.** [[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]]
sets out two explanations for the peoples who stayed. Exodus 23:29–30 and Deuteronomy 7:22 give a
slow conquest for the land's sake. Judges 2:3 gives a punishment for not listening. Judges 2:22 and
3:1–4 will add a third: to test Israel (stored WEB). Bochim is the second of these. The wiki does not
merge them.

**"Why have you done this?"** *Mah zōʾt ʿăśîtem* (WLC). It is the question Yahweh God asked the woman
in Genesis 3:13, *What have you done?* (stored WEB), there in the singular, *mah zōʾt ʿāśît* (WLC). The comparison is offered as an echo of form, not as a claim that the author meant
it. The angel asks and gets no answer in words, only weeping.

**The weeping.** *Wayyiśʾû hāʿām ʾet qôlām wayyibkû*, *the people lifted up their voice and wept* (4,
WLC). The same idiom returns at Bethel at the end of the book, in Judges 21:2,
*wayyiśʾû qôlām wayyibkû bəkî gādôl*, *and lifted up their voices, and wept severely* (WLC; stored
WEB). In both places the people weep and then sacrifice. The text does not say whether the weeping
at Bochim was repentance. No confession is reported. Nothing is said about the altars, and the
peoples stay. The next pericope's summary, *they abandoned Yahweh* (2:12–13, stored WEB), suggests it
was not lasting.

**Named before it is named.** Verse 1 already calls the place *habbōkîm*, *the Bochim*, with the
article. Verse 5 then tells how it got that name. The narrator uses the later name in advance.
*Bōkîm* is the participle of *bākâ*, *to weep*: *weepers*, or *those who weep* (WLC). See [[Bochim]].

**Where Bochim was.** The text does not locate it. The Septuagint of Judges 2:1 is often reported to
add Bethel, sending the angel *to Klauthmōn and to Bethel and to the house of Israel*. That reading is
a claim, not checked here against an edition of the Septuagint. If it is right, Bochim would be near
the *Allon Bacuth*, the *oak of weeping* below Bethel, where Rebekah's nurse Deborah was buried
(Genesis 35:8). Its name comes from the same root, *bākût* (WLC). And Bethel is where Israel weeps
and sacrifices again in Judges 20–21. See
[[The Place of Weepers - Allon Bacuth, Bochim, and Bethel]]. The wiki does not identify the three
places.

**Sacrifice away from Shiloh.** *Wayyizbəḥû šām la-YHWH*, *they sacrificed there to Yahweh* (5,
WLC). Joshua 18:1 set up the tent of meeting at Shiloh (stored WEB). Deuteronomy 12 limits offerings
to *the place which Yahweh your God shall choose* (12:11, stored WEB). Judges 2:5 reports sacrifice at Bochim without
comment, and Judges 20:26–27 will report sacrifice at Bethel, *for the ark of the covenant of God was
there in those days* (stored WEB). Whether the ark or the tent was ever at Bochim the text does not
say, and the wiki does not assume it.

**The angel and Exodus 23.** Exodus 23:20–22 sent an angel ahead of Israel with a condition: *listen
to his voice … if you indeed listen to his voice, and do all that I speak* (stored WEB). At Bochim an
angel says *you have not listened to my voice*. The text does not say whether he is the same angel.
The match between the condition and the charge is in the wording. See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]].
