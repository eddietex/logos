# Abimelech Made King at Shechem

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 9:1–6

## Summary

Six verses take Gideon's son from Shechem's family table to a throne. Abimelech goes to his mother's
kin and asks them to put one question to the town: seventy rulers, or one? He adds a reason to choose
him, *I am your bone and your flesh* (1–2). The town agrees, *He is our brother* (3). It pays him
seventy of silver from the house of Baal Berith, and he hires men with it (4). He goes to Ophrah and
kills his seventy brothers *on one stone*. Only the youngest, Jotham, survives, because he hid (5).
Then Shechem makes him king by the oak of the pillar (6).

No judge is raised here, and nobody cries out to Yahweh. God is not named until 9:7, and then only in
Jotham's mouth. The previous pericope ended with two charges against Israel: it *didn't remember
Yahweh*, and it showed no *kindness to the house of Jerubbaal* (8:34–35). This pericope shows the
second charge being carried out, by Gideon's son and the town his mother came from.

**The question put to Shechem (9:1–2).**

> **9:1** Abimelech the son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem to his mother's brothers, and spoke with them, and with all the family of the house of his mother's father, saying,

> **9:2** "Please speak in the ears of all the men of Shechem, 'Is it better for you that all the sons of Jerubbaal, who are seventy persons, rule over you, or that one rule over you?' Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh."

He is *Abimelech the son of Jerubbaal*. Chapter 9 never uses the name Gideon, only *Jerubbaal*, *Let Baal
contend*. He goes to [[Shechem (city)]] and to his *mother's brothers*, *all the family of the house of
his mother's father*. Gideon's house is in Ophrah. Abimelech goes to the other house, his mother's.

His question uses *rule*, *māšal*, twice: *Is it better … that … seventy persons rule over you, or that
one rule over you?* (WLC). Gideon used that verb three times to refuse: *I will not rule over you,
neither shall my son rule over you. Yahweh shall rule over you* (8:23). Abimelech is one of those sons.
His question offers two choices, and the third, *Yahweh shall rule*, is not among them. Nothing in the
text says the seventy were ruling Shechem, or wanted to. The question takes it for granted. See
[[Yahweh Shall Rule Over You - Judges 8.23 and 1 Samuel 8.7]].

*The men of Shechem* is *baʿălê šəkem*, literally *the lords* or *owners of Shechem*. *Baʿal*, *owner*
or *lord*, is the word in the god's name, *Baal Berith*. Chapter 9 uses the phrase over and over. In
Genesis–Judges the WLC has *baʿălê* with a city's name elsewhere only in *the men of Jericho* (Joshua
24:11), and *baʿălê bərît*, *covenant partners*, of Abram's allies (Genesis 14:13), which WEB renders
*allies of Abram* (see the Notes).

*Remember also*, *ûzəkartem*. It is the verb of the verse before, *The children of Israel didn't
remember Yahweh their God* (8:34). Israel has stopped remembering Yahweh. Abimelech asks Shechem to
remember that he is their kin. *I am your bone and your flesh*, *ʿaṣmēkem ûbəśarkem*, is the phrase of
kinship. Laban uses it to welcome Jacob, *Surely you are my bone and my flesh* (Genesis 29:14). The man
says it first, of the woman, *bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh* (Genesis 2:23). Israel's tribes
will say it to David at Hebron when they make him king, *we are your bone and your flesh* (2 Samuel
5:1, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested). See
[[Your Bone and Your Flesh - Genesis 29.14, Judges 9.2, and 2 Samuel 5.1]].

**"He is our brother" (9:3).**

> **9:3** His mother's brothers spoke of him in the ears of all the men of Shechem all these words. Their hearts inclined to follow Abimelech; for they said, "He is our brother."

*Their hearts inclined to follow Abimelech*, *wayyēṭ libbām ʾaḥărê ʾăbîmelek*. In the WLC of
Genesis–Judges *nāṭâ* with *lēb* occurs only here (see the Notes). *To follow*, *ʾaḥărê*, is the
preposition of 8:33, where Israel *played the prostitute following the Baals*. The heart goes *after*
Abimelech the way Israel went *after* the Baals.

*For they said, "He is our brother"*, *ʾāḥînû hûʾ*. The same two words are in Judah's speech at Dothan:
*let's sell him to the Ishmaelites, and not let our hand be on him; for he is our brother, our flesh*
(Genesis 37:27). Judah used kinship to argue against killing a brother. Shechem uses kinship to choose
the man who will kill his brothers. Jotham will repeat the reason back to them, *because he is your
brother* (9:18, stored WEB).

The law of the king asks for a brother too: *You shall set as king over you one from among your
brothers. You may not put a foreigner over you, who is not your brother* (Deuteronomy 17:15). The
same verse comes first, though, with *whom Yahweh your God chooses*. Shechem's choice meets the
brother clause, but no one in this scene asks Yahweh. See [[The Law for the King]].

**Seventy of silver (9:4).**

> **9:4** They gave him seventy pieces of silver out of the house of Baal Berith, with which Abimelech hired vain and light fellows, who followed him.

*Seventy pieces of silver* is *šibʿîm kesep*, *seventy of silver*; WEB supplies *pieces*, as it does in
Joseph's sale, *for twenty pieces of silver* (Genesis 37:28), where the Hebrew is the same construction,
*bəʿeśrîm kāsep* (WLC). The number matches the number of brothers. The text does not say it is a price
per head. See [[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]].

The silver comes *out of the house of Baal Berith*. Two verses after his death (8:33), Israel's new
god turns out to have a house in Shechem and money in it. This is the first time the book shows a
sanctuary's silver paying for violence. See [[Baal Berith]].

*He hired*, *wayyiśkōr*, *vain and light fellows*, *ʾănāšîm rêqîm ûpōḥăzîm*. *Rêqîm* is *empty*. It is
the word for Joseph's pit, *The pit was empty* (Genesis 37:24), and for Gideon's *empty pitchers*
(7:16). Jephthah will collect the same kind of men, *ʾănāšîm rêqîm* (11:3), which WEB there renders
*Outlaws* (stored WEB; not yet ingested). *Pōḥăzîm*, *reckless* or *wanton*, is the root of Reuben's
*Boiling over like water* (Genesis 49:4), the only other occurrence of the root in Genesis–Judges (see
the Notes). *Who followed him* is *ʾaḥărāyw* again, *after him*. Shechem's heart followed him, and now
his hired men follow him too.

**On one stone (9:5).**

> **9:5** He went to his father's house at Ophrah, and killed his brothers the sons of Jerubbaal, being seventy persons, on one stone: but Jotham the youngest son of Jerubbaal was left; for he hid himself.

He goes *to his father's house at Ophrah*, [[Ophrah (Manasseh)]], where Gideon was called, threw down
the Baal altar, set up the ephod, and was buried. Now Gideon's son kills Gideon's sons there.
*Killed*, *wayyahărōg*, is *hārag*, the verb of Cain: *Cain rose up against Abel, his brother, and killed
him* (Genesis 4:8). The same verse that says *his brothers* says *the sons of Jerubbaal*. They are his
brothers, and he has just been chosen because he is Shechem's brother.

*Seventy persons* is *šibʿîm ʾîš*, *seventy men*, the phrase of 9:2. In Genesis–Judges the WLC has it
otherwise only of the seventy elders Moses gathers (Numbers 11:16, 24, 25) (see the Notes). Gideon's
house, like Jacob's, numbered seventy (8:30; Exodus 1:5). See
[[Seventy Souls - The Count of Jacob's House in Genesis, Exodus and Acts]].

*On one stone*, *ʿal-ʾeben ʾeḥāt*. The text does not explain the stone. It could be the place of an
execution, a slaughter stone like an altar, or simply one spot. The wiki does not choose. Jotham
repeats it (9:18), so it is meant to be remembered. A later account tells of a king's seventy sons
killed together and of one royal son hidden; see
[[Seventy Sons and One Hidden - Judges 9 and 2 Kings 10-11]].

*But Jotham the youngest son of Jerubbaal was left; for he hid himself*. *Was left*, *wayyiwwātēr*, is
the niphal of *yātar*, *to remain over*. *He hid himself*, *neḥbāʾ*, is the verb of Adam, *I was afraid,
because I was naked; and I hid myself* (Genesis 3:10), and of the five kings who *hid themselves in the
cave at Makkedah* (Joshua 10:16). Gideon threshed *to hide it from the Midianites* (6:11), but WEB's
*hide* there is a different Hebrew verb, the hiphil of *nûs*, *to flee*, with *mippənê* (WLC; the
sense *bring to safety* is the usual one, reported, not checked). Jotham is the *youngest*,
*haqqāṭōn*. Gideon called himself *the least in my father's house* (6:15), with a different adjective,
*haṣṣāʿîr* (see the Notes). See [[Jotham (son of Gideon)]].

**A king by the oak (9:6).**

> **9:6** All the men of Shechem assembled themselves together, and all the house of Millo, and went and made Abimelech king, by the oak of the pillar that was in Shechem.

*All the house of Millo*, *kol-bêt millôʾ*. *Millôʾ* is from *mālēʾ*, *to fill*, and is often taken
as an earthwork or citadel (the meaning is reported, not checked). In Genesis–Judges the word occurs
only here and in 9:20. Jotham's curse will name it twice. See [[Beth Millo (Shechem)]].

*Made Abimelech king*, *wayyamlîkû ʾet-ʾăbîmelek ləmelek*. It is the first time in Genesis–Judges that
anyone is *made king*. The hiphil of *mālak* occurs in those books only here and in Jotham's charges at
9:16 and 18 (WLC; see the Notes). The name *Abimelech* has the noun *melek* in it, *my father is king*
(the usual gloss, reported, not checked). In 8:31 Gideon set that name on his son, and here Shechem
makes it true. Gideon refused rule. His son is the first man in Israel to be made king. Deuteronomy's
law speaks of a king to be set *whom Yahweh your God chooses* (17:15), and here the choice is made by a
town with silver from Baal's house.

*By the oak of the pillar that was in Shechem*, *ʿim-ʾēlôn muṣṣāb*. *ʾĒlôn* is the word of Abram's
*oak of Moreh* at Shechem (Genesis 12:6) and of Deuteronomy's *oaks of Moreh* by Gerizim and Ebal
(11:30). *Muṣṣāb* is a hophal participle of *nāṣab*, *set up*, and WEB's *pillar* interprets it. Other
translations read *the oak of the garrison* or *the standing oak* (reported, not checked). This exact
phrase is unique (see the Notes). Shechem's other trees use other words. Jacob buried the foreign gods
*under the oak which was by Shechem* (Genesis 35:4), *hāʾēlâ*. Joshua set a great stone *under the oak
that was by the sanctuary of Yahweh* (Joshua 24:26), *hāʾallâ*. The words differ, so whether this is
the same tree cannot be shown from the wording. But a stone set up under an oak at Shechem is what
Joshua made the witness of the covenant, *lest you deny your God* (24:27). Abimelech is crowned in the
same town, at an oak, by a pillar or a set-up thing. See
[[The Altar at Shechem and the Covenant at Shechem]] and
[[Golden Earrings - Shechem's Oak, Aaron's Calf, and Gideon's Ephod]].

**How the pericope sits in Judges.** The book's cycle so far has always started with Israel's sin and
an oppressor from outside. This pericope has no oppressor from outside. The violence is inside one
family, and the money comes from a temple of Israel's new god. Gideon's *I will not rule over you,
neither shall my son rule over you* (8:23) is answered by Shechem: *one … rule over you … he is our
brother*. The book has no word of judgment yet. Jotham speaks it from Gerizim in the next pericope,
and 9:56–57 says how it ended: *God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech, which he did to his father, in
killing his seventy brothers*. See [[The Fall of Abimelech]].

## Notes

**"The men of Shechem."** *Baʿălê šəkem* (9:2, 3, 6, WLC), *baʿal* (1167) in the construct plural. In
Genesis–Judges *baʿal* (1167) occurs at Genesis 14:13; 20:3; 37:19; 49:23; Exodus 21–22 (the owner of
an ox or a pit, and the like); 24:14; Leviticus 21:4; Numbers 21:28; Deuteronomy 15:2; 22:22; 24:4;
Joshua 24:11; Judges 9 (sixteen verses: 2, 3, 6, 7, 18, 20, 23, 24, 25, 26, 39, 46, 47, 51); and Judges
19:22, 23; 20:5 (WLC). Of those, only Joshua 24:11 (*baʿălê-yərîḥô*) and Judges 9 attach it to a city;
Numbers 21:28 has *baʿălê bāmôt arnōn*, *the lords of the high places of the Arnon*. Genesis 14:13 is
*baʿălê bərît-ʾabrām* (WLC); *Baal Berith* (9:4) is filed as a separate proper-name lemma (1170).

**"Remember … bone and flesh."** *Ûzəkartem* (9:2), *zākar* (2142), as at 8:34 (WLC). *ʿEṣem* (6106)
and *bāśār* (1320) in one verse, as a kinship formula, occur in Genesis–Judges at Genesis 2:23; 29:14;
and Judges 9:2 (WLC). The search also returned Genesis 17:23 and Exodus 12:46, where the two words
occur separately and not as a pair. 2 Samuel 5:1 has *ʿaṣməkā ûbəśārəkā ʾănāḥnû* (WLC, OpenScriptures
morphhb `2Sam.xml`).

**"Their hearts inclined."** *Wayyēṭ libbām* (9:3), *nāṭâ* (5186) with *lēb* (3820) within two words;
only Judges 9:3 in Genesis–Judges (WLC). *ʾĀḥînû hûʾ* (9:3): *ʾāḥ* (251) with *hûʾ* (1931); Genesis
37:27 has *ʾāḥînû bəśārēnû hûʾ* (WLC). Deuteronomy 17:15 ends *lōʾ-ʾāḥîkā hûʾ* (WLC). Judges 9:18 has
*kî ʾăḥîkem hûʾ* (WLC).

**"Seventy of silver."** *Šibʿîm kesep* (9:4, WLC), no noun for *pieces*; Genesis 37:28 has *bəʿeśrîm
kāsep*, also without one (WLC).

**"Vain and light fellows."** *ʾĂnāšîm rêqîm ûpōḥăzîm* (9:4, WLC). *Rêq* (7386) in Genesis–Judges:
Genesis 37:24; 41:27; Deuteronomy 32:47; Judges 7:16; 9:4; 11:3 (WLC). *Pāḥaz* (6348), verb, only at
Judges 9:4; the noun *paḥaz* (6349) only at Genesis 49:4 in those books (WLC). *Wayyiśkōr*, *śākar*
(7936): Genesis 30:16; Deuteronomy 23:5; Judges 9:4; 18:4 (WLC).

**"Killed … on one stone."** *Wayyahărōg* (9:5), *hārag* (2026), as at Genesis 4:8 (WLC). *Šibʿîm ʾîš*,
*šibʿîm* (7657) immediately followed by *ʾîš* (376), occurs in Genesis–Judges at Numbers 11:16, 24, 25
and Judges 9:2, 5, 18 (WLC); 2 Kings 10:6–7 has the same pair (WLC, OpenScriptures morphhb `2Kgs.xml`).
*ʾEben ʾeḥāt*, *ʾeben* (68) immediately followed by *ʾeḥād* (259): Exodus 28:10 (*the one stone* of the
ephod's shoulder-stones, each with six names); Joshua 4:5 (*a stone on his shoulder*, one per tribe);
Judges 9:5, 18 (WLC). These are lexical matches only; the wiki draws no connection from them.

**"Was left … hid himself."** *Wayyiwwātēr*, niphal of *yātar* (3498). *Neḥbāʾ*, *ḥābāʾ* (2244); in
Genesis–Judges at Genesis 3:8, 10; 31:27; Joshua 6:17, 25; 10:16, 17, 27; Judges 9:5 (WLC). In Joshua
6:17, 25 Rahab *hid* the messengers. Judges 6:11's *to hide it* is *ləhānîs*, from *nûs* (5127), not
*ḥābāʾ* (WLC). Jotham is *haqqāṭōn* (6996); Gideon at 6:15 is *haṣṣāʿîr* (6810) (WLC). 2 Kings 11:2's
*hid him* is *wayyastīrû*, from *sātar* (5641), not *ḥābāʾ* (WLC).

**"The house of Millo."** *Bêt millôʾ* (9:6, 20 twice), *millôʾ* (4407); only these verses in
Genesis–Judges (WLC). The derivation from *mālēʾ*, *to fill*, and the sense *earthwork* or *citadel*,
are reported, not checked against a lexicon.

**"Made Abimelech king."** *Wayyamlîkû … ləmelek* (9:6), hiphil of *mālak* (4427). The hiphil of *mālak*
occurs in the WLC of Genesis–Judges only at Judges 9:6, 16, 18, found by a search of the morphology
data for every hiphil form of the lemma.

**"The oak of the pillar."** *ʿIm-ʾēlôn muṣṣāb* (9:6, WLC). *ʾĒlôn* (436) in Genesis–Judges: Genesis
12:6; 13:18; 14:13; 18:1; Deuteronomy 11:30; Judges 4:11; 9:6, 37 (WLC). *Muṣṣāb*, hophal participle of
*nāṣab* (5324); *ʾēlôn* and *nāṣab* together only in Judges 9:6 (WLC). Genesis 35:4 has *hāʾēlâ* (424);
Joshua 24:26 has *hāʾallâ* (427) (WLC). The alternative renderings *garrison* and *standing* are
reported, not checked.

**The mother.** Judges 8:31 calls her *pîlegeš*, *concubine*; Jotham will call Abimelech *the son of
his female servant*, *ben-ʾămātô* (9:18, WLC, *ʾāmâ*, 519). The difference is recorded, not resolved.

**Judgment calls.** The planned boundary was kept: 9:6 ends with the crowning, and 9:7 turns to Jotham
on Gerizim. [[Jotham (son of Gideon)]] gets a page now, qualified because a king of Judah bears the
name. *The house of Millo* gets a place page, [[Beth Millo (Shechem)]], qualified from the start
because Jerusalem has a *Millo* in the books of Samuel and Kings. The oak of the pillar is recorded on
[[Shechem (city)]] rather than on a page of its own, and its identity with the oaks of Genesis 35:4 and
Joshua 24:26 is left open. Abimelech's mother, her brothers, and the hired men get no pages. The
meaning of the stone, of *Millo*, and of *muṣṣāb* are left open.
