# Micah's Idol and Levite

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 17:1–13

## Summary

*There was a man of the hill country of Ephraim, whose name was Micah* (1). He tells his mother that the
*eleven hundred pieces of silver that were taken from you, about which you uttered a curse, and also spoke
it in my ears*, are with him: *I took it*. She answers, *May Yahweh bless my son!* (2). He gives the silver
back, and she says, *I most certainly dedicate the silver to Yahweh from my hand for my son, to make a
carved image and a molten image. Now therefore I will restore it to you* (3). When he returns it, she takes
*two hundred pieces of silver* to a silversmith, *who made a carved image and a molten image out of it. It
was in the house of Micah* (4).

*The man Micah had a house of gods, and he made an ephod, and teraphim, and consecrated one of his sons,
who became his priest* (5). The narrator stops: *In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did
that which was right in his own eyes* (6).

*A young man out of Bethlehem Judah, of the family of Judah, who was a Levite*, is living there (7). He
leaves *to live where he could find a place* and comes, *as he traveled*, to Micah's house in the hill
country of Ephraim (8). *I am a Levite of Bethlehem Judah, and I am looking for a place to live* (9). Micah
offers terms: *Dwell with me, and be to me a father and a priest, and I will give you ten pieces of silver
per year, a suit of clothing, and your food* (10). The Levite is *content*, and *the young man was to him as
one of his sons* (11). Micah consecrates him, and he becomes his priest (12). *Then Micah said, "Now know I
that Yahweh will do good to me, since I have a Levite as my priest"* (13).

**What is going on.** No judge appears, and no oppressor. The book's last five chapters turn from Israel's
enemies to Israel's own house, and the first house is one family's. Every act in it is done in Yahweh's
name: a theft confessed, a curse answered with a blessing, silver dedicated *to Yahweh*, a priest hired,
and a confidence that *Yahweh will do good to me*. And every act runs against the law the wiki has already
read. The image is the thing Deuteronomy 27:15 curses by name; the shrine is a house of its own, not *the
place which Yahweh shall choose*; the priest is first a son of Ephraim and then a Levite paid by the year.
The narrator gives one verdict, in verse 6, and it is not a word of law but a description: there was no
king, and each did what was right in his own eyes.

Micah's name is the irony nobody in the story remarks on. *Mîkāyəhû*, in verses 1 and 4, is *Who is like
Yahweh?*; from verse 5 on, once the image stands in his house, the narrator calls him *Mîkâ*, without the
divine element. Whether the shortening is meant, the text does not say.

**What is notable.**

- **Micah, Micaiah.** *Ûšəmô mîkāyəhû* (1), and *bəbêt mîkāyəhû* (4); then *wəhāʾîš mîkâ* (5) and
  *mîkâ* in every later verse of chapters 17–18 (WLC, lemmas 4319 and 4318). The long form contains
  Yahweh's name; the short form is the usual spelling of the name elsewhere, including the prophet's book.
  The meaning *who is like Yahweh?* is the standard reading of the name, reported here and not argued.
  Compare Moses' song, *Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?* (Exodus 15:11, stored WEB). See
  [[Micah (of Ephraim)]] and [[Names and Naming]].
- **The curse in his ears.** *Wəʾat ʾālît wəgam ʾāmart bəʾoznay*, *you uttered a curse, and also spoke it
  in my ears* (2, WLC; the consonantal text writes *wʾty*, read *wəʾat*). The verb *ʾālâ* (lemma 422)
  occurs only here in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges; the noun of the same form, *ʾālâ* (423),
  is the *adjuration* of Leviticus 5:1, *If anyone … hears the voice of adjuration, he being a witness …
  if he doesn't report it, then he shall bear his iniquity* (stored WEB). The mother's curse was spoken
  over an unknown thief; the thief heard it, and confesses. The text does not say he confesses because of
  it. See [[The Accomplice Who Will Not Testify - Proverbs 29.24 and Leviticus 5.1]] and [[Curse]].
- **Blessed be my son of Yahweh.** *Bārûk bənî laYHWH* (2, WLC). The mother's blessing undoes her curse in
  the same breath; the law of theft is not raised. Leviticus 6:2–5 required a thief who confessed to
  *restore it even in full, and shall add a fifth part more to it* (6:5, stored WEB); Micah restores the sum only, and
  his mother gives most of it back. See [[Theft and Restitution]] and [[Blessing]].
- **Dedicated to Yahweh for a carved image and a molten image.** *Haqdēš hiqdaštî ʾet-hakkesep laYHWH
  … laʿăśôt pesel ûmassēkâ* (3, WLC, *qādaš*, lemma 6942). The two nouns stand together in the WLC files of
  Genesis through Judges only here, at 17:4 and 18:14, 18, and at Deuteronomy 27:15: *Cursed is the man who
  makes an engraved or molten image, an abomination to Yahweh, the work of the hands of the craftsman, and
  sets it up in secret* (stored WEB; same two nouns, WLC). Micah's mother pronounces a curse and is answered
  by an image the law curses. See
  [[A Carved Image and a Molten Image - Deuteronomy 27.15 and Micah's House]] and [[Idolatry]].
- **Two hundred of the eleven hundred.** She dedicates all the silver (3) and gives two hundred to the
  smith (4). The text does not say what became of the rest, nor whether she kept back part of a vow. Read
  with Leviticus 27 or Acts 5 it can look like a broken dedication; the narrator draws nothing from it.
  See [[Eleven Hundred Pieces of Silver - Delilah and Micah's Mother]].
- **The silversmith.** *Laṣṣôrēp* (4, WLC, *ṣārap*, lemma 6884). In Judges the verb occurs otherwise only at
  7:4, Yahweh's word to Gideon, *I will test them for you there* (stored WEB): the same verb for refining
  metal and for testing men. The wiki notes the shared verb and builds nothing on it.
- **A house of gods.** *Bêt ʾĕlōhîm* (5, WLC). The phrase is ambiguous by itself: *house of God* at
  Bethel (*This is none other than God's house*, Genesis 28:17, stored WEB) and *God's house in Shiloh*
  (18:31, stored WEB, with the article, *bêt-hāʾĕlōhîm*), but *the house of their god* at Shechem (9:27,
  WLC). The WEB here reads *a house of gods*. Micah's shrine and the house at Shiloh are named together at
  the end of the next chapter.
- **An ephod and teraphim.** *ʾĒpôd ûtərāpîm* (5, WLC). In Judges *ʾēpôd* occurs only at 8:27 (Gideon's)
  and in Micah's story (17:5; 18:14, 17, 18, 20); *tərāpîm* in Genesis through Judges only at Genesis 31:19,
  34, 35 (Rachel's theft) and here. Both stories begin with stolen silver or stolen gods inside a family.
  See [[The Teraphim - Household Gods in Genesis and After]] and
  [[Golden Earrings - Shechem's Oak, Aaron's Calf, and Gideon's Ephod]].
- **He filled the hand.** *Wayəmallēʾ ʾet-yad ʾaḥad mibbānāyw* (5) and *wayəmallēʾ mîkâ ʾet-yad hallēwî*
  (12, WLC): the WEB's *consecrated* renders *filled the hand*, the ordination idiom of Aaron and his sons
  (Exodus 28:41; 29:9, 33, 35; Leviticus 8:33; 16:32; 21:10; Numbers 3:3). In Exodus 32:29 it is the
  Levites' own consecration after the golden calf, *Consecrate yourselves today to Yahweh*, literally *fill
  your hand* (stored WEB; WLC). Micah does it himself, twice, first for his son. See
  [[Filling the Hand - Aaron's Sons, the Levites at the Calf, and Micah's Priests]] and [[Priesthood]].
- **No king; right in his own eyes.** *Bayyāmîm hāhēm ʾên melek bəyiśrāʾēl ʾîš hayyāšār bəʿênāyw yaʿăśeh*
  (6, WLC), the first of the four refrains (17:6; 18:1; 19:1; 21:25), and with 21:25 the full form.
  Deuteronomy 12:8 has *ʾîš kol-hayyāšār bəʿênāyw*, *every man whatever is right in his own eyes*: the same
  three words, with *kol* (WLC). See [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]]
  and [[Kingship and Empire]].
- **A Levite of the family of Judah.** *Mimmišpaḥat yəhûdâ wəhûʾ lēwî wəhûʾ gār šām* (7, WLC): *of the
  family of Judah, and he was a Levite, and he was sojourning there*. How a Levite is *of the family of
  Judah* the text does not explain; readers take it as residence in Judah's territory, or descent through
  a mother, or a sign that *Levite* names an office here more than a tribe. The wiki records the three and
  chooses none. Bethlehem is not among the Levites' cities of Joshua 21 (stored WEB).
- **Sojourning there.** *Gār šām* (7, WLC). In Deuteronomy 18:6 the Levite who comes *from any of your
  gates … where he lives as a foreigner*, *ʾăšer hûʾ gār šām*, is to come *to the place which Yahweh shall
  choose* (stored WEB; WLC). This Levite comes to Micah's house instead. The two words are also the sound
  of Gershom's name as the wiki reads it, *gēr šām*, *a sojourner there* (Exodus 2:22; see [[Gershom]]), and 18:30 will name the Danites' priest *Jonathan,
  the son of Gershom, the son of Moses* (stored WEB; see [[The Danite Migration]]). The wiki records the sound and does
  not claim the narrator intended it. See
  [[The Priests of the High Places - Deuteronomy 18.6-8 and 2 Kings 23.9]] and [[Gershom]].
- **To live where he could find a place.** *Lāgûr baʾăšer yimṣāʾ* (8, WLC). *Hammāqôm*, *the place*,
  is Deuteronomy's word for the chosen sanctuary (12:5; 18:6); this Levite is looking for any place. The
  narrator does not draw the contrast.
- **A father and a priest.** *Wehyēh-lî ləʾāb ûləkōhēn* (10, WLC). The Danites will make the same offer
  in the same words, *be to us a father and a priest* (18:19, stored WEB). Here the young man who is called
  *father* becomes *as one of his sons* (11).
- **The wage.** *ʿĂśeret kesep layyāmîm wəʿērek bəgādîm ûmiḥyātekā* (10, WLC): *ten of silver a year*,
  *layyāmîm*, literally *for the days*; *ʿērek bəgādîm*, *a set of clothing*. *ʿĒrek* is elsewhere in the
  WLC files of Genesis through Judges the *valuation* of the trespass offering and of vowed persons and
  things (Leviticus 5:15, 18; 6:6; 27:2–27, English numbering), and the *arranging* of bread and lamps (Exodus 40:4, 23). The Levite's
  service has a price; Leviticus 27 set one on people vowed to Yahweh. See [[Wealth and Possessions]].
- **Yahweh will do good to me.** *ʿAttâ yādaʿtî kî-yêṭîb YHWH lî* (13, WLC). The chapter closes on
  Micah's certainty, and the reader's knowledge contradicts it. *Yāṭab* returns at 18:20, where *the
  priest's heart was glad* to leave him (stored WEB; WLC *wayyîṭab*).

**How it connects.** The silver ties the chapter to the one before it: *eleven hundred pieces of silver*
paid by each Philistine lord for Samson (16:5), and the same sum stolen and made into an idol here (WLC;
the figure occurs nowhere else in Judges). See [[Samson and Delilah]] and
[[Eleven Hundred Pieces of Silver - Delilah and Micah's Mother]].

The shrine goes back to Gideon. *Gideon made an ephod out of it, and put it in Ophrah, his city … and it
became a snare to Gideon, and to his house* (8:27, stored WEB): gold from a victory, an ephod in a
family's town. Micah's ephod is made from silver taken from his mother. See [[Gideon's Ephod and Death]].

Behind both is the golden calf. Aaron *fashioned it with an engraving tool, and made it a molten calf*,
*ʿēgel massēkâ*, and said *These are your gods* (Exodus 32:4, stored WEB; WLC); the next verse calls a
feast *to Yahweh* (32:5). The calf was made in Yahweh's name too. And in the same chapter the Levites had
their hands filled for killing the calf's worshipers (32:29), where here a Levite's hand is filled to serve
an image. See [[Filling the Hand - Aaron's Sons, the Levites at the Calf, and Micah's Priests]].

The story is not finished. Chapter 18 sends the Danites past Micah's house, where they recognize the
Levite's voice, take the ephod, the teraphim, and the image, and make him priest to a tribe (18:3, 14–20);
see [[The Danite Migration]], which also names him [[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]]. And chapter 19 opens with a
second Levite in *the farther side of the hill country of Ephraim* and a concubine *out of Bethlehem
Judah* (19:1, stored WEB; not yet ingested): the same two places, in the opposite direction.

## Notes

**Did Micah think he was worshiping Yahweh?** Everything said aloud in the chapter names Yahweh, and nothing
names another god; the image may have been meant as an image *of* Yahweh, as Aaron's calf was proclaimed
with a feast *to Yahweh* (Exodus 32:5). Or the *house of gods* (5) may be plural in sense. The WEB's
*gods* takes the second reading; the Hebrew allows both. The wiki records both and builds on neither; on
either reading the image is what Exodus 20:4 and Deuteronomy 27:15 forbid.

**Why is the mother's curse answered with a blessing?** The text gives her no reason. Readers take the
blessing as a mother's relief, or as an attempt to cancel a curse that has fallen on her own son. The
text does not say a curse can be cancelled, and the wiki adopts neither.

**The order of verses 2–4.** Verse 3 has the mother dedicate the silver and say *I will restore it to you*;
verse 4 repeats *When he restored the money to his mother* and has her give it to the smith. Some readers
take verse 4 as a resumption of verse 3, others suspect the verses have been rearranged in transmission.
The wiki reads them as they stand.

**The Levite's name.** He is not named in chapter 17. Judges 18:30 names the priest of Dan's image
*Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Moses* (stored WEB), and most readers identify him with this
Levite, who goes with the Danites at 18:20. The Masoretic text writes the grandfather's name with a raised
*nun*, so that it can be read *Manasseh*; that is recorded on [[Gershom]]. The ingest of
[[The Danite Migration]] identified the two and renamed his page [[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]].

**Judgment calls.** Kept 17:1–13 as planned; 18:1 begins the Danites' search. The person page is
[[Micah (of Ephraim)]], qualified because *Micah* is also a book (and its prophet) and the Bible has more
than one Micah; the qualifier is his home, the only identifier the text gives. New pages for his mother
and for the Levite; the son and the smith get none.
