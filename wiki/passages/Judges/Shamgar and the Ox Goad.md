# Shamgar and the Ox Goad

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 3:31

## Summary

The shortest deliverer story in the book is a single verse:

> **3:31** After him was Shamgar the son of Anath, who struck six hundred men of the Philistines with an ox goad. He also saved Israel.

It follows [[Ehud and Eglon]] directly, and *after him* places Shamgar after Ehud. The verse has
three parts. First a name, *Shamgar the son of Anath*, which does not sound Israelite (see Notes).
Then a deed: six hundred Philistines struck down with a farm tool, *an ox goad*. Then a verdict:
*He also saved Israel*.

**What the verse leaves out.** Othniel and Ehud were each introduced by the full cycle of
[[Another Generation Arose]]: Israel does evil, Yahweh gives them to an oppressor, they cry out,
Yahweh raises up a savior, and the land rests. Shamgar's verse has none of these. Israel's sin is
not mentioned, and no oppressor is named or dated. There is no cry, and Yahweh does not raise him up.
Yahweh is not named in the verse at all. No Spirit comes on him, he is not said to *judge*, no rest
follows, and his death is not reported. The only formula word left is the verb *saved*, the verb of
2:16 and 3:9. The *also* in *He also saved Israel* sets him beside Othniel and Ehud.

**Where the verse sits.** The next verse goes back to Ehud: *The children of Israel again did that
which was evil in Yahweh's sight, when Ehud was dead* (4:1). The cycle's clock runs from Ehud's
death, not Shamgar's, as if 3:31 stood outside it. The Song of Deborah names him once more: *In the
days of Shamgar the son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the highways were unoccupied. The travelers
walked through byways* (5:6). There his name is a date. In the song, his days and Jael's were a time
when the roads were too dangerous to travel. The prose says *he also saved Israel*. The song says the
roads were empty in his days. Both stand in the text, and neither cancels the other: a man can strike
an enemy band without making the roads safe. The wiki leaves the tension as the book gives it.

**The first Philistines.** He is the first figure in Judges to fight the Philistines, the people
named first among the nations left *to test Israel* (3:3). The book's last judge, Samson, is promised
as one who *shall begin to save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines* (13:5). Shamgar's verse
already pairs the same verb with the same enemy, ten chapters before Samson. Samson's own victory is
told in a similar shape: one man, an improvised weapon, a round number of the dead. *He found a fresh
jawbone of a donkey, put out his hand, took it, and struck a thousand men with it* (15:15). See
[[One Man Against the Philistines - Shamgar's Ox Goad and Samson's Jawbone]].

## People

- [[Shamgar]] — *the son of Anath*; strikes six hundred Philistines and *also saved Israel* (31).
- [[The Philistines]] — the enemy, here for the first time in the judges' wars (31).
- [[Ehud]] — *after him* (31); the next verse dates Israel's relapse from Ehud's death (4:1).
- Anath is named only as Shamgar's father, or as part of his name. He gets no page; see Notes.

## Places

- No place is named. The Philistines' land is [[The Land of the Philistines]], but the verse does
  not say where the fight happened.
- [[Beth Anath]] — a Naphtali city with the same element *Anath* in its name (1:33). The verse does
  not connect Shamgar to it; see Notes.

## Themes

- [[Redemption]] — *He also saved Israel* (31), the savior's verb without the savior's title.
- [[War]] — six hundred men struck with an ox goad (31).
- [[The Nations]] — the Philistines, the first of the nations left *to test Israel* (3:3).

## Notes

**The whole verse in Hebrew.** *Wəʾaḥărāyw hāyâ šamgar ben-ʿănāt wayyak ʾet-pəlištîm šēš-mēʾôt ʾîš
bəmalmad habbāqār wayyōšaʿ gam-hûʾ ʾet-yiśrāʾēl* (31, WLC). Word for word: *and after him was Shamgar
son of Anath, and he struck the Philistines, six hundred men, with the goad of the cattle, and he
saved, he also, Israel*.

**"He also saved."** *Wayyōšaʿ gam-hûʾ* (31, WLC). The verb is *yāšaʿ*, the verb of *who saved them*
in 2:16 and 3:9 (WLC, the same lemma). Othniel and Ehud are each called *môšîaʿ*, *a savior* (3:9,
15), the participle of this verb. Shamgar is not given the title, only the verb, and *gam-hûʾ*, *he
too*, which counts him with the two before him. He is never said to have *judged* (*šāpaṭ*). Whether
he belongs in a count of judges is a question the book does not answer; some later lists of the judges
include him, and some leave him out. That is reported, not checked.

**"An ox goad."** *Bəmalmad habbāqār* (31, WLC). In the WLC files read for this ingest (Genesis
through Joshua, Judges, and 1 Samuel), *malmad* occurs only here. Lexicons derive it from *lāmad*,
*to learn* or, in the intensive stem, *to teach*; that derivation is reported, not checked. The
OpenScriptures lemma data lists *malmad* separately (4451) from *lāmad* (3925), so the data does not
prove it. If the derivation holds, the goad is the thing that *teaches* the ox. Judges 3:2 has just
said Yahweh left the nations *to teach them war*, *ləlammədām milḥāmâ* (WLC, lemma 3925). On that
reading, the first Philistine fight in the book is won with a *teaching* tool. That reading belongs
to the derivation and is not a statement of the text. The text gives the tool and says nothing about
why Shamgar had it. A farmer's tool, used for driving the animals that pull the plough, suggests a
man with no soldier's weapon. That is an inference from the object.

**Six hundred.** *Šēš-mēʾôt ʾîš* (31, WLC). Six hundred is the number of armed Danites who march
north in Judges 18:11, 16, 17 (stored WEB) and of the Benjamites who escape to the rock of Rimmon
(20:47). The number recurs, but no link between these passages is claimed here.

**"Shamgar the son of Anath."** *Šamgar ben-ʿănāt* (31; again at 5:6; WLC). In the WLC files read,
the name *Šamgar* (lemma 8044) and the name *ʿĂnāt* (lemma 6067) occur only in these two verses.
*Shamgar* is commonly said not to be Hebrew, often taken as Hurrian. *Anath* is the name of a Canaanite
goddess of war known from the Ugaritic texts. Both points are reported, not checked against a primary
source. If the second is right, *son of Anath* could be a real father's name built on the goddess's
name, a warrior's epithet, or a link to a town such as Beth Anath, *house of Anath*. The verse does not
choose, and the wiki does not either. The same element *ʿănāt* appears in Beth Anath, a Naphtali
city the tribe did not drive out (Joshua 19:38; Judges 1:33; WLC), but the verse does not connect them.

**Anath gets no page.** He, she, or it appears only in a patronymic, twice, and does nothing. The
same choice was made for Kenaz on [[Othniel the First Judge]] and Gera on [[Ehud and Eglon]].

**After him, but dated from Ehud.** *Wəʾaḥărāyw* (31), *after him*, follows Ehud's story; then 4:1
says *wəʾēhûd mēt*, *and Ehud was dead* (WLC). The verse in 4:1 makes Ehud's death, not Shamgar's,
the marker of the next relapse. One common reading is that Shamgar's work overlapped Ehud's eighty
years, or fell in the time of the oppression that follows. Another is that the verse was added to the
sequence later. Both are reported, not checked; the text says only *after him*.

**No rest.** Every judge so far has ended with *the land had rest* (3:11, 30). Shamgar does not. See
[[The Land Had Rest - Joshua 11.23, 14.15, and the Judges Formula]].
