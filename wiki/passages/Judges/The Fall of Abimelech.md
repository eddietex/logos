# The Fall of Abimelech

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 9:22–57

## Summary

Jotham's curse is carried out. Abimelech is *prince over Israel* for three years (22). Then *God sent
an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem*, so that the blood of the seventy would come
back on the one who killed them and on the town that *strengthened his hands* (23–24). Shechem sets
ambushes on the mountain roads and robs travellers (25).

A newcomer, Gaal the son of Ebed, moves in with his brothers and wins the town's trust. At the vintage
festival, in *the house of their god*, the men of Shechem eat, drink, and curse Abimelech, and Gaal
asks *Who is Abimelech … that we should serve him?* (26–29). Zebul, Abimelech's officer in the city,
sends word and sets the trap. Abimelech's men come down from the mountains at dawn in four companies;
Gaal, mocked by Zebul, goes out to fight, is beaten back to the gate, and is driven out of the city
(30–41).

The next day Abimelech ambushes the people as they go out into the field, takes the city, kills its
people, beats it down, and sows it with salt (42–45). The men of *the tower of Shechem* shelter in the
stronghold of *the house of Elberith*. Abimelech cuts a bough on Mount Zalmon, tells his men *do as I
have done*, and they pile the boughs against the stronghold and burn it. About a thousand men and women
die (46–49). He goes on to Thebez and takes it, but its people shut themselves into a strong tower. As
he comes near its door to burn it, *a certain woman cast an upper millstone on Abimelech's head, and
broke his skull*. He has his armor bearer kill him, *that men not say of me, "A woman killed him"*.
When Israel sees he is dead, they go home (50–55).

The narrator then says what it meant: *Thus God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech, which he did to his
father, in killing his seventy brothers; and God repaid all the wickedness of the men of Shechem on
their heads; and the curse of Jotham the son of Jerubbaal came on them* (56–57).

God was not named in 9:1–6, and only Jotham named him in 9:7–21. Here he acts twice, at the beginning
(23) and at the end (56–57). Between those verses the story has no word from God, no prophet, no
outcry, and no deliverer. It is a civil war that he set going and then judged. No foreign enemy
appears in the whole chapter.

**Three years, and an evil spirit (9:22–25).**

> **9:22** Abimelech was prince over Israel three years.

> **9:23** Then God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem; and the men of Shechem dealt treacherously with Abimelech,

> **9:24** that the violence done to the seventy sons of Jerubbaal might come, and that their blood might be laid on Abimelech their brother, who killed them, and on the men of Shechem, who strengthened his hands to kill his brothers.

> **9:25** The men of Shechem set an ambush for him on the tops of the mountains, and they robbed all who came along that way by them, and Abimelech was told about it.

*Was prince*, *wayyāśar*. The verb is *śārar* (7786), not *mālak*, the verb of 9:6, where he was *made
king*. In Genesis–Judges it occurs only here. The closest form is the hithpael *śārar* (8323) of
Numbers 16:13, where Dathan and Abiram accuse Moses of making himself *a prince over us* (WLC; the two
lemma numbers are treated as separate roots in the morphhb tagging). The narrator does not call him
*king* again. *Over Israel* is more than Shechem. Who in Israel accepted him, the text does not say.
Nobody else in the chapter calls him king. At 9:55 *the men of Israel* simply go home.

*An evil spirit*, *rûaḥ rāʿâ*. In Genesis–Judges this is the only verse with both words (WLC). The
next is the evil spirit *from Yahweh* that troubles Saul (1 Samuel 16:14; reported, not checked). Here
it is *God*, *ʾĕlōhîm*, who sends it, *wayyišlaḥ*, and it is sent *between* the king and the town that
made him. The book has twice told of *the Spirit of Yahweh* coming on a judge (3:10; 6:34). This
spirit comes on no one. It divides. See [[The Spirit of God]].

*Dealt treacherously*, *wayyibgədû*, is *bāgad* (898). Its only other occurrence in Genesis–Judges is
Exodus 21:8, the master who *has dealt deceitfully* with a slave wife (WLC). Jotham had asked whether
Shechem dealt *truly*, *beʾĕmet* (9:16, 19). Now it deals falsely, but with the man it chose.

9:24 is the narrator's statement of purpose, given before the events. *The violence done to the seventy
… might come*, *lābôʾ ḥămas*. *Ḥāmās* is the word of the flood, *the earth was filled with violence*
(Genesis 6:11, 13). Jacob used it of Shechem's earlier killers, Simeon and Levi: *Their swords are
weapons of violence* (Genesis 49:5) (WLC; stored WEB). It is the town's second massacre. In Genesis 34
the town's men were the victims. In Judges 9 they share in the guilt, and then they are victims again.
See [[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]].

*Their blood … laid on Abimelech their brother … and on the men of Shechem, who strengthened his
hands*. The guilt has two parts, as Jotham's curse did (9:20). *Strengthened his hands*, *ḥizzəqû
ʾet-yādāyw*, is the phrase Yahweh used to Gideon at Midian's camp: *afterward your hands will be
strengthened to go down into the camp* (7:11; WLC, *ḥāzaq* with *yād*). Yahweh strengthened the
father's hands to save Israel. Shechem strengthened the son's hands to kill the father's sons.

*Set an ambush for him*, *wayyāśîmû lô … məʾārəbîm*. *ʾĀrab* (693) is the verb of the ambush at Ai
(Joshua 8:2, 4, 7, 12, 14, 19, 21). It returns four times in this chapter (9:25, 32, 34, 43) and again
for Gibeah (Judges 20:29–38) (WLC). Shechem's ambush is banditry: *they robbed all who came along that
way*, and that hurt the revenue of the man who ruled the roads. The text does not say so, but it says
the robbery was reported to him. See [[Drawn Away from the City - Ai and Gibeah]].

**Gaal at the vintage (9:26–29).**

> **9:26** Gaal the son of Ebed came with his brothers, and went over to Shechem; and the men of Shechem put their trust in him.

> **9:27** They went out into the field, harvested their vineyards, trod the grapes, held festival, and went into the house of their god, and ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.

> **9:28** Gaal the son of Ebed said, "Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? Isn't he the son of Jerubbaal? Isn't Zebul his officer? Serve the men of Hamor the father of Shechem, but why should we serve him?

> **9:29** I wish that this people were under my hand! Then I would remove Abimelech." He said to Abimelech, "Increase your army, and come out!"

[[Gaal]] is not introduced. The text gives no tribe, no town, and no reason for his
coming. *Ebed*, *ʿebed*, is the ordinary word for *servant* or *slave* (WLC lemma 5651, a proper name
here). Whether it is a real name or a sneer by the narrator is disputed (reported, not checked). If it
is meant, the irony is plain: a *son of a slave* mocks Abimelech, whom Jotham called *the son of his
female servant* (9:18).

*Put their trust in him*, *wayyibṭəḥû bô*. Jotham's bramble had offered *take refuge in my shade*
(9:15). Shechem now trusts someone else.

The festival is a vintage. *Harvested … trod the grapes, held festival*, *wayyaʿăśû hillûlîm*. In
Genesis–Judges *hillûlîm* (1974) occurs only here and at Leviticus 19:24, where the fourth year's fruit
of a new tree *shall be holy, for giving praise to Yahweh* (WLC; stored WEB). In Leviticus it is
praise to Yahweh. In Shechem it is held *in the house of their god*, presumably Baal Berith (8:33; 9:4),
whose silver paid for the seventy. The vine said its wine *cheers God and man* (9:13). At this vintage
the wine leads to cursing. *Cursed*, *wayqallû*, is *qālal* (7043), the verb WEB renders *treats you with
contempt* in Genesis 12:3 (WLC; stored WEB). It is the town's curse on Abimelech. The narrator will name a different
curse in 9:57.

Gaal's speech turns on *serve*, *ʿābad*, three times. *Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we
should serve him?* has the shape of Pharaoh's *Who is Yahweh, that I should listen to his voice?*
(Exodus 5:2; *mî* with a name, WLC). *Isn't he the son of Jerubbaal?* Abimelech had claimed the town as
*your bone and your flesh* (9:2). Gaal answers that he is his father's son, and his father was the
man who tore down Baal's altar. *Serve the men of Hamor the father of Shechem*: the phrase *Hamor the
father of Shechem* reaches back to Genesis 33:19; 34:6 and Joshua 24:32 (WLC). Gaal is calling the
town back to its Hivite founders, the house Simeon and Levi killed (Genesis 34:25–26). See [[Hamor]]
and [[Shechem (son of Hamor)]].

*Zebul his officer*, *pəqîdô*. *Pāqîd* (6496) occurs in Genesis–Judges only here and at Genesis 41:34,
Joseph's *overseers over the land* (WLC). [[Zebul]] is Abimelech's man in the city, while Abimelech
lives elsewhere.

*I wish that this people were under my hand*, *mî yittēn*. Gaal wants what Abimelech asked for, one man
over the town. His challenge, *Increase your army, and come out!*, is said to an absent man. The text
does not say whether he shouted it or only boasted.

**Zebul's trap (9:30–41).**

> **9:30** When Zebul the ruler of the city heard the words of Gaal the son of Ebed, his anger burned.

> **9:31** He sent messengers to Abimelech craftily, saying, "Behold, Gaal the son of Ebed and his brothers have come to Shechem; and behold, they incite the city against you.

> **9:32** Now therefore, go up by night, you and the people who are with you, and lie in wait in the field.

> **9:33** It shall be that in the morning, as soon as the sun is up, you shall rise early, and rush on the city. Behold, when he and the people who are with him come out against you, then may you do to them as you shall find occasion."

> **9:34** Abimelech rose up, and all the people who were with him, by night, and they laid wait against Shechem in four companies.

> **9:35** Gaal the son of Ebed went out, and stood in the entrance of the gate of the city. Abimelech rose up, and the people who were with him, from the ambush.

> **9:36** When Gaal saw the people, he said to Zebul, "Behold, people are coming down from the tops of the mountains." Zebul said to him, "You see the shadow of the mountains as if they were men."

> **9:37** Gaal spoke again and said, "Behold, people are coming down by the middle of the land, and one company comes by the way of the oak of Meonenim."

> **9:38** Then Zebul said to him, "Now where is your mouth, that you said, 'Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?' Isn't this the people that you have despised? Please go out now and fight with them."

> **9:39** Gaal went out before the men of Shechem, and fought with Abimelech.

> **9:40** Abimelech chased him, and he fled before him, and many fell wounded, even to the entrance of the gate.

> **9:41** Abimelech lived at Arumah; and Zebul drove out Gaal and his brothers, that they should not dwell in Shechem.

*Craftily*, *bətormâ*. *Tormâ* (8649) occurs in Genesis–Judges only here (WLC). WEB takes it as
*deceit*. Some read it as a place, *in Tormah*, and identify it with Arumah of 9:41 (reported, not
checked). Either way Zebul is working against the town he governs.

*Do to them as you shall find occasion*, *kaʾăšer timṣāʾ yādekā*, literally *as your hand finds*.
Samuel says the same to the newly anointed Saul: *ʿăśēh ləkā ʾăšer timṣāʾ yādekā*, *do what is
appropriate for the occasion* (1 Samuel 10:7, WEB checked at bible-api.com; Hebrew read in the WLC
`1Sam.xml`). It is a phrase for a man given a free hand, used once of the bramble king and once of
Israel's first anointed king.

The dawn scene is told almost as comedy. Gaal, at the gate, sees men *coming down from the tops of the
mountains*. Zebul, beside him, says *You see the shadow of the mountains as if they were men*. *Ṣēl*,
*shadow*, is the bramble's *shade* (9:15). Jotham's bramble offered the trees its shade. Now Zebul uses
the word to fool Gaal while Abimelech's men come down. Gaal looks again: *people are coming down by the
middle of the land*, *ṭabbûr hāʾāreṣ*, and one company *by the way of the oak of Meonenim*. *Ṭabbûr*
occurs in Genesis–Judges only here (WLC). It is often glossed *navel of the land*, with Ezekiel 38:12
as its only other occurrence (reported, not checked). Whether it means a central height near Shechem,
or a figure for Shechem as the land's center, cannot be decided from Judges alone.

[[The Oak of Meonenim]], *ʾēlôn məʿônənîm*, is the *oak of the soothsayers*. *Məʿônənîm* is the
participle of *ʿānan* (6049), which the law forbids among the diviners' practices (Leviticus 19:26;
Deuteronomy 18:10, 14) (WLC; at Genesis 9:14 the morphhb tags a homonym, *bring clouds*, with the same
number). The text does not say whether this is one of the oaks already named at Shechem (Genesis 12:6;
35:4; Joshua 24:26; Judges 9:6). The name alone says that it was a diviners' tree.

Then Zebul turns: *Now where is your mouth?* He quotes Gaal back to him, *Who is Abimelech, that we
should serve him?* *Isn't this the people that you have despised?* *Māʾas*, *despised*, is otherwise
in Genesis–Judges the verb of Israel despising Yahweh's statutes and Yahweh himself (Leviticus 26:15,
43; Numbers 11:20; 14:31) (WLC). *Please go out now and fight with them.* Gaal does. He loses, the
wounded fall *even to the entrance of the gate*, and Zebul drives him and his brothers out of the town
(39–41). Gaal leaves the story as suddenly as he came into it. [[Arumah]], where Abimelech *lived*, is
named only here.

*Drove out*, *wayəgāreš*, is *gāraš*, the verb of Yahweh driving out the nations before Israel (Exodus
23:28–31; Joshua 24:12, 18), and the verb Judges 2:3 said Yahweh would no longer do (WLC). Here an
Israelite king's officer drives a man out of an Israelite town.

**The city destroyed (9:42–45).**

> **9:42** On the next day, the people went out into the field; and they told Abimelech.

> **9:43** He took the people, and divided them into three companies, and laid wait in the field; and he looked, and behold, the people came out of the city. So, he rose up against them, and struck them.

> **9:44** Abimelech and the companies that were with him rushed forward, and stood in the entrance of the gate of the city; and the two companies rushed on all who were in the field, and struck them.

> **9:45** Abimelech fought against the city all that day; and he took the city, and killed the people in it. He beat down the city, and sowed it with salt.

Gaal is gone, and the town has no quarrel left with Abimelech that the text records. *The people went
out into the field*, perhaps to work it, and he ambushes them anyway. *Divided them into three companies*,
*wayyeḥĕṣēm lišlōšâ rāʾšîm*, is Gideon's division of the three hundred, *wayyaḥaṣ … šəlōšâ rāʾšîm*
(7:16; WLC, *ḥāṣâ* with *rōʾš*). Gideon divided his men to save Israel.
Abimelech divides his to kill Israelites. One company holds the gate, so that no one can get back in,
and two strike the people in the field. It is the tactic of Ai, an ambush that cuts the city off from
its own fighters (Joshua 8:19–22).

*He beat down the city*, *wayyittōṣ*. *Nātaṣ* is the verb of the altars Israel was to tear down
(Exodus 34:13; Deuteronomy 7:5; 12:3; Judges 2:2). It is also the verb of Gideon's first act, *the altar
of Baal was broken down* (6:28, 30–32), and of his last act of vengeance: *He broke down the tower of
Penuel, and killed the men of the city* (8:9, 17; WLC). Gideon broke down a tower and killed a town's
men. His son breaks down a city, and then burns a tower with its people inside. See
[[Break Down Their Altars - Exodus 34.13, Deuteronomy 7.5, Judges 2.2, and Judges 6.25-32]] and
[[Penuel and Succoth - Jacob's Road and Gideon's Pursuit]].

*And sowed it with salt*, *wayyizrāʿehā melaḥ*. In Genesis–Judges the verb *zāraʿ* and *melaḥ* appear
together only here and in Deuteronomy 29:23 (WLC 29:22), the land made *sulfur, salt, and burning, that
it is not sown … like the overthrow of Sodom* (WLC; stored WEB). The salt is a curse on the ground, so
that nothing grows. It is the only salted city in the Bible's narrative so far. Salt elsewhere in the
law is *the salt of the covenant* (Leviticus 2:13); see
[[A Covenant of Salt - Leviticus 2 and Numbers 18]]. Here it is the opposite. The meaning of the act as
a ritual is debated (reported, not checked). The text says only what he did.

**Fire on the tower of Shechem (9:46–49).**

> **9:46** When all the men of the tower of Shechem heard of it, they entered into the stronghold of the house of Elberith.

> **9:47** Abimelech was told that all the men of the tower of Shechem were gathered together.

> **9:48** Abimelech went up to Mount Zalmon, he and all the people who were with him; and Abimelech took an ax in his hand, and cut down a bough from the trees, and took it up, and laid it on his shoulder. Then he said to the people who were with him, "What you have seen me do, make haste, and do as I have done!"

> **9:49** All the people likewise each cut down his bough, and followed Abimelech, and put them at the base of the stronghold, and set the stronghold on fire on them; so that all the people of the tower of Shechem died also, about a thousand men and women.

[[The Tower of Shechem]], *migdal šəkem*, is a place or a group distinct from *the city* just destroyed.
Its men, *baʿălê migdal-šəkem*, *the lords of the tower of Shechem*, take refuge in *the stronghold*,
*ṣərîaḥ* (6877, only 9:46, 49 in Genesis–Judges), of *the house of El Berith*, *bêt ʾēl bərît* (WLC).
*El Berith*, *God of the covenant*, occurs only here in Genesis–Judges. The god of 8:33 and 9:4 was
*Baal Berith*, *lord of the covenant*. Whether these are one god and one house is not stated. The wiki
treats them on one page, [[Baal Berith]], as most readers do, and records the difference in name. Some
identify the tower of Shechem with *the house of Millo* of 9:6, 20 (reported, not checked). The text
does not.

They had shelter *in the house of their god*, and they burn in it. The god whose silver paid for the
killing of the seventy cannot protect the people who worship there.

[[Mount Zalmon]] occurs only here in Genesis–Judges (WLC). Abimelech cuts a bough, *śôkat ʿēṣîm*, *a
branch of the trees*. *ʿĒṣîm* is the word of Jotham's fable, *the trees went out* (9:8). In the fable
the bramble threatened that fire would *come out of the bramble*. Here the king cuts branches from the
trees to make the fire. *And laid it on his shoulder*, *ʿal-šikmô*. *Šəkem*, *shoulder*, is the city's
name. Jacob had given Joseph *šəkem ʾeḥād*, *one portion* (Genesis 48:22; WLC lemma 7926 in both). The
wordplay is in the Hebrew. The text does not draw it out.

*What you have seen me do, make haste, and do as I have done!*, *mâ rəʾîtem ʿāśîtî … ʿăśû kāmônî*. It is
his father's order at the edge of Midian's camp: *Watch me, and do likewise … as I do, so you shall
do*, *mimmennî tirʾû wəkēn taʿăśû* (7:17; WLC). The verbs are the same, *rāʾâ* and *ʿāśâ*. Gideon's men
copied him with trumpets, torches, and jars, and Midian fled. Abimelech's men copy him with
branches, and a thousand Israelites burn.

*Set the stronghold on fire on them*, *wayyaṣṣîtû … bāʾēš*. *Yāṣat* with *ʾēš* occurs in
Genesis–Judges only here and at Joshua 8:8, 19, where Israel *set the city on fire* at Ai (WLC). That is
the last link to Ai in the chapter. Joshua's ambush and fire were turned against a Canaanite city. Here
the same are turned against an Israelite one.

The first half of Jotham's curse is now literal: *let fire come out from Abimelech, and devour the men
of Shechem* (9:20). *About a thousand men and women* includes the women. 9:49 is the only place in the
chapter where the dead are counted, apart from the seventy.

**The millstone at Thebez (9:50–55).**

> **9:50** Then Abimelech went to Thebez, and encamped against Thebez, and took it.

> **9:51** But there was a strong tower within the city, and all the men and women of the city fled there, and shut themselves in, and went up to the roof of the tower.

> **9:52** Abimelech came to the tower, and fought against it, and came near to the door of the tower to burn it with fire.

> **9:53** A certain woman cast an upper millstone on Abimelech's head, and broke his skull.

> **9:54** Then he called hastily to the young man his armor bearer, and said to him, "Draw your sword, and kill me, that men not say of me, 'A woman killed him.' His young man thrust him through, and he died."

> **9:55** When the men of Israel saw that Abimelech was dead, they each departed to his place.

[[Thebez]] is named only here in Genesis–Judges (WLC). The text does not say why Abimelech attacks it.
Perhaps it had joined Shechem's revolt, but the text says nothing of it. He tries the same method again:
a tower full of people, and fire at its door. *Migdal-ʿōz*, *a strong tower*, is the phrase of
*Yahweh's name is a strong tower* (Proverbs 18:10, stored WEB; see
[[The Name of Yahweh Is a Strong Tower - Proverbs 18.10-11, Psalm 61.3, and Genesis 11.4]]). At Thebez
the tower holds.

*A certain woman*, *ʾiššâ ʾaḥat*, *one woman*. She is not named; see [[The Woman of Thebez]]. *Cast*,
*wattašlēk*, is *šālak*, the verb of Jotham's *my father … risked his life*, literally *threw his life*
(9:17; WLC). Gideon threw his life before Midian for Shechem. A woman throws a millstone on his son.
*An upper millstone*, *pelaḥ rekeb*, *a piece of riding stone*, the stone that moves on top of the
lower one. *Rekeb* in this sense is the *upper millstone* the law forbids taking in pledge, *for he
takes a life in pledge* (Deuteronomy 24:6; WLC; stored WEB). Elsewhere *rekeb* is almost always
*chariot* (Exodus 14; Judges 4:3, 7, 13–16). Grinding grain was women's work, and some read the
millstone as a household tool turned into a weapon (reported, not checked).

*And broke his skull*, *wattāroṣ ʾet-gulgoltô*. *Gulgōlet* is otherwise in Genesis–Judges the word for
counting people by the *head*, *a head* in the census and the half-shekel (Exodus 16:16; 38:26;
Numbers 1:2–22; 3:47; WLC). Only here is it a skull.

He dies as Sisera died, by a blow to the head from a woman, the disgrace Deborah foretold when she
told Barak the journey *won't be for your honor* (4:9, 21). Abimelech tries to escape that disgrace. *Draw your sword, and kill me,
that men not say of me, "A woman killed him"*, *šəlōp ḥarbəkā ûmôtətēnî … ʾiššâ hărāgathû*. The young
man *thrust him through*, *wayyidqərēhû*. He does not escape it. The narrator has already said *a woman*
did it, and Joab still says so in the message he sends David: *Didn't a woman cast an upper millstone on
him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez?* (2 Samuel 11:21, WEB checked at bible-api.com). See
[[Into a Woman's Hand - Judges 4.9, 4.21, and 9.53-54]].

Saul's death repeats the scene almost word for word. *Draw your sword, and thrust me through with it*,
*šəlōp ḥarbəkā wədoqrēnî*, he says to *his armor bearer*, *nōśēʾ kēlāyw*, *lest these uncircumcised
come and thrust me through* (1 Samuel 31:4; WEB checked at bible-api.com; Hebrew read in the WLC
`1Sam.xml`). Both men fear what others will say or do to them. Abimelech's armor bearer obeys, and
Saul's will not. See [[Draw Your Sword - The Deaths of Abimelech and Saul]].

The second half of Jotham's curse was *let fire come out from the men of Shechem, and from the house of
Millo, and devour Abimelech* (9:20). That is not what happens, read literally. Abimelech dies at Thebez,
not Shechem, by a stone and a sword, not by fire. He is killed while trying to set a fire. The
narrator still says *the curse of Jotham … came on them* (57). The curse is fulfilled in its sense, two
parties destroying each other, and not in its imagery. The wiki notes the gap rather than smoothing it.

*The men of Israel … each departed to his place*. *ʾîš yiśrāʾēl*, *the men of Israel*, are named here
for the first time in the chapter. They had been fighting for him, it seems, against an Israelite
town. When he dies, they go home. No one mourns him and no one is made king after him.

**God repaid (9:56–57).**

> **9:56** Thus God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech, which he did to his father, in killing his seventy brothers;

> **9:57** and God repaid all the wickedness of the men of Shechem on their heads; and the curse of Jotham the son of Jerubbaal came on them.

*Repaid*, *wayyāšeb*, *hēšîb*, the hiphil of *šûb*, *to bring back*, *to return*, with *rāʿâ*, *evil*
or *wickedness*. In Genesis–Judges the two occur together only here and in two other verses (WLC). One is
Exodus 32:12. The other is Genesis 50:15, where Joseph's brothers fear that he *will fully pay us back
for all the evil which we did to him*, *hāšēb yāšîb lānû ʾēt kol-hārāʿâ ʾăšer gāmalnû ʾōtô*. That verse
also has *gāmal*, the root of Jotham's *gəmûl*, *the deserving of his hands* (9:16). Joseph does not
repay his brothers. God repays Abimelech for his. The *evil spirit* of 9:23 and the *wickedness* of 9:56–57
are the same word, *rāʿâ*. God sends evil between them, and the evil each has done comes back on him.

*Which he did to his father*. The narrator counts the killing of the seventy as a wrong done to Gideon.
It is the *kindness to the house of Jerubbaal* that Israel did not show (8:35). *On their heads*,
*bərōʾšām*: the same word, *rōʾš*, that names Abimelech's *head*, on which the millstone fell (53), and
the *companies*, *rāʾšîm*, he divided (34, 43, 44).

*And the curse of Jotham the son of Jerubbaal came on them*, *qilĕlat yôtām*. *Qəlālâ* (7045) is the
covenant *curse* of Deuteronomy 11:26–29 and 27:13, set on Mount Ebal across from Gerizim, where Jotham
stood (WLC). The *curse* Shechem spoke against Abimelech at the vintage was *qālal* (27). The curse
that came was Jotham's. See [[Blessing on Gerizim and Curse on Ebal - Deuteronomy 11, 27, and Joshua 8]].

The book's first story of retribution was Adoni-Bezek's: *Seventy kings … As I have done, so God has
done to me* (1:7). Abimelech kills seventy brothers and is repaid. See
[[God Repaid - Adoni-Bezek, Joseph's Brothers, and Abimelech]].

**How the pericope sits in Judges.** Judges 9 has no judge and no outside enemy. Its whole length is
Israelites destroying Israelites. The book's cycle—sin, oppression, cry, deliverance—does not run here.
The sin is Shechem's and Abimelech's, the oppression is Abimelech's, no one cries out, and God's
answer is not a deliverer but a judgment on both sides. Gideon's story ended with *Yahweh shall rule
over you* (8:23). His son's story ends with *God repaid*. The first attempt at kingship in Israel ends
in three years, a salted city, a burned tower, and a king killed by a woman. Tola, *to save Israel*,
follows at once (10:1, stored WEB; not yet ingested). See [[Kingship and Empire]] and [[Retribution]].

**Judgment calls.** The planned boundary 9:22–57 was kept as one pericope. The chapter divides into
Gaal's revolt (22–41) and the destructions (42–57), but 9:23–24 announces and 9:56–57 closes the
judgment as one movement, and splitting it would leave the purpose clause and the verdict on different
pages. Gaal and Zebul get pages as named actors. Gaal's father Ebed, the armor bearer, and the men of
the tower do not. The woman of Thebez gets a page, named for her town, since she is the agent of the
book's verdict and Joab remembers her. *El Berith* is treated on the [[Baal Berith]] page, with the
difference of name recorded, not a new page. *The tower of Shechem* gets a place page and is not
identified with Beth Millo. Five new places: Arumah, the oak of Meonenim, Mount Zalmon, Thebez, and the
tower.
