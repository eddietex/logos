# The Danite Migration

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 18:1–31

## Summary

*In those days there was no king in Israel. In those days the tribe of the Danites sought an inheritance
to dwell in; for to that day, their inheritance had not fallen to them among the tribes of Israel* (1).
They send *five men … men of valor, from Zorah, and from Eshtaol, to spy out the land*, and the five
lodge at the house of Micah in the hill country of Ephraim (2). There *they knew the voice of the young
man the Levite* and ask him, *Who brought you here? What do you do in this place? What do you have
here?* (3). He tells them Micah *has hired me, and I am become his priest* (4). They ask him to *ask
counsel of God*, and he answers, *Go in peace. Your way in which you go is before Yahweh* (5–6).

The five come to Laish and see a people living *in safety, in the way of the Sidonians, quiet and
secure*, with no one *possessing authority*, *far from the Sidonians*, and with *no dealings with anyone
else* (7). They report to Zorah and Eshtaol: *we have seen the land, and behold, it is very good. Do you
stand still? Don't be slothful … for God has given it into your hand, a place where there is no lack of
anything that is in the earth* (8–10).

*Six hundred men armed with weapons of war* set out, camp behind Kiriath Jearim in Judah, *Therefore they
called that place Mahaneh Dan, to this day*, and come to Micah's house (11–13). The five tell their
brothers there is *an ephod, and teraphim, and a carved image, and a molten image* in these houses: *Now
therefore consider what you have to do* (14). While the six hundred stand at the gate, the five go in and
take the image, the ephod, the teraphim, and the molten image (15–18). When the priest protests, they
say, *Hold your peace, put your hand on your mouth, and go with us, and be to us a father and a priest.
Is it better for you to be priest to the house of one man, or to be priest to a tribe and a family in
Israel?* (19). *The priest's heart was glad*, and he takes the ephod, the teraphim, and the image, and
goes *with the people* (20).

Micah and his neighbors overtake them. *You have taken away my gods which I made, and the priest, and
have gone away! What more do I have?* (24). The Danites warn him off — *lest angry fellows fall on you,
and you lose your life* — and Micah, seeing *that they were too strong for him*, goes home (25–26).

They come to Laish, *to a people quiet and unsuspecting, and struck them with the edge of the sword; then
they burned the city with fire. There was no deliverer* (27–28). They rebuild it and call it Dan, *after
the name of Dan their father, who was born to Israel; however the name of the city used to be Laish*
(29). *The children of Dan set up for themselves the engraved image; and Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the
son of Moses, and his sons were priests to the tribe of the Danites until the day of the captivity of the
land* (30), *all the time that God's house was in Shiloh* (31).

**What is going on.** The second half of the Micah story turns one family's shrine into a tribe's. Every
piece of chapter 17 is carried north — the image, the ephod, the teraphim, the Levite — and every piece
changes hands by force or by a better offer. Micah, who stole from his mother, is robbed; the Levite, who
was hired for ten pieces of silver, is hired away with a promotion; and the image that was made *to
Yahweh* becomes the cult object of a whole tribe at the far north of the land, while *God's house* stands
at Shiloh.

The chapter is told in the language of the conquest. Spies go out, report that the land is *very good*,
and rebuke their brothers for hanging back; God *has given it into your hand*; an oracle sends them *in
peace*; the city is struck *with the edge of the sword* and burned. But the land is not Canaan's center,
the people are not named among the nations Israel was told to drive out, and the reader is told three
times what Laish was: *quiet and secure* (7), *unsuspecting* (10, 27), with *no deliverer* (28). The
narrator's only verdict is the refrain of verse 1, *there was no king in Israel*, and the bare notice of
verses 30–31: the tribe set up the image for itself, and kept it for as long as God's house was elsewhere.

**What is notable.**

- **No king, and no inheritance.** *Bayyāmîm hāhēm ʾên melek bəyiśrāʾēl* (1, WLC), the second of the
  four refrains, here in the short form without *right in his own eyes*. The same verse says Dan's
  inheritance *had not fallen to them*, *lōʾ-nāpəlâ lô* (WLC). Joshua 19:47 told the same capture as Dan's
  border going *out beyond them*, and Judges 1:34 as the Amorites pressing Dan into the hills. See
  [[Dan Pressed from the Valley - Joshua 19, Judges 1, and Judges 18]] and
  [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].
- **From Zorah and Eshtaol.** The two towns of Samson's beginning and end (13:2, 25; 16:31, stored WEB).
  Samson's tribe, which he never led, now moves away from the Philistine border under no judge at all.
  The camp *behind Kiriath Jearim* is named *Mahaneh Dan* (12); Samson was first moved by the Spirit *in
  Mahaneh Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol* (13:25). The two notices place the camp differently, and the
  wiki does not reconcile them. See [[Mahaneh Dan]].
- **To spy out and to search.** *Ləraggēl ʾet-hāʾāreṣ ûləḥoqrāh* (2, WLC). *Rāgal*, *spy*, is the verb of
  Joseph's accusation in Genesis 42, of Moses' spies at Deuteronomy 1:24, and of Joshua's at Jericho and
  Ai. *Ḥāqar*, *search*, occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges only here (twice) and at
  Deuteronomy 13:14 (English), where Israel must *inquire, and make search* about a city that has gone
  after other gods. See [[Searched Out, Struck, and Burned - Deuteronomy 13 and Laish]].
- **They knew the voice.** *Hikkîrû ʾet-qôl hannaʿar hallēwî* (3, WLC). How they knew it the text does not
  say: an accent from the south, or a voice they had heard before. Readers have proposed both; the wiki
  chooses neither. *Hikkîr*, *recognize*, is the verb of Isaac not recognizing Jacob (Genesis 27:23) and
  of Judah recognizing his pledges (Genesis 38:26), which the wiki notes and builds nothing on.
- **Three questions.** *Who brought you here? What do you do in this place? What do you have here?* (3).
  The Levite answers the first with Micah's name and the third with a wage: *he has hired me*, *wayyiśkərēnî*
  (4, WLC).
- **Before Yahweh.** *Ləkû ləšālôm nōkaḥ YHWH darkəkem* (6, WLC). The answer is true on its face and gives
  nothing away: *before Yahweh* means under his eye, not necessarily approved. Whether the priest asked by
  the ephod and teraphim, or answered on his own, the text does not say.
- **Quiet and secure.** *Šōqēṭ ûḇōṭēaḥ* (7, 27, WLC). *Šāqaṭ* is the verb of the book's refrain *the land
  had rest* (3:11, 30; 5:31; 8:28); its last two uses in Judges describe Laish, the city Israel destroys.
  See [[The Land Had Rest - Joshua 11.23, 14.15, and the Judges Formula]].
- **No one possessing authority.** *Wəʾên-maklîm dāḇār bāʾāreṣ yôrēš ʿeṣer* (7, WLC), a difficult line.
  The WEB reads *there was no one in the land, possessing authority, that might put them to shame in
  anything*. *ʿEṣer* occurs only here in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges; *maklîm*, *put to shame*,
  otherwise only at Numbers 12:14, Miriam's *should she not be ashamed seven days?* (stored WEB). The
  sense — a city with no overlord and no protector — is clear; the exact construction is not, and the
  wiki follows the WEB.
- **The land is very good.** *Wəhinnēh ṭôḇâ məʾōḏ* (9, WLC). Joshua and Caleb at Numbers 14:7 say *ṭôḇâ
  hāʾāreṣ məʾōḏ məʾōḏ*, *an exceeding good land* (stored WEB; WLC). The Danite spies make Caleb's speech
  to a tribe that does not need persuading; Caleb's land was Canaan, theirs is one undefended town. See
  [[The Land Is Very Good - The Spies of Numbers 13-14 and Judges 18]].
- **Broad of hands.** *Wəhāʾāreṣ raḥăḇat yāḏayim* (10, WLC), *the land is large*. The phrase occurs
  elsewhere in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges only at Genesis 34:21, where Hamor and Shechem tell
  their city *the land is large enough for them* before Simeon and Levi destroy it (stored WEB; WLC). There
  the speakers are the victims; here they are the attackers. The wiki records the shared phrase only.
- **No lack.** *ʾên-šām maḥsôr kol-dāḇār* (10, WLC); *maḥsôr* is next at 19:19–20, the old man of Gibeah
  promising the Levite there is *no lack of anything* (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **The six hundred.** *Šēš-mēʾôt ʾîš ḥāḡûr kəlê milḥāmâ* (11, WLC; again 16, 17), a clan's fighting
  force, not a tribe's: Numbers 26:42–43 counts Dan's one family, the Shuhamites, at 64,400 (stored WEB). The number returns at 20:47, where six
  hundred Benjaminites survive at the rock of Rimmon (stored WEB; not yet ingested).
- **Consider what you have to do.** *Wəʿattâ dəʿû mah-taʿăśû* (14, WLC). The spies do not say *take
  them*; the tribe does not ask a second oracle. The idols are taken because they are there.
- **The four objects.** *ʾĒpôd ûtərāpîm ûpesel ûmassēḵâ* (14, 17, WLC). Verse 18 lists the same four;
  verse 20 lists only three, the priest taking *the ephod, the teraphim, and the engraved image*; and
  verses 30–31 speak only of *the engraved image*, *happesel*. Whether the molten image was a separate
  object or part of the carved one is the question already open on
  [[A Carved Image and a Molten Image - Deuteronomy 27.15 and Micah's House]]; the shifting lists do not
  settle it.
- **Hand on mouth.** *Haḥărēš śîm-yādəḵā ʿal-pîḵā* (19, WLC). *Ḥāraš* in this stem is the Gazites
  *quiet all the night* at 16:2 and the father or husband who *holds his peace* over a vow in Numbers 30
  (stored WEB; WLC). The gesture recurs in wisdom texts: *put your hand over your mouth* (Proverbs 30:32)
  and Job's *I lay my hand on my mouth* (Job 40:4; also 21:5) (stored WEB; the Hebrew of Job and Proverbs
  not checked here). See [[Lay Your Hand on Your Mouth - Job 21.5, 29.9, and 40.4]].
- **A father and a priest — again.** *Wehyēh-lānû ləʾāḇ ûləḵōhēn* (19, WLC), Micah's words of 17:10 from
  new employers. Micah offered a house; the Danites offer *a tribe and a family in Israel*. See
  [[Micah's Idol and Levite]].
- **His heart was glad.** *Wayyîṭaḇ lēḇ hakkōhēn* (20, WLC), the verb of Micah's *Yahweh will do good to
  me* (17:13, *yêṭîḇ*). The good Micah expected from the priest goes to the priest instead.
- **My gods which I made.** *ʾEt-ʾĕlōhay ʾăšer-ʿāśîtî ləqaḥtem* (24, WLC). In chapter 17 the image was
  dedicated *to Yahweh*; here Micah calls it *my gods*, and says he made them. The WEB's plural follows
  the plural form *ʾĕlōhîm*; as at 17:5, the Hebrew allows a singular sense too. *What more do I have?*
  (24): the house of gods is empty.
- **The edge of the sword, and fire.** *Wayyakkû ʾôṯām ləp̄î-ḥāreḇ wəʾet-hāʿîr śārəp̄û bāʾēš* (27, WLC).
  Deuteronomy 13:15–16 (English) orders a city that has gone after other gods to be struck *with the
  edge of the sword* and burned *with fire*, and says *it shall not be built again*. The Danites strike
  and burn a city with no idols, carrying idols, and then *built the city* (28). See
  [[Searched Out, Struck, and Burned - Deuteronomy 13 and Laish]].
- **No deliverer.** *Wəʾên maṣṣîl* (28, WLC). *Nāṣal* is the book's word for what Yahweh did for Israel
  (6:9; 8:34; 10:15); at Laish no one does it for the city. In the valley *that lies by Beth Rehob* (28):
  see [[Rehob]].
- **Dan their father, who was born to Israel.** *Bəšēm dān ʾăḇîhem ʾăšer yûllaḏ ləyiśrāʾēl* (29, WLC). The
  ancestor's name, *God has judged me* (Genesis 30:6), is given to the one city in the book taken by a
  tribe with no judge. See [[Dan (person)]] and [[Dan (city)]].
- **Jonathan, son of Gershom, son of Moses.** *Wîhônāṯān ben-gērəšōm ben-mənaššeh* (30, WLC). The WLC
  writes the last name with a *suspended* *nun*, raised above the line, and tags it as *Manasseh*
  (lemma 4519); without the *nun* the consonants are *mšh*, *Moses*. The WEB reads *Moses*. See
  [[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]] and [[Gershom]].
- **Until the captivity of the land.** *ʿAḏ-yôm gəlôṯ hāʾāreṣ* (30, WLC). *Gālâ* in Genesis through
  Judges is otherwise *uncover* or *reveal* (Leviticus 18; Numbers 24:4, 16); *exile* is a new sense
  here in those books, though the curses had threatened scattering (Deuteronomy 28:64). Which captivity
  is meant the text does not say: readers have proposed the Assyrian deportations of the north, or the
  loss of the ark at Shiloh. Verse 31 gives a second limit, *all the time that God's house was in
  Shiloh*, and the two may or may not mark the same time. Recorded, not resolved.
- **God's house in Shiloh.** *Bêt-hāʾĕlōhîm bəšilōh* (31, WLC). The Tent of Meeting was set up at Shiloh
  in Joshua 18:1 (stored WEB). The last words of the chapter set the two sanctuaries side by side: the
  one Israel had, and the one Dan made for itself. See [[Shiloh]].

**How it connects.** The chapter finishes [[Micah's Idol and Levite]] and fills in a line Joshua had
already written: *the children of Dan went up and fought against Leshem, and took it … and called Leshem,
Dan* (Joshua 19:47, stored WEB). See [[Leshem Becomes Dan - Joshua 19 and Judges 18]]. It also explains
the anachronism of Genesis 14:14, where Abram pursues the kings *as far as Dan* before the city had the
name (see [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]).

The spy story inverts three earlier ones. In Numbers 13–14 twelve spies saw a good land and ten made
the people afraid; at Jericho two spies found a Canaanite who confessed Yahweh (Joshua 2); at Bethel
spies spared a man for showing them the way in (Judges 1:22–26). Here five spies find a Levite who gives
them a blessing, and a city with no one to save it. See
[[The Land Is Very Good - The Spies of Numbers 13-14 and Judges 18]],
[[Twelve Spies and Two - Numbers 13 and Joshua 2]], and
[[The Spies at Jericho and at Bethel - Joshua 2 and Judges 1]].

Dan's blessings read differently after this chapter. Jacob said *Dan will judge his people … Dan will be
a serpent on the trail* (Genesis 49:16–17), and Moses, *Dan is a lion's cub that leaps out of Bashan*
(Deuteronomy 33:22) (stored WEB). The narrator does not cite either.

What Dan becomes is outside the stored text: 1 Kings 12:29–30 has Jeroboam set one of his two golden
calves at Dan, so that the city where a Levite served Micah's image becomes a royal shrine of the
northern kingdom. That is reported from the reference and not quoted (1 Kings not yet in raw).

## Notes

**Moses or Manasseh.** The WLC prints *mənaššeh* with the *nun* suspended above the line and tags the
word as the name *Manasseh*. The usual explanation — reported, not checked — is that scribes raised the
letter to spare Moses' name the shame of an idolatrous grandson, leaving the consonants *mšh* readable
underneath; the Septuagint manuscripts and the Vulgate are reported to divide between the two readings.
No Greek or Latin text was consulted for this page. The WEB, following the consonants without the raised
letter, reads *Moses*. The wiki follows the WEB and keeps the question open on
[[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]].

**Is Jonathan Micah's Levite?** The text does not say so in one sentence. The Levite of chapter 17 goes
with the Danites as *a priest to a tribe* (18:19–20); verse 30 names the priests of the tribe's image as
Jonathan and his sons. Most readers take them as one man, and the wiki does too; the page that was
`Micah's Levite` is renamed [[Jonathan (son of Gershom)]] in this pass. If they are one man, the Levite of
Bethlehem Judah is a grandson of Moses — which raises the chronology question of how a grandson of Moses
belongs this late in the book. Readers answer that Judges 17–21 is not in chronological order after
Samson (20:28 places Phinehas, Aaron's grandson, at Bethel during the war with Benjamin), or that *son*
means *descendant*. The wiki records both.

**Did the spies sin by asking the Levite?** They ask a hired priest at an idol shrine, and he gives an
answer in Yahweh's name. The narrator neither confirms nor condemns the oracle; the expedition succeeds.
The wiki does not infer approval from success.

**Mahaneh Dan in two places.** 13:25 sets Mahaneh Dan *between Zorah and Eshtaol*; 18:12 sets it *behind
Kiriath Jearim*, in Judah. Either the name moved, or both descriptions cover one stretch of country, or
the two notices come from different traditions. The text gives no reconciliation, and the wiki offers
none. See [[Mahaneh Dan]].

**Judgment calls.** Kept 18:1–31 as one pericope as planned; the scene at Micah's house (13–26) and the
taking of Laish (27–31) belong to one movement. Renamed `Micah's Levite` to `Jonathan (son of Gershom)`
and fixed its inbound links, because 18:30 gives the name and the identification is the reading the
chapter supports. No page for the five spies, the six hundred, Micah's neighbors, or the people of
Laish; no separate page for Laish or Beth Rehob — Laish is an alias on [[Dan (city)]], and Beth Rehob is
recorded on [[Rehob]], whose existing text already reports the identification as a proposal.
