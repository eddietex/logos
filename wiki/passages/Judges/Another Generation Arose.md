# Another Generation Arose

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 2:6–23

## Summary

The book starts over. Joshua sends the people away, and each goes to his inheritance *to possess the
land* (6). The people serve Yahweh all the days of Joshua and of the elders who outlived him, *who
had seen all the great work of Yahweh* (7). Joshua dies at a hundred and ten and is buried at
Timnath Heres, in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash (8–9). Then that whole
generation is *gathered to their fathers*, and *another generation arose after them, who didn't know
Yahweh, nor the work which he had done for Israel* (10).

What follows is not one story but a pattern, told in general terms before any judge is named (11–19):

1. **Apostasy.** Israel does evil in Yahweh's sight and serves *the Baals* (11). They abandon the God
   of their fathers who brought them out of Egypt, follow the gods of the peoples around them, and
   bow down to them; they *served Baal and the Ashtaroth* (12–13).
2. **Oppression.** Yahweh's anger burns. He gives them to raiders and *sold them into the hands of
   their enemies all around*, so that they can no longer stand (14). Wherever they go, his hand is
   against them, *as Yahweh had spoken, and as Yahweh had sworn* (15).
3. **Deliverance.** Yahweh raises up judges, who save them from the raiders (16). While a judge lives,
   Yahweh is with him and saves them, *for it grieved Yahweh because of their groaning* (18).
4. **Relapse, and worse.** They do not listen even to their judges. They *prostituted themselves to
   other gods* and *quickly turned away from the way in which their fathers walked* (17). When the
   judge dies they turn back and act *more corruptly than their fathers* (19).

The cycle ends in a speech (20–22). Because *this nation transgressed my covenant … and has not
listened to my voice*, Yahweh will no longer drive out any of the nations Joshua left when he died.
They will stay *that by them I may test Israel*, whether they will keep Yahweh's way *as their
fathers kept it, or not*. The last verse looks back: Yahweh left those nations, did not drive them
out *hastily*, and did not give them into Joshua's hand (23).

## Key verses

> **2:7** The people served Yahweh all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great work of Yahweh that he had worked for Israel.

> **2:10** After all that generation were gathered to their fathers, another generation arose after them, who didn’t know Yahweh, nor the work which he had done for Israel.

> **2:16** Yahweh raised up judges, who saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them.

> **2:17** Yet they didn’t listen to their judges; for they prostituted themselves to other gods, and bowed themselves down to them. They quickly turned away from the way in which their fathers walked, obeying Yahweh’s commandments. They didn’t do so.

> **2:18** When Yahweh raised up judges for them, then Yahweh was with the judge, and saved them out of the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge; for it grieved Yahweh because of their groaning by reason of those who oppressed them and troubled them.

> **2:22** that by them I may test Israel, to see if they will keep Yahweh’s way to walk therein, as their fathers kept it, or not.”

## People

- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — sends the people away, dies at a hundred and ten, *the servant of
  Yahweh*, and is buried in his inheritance (6–9). The nations are the ones *Joshua left when he
  died* (21), and Yahweh did not give them into his hand (23).
- [[God]] — the subject of nearly every verb in the cycle: his anger burns, he sells, his hand is
  against them, he raises up judges, he is with the judge, he is grieved, he speaks, he tests, he
  leaves the nations (14–23).
- [[Baal (deity)]] — *the Baals* (11) and *Baal* (13), the first naming of the god in Judges.
- [[Ashtoreth (deity)]] — *the Ashtaroth* (13), named beside Baal.
- [[The Canaanites]] — among *the nations that Joshua left* (21); 3:3 will name them with the others.
- The elders who outlived Joshua (7) and the judges (16–19) are unnamed here and get no page of their
  own. The first named judge is [[Othniel]] (3:9).

## Places

- [[Timnathserah]] — Joshua's grave, here spelled *Timnath Heres* (9).
- [[The Hill Country of Ephraim]] — the region of the grave (9).
- [[Mount Gaash]] — the grave lies north of it (9).
- [[Egypt]] — *who brought them out of the land of Egypt* (12).

## Notes

**A second introduction.** Judges 1:1 began *after the death of Joshua*. Here, in 2:6, Joshua is alive
again, sending the people home. Verses 6–9 retell Joshua 24:28–31, and then the book goes forward
from the same point a second way. Chapter 1 told the period as the tribes' failure to drive out the
Canaanites. Chapter 2 tells it as Israel's failure to keep Yahweh. The two introductions do not
contradict each other; they look at the same years from two sides. The wiki records the doubling and
does not explain how the book was put together.

**Seen, not known.** Joshua 24:31 says the elders *had known* (*yādəʿû*) all the work of Yahweh. Judges
2:7 says they *had seen* (*rāʾû*) it, and adds *great* (*haggādôl*). Then 2:10 says the next generation
*didn't know* (*lōʾ yādəʿû*) Yahweh or his work (all WLC). So the verb Joshua used for the elders is the
one Judges uses, negated, for their children. In Judges, the elders saw; the children did not know. The
earlier wiki page on the two death notices made no claim about these verbs; the Hebrew is now checked
and recorded there too.

**Two spellings of one grave.** Joshua 24:30 has *timnat-seraḥ*; Judges 2:9 has *timnat-ḥeres* (WLC).
The consonants of the second element are the same three letters in reverse order, *s-r-ḥ* and *ḥ-r-s*.
The OpenScriptures lemma data files both under one entry (`8556`, marked `b` and `a`). Whether one
spelling is a scribal transposition, or a deliberate change, is a question for commentaries. The
meaning of *ḥeres* as *sun* is often reported and not checked here.

**Gathered to their fathers.** *Neʾespû ʾel-ʾăbôtāyw* (10, WLC), the niphal of *ʾāsap*. Genesis uses
the same verb for the patriarchs' deaths with a different noun: Abraham and Jacob are each *gathered
to his people* (Genesis 25:8; 49:33, stored WEB). Here the phrase is *to their fathers*, and it is said
of a whole generation at once.

**Arose, and raised up.** Verse 10 says *another generation arose*, *wayyāqom*, qal of *qûm*. Exodus 1:8
has the same form: *wayyāqom melek ḥādāš*, *there arose a new king* (WLC). Verse 16 then has the
hiphil of the same root: *wayyāqem YHWH šōpəṭîm*, *Yahweh raised up judges* (WLC). A generation
*arose* that did not know him, and he *raised up* deliverers for them. The play is in the Hebrew;
the WEB keeps it in English with *arose* and *raised up*.

**"The Baals."** Verse 11 has the plural with the article, *habbəʿālîm*; verse 13 has the singular,
*labbaʿal*, with *lāʿaštārôt*, *the Ashtaroth* (WLC). *Baʿal* is an ordinary Hebrew word for *owner,
master, husband*, and as a divine title it attached to many local gods. The plural is usually read as
the Baal of many places, not many unrelated gods. That reading is reported, not settled here. *The
Ashtaroth* are usually identified with the goddess known elsewhere as Astarte; the wiki records that
as a common identification, not a checked one. See [[Baal (deity)]] and [[Ashtoreth (deity)]].
The word *ʿaštārôt* is also the name of Og's city, [[Ashtaroth]], east of the Jordan. The city and the
goddesses share the name; the text of Judges 2 means the goddesses.

**"Prostituted themselves."** *Zānû ʾaḥărê ʾĕlōhîm ʾăḥērîm* (17, WLC). The verb is *zānâ*, to act as a
prostitute. The Torah had already used it for this: Exodus 34:15–16 warned that a covenant with the
land's inhabitants would lead Israel to *play the prostitute after their gods*, and Deuteronomy 31:16
predicted *this people will rise up, and play the prostitute after the strange gods of the land*
(stored WEB; both *zānâ* in WLC). Deuteronomy 31:16 also uses *qûm*, *rise up*, the verb of Judges
2:10.

**"They quickly turned away."** *Sārû mahēr min-hadderek* (17, WLC). These are the exact words Yahweh
spoke of the golden calf at Sinai: *sārû mahēr min-hadderek ʾăšer ṣiwwîtīm*, *They have turned away
quickly out of the way which I commanded them* (Exodus 32:8; Deuteronomy 9:12, both WLC and stored
WEB). Moses repeats them in the second person at Deuteronomy 9:16. Judges uses them for every
generation of the cycle. See
[[They Turned Aside Quickly - The Golden Calf and the Cycle of Judges]].

**"More corruptly than their fathers."** *Wəhišḥîtû mēʾăbôtām* (19, WLC), hiphil of *šāḥat*. The same
verb is in Deuteronomy 9:12 for the calf: *šiḥēt ʿamməkā*, *your people … have corrupted themselves*
(WLC). Each generation is worse than the one before. The book will show this rather than assert it
again: the judges themselves grow more compromised as the cycle goes on.

**Abandoned, and left.** Israel *abandoned* Yahweh (*wayyaʿazbû*, 12, 13). The nations remain because
*Joshua left* them (*ʿāzab*, 21) when he died (WLC). The same verb names Israel's sin and Joshua's
unfinished work. The text does not draw the link; the shared verb is all it gives.

**Sold.** *Wayyimkərēm*, *he sold them* (14, WLC). The image is of Yahweh selling his own people into
the hand of their enemies. The Song of Moses had put it the same way: *unless their Rock had sold
them*, *ṣûrām məkārām* (Deuteronomy 32:30, stored WEB; WLC). Judges 3:8 will use the verb again of the
first oppressor.

**No cry in the summary.** In 2:11–19 Israel never cries out. Yahweh acts because *it grieved Yahweh
because of their groaning*, *kî yinnāḥēm YHWH minnaʾăqātām* (18, WLC). *Naʾăqâ*, *groaning*, is the
word of Exodus 2:24, *God heard their groaning* (WLC; stored WEB). *Lōḥăṣêhem*, *those who oppressed
them*, is the verb of Exodus 3:9, *the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them* (WLC). So the
judges period is described in the words of the bondage in Egypt. In the stories that follow, Israel
does *cry to Yahweh* (3:9, stored WEB), but the summary makes no repentance the condition for help.
The deliverance comes from Yahweh's compassion, not from Israel's turning.

**"It grieved Yahweh."** The verb is *nāḥam*, niphal (18, WLC). It is the verb of Genesis 6:6, *Yahweh
was sorry that he had made man*, and Exodus 32:14, *Yahweh repented of the evil* (both WLC; stored
WEB). The WEB translates it here as *grieved*, but other versions render the same form as *relent*
or *be moved to pity*; this is reported, not compared here. In Judges 2:18 the cause is Israel's
suffering, and the result is a deliverer. See [[Divine Grief and Relenting]].

**The judges.** *Šōpəṭîm* (16, WLC) is a participle, *those who judge*. In these verses their work is
to *save*, *hôšîaʿ* (16, 18). The text says nothing here of courts or verdicts. Yahweh is *with the
judge* (18), and the saving lasts *all the days of the judge*. The deliverance depends on Yahweh's
presence with one man, and it ends with his life. Joshua's own name is commonly derived from the same
root as *hôšîaʿ*, *yāšaʿ*; that etymology is reported, not checked here, and the OpenScriptures
lemma data gives Joshua's name its own entry (`3091`).

**"This nation."** In verse 20 Yahweh calls Israel *haggôy hazzeh*, *this nation* (WLC). *Gôy* is the
word the same speech uses for *the nations* Joshua left (21, 23). Israel is spoken of with the word
for the peoples it was to replace. The speech does not call Israel *my people*.

**The covenant and the voice.** *Yaʿan ʾăšer ʿābərû … ʾet-bərîtî ʾăšer ṣiwwîtî ʾet-ʾăbôtām wəlōʾ
šāməʿû ləqôlî* (20, WLC). Joshua had warned *when you disobey the covenant of Yahweh your God, which he
commanded you … then Yahweh's anger will be kindled* (Joshua 23:16, stored WEB). The Hebrew of the two
verses shares *ʿābar* with *bərît*, *ṣiwwâ*, and *ḥārâ ʾap*, *anger burned* (WLC). Judges 2:20 reports
that warning fulfilled, in its own words. *Has not listened to my voice* is also the charge of the
angel at Bochim (2:2). See
[[I Brought You Up from Egypt - The Indictments at Bochim and in Gideon's Day]].

**Testing.** *Ləmaʿan nassôt bām ʾet-yiśrāʾēl hăšōmərîm hēm ʾet-derek YHWH … ʾim-lōʾ* (22, WLC).
Deuteronomy 8:2 says the wilderness years were *to prove you, to know what was in your heart, whether
you would keep his commandments, or not* — in Hebrew *lənassōtəkā … hăṯišmōr miṣwōtāw ʾim-lōʾ* (WLC;
stored WEB). The same verb, the same question, the same *or not*. The wilderness tested one
generation; the nations left in the land will test the next. Judges 3:1–4 restates this. See
[[Testing]].

**Three reasons, two of them here.** The nations who stayed now have three explanations in the wiki.
Exodus 23:29–30 and Deuteronomy 7:22 give a slow conquest for the land's sake. Judges 2:3 and 2:21
give a punishment. Judges 2:22 gives a test. Verse 23 then adds a fourth element: Yahweh *did not
drive them out hastily*, *ləbiltî hôrîšām mahēr* (WLC). *Mahēr*, *quickly*, is the word of
Deuteronomy 7:22, *you may not consume them at once* (*mahēr*, WLC). It is also the word for how fast
Israel *turned away* in 2:17. Israel was quick to leave; Yahweh was not quick to drive the nations out.
The wiki does not merge these reasons; see
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].

**Verse 23 and Joshua.** *He didn't deliver them into Joshua's hand* (23). Joshua 21:44 had said *Not
a man of all their enemies stood before them. Yahweh delivered all their enemies into their hand*
(stored WEB). Joshua 13:1 had said *there remains yet very much land to be possessed* (stored WEB).
Judges 2:23 sides with Joshua 13 and gives a reason. It does not contradict Joshua 21:44 outright:
Joshua speaks of the enemies who stood against Israel, Judges of nations Joshua left in the land. But
*delivered … into their hand* and *didn't deliver them into Joshua's hand* sit uneasily together, and
the wiki records both. The tension within Joshua is already recorded on [[Canaan]].

**What is left to the stories.** This pericope is a frame. It names no judge, no enemy, no tribe. Each
story from Othniel to Samson will fill in the frame, and each will depart from it in some way. The
frame's last word on the period is not a hero but a test, *whether they will keep Yahweh's way … or
not*.
