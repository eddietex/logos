# Judah Goes Up First

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 1:1–7

## Summary

*After the death of Joshua*, Israel asks Yahweh who should go up first against the Canaanites
(1). Yahweh answers with a tribe: *Judah shall go up. Behold, I have delivered the land into his
hand* (2). Judah invites Simeon, *his brother*, to fight in Judah's lot, promising to fight in
Simeon's in return, and Simeon goes (3).

Yahweh delivers the Canaanites and the Perizzites into their hand, and they strike ten thousand men
at Bezek (4). There they find Adoni-Bezek, fight him, and rout his army (5). He flees; they catch
him and cut off his thumbs and his big toes (6). The king then speaks the only words given to an
enemy in the pericope: seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off used to scavenge under
his table, and *As I have done, so God has done to me* (7). He is brought to Jerusalem and dies
there.

## Key verses

> **1:1** After the death of Joshua, the children of Israel asked of Yahweh, saying, “Who should go up for us first against the Canaanites, to fight against them?”

> **1:2** Yahweh said, “Judah shall go up. Behold, I have delivered the land into his hand.”

> **1:3** Judah said to Simeon his brother, “Come up with me into my lot, that we may fight against the Canaanites; and I likewise will go with you into your lot.” So Simeon went with him.

> **1:6** But Adoni-Bezek fled. They pursued him, caught him, and cut off his thumbs and his big toes.

> **1:7** Adoni-Bezek said, “Seventy kings, having their thumbs and their big toes cut off, scavenged under my table. As I have done, so God has done to me.” They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.

## Notes

**After the death of Joshua.** The book's first words are a date by a death, as Joshua's were:
*Now after the death of Moses* (Joshua 1:1, stored WEB). But where Joshua continued *Yahweh spoke to
Joshua*, Judges continues *the children of Israel asked of Yahweh*. Moses had a successor appointed
before he died (Numbers 27:18–23). Joshua has none, and the people have to ask. The answer is not a
person but a tribe. Judges will not have a leader for all Israel again until the deliverers begin to
be raised in 3:9, and each of those is raised for one crisis. Judges 2:6–9 then retells Joshua's
death, a flashback treated at [[Joshua's Death Told Twice - Joshua 24.28-31 and Judges 2.6-9]].

**How they asked.** The text says only that they *asked of Yahweh* (*wayyišʾălû … baYHWH*). It
names no priest, no ark, no Urim, and no place. Numbers 27:21 had appointed that Joshua *shall stand
before Eleazar the priest, who shall inquire for him by the judgment of the Urim before Yahweh*
(stored WEB), and Eleazar has just been buried (Joshua 24:33). Whether this inquiry used the Urim is
a reasonable inference from that law, not something the verse states. The same verb returns at
20:18, 23, 27. Hebrew read from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb).

**Judah first.** Judah's precedence is not new. The tribe camped on the east under its standard and
set out first (Numbers 2:3; 10:14), received the first and largest allotment west of the Jordan
(Joshua 15), and was told by Jacob, *your father's sons will bow down before you* (Genesis 49:8,
stored WEB). Here, for the first time, *first* means first into battle.

**Judah and Simeon.** Simeon is *his brother* in two senses: the second and fourth sons of Leah
(Genesis 29:33–35), and the tribe whose lot lay *in the middle of the inheritance of the children of
Judah* (Joshua 19:1, 9; both stored WEB). The offer, *Come up with me into my lot … and I likewise
will go with you into your lot*, treats the two allotments as distinct, which Joshua 19:1–9 also
does. This page records no fulfilment of Judah's half of the bargain yet; verse 17 will report the
two together at Hormah. Simeon and Levi were once cursed together (Genesis 49:5–7); here Simeon is
chosen as an ally, and Judah's tribe goes first with Simeon at its side.

**Canaanites and Perizzites.** The pair is old: *The Canaanites and the Perizzites lived in the land
at that time* (Genesis 13:7), and Jacob feared to be *odious to the inhabitants of the land, among
the Canaanites and the Perizzites* after Simeon and Levi's sword at Shechem (Genesis 34:30; both
stored WEB). The tribe whose violence made Jacob fear these peoples now fights them under Yahweh's
promise.

**Thumbs and big toes.** The WEB's *thumbs and … big toes* renders one Hebrew phrase, *bəhōnôt
yāḏāyw wəraḡlāyw*, *the thumbs (or great digits) of his hands and his feet*. The verb *cut off* (6)
and the participle *having … cut off* (7) are the same root, *qāṣaṣ*, so the king's own description
of what he did matches the narrator's account of what was done to him. Hebrew read from the
Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb). It is often said that the mutilation was meant
to make a man unable to hold a weapon or to run; that is an interpretation, and the text gives no
reason. Israel's maiming of a captive king is unusual in the conquest accounts. Joshua's kings were
killed and hanged (Joshua 10:26). Deuteronomy commanded that the peoples of the land be utterly
destroyed, with no covenant and no mercy (Deuteronomy 7:2, stored WEB), and commanded no mutilation.
The narrator neither commends nor condemns it. The king's own words supply the only evaluation.

**"As I have done, so God has done to me."** This is the first human verdict on God's justice in
Judges, and a Canaanite king makes it. The Hebrew is *kaʾăšer ʿāśîtî kēn šillam-lî ʾĕlōhîm*,
literally *as I have done, so God has repaid me* (WLC, OpenScriptures morphhb). The first clause has
the *kaʾăšer … ʿāśāh … kēn* frame of Leviticus 24:19, *as he has done, so shall it be done to him*.
In place of a second *ʿāśāh*, the king uses *šillam*, *repaid* (lemma *šālam*, in the piel). The
Leviticus wording is as read on the linked connection page. The WEB's *so God has done to me*
smooths the change. He names *ʾĕlōhîm*, not Yahweh. The narrator says *Yahweh* delivered the
Canaanites (4); the king, who never uses that name, attributes his fate to *God*. Whether he means
Israel's God or the divine in general is not stated. See
[[As He Has Done to Me - Proverbs 24.29 and the Law of Retaliation]] and [[Retribution]].

**Seventy kings.** The claim is the king's, and the narrator neither confirms nor questions it.
Seventy is a common round number in the wiki's texts so far (Genesis 46:27; Exodus 1:5; 24:1), and
the count may be a boast. Either way, it reveals the scale of his tyranny over other city-kings.

**Brought to Jerusalem.** The subject of *They brought him* is not specified. The nearest subject is
Judah and Simeon, but verse 8 has Judah take Jerusalem only *after* this, so Judah would be bringing
a captive to a city not yet taken. A common alternative is that his own men brought him home to
Jerusalem. The text does not decide, and this page does not choose. The connection with Jerusalem
also invites comparison with Adoni-Zedek, *king of Jerusalem* (Joshua 10:1, stored WEB). The two
names differ by one element, *Bezek* for *Zedek*. Some readers identify the two kings. It is often
said that the Septuagint of Joshua 10 reads *Adonibezek*, but that claim has not been checked here.
The two narratives differ in place, manner of death, and captor: Adoni-Zedek was killed by Joshua
and hanged, then thrown into the cave at Makkedah (Joshua 10:26–27), while Adoni-Bezek is maimed and
dies at Jerusalem. The wiki keeps them as two people and flags the question on both pages.

**Where Bezek was.** The text does not locate it beyond Judah's campaign. 1 Samuel 11:8 names a Bezek
where Saul counted Israel (*He counted them in Bezek*, WEB checked at bible-api.com). That Bezek is
usually placed north of Shechem, well outside Judah's lot. If it is the same place, Judah fought in
another tribe's territory before turning south. Neither the identification of the two Bezeks nor the
northern location has been verified here.
