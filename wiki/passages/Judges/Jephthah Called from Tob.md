# Jephthah Called from Tob

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 11:1–11

## Summary

The question that closed chapter 10, *Who is the man who will begin to fight against the children of
Ammon?* (10:18), is answered with a name: *Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty man of valor, and he
was the son of a prostitute* (11:1). His father is Gilead. Gilead's wife bears other sons, and when they
grow up they drive Jephthah out: *You will not inherit in our father's house, for you are the son of
another woman* (2). He flees to the land of Tob, and *outlaws* gather to him and go out with him (3).

When Ammon makes war, the elders of Gilead go to Tob to bring him back. They offer him the post of
*chief* for the fight (6). He answers with a charge: *Didn't you hate me, and drive me out of my father's
house? Why have you come to me now when you are in distress?* (7). They raise the offer to *head over all
the inhabitants of Gilead* (8), the reward named in 10:18. He names his condition, *if … Yahweh delivers
them before me, will I be your head?* (9). They swear by Yahweh, *Yahweh will be witness between us*
(10). He goes with them, the people make him *head and chief*, and *Jephthah spoke all his words before
Yahweh in Mizpah* (11).

No angel appears and no prophet speaks. Yahweh does not call Jephthah. The people choose him by
agreement, and Yahweh's name enters the passage first in Jephthah's own condition. The Spirit comes on
him only later, at 11:29.

**The man (11:1–3).**

> **11:1** Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty man of valor, and he was the son of a prostitute. Gilead became the father of Jephthah.

*Jephthah*, *yiptāḥ* (WLC, lemma 3316), *he opens*. The same name is borne by a town of Judah's lowland,
*Iphtah* (Joshua 15:43, same lemma in the WLC). The meaning is from the form of the name; the text gives
no gloss. See [[Jephthah]] and [[Iphtah]].

*A mighty man of valor*, *gibbôr ḥayil* (WLC). Yahweh's angel greeted Gideon with the same two words,
*Yahweh is with you, you mighty man of valor* (6:12, *gibbôr heḥāyil*, WLC). Gideon was the least in his
father's house (6:15). Jephthah has been put out of his.

*The son of a prostitute*, *ben-ʾiššâ zônâ* (WLC, *zānâ*, lemma 2181). It is the word used of Tamar as
Judah saw her (Genesis 38:15), of Rahab (Joshua 2:1), and of the woman Samson visits at Gaza
(Judges 16:1). In Judges the same root has also been used of Israel: *all Israel played the prostitute*
with Gideon's ephod (8:27) and *after the Baals* (8:33). The book gives the judge who follows that
apostasy a mother called by the word used of it. The text does not draw the line; it is the wiki's
observation. See [[Rahab]].

*Gilead became the father of Jephthah*, *wayyôled gilʿād ʾet-yiptāḥ* (WLC). *Gilead* is also the region
and the clan: Numbers 26:29 says *Machir became the father of Gilead; of Gilead, the family of the
Gileadites*. That Gilead belongs to a generation long before Judges. Here Gilead has a wife and sons
(11:2), so the text treats him as a man. Whether a man of Jephthah's day bore the clan's name, or the
verse means only that Jephthah was born a Gileadite, the text does not settle. The wiki gives the father
no separate page. See [[Gilead]], [[Machir]], and [[The Families of Manasseh]].

> **11:2** Gilead's wife bore him sons. When his wife's sons grew up, they drove Jephthah out, and said to him, "You will not inherit in our father's house, for you are the son of another woman."

*They drove Jephthah out*, *wayəgārəšû* (WLC, *gāraš*, lemma 1644). In Judges this is the verb of the
angel at Bochim, *I will not drive them out from before you* (2:3), and of Yahweh's acts in the
prophet's speech (6:9). The book's tribes have *not driven out* the nations (in chapter 1 the verb is
always *yāraš*); here brothers drive out a brother. The verb is also Sarah's: *Cast out this servant and
her son! For the son of this servant will not be heir with my son, Isaac* (Genesis 21:10, *gārēš*, WLC).
Both speeches pair the verb with an inheritance refused to the son of a second woman. The verbs of
inheriting differ: Sarah's is *yāraš*, the brothers' is *nāḥal* (*lōʾ-tinḥal*, WLC). See
[[The Son of Another Woman - Ishmael, Abimelech, and Jephthah]].

*The son of another woman*, *ben-ʾiššâ ʾaḥeret* (WLC). The pairing of *woman* and *another* occurs only
here in the WLC files read for this ingest (Genesis, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges). The law of
the firstborn protected *the son of the hated* wife's right (Deuteronomy 21:15–17), but it speaks of two
wives, not of a prostitute's son. Deuteronomy 23:2 bars *a person born of a forbidden union* from
Yahweh's assembly; the Hebrew is *mamzēr* (Deuteronomy 23:3 in the WLC's numbering), and Judges does not
use that word of Jephthah. The brothers cite no law. See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] and
[[Brotherhood]].

> **11:3** Then Jephthah fled from his brothers, and lived in the land of Tob. Outlaws joined up with Jephthah, and they went out with him.

*The land of Tob*, *ʾereṣ ṭôb* (WLC). Named only here and in 11:5 in the WLC files read. The text gives
no location beyond its being a place the elders of Gilead go to and bring him back from. *Ṭôb* is also
the ordinary word *good*. See [[Tob]].

*Outlaws*, *ʾănāšîm rêqîm* (WLC), *empty men*. Abimelech hired the same men: *vain and light fellows*,
*ʾănāšîm rêqîm ûpōḥăzîm* (9:4, WLC). Those two verses are the only places the phrase occurs in the WLC
files read. Abimelech was the son of a concubine (8:31) and gathered empty men after taking his brothers'
place by murder. Jephthah is the son of a prostitute and gathers them after his brothers take his place
from him. *They went out with him*; the text does not say what they went out to do.

**The elders come (11:4–6).**

> **11:4** After a while, the children of Ammon made war against Israel.

> **11:5** When the children of Ammon made war against Israel, the elders of Gilead went to get Jephthah out of the land of Tob.

> **11:6** They said to Jephthah, "Come and be our chief, that we may fight with the children of Ammon."

*The elders of Gilead*, *ziqnê gilʿād* (WLC). In 10:18 the speakers were *the princes of Gilead*,
*śārê gilʿād*. The text does not say whether they are the same men.

*Chief*, *qāṣîn* (WLC, lemma 7101). A rare word. In the WLC files read it occurs elsewhere only in
Joshua 10:24, *the chiefs of the men of war* whom Joshua told to put their feet on the kings' necks. It
names a commander in the field. The elders first offer only that: lead the fight.

**The bargain (11:7–10).**

> **11:7** Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "Didn't you hate me, and drive me out of my father's house? Why have you come to me now when you are in distress?"

*Didn't you hate me*, *śənēʾtem ʾôtî* (WLC, *śānēʾ*, lemma 8130). Jephthah blames the elders, not only
his brothers. The text has not said they took part in 11:2. Perhaps he means that the elders allowed
it, or settled it; the text does not say. The verb is the one used of Joseph's brothers, who *hated him,
and couldn't speak peaceably to him* (Genesis 37:4, *wayyiśnəʾû*, WLC). Joseph's brothers asked, *Will
you indeed reign over us?* (37:8). Here the man they hated is asked to.

*When you are in distress*, *kaʾăšer ṣar lākem* (WLC). Yahweh has just said to Israel, *Let them save you
in the time of your distress!* (10:14, *bəʿēt ṣārātkem*, WLC). The words are from the same root, though
the WLC files them under two lemmas (6862 and 6869). Yahweh turned away a people who came to him only in
distress; Jephthah asks the elders why they come to him only now. Whether the narrator meant the echo is
not stated. Jephthah does not refuse, as Yahweh did. He bargains.

> **11:8** The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "Therefore we have turned again to you now, that you may go with us, and fight with the children of Ammon. You will be our head over all the inhabitants of Gilead."

*We have turned again to you*, *šabnû ʾēlêkā* (WLC, *šûb*). They do not answer the charge. They answer
with a higher offer.

*Head over all the inhabitants of Gilead*, *ləroʾš ləkōl yōšəbê gilʿād* (WLC). These are the exact words
of the reward promised in 10:18 (*lərōʾš ləkōl yōšəbê gilʿād*). The *chief* of 11:6 has become the
*head*. A commander for the war has become a ruler after it. See [[Kingship and Empire]].

> **11:9** Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "If you bring me home again to fight with the children of Ammon, and Yahweh delivers them before me, will I be your head?"

*If you bring me home again*, *ʾim-məšîbîm ʾattem ʾôtî* (WLC). The same verb *šûb* as the elders' *we
have turned again*, now in the causative. They turn back to him; he asks whether they will bring him
back.

*And Yahweh delivers them before me*, *wənātan YHWH ʾôtām ləpānāy* (WLC). The first mention of Yahweh in
the passage, and it comes from Jephthah. Earlier judges were told by Yahweh, or by his angel or
prophetess, that he would give the enemy into their hand (4:7; 7:9). Jephthah makes it the condition
of a contract. Victory will be Yahweh's, and the headship will be his own.

> **11:10** The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "Yahweh will be witness between us. Surely we will do what you say."

*Yahweh will be witness between us*, *YHWH yihyeh šōmēaʿ bênôtênû* (WLC). The word rendered *witness* is
*šōmēaʿ* (lemma 8085), *one who hears*. It is not *ʿēd*, the word in Laban's *God is witness between me
and you* (Genesis 31:50, WLC), said at a heap in Gilead also called *Mizpah* (31:49). Laban called on God
to *watch*; the elders call on Yahweh to *hear*. The agreement is sworn as an oath. See
[[Oaths and Vows]] and [[A Witness Between Us - Galeed, the Jordan Altar, and the Shechem Stone]].

**Made head at Mizpah (11:11).**

> **11:11** Then Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people made him head and chief over them. Jephthah spoke all his words before Yahweh in Mizpah.

*Head and chief*, *ləroʾš ûləqāṣîn* (WLC). He receives both titles, the one he was offered first and the
one he bargained for. *The people* made him, *hāʿām*. In every earlier cycle Yahweh *raised up* the
savior (3:9, 15) or called him (6:11–14). Here the people make him by agreement, and the agreement is
then said *before Yahweh*. See [[Call and Election]].

*Spoke all his words before Yahweh in Mizpah*. The text does not say what the words were. Most likely
they are the terms just agreed, repeated in Yahweh's presence, since the elders had made Yahweh the
hearer. That is the wiki's reading. *In Mizpah*, *bammiṣpâ*, is where Israel was encamped against Ammon
(10:17). See [[Mizpah (Gilead)]].

**What the passage does.** Chapter 10 ended with Yahweh refusing to save and the princes of Gilead
looking for a man. Chapter 11 gives them one, and gives him the history Israel has with Yahweh: hated,
driven out, and asked back in a time of distress. The rejected son becomes head over the house that
rejected him. Gideon refused rule (8:23); Abimelech took it by murder (9:5); Jephthah takes it by
contract. The passage also prepares the next ones. Jephthah is a man who negotiates, and his next act
is to send a message to Ammon's king, [[Jephthah's Message to the King of Ammon]] (11:12–28). His words *before Yahweh* here are followed by a vow
before Yahweh at 11:30–31.

## Notes

**Boundaries.** The plan's 11:1–11 is kept. 11:1 starts with a new subject, and 11:11 ends with
Jephthah installed and his words said before Yahweh. 11:12 starts a new action, messengers to Ammon's
king.

**Is he called by God?** The passage never says so. The elders call him, the people make him, and
Yahweh is invoked as the hearer of the bargain. Whether Yahweh's silence means disapproval, or only
that the narrator saves the Spirit for 11:29, the text does not say. Hebrews 11:32 is recalled as naming
Jephthah in its list of the faithful; that verse is not stored, and the reference is unverified here.
