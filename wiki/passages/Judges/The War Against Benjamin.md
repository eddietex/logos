# The War Against Benjamin

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 20:1–48

## Summary

*Then all the children of Israel went out, and the congregation was assembled as one man, from Dan even
to Beersheba, with the land of Gilead, to Yahweh at Mizpah* (1): four hundred thousand footmen *who drew
sword*, *in the assembly of the people of God* (2). Benjamin hears of it (3). Asked *how did this wickedness
happen?*, the Levite, *the husband of the woman who was murdered*, tells it: *The men of Gibeah rose against
me … They intended to kill me, and they raped my concubine, and she is dead. I took my concubine, and cut
her in pieces, and sent her throughout all the country of the inheritance of Israel; for they have
committed lewdness and folly in Israel* (4–6). *Give here your advice and counsel* (7).

*All the people arose as one man*: no one goes home until it is done to Gibeah, *by lot*, with one man in
ten to bring food (8–10), and *all the men of Israel were gathered against the city, knit together as one
man* (11). The tribes send through Benjamin: *What wickedness is this … deliver up the men, the wicked
fellows, who are in Gibeah, that we may put them to death, and put away evil from Israel. But Benjamin
would not listen to the voice of their brothers the children of Israel* (12–13). Benjamin gathers at
Gibeah instead: twenty-six thousand swordsmen and seven hundred *chosen men* of Gibeah, and among them
seven hundred left-handed slingers who *could sling a stone at a hair and not miss* (14–16). Israel
without Benjamin numbers four hundred thousand (17).

Three days of battle follow, each opened at Bethel. **The first day**, Israel asks counsel of God, *Who
shall go up for us first to battle against the children of Benjamin?*, and Yahweh says, *Judah first*
(18). Benjamin comes out of Gibeah and kills twenty-two thousand (19–21). **The second day**, after they
*wept before Yahweh until evening* and asked, *Shall I again draw near to battle against the children of
Benjamin my brother?* — *Go up against him* — Benjamin kills eighteen thousand more (22–25). Then all the
people come to Bethel, weep, sit, fast until evening, and offer burnt offerings and peace offerings (26).
*The ark of the covenant of God was there in those days, and Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of
Aaron, stood before it*; they ask, *Shall I yet again go out … or shall I cease?*, and Yahweh says, *Go
up; for tomorrow I will deliver him into your hand* (27–28).

**The third day** is the stratagem of Ai. Israel sets ambushes around Gibeah, draws Benjamin out onto the
highways to Bethel and Gibeah, loses about thirty men, and lets them say *They are struck down before us,
as at the first* (29–32). The main army forms at Baal Tamar; the ambush breaks out *of Maareh Geba*, ten
thousand chosen men, *but they didn't know that disaster was close to them* (33–34). *Yahweh struck
Benjamin before Israel*, and twenty-five thousand one hundred fall (35).

Verses 36–46 tell the same day again, closer up. The ambush rushes the city and strikes it *with the edge
of the sword*; the agreed sign is smoke (36–38). Benjamin kills about thirty and says *as in the first
battle* (39); then *the cloud began to arise up out of the city in a pillar of smoke, the Benjamites
looked behind them; and behold, the whole city went up in smoke to the sky* (40). They break *to the way of
the wilderness*, are caught between the army and the men coming out of the cities, and fall: eighteen
thousand; five thousand *gleaned* on the highways; two thousand at Gidom; twenty-five thousand in all
(41–46). Six hundred reach *the rock of Rimmon* and stay there four months (47). Israel turns back on
Benjamin's towns and strikes *both the entire city, and the livestock, and all that they found. Moreover
all the cities which they found they set on fire* (48).

**What is going on.** The chapter answers the Levite's summons, *Consider it, take counsel, and speak*
(19:30), and it is the only war in Judges that every tribe fights, and fights against a tribe. Its
language is the language of Israel's best moments turned inward: the assembly *as one man*, the question
of 1:1, the oracle *Judah first*, the ambush of Ai, the fire of a city given over. Each element is in its
place, and the result is the near-destruction of a tribe of Israel.

The narrator's own judgments are few. Yahweh answers three times and *struck Benjamin before Israel* (35);
the men of Gibeah are *the wicked fellows* (13); Benjamin *would not listen to the voice of their brothers*
(13). Nothing is said for or against the Levite's speech, the oath not to go home, the forty thousand dead
of the first two days, or the burning of every town in verse 48. Chapter 21 reports the cost:
*why has this happened in Israel, that there should be one tribe lacking in Israel today?* (21:3, stored WEB;
not yet ingested).

**What is notable.**

- **As one man.** *Kəʾîš ʾeḥāḏ* (1, 8, 11, WLC), three times. In the WLC files of Genesis through 1 Samuel
  the phrase occurs otherwise only at Numbers 14:15 (*if you killed this people as one man*), Judges 6:16
  (*you shall strike the Midianites as one man*), and 1 Samuel 11:7, Saul's muster after the cut oxen
  (lemma sequence search). Verse 11 adds *ḥăḇērîm*, *knit together*, a lemma (2270) found in those files only
  here. See [[Cut Limb by Limb - The Burnt Offering, the Concubine, and Saul's Oxen]].
- **From Dan to Beersheba.** *Middān wəʿaḏ-bəʾēr šeḇaʿ* (1, WLC), the first occurrence of the formula in
  Genesis through 1 Samuel; the next is 1 Samuel 3:20 (lemma search, WLC). The northern marker is the city
  that received the name *Dan* only at 18:29. See [[Dan (city)]] and [[Beersheba]].
- **The assembly of the people of God.** *Biqhal ʿam hāʾĕlōhîm* (2, WLC). *ʿAm hāʾĕlōhîm* with *qāhāl*
  occurs only here in the WLC files searched. *Wattiqqāhēl hāʿēḏâ* (1), *the congregation was assembled*,
  is the verb and noun of Joshua 22:12, when *the whole congregation … gathered themselves together at
  Shiloh, to go up against* the eastern tribes. See
  [[Gathered to Go Up Against Their Brothers - Joshua 22 and Judges 20]].
- **The woman who was murdered.** *Hāʾiššâ hanniršāḥâ* (4, WLC). *Rāṣaḥ* is the verb of *You shall not
  murder* (Exodus 20:13) and of the cities of refuge (Numbers 35; Deuteronomy 19; Joshua 20–21). In
  the WLC files of Genesis through Judges this is its only occurrence in a narrative of a killing (lemma
  7523).
- **The Levite's account.** *Baʿălê haggiḇʿâ*, *the lords of Gibeah* (5, WLC; WEB *the men of Gibeah*), the
  title of the *baʿălê šəḵem* of chapter 9. *ʾÔṯî dimmû lahărōḡ*, *me they intended to kill*: the men at
  the door demanded to *know* him (19:22), not to kill him. *They raped my concubine* is *ʿinnû* (5, WLC),
  the verb of 19:24. What he leaves out is the act of 19:25, *the man laid hold of his concubine, and
  brought her out to them*. In 19:29 he *cut her*; in 20:6 he says so, and gives the reason: *for they
  have committed lewdness and folly in Israel*. See [[The Levite (husband of the concubine)]].
- **Lewdness and folly.** *Zimmâ ûnəḇālâ* (6, WLC). In Genesis through Judges *zimmâ* occurs only at
  Leviticus 18:17, 19:29, and 20:14 — the laws of a woman and her daughter, of a daughter made a
  prostitute, and of a man who takes a wife and her mother (WEB *wickedness*) — and here (lemma 2154).
  *Nəḇālâ* is repeated at verse 10. See [[Folly in Israel - Nebalah from Genesis 34 to 2 Samuel 13]].
- **Geba or Gibeah.** In verse 10 the WEB has *Gibeah of Benjamin*; the WLC reads *ləḡeḇaʿ binyāmin*,
  *Geba of Benjamin* (lemma 1387). In verse 33 *Maareh Geba* is tagged in the morphhb data with Gibeah's
  lemma (1390). The two names differ only in their ending, and the WEB reads Gibeah in verse 10. The wiki
  records the Hebrew and follows the WEB. See [[Geba]].
- **Put away evil from Israel.** *Ûnəḇaʿărâ rāʿâ miyyiśrāʾēl* (13, WLC), the formula of Deuteronomy's death
  sentences, in its *from Israel* form (Deuteronomy 17:12; 22:22). Deuteronomy 17:12 applies it to *the
  man who does presumptuously, in not listening to the priest … or to the judge*; here the tribe *would
  not listen*, *lōʾ ʾāḇû … lišmōaʿ* (13). See
  [[Remove the Evil from Among You - Deuteronomy 13.5 and 1 Corinthians 5.13]].
- **Left-handed, and not missing.** *ʾIṭṭēr yaḏ-yəmînô* (16, WLC), the phrase used of Ehud (3:15). *Wəlōʾ
  yaḥăṭiʾ*, *and not miss*, is *ḥāṭāʾ* (lemma 2398), the usual Hebrew verb for *sin*, here in the physical
  sense of missing a mark. The sling, *qālaʿ*, is David's at 1 Samuel 17:49, and the *hair*, *śaʿărâ*, is
  Jonathan's at 14:45 (*not one hair of his head*); in the files searched each word occurs only in these
  verses and 1 Samuel 25:29. See [[Left-Handed Men of Benjamin - Judges 3.15 and 20.16]].
- **Judah first.** *Mî yaʿăleh-lānû battəḥillâ* … *yəhûḏâ battəḥillâ* (18, WLC), the question and the
  tribe of 1:1–2, without 1:2's promise. See [[Who Shall Go Up First - Judges 1.1-2 and 20.18]].
- **Benjamin my brother.** *ʿIm-bənê ḇinyāmin ʾāḥî* (23, 28, WLC). Twice Israel calls the enemy *my brother*,
  in the singular, as one man.
- **Weeping and fasting at Bethel.** *Wayyiḇkû … wayyāṣûmû* (26, WLC). In the WLC files of Genesis through
  Judges this is the only occurrence of the verb *ṣûm*, *to fast* (lemma 6684); the next, in 1 Samuel, is
  at Mizpah (7:6), with burnt offering (7:9). The weeping joins Bochim and the oak below Bethel. See
  [[The Place of Weepers - Allon Bacuth, Bochim, and Bethel]].
- **The ark at Bethel, and Phinehas.** *Wəšām ʾărôn bərîṯ hāʾĕlōhîm bayyāmîm hāhēm* (27, WLC). The ark is
  not mentioned elsewhere in Judges; the stored text last had the tent at Shiloh (Joshua 18:1) and God's
  house *in Shiloh* (18:31). Verses 27–28 are a parenthesis, and *in those days* twice dates the scene to
  the time of Phinehas, Aaron's grandson, who stopped the plague at Peor (Numbers 25:7–8) and led the
  embassy of Joshua 22. A Phinehas still serving puts the epilogue early in the period of the judges, as
  *Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Moses* did in 18:30 — the grandsons of the two brothers, one
  at the image at Dan and one before the ark. See [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]].
- **Or shall I cease?** *ʾIm-ʾeḥdāl* (28, WLC). The third inquiry is the first to offer stopping as an
  option, and the only one answered with a promise, *kî māḥār ʾettənennû bəyāḏeḵā*.
- **Ai, retold.** Verses 29–43 share with Joshua 8 the ambush, *ʾōrēḇ* (20:29, 33, 36–38; Joshua 8:2);
  *as at the first*, *kəḇāriʾšōnâ* (32; Joshua 8:5–6 *kaʾăšer bāriʾšōnâ*); *drawn away*, *nātaq* (31–32;
  Joshua 8:6, 16); *didn't know*, *lōʾ yāḏaʿ* (34; Joshua 8:14); *the appointed* time or sign, *môʿēḏ* (38;
  Joshua 8:14); *looked behind them* and the city's smoke going up *to the sky*, *haššāmaymâ* (40; Joshua
  8:20); and *the way of the wilderness* (42; Joshua 8:15) (WLC). See
  [[Drawn Away from the City - Ai and Gibeah]].
- **A pillar of smoke.** *ʿAmmûḏ ʿāšān* (40, WLC), in the files searched only here. *ʿAmmûḏ* is the pillar of
  cloud and fire in the wilderness (Exodus 13:21–22), and in Judges before this, the pillars of Dagon's
  house that Samson pulled down (16:25–29). *Maśʾaṯ heʿāšān*, *the cloud of smoke* (38; *hammaśʾēṯ*, 40),
  uses a noun that occurs elsewhere in Genesis through 1 Samuel only as Benjamin's portion *five times as
  much* at Joseph's table (Genesis 43:34, lemma 4864). The wiki notes that coincidence and builds nothing
  on it.
- **The whole city went up.** *Wəhinnēh ʿālâ ḵəlîl-hāʿîr haššāmaymâ* (40, WLC). *Kālîl*, *whole*, is the
  whole burnt offering (Deuteronomy 33:10; 1 Samuel 7:9), and it is the word of Deuteronomy 13:16 (Hebrew
  13:17): the apostate Israelite city is burned *with all of its plunder, kālîl, to Yahweh your God*. The
  WEB there leaves *kālîl* untranslated. Verses 37 and 48 add the law's *edge of the sword* and *livestock*.
  See [[The Whole City Went Up - Deuteronomy 13 and Gibeah]].
- **Gleaned.** *Wayəʿōləluhû* (45, WLC), *gleaned* (lemma 5953 b). It is a different tagged sense from the
  *abused* of 19:25 (5953 a), and the wiki draws no link between them.
- **The resting place.** *Mənûḥâ hiḏrîḵuhû* (43, WLC), WEB *trod them down at their resting place*.
  *Mənûḥâ* is the *rest* of Deuteronomy 12:9 (*you haven't yet come to the rest and to the inheritance*);
  some read it here as a place-name, *from Menuhah* — reported, not checked. The verse is difficult, and
  *kittərû*, *surrounded*, occurs only here in the files searched.
- **The numbers.** Benjamin musters 26,700 (15); 25,100 fall in verse 35 and 25,000 in verse 46
  (18,000 + 5,000 + 2,000, 44–45); 600 survive (47). Neither total plus the survivors reaches the muster:
  about a thousand are unaccounted for. Israel loses 22,000 and 18,000 on the first two days and *about
  thirty* on the third, given twice (31, 39). The text does not reconcile the figures. That *ʾelep*,
  *thousand*, can also mean a clan unit, which would shrink all these numbers, is a proposal readers make
  — reported, not checked.
- **The entire city.** *Mēʿîr məṯōm ʿaḏ-bəhēmâ* (48, WLC). *Məṯōm*, *soundness*, occurs nowhere else in the
  files searched; a slight change of vowels gives *məṯîm*, *men*, *from the city of men to the beasts*.
  Reported, not checked. The WEB's *the entire city* follows *məṯōm*.

**How it connects.** The chapter closes three threads the wiki has followed. The question *who shall go up
first* has framed the book since 1:1. Benjamin's left-handed men began with Ehud, who saved Israel; here
seven hundred of them fight it. And the conquest's stratagem returns from Ai, turned on an Israelite
town. The assembly *as one man*, the cut body sent through the land, and the Benjamite town of Gibeah
all return with Saul in 1 Samuel 10–11 — *Gibeah of Saul*, the oxen cut in pieces, and *they came out as
one man* (1 Samuel 11:4, 7; WEB checked at bible-api.com; not stored). Hosea's *Israel, you have sinned
from the days of Gibeah … The battle against the children of iniquity doesn't overtake them in Gibeah*
(10:9, WEB checked at bible-api.com) names a battle at Gibeah; whether he means this one, the wiki does
not decide.

## Notes

**Was the war just?** The text gives both sides their facts and does not sum them. For the assembly: the
crime was real, the tribes first demanded only *the wicked fellows* (13), and Yahweh answered every
inquiry and finally *struck Benjamin* (35). Against: the case was put by a man who left out his own act;
the oath not to go home came before any inquiry of God; the oracle twice sent Israel to a defeat; and verse
48 strikes every town and its animals, far past the men of Gibeah. Readers weigh these differently. The
wiki records them and does not give a verdict.

**Why the first two defeats?** The text does not say. *Judah first* answers only *who*, not *whether*; the
second answer, *Go up against him*, is a command without a promise; only the third, after fasting and
sacrifice, promises victory. Some read the defeats as judgment on Israel too, others as a test, others as
the cost of a war asked about in the wrong order. None of these is stated.

**One day told twice.** Verse 35 already gives the day's result; verses 36–46 go back to the ambush and
tell it again, with its own *about thirty* and its own total. The wiki reads 36–46 as a second account
of the same day. Whether two sources were joined is a question readers raise; the text does not answer it.

**Mizpah.** Judges 20:1 does not say which Mizpah. The wiki links Benjamin's [[Mizpeh]] (Joshua 18:26),
which is near Gibeah and Bethel; Jephthah's [[Mizpah (Gilead)]] is across the Jordan. The identification
is commonly made and not stated in the text.

**Judgment calls.** Kept 20:1–48 as one pericope as planned; the assembly (1–17) and the battle (18–48)
are one movement and chapter 21 opens with a new problem. Created place pages for [[Baal Tamar]],
[[The Rock of Rimmon]], and [[Gidom]], each named once in the stored text so far (the rock returns in
21:13). Filed *Maareh Geba* on [[Geba]] rather than as its own place, because the Hebrew *maʿărê* may be a
common noun (*the bare place* of Geba) — reported, not checked. No person pages for the groups listed above.
