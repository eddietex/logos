# The Death of Samson

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 16:23–31

## Summary

*The lords of the Philistines gathered them together to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god, and
to rejoice; for they said, "Our god has delivered Samson our enemy into our hand"* (23). The people see
him and praise their god in the same words, adding *the destroyer of our country, who has slain many of
us* (24).

*When their hearts were merry*, they call for Samson *that he may entertain us*, bring him *out of the
prison*, and *he performed before them*. They set him *between the pillars* (25). He says to *the boy who
held him by the hand*, *Allow me to feel the pillars whereupon the house rests, that I may lean on them*
(26). The house is full; *all the lords of the Philistines were there*, and *on the roof about three
thousand men and women, who saw while Samson performed* (27).

*Samson called to Yahweh, and said, "Lord Yahweh, remember me, please, and strengthen me, please, only
this once, God, that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes"* (28). He takes hold of
*the two middle pillars*, *the one with his right hand, and the other with his left* (29). *Let me die with
the Philistines!* He bows *with all his might*, and the house falls *on the lords, and on all the people
who were therein. So the dead that he killed at his death were more than those who he killed in his life*
(30).

*His brothers and all the house of his father* come down, carry him up, and bury him *between Zorah and
Eshtaol in the burial site of Manoah his father. He judged Israel twenty years* (31).

**What is going on.** The Philistines hold a festival of thanks for a victory their god did not win.
Their song, *Our god has delivered … into our hand*, is the formula of Judges for what Yahweh does for
Israel. The reader knows the truth the feast cannot: Samson was given up by his own secret and a paid
woman, and *Yahweh had departed from him* (16:20). The pericope answers the boast. The god they thank is
in the house that falls.

The story ends where it began. Samson's first stirring of the Spirit was *between Zorah and Eshtaol*
(13:25), and he is buried *between Zorah and Eshtaol* (31). The angel said he would be a Nazirite *from the
womb to the day of his death* (13:7). This is that day.

**What is notable.**

- **Dagon.** *Lədāgôn ʾĕlōhêhem*, *to Dagon their god* (23, WLC, lemma 1712). In the WLC files of Genesis
  through Judges, Dagon as a god occurs only here; the name is otherwise inside the place-name Beth Dagon
  (Joshua 15:41; 19:27). 1 Samuel 5:1–7 will put the ark *into the house of Dagon* in Ashdod (WEB at
  bible-api.com). What sort of god Dagon was is not said in Judges; a popular derivation from *dāg*,
  *fish*, and a grain derivation are both reported, not checked, and the wiki builds nothing on either.
  See [[Dagon]] and [[Dagon Fallen - Samson's Pillars and the Ark in Ashdod]].
- **Our god has delivered him into our hand.** *Nātan ʾĕlōhênû bəyādēnû* (23, 24, WLC). In Judges this is
  Yahweh's formula for Israel's wars: *Yahweh has delivered your enemies the Moabites into your hand*
  (3:28); *I will deliver him into your hand* (4:7); *I have delivered it into your hand* (7:9). Here it is
  in Philistine mouths, given to Dagon.
- **The destroyer of our country.** *Maḥărîb ʾarṣēnû* (24, WLC, *ḥārab*, lemma 2717 b). Delilah's
  first binding was *seven green cords that were never dried*, *ʾăšer lōʾ-ḥōrābû* (16:7–8, WLC, lemma
  2717 a). The WLC data gives the two different lemma senses, *lay waste* and *dry up*; the sound repeats,
  and the wiki does not claim a pun.
- **To rejoice; their hearts were merry.** *Ûləśimḥâ* (23) and *kəṭôb libbām* (25, WLC; the consonantal
  text has *kî ṭôb*, the margin *kəṭôb*). Merry hearts at a feast run through the book's last chapters:
  the Levite's father-in-law urges him to *let your heart be merry* (19:6, 9, stored WEB; not yet
  ingested).
- **Entertain us; he performed.** Two spellings of one root: *wîśaḥeq-lānû*, *that he may entertain us*
  (śāḥaq, lemma 7832), and *wayṣaḥēq lipnêhem*, *he performed before them* (ṣāḥaq, lemma 6711), then
  *biśḥôq šimšôn*, *while Samson performed* (27) (all WLC). *Ṣāḥaq* is Isaac's name-verb, *God has made me
  laugh* (Genesis 21:6), and in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges it occurs otherwise only in
  Genesis (17:17; 18:12–15; 19:14; 21:6, 9; 26:8; 39:14, 17) and Exodus 32:6, *rose up to play* at the
  golden calf (stored WEB). What Samson did before them, sport, mockery, or feats, the text does not say.
- **The prison.** *Mibbêt hāʾăsûrîm* (25, WLC; consonantal *hāʾăsîrîm*), the same phrase and the same
  variant as 16:21: the house of the bound. See [[Samson and Delilah]].
- **The pillars.** *ʿAmmûdîm* (25, 26, 29, WLC, lemma 5982). In Judges the word occurs otherwise only at
  20:40, *a pillar of smoke*. In the WLC files of Genesis through Deuteronomy it is the pillar of cloud and
  fire (Exodus 13:21–22) and the posts of the tabernacle (Exodus 26–27). Samson is set *between the
  pillars* and takes *the two middle pillars*, *ʿammûdê hattāwek* (29); how the building stood on them is
  not described, and the wiki does not reconstruct it.
- **The boy who held his hand.** *Hannaʿar hammaḥăzîq bəyādô* (26, WLC): *ḥāzaq*, *hold firm*, the verb
  of Samson's prayer two verses later, *wəḥazzəqēnî*, *strengthen me* (28). The boy holds him; he asks
  God to make him strong. *Naʿar* is unnamed and has no page.
- **Let me feel.** *Hămîšēnî* (26, WLC margin; consonantal *hêmišēnî*), from *mûš* (lemma 4184). In the WLC
  files of Genesis through Judges *mûš* occurs otherwise only in Genesis 27:21, the blind Isaac: *Please
  come near, that I may feel you, my son* (stored WEB); 27:12 and 27:22 use the related *māšaš*. Two blind
  men feel their way, one to bless and one to destroy. See [[Two Blind Fathers - Genesis 27 and Genesis 48]].
- **The roof.** *ʿAl-haggāg* (27, WLC, lemma 1406), *about three thousand men and women* watching. In the
  WLC files of Genesis through Judges *gāg* is otherwise the top of the incense altar (Exodus 30:3; 37:26),
  the parapet law (Deuteronomy 22:8), Rahab's roof where she hid the spies (Joshua 2:6, 8), and the roof of
  the tower at Thebez where the men and women of the city fled from Abimelech (Judges 9:51, all stored WEB).
  At Thebez a woman on the roof killed Abimelech; here the roof falls with the crowd on it. See
  [[The Fall of Abimelech]].
- **Lord Yahweh.** *ʾĂdōnāy YHWH* (28, WLC; the divine name pointed for *ʾĕlōhîm*, lemma 3069). In Judges
  the address occurs otherwise only at 6:22, Gideon's *Alas, Lord Yahweh!* when he sees the angel.
- **Remember me.** *Zākərēnî nāʾ* (28, WLC, *zākar*, lemma 2142). In Judges *zākar* occurs otherwise only
  at 8:34, *The children of Israel didn't remember Yahweh their God*, and 9:2, Abimelech's *Remember also
  that I am your bone and your flesh*. Israel forgot Yahweh; Samson asks to be remembered. See
  [[Remember Me - Genesis 40.14 and the Prayers of the Condemned]] and [[Remembrance]].
- **Only this once.** *ʾAk happaʿam hazzeh* (28, WLC): Gideon's words to God, *I will speak but this once*,
  *ʾak happaʿam* (6:39). *Paʿam*, *time*, has threaded the Delilah story: *these three times* (16:15), *this
  once* (16:18), *as at other times* (16:20). See
  [[Don't Let Your Anger Be Kindled - Genesis 18.30-32 and Judges 6.39]].
- **One vengeance for my two eyes.** *Wəʾinnāqəmâ nəqam-ʾaḥat miššətê ʿênay* (28, WLC), literally *that I
  may be avenged with one vengeance for my two eyes*; the WEB's *at once* renders *ʾaḥat*, *one*. The verb is
  the verb of 15:7, *surely I will take revenge on you*; in Judges *nāqam* occurs only in those two verses.
  The prayer asks nothing for Israel. It asks for his eyes. See [[Vengeance]].
- **His strength, his might.** *Wayyēṭ bəkōaḥ* (30, WLC): *he bowed with strength*, *kōaḥ*, the word of
  *your great strength* that Delilah asked after six times (16:5, 6, 9, 15, 17, 19). In Judges *kōaḥ*
  occurs otherwise only at 6:14, *Go in this your might*, to Gideon. The strength he lost to her returns once,
  asked for.
- **Let me die with the Philistines.** *Tāmôt napšî ʿim-pəlištîm* (30, WLC), literally *let my soul die*.
  Balaam's wish uses the same two words: *Let me die the death of the righteous!*, *tāmōt napšî môt
  yəšārîm* (Numbers 23:10, stored WEB; WLC). See [[Let Me Die - Balaam's Wish and Samson's]].
- **More at his death than in his life.** *Hammētîm ʾăšer hēmît bəmôtô rabbîm mēʾăšer hēmît bəḥayyāyw*
  (30, WLC). The narrator gives the sum without praise or blame. Lehi was *a thousand men* (15:16);
  the roof alone held about three thousand (27).
- **His brothers and his father's house.** *ʾEḥāyw wəkol-bêt ʾābîhû* (31, WLC). Samson's mother was barren
  (13:2), and no brothers have appeared. The text does not say whether these are younger brothers or
  kinsmen; *ʾāḥ* covers both elsewhere in Judges (*my brothers, the sons of my mother*, 8:19, distinguishes
  them). Manoah is named again, now only as the owner of a grave.
- **The burial formula.** *Wayyiqbərû ʾôtô … bəqeber mānôaḥ ʾābîw* (31, WLC), the form of Gideon's burial
  *in the tomb of Joash his father* (8:32), and the minor judges' notices, *judged Israel … and died, and
  was buried* (10:2; 12:7). *He judged Israel twenty years* repeats 15:20. See
  [[The Minor Judges - Judges 10.1-5 and 12.8-15]] and [[Burial and the Grave]].

**How it connects.** The scene closes the frame of Samson's beginning and end: the Spirit *began*
between Zorah and Eshtaol (13:25) and the grave is between them (31); the angel's Nazirite *to the day of
his death* (13:7) is closed by this death. The *begin to save* of 13:5 is fulfilled only as a beginning:
the lords are dead, but the Philistines are still Israel's enemy into 1 Samuel. See
[[He Shall Begin to Save - Judges 13.5 and the Philistine Wars of Samuel]] and
[[The Announcement of Samson's Birth]].

Samson prays twice, both times at the edge of death: for water at Lehi, *shall I die of thirst?* (15:18),
and for strength here, followed by *Let me die*. Both are answered. The text does not say that Yahweh
answered the second; it says the house fell. See [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]] and
[[Prayer]].

Dagon's house will fall again. In 1 Samuel 5 the Philistines set the captured ark beside Dagon in Ashdod,
and twice Dagon is found *fallen on his face to the ground before Yahweh's ark* (5:3–4, WEB at
bible-api.com). Judges ends the Philistines' feast with their god's house down; 1 Samuel ends their
triumph with their god's image down. See [[Dagon Fallen - Samson's Pillars and the Ark in Ashdod]].

## Notes

**Was it suicide?** The text says *Let me die with the Philistines*, and he dies by his own act. It does
not call the act sin or sacrifice, and it does not say that Yahweh answered the prayer, only that the
house fell. Hebrews 11:32 names Samson among those of faith without naming this act. Interpreters divide
on whether the death is a judge's last victory or a man's last revenge, and the prayer itself gives both
readings their verse: it is addressed to Yahweh, and it asks for *my two eyes*. The wiki records both and
does not choose.

**The Spirit is not named.** At Timnah, Ashkelon, and Lehi *Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on him* (14:6,
19; 15:14). Here Samson prays and the house falls; the Spirit is not mentioned. The hair *began to grow
again* (16:22), but the text does not say the strength came back with it, nor that it came from the hair.
See [[The Spirit Rushed on Him - Samson, Saul, and David]].

**Where was the house?** Gaza is the city of the prison (16:21), and verse 25 brings Samson *out of the
prison* to the feast, so the narrative places the house in Gaza; verses 23–31 do not name the city.

**Three thousand on the roof.** *Kišlōšet ʾălāpîm*, *about three thousand* (27, WLC, with the
comparative *kə-*). Judah's three thousand bound him at Etam (15:11). The repetition is noted; the wiki
does not read it as a pattern.

**Judgment calls.** Kept 16:23–31 as planned, the end of the Samson cycle and of the judges proper
(3:7–16:31). New pages: [[Dagon]] (filed with the deities under people, as [[Chemosh]] is), and two
connections, on Dagon's fall and on *let me die*. The boy, the brothers, and the three thousand get no
pages; the house of Dagon is filed on [[Dagon]] and [[Gaza]] rather than given a place page.
