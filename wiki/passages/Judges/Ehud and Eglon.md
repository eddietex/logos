# Ehud and Eglon

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 3:12–30

## Summary

The second judge's story opens with the same formula as the first, and then the formula gives way to
a story. Othniel's took five verses and had no speech. Ehud's takes nineteen, and almost all of it is
detail: a sword and its length, a fat king, a locked door, a key, waiting servants.

**Sin and oppression (12–14).** *The children of Israel again did that which was evil in Yahweh's
sight, and Yahweh strengthened Eglon the king of Moab against Israel* (12). Eglon gathers *the
children of Ammon and Amalek*, strikes Israel, and takes *the city of palm trees*, which Deuteronomy
34:3 names as Jericho. Israel serves him eighteen years, more than twice as long as it served Cushan
Rishathaim.

**Cry and savior (15).** *When the children of Israel cried to Yahweh, Yahweh raised up a savior for
them, Ehud the son of Gera, the Benjamite, a left-handed man.* In the same verse Israel sends the
tribute to Eglon by him. The man Yahweh has raised up is also the man carrying Israel's payment.

**The sword (16–18).** Ehud makes *a sword which had two edges, a cubit in length*, and straps it
*under his clothing on his right thigh*. He presents the tribute. The narrator adds one fact about the
king: *Now Eglon was a very fat man* (17). Ehud sends the tribute-bearers away.

**The two messages (19–20).** At *the stone idols that were by Gilgal* Ehud turns back alone and says,
*I have a secret message for you, O king.* The king says *Keep silence!*, and everyone leaves. Ehud
comes to him in *the cool upper room*, where he sits *by himself alone*, and says a second thing: *I
have a message from God to you.* Eglon rises from his seat.

**The killing (21–22).** *Ehud put out his left hand, and took the sword from his right thigh, and
thrust it into his body.* The handle goes in after the blade, the fat closes over it, and Ehud leaves
it there.

**The locked doors (23–25).** Ehud goes out, shuts and locks the doors of the upper room. The servants
find them locked and say *Surely he is covering his feet in the upper room* (24). They wait *until
they were ashamed*. Then they take the key, open the doors, *and behold, their lord had fallen down
dead on the floor* (25).

**The escape and the war (26–29).** *Ehud escaped while they waited*, passes the stone idols, and
reaches Seirah. He blows a trumpet *in the hill country of Ephraim*, and Israel comes down after him.
*Follow me; for Yahweh has delivered your enemies the Moabites into your hand* (28). They take *the
fords of the Jordan* and let no one cross. About ten thousand Moabites die, *every strong man, and
every man of valor. No man escaped* (29).

**Rest (30).** *So Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel. Then the land had rest eighty
years.*

Ehud is the cycle's second savior, and the first one the book shows working. He does not receive the
Spirit; the text never says the Spirit came on him. He gets a plan instead, and a weapon he made
himself, and he uses a lie, or something close to one, to get the room cleared. Yahweh is named at the
start (12, 15) and once at the end, in Ehud's own mouth (28). In between, the narrator does not name
him at all.

## People

- [[Ehud]] — the savior, a left-handed Benjamite (15–30).
- [[Eglon (person)]] — *the king of Moab*, Israel's master for eighteen years; killed in his upper
  room (12–25).
- [[God]] — strengthens Eglon, raises up Ehud, and is said by Ehud to have delivered Moab (12, 15,
  28).
- [[Benjamin]] — Ehud's tribe, *the Benjamite* (15).
- [[Amalek]] — *Amalek*, Eglon's ally (13).
- Gera, Ehud's father, is named once (15) and does nothing. He gets no page; see Notes.
- Eglon's servants (24–25) and the tribute-bearers (18) are unnamed.

## Notes

**What the formula keeps and what it drops.** The opening follows Othniel's in its order (all WLC):
the evil done, now *wayyōsipû … laʿăśôt hāraʿ bəʿênê YHWH* (12), *they again did evil*, the same
opening as 4:1; then the oppressor; then *served … eighteen years* (14); then *wayyizʿăqû …
ʾel-YHWH* (15), and *wayyāqem YHWH lāhem môšîaʿ* (15). Four things from Othniel's story are missing. There is no *anger
burned* and no *sold them*: here Yahweh *strengthened* the enemy instead. There is no Spirit. And the
narrator never says Ehud *judged Israel*. He is called *môšîaʿ*, *savior*, and nothing else. Judges
4:1 assumes his role (*when Ehud was dead*, stored WEB), but the verb *šāpaṭ* is not used of him.

**"Yahweh strengthened Eglon."** *Wayḥazzēq YHWH ʾet-ʿeglôn* (12, WLC). The verb is *ḥāzaq* in the
intensive stem, the same form as *wayḥazzēq YHWH ʾet-lēb parʿōh*, *Yahweh hardened the heart of
Pharaoh* (Exodus 9:12, WLC; also 10:20, 27; 11:10; 14:8). Joshua 11:20 uses it of the Canaanite kings'
hearts. But there the object is always *the heart*. Here it is Eglon himself: Yahweh made him strong,
not stubborn. The English *strengthened* and *hardened* hide the shared verb, and the Hebrew shows the
difference in object. The verse says the reason twice, at both ends: *because they had done that which
was evil in Yahweh's sight*.

**The coalition.** Moab gathers *the children of Ammon and Amalek* (13). Moab and Ammon are the two
nations born in Lot's cave (Genesis 19:37–38), and Deuteronomy 2:9 and 2:19 forbade Israel to fight
them. Amalek is the nation of Exodus 17 and Deuteronomy 25:17–19. All three are here on Israel's side
of the Jordan. See [[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].

**The city of palm trees.** *ʿîr hattəmārîm* (13, WLC), the name Deuteronomy 34:3 gives Jericho
(*yərēḥô ʿîr hattəmārîm*, WLC). Joshua burned Jericho and cursed whoever rebuilt it (Joshua 6:26). The
text does not say Eglon rebuilt it, only that Moab *possessed* (*wayyîrəšû*) it, the verb Israel was
told to do to the land. The Kenites had just *gone up* from the same place (1:16, stored WEB).

**"The Benjamite, a left-handed man."** *Ben-hayəmînî ʾîš ʾiṭṭēr yad-yəmînô* (15, WLC). *Ben-yāmîn*
means *son of the right hand*, the name Jacob gave in Genesis 35:18. The phrase for *left-handed* is
literally *bound* or *restricted as to his right hand*. So the verse calls him a son of the right hand
whose right hand is restricted. The WLC files read for this ingest (Genesis through 1 Samuel) have
*ʾiṭṭēr* only here and at Judges 20:16, where Benjamin has *seven hundred chosen men who were
left-handed* (stored WEB). Whether *ʾiṭṭēr* means a disability, natural left-handedness, or training to
fight left-handed is not settled by the two verses. See
[[Left-Handed Men of Benjamin - Judges 3.15 and 20.16]].

**Tribute.** *Minḥâ* (15, 17, 18; WLC), four times in four verses. It is also the ordinary word for
the grain offering brought to Yahweh. The text uses *wayyaqrēb … hamminḥâ*, *he brought near the
offering* (17, WLC), the verb Leviticus 2:8 uses for bringing the grain offering to the priest (WLC). The narrator does not draw the comparison; the
vocabulary is there for the reader to notice.

**The sword.** *Ḥereb wəlāh šənê pêyôt gōmed ʾorkāh* (16, WLC): *a sword with two mouths, a gōmed its
length*. *Gōmed* occurs nowhere else in the WLC files read (Genesis through 1 Samuel). The WEB gives
*a cubit*, but the length of a *gōmed* is not known; most take it as shorter than the standard cubit.
That is reported, not checked. A short blade on the right thigh, drawn with the left hand, is the
whole plan.

**"A very fat man."** *ʾÎš bārîʾ məʾōd* (17, WLC). *Bārîʾ* is the word for the *fat* cattle of
Pharaoh's dream (Genesis 41:2, 4, 5, 7, 18, 20; WLC); in those WLC files those are its only other
uses. The fat cattle were eaten in the dream; here the fat man is killed. The name *ʿeglôn* is spelled
with the same three root letters as *ʿēgel*, *calf*. That is often read as a joke on the king, a
fatted calf brought a *minḥâ*. The connection is reported, not checked: the OpenScriptures lemma data
gives *ʿeglôn* its own entry (5700), and the same entry covers the city of Eglon in Joshua 10 and 15,
so the name was not unique to him.

**The stone idols.** *Happəsîlîm ʾăšer ʾet-haggilgāl* (19, again at 26; WLC). Elsewhere in the WLC
files read, *pəsîlîm* are the *engraved images* of the nations that Israel must burn (Deuteronomy 7:5,
25; 12:3; stored WEB) and Micah's *engraved image* (Judges 18:17). The WEB translates *stone idols*.
Some readers take them as quarries, and some identify them with the twelve stones Joshua set up at
Gilgal (Joshua 4:20). The text says neither; both are reported, not checked. What the text does show
is that the stones mark a boundary: Ehud turns back at them (19) and is safe once he has passed them
(26).

**Two messages.** *Dəbar-sēter lî ʾēlêkā* (19) and *dəbar-ʾĕlōhîm lî ʾēlêkā* (20, WLC). The sentences
are the same except for one word: *a secret word* and *a word of God*. *Dābār* means both *word* and
*thing*. What Ehud has for the king is a sword. The first message clears the room; the second brings
Eglon to his feet. Whether Eglon stood out of respect for a divine word, or simply stood, the text does
not say. It is the only thing the pericope reports Eglon doing on his own.

**The killing.** Verse 22 is hard to translate. *Wayyēṣēʾ happaršədōnâ* (22, WLC) is one word, used
nowhere else in the WLC files read; the WEB renders it *it came out behind*, and others take it as
excrement or as Ehud going out by some opening. The next verse has another such word: *wayyēṣēʾ ʾēhûd
hammisdərônâ*, which the WEB gives as *onto the porch* (23). That word also occurs nowhere else in the
WLC files read. Both hapax claims are limited to Genesis through 1 Samuel. What is clear: the sword
went in hilt and all, *the fat closed on the blade*, and Ehud *didn't draw the sword out* (22). The
verb for *draw* is *šālap*, the word Judges 20:15 uses for Benjamin's *men who drew sword* (WLC).

**"He thrust," "he blew."** *Wayyitqāʿehā bəbiṭnô*, *he thrust it into his belly* (21), and
*wayyitqaʿ baššôpār*, *he blew the ram's horn* (27, WLC). The same verb, *tāqaʿ*, does both. It is the
verb of the trumpets at Jericho (Joshua 6:4–20), of Jael's tent peg (*wattitqaʿ*, Judges 4:21), and of
Gideon's horn (6:34) and his three hundred (7:18–22; all WLC). In Ehud's story the stab and the signal
are one word.

**"Covering his feet."** *ʾAk mēsîk hûʾ ʾet-raglāyw* (24, WLC). The same idiom describes Saul going
into the cave at En Gedi, *ləhāsēk ʾet-raglāyw* (1 Samuel 24:4 in the Hebrew numbering, 24:3 in
English; WLC). There the WEB translates the idiom: *Saul went in to relieve himself* (1 Samuel 24:3,
WEB, checked against bible-api.com). In Judges 3:24 the WEB keeps it literal. The servants assume the
king is using the latrine, and their embarrassment, *until they were ashamed* (*ʿad-bôš*, 25),
gives Ehud the time he needs. The text says only that they *assumed* it; it does not say where Eglon
died except *the upper room* and *the floor*.

**The key.** *Hammaptēaḥ* (25, WLC). They have a key; Ehud did not need one to lock the doors (23,
*nāʿāl*). How he locked them from outside, or whether he went out some other way, depends on the two
rare words of 22–23. The text does not explain it.

**Seirah.** *Haśśəʿîrātâ* (26, WLC), *toward Seirah*. The name occurs nowhere else in the WLC files
read, and no location is given beyond its connection with the hill country of Ephraim in the next
verse. See [[Seirah]].

**"Follow me."** *Ridpû ʾaḥăray* (28, WLC), literally *pursue after me*. *Kî-nātan YHWH ʾet-ʾōyəbêkem
ʾet-môʾāb bəyedkem*, *for Yahweh has given your enemies, Moab, into your hand*. This is the only place
in the pericope where Yahweh acts in the past tense in someone's speech. Othniel's story said *Yahweh
delivered Cushan … into his hand* (3:10) in the narrator's voice. Here the narrator lets Ehud say it.

**The fords.** *Wayyilkədû ʾet-maʿbərôt hayyardēn ləmôʾāb* (28, WLC). Joshua's spies were pursued to
*the fords of the Jordan* (Joshua 2:7, the same noun). Here Israel holds them. Gideon's men will hold
the waters against Midian (7:24), and Gilead will hold the fords against Ephraim and kill those who
cannot say *Shibboleth* (12:5–6, stored WEB). The first time the fords are held, the enemy is Moab;
the last time, it is a brother tribe. See [[The Fords of the Jordan - Joshua 2, Judges 3, 7, and 12]].

**"Every strong man."** *Kol-šāmēn wəkol-ʾîš ḥāyil* (29, WLC). *Šāmēn* is literally *fat*, the word
for rich land in Numbers 13:20 (*whether it is fat or lean*, stored WEB) and for Asher's bread in Genesis 49:20 (WLC). The WEB's *strong* is a
fair sense, but the Hebrew repeats the joke: the fat king dies, and then the fat men of Moab die. *Lōʾ
nimlaṭ ʾîš*, *no man escaped* (29), uses the verb of Ehud's own escape, *nimlaṭ* (26). Ehud escaped;
no Moabite did.

**"Moab was subdued."** *Wattikkānaʿ môʾāb* (30, WLC). In the WLC files read, *kānaʿ* is the verb of
Judges' victory summaries: after Ehud (3:30), after Deborah (4:23), after Gideon (8:28), and after
Jephthah (11:33); and again of the Philistines in 1 Samuel 7:13. Deuteronomy 9:3 had promised it:
Yahweh *will bring them down* (*yaknîʿēm*, WLC). It is never said of Othniel's enemy.

**Eighty years.** *Wattišqōṭ hāʾāreṣ šəmônîm šānâ* (30, WLC). Double Othniel's forty and the longest
rest in the book. If *forty* is a round figure for a generation, eighty is two. That reading is reported,
not checked.

**What is not said.** The narrator does not praise Ehud, blame him, or tell the reader what Yahweh
thought of the plan. The lie, if it is one, goes without comment. *A message from God* may be true in
the sense that Yahweh had *raised up* the messenger (15). The book leaves that judgment open.

**Gera gets no page.** *Gera* is one of Benjamin's sons in Genesis 46:21 (stored WEB; *gērāʾ*, the
same lemma, WLC), so *the son of Gera* may name a clan rather than a father. He does nothing in the
pericope. As with Kenaz on [[Othniel the First Judge]], the decision is recorded in the log.
