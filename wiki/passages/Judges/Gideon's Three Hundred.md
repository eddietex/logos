# Gideon's Three Hundred

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 7:1–8

## Summary

The fleece ended with God doing what Gideon asked, and saying nothing (6:40). The next morning the
army moves:

> **7:1** Then Jerubbaal, who is Gideon, and all the people who were with him, rose up early, and encamped beside the spring of Harod. Midian’s camp was on the north side of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley.

**The two camps (7:1).** The narrator gives both of his names, *Jerubbaal, who is Gideon*, the name
from 6:32 first. Gideon *rose up early* again, as he did to wring out the fleece (6:38). His army camps
*beside the spring of Harod*. Midian camps to the north, *by the hill of Moreh, in the valley*, the valley
of Jezreel where the eastern army gathered (6:33). This hill of Moreh is not the *oak of Moreh* at
Shechem (Genesis 12:6) or the *oaks of Moreh* near Gilgal (Deuteronomy 11:30). The Hebrew name is the same,
*môreh*, and the text does not connect the places. The rest of the pericope happens at the spring. The
last verse looks down at the enemy: *the camp of Midian was beneath him in the valley* (7:8).

**Too many (7:2).** Then Yahweh speaks. It is the first time he has spoken since the altar scene. He
did not speak during the fleece (6:36–40).

> **7:2** Yahweh said to Gideon, “The people who are with you are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel brag against me, saying, ‘My own hand has saved me.’

Gideon asked twice that God would *save Israel by my hand* (6:36–37). Yahweh's answer is about whose
hand gets the credit. The army is not too small to win. It is too large to win in a way that shows
who won. Deuteronomy 20:1 tells Israel not to be afraid when it sees *a people more than you*. Here the
*people more* is Israel, and Yahweh reduces it. The boast he heads off is the one Moses warned against
in the land: *lest you say in your heart, "My power and the might of my hand has gotten me this wealth"*
(Deuteronomy 8:17). Both sentences put *my hand* in Israel's mouth and say it acted *for me*. See
[[My Hand Has Saved Me - Deuteronomy 8.17 and Judges 7.2]].

**The first cut: the fearful (7:3).**

> **7:3** Now therefore proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, ‘Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return and depart from Mount Gilead.’” So twenty-two thousand of the people returned, and ten thousand remained.

This is the law of war. Deuteronomy 20:8 has the officers ask *What man is there who is fearful and
faint-hearted? Let him go and return to his house*. Judges has the same adjective, *fearful*, and the
same verb, *return* (see the Notes and
[[Let Him Return to His House - Deuteronomy 20, 24.5, 28.30, Judges 7, and Luke 14]]). The law gives a
different reason, *lest his brother's heart melt as his heart*. In Deuteronomy the fearful man is sent
home to protect the army's courage. Here the army is cut down so that it cannot take the credit. More
than two-thirds go. The text never totals the army. Its size follows only from the two numbers in this
verse, twenty-two thousand gone and ten thousand left.

The word *fearful* matters for Gideon himself. He obeyed at night *because he feared* (6:27). Three
verses after this proclamation, Yahweh says to him *if you are afraid to go down* (7:10, stored WEB; not
yet ingested), with the same Hebrew word as 7:3. The man who sends the fearful home is one of them.
Yahweh keeps him and gives him another sign.

*Mount Gilead* is a difficulty. Gilead is east of the Jordan (see [[Gilead]]). Gideon is camped west of it,
by the spring of Harod in the valley of Jezreel. The text does not explain the name. Explanations are
given in the Notes, and the wiki does not choose between them.

**The second cut: the water (7:4–7).** Ten thousand are still *too many* (7:4). Yahweh says *I will
test them for you there*. The verb is a smelter's word for refining metal, not the *nāsâ* of the fleece
(see the Notes). The test is how the men drink:

> **7:5** So he brought down the people to the water; and Yahweh said to Gideon, “Everyone who laps of the water with his tongue, like a dog laps, you shall set him by himself; likewise everyone who bows down on his knees to drink.”

> **7:6** The number of those who lapped, putting their hand to their mouth, was three hundred men; but all the rest of the people bowed down on their knees to drink water.

The narrator does not say what lapping meant, or why the three hundred are the ones kept. Verse 5
compares the lappers to *a dog*. Verse 6 says they lapped *putting their hand to their mouth*. That
seems to mean they scooped the water and lapped it from the hand, but the two descriptions are hard to fit
together, and the Notes give the textual question. One common reading says the three hundred were the
watchful soldiers, who kept their eyes up while drinking. That is a reading. The text gives no virtue to
either group. What it does make plain is the arithmetic. Of ten thousand, the smaller group is kept,
and it is smaller by far. Yahweh says only:

> **7:7** Yahweh said to Gideon, “By the three hundred men who lapped will I save you, and deliver the Midianites into your hand. Let all the other people go, each to his own place.”

Gideon's request was *save Israel by my hand* (6:36). The answer keeps both parts but changes the
emphasis. *I* will save, and *you* are the plural, *ʾetkem*, all Israel. Midian is still given *into
your hand*, Gideon's, singular. Gideon will strike Midian. But no one will be able to say his hand saved
Israel.

**Sent home, and three hundred kept (7:8).**

> **7:8** So the people took food in their hand, and their trumpets; and he sent all the men of Israel every man to his tent, but retained the three hundred men; and the camp of Midian was beneath him in the valley.

The provisions and the *trumpets*, *šôpārôt*, rams' horns, are the equipment of the attack to come.
Gideon will put a horn in each of the three hundred men's hands (7:16, 22, stored WEB; not yet
ingested), and the Hebrew of verse 8 may mean that the three hundred took the departing men's
supplies and horns (see the Notes). Ten thousand go home to their tents. Three hundred are *retained*.
The enemy below is the army of 6:33, which 7:12 will describe *like locusts for multitude* (stored WEB;
not yet ingested).

**How the pericope sits in Gideon's story.** The army Gideon mustered in 6:34–35 was the answer to
Midian's numbers. Yahweh dismantles it before the battle. In Joshua the promise was *One man of you
shall chase a thousand; for it is Yahweh your God who fights for you* (Joshua 23:10). Gideon's three
hundred will face an army of uncounted size. See
[[Five Shall Chase a Hundred - Leviticus 26.8, Deuteronomy 32.30, and Joshua 23.10]]. The pericope is
the opposite of the fleece. There Gideon tested God and God said nothing. Here God tests the army, does
all the talking, and Gideon says nothing at all. He does what he is told in every verse.

## People

- [[Gideon]] — *Jerubbaal, who is Gideon* (1); camps at Harod, makes the proclamation, brings the men
  to the water, sends all but three hundred home (1–8). He does not speak.
- [[God]] — *Yahweh*, who speaks four times (2–3, 4, 5, 7), reduces the army, and promises *By the three
  hundred … will I save you* (7).
- [[Midian]] — encamped north, by the hill of Moreh (1), *beneath him in the valley* (8). The wiki
  files Midian under places.

## Places

- [[The Spring of Harod]] — Gideon's camp (1), and the water of the test (4–6).
- [[The Hill of Moreh]] — where Midian camps, north of Harod (1).
- [[The Valley of Jezreel]] — *in the valley* (1, 8), the valley of 6:33.
- [[Gilead]] — *depart from Mount Gilead* (3), a name hard to place west of the Jordan.
- [[Midian]] — the camp below (1, 8).

## Themes

- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — *lest Israel brag against me, saying, "My own hand has saved me"* (2).
- [[Testing]] — *I will test them for you there* (4), with the verb of refining metal, not *nāsâ*.
- [[The Fear of God]] — *whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return* (3); fear of the battle, which
  Gideon shares (6:27; 7:10).
- [[War]] — the law of war's dismissal of the fearful applied (3; Deuteronomy 20:8), and the army reduced
  to three hundred (4–8).

## Notes

**"Rose up early … beside the spring of Harod."** *Wayyaškēm yərubbaʿal hûʾ gidʿôn … wayyaḥănû
ʿal-ʿên ḥărōd* (1, WLC). *ʿÊn* is a spring. *Ḥărōd* (5878) occurs in the WLC of Genesis–Deuteronomy,
Joshua, and Judges only here. It has the same consonants, *ḥ-r-d*, as *ḥārēd*, *trembling* (2730), in
verse 3. A wordplay between the *spring of trembling* and the men *trembling* is often noted, and the
consonants support it. The narrator does not point it out.

**"By the hill of Moreh."** *Miggiḇʿat hammôreh bāʿēmeq* (1, WLC), *from the hill of the Moreh*. *Môreh*
(4176) occurs in the books checked only here, in *ʾēlôn môreh* at Shechem (Genesis 12:6), and in *ʾēlônê
mōreh* near Gilgal (Deuteronomy 11:30) (WLC). The hill is in the valley. The oaks are by Shechem. The
wiki makes separate pages for them and does not connect them.

**"Too many for me to give."** *Rab hāʿām ʾăšer ʾittāk mittittî ʾet-midyān bəyādām* (2, WLC), word for word
*many is the people with you, beyond my giving Midian into their hand*. *Rab* (7227) is the adjective of
Deuteronomy 20:1, *ʿam rab mimməkā*, *a people more than you* (WLC). Verse 4 repeats *ʿôd hāʿām rāb*, *the
people is still many*.

**"Lest Israel brag against me."** *Pen-yitpāʾēr ʿālay yiśrāʾēl lēʾmōr yādî hôšîʿâ lî* (2, WLC). *Pāʾar*
(6286) in the hitpael, *to glorify oneself* or *boast*. In the WLC of Genesis–Deuteronomy, Joshua, and
Judges the hitpael occurs only here and at Exodus 8:5 (WEB 8:9), where Moses says to Pharaoh
*hitpāʾēr ʿālay*, which WEB renders *I give you the honor*. The same verb stem and the same *ʿālay*, *over me*, appear
in both. In Exodus it is a courtesy and in Judges it is a warning, and the wiki does not draw a
connection between the two. The other WLC occurrence of the lemma in those books, Deuteronomy 24:20, is
a different sense, *go over the boughs* of an olive tree.

**"My own hand has saved me."** *Yādî hôšîʿâ lî* (WLC), *my hand has saved for me*. *Hôšîʿâ* is the
hiphil of *yāšaʿ* (3467), the verb of Gideon's commission (6:14) and of his fleece request (6:36–37). In
Deuteronomy 8:17 the boast is *kōḥî wəʿōṣem yādî ʿāśâ lî ʾet-haḥayil hazzeh*, *my strength and the might
of my hand has made for me this wealth* (WLC). Both have *yādî*, *my hand*, and *lî*, *for me*. The
verbs differ. Judges does not cite Deuteronomy. See
[[My Hand Has Saved Me - Deuteronomy 8.17 and Judges 7.2]].

**"Fearful and trembling."** *Mî-yārēʾ wəḥārēd yāšōb* (3, WLC). *Yārēʾ* (3373) is the adjective of
Deuteronomy 20:8, *mî-hāʾîš hayyārēʾ wərak hallēbāb yēlēk wəyāšōb ləbêtô* (WLC). Judges lacks the
article and has *ḥārēd*, *trembling*, where Deuteronomy has *rak hallēbāb*, *soft of heart*. *Yāšōb*,
*let him return*, is the same verb, *šûb*. In the WLC of Judges, *yārēʾ* (3373) occurs only here and at
7:10, *ʾim-yārēʾ ʾattâ*, *if you are afraid* (the morphology tags it as an adjective in 7:3 and as a
participle in 7:10; the form is the same). *Ḥārēd* (2730) occurs in Genesis–Deuteronomy, Joshua, and
Judges only here (WLC).

**"And depart from Mount Gilead."** *Wəyiṣpōr mēhar haggilʿād* (3, WLC). *Ṣāpar* (6852) occurs in these
books only here, and its sense is uncertain. WEB's *depart* is one rendering. Others take it as *turn
back* or *go off early*. *Har haggilʿād* is the ordinary name of the highland east of the Jordan. Three
explanations are commonly given for it here, and none is checked: a scribal error for *Gilboa*, the
mountain south of the valley; a local name for a height near Harod otherwise unknown; or a phrase that
means the men went home east across the Jordan. The wiki records the problem and leaves it open.

**"I will test them for you there."** *Wəʾeṣrəpennû ləkā šām* (4, WLC). *Ṣārap* (6884) is the verb for
smelting or refining metal. In the WLC of Judges it occurs only here and at 17:4, where it is the
participle for the *silversmith* who makes Micah's idol. It does not occur in Genesis–Deuteronomy or Joshua.
The fleece used *nāsâ*, *test* (6:39). Here Yahweh tests the army as a smelter tests silver, separating
a small part from the rest. The image is in the verb. The text does not say which part is the silver.

**"Laps … like a dog laps."** *Kōl ʾăšer-yālōq bilšônô min-hammayim kaʾăšer yālōq hakkeleb taṣṣîg ʾôtô
ləbād* (5, WLC). *Lāqaq* (3952) occurs in these books only in Judges 7:5–7. Verse 6 has *hamlaqqîm
bəyādām ʾel-pîhem*, *the lappers with their hand to their mouth* (WLC). In the received Hebrew that
phrase stands with the lappers. That a version or a commentator moves it to the kneelers, which would
make the three hundred those who put their faces to the water, is reported, not checked. No Greek or
Latin text was consulted for this pericope.

**"I will save you."** *Bišlōš mēʾôt hāʾîš hamlaqqîm ʾôšîaʿ ʾetkem wənātattî ʾet-midyān bəyādekā* (7, WLC).
*ʾEtkem* is plural, *you all*. *Bəyādekā* is singular, *into your hand*, Gideon's.

**"Took food … and their trumpets."** *Wayyiqḥû ʾet-ṣēdâ hāʿām bəyādām wəʾēt šôpərōtêhem* (8, WLC).
*Ṣēdâ* (6720) is provisions for a journey. WEB takes *hāʿām*, *the people*, as the subject: the people
took food and trumpets. The word order also allows *they took the people's provisions … and their horns*,
meaning the three hundred took supplies and horns from the men leaving. That would explain where
three hundred *šôpārôt* come from in 7:16. Both readings are reported. The grammar was not resolved
here. *Heḥĕzîq*, *retained*, is the hiphil of *ḥāzaq* (2388), *held on to*.

**The numbers.** Twenty-two thousand leave and ten thousand stay (3). Three hundred are kept (6–7).
The text does not add these up, and gives no count for Midian until 8:10 (stored WEB; not yet
ingested).

**Judgment calls.** The boundary was kept as planned. Verse 1 begins a new scene at a new place, and
verse 9, *That same night*, begins the night reconnaissance. New pages were made for the spring of
Harod and the hill of Moreh, the first named places in Gideon's battle. No page was made for the
water's edge or for the dog image. *Mount Gilead* is filed under [[Gilead]] with the difficulty
stated. A connection to Deuteronomy 8:17 was made because the shared *yādî … lî* was checked in the
Hebrew. The Exodus 8:9 use of *hitpāʾēr ʿālay* is recorded here and not made a connection. The
lapping test is not interpreted.
