# Jephthah's Message to the King of Ammon

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 11:12–28

## Summary

Jephthah has just been made *head and chief* at Mizpah (11:11). Before he fights, he sends messengers.
*What have you to do with me, that you have come to me to fight against my land?* (12). The king of
Ammon answers with a claim: *Israel took away my land, when he came up out of Egypt, from the Arnon even
to the Jabbok, and to the Jordan. Now therefore restore that territory again peaceably* (13).

Jephthah sends again (14). The second message tells the
journey from Egypt as a legal case. Israel did not take the land of Moab or of Ammon (15). It asked Edom
and Moab for passage, was refused, and waited at Kadesh (16–17). It went around both and stayed outside
Moab's border, *for the Arnon was the border of Moab* (18). It asked Sihon king of the Amorites for
passage. Sihon fought, *Yahweh, the God of Israel, delivered Sihon and all his people into the hand of
Israel*, and Israel took the Amorites' land *from the Arnon even to the Jabbok* (19–22). The land was the
Amorites', not Ammon's, and Yahweh gave it.

Then come three arguments. A god's gift: *Won't you possess that which Chemosh your god gives you to
possess? So whoever Yahweh our God has dispossessed from before us, them will we possess* (24). A
precedent: Balak king of Moab never fought for this land (25). Time: Israel has lived in Heshbon and
Aroer *three hundred years; why didn't you recover them within that time?* (26). And a verdict left to
Yahweh: *I therefore have not sinned against you, but you do me wrong to war against me. May Yahweh the
Judge be judge today between the children of Israel and the children of Ammon* (27).

*However the king of the children of Ammon didn't listen to the words of Jephthah which he sent him*
(28). The next verse is the Spirit, and the war (11:29).

**The first message and the claim (11:12–13).**

> **11:12** Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the children of Ammon, saying, "What have you to do with me, that you have come to me to fight against my land?"

*What have you to do with me*, *mah-lî wālāk* (WLC), literally *what to me and to you*. The question
asks what quarrel lies between the two sides. *My land*, *bəʾarṣî*: Jephthah speaks for Gilead in the
first person, as its new head.

> **11:13** The king of the children of Ammon answered to the messengers of Jephthah, "Because Israel took away my land, when he came up out of Egypt, from the Arnon even to the Jabbok, and to the Jordan. Now therefore restore that territory again peaceably."

*From the Arnon even to the Jabbok*, *mēʾarnôn wəʿad-hayyabbōq* (WLC). These are the bounds Numbers gives
for Sihon's land: Israel *possessed his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok, even to the children of Ammon;
for the border of the children of Ammon was strong* (Numbers 21:24). Numbers puts Ammon's border beyond
the conquest. The king claims the conquest itself as his.

*Restore … peaceably*, *hāšîbâ ʾethen bəšālôm* (WLC). The object *ʾethen* is a feminine plural,
*them*; WEB renders it *that territory*. The king offers peace on the condition that Israel gives the
land back. The verb is *šûb*, the one the elders used when they *turned again* to Jephthah (11:8–9).
Jephthah's reply never uses it (11:15–27); he answers about whose land it was, not about returning it.

**The second message: the journey (11:14–18).**

> **11:14** Jephthah sent messengers again to the king of the children of Ammon;

> **11:15** and he said to him, "Thus says Jephthah: Israel didn't take away the land of Moab, nor the land of the children of Ammon,

*Thus says Jephthah*, *kōh ʾāmar yiptāḥ* (WLC). It is the messenger formula. Moses' embassy to Edom
opened the same way, *Thus says your brother Israel* (Numbers 20:14, *kōh ʾāmar ʾāḥîkā yiśrāʾēl*,
WLC). Jephthah is now doing what Israel did on the road: sending messengers to a king.

*Didn't take away*, *lōʾ-lāqaḥ* (WLC). He answers the king's *took away*, *lāqaḥ* (13), with the same
verb negated. His denial matches Yahweh's commands in Deuteronomy: of Moab, *I will not give you any of
his land for a possession* (Deuteronomy 2:9), and of Ammon, *I will not give you any of the land of the
children of Ammon for a possession* (2:19). Jephthah does not quote those commands. He argues from what
Israel did.

> **11:16** but when they came up from Egypt, and Israel went through the wilderness to the Red Sea, and came to Kadesh;

*The Red Sea*, *yam sûp* (WLC). Its only occurrence in Judges. The order of the verse is wilderness, Red
Sea, Kadesh. In Numbers, Israel is at Kadesh first (20:1, 14) and travels *by the way to the Red Sea, to
go around the land of Edom* only after (21:4). Jephthah may mean the crossing of the sea after Egypt, or
the later road; the verse does not say which. See [[The Red Sea]] and [[Kadesh]].

> **11:17** then Israel sent messengers to the king of Edom, saying, 'Please let me pass through your land;' but the king of Edom didn't listen. In the same way, he sent to the king of Moab; but he would not: and Israel stayed in Kadesh.

The embassy to Edom follows Numbers 20:14–21: *Please let us pass through your land* (20:17), and *Edom
refused to give Israel passage through his border* (20:21). *Didn't listen*, *lōʾ šāmaʿ* (WLC); the same
words close this passage, of Ammon's king (28).

**The embassy to Moab is not in Numbers.** Jephthah says Israel sent to the king of Moab and *he would
not*, *lōʾ ʾābâ* (WLC). Numbers records no such message. Deuteronomy 2:29 points the other way: Moses
asks Sihon for passage *as the children of Esau who dwell in Seir, and the Moabites who dwell in Ar, did
to me*. There Moab, and Edom too, appear to have let Israel buy food and pass. Jephthah's account and
Deuteronomy 2:29 do not agree on Moab, and Numbers 20 and Deuteronomy 2:29 already differ on Edom. The
wiki records the three accounts and does not harmonize them. One possibility is that Jephthah speaks of
a refusal to pass *through* the land, and Deuteronomy of trade along its edge; the texts do not say so.
See [[Jephthah's History of the Journey - Numbers 20-22, Deuteronomy 2, and Judges 11]].

*Israel stayed in Kadesh*, *wayyēšeb yiśrāʾēl bəqādēš* (WLC). Deuteronomy says the same: *So you stayed
in Kadesh many days* (Deuteronomy 1:46).

> **11:18** Then they went through the wilderness, and went around the land of Edom, and the land of Moab, and came by the east side of the land of Moab, and they encamped on the other side of the Arnon; but they didn't come within the border of Moab, for the Arnon was the border of Moab.

*For the Arnon was the border of Moab*, *kî ʾarnôn gəbûl môʾāb* (WLC). The four Hebrew words are the same
as Numbers 21:13, *for the Arnon is the border of Moab, between Moab and the Amorites*. Jephthah's
history uses the itinerary's own wording. Numbers adds *between Moab and the Amorites*, which is the
point of Jephthah's case: north of the Arnon was Amorite land. See [[The Arnon]].

**Sihon (11:19–22).**

> **11:19** Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, the king of Heshbon; and Israel said to him, 'Please let us pass through your land to my place.'

> **11:20** But Sihon didn't trust Israel to pass through his border; but Sihon gathered all his people together, and encamped in Jahaz, and fought against Israel.

Close to Numbers 21:23 in the Hebrew. Both have *Sihon gathered all his people*, *wayyeʾĕsōp sîḥōn
ʾet-kol-ʿammô*, then Jahaz, then *fought*, *wayyillāḥem*. The difference is the first verb. Numbers has
*Sihon would not allow*, *wəlōʾ-nātan* (*give*). Judges has *didn't trust*, *wəlōʾ-heʾĕmîn* (*ʾāman*,
lemma 539, WLC). Its only occurrence in Judges. In Jephthah's telling, Sihon refused because he did not
believe Israel's word. Deuteronomy gives a third reason: *Yahweh your God hardened his spirit*
(Deuteronomy 2:30). See [[Sihon]] and [[Jahaz]].

> **11:21** Yahweh, the God of Israel, delivered Sihon and all his people into the hand of Israel, and they struck them. So Israel possessed all the land of the Amorites, the inhabitants of that country.

> **11:22** They possessed all the border of the Amorites, from the Arnon even to the Jabbok, and from the wilderness even to the Jordan.

*Yahweh … delivered Sihon*. Numbers 21:24 says only *Israel struck him with the edge of the sword*.
Deuteronomy credits Yahweh: *Yahweh our God delivered him up before us* (Deuteronomy 2:33). Jephthah's
account follows Deuteronomy here.

*From the Arnon even to the Jabbok, and from the wilderness even to the Jordan.* He repeats the king's
bounds (13) and adds a second pair, east to west. The king said *my land*. Jephthah says *the border of
the Amorites*. Both name the same ground. See [[The Jabbok]] and [[The Amorites]].

**The three arguments (11:23–26).**

> **11:23** So now Yahweh, the God of Israel, has dispossessed the Amorites from before his people Israel, and should you possess them?

> **11:24** Won't you possess that which Chemosh your god gives you to possess? So whoever Yahweh our God has dispossessed from before us, them will we possess.

*Possessed … dispossessed … possess*. All are one verb, *yāraš* (lemma 3423, WLC), eight times in four
verses (21, 22, 23 twice, 24 four times). The simple stem means *possess*, the causative *dispossess*:
*hôrîš*, *made heirs* of the land by removing those in it. The argument is built from the verb. Yahweh
dispossessed; Israel possesses; Ammon may possess what its own god dispossesses for it.

*Chemosh your god*, *kəmôš ʾĕlōhêkā* (WLC). Elsewhere in the files read, Chemosh occurs only in Numbers
21:29, where Moab is *people of Chemosh*. Chemosh is Moab's god, and Jephthah names him to the king of
Ammon. The text does not explain this. Several readings are possible, and none is stated:

- The land in dispute was once Moab's. Numbers 21:26 says Sihon had *fought against the former king of
  Moab, and taken all his land out of his hand, even to the Arnon*. So the claim the king makes is really
  Moab's old claim, and Jephthah answers it with Moab's god.
- Ammon and Moab act together elsewhere. Eglon *gathered the children of Ammon and Amalek to himself*
  (3:13), and Deuteronomy 23:3–4 bars *an Ammonite or a Moabite* together for the hire of Balaam. The
  king may speak for both peoples.
- Jephthah's knowledge of the gods of the nations may be loose, or the narrator's is. The god of Ammon is
  named elsewhere in Scripture as Milcom or Molech (recalled from 1 Kings 11, which is not stored; not
  verified here).

*Won't you possess … ?* is an argument from the other side's own belief. It does not say Chemosh gives
land. Judges has just listed *the gods of Moab* and *the gods of the children of Ammon* among the gods
Israel served and was punished for (10:6), so the book does not grant Chemosh power. Jephthah speaks to
the king in terms the king accepts. See [[Chemosh]] and [[Idolatry]].

> **11:25** Now are you anything better than Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab? Did he ever strive against Israel, or did he ever fight against them?

*Are you anything better*, *hăṭôb ṭôb ʾattâ* (WLC), with the word doubled for force. *Did he ever
strive … did he ever fight*, *hărôb rāb … ʾim-nilḥōm nilḥam* (WLC), both verbs doubled the same way.
*Strive* is *rîb* (lemma 7378), the verb of the quarrel and the lawsuit (see 6:31–32; 8:1). The answer
Jephthah expects is no. Balak hired Balaam to curse Israel, *Perhaps I shall prevail, that we may strike
them* (Numbers 22:6), and Numbers narrates no battle.

**Joshua 24:9 says the opposite.** *Then Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab, arose and fought against
Israel*. Its verb is *wayyillāḥem* (WLC), the same verb *lāḥam* that Jephthah says Balak never used. The
two statements stand side by side in the canon. Joshua 24:9 goes on, *He sent and called Balaam … to
curse you*, so it may count the hired curse as the fight. Jephthah may mean that Balak never went to war
for the land. The wiki records both statements and does not harmonize them. See [[Balak]].

> **11:26** While Israel lived in Heshbon and its towns, and in Aroer and its towns, and in all the cities that are along by the side of the Arnon, three hundred years; why didn't you recover them within that time?

*Its towns*, *bənôtêhā* (WLC), literally *its daughters*, the villages of a city. *Aroer*, beside the
Arnon, is the Aroer of Deuteronomy 2:36, *on the edge of the valley of the Arnon*, not the Aroer near
Rabbah (Joshua 13:25). See [[Heshbon]] and [[Aroer]].

*Three hundred years*, *šəlōš mēʾôt šānâ* (WLC). The book's other numbers measure single oppressions,
rests, and judgeships; this one reaches back to Moses' war with Sihon. The book's own numbers from 3:8 to 10:8, added together, already come to 319 years
before Jephthah: eight, forty, eighteen, eighty, twenty, forty, seven, forty, three, twenty-three,
twenty-two, eighteen. That sum leaves out the wilderness, Joshua's years, and Shamgar, who has no number.
The addition is the wiki's; the text gives no total. Jephthah's three hundred may be a round number
said in a dispute, or some of the book's periods may overlap. The text does not say. The number is also
one of the anchors weighed in [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]].

*Recover*, *hiṣṣaltem* (*nāṣal*, lemma 5337, WLC), *deliver, snatch back*. If the land was Ammon's,
Ammon left it alone for three hundred years.

**The verdict (11:27–28).**

> **11:27** I therefore have not sinned against you, but you do me wrong to war against me. May Yahweh the Judge be judge today between the children of Israel and the children of Ammon."

*I … have not sinned against you*, *lōʾ-ḥāṭāʾtî lāk* (WLC). Israel said to Yahweh, *We have sinned
against you*, *ḥāṭāʾnû lāk* (10:10, WLC), and again at 10:15. Those are the only uses of *ḥāṭāʾ* in
Judges before this one. Israel has sinned against Yahweh; Israel has not sinned against Ammon. The same
words make both points.

*May Yahweh the Judge be judge*, *yišpōṭ YHWH haššōpēṭ* (WLC). In Judges the singular *the judge*,
*haššōpēṭ*, occurs otherwise only in 2:18–19, of the human judges Yahweh raised up. Here it is a title
of Yahweh. The book is named for judges, and the one figure it calls *the Judge* in a speech is Yahweh.
Jephthah himself is later said to have *judged Israel six years* (12:7), with the verb, not the title.

*Judge … between*, *yišpōṭ … bên … ûbên* (WLC). Sarai used the same words, *Yahweh judge between me and
you* (Genesis 16:5), and Laban, *The God of Abraham, and the God of Nahor … judge between us* (Genesis
31:53). Each time a party in a dispute hands the case to God. Abraham first called God *the Judge of all
the earth* (Genesis 18:25, *hăšōpēṭ kol-hāʾāreṣ*). Jephthah calls him the Judge of a border. See
[[Shall Not the Judge of All the Earth Do Right]] and [[Judgment]].

Jephthah has done this once already. At Mizpah the elders said *Yahweh will be witness between us*,
*šōmēaʿ*, *the one who hears* (11:10). Here he makes Yahweh the judge between Israel and Ammon.

> **11:28** However the king of the children of Ammon didn't listen to the words of Jephthah which he sent him.

*Didn't listen*, *lōʾ šāmaʿ* (WLC), as the king of Edom *didn't listen* (17). Yahweh was named the
hearer, *šōmēaʿ*, in 11:10. The king does not hear, and the case goes to the Judge.

**What the passage does.** It is the only exchange of embassies with a foreign king in Judges, and it
delays the battle by a whole speech. The man the elders chose for his strength (11:1) opens with words. He makes four
arguments: history (Israel took the land from Sihon, not from Ammon), theology (Yahweh gave it), precedent
(Balak did not fight for it), and time (three hundred years unchallenged). Then he gives the verdict to
Yahweh. The arguments do not persuade, and the passage does not say they were meant to. They put on
record that Israel's war is defensive, and they ask Yahweh to decide it. The next verse is Yahweh's
answer: *Then Yahweh's Spirit came on Jephthah* (11:29).

The history he tells also stands beside the Torah's. It follows Numbers' wording closely (21:13, 23–24),
takes Deuteronomy's credit to Yahweh (2:33), adds an embassy to Moab that neither records, and denies a
fight that Joshua 24:9 affirms. Read this way, the speech is Israel's history as a Gileadite remembered
it, three centuries on. That is the wiki's reading; the narrator neither corrects nor endorses the
details.

## Notes

**Boundaries.** The plan's 11:12–28 is kept. 11:12 opens a new action, the embassy, and 11:28 closes it
with the king's refusal to listen. 11:29 begins with the Spirit and moves to war.

**Contradictions recorded.** (1) Jephthah says the king of Moab refused Israel (11:17); Numbers has no
embassy to Moab, and Deuteronomy 2:29 says the Moabites in Ar dealt with Israel as Moses asked Sihon to.
(2) Jephthah says Balak never fought Israel (11:25); Joshua 24:9 says he *arose and fought against
Israel*, with the same verb. (3) Jephthah names Chemosh, Moab's god, as the god of Ammon's king (11:24).
Each is written out above with both sides; none is resolved.
