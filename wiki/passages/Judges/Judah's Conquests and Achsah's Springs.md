# Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 1:8–21

## Summary

Judah fights against Jerusalem, takes it, strikes it with the sword, and sets the city on fire (8).
Then Judah goes down against the Canaanites of the hill country, the South, and the lowland (9). At
Hebron, once called Kiriath Arba, they strike Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai (10). From there Judah goes
against Debir, once Kiriath Sepher (11).

Caleb offers his daughter Achsah as wife to the man who takes Kiriath Sepher (12). Othniel the son of
Kenaz, *Caleb's younger brother*, takes it and receives her (13). When she comes, she gets him to ask
her father for a field; she gets off her donkey, and Caleb asks what she wants (14). She asks for a
blessing: since he has set her in the land of the South, he should give her springs of water too.
Caleb gives her the upper springs and the lower springs (15).

The children of the Kenite, Moses' brother-in-law, go up from the city of palm trees with Judah into
the wilderness of Judah, south of Arad, and live with the people (16). Judah goes with Simeon his
brother; they strike the Canaanites of Zephath, devote it to destruction, and name the city Hormah
(17). Judah takes Gaza, Ashkelon, and Ekron, each with its border (18).

*Yahweh was with Judah*, and Judah drives out the hill country, but not the inhabitants of the valley,
*because they had chariots of iron* (19). Hebron goes to Caleb *as Moses had said*, and he drives out
the three sons of Anak (20). Last, Benjamin does not drive out the Jebusites of Jerusalem, and *the
Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem to this day* (21).

## Key verses

> **1:8** The children of Judah fought against Jerusalem, took it, struck it with the edge of the sword, and set the city on fire.

> **1:13** Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, took it, so he gave him Achsah his daughter as his wife.

> **1:15** She said to him, “Give me a blessing; because you have set me in the land of the South, give me also springs of water.” Then Caleb gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.

> **1:19** Yahweh was with Judah; and drove out the inhabitants of the hill country; for he could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron.

> **1:21** The children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites who inhabited Jerusalem, but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem to this day.

## Notes

**Jerusalem taken, and Jerusalem not taken.** Verse 8 is unqualified: Judah *took it, struck it with
the edge of the sword, and set the city on fire*. Verse 21, fourteen verses later, has the Jebusites
still living in Jerusalem *to this day*, now beside Benjamin. And Joshua 15:63 had already said, *the
children of Judah couldn't drive them out; but the Jebusites live with the children of Judah at
Jerusalem to this day* (stored WEB). Verse 21 is that sentence with *Benjamin* in place of *Judah*,
which matches Joshua 18:28, where Jerusalem is one of Benjamin's cities. The three statements are not
reconciled in the text. Taking and burning a city is not the same as settling it, as Joshua 12's list of
kings *struck* is not a list of cities held. That is a reading that eases the strain, not one the
text gives. The wiki keeps all three. On the source-language wording, see the note on verse 19 below.

**The household scene, copied with small changes.** Verses 10–15 run parallel to Joshua 15:13–19,
almost word for word in the Hebrew as well as the WEB. Three differences are worth recording, each
checked in the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb):

- **Who struck the three.** Joshua 15:14 says *Caleb drove out* Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai. Judges
  1:10 says *Judah* went and *they struck* them. Then Judges 1:20 says Caleb *drove out from there the
  three sons of Anak*, in Joshua's terms. So the chapter itself attributes the act both to the tribe
  and to Caleb, and Joshua 10:36–37 and 11:21 credit Joshua. Judah is Caleb's tribe, and a tribe's
  deed can be told as its leader's, but the text does not say that. See
  [[Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1]].
- ***Younger*.** Joshua 15:17 has *ʿotniʾēl ben-qənaz ʾăḥî kālēb*, *Othniel son of Kenaz, brother of
  Caleb*. Judges 1:13 adds *haqqāṭōn mimmennû*, *the younger than he*. The WEB attaches it to Othniel
  (*Caleb's younger brother*), and Judges 3:9 in the WEB reads the same. Grammatically the phrase
  could also describe Kenaz, making Othniel Caleb's nephew; this page has not checked how 3:9 reads in
  Hebrew and does not decide the family tree.
- ***The field*.** Joshua 15:18 has *śādeh*, *a field*; Judges 1:14 has *haśśādeh*, *the field*. A
  small change, perhaps a field already in view. Nothing in the text says which.

The verb behind *she got him to ask* (14; Joshua 15:18 *she had him ask*) is the same in both,
*wattəsîtēhû*, from *sût*, *to incite or urge*. The WEB renders one Hebrew word two ways.

**Achsah's request.** She is the prize in Caleb's offer and then the one who speaks. She does not ask
for land but for what makes land usable: *you have set me in the land of the South*, the Negev, and
the South is dry; *give me also springs of water* (15). Her word for the gift is *bərāḵāh*,
*blessing*, so a blessing here is something you can drink. The springs are *gullōt* (WLC), a rare
word; the WEB's *springs* is the usual rendering. Caleb gives more than one: upper and lower.

**Othniel.** He is introduced here, as in Joshua, by his capture of a city and his marriage. In two
chapters he will be the first judge: *Yahweh raised up a savior … even Othniel the son of Kenaz,
Caleb's younger brother* (3:9, stored WEB). Judges thus introduces its first deliverer through
Caleb's family, in the generation that knew the conquest.

**The Kenite.** *The children of the Kenite, Moses' brother-in-law* (16). The Hebrew is *qênî ḥōtēn
mōšeh* (WLC), the same *ḥōtēn* that Numbers 10:29 attaches to Hobab or Reuel and that the WEB there
renders *father-in-law*. The man is not named. Judges 4:11 names *Hobab*, which is why the wiki links
the two; see [[Hobab and the Kenites - Numbers 10, Judges 1 and 4, and 1 Samuel 15]]. *The city of
palm trees* is Jericho by Deuteronomy 34:3, *the valley of Jericho the city of palm trees* (stored WEB);
Judges 3:13 has Eglon of Moab *possess the city of palm trees*. The Kenites leave Jericho, the first
city Israel took, for the dry country south of Arad, and *lived with the people*. Which people is not
said. The Hebrew *ʾet-hāʿām* is ordinarily Israel or Judah. Moses had promised Hobab, *whatever good
Yahweh does to us, we will do the same to you* (Numbers 10:32, stored WEB). This verse is the nearest
the text comes to showing that promise kept; it does not say so.

**Hormah, and Simeon's half of the bargain.** In 1:3 Judah promised Simeon, *I likewise will go with
you into your lot*. Verse 17 has *Judah went with Simeon his brother* to Zephath. The order of the
names is reversed from verse 3 in the Hebrew (*wayyēleḵ yəhûdāh ʾet-šimʿôn ʾāḥîw*, *Judah went with
Simeon his brother*), and Hormah is in Simeon's lot (Joshua 19:4, stored WEB). The promise is kept.
The Hebrew of the naming is *wayyaḥărîmû ʾôtāh wayyiqrāʾ ʾet-šēm-hāʿîr ḥormāh*: they *devoted it*
(*ḥāram*) and called the city *Ḥormah*. Numbers 21:3 had already named a place Hormah after a
devoting. The two namings are kept side by side at
[[Even to Hormah - Numbers 14, Numbers 21, Deuteronomy 1, and Judges 1]].

**Gaza, Ashkelon, Ekron.** Verse 18 says Judah *took* (*wayyilkōd*) three of the five Philistine
cities named in Joshua 13:3, which listed them among *the land that still remains* (13:2). The verse
stands awkwardly before verse 19, where Judah cannot dislodge the people of the valley, and before the
rest of the book, where Gaza is a Philistine city (Samson at Gaza, 16:1–2, stored WEB). It is often
said that the Septuagint reads verse 18 as a negative, *Judah did not take Gaza*. That reading has not
been checked here and is reported only as a claim. The WLC reads it as a positive statement.

**"He could not drive out."** The WEB reads *for he could not drive out the inhabitants of the
valley* (19). The Hebrew is *kî lōʾ ləhôrîš ʾet-yōšəḇê hāʿēmeq*, literally *for not to drive out the
inhabitants of the valley*. There is no verb *could* (*yāḵōl*). The WEB supplies it, as most English
versions do. Joshua 15:63 and 17:12 do contain the verb: *lōʾ yāḵəlû … ləhôrîš*, *they were not able
to drive out*. Judges 1:21 has *lōʾ hôrîšû*, *they did not drive out*, without it. All four verses
were read in the WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb). So the question left open at
[[Kings Struck and Cities Not Driven Out - Joshua 12, 15-17, and Judges 1]] has a partial answer: the
difference between *couldn't* and *didn't* there is in the Hebrew, and Judges 1:19 is neither, an
elliptical construction that does not say whether the failure was one of ability.

**"Yahweh was with Judah."** The verse opens with the presence of Yahweh, then reports the valley not
taken, *because they had chariots of iron*. The text gives that reason and stops. Joshua 17:18 had
promised Joseph, *you shall drive out the Canaanites, though they have chariots of iron* (stored WEB),
and Deuteronomy 20:1 told Israel, facing *horses, chariots, and a people more than you*, not to be
afraid, *for Yahweh your God is with you* (stored WEB). Verse 19 opens with the same assurance in
narrative form and still reports the failure. It does not say
whether Judah failed for want of faith or the promise had a limit. Judges 4:3 will give Jabin *nine
hundred chariots of iron* (stored WEB), and that will be the chariot army Yahweh routs. The wiki notes
the tension and leaves it. A grammatical point: the subject of *drove out* (*wayyōreš*, singular) is
not named. The nearest subject is Yahweh, but *Judah* is the usual reading, and the WEB's *he could
not* treats them as one.

**Two endings for Hebron.** Verse 20 returns to Hebron and gives the city to Caleb *as Moses had
said*. That is Moses' oath of Joshua 14:9, *the land where you walked shall be an inheritance to you*
(stored WEB), and Numbers 14:24. It is told after Judah's whole southern campaign, as though to close
the account of Judah with Caleb's faithfulness kept, before the chapter turns to what the other tribes
did not do.

**Benjamin, and the turn of the chapter.** Verse 21 is the first sentence in Judges with *did not
drive out*. From 1:22 to 1:36 it becomes the refrain, tribe by tribe. So the pericope closes Judah's
success and opens the record of failures. Benjamin's first appearance in Judges is a failure, and the
book will end with Benjamin nearly destroyed (chapters 20–21).
