# Ephraim, Succoth, and Penuel

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 8:1–21

## Summary

The battle is over. Yahweh won it in the valley with the enemy's own swords (7:22), and Ephraim has
brought Gideon the heads of two princes (7:25). This pericope is what comes after the battle. Gideon
meets three groups of Israelites and two kings of Midian, and his dealings with them grow harsher in
order. He calms Ephraim with words (1–3). He threatens Succoth and Penuel (4–9). He captures the kings
(10–12). He punishes Succoth and Penuel (13–17). Then he kills the kings for a private reason: they
killed his brothers (18–21). God is named three times in the pericope, every time by Gideon (3, 7, 19),
and does nothing in it. No one in it calls on him or hears from him.

**Ephraim's complaint (8:1–3).**

> **8:1** The men of Ephraim said to him, "Why have you treated us this way, that you didn't call us when you went to fight with Midian?" They rebuked him sharply.

Ephraim *was* called, in 7:24, but only after the battle, to cut off the retreat. Its complaint is that
it was not called to the fight, and so not to the glory. *They rebuked him sharply* is, in Hebrew,
*they contended with him*, *wayrîbûn ʾittô*. The verb is *rîb*, the verb of Gideon's other name. Joash
said *Let Baal contend against him* (6:32), and the first to contend with Jerubbaal is an Israelite
tribe (see the Notes). *Sharply*, *bəḥozqâ*, *with force*, is the word for how Jabin *mightily
oppressed* Israel (4:3).

> **8:2** He said to them, "What have I now done in comparison with you? Isn't the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer?

> **8:3** God has delivered into your hand the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb! What was I able to do in comparison with you?" Then their anger was abated toward him, when he had said that.

Gideon answers with a proverb from the grape harvest. *The gleaning of the grapes*, *ʿōlĕlôt*, is what
is left on the vines after the harvest, the part the law leaves for the poor (Leviticus 19:10). *The
vintage*, *bāṣîr*, is the whole harvest. Ephraim's leftovers, he says, are worth more than the whole
harvest of his own clan of Abiezer. Its late part in the war, the two princes, outweighs his. And he
gives the victory to God. *God has delivered into your hand* is the sentence the Midianite used of
Gideon (7:14) and Gideon used of his own men (7:15). Now he gives it to Ephraim.

It works. *Their anger was abated* is *their spirit sank*, *rāpətâ rûḥām* (see the Notes). This is
Proverbs 15:1 acted out: *A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger*. See
[[A Gentle Answer - Gideon's Reply to Ephraim and Proverbs 15.1]]. Jephthah will meet the same
complaint, *didn't call us to go with you?* (12:1), with an argument rather than a proverb, and the
quarrel will end with forty-two thousand of Ephraim dead at the fords (12:6, stored WEB; not yet
ingested). See [[The Fords of the Jordan - Joshua 2, Judges 3, 7, and 12]].

**Faint, yet pursuing (8:4–9).**

> **8:4** Gideon came to the Jordan, and passed over, he, and the three hundred men who were with him, faint, yet pursuing.

> **8:5** He said to the men of Succoth, "Please give loaves of bread to the people who follow me; for they are faint, and I am pursuing after Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian."

The story turns back to Gideon. In 7:25 the heads were already *beyond the Jordan*; here he crosses
(see the Notes on [[The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon]]). It is the three hundred again, alone. The
tribes who pursued in 7:23 are not mentioned. *Faint, yet pursuing* is two Hebrew words, *ʿăyēpîm
wərōdəpîm*. *Faint* is the word for Esau coming in from the field, *famished* (Genesis 25:29–30), and for
Israel when Amalek struck the stragglers *when you were faint and weary* (Deuteronomy 25:18) (see the
Notes). Amalek is one of Midian's partners in this war (6:3, 33).

The enemy now has new names. Oreb and Zeeb were *princes*, *śārîm*. [[Zebah]] and [[Zalmunna]] are *the
kings of Midian*. They have not been named before.

[[Succoth (Transjordan)]] is the town Jacob named for the shelters he built there after he parted from
Esau (Genesis 33:17). The request is small and polite: *Please give loaves of bread*. Gideon gives his
reason, and the reason is true.

> **8:6** The princes of Succoth said, "Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand, that we should give bread to your army?"

Succoth has *princes*, *śārîm*, the same word as Oreb and Zeeb. Their answer is a sneer, and it is also
caution. *Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand?* The Hebrew is singular, *the palm*,
*kap*, of the kings (see the Notes). Gideon has three hundred tired men. The kings have fifteen thousand
(10). If Midian wins, a town that fed Gideon's army will pay for it. Succoth wants to see the kings
taken before it takes a side.

> **8:7** Gideon said, "Therefore when Yahweh has delivered Zebah and Zalmunna into my hand, then I will tear your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers."

Gideon's answer begins with Yahweh: *when Yahweh has delivered*. The sentence is about Yahweh's victory
and Gideon's revenge. *I will tear* is *I will thresh*, *wədaštî*, from *dûš*, the verb of the ox that
*treads out the grain* (Deuteronomy 25:4) (see the Notes). The man who was found threshing wheat (6:11,
a different verb) threatens to thresh a town's elders. *Thorns*, *qôṣ*, is the word of the curse on the
ground, *thorns and thistles* (Genesis 3:18).

> **8:8** He went up there to Penuel, and spoke to them in the same way; and the men of Penuel answered him as the men of Succoth had answered.

> **8:9** He spoke also to the men of Penuel, saying, "When I come again in peace, I will break down this tower."

[[Peniel]] is where Jacob wrestled until daybreak and said *I have seen God face to face, and my life is
preserved* (Genesis 32:30). Gideon said nearly the same words of the angel, *I have seen Yahweh's angel
face to face!* (6:22). The Hebrew of both is *pānîm ʾel-pānîm* (see the Notes). Now Gideon comes to
Jacob's Peniel with a threat. Jacob went from Peniel to Succoth. Gideon goes the other way, from Succoth
up to Penuel. See [[Penuel and Succoth - Jacob's Road and Gideon's Pursuit]].

*When I come again in peace* is *bəšûbî bəšālôm*. Jacob used the same words in his vow at Bethel, *so that
I come again to my father's house in peace* (Genesis 28:21), and Jephthah will use them in his vow,
*when I return in peace from the children of Ammon* (11:31, stored WEB; not yet ingested). In the WLC of
Genesis–Judges the phrase occurs only in those three verses (see the Notes). Two vows to God, and
between them Gideon's threat. See
[[When I Return in Peace - Jacob's Vow, Gideon's Threat, and Jephthah's Vow]].

*This tower*, *migdāl*, is a fortified tower in the town. The first *migdāl* in the Bible was Babel's,
*a tower whose top reaches to the sky* (Genesis 11:4). The next is the tower of Shechem, in the story of
Gideon's son (9:46–52). Gideon's son *beat down* Shechem with the same verb, *nātaṣ* (9:45, WLC). See
[[The Fall of Abimelech]] and [[The Tower of Shechem]].

**The kings taken (8:10–12).**

> **8:10** Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, and their armies with them, about fifteen thousand men, all who were left of all the army of the children of the east; for there fell one hundred twenty thousand men who drew sword.

For the first time the narrator gives numbers. The camp *like locusts for multitude* (7:12) was one
hundred thirty-five thousand men, and one hundred twenty thousand of them have fallen. Three hundred
are pursuing the rest. *Who drew sword*, *šōlēp ḥereb*, is the phrase the book will use for the armies
in the war against Benjamin (20:2, 15, 17, 25, 35, 46) (see the Notes). [[Karkor]] is named only here.

> **8:11** Gideon went up by the way of those who lived in tents on the east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and struck the army; for the army was secure.

He comes the way the nomads come, by the tent-dwellers' road east of [[Nobah (town)]] and [[Jogbehah]],
two towns named in the eastern settlement (Numbers 32:35, 42). The army *was secure*, *beṭaḥ*. It thought it was
safe, so far east and so far from the battle. The word is used of Shechem when Simeon and Levi came on
*the unsuspecting city* (Genesis 34:25), and of Laish before Dan takes it, *quiet and secure* (18:7)
(see the Notes). In all three, a city or camp that feels safe is struck.

> **8:12** Zebah and Zalmunna fled; and he pursued them. He took the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and confused all the army.

*He took*, *wayyilkōd*, is the verb of Ephraim *taking* the waters and the princes (7:24–25). Ephraim
took two princes; Gideon takes two kings. *Confused* is *heḥĕrîd*, *made to tremble*, from *ḥārad*. The
spring where the story began was
[[The Spring of Harod]], *ʿên ḥărōd* (7:1), and the men sent home were the *fearful and
trembling*, *ḥārēd* (7:3). The OpenScriptures lemma data file the name, the adjective, and the verb
separately. They share the root letters, and the narrator may mean the reader to hear them (see the
Notes). It is Midian's army that trembles now.

**Succoth and Penuel punished (8:13–17).**

> **8:13** Gideon the son of Joash returned from the battle from the ascent of Heres.

> **8:14** He caught a young man of the men of Succoth, and inquired of him: and he described for him the princes of Succoth, and its elders, seventy-seven men.

He comes back by [[The Ascent of Heres]], a pass named only here. He *caught*, the same verb again, a
*young man*, *naʿar*, of Succoth. *Described* is in Hebrew *wrote*, *wayyiktōb* (see the Notes). The
young man writes down seventy-seven names. The narrator does not say how, or on what. It is the only
use of *kātab* in the WLC of Judges, and it is a list of men to be punished.

> **8:15** He came to the men of Succoth, and said, "See Zebah and Zalmunna, concerning whom you taunted me, saying, 'Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand, that we should give bread to your men who are weary?'"

> **8:16** He took the elders of the city, and thorns of the wilderness and briers, and with them he taught the men of Succoth.

> **8:17** He broke down the tower of Penuel, and killed the men of the city.

*See Zebah and Zalmunna*. He brings the kings to show them. Then he quotes the princes, but not exactly.
They said *your army*, *ṣəbāʾăkā* (6). He remembers *your men who are weary*, *ʾănāšêkā hayyəʿēpîm*, with a
second word for *weary* that occurs nowhere else in Genesis–Judges (see the Notes). The change makes the
refusal crueler than it was.

*He taught the men of Succoth*. The verb is the causative of *yādaʿ*, *know*: he *made them know*. It
does not say what they were made to know. The threat in 8:7 was *I will thresh your flesh*. Many readers
propose that 8:16 once read *he threshed*, a different verb with similar letters. The wiki reports that
proposal and does not adopt it (see the Notes). Then Penuel. He threatened the tower and he breaks it
down. And he does more than he threatened: *he killed the men of the city*. He had not said that he
would.

These are Israelite towns. Gad received Succoth in Joshua's allotment (Joshua 13:27). In the Song,
Yahweh's angel cursed Meroz for not coming (5:23), and the curse stayed a word. Here a judge punishes
Israelite towns for refusing him bread. See
[[Those Who Did Not Come - Meroz, Succoth and Penuel, and Jabesh Gilead]]. Abram, coming back with
three hundred and eighteen men from a pursuit of kings, was met with bread and wine by a king he had
not asked (Genesis 14:14–18). Gideon asks, is refused, and takes his revenge. See
[[Three Hundred Against the Kings - Abram's Pursuit and Gideon's]].

**The kings killed (8:18–21).**

> **8:18** Then he said to Zebah and Zalmunna, "What kind of men were they whom you killed at Tabor?" They answered, "They were like you. Each one resembled the children of a king."

> **8:19** He said, "They were my brothers, the sons of my mother. As Yahweh lives, if you had saved them alive, I would not kill you."

Now the reader learns something the story has not told. The kings had killed Gideon's brothers *at
Tabor*. When, and how, the text does not say. [[Tabor]] is the mountain of Barak's muster (4:6). *What
kind of men* is the Hebrew interrogative *ʾêpōh*, which usually means *where*. In Genesis–Judges it
occurs only here and in Joseph's *Tell me, please, where they are feeding the flock*, when Joseph is
looking for his brothers (Genesis 37:16) (see the Notes). The kings' answer is a compliment, or an
insult, or both: *like you … the children of a king*. The Hebrew is *the sons of the king*. Three verses
later Israel will ask Gideon to rule (8:22), and he will name a son *Abimelech* (8:31, stored WEB; not yet
ingested), a name usually glossed *my father is king*.

*The sons of my mother* means full brothers. This explains the whole pursuit across the Jordan. The war
was Yahweh's (7:22). The pursuit is also Gideon's own blood feud. *As Yahweh lives* is an oath. In the
WLC of Genesis–Judges this is the first time a human being swears *ḥay YHWH*, *as Yahweh lives* (see
the Notes). It is a strange first use. Gideon swears by Yahweh's life that he would have spared Yahweh's
enemies if they had spared his family. He says nothing about the ban on Midian or about what Yahweh had
commanded. The reason for the deaths is personal.

> **8:20** He said to Jether his firstborn, "Get up, and kill them!" But the youth didn't draw his sword; for he was afraid, because he was yet a youth.

> **8:21** Then Zebah and Zalmunna said, "Rise and fall on us; for as the man is, so is his strength." Gideon arose, and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and took the crescents that were on their camels' necks.

[[Jether (son of Gideon)]] is the firstborn. The text does not say why Gideon gives him the task; a common
reading is that death by a boy's hand would shame the kings, and 8:21 suggests they took it so. The boy *didn't draw his sword*, *lōʾ šālap*, the verb of 8:10's *men who drew sword*, and of Ehud, who
*didn't draw the sword out of* Eglon's body (3:22). *For he was afraid*, *yārēʾ*. Gideon was the one who
was afraid (6:27; 7:10), and the fearful were sent home (7:3). Now the son is afraid, *because he was
yet a youth*, *naʿar*, the word for Purah (7:10) and for the young man of Succoth (8:14). Jether's
Hebrew name, *Yeter*, is also how Moses' father-in-law, the Midianite priest, is written at Exodus 4:18
(WEB *Jethro*) (see the Notes). The narrator does not mention it.

The kings ask to die by the father's hand. *As the man is, so is his strength*, *gəbûrâ*. A grown
man's strength for a death. *Strength* is the word of the Song's last line, *as the sun when it rises
in its strength* (5:31). Gideon was greeted as *mighty man of valor*, *gibbôr* (6:12), the same root.
The kings call him a man of strength. He kills them.

Then he *took the crescents*, *śahărōnîm*, ornaments from the camels' necks. The word occurs in
Genesis–Judges only here and in 8:26, where the crescents are part of the gold he turns into an ephod
(8:24–27, not yet ingested). The pericope ends on the first thing Gideon takes for himself.

**How the pericope sits in Gideon's story.** Judges 7 was about Yahweh's victory, and Gideon was afraid
and obedient. Judges 8:1–21 is about Gideon's victory, and he is neither afraid nor told what to do.
Yahweh does not speak. The judge who once hid from Midian now threatens Israelite towns and keeps his
promise to them. The narrator does not comment, but the order of the pericope shows the change: a soft
answer to a strong tribe, threats to weak towns, punishment, and a death sentence given for his own
brothers. The next pericope opens with the offer of a crown (8:22).

## People

- [[Gideon]] — answers Ephraim (2–3); pursues with the three hundred (4); threatens Succoth and Penuel
  (7, 9); takes the kings (12); punishes the towns (16–17); kills the kings for his brothers (18–21).
- [[Ephraim]] — *contends* with Gideon for not being called; calmed (1–3).
- [[Oreb]] and [[Zeeb]] — named by Gideon as Ephraim's share of the victory (3).
- [[Zebah]] and [[Zalmunna]] — *the kings of Midian*; fled to Karkor, taken, and killed (5–21).
- [[Jether (son of Gideon)]] — Gideon's firstborn, too afraid to kill the kings (20).
- [[The Children of the East]] — fifteen thousand left of their army (10).
- [[Joash (father of Gideon)]] — *Gideon the son of Joash* (13).
- [[God]] — named only in Gideon's mouth: *God has delivered* (3), *when Yahweh has delivered* (7), *As
  Yahweh lives* (19).

- [[Succoth (Transjordan)]] — refuses bread; its seventy-seven princes and elders punished (5–8, 14–16).
- [[Peniel]] — *Penuel*; refuses bread; its tower broken down and its men killed (8–9, 17).
- [[The Jordan]] — crossed by Gideon and the three hundred (4).
- [[Karkor]] — where the kings and their fifteen thousand rest (10).
- [[Nobah (town)]] and [[Jogbehah]] — the tent-dwellers' road east of them (11).
- [[The Ascent of Heres]] — Gideon's way back from the battle (13).
- [[Tabor]] — where the kings had killed Gideon's brothers (18).
- [[Midian]] — the kings and the army (5, 12).

## Notes

**"They rebuked him sharply."** *Wayrîbûn ʾittô bəḥozqâ* (8:1, WLC). *Rîb* (7378) in the WLC of Judges
occurs at 6:31 (three times), 6:32, 8:1, 11:25 (twice), and 21:22 (twice). *Ḥozqâ* (2394) occurs in
Genesis–Judges only at 4:3 and 8:1 (WLC).

**"Gleaning … vintage."** *ʿŌlĕlôt* (5955) and *bāṣîr* (1210) (8:2, WLC). The gloss *gleanings* for
*ʿōlĕlôt* and its link to the gleaning law of Leviticus 19:10 are the standard reading, not checked
against a lexicon in this pass. *Abiezer* (44) is the clan name of Joshua 17:2 and Judges 6:34 (WLC).

**"Their anger was abated."** *Rāpətâ rûḥām mēʿālāyw* (8:3, WLC), *their spirit sank away from him*.
*Rāpâ* (7503) is the verb of Moses' *will not fail you* (Deuteronomy 31:6, 8) and of Judges 11:37 and
19:9. *Rûaḥ* is the word for the Spirit that clothed Gideon (6:34). Recorded as an observation; the
narrator does not join them.

**"Faint, yet pursuing."** *ʿĂyēpîm wərōdəpîm* (8:4, WLC). *ʿĀyēp* (5889) occurs in Genesis–Judges at
Genesis 25:29, 30, Deuteronomy 25:18, and Judges 8:4, 5 (WLC). In 8:15 Gideon's quotation uses
*hayyəʿēpîm*, from *yāʿēp* (3286), which occurs in Genesis–Judges only there (WLC). In 8:6 the princes
said *liṣbāʾăkā*, *to your army* (WLC). So the princes' words in 8:15 are not those of 8:6.

**"The hands of Zebah and Zalmunna."** *Hăkap zebaḥ wəṣalmunnāʿ* (8:6, 15, WLC), *the palm*, singular.

**"I will tear your flesh."** *Wədaštî ʾet-bəśarkem* (8:7, WLC). *Dûš* (1758) occurs in Genesis–Judges only
at Deuteronomy 25:4 and Judges 8:7 (WLC). Judges 6:11's *beating out wheat* is *ḥōbēṭ*, from *ḥābaṭ*
(2251), a different verb. *Qôṣ* (6975), *thorns*, occurs in Genesis–Judges at Genesis 3:18, Exodus 22:5
(Hebrew numbering; WEB 22:6), and Judges 8:7, 16. *Barqōnîm* (1303), *briers*, occurs only in Judges
8:7, 16 (WLC, Genesis–Judges).

**Penuel.** *Pənûʾēl* (6439) in 8:8, 9, 17 (WLC), the spelling of Genesis 32:32 (Hebrew numbering; WEB
32:31). Genesis 32:31 (Hebrew; WEB 32:30) has *Pənîʾēl* and *pānîm ʾel-pānîm*. Judges 6:22 also has
*pānîm ʾel-pānîm* (WLC). Judges does not refer to Jacob.

**"When I come again in peace."** *Bəšûbî bəšālôm* (8:9, WLC). A search of the WLC of Genesis, Exodus,
Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, and Judges for *šûb* (7725) followed immediately by *šālôm* (7965) finds
only Genesis 28:21, Judges 8:9, and Judges 11:31. Leviticus was not searched.

**"Tower."** *Migdāl* (4026) in Genesis–Judges: Genesis 11:4, 5; Judges 8:9, 17; 9:46, 47, 49, 51 (twice),
52 (twice) (WLC).

**"Who drew sword."** *Šōlēp ḥereb* (8:10, WLC). *Šālap* (8025) in Judges occurs at 3:22, 8:10, 8:20,
9:54, and 20:2, 15, 17, 25, 35, 46 (WLC). Elsewhere in Genesis–Judges it is the angel's *drawn sword*
(Numbers 22:23, 31; Joshua 5:13).

**"Karkor."** *Baqqarqōr* (8:10, WLC), lemma 7174; only here in Genesis–Judges. The site is not
identified here.

**"Secure."** *Beṭaḥ* (983) in Genesis–Judges: Genesis 34:25 (WEB *unsuspecting*), Deuteronomy 12:10;
33:12, 28, and Judges 8:11; 18:7 (WLC). In Deuteronomy the word is a blessing, *in safety*.

**"Nobah."** *Lənōbaḥ* (5025) in 8:11 (WLC). Numbers 32:42 says Nobah renamed Kenath after himself.
Whether the Nobah of Judges 8:11 is that town is not stated; it has its own page, [[Nobah (town)]], and
is not identified with [[Kenath]].

**"Confused."** *Heḥĕrîd* (8:12, WLC), hiphil of *ḥārad* (2729). Elsewhere in Genesis–Judges the verb is
at Genesis 27:33; 42:28; Exodus 19:16, 18; and Deuteronomy 28:26 (WLC). *ʿÊn ḥărōd* (7:1) is lemma 5878
and *ḥārēd* (7:3) is lemma 2730. That the three share the root letters *ḥ-r-d* is plain from the
spelling. That the name *Harod* means *trembling* is the usual explanation, reported, not checked.

**"The ascent of Heres."** *Milmaʿălēh heḥāres* (8:13, WLC), lemma 2775a. The *Mount Heres* of 1:35 is
*har-ḥeres*, lemma 2776 (WLC). The two are spelled with the same consonants and filed under different
lemmas. The wiki does not identify them. See [[Mount Heres]].

**"He described."** *Wayyiktōb* (8:14, WLC), *kātab* (3789), *write*. WEB *described*. *Seventy-seven*,
*šibʿîm wəšibʿâ* (WLC).

**"He taught."** *Wayyōdaʿ bāhem ʾēt ʾanšê sukkôt* (8:16, WLC), hiphil of *yādaʿ*. The proposal to read
*wayyādoš*, *he threshed*, to match 8:7 is reported from the commentary tradition and was not checked
against the Greek or any other version.

**"What kind of men."** *ʾÊpōh* (375) occurs in Genesis–Judges only at Genesis 37:16 and Judges 8:18
(WLC). WEB renders it *where* in Genesis 37:16 and *what kind of* here. *Bənê hammelek* (8:18, WLC),
*the sons of the king*. The gloss of *Abimelech* as *my father is king* is the standard one, not checked.

**"As Yahweh lives."** *Ḥay YHWH* (8:19, WLC). A search of the WLC of Genesis, Exodus, Numbers,
Deuteronomy, Joshua, and Judges for *ḥay* (2416) immediately followed by *YHWH* (3068) finds only Judges
8:19. Oaths by God's life in other forms, such as God's own *as I live*, are not counted by that search.
*Lû haḥăyitem*, *if you had saved alive* (WLC).

**"Jether."** *Yeter* (3500) in the WLC lemma data occurs in Genesis–Judges at Exodus 4:18, where WEB
reads *Jethro*, and Judges 8:20. The WLC lemma data file both under the same number. Whether the two
names are the same name is a question of lemma tagging; the wiki notes it and draws nothing from it.
*Naʿar* is used of Purah (7:10, 11), the young man of Succoth (8:14), and Jether (8:20, twice) (WLC).

**"Strength."** *Gəbûrâ* (1369) in Genesis–Judges: Exodus 32:18; Deuteronomy 3:24; Judges 5:31; 8:21 (WLC).
*Gibbôr* (6:12) is lemma 1368, from the same root.

**"Crescents."** *Haśśahărōnîm* (7720), only at 8:21 and 8:26 in Genesis–Judges (WLC). The gloss
*crescents* is WEB's; the shape is not described in the text.

**Judgment calls.** The boundary was kept as planned: 8:1–3 (Ephraim) is short and belongs with the
pursuit it interrupts, and 8:21 ends with the kings' deaths before Israel's offer of a crown at 8:22.
Zebah, Zalmunna, and Jether get person pages; Karkor, the ascent of Heres, and Nobah get place pages,
following the wiki's practice for named places. The Nobah of 8:11 gets its own page, `Nobah (town)`,
because the man already has [[Nobah (person)]] and the text does not say the town is Kenath. The
ascent of Heres is not identified with Mount Heres. Gideon's brothers, the young man of Succoth, and
the princes and elders of Succoth get no pages. The killing at Tabor is not dated or placed in the
earlier battle. The *threshed* reading of 8:16 and the meaning of *Harod* are reported, not adopted.
