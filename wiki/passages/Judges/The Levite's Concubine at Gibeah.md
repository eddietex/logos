# The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah

**Book:** [[Judges]]
**Reference:** Judges 19:1–30

## Summary

*In those days, when there was no king in Israel, there was a certain Levite living on the farther side of
the hill country of Ephraim, who took for himself a concubine out of Bethlehem Judah* (1). She *played the
prostitute against him*, goes back to her father's house in Bethlehem Judah, and stays four months (2).
Her husband goes after her *to speak kindly to her, to bring her again*, with his servant and two donkeys;
her father *rejoiced to meet him* (3).

The father keeps him three days, eating and drinking (4). On the fourth morning he says, *Strengthen your
heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward you shall go your way*, and then, *Please be pleased to stay
all night, and let your heart be merry* (5–7). On the fifth day the same again — *stay until the day
declines* — and at evening, *Behold, the day is ending. Stay here, that your heart may be merry; and
tomorrow go on your way early* (8–9). This time *the man wouldn't stay that night*; he sets out late, with
the concubine and the saddled donkeys, and comes *toward Jebus (also called Jerusalem)* (10).

With the day *far spent*, the servant asks to lodge in the city of the Jebusites. *We won't enter into the
city of a foreigner that is not of the children of Israel*, his master says; *we will pass over to Gibeah*
(11–12). The sun goes down *near Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin* (14). They sit in the street, *for
there was no one who took them into his house to stay* (15). An old man coming in from the field at
evening, *from the hill country of Ephraim* and living in Gibeah among Benjamites, asks *Where are you
going? Where did you come from?* (16–17). The Levite says he is going *to Yahweh's house*, that no one has
taken him in, and that he has straw, feed, bread, and wine: *There is no lack of anything* (18–19). *Peace
be to you*, the old man says; *let me supply all your needs. Just don't sleep in the street* (20). He
takes them in, feeds the donkeys, and they wash their feet, eat, and drink (21).

*As they were making their hearts merry, behold, the men of the city, certain wicked fellows, surrounded
the house, beating at the door*, demanding, *Bring out the man who came into your house, that we can have
sex with him!* (22). The old man goes out: *No, my brothers, please don't act so wickedly … don't do this
folly. Behold, here is my virgin daughter and his concubine. I will bring them out now. Humble them, and do
with them what seems good to you* (23–24). The men will not listen; *so the man laid hold of his concubine,
and brought her out to them; and they had sex with her, and abused her all night until the morning* (25).
At dawn she falls down *at the door of the man's house where her lord was, until it was light* (26).

*Her lord rose up in the morning, and opened the doors of the house, and went out to go his way; and
behold, the woman his concubine had fallen down at the door of the house, with her hands on the threshold.*
*Get up, and let us be going!* — *but no one answered* (27–28). He puts her on the donkey and goes home.
There *he took a knife, and cut up his concubine, and divided her, limb by limb, into twelve pieces, and
sent her throughout all the borders of Israel* (29). All who saw said, *Such a deed has not been done or
seen from the day that the children of Israel came up out of the land of Egypt to this day! Consider it,
take counsel, and speak* (30).

**What is going on.** The second story of the epilogue begins with the same refrain and the same two
places as the first — a Levite, the hill country of Ephraim, and Bethlehem Judah — and travels the road in
the opposite direction. It is told in three scenes of hospitality. At Bethlehem the father-in-law gives too
much of it, five days of eating and urging; at Jebus the Levite refuses to ask it of foreigners; at Gibeah,
an Israelite town of Benjamin, nobody offers it but a man from somewhere else, and the town comes to his
door. The third scene is Genesis 19 replayed almost clause by clause, with no angels in the house — see
[[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]].

The narrator passes no verdict on anyone. The only judgment in the chapter is the one put into the mouths
of *all who saw it* (30): nothing like it since the Exodus. What is said and not said does the work. The
concubine never speaks; the men call her *his concubine*, and the narrator calls the husband *her lord*
at the moment he steps over her (26–27). The Levite says *Get up* and *no one answered* (28), and the text
does not say whether she was dead then, or when she died. His account of the night in 20:4–6 leaves out
what he did.

**What is notable.**

- **No king — the third refrain.** *Bayyāmîm hāhēm ûmeleḵ ʾên bəyiśrāʾēl* (1, WLC), the short form again,
  as at 18:1; the full form with *right in his own eyes* closes the book (21:25, stored WEB; see
  [[Wives for Benjamin]]). See [[Right in His Own Eyes - Deuteronomy 12.8 and Judges 17.6 and 21.25]].
- **Two sojourners.** The Levite is *gār*, sojourning, in the far hill country of Ephraim (1, WLC), as
  Micah's Levite was *gār* in Bethlehem (17:7, WLC); the old man of Gibeah is *gār baggiḇʿâ* (16, WLC).
  The only host in Gibeah is not from Gibeah. See
  [[Two Levites from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17 and 19]].
- **A concubine, and a wife.** *ʾIššâ pîleḡeš* (1, WLC), literally *a woman, a concubine*. *Pîleḡeš*
  occurs nine times in this chapter and the next (19:1, 2, 9, 10, 24, 25, 27, 29; 20:4–6) and before that
  in Genesis through Judges only for Nahor's Reumah, Abraham's concubines, Bilhah, Timna, and Gideon's
  Shechemite (Genesis 22:24; 25:6; 35:22; 36:12; Judges 8:31) (WLC lemma 6370). Yet her father is the
  Levite's *ḥōtēn*, father-in-law, and he is her father's *ḥāṯān*, son-in-law (5, 9, WLC): the text
  treats the union as a marriage.
- **Played the prostitute against him.** *Wattizneh ʿālāyw* (2, WLC). *Zānâ* is the book's word for
  Israel *playing the prostitute* after other gods (2:17; 8:27, 33). Some ancient versions are reported
  to read *she was angry with him* here instead — reported, not checked; no Greek text was consulted.
  Why she left the text does not explain beyond the verb; that she went home to her father, not to
  another man, is all it narrates.
- **To speak to her heart.** *Ləḏabbēr ʿal-libbāh* (3, WLC), *to speak kindly to her*. The idiom is
  Shechem's to Dinah after he *humbled her* (Genesis 34:3) and Joseph's to his brothers (Genesis 50:21)
  (stored WEB; WLC). The written text has *ləhăšîḇô*, *to bring him back*, and the Masoretic reading
  *ləhăšîḇāh*, *to bring her back* (3, WLC ketiv and qere); the WEB follows the reading. Nowhere in the
  chapter does he speak to her heart.
- **A morsel of bread.** *Səʿāḏ libbəḵā pat-leḥem* (5, WLC). *Sāʿaḏ*, *strengthen*, occurs in the WLC
  files of Genesis through Judges only here, at verse 8, and at Genesis 18:5, where Abraham offers his
  three visitors *a morsel of bread so you can refresh your heart* — *pat leḥem* and *səʿăḏû libbəḵem*
  together. See [[A Morsel of Bread - Genesis 18.5 and Judges 19.5]].
- **Let your heart be merry.** *Wəyiṭaḇ libbeḵā* (6; *wəyîṭaḇ ləḇāḇeḵā*, 9, WLC). The same heart is merry
  in Gibeah when the mob arrives — *hēmmâ mêṭîḇîm ʾet-libbām* (22, WLC) — and the Philistines' heart was
  merry when they called for Samson (16:25, *kəṭôḇ libbām*). See [[The Death of Samson]].
- **Five days, and a late start.** The father's urging is told four times (5–9). The Levite's refusal to
  stay the fifth night is what puts him on the road at evening, near a foreign city, with no light left.
  The text gives no motive for either man.
- **The city of a foreigner.** *Lōʾ nāsûr ʾel-ʿîr nāḵrî ʾăšer lōʾ-mibbənê yiśrāʾēl hēnnâ* (12, WLC).
  *Nāḵrî* is the word of Deuteronomy 17:15, *You may not put a foreigner over you* (stored WEB; WLC).
  Jebus — the name *Yəḇûs* occurs in the WLC files of Genesis through Judges only at 19:10–11 — was the
  city Benjamin had not driven out (1:21; Joshua 18:28). The Levite trusts Israel and is wrong. See
  [[The City of a Foreigner - Jebus Passed By for Gibeah]] and [[Jerusalem]].
- **Gibeah, or Ramah.** *Let us draw near to one of these places* (13): two Benjaminite towns a few miles
  north of Jebus. [[Ramah]] is the Ramah of Deborah's palm (4:5). The new place is
  [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], kept apart from Judah's [[Gibeah (Judah)]] and Benjamin's listed [[Gibeath]].
- **No one gathers them in.** *Wəʾên ʾîš məʾassēp̄ ʾôṯām habbaytâ lālûn* (15, WLC), said by the
  narrator and again, of himself, by the Levite (18). *Sit in the street*, *bireḥôḇ hāʿîr* (15), is the
  place the angels chose at Sodom, *bāreḥôḇ nālîn*, *we will stay in the street all night* (Genesis 19:2,
  WLC); the old man's *raq bāreḥôḇ ʾal-tālan*, *just don't sleep in the street* (20, WLC), answers it. See
  [[Hospitality]].
- **To Yahweh's house.** *Wəʾet-bêt YHWH ʾănî hōlēḵ* (18, WLC). The Levite says he is going *home*, in
  effect, to the far hill country of Ephraim; the Hebrew says *Yahweh's house*. The Greek is reported to
  read *my house* — reported, not checked. Whether he meant [[Shiloh]], which lies in the hill country of
  Ephraim, or served at a sanctuary, the text does not say. He does not arrive there; he goes *to his
  place* (28).
- **No lack.** *ʾÊn maḥsôr kol-dāḇār* (19, WLC), the Danite spies' phrase for Laish (18:10); the old man
  replies *kol-maḥsôrəḵā ʿālāy*, *all your lack on me* (20, WLC). See [[The Danite Migration]].
- **Sons of Belial.** *ʾAnšê ḇənê-ḇəliyyaʿal* (22, WLC), *certain wicked fellows*. Deuteronomy 13:14
  (Hebrew; English 13:13) has *ʾănāšîm bənê-ḇəliyyaʿal* who lead a city after other gods, and Deuteronomy
  15:9 the *ḇəliyyaʿal* thought; in Genesis through Judges the word occurs only in those two verses and in
  Judges 19:22 and 20:13 (WLC lemma 1100). Israel will demand *the wicked fellows who are in Gibeah* (20:13;
  see [[The War Against Benjamin]]).
- **Beating at the door.** *Mitdappəqîm ʿal-haddālet* (22, WLC). *Dāp̄aq* occurs elsewhere in Genesis
  through Judges only at Genesis 33:13, Jacob's flocks that will die *if they overdrive them one day*
  (stored WEB; WLC). The wiki records the rare verb and builds nothing on it.
- **Bring him out.** *Hôṣēʾ ʾet-hāʾîš … wənēḏāʿennû* (22, WLC), Sodom's *hôṣîʾēm ʾēlênû wənēdəʿâ ʾōṯām*
  (Genesis 19:5) in the singular. See
  [[Bring Him Out - Genesis 19.5, Deuteronomy 17.5, Judges 6.30, and Judges 19.22]].
- **My brothers, don't act so wickedly.** *ʾAl-ʾaḥay ʾal-tārēʿû nāʾ* (23, WLC) against Lot's *ʾal-nāʾ ʾaḥay
  tārēʿû* (Genesis 19:7). The offer that follows has the same verb, *ʾôṣîʾâ-nāʾ*, *let me bring out*, and
  the same *ṭôḇ bəʿênêḵem*, *what seems good to you* (24; Genesis 19:8, WLC). The refrain of these chapters
  is *yāšār bəʿênāyw*, *right in his own eyes*; here the phrase is offered to a mob.
- **This folly.** *ʾAl-taʿăśû ʾet-hannəḇālâ hazzōʾṯ* (23; again 24, WLC). *Nəḇālâ* in Genesis through
  Judges: Genesis 34:7 (Dinah), Deuteronomy 22:21, Joshua 7:15 (Achan), here, and 20:6, 10 (WLC lemma
  5039). See [[Folly in Israel - Nebalah from Genesis 34 to 2 Samuel 13]].
- **Humble them.** *Wəʿannû ʾôṯām* (24, WLC). *ʿInnâ* is the verb of Shechem with Dinah (Genesis 34:2) and
  of the law of the betrothed and unbetrothed girl (Deuteronomy 22:24, 29); in Judges it was Delilah's
  commission, *that we may bind him to afflict him* (16:5, 19) (WLC; stored WEB). The Levite will use it of
  this night himself (20:5, WLC; see [[The War Against Benjamin]]).
- **The man laid hold of his concubine.** *Wayyaḥăzēq hāʾîš bəp̄îlagšô* (25, WLC). Which man — the host or
  the Levite — the Hebrew of verse 25 does not say; *his concubine* and 20:5 point to the Levite. The same
  words, *wayyaḥăzēq bəp̄îlagšô*, return in verse 29 with the knife.
- **They abused her.** *Wayyiṯʿallǝlû-ḇāh kol-hallaylâ* (25, WLC). This form of *ʿālal* occurs in Genesis
  through Judges only here, at Exodus 10:2 (*what things I have done to Egypt*), and at Numbers 22:29
  (Balaam's *you have mocked me*) (WLC; stored WEB): to make sport of, to deal ruthlessly with.
- **Her lord, and the threshold.** The woman *came in the dawning of the day, and fell down at the door …
  where her lord was* (26); in the morning *her lord* opens the doors *to go his way*, and she is there,
  *wəyāḏehā ʿal-hassap̄*, *her hands on the threshold* (27, WLC). The chapter called him *her husband*,
  *ʾîšāh*, when he went after her (3); at the door he is *ʾăḏōnehā*.
- **No one answered.** *Wəʾên ʿōneh* (28, WLC). The Hebrew does not say she was dead. The Greek is
  reported to add *for she was dead* — reported, not checked. In 20:5 the Levite says *she is dead*. The
  wiki leaves open whether she died in the night, on the road, or by the knife.
- **The knife.** *Wayyiqqaḥ ʾet-hammaʾăḵelet* (29, WLC), *the knife* with the article. The word occurs in
  the Hebrew Bible only here, at Genesis 22:6 and 10, and at Proverbs 30:14 (WLC lemma 3979). Abraham *took
  the knife* — *wayyiqqaḥ ʾet-hammaʾăḵelet* (Genesis 22:10, WLC) — and was stopped. See
  [[The Binding of Isaac]] and [[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]].
- **Limb by limb, twelve pieces.** *Wayənattəḥehā laʿăṣāmehā lišnêm ʿāśār nəṯāḥîm* (29, WLC), *cut her up
  by her bones into twelve pieces*. *Nittaḥ … nəṯāḥîm* is the language of the burnt offering, *cut it into
  its pieces* (Leviticus 1:6, 12; Exodus 29:17; Leviticus 8:20), and is used of nothing else in Genesis
  through Judges. *Wayəšalləḥehā bəḵol gəḇûl yiśrāʾēl*, *sent her throughout all the borders of Israel*, is
  word for word Saul's summons with his oxen in 1 Samuel 11:7 (WLC; WEB checked at bible-api.com). See
  [[Cut Limb by Limb - The Burnt Offering, the Concubine, and Saul's Oxen]].
- **Since Egypt.** *Lōʾ-nihyəṯâ wəlōʾ-nirʾăṯâ kāzōʾṯ ləmiyyôm ʿălôṯ bənê-yiśrāʾēl mēʾereṣ miṣrayim* (30,
  WLC). The measure of the deed is the whole of Israel's history as a people. *Śîmû-lāḵem ʿāleyhā ʿuṣû
  wəḏabbērû*, *Consider it, take counsel, and speak*: the next chapter is the counsel (20:7, stored WEB; not
  yet ingested). The WEB's *It was so, that all who saw it said* renders the Hebrew's *and it was, all who
  saw said*; whether the last three imperatives are the Levite's message or the onlookers' words the Hebrew
  leaves open. A longer Greek text in which the Levite instructs his messengers is reported — not checked.

**How it connects.** The chapter is the hinge of the epilogue. Its first verse matches 17:7–8 — a Levite,
Bethlehem Judah, the hill country of Ephraim — and its last points forward to the assembly *as one man*
at Mizpah, the war, and the near-extinction of Benjamin ([[The War Against Benjamin]], 20:1–48;
[[Wives for Benjamin]], 21:1–25). The wiki's
earlier pages already anticipate it: [[Gibeah and Sodom - Judges 19 as a Retelling]] lays the two
chapters side by side; [[Folly in Israel - Nebalah from Genesis 34 to 2 Samuel 13]] sets the *nəḇālâ* of
Gibeah beside Dinah; [[Hospitality]] sets the two door scenes against the two annunciations of Genesis 18
and Judges 13.

Outside the stored text, Gibeah has two afterlives. It is Saul's town — *Saul also went to his house to
Gibeah* (1 Samuel 10:26), *Gibeah of Saul* (11:4) — and Saul summons Israel by cutting oxen and sending the
pieces *throughout all the borders of Israel* (11:7) (WEB checked at bible-api.com; 1 Samuel not yet in
raw). And Hosea makes it a byword: *They have deeply corrupted themselves, as in the days of Gibeah*
(9:9); *Israel, you have sinned from the days of Gibeah* (10:9) (WEB checked at bible-api.com; WLC checked
for *kîmê haggiḇʿâ* and *mîmê haggiḇʿâ*). Whether Hosea means this night, the war that followed, or Saul's
kingship, the text does not say; readers propose each.

## Notes

**Whose fault the text says.** It names the crime and those who did it: *the men of the city, certain
wicked fellows* (22). It does not comment on the Levite's putting her out, on the host's offer, or on the
husband's *Get up, and let us be going!*. Readers who find the Levite condemned point to the narrator's
shift from *her husband* to *her lord*, to the unanswered *Get up*, and to his own retelling in 20:4–6,
which says *They intended to kill me* and omits his act; readers who find him a wronged man point to the
assembly that takes his side. The wiki records the details and not a verdict.

**Was she dead?** Verse 28's *no one answered* leaves it open; verse 29 says nothing; 20:5 says *she is
dead*, and 20:4 calls her *the woman who was murdered* (see [[The War Against Benjamin]]). The wiki does not
decide whether the knife fell on the living. The Greek's *for she was dead* at 19:28 is reported, not
checked.

**Her offense in verse 2.** *Wattizneh ʿālāyw* is the reading of the WLC; the reported alternative, *she
was angry with him*, would make her flight a quarrel rather than infidelity. That she goes home to her
father, and that the husband goes *to speak to her heart*, fits either. Recorded, not resolved.

**Yahweh's house.** If *bêt YHWH* is right, the Levite was a sanctuary servant and the chapter's one
mention of God is in his mouth. If the reported Greek *my house* is right, the Hebrew could have arisen
from an abbreviation; the wiki has not checked either the Greek or the proposal, and follows the WEB.

**Judgment calls.** Kept 19:1–30 as one pericope as planned; the journey (1–15), the night (16–28), and the
pieces (29–30) are one movement. Created pages for the Levite, the concubine, the father-in-law, and the
old man, because each acts and each recurs in the wiki's comparisons; the servant, the old man's daughter,
and the men of Gibeah have none. Named the person pages by role and relation, following
[[Manoah's Wife]] and the former `Micah's Levite`. Created [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]] as its own place, kept
apart from [[Gibeah (Judah)]] and from [[Gibeath]] in Benjamin's list (Joshua 18:28), whose identification
