# The Borders of Judah

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 15:1–12

## Summary

Judah’s lot is described according to its families, beginning at the southern edge beside
Edom and Zin. The southern line runs from the Salt Sea to the brook of Egypt and the sea;
the east follows the Salt Sea to the Jordan’s mouth. A longer northern line runs from that
junction past Jerusalem’s southern side, Kiriath Jearim, Beth Shemesh, Timnah, and Ekron
to the sea. The great sea’s shore closes the west. This is a territorial description,
not a travel story or a new campaign, and it does not identify the surveyors or the lot’s mechanism.

## Key verses

Full pericope, WEB, copied from the stored source:

> **1** The lot for the tribe of the children of Judah according to their families was to the border of Edom, even to the wilderness of Zin southward, at the uttermost part of the south.

> **2** Their south border was from the uttermost part of the Salt Sea, from the bay that looks southward;

> **3** and it went out southward of the ascent of Akrabbim, and passed along to Zin, and went up by the south of Kadesh Barnea, and passed along by Hezron, went up to Addar, and turned towards Karka;

> **4** and it passed along to Azmon, went out at the brook of Egypt; and the border ended at the sea. This shall be your south border.

> **5** The east border was the Salt Sea, even to the end of the Jordan. The border of the north quarter was from the bay of the sea at the end of the Jordan.

> **6** The border went up to Beth Hoglah, and passed along by the north of Beth Arabah; and the border went up to the stone of Bohan the son of Reuben.

> **7** The border went up to Debir from the valley of Achor, and so northward, looking toward Gilgal, that faces the ascent of Adummim, which is on the south side of the river. The border passed along to the waters of En Shemesh, and ended at En Rogel.

> **8** The border went up by the valley of the son of Hinnom to the side of the Jebusite (also called Jerusalem) southward; and the border went up to the top of the mountain that lies before the valley of Hinnom westward, which is at the farthest part of the valley of Rephaim northward.

> **9** The border extended from the top of the mountain to the spring of the waters of Nephtoah, and went out to the cities of Mount Ephron; and the border extended to Baalah (also called Kiriath Jearim);

> **10** and the border turned about from Baalah westward to Mount Seir, and passed along to the side of Mount Jearim (also called Chesalon) on the north, and went down to Beth Shemesh, and passed along by Timnah;

> **11** and the border went out to the side of Ekron northward; and the border extended to Shikkeron, and passed along to Mount Baalah, and went out at Jabneel; and the goings out of the border were at the sea.

> **12** The west border was to the shore of the great sea. This is the border of the children of Judah according to their families.

## People
- [[Judah]] — The tribal descendants, not the patriarch personally, receive the lot according to their families (1, 12). The boundary circuit gives that corporate inheritance a defined extent.
- [[Reuben]] — Bohan is called son of Reuben in the name of the boundary stone (6). This does not narrate an action of the patriarch or establish the precise generation intended.
- [[Bohan]] — Named only as son of Reuben in the stone that marks Judah’s north border (6). The passage supplies no deeds, dates, or explanation of the memorial’s name.

## Places
- [[Edom]] — The lot reaches Edom’s border at the southern wilderness (1). This is a limit of Judah, not a grant of Edom.
- [[The Wilderness of Zin]] — The southern extent of Judah reaches Zin; the line passes along Zin south of the ascent of Akrabbim (1, 3).
- [[The Salt Sea]] — Its southern bay begins the south border; the sea itself forms the east border up to the Jordan’s end, where the north border begins (2, 5).
- [[The Ascent of Akrabbim]] — The south border runs south of this ascent before passing along Zin (3), repeating a landmark of Numbers 34:4.
- [[Kadesh]] — The boundary passes south of Kadesh Barnea (3); this is a boundary description, not another visit to the wilderness camp.
- [[Hezron (place)]] — A southern landmark after Kadesh Barnea and before Addar (3). Distinguished from Hezron son of Perez; no personal namesake or modern site is established.
- [[Addar]] — A southern landmark after Hezron and before the turn toward Karka (3). Numbers 34:4 names Hazar Addar on the comparable route, but the texts do not explicitly identify the names.
- [[Karka]] — The southern boundary turns toward Karka after Addar and before passing to Azmon (3–4).
- [[Azmon]] — The southern boundary passes here and reaches the brook of Egypt, ending at the sea (4), as in Numbers 34:4–5.
- [[The Brook of Egypt]] — The south border reaches this brook before ending at the sea (4). No equation with the Nile or Genesis 15:18’s river of Egypt is supplied.
- [[The Great Sea]] — The southern and northern boundary routes terminate at the sea, whose shore forms Judah’s west border (4, 11–12).
- [[The Jordan]] — The river’s end at the Salt Sea locates the north end of Judah’s east border and the start of its northern boundary (5).
- [[Beth Hoglah]] — The northern boundary ascends to Beth Hoglah from the Salt Sea and Jordan junction, then passes north of Beth Arabah (5–6).
- [[Beth Arabah]] — The northern line passes north of Beth Arabah before ascending to Bohan’s stone (6).
- [[The Stone of Bohan]] — A stone named for Bohan son of Reuben marks the northern boundary after Beth Arabah (6). No account of its erection or reason for its name is given.
- [[Debir (Judah border)]] — The north border ascends to Debir from the valley of Achor (7). This boundary occurrence is kept distinct from Debir near Hebron in Joshua 15:15 and Debir in Gad (13:26); no identification is supplied by this verse.
- [[Valley of Achor]] — The northern boundary ascends from this valley to Debir (7). The scene of judgment in Joshua 7 now serves as a territorial landmark.
- [[Gilgal]] — The border looks toward Gilgal opposite the ascent of Adummim (7). The shared name alone does not establish that this landmark is Joshua’s camp; this occurrence is recorded here with that identification left open.
- [[The Ascent of Adummim]] — The ascent faces the Gilgal bearing on Judah’s north border and is south of the river mentioned in verse 7. The river is unnamed in that verse.
- [[En Shemesh]] — The northern boundary passes to these waters before ending at En Rogel in this stage of the route (7).
- [[En Rogel]] — The boundary reaches En Rogel after En Shemesh, before ascending by the valley of the son of Hinnom (7–8).
- [[The Valley of Hinnom]] — Called the valley of the son of Hinnom and the valley of Hinnom in verse 8. The boundary ascends beside it to the south side of Jerusalem and onward to the mountain. No biography of Hinnom or his son is supplied.
- [[Jerusalem]] — Verse 8 identifies the Jebusite location with Jerusalem and traces the line on its south side. The border description does not report the city captured.
- [[The Valley of Rephaim]] — The mountain reached west of the valley of Hinnom lies at the northern extremity of this valley (8). The geographical name is not a report of an encounter with Rephaim.
- [[Nephtoah]] — The spring of the waters of Nephtoah lies on the northern boundary between the mountain and the cities of Mount Ephron (9).
- [[Mount Ephron]] — The route reaches the cities of Mount Ephron after Nephtoah and then extends to Baalah (9). No relation to Ephron the Hittite is stated.
- [[Kiriath Jearim]] — The text expressly identifies Baalah with Kiriath Jearim (9). The boundary reaches it from Mount Ephron and turns westward toward Mount Seir (9–10).
- [[Mount Seir (Judah border)]] — A mountain reached westward from Baalah on Judah’s northern boundary (10). Kept distinct from the Seir associated with Edom; the shared name does not make this route run through Edom.
- [[Mount Jearim]] — The route passes the north side of Mount Jearim, also called Chesalon in the WEB, then descends to Beth Shemesh (10). No renaming event is narrated.
- [[Beth Shemesh]] — The boundary descends here after Mount Jearim and passes by Timnah (10). No identification with another similarly named town is imposed.
- [[Timnah]] — The boundary passes Timnah after Beth Shemesh (10). This is a secure occurrence of the name; whether it is the sheep-shearing destination of Genesis 38 remains unresolved, as the existing identification discussion records.
- [[Ekron]] — The boundary goes to Ekron’s northern side before extending to Shikkeron (11). Inclusion in the route is not a report of conquest or occupation.
- [[Shikkeron]] — The boundary extends here after the northern side of Ekron, then passes to Mount Baalah (11).
- [[Mount Baalah]] — The northern boundary passes this mountain after Shikkeron and before Jabneel (11). The text does not equate it with Baalah/Kiriath Jearim of verse 9.
- [[Jabneel]] — The route passes through Jabneel before ending at the sea (11). No modern identification is assigned.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The lot for Judah is delimited on four sides and framed by reference to its families (1, 12). Allotment defines an inheritance; it does not by itself assert occupation of every named place.
- [[Names and Naming]] — The boundary supplies paired designations: the Jebusite location/Jerusalem, Baalah/Kiriath Jearim, and Mount Jearim/Chesalon (8–10). These are explanatory identifications, not narrated acts of renaming.

## Notes

**One tribe within the larger grant.** Numbers 34:3–6 and this unit share much of the
southern line and the western sea. The northern line here belongs to Judah, not to all
Canaan. Hazar Addar in Numbers and Hezron/Addar/Karka here remain distinct textual lists.
The connection page records the correspondence without flattening their differences.

**Allocation and occupation.** The repeated family formula (1, 12) frames a territorial
share. It does not cancel the remaining-land report of Joshua 13:1–7 or establish that
Jerusalem and Ekron have been occupied. The tension between the conquest summaries and
unfinished possession remains; this boundary list does not resolve it.

**Names require restraint.** Debir on this north boundary is not silently merged with
Debir near Hebron or Debir in Gad. Mount Seir on this route is distinguished from Edom’s
Seir. Gilgal’s relationship to the camp and this Timnah’s relationship to Genesis 38’s town
remain open. Mount Baalah is not automatically Baalah/Kiriath Jearim. The text explicitly
pairs some names (8–10), but does not provide modern coordinates for the whole circuit.

**People embedded in geography.** Bohan receives a brief page because the text names him
and his relationship to Reuben. Hinnom and his unnamed son remain covered by the valley
page: the text provides no independent biography. The Jebusite designation is covered with
Jerusalem, its explicit geographical gloss here. Ephron and Rephaim in place-names do not
establish the presence of the similarly named person or people. Judah denotes descendants.
