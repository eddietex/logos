# The Cities of Refuge Appointed

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 20:1–9

## Summary

Yahweh tells Joshua to have Israel assign the refuges previously commanded through Moses
(1–2). An unintentional killer can flee from the avenger of blood, explain his case at the
city gate, and receive a place among the inhabitants. The elders must not surrender him
when he killed without prior hatred (3–5). Residence, congregational judgment, and the
high priest’s death all figure in the provision; return is to his own house and city (6).

The community sets apart three western cities—Kedesh, Shechem, and Hebron—and three
eastern cities—Bezer, Ramoth, and Golan (7–8). The closing explanation includes both
Israelites and resident aliens and protects the fugitive from death before trial (9).
After the tribal lots, the land is organized to protect life as well as distribute holdings.

## Key verses

> **20:4** He shall flee to one of those cities, and shall stand at the entrance of the gate of the city, and declare his case in the ears of the elders of that city. They shall take him into the city with them, and give him a place, that he may live among them.

> **20:6** He shall dwell in that city until he stands before the congregation for judgment, until the death of the high priest that shall be in those days. Then the man slayer shall return, and come to his own city, and to his own house, to the city he fled from.’”

> **20:9** These were the appointed cities for all the children of Israel, and for the alien who lives among them, that whoever kills any person unintentionally might flee there, and not die by the hand of the avenger of blood, until he stands trial before the congregation.

## People
- [[God]] — Yahweh commands Joshua to implement the refuge provision previously spoken through Moses (1–2). The command protects the unintentional killer pending judgment rather than authorizing unrestricted retaliation.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — He receives the command to tell Israel to appoint the refuge cities (1–2). The following communal designation implements the law; no individual asylum hearing by Joshua is narrated.
- [[Moses]] — Yahweh identifies the refuge instruction as previously given through Moses (2). Moses is the remembered mediator, not a living participant in this appointment.
- [[Naphtali]] — Kedesh in Galilee is located in the hill country of Naphtali (7). The name refers to the descendant tribe and its territory, not an action by the patriarch.
- [[Ephraim]] — Shechem is located in Ephraim’s hill country among the western refuge cities (7). This is tribal geography, not an appearance by the patriarch.
- [[Judah]] — Kiriath Arba, expressly identified as Hebron, is the refuge in Judah’s hill country (7). The patriarch is not an actor.
- [[Reuben]] — Bezer in the wilderness plain is assigned from Reuben’s tribe as an eastern refuge (8), repeating the city named in Deuteronomy 4:43. The descendants, not the patriarch, are meant.
- [[Gad]] — Ramoth in Gilead is assigned from Gad’s tribe as an eastern refuge (8), repeating Deuteronomy 4:43. The patriarch is not present.
- [[Manasseh]] — Golan in Bashan is assigned from Manasseh’s tribe as an eastern refuge (8), repeating Deuteronomy 4:43. The name denotes the descendants.

The elders, killer, avenger, congregation, and high priest are legal roles. No named
fugitive, victim, or current high priest is supplied; no separate figure is invented.

## Notes

- **Admission is not acquittal.** Verse 4 requires a statement before the refuge city’s elders;
  verses 6 and 9 retain congregational judgment. Verse 6 compresses trial and the priest’s
  death into one residence instruction. Numbers 35:24–28 clarifies the sequence of judgment,
  restoration to refuge, and release after the priest dies; this is a comparison, not wording
  supplied by Joshua itself.
- **Different duties of elders.** Deuteronomy 19:12 describes the murderer’s own city’s elders
  retrieving him for execution. Joshua 20:4–5 describes the refuge city’s elders receiving
  and protecting an unintentional killer. These instructions address different cases and
  communities; neither should be flattened into unconditional sanctuary or automatic surrender.
- **Six and the conditional addition.** Joshua records six cities, not the conditional extra
  three discussed in Deuteronomy 19:8–9. The relationship of that extension to the eastern
  three remains an interpretive issue, recorded in the existing connection page; this list
  does not state why a further enlargement is absent.
- **Earlier designation retained.** Deuteronomy 4:41–43 attributes the eastern appointments
  to Moses. Joshua repeats their names within the completed system without explaining whether
  this is confirmation or a new administrative act.
- **Death of the priest.** It marks return home, but this passage does not identify the priest,
  say his death is a sacrifice, or explicitly describe it as atonement.
- **Scope and sources.** The planned 20:1–9 unit is retained. Quotations are copied verbatim
  from stored WEB Joshua; comparisons use stored Numbers 35 and Deuteronomy 4 and 19.
  No new Hebrew or Greek wording claim, unstored-book quotation, or modern site identification
  is made.
