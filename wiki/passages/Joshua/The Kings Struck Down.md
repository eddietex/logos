# The Kings Struck Down

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 12:1–24

## Summary

The conquest narrative ends in a list. Its first half turns back across the Jordan to the land
Israel took *toward the sunrise*, from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon (1). Two kings are
named and their realms traced: Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon and ruled from
Aroer on the Arnon through half of Gilead to the Jabbok, the border of Ammon, and down the Arabah
to the sea of Chinneroth and the Salt Sea under the slopes of Pisgah (2–3); and Og king of
Bashan, *of the remnant of the Rephaim*, who lived at Ashtaroth and Edrei and ruled Mount Hermon,
Salecah, and all Bashan to the border of the Geshurites and the Maacathites (4–5). *Moses the
servant of Yahweh* and Israel struck them, and Moses gave that land to Reuben, Gad, and the
half-tribe of Manasseh (6).

The second half lists the kings whom *Joshua and the children of Israel* struck west of the
Jordan, between Baal Gad in the valley of Lebanon and Mount Halak that goes up to Seir. Joshua
gave this land to the tribes by their divisions — hill country, lowland, Arabah, slopes,
wilderness, and the South, the land of six named peoples (7–8). Then the kings are counted one
by one, each with the word *one*, from the king of Jericho to the king of Tirzah: *all the kings
thirty-one* (9–24).

## Key verses

> **12:1** Now these are the kings of the land, whom the children of Israel struck, and possessed their land beyond the Jordan toward the sunrise, from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon, and all the Arabah eastward:

> **12:6** Moses the servant of Yahweh and the children of Israel struck them. Moses the servant of Yahweh gave it for a possession to the Reubenites, and the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh.

> **12:7** These are the kings of the land whom Joshua and the children of Israel struck beyond the Jordan westward, from Baal Gad in the valley of Lebanon even to Mount Halak, that goes up to Seir. Joshua gave it to the tribes of Israel for a possession according to their divisions;

> **12:9** the king of Jericho, one; the king of Ai, which is beside Bethel, one;

> **12:24** the king of Tirzah, one: all the kings thirty-one.

## Notes

**Why a list.** After eleven chapters of narrative, the conquest half of the book closes with a
register. It does what the battle reports could not: it puts Moses' war and Joshua's war on one
page, under one heading, *the kings of the land*. The east bank (1–6) and the west bank (7–24) are
introduced by the same formula — *these are the kings of the land whom … struck* — and close on
the same act of giving: *Moses … gave it for a possession* (6), *Joshua gave it … for a
possession* (7). The successor is measured by the predecessor in the form of the list itself, and
the land is one land on both sides of the river. Chapter 13 opens the allotment that verse 7 has
already announced.

**Two kings and thirty-one.** The east bank has two named kings with their realms described
border by border; the west bank has thirty-one unnamed kings with nothing but a city and the word
*one*. The east is mapped as territory; the west is counted as defeated rulers. The count is the
text's own (24), and the list does produce it: fifteen verses of two kings each and a single king
in verse 24.

**The east bank recalled.** Verses 2–5 compress what Numbers 21:21–35 and Deuteronomy 2:24–3:17
narrate at length — see [[Sihon and Og]], [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]], and
[[The Defeat of Og Recounted]]. The boundary language is close to Deuteronomy 3:8–17: from the
Arnon to Hermon (3:8), Salecah and Edrei (3:10), Og *of the remnant of the Rephaim* (3:11), the
border of the Geshurites and the Maacathites (3:14), the Jabbok as Ammon's border (3:16), and
*from Chinnereth even to the sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea, under the slopes of Pisgah* (3:17).
Joshua 12 adds Beth Jeshimoth and spells the northern sea *Chinneroth*. Joshua 13:8–13 will walk
the same ground a third time and add that Israel *didn't drive out the Geshurites, nor the
Maacathites* (13:13).

**The order of the western list.** The list roughly follows the narrative. Jericho and Ai come
first, in the order they fell (9). The next five — Jerusalem, Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, Eglon —
are the coalition of 10:3, though here they are cities, not the named men who died at Makkedah
(10–12). Gezer and Debir close the southern campaign of chapter 10 (12–13). Madon, Hazor,
Shimron Meron, and Achshaph (19–20) open the northern coalition of 11:1. Between and after these
come cities the conquest narrative never mentions. The grouping is the wiki's reading of the
sequence; the list itself gives no headings and no regions after verse 8.

**Kings the narrative never fought.** Over a dozen of the thirty-one have no battle in Joshua:
Geder, Hormah, Arad, Adullam, Bethel, Tappuah, Hepher, Aphek, Lassharon, Taanach, Megiddo, Kedesh,
Jokneam, Dor, Goiim, and Tirzah. The list therefore records more than chapters 6–11 narrate; it
does not claim to be a summary of them. Several of those names carry a later qualification:

- *Bethel*: its men pursue Israel from Ai (8:17), but no capture of Bethel or death of its king is
  told in Joshua. Judges 1:22–25 (WEB checked, not yet ingested) narrates the house of Joseph
  taking Bethel after Joshua's death.
- *Taanach, Megiddo, and Dor*: Manasseh *couldn't drive out the inhabitants* of these cities
  (17:11–12), and Judges 1:27 (WEB checked) repeats it.
- *Gezer*: its king Horam was struck while helping Lachish (10:33), but the Canaanites of Gezer
  were not driven out (16:10; Judges 1:29, WEB checked).
- *Jerusalem*: its king died at Makkedah (10:23–26), but Judah *couldn't drive out* the Jebusites
  (15:63), and Judges 1:21 (WEB checked) says the same of Benjamin.

A king struck is not a city held. The list counts the first; the book keeps the second in view
elsewhere. See [[Kings Struck and Cities Not Driven Out - Joshua 12, 15-17, and Judges 1]].

**Hormah and Arad.** Verse 14 places the kings of Hormah and Arad among those *Joshua and the
children of Israel struck beyond the Jordan westward*. Numbers 21:1–3 has *the king of Arad* fight
Israel in Moses' time, and Israel destroy *them and their cities* and name the place Hormah.
Judges 1:17 (WEB checked) has Judah and Simeon destroy Zephath after Joshua's death and call it
Hormah. Joshua 12:14 sits between the two and fits neither easily. The wiki links the king of
Arad here to [[King of Arad (of Moses' time)]] only as a reference to the earlier account, not as
an identification; whether Joshua 12:14 means the same man, a successor, or a summary that folds
Moses' southern victory into Joshua's list is not stated. See
[[Even to Hormah - Numbers 14, Numbers 21, Deuteronomy 1, and Judges 1]].

**Hebron's king again.** Hoham of Hebron dies at Makkedah (10:23–26), 10:37 kills *its king*
again when the city is taken, and verse 10 here counts *the king of Hebron, one*. The count of one
does not settle which death it means. The wiki keeps both notices and claims neither a successor
nor a double death; see [[The Southern Conquest and the Land Still Remaining]].

**Names and qualifiers.** New place pages are created for the ten royal cities first named here,
and for Geshur and Maacath, which Joshua 13:13 names as places. *Hepher* takes `(city)` because
Hepher is also a man of Manasseh's line in the ingested Numbers 26:32–33. *Tappuah*, *Aphek*, and
*Kedesh* are each named again later in Joshua for places that may or may not be these (Tappuah
15:34 and 16:8; Aphek 13:4 and 19:30; Kedesh 15:23, 19:37, and 20:7); the pages say so, and the
ingest that reaches them decides whether to split. *Shimron Meron* is linked to the existing
[[Shimron]] of 11:1 as the probable same royal city; the text does not equate the two names.
*Lassharon* gets its own page; any relation to [[Sharon]] is not claimed. *Jokneam in Carmel* is
linked to [[Carmel (mountain)]] because the setting is northern and Joshua 19:26 puts Carmel on
Asher's western border; the verse itself says only *Carmel*. *Goiim in Gilgal* takes the whole
phrase as its title so as not to identify it with the Goiim of [[Chedorlaomer]]'s ally Tidal
(Genesis 14:1), or the Gilgal with Israel's camp. The thirty-one unnamed kings do not receive new
person pages; where the narrative already gave a king a page, that page is linked. No modern site
is assigned to any city.
