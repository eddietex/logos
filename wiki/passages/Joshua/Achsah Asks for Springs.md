# Achsah Asks for Springs

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 15:13–19

## Summary

Caleb’s portion within Judah becomes a local conquest and a household story. He drives out
three sons of Anak from Hebron, then offers Achsah his daughter as wife to whoever captures
Kiriath Sepher. Othniel takes the city and marries her. Achsah involves him in a request for
a field, then asks Caleb directly for springs to accompany her southern land. He grants both
the upper and lower springs. The story moves from obtaining territory to obtaining the water
needed to live on it.

## Key verses

Complete pericope, WEB, copied from the stored source:

> **13** He gave to Caleb the son of Jephunneh a portion among the children of Judah, according to the commandment of Yahweh to Joshua, even Kiriath Arba, named after the father of Anak (also called Hebron).

> **14** Caleb drove out the three sons of Anak: Sheshai, and Ahiman, and Talmai, the children of Anak.

> **15** He went up against the inhabitants of Debir: now the name of Debir before was Kiriath Sepher.

> **16** Caleb said, “He who strikes Kiriath Sepher, and takes it, to him I will give Achsah my daughter as wife.”

> **17** Othniel the son of Kenaz, the brother of Caleb, took it: and he gave him Achsah his daughter as wife.

> **18** When she came, she had him ask her father for a field. She got off her donkey, and Caleb said, “What do you want?”

> **19** She said, “Give me a blessing. Because you have set me in the land of the South, give me also springs of water.” So he gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.

## People
- [[Caleb (son of Jephunneh)]] — Caleb receives Hebron under Yahweh’s command, drives out Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, and offers Achsah in marriage to the conqueror of Debir. He grants her both upper and lower springs when she asks (13–19). The land grant of chapter 14 now leads to action and household provision.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — The grant to Caleb is made according to Yahweh’s command to Joshua (13). The local victories credited to Caleb and Othniel remain alongside the broader victories credited to Joshua in 10:36–39 and 11:21; the chronology is not explained.
- [[God]] — Yahweh’s command to Joshua grounds Caleb’s portion among Judah (13). No new divine speech is quoted in this scene; the springs are granted by Caleb in response to Achsah.
- [[Judah]] — Caleb’s portion lies among the children of Judah (13). The name denotes the tribal descendants here, not the patriarch acting personally; the tribal allotment contains a particular household’s grant.
- [[The Anakim]] — Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai are named as the three sons of Anak whom Caleb drives out (14). Verse 13 associates Kiriath Arba with the father of Anak. This local expulsion remains in tension with Joshua’s comprehensive removal in 11:21–22; no return is narrated.
- [[Achsah]] — Daughter of Caleb and wife of Othniel. After Caleb’s offer of marriage to the conqueror of Kiriath Sepher is fulfilled, she initiates a request for a field and asks Caleb directly for springs (16–19). Her request links household land to water provision; the narrator records no rebuke.
- [[Othniel]] — Son of Kenaz who captures Kiriath Sepher (Debir) and receives Caleb’s daughter Achsah as wife (17). Verse 18 presents Achsah as having him ask her father for a field. The WEB places the brother-of-Caleb description beside his patronymic; this page does not settle the family tree beyond the translated wording.

## Places
- [[Hebron]] — Kiriath Arba is identified as Hebron and as named after Anak’s father (13). Caleb’s grant is followed by the expulsion of the three sons of Anak (14). The earlier conquest in 10:36–37 and removal in 11:21 remain alongside this account without a supplied chronology.
- [[Debir (city)]] — Formerly Kiriath Sepher, Debir is attacked after Caleb’s action at Hebron. Othniel takes it and receives Achsah as wife (15–17). Joshua’s capture in 10:38–39 and removal of the Anakim in 11:21 remain in tension with this further capture; the text does not describe a reoccupation.
- [[The Negev]] — Achsah describes her grant as land of the South and asks for springs; Caleb gives both upper and lower springs (19). Linked here under the existing South alias, without locating the field or identifying the springs at a modern site.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Inheritance moves from Judah’s tribal boundaries to Caleb’s portion and Achsah’s request for water (13–19). A land grant and the resources needed to use it are distinct gifts; the request for springs makes that practical distinction visible.
- [[War]] — Caleb expels the three sons of Anak and Othniel captures Debir (14–17). These local actions stand beside Joshua’s earlier comprehensive victories (10:36–39; 11:21–23) and the rest notice of 14:15. Their chronological relationship is not stated.
- [[Marriage]] — Caleb promises his daughter to the man who captures Kiriath Sepher; Othniel succeeds and receives Achsah (16–17). Her consent to that arrangement is not narrated. Her subsequent initiative in seeking a field and springs is explicitly narrated (18–19).
- [[Blessing]] — Achsah asks her father for a blessing and specifies springs of water (19). Caleb answers with both upper and lower springs. Here blessing takes the concrete form of a resource grant rather than a recorded spoken benediction.
- [[Names and Naming]] — Kiriath Arba is identified with Hebron and linked to Anak’s father (13); Debir’s former name is Kiriath Sepher (15). The passage supplies these identifications without narrating a renaming ceremony or explaining the meaning of Kiriath Sepher.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Caleb’s portion is assigned according to Yahweh’s command to Joshua (13). This authorizes the grant; the passage does not say that Caleb’s marriage offer or Achsah’s request was separately commanded.

## Notes

**Grant and conquest remain distinct.** Verse 13 resumes Caleb’s portion after Judah’s border
circuit. The expulsion of the Anakim and capture of Debir stand in explicit tension with the
comprehensive conquest notices of chapters 10–11. A summary followed by local detail is a
possible reading; a new campaign is another. No return or resettlement is narrated here, so
neither explanation is asserted as the text’s own chronology.

**Achsah’s initiative.** The WEB says she had him ask for a field (18), then records her own
request for springs (19). It does not record Othniel refusing, nor a separate speech in which
he asks. Caleb’s marriage offer treats her marriage as a reward for victory; her consent to
that arrangement is not narrated. Her later initiative should neither erase that arrangement
nor be erased by it. The blessing she requests is specified materially, and Caleb grants both
sets of springs without a narrated objection.

**Names and family.** Jephunneh and Kenaz remain patronymics, not independent actors requiring
new pages in this unit. Kenaz is not identified with the Edomite namesake. Arba, Anak,
Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai remain covered by the existing collective Anakim page. No Hebrew
etymology or settled kinship reconstruction is asserted. The springs are unnamed beyond upper
and lower; no modern identification is supplied. The South is linked under the existing Negev
alias without claiming a precise location for Achsah’s field.
