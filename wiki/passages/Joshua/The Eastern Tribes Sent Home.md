# The Eastern Tribes Sent Home

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 22:1–9

## Summary

Joshua summons Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh, commends their obedience to Moses and to
himself, and declares their service to their brothers complete. Yahweh has given the other
tribes rest, so the eastern warriors may return to their own possession. Their military
assignment ends; their duty to love, obey, cling to, and serve Yahweh does not.

Joshua blesses them and sends them home with livestock, metals, and clothing, commanding
them to share the plunder with their brothers. The narrator recalls Manasseh's inheritance
on both sides of the Jordan and locates the departure at Shiloh in Canaan, toward Gilead.

## Key verses

> **22:3** You have not left your brothers these many days to this day, but have performed the duty of the commandment of Yahweh your God.

> **22:4** Now Yahweh your God has given rest to your brothers, as he spoke to them. Therefore now return and go to your tents, to the land of your possession, which Moses the servant of Yahweh gave you beyond the Jordan.

> **22:5** Only take diligent heed to do the commandment and the law which Moses the servant of Yahweh commanded you, to love Yahweh your God, to walk in all his ways, to keep his commandments, to hold fast to him, and to serve him with all your heart and with all your soul.”

> **22:8** and spoke to them, saying, “Return with much wealth to your tents, with very much livestock, with silver, with gold, with brass, with iron, and with very much clothing. Divide the plunder of your enemies with your brothers.”

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh gives the brothers rest and remains the object of wholehearted service.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — commends, exhorts, blesses, and dismisses the eastern tribes.
- [[Moses]] — remembered giver of the eastern grant and mediator of the continuing command.
- [[Reuben]], [[Gad]], and [[Manasseh]] — their descendants are addressed, not the patriarchs.
- [[Jacob]] — the children of Israel are the collective descendants, not Jacob personally.

## Places

- [[Shiloh]] — point of departure.
- [[Canaan]] — the land containing Shiloh, distinguished here from the eastern destination.
- [[The Jordan]] — geographical divider of the grants.
- [[Bashan]] — Moses' inheritance for half of Manasseh.
- [[Gilead]] — the return destination named collectively in verse 9.

- [[Love]] — devotion expressed in walking, keeping, holding fast, and serving.
- [[Covenant]] — continuing allegiance after the campaign; no new covenant ceremony.
- [[Brotherhood]] — shared service followed by shared plunder.

## Notes

**Released, not detached.** Joshua's praise in verses 2–3 answers the possibility of abandoned
brothers raised in Numbers 32:6–15. The return is authorized because the condition of
Deuteronomy 3:20 and Joshua 1:15 has been met. The text says the service lasted many days;
it does not give its duration here. It does not repeat the troop count of Joshua 4:13.

**Wholehearted service continues.** Verse 5 gathers love, conduct, commandment keeping,
holding fast, and service with heart and soul into a single charge. Its close relationship
to Deuteronomy is visible in the stored WEB wording; no claim about exact Hebrew equivalence
is needed. Discharge from one assignment is compatible with a continuing call to obedience.

**One people, two sides.** Verse 7 explains the two halves of Manasseh by distinguishing
Moses' eastern gift from Joshua's western gift. Gilead in verse 9 names the return region
collectively; it should not erase Bashan's distinct mention in verse 7. Moses is recalled,
not present. Tribal names refer to descendants.

**Sharing the wealth.** Verse 8 lists livestock, silver, gold, brass, iron, and clothing.
It commands sharing with brothers but gives no fraction or named recipients. Reading this
as provision for those who remained at home is reasonable in the return setting, but is
an inference, not an explicitly identified group in this verse. No equal-share statute
should be supplied from another narrative.

**Rest and remaining land.** The declaration of rest in verse 4 follows 21:44 and authorizes
return; it does not erase the remaining land in 13:1 or remaining inhabitants in 16:10 and
17:12–13. The comprehensive completion language and incomplete occupation remain in tension.
The next unit, 22:10–34, narrates the altar dispute; the present praise does not already
accuse these tribes of rebellion. The planned boundary at verse 9 is retained.
