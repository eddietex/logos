# The Five Kings in the Cave at Makkedah

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 10:16–27

## Summary

The five kings escape into a cave at Makkedah. Joshua seals its entrance and posts guards,
but sends the rest of the army onward to prevent the fleeing enemy from reaching its cities.
A remnant nevertheless reaches fortified cities. Israel’s troops return in peace to the camp
at Makkedah, and no one speaks against them.

Joshua brings out the kings and has the commanders place their feet on their necks before
all Israel’s men. He makes their defeat a pledge of Yahweh’s future victories and urges courage.
He then kills the kings, hangs their bodies on five trees until evening, and orders them
removed at sunset. Their hiding place becomes their grave, sealed by stones still present
in the narrator’s day.

## Key verses

> **10:19** but don’t stay there. Pursue your enemies, and attack them from the rear. Don’t allow them to enter into their cities; for Yahweh your God has delivered them into your hand.”

> **10:24** When they brought those kings out to Joshua, Joshua called for all the men of Israel, and said to the chiefs of the men of war who went with him, “Come near. Put your feet on the necks of these kings.” They came near, and put their feet on their necks.

> **10:25** Joshua said to them, “Don’t be afraid, nor be dismayed. Be strong and courageous, for Yahweh will do this to all your enemies against whom you fight.”

> **10:26** Afterward Joshua struck them, put them to death, and hanged them on five trees. They were hanging on the trees until the evening.

> **10:27** At the time of the going down of the sun, Joshua commanded, and they took them down off the trees, and threw them into the cave in which they had hidden themselves, and laid great stones on the mouth of the cave, which remain to this very day.

## Notes

**Boundary:** The planned 10:16–27 unit remains intact. The capture of the city of Makkedah
begins at verse 28 and belongs to the next pericope. The five rulers are identified by city
in verse 23; their names are supplied by 10:3. The commanders and guards receive no separate
person pages because the text provides no individual identities.

**Survivors and victory:** Verse 19 orders that the enemy be kept out of its cities, while
verse 20 expressly records a surviving remnant entering fortified cities. Both statements
stand; the sweeping slaughter language cannot erase the qualification within the same verse.
Verse 21’s peaceful return and silence of opposition do not specify a casualty count.

**Execution and display:** The WEB says Joshua puts the kings to death before hanging them
(26). The passage supplies no precise execution method. Removal at sundown parallels
[[The Capture of Ai]] (Joshua 8:29) and the burial requirement in
[[Captives, Firstborn Sons, and Public Shame]] (Deuteronomy 21:22–23).
The cave is a punitive burial site; no honorable funeral is described.

**Courage passed on:** Joshua’s charge at 10:25 recalls the encouragement he receives in
[[After the Death of Moses]] (Joshua 1:6–9). The physical subjection of the kings is a public
demonstration of victory, interpreted by Joshua as grounds for the commanders’ future courage.
The text describes violent domination; it does not offer this action as a general practice
for readers to imitate.

**Sequence tension:** [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]] ends with a return to Gilgal (10:15).
Here operations resume at Makkedah, where verse 21 locates the camp; 10:43 again reports
the return to Gilgal. An anticipatory summary at verse 15 is a possible reading, not an
explicit explanation supplied by the narrator.
