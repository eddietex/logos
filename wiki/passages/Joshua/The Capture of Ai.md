# The Capture of Ai

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 8:1–29

## Summary

With the devoted thing removed at Achor, Yahweh speaks to Joshua again, and his first words are the
ones Moses spoke to Israel at Kadesh: *Don't be afraid, and don't be dismayed* (1). This time the
order is to take *all the warriors*, not the two or three thousand the spies had advised (7:3), and
the city is promised before the march: *I have given into your hand the king of Ai, with his people,
his city, and his land.* Ai is to be treated as Jericho was, with one change — *you shall take its
goods and its livestock for yourselves* (2). And Yahweh himself names the tactic: *Set an ambush for
the city behind it.*

Joshua's plan turns the earlier defeat into the bait (3–8). The ambush lies hidden west of Ai,
between Ai and Bethel. The main force approaches from the north, and when Ai's men come out, Israel
will flee *as at the first*, so that the defenders will say *They flee before us, like the first
time*. When the city is empty the ambush takes it and sets it on fire. The plan is carried out
(9–17): the king of Ai sees the army in the valley, comes out early *at the time appointed*, and
does not know there is an ambush behind the city. Israel *made as if they were beaten*; every man
of Ai, and of Bethel with them, goes out in pursuit and leaves the city open.

Then Yahweh tells Joshua to stretch out the javelin in his hand toward Ai (18). The ambush rises at
the signal, enters the city, and fires it. Ai's men look back, see the smoke going up to heaven, and
have nowhere to go; the fleeing army turns, the ambush comes out behind, and Ai's force is caught
between them (19–22). The king is taken alive. Israel kills the inhabitants in the field and then in
the city — twelve thousand, men and women, *all the men of Ai* (25) — and Joshua does not draw back
the hand that held out the javelin *until he had utterly destroyed all the inhabitants of Ai* (26).
Israel keeps the livestock and goods *according to Yahweh's word* (27). Joshua burns Ai and makes it
*a heap forever, even a desolation, to this day* (28). The king is hanged on a tree until evening,
taken down at sundown, thrown at the city gate, and covered with *a great heap of stones that
remains to this day* (29).

## Key verses

> **8:1** Yahweh said to Joshua, “Don’t be afraid, and don’t be dismayed. Take all the warriors with you, and arise, go up to Ai. Behold, I have given into your hand the king of Ai, with his people, his city, and his land.

> **8:2** You shall do to Ai and her king as you did to Jericho and her king, except you shall take its goods and its livestock for yourselves. Set an ambush for the city behind it.”

> **8:6** They will come out after us, until we have drawn them away from the city; for they will say, ‘They flee before us, like the first time.’ So we will flee before them,

> **8:17** There was not a man left in Ai or Beth El who didn’t go out after Israel. They left the city open, and pursued Israel.

> **8:18** Yahweh said to Joshua, “Stretch out the javelin that is in your hand toward Ai, for I will give it into your hand.” Joshua stretched out the javelin that was in his hand toward the city.

> **8:26** For Joshua didn’t draw back his hand, with which he stretched out the javelin, until he had utterly destroyed all the inhabitants of Ai.

> **8:27** Israel took for themselves only the livestock and the goods of that city, according to Yahweh’s word which he commanded Joshua.

> **8:28** So Joshua burned Ai, and made it a heap forever, even a desolation, to this day.

> **8:29** He hanged the king of Ai on a tree until the evening, and at sundown Joshua commanded, and they took his body down from the tree, and threw it at the entrance of the gate of the city, and raised a great heap of stones on it that remains to this day.

## Notes

**The word of Kadesh, spoken again.** Yahweh's opening — *ʾal-tîrāʾ wǝʾal-tēḥāt … ʿălēh … rǝʾēh*,
*don't be afraid, don't be dismayed … go up … see* (WLC Joshua 8:1) — uses the same three verbs, in
the same forms, as Moses' word to the first generation at Deuteronomy 1:21: *Behold, Yahweh your God
has set the land before you. Go up, take possession … Don't be afraid, neither be dismayed.* That
generation refused, and its first attempt to fight without Yahweh's presence ended in a rout
(Deuteronomy 1:41–44). The second generation has now lived its own small version of that story
at Ai, and is told to go up again with the same words. Joshua 1:9's charge used the second verb but
a different first one (*ʾal-taʿărōṣ*, WLC); the exact pairing here is Deuteronomy 1:21's, and
Joshua will repeat it to his own captains at 10:25.

**What changed between the two battles.** The text names three things. The presence: Yahweh, who
had said *I will not be with you any more* (7:12), now speaks first and promises the city. The
force: *all the warriors*, reversing the spies' advice that *all the people* need not go (7:3).
And the plan, which Yahweh gives in outline (*Set an ambush*, 2) and Joshua fills in. What is
absent is also notable: no spies are sent this time, no trumpets, no ark, no procession. Jericho
fell to a liturgy; Ai falls to a stratagem.

**The first defeat as bait.** The plan works because Ai remembers the rout. *As at the first* and
*like the first time* (5–6) are Joshua's words, and the defenders' confidence is exactly what he
predicts. The defeat of chapter 7 is not erased; it is used. See [[Deception]] for the place of
the ruse — a feint in battle, ordered by Yahweh — in a wiki that has so far mostly met deception as
sin; the text passes no judgment on it and gives no sign that it needs one.

**Thirty thousand or five thousand?** Verse 3 says Joshua chose *thirty thousand men … and sent
them out by night*; verse 12 says he *took about five thousand men, and set them in ambush between
Bethel and Ai*. The stored text gives both numbers and does not reconcile them. Readings offered
include two separate detachments (one ambush, one to block Bethel), a summary in 3–9 followed by a
detailed retelling in 10–13, or a textual problem in one number; the Greek (Septuagint)
text is reported to be shorter in this section, which this wiki has **not** verified. The
wiki records both figures without choosing. Twelve thousand dead (25) against the spies' *few*
(7:3) is a related tension, likewise left as the text has it. Several readers take *ʾelep*,
"thousand", in such lists as a military unit rather than a count; that proposal is noted, not
adopted.

**Bethel's men.** Verse 17 says *not a man left in Ai or Beth El* failed to join the pursuit. Bethel
is a mile or two west and is the western limit of the ambush's hiding place (9, 12). The text does
not say what became of Bethel itself; it reappears in the boundary lists (16:1–2; 18:13),
and its capture is narrated only in Judges 1:22–26 (WEB not checked in this pass; cited by
reference). See [[Bethel]].

**The javelin.** *Kîḏôn* (WLC 8:18, 26) is translated *javelin* by WEB; its exact form — a throwing
spear, a curved sword, or a battle standard — is debated, and the wiki does not settle it. Its
function is clear enough: it is a signal the ambush can see, and it is held out until the end. The
verb is *nāṭâ*, *stretch out*, the verb of Moses' hand over the sea at Exodus 14:16 and 14:26 (WLC).
Verse 26 adds what the plan in 3–8 never mentioned: Joshua does not *draw back* (*hēšîḇ*) his hand
until Ai is destroyed. The nearest earlier picture is Moses on the hill at Rephidim, whose raised
hand decided the battle that Joshua himself fought (Exodus 17:11–13). See
[[The Hand Held Out - Moses at Rephidim and Joshua at Ai]].

**Ḥērem with plunder.** Verse 26's *utterly destroyed* is *heḥĕrîm* (WLC), the verb of the devoted
thing, and verse 2 places Ai under the Jericho pattern. But the goods and livestock are given to
Israel. Deuteronomy 20:14 allowed plunder only from distant cities; for *the cities of these peoples*
20:16 says *you shall save alive nothing that breathes*, and says nothing explicit about their goods.
Deuteronomy 2:34–35 and 3:6–7 had already narrated the same combination east of the Jordan — people
destroyed, livestock and plunder kept. So Jericho, where even the metals went to Yahweh's treasury,
is the stricter case, and Ai is the ordinary one. What Achan took by theft at Jericho, Israel
receives by command at Ai — an irony many readers note and the text leaves implicit. See
[[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]].

**A heap forever.** Verse 28's *a heap forever* is *tēl ʿôlām* (WLC), the exact phrase of
Deuteronomy 13:16 (13:17 in WLC numbering) for an Israelite town that turns to other gods: *It shall
be a heap forever. It shall not be built again.* In the Hebrew books searched for this pass the word
*tēl* occurs only five times. The name Ai, *the ruin*, and what Joshua makes of it coincide here; see
[[Ai]] for the archaeological question this pericope does not settle, and
[[A Heap Forever - Deuteronomy 13.16, Joshua 8.28, and Jeremiah]].

**The king on the tree.** The king of Ai is the second king named in the conquest and the first
whose death is narrated (Jericho's king, 6:2, is not given a separate scene). He is hanged *until the
evening* and taken down *at sundown* — the timing Deuteronomy 21:22–23 requires — and the word for
his body, *niḇlātô* (WLC 8:29), is the *nǝḇēlâ* of 21:23. The text does not say how he died; read
with Deuteronomy 21:22, *hanged* means the display of a body, not the means of death. His body is
thrown at the gate — the place of the city's government — and covered with stones. See
[[Hanged on a Tree - Genesis 40, Deuteronomy 21, and Galatians 3]].

**Two heaps, one sentence.** The heap over the king is described in exactly the words of the heap
over Achan: *gal-ʾăḇānîm gāḏôl ʿaḏ hayyôm hazzeh*, *a great heap of stones … to this day* (WLC 7:26;
8:29). The sin within Israel and the enemy outside it end in the same monument, one chapter apart.
Together with the twelve stones at Gilgal (4:20) and the stones of the altar that follow at Ebal
(8:30–32), the book's first eight chapters set up four stone memorials.

**Total war, stated.** The text says *both of men and women* (25) and *all the inhabitants* (24,
26). It does not mention children explicitly here, as 6:21 had at Jericho, but does not exempt
them. As on [[The Fall of Jericho]] and [[War]], the wiki records the scope of the killing as the
text states it and does not soften it.

**Gibeah, later.** The ambush, the drawing away from the city, the defenders' *as at the first*, the
smoke as signal, and even the road to Bethel recur in Israel's war against Benjamin at Gibeah
(Judges 20:29–38, WEB verified at bible-api.com). There the stratagem is turned on a tribe of
Israel. See [[Drawn Away from the City - Ai and Gibeah]].
