# After the Death of Moses

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 1:1–9

## Summary

After the death of Moses, *the servant of Yahweh*, Yahweh speaks to Joshua son of Nun, *Moses'
servant*. Moses is dead; Joshua is to rise and cross the Jordan with all the people into the land
Yahweh is giving them. Every place the sole of their foot treads is given, *as I told Moses*, and
the border runs from the wilderness and Lebanon to the Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites, to
the great sea in the west. No one will stand before Joshua; as Yahweh was with Moses he will be
with Joshua, and will not fail or forsake him. Three times Joshua is told to be strong and
courageous: first because he will cause the people to inherit the sworn land, then — *only … very
courageous* — to keep all the law Moses commanded, turning neither right nor left, with *this book
of the law* never departing from his mouth but meditated day and night; and last, *Haven't I
commanded you?*, because Yahweh is with him wherever he goes.

## Key verses

Copied verbatim from stored WEB, raw/bible/Joshua.md.

> **1** Now after the death of Moses the servant of Yahweh, Yahweh spoke to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses’ servant, saying,

> **2** “Moses my servant is dead. Now therefore arise, go across this Jordan, you, and all these people, to the land which I am giving to them, even to the children of Israel.

> **3** I have given you every place that the sole of your foot will tread on, as I told Moses.

> **5** No man will be able to stand before you all the days of your life. As I was with Moses, so I will be with you. I will not fail you nor forsake you.

> **7** Only be strong and very courageous. Be careful to observe to do according to all the law, which Moses my servant commanded you. Don’t turn from it to the right hand or to the left, that you may have good success wherever you go.

> **8** This book of the law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate on it day and night, that you may observe to do according to all that is written in it; for then you shall make your way prosperous, and then you shall have good success.

> **9** Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be dismayed, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go.”

## Notes

**Two servants.** Verse 1 calls Moses *ʿebed YHWH*, *the servant of Yahweh*, the title of
Deuteronomy 34:5, and Joshua *məšārēt mōšeh*, *Moses' servant* (MAM via Sefaria). The two
English words render different Hebrew. *Məšārēt* is the attendant's word used of Joshua at
Exodus 24:13, Exodus 33:11 and Numbers 11:28 (MAM via Sefaria, checked for those three verses).
Yahweh then says *Moses my servant* (*mōšeh ʿabdî*) twice, at verses 2 and 7. The book begins by
putting the two men in their old relation before it moves the office: Joshua is introduced as
Moses' attendant, and Moses keeps the title. In WEB Joshua is first called *the servant of
Yahweh* only at his own death (24:29, stored WEB; the Hebrew of that verse was not checked).

**The first word is about a death.** *Moses my servant is dead. Now therefore arise* (2). There is
no interval in the text between the thirty days of mourning (Deuteronomy 34:8) and this speech,
and no theophany is described: *Yahweh spoke to Joshua*, as he had spoken to Moses. Nothing in the
passage says how Joshua received it.

**The land promised in Deuteronomy's words.** Verses 3–5 repeat Deuteronomy 11:24–25 nearly clause
for clause: *every place … the sole of your foot*, *from the wilderness, and Lebanon*, *the river,
the river Euphrates*, the western sea, and *No man will be able to stand before you*. Joshua adds
*all the land of the Hittites* and *as I told Moses*, and turns *shall be yours* into *I have
given you* (both stored WEB). Deuteronomy made the promise conditional on keeping the commands
(11:22); Joshua 1 states the gift and puts the obedience three verses later, so the condition has
moved, not vanished. The *Hittites* (*haḥittîm*, MAM via Sefaria) are the people Genesis calls
the children of Heth; the wiki files them on [[The Children of Heth]] without deciding how
this phrase relates to them historically. See [[From the River of Egypt to the Euphrates]].

**The presence promised in Moses' words.** *I will not fail you nor forsake you* (5) is *lōʾ
ʾarpəkā wəlōʾ ʾeʿezbekkā*; in Deuteronomy 31:6, 8 Moses said *lōʾ yarpəkā wəlōʾ yaʿazbekkā* (MAM via
Sefaria). The verbs match; the person shifts from Moses' third-person assurance to Yahweh's first
person. *As I was with Moses, so I will be with you* makes the predecessor the measure of the
promise. The people echo it at 1:17 (*Only may Yahweh your God be with you, as he was with Moses*;
stored WEB, next pericope) — there it is a wish with a condition attached, not yet a fact. See
[[I Will Not Forsake You - Deuteronomy 31, Joshua 1, and Hebrews 13]] and
[[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]].

**Three charges to courage, one aimed at the book.** *Ḥăzaq weʾĕmāṣ*, *be strong and courageous*,
comes at verses 6, 7 and 9, and again from the people at 18 (MAM via Sefaria). Moses used the
same formula over Israel and Joshua in Deuteronomy 31:6–7, and it is spoken to Joshua again at
31:23 (stored WEB; the plural *ḥizqû wəʾimṣû* at 31:6, MAM via Sefaria). The middle charge is the odd one. It is introduced
by *raq*, *only*, intensified by *məʾōd*, *very*, and its object is not the enemy but the law:
*to observe to do according to all the law … Don't turn from it to the right hand or to the
left*. The phrase *right hand or … left* is Deuteronomy's — Israel at 5:32, the king at 17:20
(stored WEB). The passage makes keeping the law the act that needs the most courage, more than
the war ahead. It does not say why, and the wiki does not supply a reason.

**The book of the law.** *This book of the law* (*sēper hattôrâ hazzeh*, MAM via Sefaria) is the
written document Deuteronomy 31:24–26 had Moses finish and lay beside the ark; the demonstrative
*this* assumes a known text. Joshua is not told to make a copy, as the king of Deuteronomy 17:18
is, but to keep it in his mouth. *Wəhāgîtā bô yômām wālaylâ* — *you shall meditate on it day and
night* — uses *hāgâ*, a verb of low audible speech or murmuring as much as of thought; the
*mouth* earlier in the verse fits that. Psalm 1:2 has the same verb and the same *yômām
wālāylâ* (*yehgeh*, MAM via Sefaria; WEB *On his law he meditates day and night*, verified at
bible-api.com). Neither text cites the other, and the wiki makes no claim about which is earlier.
See [[Day and Night - Joshua 1.8, Deuteronomy 17.18-20, and Psalm 1.2]] and
[[The Two Ways - Proverbs 4, Psalm 1, and Matthew 7]].

**Success.** WEB's *good success* renders *taśkîl* in 7 and 8, a verb otherwise used for acting
wisely or with insight; *make your way prosperous* renders *taṣlîaḥ* (MAM via Sefaria). The
promised success is a consequence of the meditation, not a reward stated separately from it:
*for then* (*kî-ʾāz*). Because *śkl* is the language of insight, the passage links the leader's
competence to wisdom, and the wiki files it under [[Wisdom]]; the verse does not use
*ḥokmâ*, which Deuteronomy 34:9 had already ascribed to him.

**What is not here.** Unlike Moses at the bush, Joshua raises no objection, and none is answered;
the reassurance in verse 9 (*Don't be afraid. Don't be dismayed*) is unprompted. Nothing is said
about Joshua's feelings. Nor does the passage mention the ark, the priests, Eleazar or the Urim of
Numbers 27:21: the divine word comes to Joshua directly, at least here. How that relates to the
priestly inquiry Numbers 27 promised is not addressed.

**The name.** Joshua's name (*Yəhôšuaʿ*) is the Hebrew form behind Greek *Iēsous*; see
[[Joshua and Jesus - One Name]]. This passage makes nothing of the name.
