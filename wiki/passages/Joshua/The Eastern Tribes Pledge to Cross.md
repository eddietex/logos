# The Eastern Tribes Pledge to Cross

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 1:10–18

## Summary

Joshua's first act after Yahweh's charge is to pass a command down: the officers go through the
camp and tell the people to prepare provisions, because within three days they will cross the
Jordan to possess the land Yahweh is giving them (10–11). Then he turns to the Reubenites, the
Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh, whose land east of the Jordan Moses had already given
them, and reminds them of *the word which Moses the servant of Yahweh commanded you*: their wives,
children and livestock stay on the east bank, while all their fighting men cross armed ahead of
their brothers and help them, until Yahweh gives the other tribes rest as he has given it to
them; only then may they go home (12–15). They answer with an unconditional pledge — *All that you
have commanded us we will do, and wherever you send us we will go* — promise to listen to Joshua
as they listened to Moses, add a wish that Yahweh be with him as he was with Moses, set death as
the penalty for anyone who rebels against his word, and close with the charge Yahweh had just
given him: *Only be strong and courageous* (16–18).

## Notes

**The command goes down the chain.** Yahweh commanded Joshua (9, *Haven't I commanded you?*);
Joshua now commands the *officers* (*šōṭərê hāʿām*, MAM via Sefaria), who are to command the
people. *Šōṭərîm* is the word for the Israelite foremen beaten under Pharaoh's taskmasters
(Exodus 5:14), for the tribal officers Moses appointed beside the captains of thousands
(Deuteronomy 1:15), and for the officials who address the army before battle (Deuteronomy 20:5)
— all three checked in MAM via Sefaria. In Joshua 3:2 they go through the camp again, this time
*after three days* (stored WEB). The wiki has no page for the office; it is noted here.

**Three days, and food.** *Prepare food* (*hākînû lākem ṣêdâ*, MAM via Sefaria) — *ṣêdâ* is
travelling provisions. Two things in the surrounding chapters sit awkwardly with the verse, and
the text does not resolve either. The manna has not yet stopped; it ceases only after Israel eats
the produce of the land at Gilgal (5:12, stored WEB), so the provisions are not said to replace
it. And *within three days* (11) is followed by the spies' mission in chapter 2, in which the men
alone hide *three days* in the hills before returning (2:22), and then by the officers' *after
three days* (3:2). The chronology of chapters 1–3 does not add up cleanly as written; whether the
spies were sent before verse 10 or the *three days* is a stock interval is a question commentators
answer differently, and the wiki records the tension rather than choosing.

**Joshua quotes Deuteronomy, not Numbers.** The tribes are told to *remember the word which Moses
… commanded you* (13), and what follows reproduces Deuteronomy 3:18–20 rather than the
negotiation of Numbers 32. Verse 15's *until Yahweh has given your brothers rest, as he has given
you, and they have also possessed the land* is *ʿad ʾăšer-yānîaḥ YHWH laʾăḥêkem kākem wəyārəšû
gam-hēmmâ* — the same clause as Deuteronomy 3:20 (*gam-hēm*), word for word except for the longer
pronoun (MAM via Sefaria, both verses). *Give you … this land* (13) is Deuteronomy 3:18's *nātan
lākem ʾet-hāʾāreṣ hazzōʾt*. Joshua alters three things. He opens with rest — *Yahweh your God
gives you rest* (*mēnîaḥ lākem*, a participle; MAM via Sefaria) — where Deuteronomy 3:18 opened
with the gift of the land. His word for *armed* is *ḥămušîm*, not Deuteronomy's *ḥălûṣîm* (3:18)
or Numbers 32:17's *ḥušîm*; *ḥămušîm* is the word used of Israel leaving Egypt in Exodus 13:18
(*the children of Israel went up armed*, stored WEB; all four verses MAM via Sefaria). And he adds
*and shall help them* (*waʿăzartem*), which neither earlier text has. The meaning of *ḥămušîm*
(*armed*, or *in battle array*, sometimes connected with *five*) is disputed; the wiki gives WEB's
rendering and does not decide.

**Rest before the land.** The order of verse 13 is striking: the eastern tribes already have
*rest*, and the rest of Israel does not yet. Deuteronomy 12:9 had told all Israel *you haven't yet
come to the rest and to the inheritance* (stored WEB; *hammənûḥâ*, MAM via Sefaria). For two and a
half tribes the rest has come early, east of the river and outside the land Numbers 34 calls
Canaan; their obligation is to stay out of it until their brothers share it. Joshua 21:44, 22:4
and 23:1 (stored WEB; *nwḥ* in all three, MAM via Sefaria) report that it came. See
[[Rest from All Your Enemies - Deuteronomy 12, Joshua 1, 21-23, and Hebrews 4]].

**Brothers.** *Your brothers* comes three times in two verses (14–15). The arrangement exists to
answer Moses' question at Numbers 32:6 — *Shall your brothers go to the war, and shall you sit
here?* (stored WEB) — and Joshua's wording keeps the families, the livestock and the land on one
side of the river and the obligation to brothers on the other. See [[Brotherhood]] and
[[The Two and a Half Tribes - Numbers 32, Deuteronomy 3, and Joshua 1, 4, 22]].

**Who answers.** *They answered Joshua* (16). The nearest antecedent is the two and a half tribes
of verse 12, and the reply fits them; but *Just as we listened to Moses in all things, so will we
listen to you* sounds like all Israel, and some readers take it that way. The text does not name
the speakers. The wiki reads it as the eastern tribes' reply, the natural syntax, and notes the
alternative.

**What the reply promises, and what it leaves out.** *All that you have commanded us we will do*
(*kōl ʾăšer-ṣiwwîtānû naʿăśeh*) is close to Israel's word at Sinai, *All that Yahweh has spoken
we will do* (Exodus 19:8, stored WEB; *kōl ʾăšer-dibber YHWH naʿăśeh*, MAM via Sefaria). The
verb and the construction match; the object has changed from what Yahweh spoke to what Joshua
commanded. The comparison is the wiki's; the text does not draw it. *In all things* (17) is a large claim for a people whose record with Moses
Deuteronomy has just spent chapters 1–9 recounting; the passage makes no comment on it, and
neither does the wiki beyond noting that the record exists. Then a condition: *Only may Yahweh
your God be with you, as he was with Moses* (*raq*, *only*, the word that introduced Yahweh's
middle charge at 1:7). It is the presence Yahweh had promised Joshua at 1:5, repeated by the people
as a wish. Whether it is a blessing or the limit of their loyalty, the text does not say.

**Rebellion against his mouth.** *Whoever rebels against your commandment* is literally *every man
who rebels against your mouth* (*yamreh ʾet-pîkā*, MAM via Sefaria). Deuteronomy uses the same
verb and object of Israel at Kadesh: *you rebelled against the commandment of Yahweh your God*
(1:26, 1:43, 9:23, stored WEB; *wattamrû ʾet-pî YHWH*, MAM via Sefaria, all three). The people
now set a death penalty on the act their parents committed against Yahweh. Deuteronomy 17:12 had
ordered death for one who acts presumptuously and does not listen to the priest or judge (stored
WEB); Joshua 1:18 extends the sanction to Joshua's own word, and it is the people, not Yahweh, who
pronounce it. No case under it is narrated here; Achan in chapter 7 is judged on other grounds.

**The courage formula returned.** The speech ends *raq ḥăzaq weʾĕmāṣ*, *Only be strong and
courageous* (18, MAM via Sefaria) — the fourth use in the chapter, the first from human lips in
Joshua, and the same singular imperative Yahweh used at 1:6, 7 and 9. Moses' encouragement had
gone to Joshua through Yahweh (Deuteronomy 3:28, 31:7, 31:23); here it comes back from the people
he leads. See [[After the Death of Moses]] and
[[I Will Not Forsake You - Deuteronomy 31, Joshua 1, and Hebrews 13]].

**What follows.** The tribes cross armed in 4:12–13 (*about forty thousand men*, stored WEB) and
are sent home with commendation in 22:1–9. The pledge is kept.
