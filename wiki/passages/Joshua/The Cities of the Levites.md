# The Cities of the Levites

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 21:1–42

## Summary

At Shiloh the Levite family heads approach Eleazar, Joshua, and Israel’s tribal heads to
claim the cities and livestock land commanded through Moses. Israel supplies these from its
inheritances, and lots distribute them among the Levitical families (1–8).

Aaron’s priestly descendants receive thirteen cities from Judah, Simeon, and Benjamin.
The remaining Kohathites receive ten from Ephraim, Dan, and half-Manasseh; Gershon’s descendants
receive thirteen from Issachar, Asher, Naphtali, and Manasseh in Bashan; Merari’s descendants
receive twelve from Zebulun, Reuben, and Gad. All forty-eight include suburbs (9–42).

Hebron’s allocation expressly preserves Caleb’s fields and villages (12). Provision for the
Levites therefore refines the earlier inheritance register rather than simply replacing it.
The six refuge cities belong within this network, connecting settlement with protection.

## Key verses

> **21:2** They spoke to them at Shiloh in the land of Canaan, saying, “Yahweh commanded through Moses to give us cities to dwell in, with their suburbs for our livestock.”

> **21:3** The children of Israel gave to the Levites out of their inheritance, according to the commandment of Yahweh, these cities with their suburbs.

> **21:12** But they gave the fields of the city and its villages to Caleb the son of Jephunneh for his possession.

> **21:41** All the cities of the Levites among the possessions of the children of Israel were forty-eight cities with their suburbs.

> **21:42** Each of these cities included their suburbs around them. It was this way with all these cities.

## Themes
- [[Priesthood]] — The list distinguishes Aaron’s thirteen priestly cities from the remaining Kohathites’ ten, Gershon’s thirteen, and Merari’s twelve (4–7, 19–40). Not every Levite is thereby called an Aaronic priest.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Forty-eight cities and their suburbs belong to Levi within the other tribes’ possessions (41–42). No separate tribal territory does not mean no homes or land; Caleb’s fields and villages are expressly retained at Hebron (12).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The Levite heads invoke the command through Moses, and Israel gives cities accordingly (2–3, 8). The named distribution implements the earlier command of Numbers 35:1–8.
- [[City and Civilization]] — The cities include surrounding land for livestock (2, 42). This is settlement provision, not a register of new conquests.
- [[Compassion and Mercy]] — The allocation includes the six refuges from Joshua 20: Hebron, Shechem, Golan, Kedesh, Bezer, and Ramoth. This chapter does not repeat the admission and trial procedures.
- [[Brotherhood]] — Israel supplies Levitical households from its own inheritances (3, 8); the tribal grants make room for those without a separate territorial allotment.

## Notes

- **Boundary retained.** Verses 1–42 form the petition and city register. The land-and-rest
  summary at 43–45 remains the next pericope.
- **Command and provision.** Numbers 35:1–8 supplies the earlier command; the repeated appeal
  to Moses frames the list as obedience. No exact Hebrew or Greek wording is claimed here.
- **Property and priesthood.** No independent tribal territory does not mean no property.
  The forty-eight allocations also do not make all Levites Aaronic priests.
- **Refuge labels.** Hebron, Shechem, Golan, Kedesh, and Ramoth are expressly called refuges
  here; Bezer is included without repeating the label. Joshua 20:8 supplies its refuge status.
- **Names and uncertainty.** Eshtemoa/Eshtemoh, Juttah/Jutah, Elteke/Eltekeh, and
  En Gannim/Engannim are treated as probable spelling correspondences, not express equations.
  Jarmuth in Issachar is kept apart from Judah’s Jarmuth. No equation of Be Eshterah with
  Ashtaroth, Hammothdor with Hammath, or Kartan with Kartah is assumed.
- **Repeated Gath Rimmon.** WEB names it under both Dan (24) and half-Manasseh (25).
  Both allocations are preserved on the existing page without deciding whether they concern
  two cities, overlapping rights, or a textual problem. The family totals sum to forty-eight
  allocations; the repeated name is not silently replaced to produce forty-eight unique names.
- **Grant and occupation.** Gezer and Taanach retain the non-removal notices of Joshua 16:10
  and 17:11–13. A Levitical allocation is not a new conquest report. Daberath’s position on
  Zebulun’s boundary (19:12) remains alongside its contribution from Issachar (21:28).
- **Sources.** Quotations copied verbatim from stored WEB Joshua 21. Comparisons use stored
  WEB Numbers 35 and Joshua 14, 16–17, 19–20. No unstored-book quotation is introduced.
