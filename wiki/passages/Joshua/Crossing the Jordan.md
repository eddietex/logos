# Crossing the Jordan

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 3:1–17

## Summary

Joshua leads Israel from Shittim to the Jordan. After three days, the officers tell the people
to follow the covenant ark at a distance of about two thousand cubits, so they can see the way
they have never traveled. Joshua commands sanctification in preparation for Yahweh’s wonders,
and the priests take the ark ahead.

Yahweh promises to establish Joshua publicly as Moses’ successor. Joshua announces that the
crossing will demonstrate the living God’s presence and his future expulsion of seven peoples.
Twelve men, one per tribe, are to be selected, though their task is not explained here.
At harvest, with the river overflowing, the priests step into its edge. Upstream waters stand
at Adam beside Zarethan; the downstream flow toward the Salt Sea is cut off. The priests remain
on dry ground in the river while the entire nation crosses near Jericho.

## Notes

**Knowledge through the event.** Two assurances interpret the crossing before it happens:
Israel will recognize Joshua’s God-given authority (7), and know the living God is among them
(10). The passage connects those claims without identifying the leader with God. Driving out
the named peoples remains future; the river crossing does not itself accomplish the conquest.

**The ark leads, the priests stand.** The ark’s repeated covenant designation binds the action
to Israel’s relationship with Yahweh. The people follow at a prescribed distance so they can
know the route (4); the text itself supplies that practical reason. The priests enter before
the water stops, then hold their place while the nation passes. No named high priest appears,
and no account of the priests’ emotions is given. The command to sanctify themselves (5) supplies
no particular purification procedure.

**Not a second account of identical water behavior.** The river is overflowing at harvest
(15), and the interruption is placed upstream at a named city (16). Exodus 14:21–22 instead
describes the wind and walls of water on either side. Joshua gives neither a natural mechanism
nor a modern location for Adam or Zarethan. A proposed landslide would be an external explanation,
not something stated here. The account attributes the promised wonder to Yahweh.

**Chronology left open.** The three days here (2), the spies’ three days hiding in Joshua 2:22,
and the within-three-days command of Joshua 1:11 do not come with a fully explained synchronized
timeline. Sequential and overlapping readings should not be silently substituted for the
narrative’s notices. This retains the tension already noted on the preceding passage pages.

**The next unit is prepared, not ingested here.** The twelve men in verse 12 are selected without
an assigned task in this chapter. Joshua 4:2–8 will explain their work with stones. Verse 17 ends
with the nation across and the priests still in the river; the ark’s departure and returning
water are also narrated in chapter 4. The existing pericope boundary is retained.
