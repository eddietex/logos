# The Land Surveyed at Shiloh

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 18:1–10

## Summary

The whole congregation assembles at Shiloh and sets up the Tent of Meeting. Seven tribes still lack
a divided inheritance. Joshua challenges their neglect and orders three men from each tribe to walk
through the land, describe it, and return. The existing southern territory of Judah, northern
territory of Joseph, and eastern grants remain outside this seven-part division; the Levites have a
different inheritance.

The men record the land by cities in seven portions in a book and return to Joshua at Shiloh. He
casts lots there before Yahweh and distributes the land. The unit moves from delay to a completed
survey and allocation; Benjamin’s particular boundaries begin the next pericope.

## Key verses

Full pericope, WEB, copied from the stored source:

> **1** The whole congregation of the children of Israel assembled themselves together at Shiloh, and set up the Tent of Meeting there. The land was subdued before them.

> **2** Seven tribes remained among the children of Israel, which had not yet divided their inheritance.

> **3** Joshua said to the children of Israel, “How long will you neglect to go in to possess the land, which Yahweh, the God of your fathers, has given you?

> **4** Appoint for yourselves three men from each tribe. I will send them, and they shall arise, walk through the land, and describe it according to their inheritance; and they shall come to me.

> **5** They shall divide it into seven portions. Judah shall live in his borders on the south, and the house of Joseph shall live in their borders on the north.

> **6** You shall survey the land into seven parts, and bring the description here to me; and I will cast lots for you here before Yahweh our God.

> **7** However, the Levites have no portion among you; for the priesthood of Yahweh is their inheritance. Gad, Reuben, and the half-tribe of Manasseh have received their inheritance east of the Jordan, which Moses the servant of Yahweh gave them.”

> **8** The men arose and went. Joshua commanded those who went to survey the land, saying, “Go walk through the land, survey it, and come again to me. I will cast lots for you here before Yahweh in Shiloh.”

> **9** The men went and passed through the land, and surveyed it by cities into seven portions in a book. They came to Joshua to the camp at Shiloh.

> **10** Joshua cast lots for them in Shiloh before Yahweh. There Joshua divided the land to the children of Israel according to their divisions.

## People
- [[God]] — Joshua identifies Yahweh as the giver of the land and casts the lots before him (3, 6, 8, 10). No new divine speech or visible manifestation is narrated.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — He rebukes delayed possession, commissions the survey, receives its written results, and casts the lots at Shiloh (3–10). Leadership joins exhortation, practical administration, and allocation before Yahweh.
- [[Moses]] — His grants east of the Jordan remain settled and are excluded from the new division (7). He is recalled as Yahweh’s servant, not present at Shiloh.
- [[Judah]] — The descendant tribe retains its southern borders while the remaining land is surveyed (5). The patriarch is not personally present, and his allotment is not reopened.
- [[Joseph]] — His house retains its northern borders (5). This is the collective territory of his descendants, not the patriarch’s personal residence.
- [[Ephraim]] — Included within the house of Joseph whose northern borders remain in place (5; compare 17:17). Ephraim is not separately named in this unit.
- [[Manasseh]] — The eastern half-tribe retains Moses’ grant (7); the house of Joseph retains its northern borders (5). The corporate descendants, not the patriarch, are meant.
- [[Gad]] — The descendant tribe already has its inheritance east of the Jordan through Moses and receives no new western lot here (7).
- [[Reuben]] — The descendant tribe’s eastern inheritance through Moses is confirmed as already received (7), outside the seven new portions.
- [[Levi]] — The Levites have no portion among the seven because Yahweh’s priesthood is their inheritance (7). This collective description does not explain the internal priest/Levite distinctions of Numbers 18:1–7; Joshua 14:4 already allows cities and surrounding land.

## Places
- [[Shiloh]] — The assembly, Tent, surveyors’ return, and casting of lots are located here (1, 8–10).
- [[The Jordan]] — The river distinguishes the eastern grants to Gad, Reuben, and half of Manasseh from the remaining western division (7). It functions as a geographic division, not as the scene of a crossing.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The gift of the land requires action: seven tribes have not divided their inheritance, and Joshua rebukes their delay in possessing it (2–3). A written city survey precedes allocation by lot (9–10); the already allotted shares remain in place (5, 7).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Joshua challenges neglect of the land already given by Yahweh (3). The surveyors obey his commission and return with their record (8–9); Moses’ eastern grants are honored rather than redistributed (7).
- [[Priesthood]] — Yahweh’s priesthood is called the Levites’ inheritance instead of a territorial portion (7). The broad tribal wording stands beside Numbers 18:1–7’s restricted altar priesthood; it does not explicitly erase that distinction or the cities allowed in Joshua 14:4.
- [[Providence]] — Joshua casts lots before Yahweh only after the men have surveyed the land by cities and recorded seven portions (6–10). Divine allocation and human preparation work together; the physical lot mechanism remains unspecified.
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — The whole congregation establishes the Tent of Meeting at Shiloh (1), where Joshua casts lots before Yahweh (10). The text narrates a sanctuary location without describing a permanent stone temple or a new glory-cloud manifestation.
- [[War]] — The narrator says the land was subdued (1), yet Joshua immediately rebukes the seven tribes for failing to possess it (2–3). This preserves the tension of the conquest summaries with unfinished occupation; political or military subjection and completed settlement need not be identical.

## Notes

**Subdued, yet unpossessed.** Verse 1’s statement that the land was subdued stands beside
Joshua’s rebuke in verse 3, the remaining-land report of 13:1–7, and the non-removal in
17:12–13. These are not interchangeable claims. Regional subjection followed by incomplete
local possession is a possible reading; the narrative itself leaves the statements together.
The survey and lot do not prove that all inhabitants have been expelled.

**A written survey.** Verse 9 explicitly describes a record by cities in a book. It does not
describe a drawn map, equal acreage, or surveying instruments. Three men from each tribe are
requested (4), but the passage does not give their names or an explicit total. They remain an
unnamed commission here rather than receiving invented individual identities.

**Seven portions.** Judah and Joseph retain their borders; the eastern two-and-a-half tribes
already possess grants; the Levites’ inheritance is priestly service (5–7). Tribal names denote
descendants, not patriarchs attending the assembly. The priesthood description has a scope
tension with Numbers 18:1–7’s distinction between Aaron’s priesthood and other Levites’ service.
It should not silently make every Levite an altar priest, or negate Joshua 14:4’s cities.

**Shiloh.** The Tent of Meeting is established here, not a new stone temple. No modern site,
date of arrival, or identification with Taanath Shiloh is supplied by these verses.
