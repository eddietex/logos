# The Inheritance of Benjamin

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 18:11–28

## Summary

Benjamin’s lot falls between Judah and Joseph. The text traces its north, west, south, and
east borders, then lists twelve cities and fourteen cities with their villages. Family
inheritance frames both the boundary circuit and the city register. This is the first
particular lot after the survey at Shiloh, not a new campaign or a report that every city
has been occupied. The Jordan closes the eastern boundary.

## Key verses

Full pericope, WEB, copied from the stored source:

> **11** The lot of the tribe of the children of Benjamin came up according to their families. The border of their lot went out between the children of Judah and the children of Joseph.

> **12** Their border on the north quarter was from the Jordan. The border went up to the side of Jericho on the north, and went up through the hill country westward. It ended at the wilderness of Beth Aven.

> **13** The border passed along from there to Luz, to the side of Luz (also called Bethel), southward. The border went down to Ataroth Addar, by the mountain that lies on the south of Beth Horon the lower.

> **14** The border extended, and turned around on the west quarter southward, from the mountain that lies before Beth Horon southward; and ended at Kiriath Baal (also called Kiriath Jearim), a city of the children of Judah. This was the west quarter.

> **15** The south quarter was from the farthest part of Kiriath Jearim. The border went out westward, and went out to the spring of the waters of Nephtoah.

> **16** The border went down to the farthest part of the mountain that lies before the valley of the son of Hinnom, which is in the valley of Rephaim northward. It went down to the valley of Hinnom, to the side of the Jebusite southward, and went down to En Rogel.

> **17** It extended northward, went out at En Shemesh, and went out to Geliloth, which is over against the ascent of Adummim. It went down to the stone of Bohan the son of Reuben.

> **18** It passed along to the side over against the Arabah northward, and went down to the Arabah.

> **19** The border passed along to the side of Beth Hoglah northward; and the border ended at the north bay of the Salt Sea, at the south end of the Jordan. This was the south border.

> **20** The Jordan was its border on the east quarter. This was the inheritance of the children of Benjamin, by the borders around it, according to their families.

> **21** Now the cities of the tribe of the children of Benjamin according to their families were Jericho, Beth Hoglah, Emek Keziz,

> **22** Beth Arabah, Zemaraim, Bethel,

> **23** Avvim, Parah, Ophrah,

> **24** Chephar Ammoni, Ophni, and Geba; twelve cities with their villages.

> **25** Gibeon, Ramah, Beeroth,

> **26** Mizpeh, Chephirah, Mozah,

> **27** Rekem, Irpeel, Taralah,

> **28** Zelah, Eleph, the Jebusite (also called Jerusalem), Gibeath, and Kiriath; fourteen cities with their villages. This is the inheritance of the children of Benjamin according to their families.

## People
- [[Benjamin]] — The descendant tribe receives a lot by families between Judah and Joseph (11), with a boundary circuit and two city groups (12–28). The patriarch is not personally present.
- [[Judah]] — The descendant tribe borders Benjamin (11); Kiriath Jearim remains explicitly Judah’s city (14). Beth Arabah’s inclusion in both tribal lists (15:61; 18:22) remains an unresolved overlap.
- [[Joseph]] — His descendant tribes form the neighboring share on Benjamin’s other side from Judah (11); no action of the patriarch is narrated.
- [[Bohan]] — His name and relation to Reuben recur in the boundary stone (17); no new biography or account of its erection is supplied.
- [[Reuben]] — Named as Bohan’s father in the boundary stone (17). This geographical notice does not narrate an action by the patriarch or fix Bohan’s generation.

## Places
- [[The Jordan]] — The northern boundary begins at the Jordan (12); the river forms the entire eastern side (20). Its southern end meets the Salt Sea at the end of the south border (19).
- [[Jericho]] — The northern boundary passes its north side (12), and it heads the first group of twelve cities (21–24). A city listed as inheritance does not itself imply rebuilding after Joshua 6.
- [[Beth Aven]] — The northern boundary ends this stage at the wilderness of Beth Aven (12). The passage names a wilderness bearing, not a new occupation or an equation with Bethel.
- [[Bethel]] — Luz is explicitly identified as Bethel on the north boundary (13), and Bethel is listed among the twelve cities (22–24). Joshua 16:2 describes movement from Bethel to Luz: that wording remains in tension with their explicit identification here; the text does not explain it.
- [[Ataroth Addar]] — The north boundary descends here by the mountain south of lower Beth Horon (13). Joshua 16:5 names it with upper Beth Horon; these are related boundary descriptions, not an equation of the two Beth Horons.
- [[Beth Horon]] — The mountain south of lower Beth Horon locates Ataroth Addar (13) and the turn of the western border (14). Upper and lower Beth Horon remain distinguished.
- [[Kiriath Jearim]] — Kiriath Baal is explicitly Kiriath Jearim, a city of Judah, at the end of Benjamin’s west border (14). The south border starts at its farthest part (15). Kiriath in verse 28 is not explicitly identified with it.
- [[Nephtoah]] — The spring of its waters is reached from Kiriath Jearim on the south border (15), corresponding to Judah’s north boundary in Joshua 15:9.
- [[The Valley of Hinnom]] — The south border descends by the mountain before this valley, past Jerusalem’s south side to En Rogel (16). The place-name supplies no independent biography of Hinnom or his son.
- [[The Valley of Rephaim]] — The mountain before Hinnom is located at this valley’s northern part (16); the name does not report an encounter with the Rephaim.
- [[Jerusalem]] — The south border runs along the Jebusite’s south side (16); the city list explicitly identifies the Jebusite as Jerusalem (28). Its assignment to Benjamin stands beside Judah’s inability to expel its inhabitants in Joshua 15:63; allocation is not a report of occupation.
- [[En Rogel]] — The boundary descends here from the south side of the Jebusite location (16), before turning northward toward En Shemesh (17).
- [[En Shemesh]] — The south boundary reaches this place before Geliloth (17), reversing the direction of the shared En Shemesh–En Rogel sequence of Joshua 15:7.
- [[Geliloth]] — A southern boundary landmark opposite the ascent of Adummim, before the descent to Bohan’s stone (17). Joshua 15:7 names Gilgal opposite the same ascent; correspondence does not establish identity, so the names remain separate.
- [[The Ascent of Adummim]] — Geliloth stands opposite this ascent (17). Joshua 15:7 places Gilgal opposite it; the two landmark names are not silently equated.
- [[The Stone of Bohan]] — The boundary descends to the stone of Bohan son of Reuben (17), reusing the landmark of Judah’s north border in Joshua 15:6. No erection story is given.
- [[Arabah]] — The south border passes the side facing the Arabah northward and descends to it (18), before passing north of Beth Hoglah (19).
- [[Beth Hoglah]] — The south boundary passes its northern side (19); it is also included in Benjamin’s first city group (21).
- [[The Salt Sea]] — The northern bay at the southern end of the Jordan terminates Benjamin’s south border (19). The river, rather than the sea, is the east border (20).
- [[Beth Arabah]] — Listed among Benjamin’s first twelve cities (22–24), although Joshua 15:61 also lists it among Judah’s wilderness towns. This overlapping assignment is a textual tension; no transfer or distinct second town is asserted.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Inheritance is defined by family, borders, cities, and villages (11–28). The list does not establish occupation. Beth Arabah’s inclusion in both Judah’s and Benjamin’s lists (15:61; 18:22) remains an explicit unresolved overlap.
- [[Names and Naming]] — The text explicitly pairs Luz with Bethel, Kiriath Baal with Kiriath Jearim, and the Jebusite with Jerusalem (13–14, 28). It does not similarly equate Geliloth with Gilgal or Kiriath with Kiriath Jearim.
- [[Providence]] — Benjamin’s lot comes up between Judah and Joseph (11), giving a concrete result of the lots cast before Yahweh in 18:10. The passage does not describe the physical mechanism or infer equal acreage.

## Notes

**A boundary and a register.** The two city groups total twenty-six named towns, with villages
additional rather than counted individually. The western boundary turns south at a mountain
by Beth Horon; it is not the Mediterranean coast. Kiriath Jearim is expressly a Judahite
city on that boundary. Neither equal acreage nor the objects used for lots are specified.

**Tensions retained.** Beth Arabah occurs in both Judah’s and Benjamin’s city lists
(15:61; 18:22). Jerusalem is assigned to Benjamin (18:28), while Judah’s failure there is
reported in 15:63. Bethel and Luz are equated in 18:13, whereas 16:2 describes movement
from Bethel to Luz. These are distinct difficulties: overlapping assignment, allocation
versus occupation, and place-name usage. None receives an invented resolution here.

**Names and people.** Tribal names denote descendants, not living patriarchs. Bohan’s name
and relationship recur without further biography; Hinnom and Rephaim remain covered by
their geographical pages. Gibeath is not silently merged with Judah’s Gibeah, nor Kiriath
with Kiriath Jearim, nor Geliloth with Gilgal. Minor towns receive brief place pages because
each is an explicit member of the inheritance register. Modern identifications remain open.
