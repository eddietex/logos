# The Sin of Achan

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 7:1–26

## Summary

The narrator tells the reader at once what Israel does not know: Achan of Judah has taken from the
devoted things of Jericho, and Yahweh's anger burns against *the children of Israel* as a whole (1).
Joshua sends spies from Jericho to Ai, accepts their advice to send only two or three thousand men,
and the force is routed, losing about thirty-six men. *The hearts of the people melted* (5) — the
phrase that had described Canaan's terror of Israel.

Joshua and the elders tear their clothes and lie before the ark until evening with dust on their
heads. Joshua's prayer asks why Yahweh brought Israel over the Jordan at all and what Yahweh will do
for his *great name* (7–9). Yahweh answers with a charge: Israel has sinned, transgressed the covenant,
taken, stolen, deceived, and hidden devoted things among its own goods, and has itself *become
devoted for destruction*. Yahweh will not be with them unless the devoted thing is removed (10–12).

The people sanctify themselves, and the next morning Yahweh *selects* tribe, clan, household, and man:
Judah, the Zerahites, Zabdi, Achan (16–18). Joshua calls him *My son*, urges him to give glory to
Yahweh and confess, and Achan does: he saw a Babylonian robe, two hundred shekels of silver, and a
fifty-shekel wedge of gold, *coveted them and took them*, and hid them in the ground under his tent
(20–21). Messengers run and find them. Achan, the goods, his sons and daughters, his animals, his tent,
and all he had are brought to the valley of Achor, stoned and burned. A great heap of stones is raised;
Yahweh turns from his anger; the place is called *The valley of Achor* — *trouble* — *to this day*.

## Key verses

> **7:1** But the children of Israel committed a trespass in the devoted things; for Achan, the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah, took some of the devoted things. Therefore Yahweh’s anger burned against the children of Israel.

> **7:5** The men of Ai struck about thirty-six men of them, and they chased them from before the gate even to Shebarim, and struck them at the descent. The hearts of the people melted, and became like water.

> **7:11** Israel has sinned. Yes, they have even transgressed my covenant which I commanded them. Yes, they have even taken some of the devoted things, and have also stolen, and also deceived. They have even put it among their own stuff.

> **7:12** Therefore the children of Israel can’t stand before their enemies. They turn their backs before their enemies, because they have become devoted for destruction. I will not be with you any more, unless you destroy the devoted things from among you.

> **7:19** Joshua said to Achan, “My son, please give glory to Yahweh, the God of Israel, and make confession to him. Tell me now what you have done! Don’t hide it from me!”

> **7:21** When I saw among the plunder a beautiful Babylonian robe, two hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold weighing fifty shekels, then I coveted them and took them. Behold, they are hidden in the ground in the middle of my tent, with the silver under it.”

> **7:25** Joshua said, “Why have you troubled us? Yahweh will trouble you today.” All Israel stoned him with stones, and they burned them with fire and stoned them with stones.

> **7:26** They raised over him a great heap of stones that remains to this day. Yahweh turned from the fierceness of his anger. Therefore the name of that place was called “The valley of Achor” to this day.

## Notes

**One man, and *the children of Israel*.** Verse 1 says *Israel* trespassed and then that *Achan*
took. Yahweh's charge in verse 11 uses plural verbs throughout — *they* have taken, stolen, deceived,
put it among *their* stuff. The narrative never suggests anyone else knew, and the lot isolates a
single man. The text holds the collective and the individual together without explaining how one
man's act is Israel's act; Joshua 22:20 later draws the lesson as *That man didn't perish alone in
his iniquity.* Joshua 6:18 had already said that taking the devoted thing would *make the camp of
Israel accursed, and trouble it*. And Joshua 1:5's promise, *No man will be able to stand before you*,
is answered by its reverse: *the children of Israel can't stand before their enemies* (7:12).

**The devoted thing spreads.** Verse 12's *they have become devoted for destruction* uses the same
noun, *ḥērem*, as the devoted things themselves (*lǝḥērem*, WLC 7:12). Deuteronomy 7:26 had warned
that whoever brings an abomination into the house will *become a devoted thing like it*; Joshua 7
narrates that sentence coming true for the whole camp and then, specifically, for Achan's household.
Deuteronomy 13:17 had promised that when *nothing of the devoted thing* clings to Israel's hand,
*Yahweh may turn from the fierceness of his anger*; Joshua 7:26 reports exactly that, in the same
English words. See
[[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]].

**The melting heart reversed.** Rahab told the spies that Canaan's heart melted at Israel's coming
(Joshua 2:11), and 5:1 says the same of the Amorite and Canaanite kings. At Ai the phrase turns on
Israel (7:5). Joshua's prayer then fears what Rahab had reported in reverse — that the Canaanites
*will hear of it* (9). The reversal is the text's own, made with the same idiom.

**Joshua's lament.** *I wish that we had been content and lived beyond the Jordan!* (7) echoes the
wilderness complaint of Numbers 14:2–3, *Why does Yahweh bring us to this land, to fall by the
sword?* Yahweh's *Get up!* does not
answer the question Joshua asked; it redirects him to a cause he did not know. His appeal to
Yahweh's *great name* (9) is the Mosaic intercession of Numbers 14:13–16 and Deuteronomy 9:28 in
compressed form — the argument that the nations will misread a defeat — but this time the answer is
not forgiveness but disclosure.

**The selection.** The verb for *selects* and *taken* in 14–18 is *lāḵad*, *capture* (*hannilkād*,
WLC 7:15; *wayyillākēd*, 7:18). The text never names the mechanism — lots, Urim and Thummim, or
something else — and the wiki does not supply one. 1 Samuel 14:41–42, where Saul's *Cast lots*
narrows to Jonathan and WEB renders the result *Jonathan was selected*, is the closest later
parallel; see [[The Lot Is Cast into the Lap - Proverbs 16.33 and the Casting of Lots]].

**Give glory and confess.** Joshua's *My son* is gentle, and his request — *give glory* (*kāḇôḏ*)
and *make confession* (*tôḏâ*, WLC 7:19) — asks Achan to vindicate Yahweh's judgment by speaking it
aloud. Confession is made and verified, but in this narrative it does not alter the sentence
pronounced in verse 15. The text records no plea and no mitigation.

**Saw, coveted, took.** Achan's own account is three verbs in sequence — *wāʾēreʾ … wāʾeḥmǝḏēm
wāʾeqqāḥēm* (WLC 7:21) — the same three roots (*rāʾâ*, *ḥāmaḏ*, *lāqaḥ*) that run through the
woman's taking of the fruit in Genesis 3:6, where the tree is *to be desired* (*neḥmāḏ*). Both texts
also call the object *good* (*ṭôḇ*). Deuteronomy 7:25 had forbidden exactly this: *You shall not
covet the silver or the gold … nor take it for yourself*. See
[[Saw, Coveted, Took - Eve and Achan]]. *Hidden in the ground* is *ṭǝmûnîm* (WLC 7:21), the verb
already noted at [[Moses Flees to Midian]] for Moses hiding the Egyptian's body in the sand.

**The robe from Shinar.** WEB's *a beautiful Babylonian robe* is Hebrew *ʾadderet šinʿār*, *a mantle
of Shinar* (WLC 7:21) — the Mesopotamian plain of [[Shinar]] and Babel. The robe's precise origin and
quality are not otherwise described; the text names it as foreign and fine, not why it was in
Jericho.

**Whose death?** Verse 15's sentence was that the one taken *shall be burned with fire, he and all
that he has*. Verse 24 lists *his sons, his daughters* among what is brought to Achor, and verse 25's
pronouns shift from *him* (stoned) to *them* (burned, stoned). The text does not say outright that
the children were executed, though the plain reading of 24–25 is that they were; nor does it say
they were complicit, though the goods were buried in the family tent. Deuteronomy 24:16 — *the
children shall not be put to death for the fathers* — stands in evident tension with that reading.
Interpreters have resolved the tension by arguing knowledge or complicity, by limiting *them* to the
animals and goods, or by treating the household as included in the *ḥērem*; none of these is stated
by the text, and the wiki records the tension rather than choosing.

**Trouble and its valley.** Joshua's last words to Achan play on the place name: *Why have you
troubled us? Yahweh will trouble you today* (*meh ʿăḵartānû yaʿkārǝḵā*, WLC 7:25), and the place is
*ʿēmeq ʿāḵôr* (7:24, 26). The verb is the one Jacob used of Simeon and Levi at Genesis 34:30, *You
have troubled me*. The Chronicler later spells Achan's name *Achar, the troubler of Israel*
(1 Chronicles 2:7, WEB, verified at bible-api.com). Hosea 2:15 and Isaiah 65:10 take up the valley
as a place of future hope and pasture; see
[[The Valley of Achor - Trouble Turned to a Door of Hope]].

**Two stone heaps.** Joshua 4's twelve stones at Gilgal mark the Jordan crossing for children's
questions; Achor's *great heap of stones* marks a grave and a sin, also *to this day*. The same
narrator builds memorials to grace and to judgment. Another heap follows over the king of Ai (8:29).
Two different verbs for stoning appear in verse 25 (*rāḡam* and *sāqal*, WLC); the duplication is
reported without an explanation of it.

**Places.** Ai is located *beside Beth Aven, on the east side of Bethel* (2). Beth Aven and Shebarim
receive no pages of their own in this pass; neither is identified with certainty, and Shebarim
(possibly *the quarries* or *the breaks*) occurs only here in the stored text. See [[Ai]] for the
archaeological problem of the site, which this pericope does not resolve.
