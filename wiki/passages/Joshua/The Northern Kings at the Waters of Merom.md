# The Northern Kings at the Waters of Merom

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 11:1–15

## Summary

Jabin of Hazor summons a northern coalition, including Jobab of Madon and the kings of
Shimron and Achshaph. Its vast force, with horses and chariots, camps at the waters of Merom.
Yahweh assures Joshua of victory and orders the horses disabled and the chariots burned.
Joshua attacks suddenly; Israel defeats and pursues the coalition toward Sidon,
Misrephoth Maim, and the valley of Mizpah.

Joshua returns to Hazor, kills its king and inhabitants, and burns the city. The other
royal cities are captured and their inhabitants killed, but the cities on their mounds
are not burned. Israel keeps livestock and plunder. The closing judgment traces the
command from Yahweh through Moses to Joshua and declares that Joshua carried it out.

## Key verses

> **11:6** Yahweh said to Joshua, “Don’t be afraid because of them; for tomorrow at this time, I will deliver them up all slain before Israel. You shall hamstring their horses and burn their chariots with fire.”

> **11:13** But as for the cities that stood on their mounds, Israel burned none of them, except Hazor only. Joshua burned that.

> **11:14** The children of Israel took all the plunder of these cities, with the livestock, as plunder for themselves; but every man they struck with the edge of the sword, until they had destroyed them. They didn’t leave any who breathed.

> **11:15** As Yahweh commanded Moses his servant, so Moses commanded Joshua. Joshua did so. He left nothing undone of all that Yahweh commanded Moses.

## Notes

**Boundary:** The planned 11:1–15 unit is retained. Verse 15 closes the campaign with
an obedience verdict; the wider conquest summary beginning at verse 16 remains next.

**Violence and material survival:** Verses 11 and 14 describe killing inhabitants,
not merely defeating soldiers. The text presents this as commanded action, with no attempt
here to soften its human cost. It distinguishes retained livestock and goods from
destroyed people, and Hazor’s burning from the other cities left unburned.

**Scope and tension:** Pursuit to Sidon and Misrephoth Maim (8) is not a separate report
of their capture. Joshua 13:4–6 still describes northern populations awaiting dispossession.
The comprehensive destruction language must remain beside that notice, without inventing
a chronology that makes the tension disappear. Joshua’s obedience in verse 15 also stands
beside his failure to seek counsel in 9:14; the commendation does not delete that episode.

**Names and geography:** Jobab of Madon is distinguished from the existing Edomite Jobab.
The unnamed kings receive narrative-period qualifiers. Jabin is qualified by Joshua’s
time to leave later namesakes distinct. Chinneroth remains a marker of uncertain kind
rather than automatically identified with the sea. The land and valley of Mizpah are
provisionally grouped under the Hermon qualifier. Generic terrain and directional
descriptions receive no speculative site pages. The peoples of verse 3 are treated on
[[The Nations]], not identified with their named ancestors.
