# The Fall of Jericho

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 6:1–27

## Summary

Yahweh announces Jericho’s delivery while the city remains shut. Joshua orders the armed men,
seven priests with rams’ horns, and the covenant ark to circle it once a day for six days.
The people remain silent until the seventh day’s seventh circuit. At Joshua’s command they
shout, the wall falls, and they enter straight ahead.

The destruction includes men and women, young and old, and livestock. The city is burned;
silver, gold, brass, and iron vessels go to Yahweh’s treasury. Joshua warns that taking devoted
things would bring trouble upon Israel’s own camp. He sends the two spies to rescue Rahab’s
household as they swore. Initially placed outside the camp, she is subsequently described as
living among Israel. Joshua curses the future rebuilder, and his fame spreads as Yahweh is with him.

## Key verses

> **6:2** Yahweh said to Joshua, “Behold, I have given Jericho into your hand, with its king and the mighty men of valor.

> **6:17** The city shall be devoted, even it and all that is in it, to Yahweh. Only Rahab the prostitute shall live, she and all who are with her in the house, because she hid the messengers that we sent.

> **6:18** But as for you, only keep yourselves from what is devoted to destruction, lest when you have devoted it, you take of the devoted thing; so you would make the camp of Israel accursed, and trouble it.

> **6:21** They utterly destroyed all that was in the city, both man and woman, both young and old, and ox, sheep, and donkey, with the edge of the sword.

> **6:25** But Rahab the prostitute, her father’s household, and all that she had, Joshua saved alive. She lives in the middle of Israel to this day, because she hid the messengers, whom Joshua sent to spy out Jericho.

> **6:26** Joshua commanded them with an oath at that time, saying, “Cursed is the man before Yahweh, who rises up and builds this city Jericho. With the loss of his firstborn he will lay its foundation, and with the loss of his youngest son he will set up its gates.”

## Notes

**Gift and commanded action.** Yahweh’s announcement precedes victory (2). The lengthy procession
stresses ordered obedience, not a siege technique whose acoustic or engineering mechanism the
text explains. Seven days, priests, horns, and final circuits organize the scene; the passage does
not identify the seventh day as the weekly Sabbath or give the date on which the march began.

**Destruction and exception.** The violence in verse 21 includes noncombatants. Deuteronomy
20:16–18’s command to save nothing that breathes stands in tension with Rahab’s household being
spared. Joshua expressly records her exception and ties it to her sheltering the spies and their
oath; it does not offer a general legal harmonization. Neither the killing nor the rescue should
be omitted to make the account simpler.

**A house on the wall.** Joshua 2:15 locates Rahab’s house on the wall; 6:20 describes the wall’s
collapse before the spies enter her house in 6:22–23. These notices create a physical question
that the text does not resolve. A surviving wall segment is a possible reconstruction, not a
stated fact. No modern site, collapse mechanism, or archaeological chronology is asserted here.

**Treasury and camp.** Reserved metal survives destruction, while private taking threatens the
whole camp (18–19). Rahab’s initial location outside the camp does not by itself prove ritual
uncleanness or a particular admission ceremony. Verse 25 supplies the eventual outcome.
