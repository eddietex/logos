# Passover at Gilgal and the End of the Manna

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 5:10–12

## Summary

Israel keeps Passover at Gilgal on the fourteenth day of the month at evening, in the
plains of Jericho. After the circumcision and healing of the preceding unit, the narrative
turns to the feast and to food. On the next day after Passover the people eat unleavened
cakes and parched grain from the land. The manna ceases the next day, after they have eaten
its produce; Canaan's fruit supplies their food that year.

These three verses close the wilderness food story without depicting a food crisis.
The provision changes as Israel enters the land. Passover observance continues across
that change, before the narrative turns to Jericho's capture.

## Key verses

The complete pericope, copied verbatim from stored WEB, raw/bible/Joshua.md.

> **10** The children of Israel encamped in Gilgal. They kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the month at evening in the plains of Jericho.

> **11** They ate unleavened cakes and parched grain of the produce of the land on the next day after the Passover, in the same day.

> **12** The manna ceased on the next day, after they had eaten of the produce of the land. The children of Israel didn’t have manna any more; but they ate of the fruit of the land of Canaan that year.

## Notes

**Calendar and sequence.** Joshua 4:19 dates arrival to the first month; 5:10 supplies the
fourteenth day. The WEB places eating produce on the next day after Passover, and cessation
of manna on the next day after that eating. This page preserves that sequence rather than
compressing all three into one day. No independent Hebrew calendrical claim is made.

**What the produce does not establish.** The WEB says produce, unleavened cakes, parched
grain, and fruit. It does not explain how the food was acquired, who planted it, or whether
it came from stored or newly harvested grain. It does not say that Israel's own crops had
already grown. Nor does eating in Canaan mean the conquest or allotment is complete.

**Circumcision and observance.** Exodus 12:48 excludes the uncircumcised from eating Passover.
The sequence of Joshua 5:1–12 fits that requirement, but these verses do not give a complete
history of feast observance during the wilderness years. Numbers 9:1–5 expressly records
one at Sinai; the absence of further narratives cannot prove no further observance.

**Provision and fulfillment.** Exodus 16:35 summarizes manna lasting to Canaan's borders;
Joshua gives the local sequence after crossing, at Gilgal. A reading that insists manna
stopped before the crossing would conflict with Joshua 5:10–12. The broad summary and
precise arrival narrative can be read together without moving Gilgal outside the land.

**Sources and scope.** Quotations are from stored WEB Joshua 5:10–12. Comparisons were
checked in stored WEB Joshua 4–5, Exodus 12:25, 48 and 16:35, Numbers 9:1–5,
Deuteronomy 8:3, 7–10, 16, and Leviticus 23:10–14. No unstored-book quotation or
source-language claim is introduced. The planned boundary is retained; Israel remains a
collective actor without a new person page.
