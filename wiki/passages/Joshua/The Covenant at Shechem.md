# The Covenant at Shechem

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 24:1–28

## Summary

Joshua gathers *all the tribes of Israel to Shechem*, summons the elders, heads, judges, and
officers, and *they presented themselves before God* (1). He then speaks a message introduced
as Yahweh's own: *Yahweh says, the God of Israel* (2).

The first half (2–13) is a history spoken in Yahweh's first person. Israel's fathers lived
*beyond the River*, and Terah, *the father of Abraham, and the father of Nahor*, *served other
gods* (2). Yahweh *took* Abraham from there, led him through Canaan, multiplied his offspring,
and gave him Isaac. He gave Isaac Jacob and Esau, gave Esau Mount Seir, and let Jacob and his
children go down into Egypt (3–4). He sent Moses and Aaron, plagued Egypt, brought Israel out,
put darkness between them and the Egyptians at the Red Sea, and brought the sea on the pursuers.
Israel then *lived in the wilderness many days* (5–7). He gave the Amorites beyond the Jordan into
Israel's hand (8). He would not listen to Balaam, *therefore he blessed you still*, and Balak's
plan failed (9–10). Across the Jordan the men of Jericho and the seven peoples fought and were
delivered up. Yahweh *sent the hornet before you*, *not with your sword, nor with your bow*
(11–12). The history ends in a land Israel did not labor for, cities it did not build, and
vineyards and olive groves it did not plant (13).

The second half (14–28) turns that history into a demand. *Now therefore fear Yahweh, and serve
him in sincerity and in truth*. Israel must put away the gods served beyond the River and in
Egypt (14). If serving Yahweh seems evil, it must *choose today* between the fathers' gods and
the Amorites' gods; *but as for me and my house, we will serve Yahweh* (15). The people refuse
to forsake Yahweh and retell the exodus and conquest in their own words (16–18). Joshua answers
with something unexpected: *You can't serve Yahweh, for he is a holy God. He is a jealous God.*
He *will not forgive*, and he will turn and consume them *after he has done you good* (19–20).
The people insist, *No, but we will serve Yahweh* (21). Joshua makes them witnesses against
themselves, and they accept: *We are witnesses* (22). He tells them again to put away the
foreign gods *which are among you*, and they promise to serve and to listen (23–24).

Joshua then makes a covenant with the people that day, with a statute and an ordinance at
Shechem (25). He writes *these words in the book of the law of God* and sets up a great stone
*under the oak that was by the sanctuary of Yahweh* (26). The stone *has heard all Yahweh's
words*, and it will be *a witness against you, lest you deny your God* (27). He sends the people
away, *each to his own inheritance* (28).

## Key verses

> **24:2** Joshua said to all the people, “Yahweh says, the God of Israel, ‘Your fathers lived of old time beyond the River, even Terah, the father of Abraham, and the father of Nahor: and they served other gods.

> **24:3** I took your father Abraham from beyond the River, and led him throughout all the land of Canaan, and multiplied his offspring, and gave him Isaac.

> **24:12** I sent the hornet before you, which drove them out from before you, even the two kings of the Amorites; not with your sword, nor with your bow.

> **24:13** I gave you a land whereon you had not labored, and cities which you didn’t build, and you live in them. You eat of vineyards and olive groves which you didn’t plant.’

> **24:14** “Now therefore fear Yahweh, and serve him in sincerity and in truth. Put away the gods which your fathers served beyond the River, in Egypt; and serve Yahweh.

> **24:15** If it seems evil to you to serve Yahweh, choose today whom you will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you dwell; but as for me and my house, we will serve Yahweh.”

> **24:19** Joshua said to the people, “You can’t serve Yahweh, for he is a holy God. He is a jealous God. He will not forgive your disobedience nor your sins.

> **24:22** Joshua said to the people, “You are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen Yahweh yourselves, to serve him.” They said, “We are witnesses.”

> **24:23** “Now therefore put away the foreign gods which are among you, and incline your heart to Yahweh, the God of Israel.”

> **24:26** Joshua wrote these words in the book of the law of God; and he took a great stone, and set it up there under the oak that was by the sanctuary of Yahweh.

> **24:27** Joshua said to all the people, “Behold, this stone shall be a witness against us, for it has heard all Yahweh’s words which he spoke to us. It shall be therefore a witness against you, lest you deny your God.”

## Notes

**Who is speaking.** Verses 2–13 are a prophetic speech. Joshua opens with *Yahweh says* and then
speaks as Yahweh: *I took*, *I gave*, *I sent*, *I plagued*, *I brought*, *I delivered*. The
history is not Israel's account of its achievements. In the WEB, Israel's own verbs are going
down, coming, crying out, living, crossing, possessing, and eating; every turn in the story is
Yahweh's. The people's reply (16–18) retells the same story in the third person —
*it is Yahweh our God who brought us* — so the recital is spoken twice, once by Yahweh and once
back to him.

**Beginning with the idolaters.** Deuteronomy 26:5 begins Israel's recited history with *My
father was a Syrian ready to perish* (stored WEB). This speech goes further back, to Terah, and
opens with a fact Genesis never states: *they served other gods*. Genesis 11:31 says Terah left
Ur *to go into the land of Canaan* and does not mention his religion (stored WEB). So the founding
family is introduced here not as the first worshipers of Yahweh but as people Yahweh took out of
idolatry. The same point is the ground of the demand in verse 14. The gods *beyond the River* are
not foreign to Israel's story; they are where its family started. See
[[Out of Ur - Terah's Journey and Abram's Call]].

**Three sets of gods.** Verses 14–15 name the gods of the fathers beyond the River, the gods
served *in Egypt*, and *the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you dwell*. Only the last are
local. The first two are ancestral and past, but verse 23 says *the foreign gods which are among
you*. Whether that means images actually present in the camp or is a call to inner allegiance
is not settled by the text. Verse 23 pairs the putting away with *incline your heart to Yahweh*,
which supports both readings. The narrative reports no images handed over, unlike Genesis 35:4,
where Jacob's household gives up its gods and Jacob hides them *under the oak which was by
Shechem* (stored WEB). See [[Put Away the Foreign Gods - Genesis 35.2-4 and Joshua 24.14-23]].

**Serve.** In the WEB of verses 1–28, *serve*, *served*, or *serves* appears fifteen times; every
one of them in verses 2 and 14–24. The people's reply recalls *the house of bondage* (17), so the
choice is between masters, not between serving and freedom. This page makes no claim about the
Hebrew verb or verbs behind the English, which were not checked in this pass.

**"You can't serve Yahweh."** Joshua's answer to the people's first pledge is a refusal. He has
just told them to choose; when they choose Yahweh, he says they are not able to. The text does
not explain the move, and the wiki records three readings without choosing among them. It may be
a test that draws out a firmer commitment, which is what happens (21, 24). It may be a sober
forecast, like Moses' *I know that after my death you will utterly corrupt yourselves*
(Deuteronomy 31:29, stored WEB). Or it may be a statement about Yahweh rather than about Israel:
*he is a holy God … a jealous God*, and that is not a God whom a people serves alongside others.
The third reading fits verse 20, which says what follows if Israel serves *foreign gods* too.

**"He will not forgive" — a tension, flagged.** Verse 19 says Yahweh *will not forgive your
disobedience nor your sins*. Exodus 34:7 proclaims him *forgiving iniquity and disobedience and
sin* (stored WEB). The two are not reconciled here. Exodus 34:7 itself goes on to *will by no
means clear the guilty*, and Exodus 23:21 says of the angel sent ahead *he will not pardon your
disobedience, for my name is in him* (stored WEB). Joshua's words are closest to Exodus 23:21:
spoken about apostasy, not about every failure. The [[Forgiveness]] page records both claims.

**Two tensions with the earlier narrative.**

- *The men of Jericho fought against you* (11). Joshua 6 narrates no battle: *Jericho was
  tightly shut up … No one went out, and no one came in* (6:1), and the walls fall without combat
  (stored WEB). The summary may mean resistance in general, or may count the city's closed gates
  as war. The wiki leaves the difference standing; see [[The Fall of Jericho]].
- *Balak … arose and fought against Israel* (9). Numbers 22 narrates no battle; Balak hires
  Balaam *that we may strike them* (Numbers 22:6), and Deuteronomy 2:9 forbade Israel to *contend
  with* Moab in battle (stored WEB). Judges 11:25 asks of Balak, *did he ever fight against
  them?* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com; Judges not yet ingested). Joshua 24:9 can be read as
  calling the hire of a curse an act of war, which is what Balak intended it to be. Both the
  statement and the question are recorded on [[Balak]] without harmonizing them.

**The hornet and the two kings.** Verse 12 says the hornet drove out *the two kings of the
Amorites*, and verse 8 places the Amorites *beyond the Jordan* — that is, Sihon and Og. Numbers
21:24 narrates Sihon's defeat by *the edge of the sword* (stored WEB). Verse 12's *not with your
sword, nor with your bow* does not deny that swords were drawn; it denies that they were the
reason. The book has said the same thing in its own voice: *Yahweh, the God of Israel, fought for
Israel* (10:42, stored WEB). Joshua's narrative never otherwise mentions the hornet; it appears
only as promise (Exodus 23:28; Deuteronomy 7:20) and here as memory. See
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].

**Gift without labor.** Verse 13 is close to Deuteronomy 6:10–11 — cities *which you didn't
build*, vineyards and olive trees *which you didn't plant* (stored WEB). Moses attached a warning:
*then beware lest you forget Yahweh* (6:12), and in the next verses, *You shall not go after other
gods* (6:14) because *Yahweh your God among you is a jealous God* (6:15). Joshua follows the same
order: the unearned land, the command to fear and serve, the ban on other gods, and the jealous
God. See [[Cities You Did Not Build - Deuteronomy 6.10-12 and Joshua 24.13]] and
[[A Jealous God - Exodus 20.5, 34.14, Deuteronomy 4.24, 6.15, and Joshua 24.19]].

**A covenant ceremony like Sinai's.** At Sinai the people answered *All that Yahweh has spoken we
will do* (Exodus 19:8), Moses *wrote all Yahweh's words*, built an altar, *took the book of the
covenant and read it*, and the people answered again (Exodus 24:3–7; stored WEB). At Shechem the
people answer three times (16–18, 21, 24), Joshua writes *these words in the book of the law of
God*, and he sets up a stone. There is no altar, sacrifice, or blood here, unlike Exodus 24:5–8.
What replaces them is witness: the people *against yourselves* (22), and the stone that *has
heard* (27). The wiki notes the resemblance and makes no claim about ancient treaty forms.

**The stone that heard.** Galeed was a witness *between me and you* (Genesis 31:48) and the
Jordan altar *a witness between us and you* (Joshua 22:27; both stored WEB). The Shechem stone
is *a witness against us … a witness against you* (27). The first two guard a relationship
between human parties; this one guards Israel's relationship with God. It is placed by *the
sanctuary of Yahweh*, a phrase this pericope does not explain. Shiloh is where the tent of
meeting stood (18:1). Whether a separate shrine stood at Shechem, or the ark was brought there
for the assembly, the text does not say. See
[[A Witness Between Us - Galeed, the Jordan Altar, and the Shechem Stone]] and
[[The Trees of the Patriarchs]].

**Joshua's house.** *As for me and my house* (15) is the first time the book gives Joshua a
household of his own. It has given him an inheritance, Timnathserah, where *he built the city,
and lived there* (19:49–50), but no named family. The declaration is made before the people
answer; Joshua does not wait to see what Israel will do.

**Sent to their inheritances.** The pericope ends where the allotment chapters ended, with every
man in his own inheritance (28). The land is now held under a written covenant. The next verses
(29–33) record three burials, and Judges 2:7 (WEB, checked at bible-api.com) says the people
served Yahweh all the days of Joshua and of the elders who outlived him.
