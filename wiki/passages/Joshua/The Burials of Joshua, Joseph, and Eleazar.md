# The Burials of Joshua, Joseph, and Eleazar

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 24:29–33

## Summary

*After these things* — the covenant at Shechem and the people sent each to his own inheritance —
Joshua dies at one hundred ten, called for the first time *the servant of Yahweh* (29). He is
buried *in the border of his inheritance* at Timnathserah, in the hill country of Ephraim, north
of the mountain of Gaash (30). The narrator adds a verdict on his generation: Israel *served
Yahweh* all of Joshua's days and all the days of the elders who outlived him and *had known all the
work of Yahweh* (31).

Two more burials follow. The bones of Joseph, *which the children of Israel brought up out of
Egypt*, are buried at Shechem, in the parcel of ground Jacob bought from the sons of Hamor, and it
becomes *the inheritance of the children of Joseph* (32). Then Eleazar the son of Aaron dies and is
buried in *the hill of Phinehas his son*, also in the hill country of Ephraim (33). The book ends
with three graves, all in the land.

## Key verses

> **24:29** After these things, Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of Yahweh, died, being one hundred ten years old.

> **24:30** They buried him in the border of his inheritance in Timnathserah, which is in the hill country of Ephraim, on the north of the mountain of Gaash.

> **24:31** Israel served Yahweh all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, and had known all the work of Yahweh, that he had worked for Israel.

> **24:32** They buried the bones of Joseph, which the children of Israel brought up out of Egypt, in Shechem, in the parcel of ground which Jacob bought from the sons of Hamor the father of Shechem for a hundred pieces of silver. They became the inheritance of the children of Joseph.

> **24:33** Eleazar the son of Aaron died. They buried him in the hill of Phinehas his son, which was given him in the hill country of Ephraim.

## Notes

**The servant of Yahweh.** The book of Joshua calls Moses *the servant of Yahweh* fourteen times in
the WEB, and Yahweh twice calls him *Moses my servant* (1:2, 7). Joshua is introduced in the first
verse as *Moses' servant* (1:1). Only here, in his death notice, is he called *the servant of
Yahweh* — the same title Deuteronomy 34:5 uses in Moses' death notice: *So Moses the servant of
Yahweh died there* (stored WEB). The book opened with one servant of Yahweh dead and closes with
another. See [[The Servant of Yahweh - Moses' Title Given to Joshua]]. This is a count of the
English WEB only; no claim is made about the Hebrew.

**One hundred ten.** Joseph died at the same age: *So Joseph died, being one hundred ten years old*
(Genesis 50:26, stored WEB). Moses died at one hundred twenty (Deuteronomy 34:7). Joshua is of
Ephraim (Numbers 13:8), Joseph's son's tribe, and the verse that buries Joseph comes two verses after
the one that gives Joshua's age. The narrative does not comment on the match. The note on
[[Another Generation Arose - Exodus 1.6-8 and Judges 2.8-10]] records that a hundred and ten years
is often said to be an Egyptian ideal lifespan; that claim is not verified in this wiki.

**Buried in his inheritance.** Joshua was the last man to receive an inheritance (19:49). He asked
for Timnathserah, *built the city, and lived there* (19:50), and is now buried *in the border of his
inheritance*. Moses saw the land from Nebo and was buried in Moab, *but no man knows where his tomb
is to this day* (Deuteronomy 34:6, stored WEB); Aaron died on Mount Hor (Numbers 20:28). The
wilderness leaders were buried outside the land. All three graves here are inside it. Israel wept
thirty days for Aaron (Numbers 20:29) and for Moses (Deuteronomy 34:8; both stored WEB); no
mourning is recorded for Joshua or Eleazar. *Mount Gaash*
appears here for the first time in the wiki; this passage locates the grave north of it and says no
more.

**The generation that knew.** Verse 31 says Israel served Yahweh *all the days of Joshua, and all the
days of the elders who outlived Joshua*. The faithfulness is tied to people who *had known all the
work of Yahweh*. The statement looks back on [[The Covenant at Shechem]], where Joshua said *You
can't serve Yahweh* (24:19) and the people insisted they would: for one generation, they did.
Judges 2:7 repeats the sentence with *who had seen all the great work of Yahweh*, and 2:10 adds what
the verse here leaves unsaid: *another generation arose after them, who didn't know Yahweh, nor the
work which he had done for Israel* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com). See
[[Joshua's Death Told Twice - Joshua 24.28-31 and Judges 2.6-9]].

**Joseph's bones.** At his death Joseph made the children of Israel swear: *God will surely visit
you, and you shall carry up my bones from here* (Genesis 50:25). Moses took the bones at the exodus
(Exodus 13:19; both stored WEB). This verse finishes the oath. It does not say when the burial
happened. The verse sits after Joshua's death, but nothing in it requires that order, and the bones
may have been buried any time after the land was divided; the wiki leaves the timing open. The
place is Jacob's purchase of Genesis 33:19, *at the hand of the children of Hamor, Shechem's father,
for one hundred pieces of money* (stored WEB). The WEB here says *pieces of silver*; the two English
renderings differ, and this page makes no claim about whether the Hebrew does. Jacob had told Joseph,
*I have given to you one portion above your brothers* (Genesis 48:22, stored WEB). Joseph is not
buried at Machpelah with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, but in the ground his father gave him. See
[[Carry Up My Bones - Joseph's Oath and Its Keeping]] and
[[Bought, Sacked, or Taken by the Sword - How Jacob Got Shechem]].

**"They became the inheritance."** The WEB's *They became the inheritance of the children of Joseph*
has a plural subject. The nearest plural is *the bones*, but the sense seems to be the parcel of
ground. The page reports the wording and does not resolve it. Shechem itself is a refuge city in
the hill country of Ephraim (20:7) and was given with its suburbs to the Kohathite Levites (21:21);
the parcel is a field at Shechem, not the city, so the two notices need not conflict.

**Eleazar and the hill of Phinehas.** Eleazar received the high priest's garments on the day Aaron
died (Numbers 20:28), stood beside Joshua at his commissioning (Numbers 27), and distributed the land
with him (14:1; 19:51). The two who divided the land die in the same paragraph. No successor is
installed for either. Phinehas is named, but only as the owner of the hill, and the text does not say
he became high priest here.

**A tension, flagged.** The hill was *given* to Phinehas (33), yet Joshua says twice that the tribe
of Levi received no inheritance: *The offerings of Yahweh … are his inheritance* (13:14) and *Yahweh,
the God of Israel, is their inheritance* (13:33; both stored WEB). The Levites did receive
forty-eight cities with their suburbs (21:41), and the priests' cities were in Judah, Simeon, and
Benjamin (21:4). The hill of Phinehas is in Ephraim. The text does not say who gave it, whether it
was a Levitical holding, or whether it was a town or a burial site. Both statements stand; the
tension is recorded on [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]].

**Three graves.** Genesis ends with Joseph *put in a coffin in Egypt* (50:26). Joshua ends with
that coffin's contents in the ground at Shechem, the leader of the conquest in his own city, and the
high priest on his son's hill. The patriarchs owned one grave in Canaan, Machpelah, bought from the
Hittites. The book of Joshua ends with three graves in the land, each in ground Israel now holds.
