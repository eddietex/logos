# The Commander of Yahweh's Army

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 5:13–15

## Summary

By Jericho, Joshua sees a man with a drawn sword and asks whether he is for Israel or its
enemies. The answer begins with a refusal of that choice and identifies the speaker as
commander of Yahweh's army. Joshua falls face down, worships, and asks for his lord's word
as a servant. The commander orders him to remove his shoes because the place is holy;
Joshua obeys.

Before Joshua directs the coming battle, he receives a command about his own approach.
The encounter places Israel's leader under authority. It does not describe a battle,
identify the army's members, or give the commander a personal name.

## Key verses

The complete pericope, copied verbatim from stored WEB, raw/bible/Joshua.md.

> **13** When Joshua was by Jericho, he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man stood in front of him with his sword drawn in his hand. Joshua went to him and said to him, “Are you for us, or for our enemies?”

> **14** He said, “No; but I have come now as commander of Yahweh’s army.” Joshua fell on his face to the earth, and worshiped, and asked him, “What does my lord say to his servant?”

> **15** The prince of Yahweh’s army said to Joshua, “Take off your shoes; for the place on which you stand is holy.” Joshua did so.

## Notes

**Authority and allegiance.** The WEB's answer begins “No”; it does not simply affirm
either side of Joshua's question. The declaration of office changes the conversation:
Joshua now asks what he should hear and do. This does not establish divine neutrality
between Israel and Jericho, nor does it cancel the land promise. It checks the assumption
that the figure can be recruited on Joshua's terms.

**Identity remains an interpretive question.** The WEB introduces a man and calls him
commander in verse 14 and prince in verse 15. These are descriptions within one encounter,
not two figures. The holy place and Joshua's worship invite a reading as divine presence;
a heavenly representative is also a possible reading. These verses do not explicitly call
him Yahweh's angel or identify him as Christ. The comparison with [[The Angel of Yahweh]]
is recorded without merging their person pages. No source-language claim is made about
the titles or the verb translated worshiped.

**Moses and Joshua.** The footwear command closely recalls Exodus 3:5. This supports
reading Joshua's leadership in continuity with Moses' dependence on holy presence.
The scenes differ: Moses receives a lengthy commission; this unit ends with Joshua's
obedience to a short command. No altar, sacrifice, or permanent sanctuary is established.

**Scope and sources.** The planned boundary is retained. Quotations come only from the
stored WEB Joshua 5:13–15; comparisons were checked in stored WEB Exodus 3:1–12 and
Numbers 22:22–35. No unstored-book quotation or unverified Hebrew assertion is added.
The unnamed commander receives a title qualified by Joshua's time, following the
narrative-period convention; this label makes no claim to identify him across appearances.
