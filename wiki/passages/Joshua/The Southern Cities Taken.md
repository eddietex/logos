# The Southern Cities Taken

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 10:28–43

## Summary

After executing the five allied kings, Joshua takes Makkedah and advances with all Israel
through Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron, and Debir. Horam of Gezer tries to relieve Lachish
and is defeated. The repeated accounts describe killing the cities’ inhabitants, not merely
their kings or soldiers, and compare each victory with preceding ones.

The closing summary gathers hill country, South, lowland, and slopes into a sweeping account
of conquest. The narrator presents the destruction as obedience to Yahweh and explains the
success by Yahweh’s fighting for Israel. Joshua and the army then return to Gilgal.

## Key verses

> **10:33** Then Horam king of Gezer came up to help Lachish; and Joshua struck him and his people, until he had left him no one remaining.

> **10:40** So Joshua struck all the land, the hill country, the South, the lowland, the slopes, and all their kings. He left no one remaining, but he utterly destroyed all that breathed, as Yahweh, the God of Israel, commanded.

> **10:42** Joshua took all these kings and their land at one time because Yahweh, the God of Israel, fought for Israel.

> **10:43** Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp to Gilgal.

## Notes

**Boundary and time:** The planned 10:28–43 unit is retained. Verse 28 continues the day
of the kings’ execution; Lachish falls on the second day (32). Verse 42 compresses the
campaign without making every siege occur on a single day.

**Rulers:** The unnamed king of Hebron in 37 is not securely identified with [[Hoham]],
who has already died in 23–26. A successor is possible but not specified. No second
person page is created for a conjectured successor. The explicitly distinct unnamed
kings of Makkedah, Libnah, and Debir receive pages qualified by Joshua’s time.

**Geography:** Gezer sends a relief force; its capture is not reported. Kadesh Barnea,
Gaza, Goshen, and Gibeon belong to the summary’s geographical frame rather than four
additional siege accounts. The hill country, South, lowland, and slopes are treated as
regional descriptions here. Libnah is distinguished from the wilderness camp of the same
name; Debir the city from the king of Eglon; and Goshen in Canaan from Egyptian Goshen.

**Tensions retained:** Joshua 10:40–42’s comprehensive victory stands beside remaining
land in 13:1–3 and further action at Hebron and Debir in 15:13–17. These later passages
have been read in the stored WEB, without ingesting them. Reoccupation or overlapping
accounts may explain the juxtaposition, but neither is expressly stated. See
[[The Southern Conquest and the Land Still Remaining]].

**Return to camp:** Verse 43 repeats the return to Gilgal in 10:15, after the intervening
Makkedah and southern city accounts. An anticipatory summary at 15 is a possible reading,
not the narrator’s explicit explanation.

**Sources:** All quotations are copied from stored WEB Joshua 10. Cross-references were
checked in stored WEB Joshua 13, 15, 16 and Deuteronomy 20. No source-language assertion,
external quotation, or precise modern site identification is introduced.
