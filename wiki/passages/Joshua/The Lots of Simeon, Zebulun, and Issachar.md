# The Lots of Simeon, Zebulun, and Issachar

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 19:1–23

## Summary

The second, third, and fourth lots distribute inheritance to Simeon, Zebulun, and Issachar
by their families. Simeon receives towns within Judah because Judah’s portion was too large.
Zebulun’s boundary runs west and east from Sarid before its northern turn; Issachar’s
register ends at the Jordan. City totals and surrounding villages define usable inheritance,
without narrating a new campaign or confirming occupation of every location.

## People
- [[Simeon]] — The descendants receive the second lot by families, with thirteen towns and a further four inside Judah’s share (1–9). The explanation is Judah’s surplus. Earlier synthesis of absorption must be qualified: this passage still calls Simeon a tribe with its own inheritance; it does not narrate disappearance.
- [[Judah]] — Simeon’s inheritance is taken from Judah’s oversized portion (1, 9). This qualifies the earlier overview’s statement that Judah’s allotment is not reopened: Joshua 18:5 retains its borders, while 19:9 redistributes holdings within them.
- [[Zebulun]] — The descendants receive the third lot, a boundary circuit and twelve cities with villages (10–16). Genesis 49:13 speaks of ships, sea, and Sidon; this register names neither a sea endpoint nor Sidon. The geographical tension remains unresolved; no commercial corridor is invented.
- [[Issachar]] — The descendants receive the fourth lot with sixteen cities and a border ending at the Jordan (17–23). Genesis 49:14–15’s forced labor is not reported here: this is an allotment register, not a report of the tribe’s labor status.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Family inheritance has different forms: Simeon’s towns lie inside Judah’s oversized share, Zebulun receives a boundary circuit and twelve cities, and Issachar receives sixteen cities with a Jordan endpoint. Allotment does not itself establish occupation (1–23).
- [[Providence]] — The second, third, and fourth lots continue the Shiloh distribution (1, 10, 17; compare 18:10). Allocation by lot coexists with the practical explanation that Judah’s portion was too large (9); neither equal acreage nor the lot mechanism is specified.
- [[Names and Naming]] — The WEB renders Beersheba (or Sheba) as one entry (2), fitting the stated thirteen towns. Similar names in different tribal settings are not automatically identical: the two Rimmons, the northern Bethlehem, and Issachar’s Beth Shemesh need distinct treatment.

## Notes

**Counts and names.** Simeon’s thirteen towns depend on the WEB’s parenthetical alternative
Sheba (2); the next group explicitly has four (7). Zebulun’s verse 15 lists five names
before giving twelve as the total. Boundary landmarks and settlement names are interwoven;
the text does not identify exactly which entries make up its twelve. Issachar’s sixteen
names are supplied in verses 18–22. Villages are additional, not separately enumerated.

**Promise and allocation.** Genesis 49:7’s dispersal of Simeon can be compared with a lot
inside Judah, but Joshua gives surplus territory as the reason, not Simeon’s guilt.
Genesis 49:13’s sea, ships, and Sidon do not appear as endpoints in Zebulun’s register.
That tension is retained rather than resolved by an unmentioned trade route. Issachar’s
forced labor in Genesis 49:15 is neither narrated nor explicitly denied here.

**Disambiguation.** Japhia the city is distinct from the king of Lachish; both pages now
carry qualifiers. Rimmon (Zebulun), Bethlehem (Zebulun), Beth Shemesh (Issachar), and Jezreel
are kept distinct from the southern places bearing those names. Baalath Beer’s southern
Ramah designation is covered on one page, without merging it into Benjamin’s Ramah.
Similar names such as Chesulloth and Chisloth Tabor remain separate. No etymology, precise
modern site, or source-language assertion is supplied.

**Sources and scope.** Quotations above come directly from stored WEB Joshua 19:1–23.
Comparisons use stored WEB Joshua 15, 18, and Genesis 49. Tribal names refer to descendants,
not patriarchs personally present. The planned boundary is unchanged.
