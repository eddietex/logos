# The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 13:8–33

## Summary

The narrative pauses western allocation to register what Moses already gave east of the Jordan.
A general survey of Sihon’s and Og’s former territories (8–12) is immediately qualified:
Geshur and Maacath remain within Israel (13). Levi receives no territorial share (14).

Reuben’s towns and boundaries occupy verses 15–23, Gad’s verses 24–28, and eastern Manasseh’s
verses 29–31. Each is organized by families. The Reuben list recalls the five Midianite chiefs
and Balaam among those killed; the Manasseh list includes sixty villages of Jair and the
portion of Machir’s descendants. The closing notice locates Moses’ distribution in Moab’s
plains and again exempts Levi, whose inheritance is Yahweh himself (32–33).

The register anchors Joshua’s allocation in Moses’ earlier work while refusing to equate
an inherited territory with the removal of all its peoples. Levi’s different portion frames
the tribal lists: provision is real even where it does not take the form of a regional allotment.

## People
- [[God]] — Yahweh is Levi’s inheritance (33); his offerings are the tribe’s portion (14). The absence of a territorial allotment is paired with provision, not abandonment.
- [[Moses]] — The eastern conquests and allotments are repeatedly credited to Moses (8–32). This is retrospective: Joshua’s book preserves his predecessor’s grants, not a new action by the deceased leader.
- [[Reuben]] — The descendants receive the plain and cities from the Arnon-valley Aroer, with the Jordan as border (15–23). Dibon, built by Gad in Numbers 32:34, is listed here in Reuben’s share (17); the difference is retained, not explained by an invented transfer.
- [[Gad]] — The descendants receive Jazer, Gilead territory, and valley cities up to Chinnereth (24–28). Verse 25 includes half the land of Ammon, in tension with Deuteronomy 2:19, 37’s prohibition and avoidance. The passage does not explain the territorial history.
- [[Manasseh]] — The eastern half-tribe receives Bashan, the sixty villages of Jair, and holdings for half the children of Machir (29–31). These are descendants’ allotments, not the patriarch’s personal occupation.
- [[Levi]] — The tribe receives no territorial inheritance; offerings made by fire (14) and Yahweh himself (33) supply the two descriptions of its inheritance. These complement Deuteronomy 18:1–2 without resolving the priest/Levite distinctions in Numbers 18.
- [[Sihon]] — His former kingdom provides the setting for the grants (10, 21, 27). The five Midianite chiefs are called princes of Sihon (21), a relationship not supplied by Numbers 31:8, which calls them kings of Midian.
- [[Og]] — His former realm, including Ashtaroth and Edrei, is allotted to eastern Manasseh (12, 30–31). His membership in the remnant of the Rephaim is recalled, not a new encounter.
- [[The Rephaim]] — Og is recalled as remaining from their remnant (12). The statement belongs to the retrospective account of his defeated kingdom.
- [[Balaam]] — Israel’s killing of Balaam is recalled amid the eastern territorial register (22), which calls him the soothsayer in the WEB. Numbers 31:8 supplies the matching death notice; neither account explains his movements after Numbers 24:25.
- [[Beor (father of Balaam)]] — Named only as Balaam’s father in the death notice (22). No independent action is attributed to him.
- [[Jair (son of Manasseh)]] — The villages bearing Jair’s name are sixty cities in Bashan (30). The verse names the settlements rather than narrating a new conquest by Jair; compare Numbers 32:41 and Deuteronomy 3:14.
- [[Machir]] — Half his descendants receive half Gilead, Ashtaroth, and Edrei (31). The verse identifies him as Manasseh’s son; the recipients are families, not an account of the ancestor himself campaigning.
- [[Evi]] — Named among the five chiefs of Midian, princes of Sihon, whom Moses struck (21). Numbers 31:8 calls the same five kings of Midian. The descriptions differ in title and political framing; neither text explains their precise relationship to Sihon.
- [[Rekem (person)]] — Named among the five chiefs of Midian, princes of Sihon, whom Moses struck (21). Numbers 31:8 calls the same five kings of Midian. The descriptions differ in title and political framing; neither text explains their precise relationship to Sihon.
- [[Zur]] — Named among the five chiefs of Midian, princes of Sihon, whom Moses struck (21). Numbers 31:8 calls the same five kings of Midian. The descriptions differ in title and political framing; neither text explains their precise relationship to Sihon.
- [[Hur (king of Midian)]] — Named among the five chiefs of Midian, princes of Sihon, whom Moses struck (21). Numbers 31:8 calls the same five kings of Midian. The descriptions differ in title and political framing; neither text explains their precise relationship to Sihon.
- [[Reba]] — Named among the five chiefs of Midian, princes of Sihon, whom Moses struck (21). Numbers 31:8 calls the same five kings of Midian. The descriptions differ in title and political framing; neither text explains their precise relationship to Sihon.

## Places
- [[Aroer]] — Two bearings distinguish the Arnon-valley Aroer in Reuben’s portion (9, 16) from Aroer near Rabbah in Gad’s (25). Numbers 32:34 does not specify which Gad built; this page retains both notices without collapsing their sites.
- [[The Arnon]] — Its valley locates Aroer and the unnamed valley city at the start of the eastern register and Reuben’s grant (9, 16).
- [[Medeba]] — Its plain extends toward Dibon in the general register (9) and belongs to Reuben’s described portion (16).
- [[Dibon]] — Listed in Reuben’s portion (17), though Gad builds it in Numbers 32:34. Building and allotment are different actions, but neither account narrates a transfer; the attribution tension remains.
- [[Heshbon]] — Sihon’s royal center (10, 21), listed with Reuben’s cities (17) and used again as a starting point in Gad’s boundary (26). A boundary reference need not be an exclusive grant of the same city twice.
- [[Ammon]] — Gad’s portion includes half the land of the children of Ammon (25), whereas Deuteronomy 2:19 forbids its acquisition and 2:37 reports avoidance. Former Ammonite land held by Sihon is a possible explanation, but Joshua 13 does not state it. Both claims remain explicit.
- [[Gilead]] — Named in the overall eastern territory (11), Gad’s list (25), and the half-Gilead share of Machir’s descendants (31). The regional descriptions must be read with the division, not as exclusive grants of all Gilead to each group.
- [[Geshur]] — The eastern border population remains within Israel, not driven out (11, 13). The conquered-kingdom summary of verse 12 does not erase this explicit exception. Its relation to the southern Geshurites of 13:2 remains unspecified.
- [[Maacath]] — The border population remains within Israel with Geshur (11, 13). The explicit non-removal qualifies the preceding conquest summary.
- [[Hermon]] — All Mount Hermon appears in the general description of the eastern inheritance (11).
- [[Bashan]] — Og’s former kingdom extends to Salecah (11–12); the Manassite allocation includes all Bashan and sixty villages of Jair (30).
- [[Salecah]] — Marks the reach of Bashan in the eastern territorial summary (11).
- [[Ashtaroth]] — Og’s royal city (12), assigned with Edrei and half Gilead to half the children of Machir (31).
- [[Edrei]] — Og’s royal city (12), assigned with Ashtaroth to Machir’s descendants (31); no fresh battle is narrated.
- [[Baal Meon]] — Beth Baal Meon is listed in Reuben’s portion (17). This uses the spelling already held as an alias here; the text itself does not narrate the name changes mentioned in Numbers 32:38.
- [[Jahaz]] — The former battle setting in Numbers 21:23 is now listed among Reuben’s towns (18).
- [[Kedemoth]] — Listed among Reuben’s towns (18). Deuteronomy 2:26 names the wilderness of Kedemoth as the embassy’s departure point; the town and wilderness references are related bearings, not identical descriptions.
- [[Kiriathaim]] — Listed in Reuben’s portion (19), corresponding to its building by Reuben in Numbers 32:37.
- [[Sibmah]] — Listed in Reuben’s portion (19), corresponding to its building by Reuben in Numbers 32:38.
- [[Beth Peor]] — Listed in Reuben’s portion with Pisgah’s slopes and Beth Jeshimoth (20).
- [[Pisgah]] — Its slopes are included in Reuben’s territorial description (20), not a new ascent of its summit.
- [[Beth Jeshimoth]] — Listed in Reuben’s portion (20), alongside Beth Peor and Pisgah’s slopes.
- [[Midian]] — The five chiefs are recalled as princes of Sihon (21); Numbers 31:8 calls them kings of Midian. Their deaths are remembered within the land register, without a new battle narrative.
- [[The Jordan]] — Its bank bounds Reuben (23) and Gad (27); the allotments are explicitly eastward beyond it (8, 32).
- [[Jazer]] — Heads Gad’s territorial list (25), agreeing with its building by Gad in Numbers 32:35.
- [[Rabbah]] — Locates the Aroer in Gad’s boundary (25). The verse does not report Israel conquering Rabbah.
- [[Mahanaim]] — Marks a reach of Gad’s boundary (26) and the starting point of Manasseh’s description (30).
- [[Beth Haran]] — The WEB spelling Beth Haram occurs among Gad’s valley towns (27). The existing identification with Numbers 32:36’s Beth Haran rests on the paired Beth Nimrah and similar name; Joshua does not explicitly equate the spellings.
- [[Beth Nimrah]] — Named in Gad’s valley portion (27), corresponding to its building by Gad in Numbers 32:36.
- [[Succoth (Transjordan)]] — Named among Gad’s valley towns (27); this is the eastern Succoth, not the Egyptian departure station.
- [[The Sea of Chinnereth]] — Its extremity marks the northern reach of Gad’s Jordan-bank description (27).
- [[Havvoth Jair]] — The villages of Jair are sixty cities in Bashan within Manasseh’s portion (30). This confirms the Joshua notice already compared here; it does not resolve the differing later Jair account.
- [[Moab (place)]] — Its plains beyond the Jordan at Jericho are the remembered setting of Moses’ distribution (32).
- [[Jericho]] — Locates the plains east of the Jordan where Moses distributed the inheritances (32); the distribution is not placed inside Jericho.
- [[Bamoth Baal]] — Listed in Reuben’s portion between Dibon and Beth Baal Meon (17). The passage does not expressly identify it with Bamoth in Numbers 21:19 or the high places of Baal in Numbers 22:41; this page keeps the allotment name separate.
- [[Mephaath]] — Named among Reuben’s towns beside Jahaz and Kedemoth (18). No precise modern site is established here.
- [[Zereth Shahar]] — Named in Reuben’s portion, in the mount of the valley (19). The description is retained without a modern identification.
- [[Ramath Mizpeh]] — A boundary point in Gad’s portion, in the reach from Heshbon with Betonim (26). It is not silently identified with Ramoth or the Mizpah under Hermon.
- [[Betonim]] — A named point in Gad’s boundary alongside Ramath Mizpeh (26). No modern site is assigned.
- [[Debir (Gad)]] — The border of Debir is a terminus of Gad’s boundary from Mahanaim (26). Its eastern context is distinct from the southern Debir described with Hebron in Joshua 10:38–39. The verse does not identify its kind of settlement or a modern site.
- [[Zaphon]] — Named among Gad’s valley towns with Beth Haram, Beth Nimrah, and Succoth (27). No modern identification is assigned.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Moses’ eastern grants are recorded by families (15–32), yet Geshur and Maacath remain (13). Levi’s inheritance is described through offerings and Yahweh rather than a territorial share (14, 33). The Ammon wording in verse 25 remains in tension with Deuteronomy 2:19, 37.
- [[Priesthood]] — Levi receives no territorial share: offerings made by fire are its inheritance (14), and Yahweh is its inheritance (33). Compare Deuteronomy 18:1–2; the collective wording does not resolve Numbers 18’s distinctions between priests and other Levites.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — Offerings made by fire provide Levi’s inheritance (14), framing sacrifice as support alongside the divine-inheritance statement in verse 33. No new sacrificial ceremony is narrated.
- [[War]] — The register recalls the defeats of Sihon, Og, the Midianite chiefs, and Balaam (10–12, 21–22), but expressly records populations not driven out (13). Conquest summaries do not establish universal removal.
- [[The Nations]] — Geshur and Maacath remain within Israel (13). Ammon’s land appears in Gad’s portion (25), creating a territorial tension with the protected land in Deuteronomy 2:19, 37, not a narrated conquest of Rabbah.
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — Balaam is called the soothsayer in the WEB death notice (22). This designation stands beside his constrained blessing of Israel in Numbers 22–24; the notice gives no new oracle or account of his counsel.

## Notes

**Retrospective order.** The land was given by Moses, not newly conquered in this chapter.
Joshua 13:21 compresses Sihon and the Midianite chiefs into the register; Numbers 21 and 31
narrate their defeats separately. No simultaneous battle should be inferred from the summary.
The chiefs’ designation as princes of Sihon adds a political relationship absent from Numbers 31.

**Tensions retained.** Verse 12’s removal language is qualified immediately by verse 13.
Dibon was built by Gad (Numbers 32:34) but is listed for Reuben here (17); a later transfer is
possible, not narrated. Gad receives half Ammon’s land (25), whereas Deuteronomy 2:19, 37
protects it and reports Israel’s avoidance. The connection page preserves both claims without
making a conjectural territorial history into the text’s explanation.

**Names and groups.** Patriarchal pages carry tribal references with descendants explicitly
identified. Beor is only a patronymic; Jair is remembered through village names. The eastern
Debir is kept separate as [[Debir (Gad)]] from [[Debir (city)]], the southern conquest site.
Aroer’s existing page records its two explicitly different bearings. Bamoth Baal receives a
separate page because this text does not explicitly equate it with Bamoth or the high places
of Baal. Existing Baal Meon/Beth Baal Meon and Beth Haran/Beth Haram groupings are retained
with their inferential basis stated. The unnamed city in the Arnon valley receives no invented
proper name or individual page.

**Sources and scope.** Quotations use the stored WEB only. Comparisons use stored Numbers
21, 24:25, 31:8, 32; Deuteronomy 2:19, 37, 3:12–17, 18:1–2; and Joshua 10:38–39.
No new quotation from an unstored book, original-language assertion, or modern site identification
is introduced. The planned boundary is unchanged; chapter 14 begins the western distribution.
