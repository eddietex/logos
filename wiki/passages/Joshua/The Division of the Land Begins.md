# The Division of the Land Begins

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 14:1–5

## Summary

Eleazar the priest, Joshua, and the tribal household heads distribute the western inheritance
by lot under Yahweh’s command through Moses. The eastern two-and-a-half tribes already have
their grants. Joseph’s descendants count as two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim, while Levi receives
cities and surrounding land rather than a tribal territory. The paragraph closes by declaring
Israel’s compliance with the command.

This is the administrative introduction to the western allotments, not a separate battle or a
claim that every district is already occupied. The brief unit explains who distributes, how,
and why the western recipients number nine tribes and a half.

## Key verses

Full pericope, WEB, copied from the stored source:

> **1** These are the inheritances which the children of Israel took in the land of Canaan, which Eleazar the priest, Joshua the son of Nun, and the heads of the fathers’ houses of the tribes of the children of Israel, distributed to them,

> **2** by the lot of their inheritance, as Yahweh commanded by Moses, for the nine tribes, and for the half-tribe.

> **3** For Moses had given the inheritance of the two tribes and the half-tribe beyond the Jordan; but to the Levites he gave no inheritance among them.

> **4** For the children of Joseph were two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim. They gave no portion to the Levites in the land, except cities to dwell in, with their suburbs for their livestock and for their property.

> **5** The children of Israel did as Yahweh commanded Moses, and they divided the land.

## People
- [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]] — Eleazar is named first among the distributors, alongside Joshua and the heads of tribal fathers’ houses (1). The appointment in Numbers 34:17–29 now becomes an account of allocation; his priesthood includes this public responsibility.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — Joshua shares the distribution with Eleazar and tribal household heads (1). Leadership here means administering the inheritance by lot under the command transmitted through Moses, not acting as sole owner or distributor.
- [[Nun]] — Named only as Joshua’s father (1); the patronymic supplies identification, not an action or presence for Nun.
- [[Moses]] — His eastern grants stand, and his transmitted commands govern the western allotment (2–3, 5). Moses is remembered authority rather than a living participant.
- [[God]] — Yahweh’s command through Moses governs the lot and the people’s division of the land (2, 5). No new divine speech is narrated; the report presents obedience to an earlier command.
- [[Joseph]] — His descendants constitute two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim (4). This gives territorial expression to the sons’ standing in Genesis 48:5–6; Joseph himself is not present.
- [[Manasseh]] — The descendants are one of Joseph’s two tribes (4). With the eastern half already allotted in Joshua 13:29–31, the western division is for nine tribes and a half (2–3). The patriarch is not the recipient in person.
- [[Ephraim]] — The descendants form a tribe distinct from Manasseh (4). The count concerns territorial shares, not a ranking of the two brothers; their individual western boundaries are not supplied in this introduction.
- [[Levi]] — The descendants receive no tribal territorial portion, but do receive cities and surrounding land for livestock and property (3–4). This explicitly qualifies any description of Levi as having no land at all: lack of a tribal territory is compatible with housing and land use, as Numbers 35:1–8 already commands.

## Places
- [[Canaan]] — The western inheritance is located in the land of Canaan (1), distinguished from Moses’ grants beyond the Jordan (3). This is an allotment framework, not a new claim that all inhabitants have been removed.
- [[The Jordan]] — The river distinguishes the previously assigned eastern inheritance from the western distribution (3). No new crossing is narrated.

## Themes
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Nine tribes and a half receive the western inheritance by lot; Moses’ eastern grants remain in force (1–3). Levi’s cities and surrounding land qualify the denial of a territorial share (4). Allocation does not erase the remaining-land report of Joshua 13:1–7.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The report brackets distribution with Yahweh’s command through Moses (2, 5). The procedure, administrators, and provision for Levites recall Numbers 34:13–29 and 35:1–8. Obedience now takes administrative rather than military form.
- [[Priesthood]] — Eleazar the priest participates with Joshua and tribal heads in allocation (1). The Levites’ exclusion from tribal territory is paired with cities and land for livestock and property (3–4); the passage does not collapse the distinction between priests and other Levites.
- [[Adoption]] — Joseph’s two tribal branches (4) give territorial expression to the standing of Ephraim and Manasseh in Genesis 48:5–6. Joshua does not repeat the adoption scene or explicitly explain its motive; the connection is a comparison of the two passages.
- [[Providence]] — The land is distributed by lot as Yahweh commanded (2), while named leaders administer it (1). Read beside Proverbs 16:33, this joins divine direction with human responsibility; Joshua supplies no description of the physical lot procedure.

## Notes

**Territory and provision.** Verses 3–4 explicitly place the absence of a Levitical inheritance
beside the provision of cities and their suburbs. Reading the first claim as absolute
landlessness would contradict the next verse. The exception specifies the kind of property
provided; it does not give Levi a thirteenth tribal district.

**Collective names.** Joseph, Manasseh, Ephraim, and the Levites identify descendants and tribal
shares, not living patriarchs. Nun is only Joshua’s patronymic. The unnamed household heads
remain a group; this paragraph does not repeat the individual roster of Numbers 34.

**Summary and sequence.** Verse 5 summarizes obedience before the detailed western allotments.
It need not mean that all the later allocations happened before the next narrated scene.
Neither the mechanics of the lot nor a precise venue is specified in these five verses.
Caleb’s request begins a distinct scene at 14:6 and remains the next queued pericope.
