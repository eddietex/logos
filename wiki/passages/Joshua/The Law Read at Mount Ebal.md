# The Law Read at Mount Ebal

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 8:30–35

## Summary

After the capture of Ai, Joshua builds an altar to Yahweh on Mount Ebal according to Moses’
written instruction. Its stones are uncut and untouched by iron; burnt and peace offerings
are offered. Joshua writes a copy of Moses’ law on stones in Israel’s presence (30–32).

The assembly stands on both sides of the covenant ark before the Levitical priests, half
before Gerizim and half before Ebal. Officials do not monopolize the gathering: foreigners
stand with natives, and the reading expressly includes women and little ones (33–35).
Joshua reads the blessing and the curse, leaving out none of Moses’ commanded words.

The movement from battle to altar and instruction makes the conquest answerable to the law.
Joshua acts as Moses’ obedient successor, and the people who have entered the land must hear
the terms under which they live there.

## Key verses

Complete unit, copied from stored WEB:

> **8:30** Then Joshua built an altar to Yahweh, the God of Israel, on Mount Ebal,

> **8:31** as Moses the servant of Yahweh commanded the children of Israel, as it is written in the book of the law of Moses, an altar of uncut stones, on which no one had lifted up any iron. They offered burnt offerings on it to Yahweh and sacrificed peace offerings.

> **8:32** He wrote there on the stones a copy of Moses’ law, which he wrote in the presence of the children of Israel.

> **8:33** All Israel, their elders and officers, and their judges, stood on both sides of the ark before the priests the Levites, who carried the ark of Yahweh’s covenant, the foreigner as well as the native; half of them in front of Mount Gerizim, and half of them in front of Mount Ebal, as Moses the servant of Yahweh had commanded at the first, that they should bless the people of Israel.

> **8:34** Afterward he read all the words of the law, the blessing and the curse, according to all that is written in the book of the law.

> **8:35** There was not a word of all that Moses commanded, which Joshua didn’t read before all the assembly of Israel, with the women, the little ones, and the foreigners who were among them.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh receives the altar and burnt and peace offerings (30–31). His covenant ark stands before the assembled people (33); his instruction reaches the whole assembly through the law Moses commanded, rather than through a new speech in this unit.
- [[Moses]] — Although dead, Moses remains the named authority for the altar, the assembly, and the reading (31, 33, 35). Joshua’s actions enact the written instruction; Moses is not a participant physically present at Ebal.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — Joshua builds the altar, writes a copy of Moses’ law on stones, and reads it to the entire assembly (30–35). His leadership after Ai includes public instruction, not only military command; compare his charge in Joshua 1:7–8.
- The priests, elders, officers, judges, and assembly are collective participants; no additional individual is named.

## Notes

**Boundary.** Verses 30–35 form the altar-and-reading unit after Ai’s destruction and before
the kings’ response in chapter 9. The planned boundary is retained.

**Command and performance.** Deuteronomy 27:1–26 commands the ceremony; Joshua reports it.
Deuteronomy names tribes on each mountain and gives curses spoken by Levites with communal
responses. Joshua places the people before the mountains, names Joshua as reader, and summarizes
both blessing and curse. These differences should remain visible rather than be silently
harmonized. They do not explicitly contradict one another.

**What is not specified.** Joshua does not narrate plastering, eating, or rejoicing, though
Deuteronomy 27:2–8 prescribes them. Nor does it give a journey itinerary from Ai to Ebal,
the precise extent of the inscribed copy, or a modern textual identification of the book read.
Its emphasis on completeness (34–35) should not become an unsupported claim about inscription
size or the exact contents of a later canon. No Hebrew wording claim is made here.

**The foreigner’s inclusion.** Verses 33 and 35 explicitly include foreigners. They do not
identify those people as particular named households or erase the distinctions made in other
laws. The immediate claim is their presence at the law’s public reading.
