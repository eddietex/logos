# Joshua's Farewell to the Leaders

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 23:1–16

## Summary

*After many days*, with Israel at rest from its enemies, the aged Joshua summons all Israel
through its elders, heads, judges, and officers. He does not narrate a new battle. He looks back
on what Yahweh has done to the nations, *for it is Yahweh your God who has fought for you* (3).
He says he has allotted to the tribes both the land already taken and *these nations that
remain*, from the Jordan to the great sea, and he promises that Yahweh will drive the remaining
peoples out (4–5).

The rest of the speech is a charge with a warning attached. Israel is to be *very courageous* in
keeping the book of the law of Moses without turning right or left (6). It must keep apart from
the remaining nations and their gods: it must not mention their names, swear by them, serve
them, or bow to them. Instead it must *hold fast to Yahweh your God, as you have done to this
day* (7–8). Yahweh's past victories ground the promise that *one man of you shall chase a
thousand* (9–10), and the charge comes to its centre in *love Yahweh your God* (11).

If Israel instead clings to the remnant of these nations and intermarries with them, Yahweh will
stop driving them out. They will become *a snare and a trap*, *a scourge in your sides, and
thorns in your eyes*, until Israel perishes from the good land (12–13). Joshua closes by
announcing his own death and repeating the narrator's verdict of 21:45: not one good thing has
failed. He then turns that certainty around. The God whose good words all came true will just
as surely bring *all the evil things* if Israel breaks the covenant and serves other gods
(14–16).

## Key verses

> **23:3** You have seen all that Yahweh your God has done to all these nations because of you; for it is Yahweh your God who has fought for you.

> **23:6** “Therefore be very courageous to keep and to do all that is written in the book of the law of Moses, that you not turn away from it to the right hand or to the left;

> **23:7** that you not come among these nations, these that remain among you; neither make mention of the name of their gods, nor cause to swear by them, neither serve them, nor bow down yourselves to them;

> **23:8** but hold fast to Yahweh your God, as you have done to this day.

> **23:10** One man of you shall chase a thousand; for it is Yahweh your God who fights for you, as he spoke to you.

> **23:11** Take good heed therefore to yourselves, that you love Yahweh your God.

> **23:13** know for a certainty that Yahweh your God will no longer drive these nations from out of your sight; but they shall be a snare and a trap to you, a scourge in your sides, and thorns in your eyes, until you perish from off this good land which Yahweh your God has given you.

> **23:14** “Behold, today I am going the way of all the earth. You know in all your hearts and in all your souls that not one thing has failed of all the good things which Yahweh your God spoke concerning you. All have happened to you. Not one thing has failed of it.

> **23:15** It shall happen that as all the good things have come on you of which Yahweh your God spoke to you, so Yahweh will bring on you all the evil things, until he has destroyed you from off this good land which Yahweh your God has given you,

## People

- [[God]] — the one who has fought for Israel, will drive out the remaining nations, has kept
  every good word, and will as surely bring the evil ones on a covenant breach.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — old, *well advanced in years* (1–2), giving his first farewell
  address; he says he has allotted the land and is *going the way of all the earth* (14).
- [[Moses]] — named as the source of *the book of the law* (6).
- [[Jacob]] — *Israel* here is his descendants and their leaders, not the patriarch.

## Places

- [[Canaan]] — *this good land* (13, 15, 16), named three times as the land that can be lost.
- [[The Jordan]] — eastern limit of the allotment described in verse 4.
- [[The Great Sea]] — western limit, *toward the going down of the sun* (4).

## Themes

- [[Law and Obedience]] — *the book of the law of Moses*, kept without turning right or left.
- [[Love]] — the charge of verse 11, and holding fast in verse 8.
- [[Covenant]] — *the covenant of Yahweh your God* (16), whose breach brings loss of the land.
- [[Idolatry]] — mentioning, swearing by, serving, and bowing to the nations' gods.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — swearing by other gods forbidden (7).
- [[Marriage]] — intermarriage with the remaining nations as the path to apostasy (12).
- [[The Nations]] — the peoples who remain, as promised expulsion and as possible snare.
- [[War]] — Yahweh as the one who fights; one chasing a thousand.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — the good land given, allotted, and conditionally held.
- [[Blessing]] and [[Curse]] — the *good things* and the *evil things*, both from Yahweh's word.
- [[Judgment]] — perishing *quickly* from the good land (16).
- [[Life and Death]] — *the way of all the earth* (14).

## Notes

**Two farewells.** Joshua 23 and 24 are both final assemblies, and both open with Joshua calling
*the elders … heads … judges … officers* (23:2; 24:1, stored WEB). Chapter 23 has no stated place,
no history recited from Abraham, and no covenant ceremony. It is a personal charge to the
leaders, made of retrospect, promise, and warning. Chapter 24 is set at Shechem and ends in a
covenant and a written record. The two are kept as separate pericopes, as planned, because they
differ in setting and in form. The text does not say how much time lies between them.

**The old man and the remaining land.** Verse 1 repeats the words of 13:1, *Joshua was old and
well advanced in years*, and verse 2 has Joshua say them himself. In chapter 13 the phrase came
with Yahweh's notice of *very much land to be possessed*. Here it comes with *these nations that
remain* (4, 7, 12). Joshua does not present the remnant as a failure. He says he has already
allotted their land (4) and that Yahweh will drive them out (5). Whether they are in fact driven
out depends on Israel (12–13). That joins the two explanations recorded on
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]]: here Joshua himself sets a
promised expulsion beside a warning that it can stop.

**The same verb, two objects (in the WEB).** In the WEB, Israel is to *hold fast to Yahweh* (8)
and is warned against holding fast *to the remnant of these nations* (12). The English sets the
two loyalties against each other in a single verb. This page claims nothing about the Hebrew;
whether one Hebrew verb stands behind both verses was not checked in this pass.

**Four acts of allegiance, refused.** Verse 7 lists mentioning the gods' names, causing anyone to
swear by them, serving them, and bowing to them. The first two are acts of speech. Exodus 23:13
already forbade invoking other gods' names (stored WEB). Deuteronomy 6:13 and 10:20 tell Israel to
swear by Yahweh's name (stored WEB). Joshua's list brings the two together; see
[[Swear by His Name, Not Theirs - Exodus 23.13, Deuteronomy 6.13 and 10.20, and Joshua 23.7]].

**Thorns, transposed.** Numbers 33:55 warned that inhabitants left in the land would be *pricks
in your eyes and … thorns in your sides* (stored WEB). Joshua 23:13 has *a scourge in your sides,
and thorns in your eyes*. In the WEB the imagery is close but the body parts are rearranged. The
condition has also shifted. Numbers speaks of not driving the inhabitants out. Joshua speaks of
clinging to them and intermarrying with them, the danger Deuteronomy 7:3–4 names (stored WEB).

**Fulfillment as warning.** Verse 14 repeats 21:45's verdict twice: not one thing has failed.
Verse 15 then makes Yahweh's reliability a threat as well as a comfort. Every good word came
true, so every evil word will too. The repeated *good land … which Yahweh your God has given you*
(13, 15, 16) stresses that the land is a gift, and that what was given can be lost.
Deuteronomy 4:26 already spoke of perishing *from off the land* (stored WEB). The wiki does not
claim that Joshua quotes it. This unit says nothing on whether or when the warning is carried out.

**The way of all the earth.** Joshua describes his coming death as a road everyone walks. The
phrase recurs in David's charge to Solomon (1 Kings 2:2). It is cited here by reference only,
not quoted, because 1 Kings is not stored and was not checked in this pass. Joshua's death is
narrated at 24:29.

**Courage redefined.** Yahweh's charge in 1:7 is *be strong and very courageous* to keep the law
without turning right or left. Joshua passes the same charge on in 23:6. Here courage is given no
military object at all. The fighting, in this speech, is Yahweh's (3, 10).

6:13–14, 7:3–4, 10:20. 1 Kings 2:2 is cited by reference only. No source-language claim is made.
