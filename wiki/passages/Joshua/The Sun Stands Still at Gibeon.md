# The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 10:1–15

## Summary

Adoni-Zedek of Jerusalem fears the news of Ai’s destruction and Gibeon’s peace with Israel. He
recruits four other Amorite kings to attack Gibeon. Its inhabitants appeal to Joshua at Gilgal as
his servants, and Joshua brings the whole army in an overnight march. Yahweh promises victory and
throws the attackers into confusion. In the pursuit through Beth Horon toward Azekah and Makkedah,
hail kills more of the enemy than Israel’s swords.

Joshua publicly addresses sun and moon after speaking to Yahweh. The sun stands still and delays
setting about a whole day until the nation takes vengeance. The narrator cites the book of Jashar
and calls this day unparalleled because Yahweh listened to a man and fought for Israel. Verse 15
reports the return to Gilgal.

## Key verses

> **10:8** Yahweh said to Joshua, “Don’t fear them, for I have delivered them into your hands. Not a man of them will stand before you.”

> **10:11** As they fled from before Israel, while they were at the descent of Beth Horon, Yahweh hurled down great stones from the sky on them to Azekah, and they died. There were more who died from the hailstones than those whom the children of Israel killed with the sword.

> **10:12** Then Joshua spoke to Yahweh in the day when Yahweh delivered up the Amorites before the children of Israel. He said in the sight of Israel, “Sun, stand still on Gibeon! You, moon, stop in the valley of Aijalon!”

> **10:13** The sun stood still, and the moon stayed, until the nation had avenged themselves of their enemies. Isn’t this written in the book of Jashar? The sun stayed in the middle of the sky, and didn’t hurry to go down about a whole day.

> **10:14** There was no day like that before it or after it, that Yahweh listened to the voice of a man; for Yahweh fought for Israel.

## Notes

The boundary remains 10:1–15. The five kings’ hiding, execution, and burial belong to the next unit.
Their named identities warrant individual pages; the unnamed troops and envoys remain collective
actors.

**Treaty tension:** Joshua 9:14 criticizes the failure to consult Yahweh, and the treaty conflicts
with Deuteronomy 7:1–2. Joshua 10:8 explicitly promises help in the resulting battle. Both claims
stand: protection of the treaty partners now receives divine aid, without an explicit declaration
that making the original treaty was lawful.

**Sun and moon:** The WEB describes stopped movement and a delayed sunset, rather than merely a
metaphor for military success (12–13). It does not explain an astronomical mechanism. The book of
Jashar is cited as a source (13), but no independent copy is used here. The uniqueness statement in
verse 14 concerns this extraordinary response and battle; it should not be expanded into a denial
that God hears other prayers.

**Sequence tension:** Verse 15 returns Joshua and all Israel to Gilgal. Verses 16–27 then resume the
kings’ flight and operations at Makkedah, and verse 43 repeats the return. Reading verse 15 as an
anticipatory summary is possible; a smooth chronological reconstruction is not stated by the text.
This ingest preserves the notice and the tension.
