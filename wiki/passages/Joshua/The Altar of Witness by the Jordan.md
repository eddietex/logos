# The Altar of Witness by the Jordan

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 22:10–34

## Summary

On their way home, Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh build *a great altar to look at* by the
Jordan, in the region the WEB places *in the land of Canaan* (10–11). The western tribes hear of
it and gather at Shiloh *to go up against them to war* (12). Before any fighting, they send
Phinehas son of Eleazar the priest and ten princes, one for each western tribe, into Gilead (13–14).

The delegation speaks for *the whole congregation of Yahweh* (16). It calls the altar a trespass
and a rebellion, and it recalls Peor and Achan. In both of those cases one party's sin brought
wrath on the whole congregation (17–20). The delegation also makes an offer. If the eastern land
is unclean, those tribes may cross over and take possession beside the western tribes, *in which
Yahweh's tabernacle dwells* (19).

The eastern tribes answer with a doubled oath, *The Mighty One, God, Yahweh* (22). They deny
building the altar for burnt offering or sacrifice. They built it from fear that a later
generation would say *You have no portion in Yahweh*, with the Jordan as the border between them
(24–25). The altar is to be *a witness between us and you* and *the pattern of Yahweh's altar*
(27–28). It is not a second place of sacrifice (29). Phinehas accepts the answer: *Today we know
that Yahweh is among us* (31). The delegation reports back. Israel blesses God and drops the
plan for war (32–33). Reuben and Gad name the altar *A Witness Between Us that Yahweh is God*
(34).

> **22:25** For Yahweh has made the Jordan a border between us and you, you children of Reuben and children of Gad. You have no portion in Yahweh.”’ So your children might make our children cease from fearing Yahweh.

> **22:28** “Therefore we said, ‘It shall be, when they tell us or our generations this in time to come, that we shall say, “Behold the pattern of Yahweh’s altar, which our fathers made, not for burnt offering, nor for sacrifice; but it is a witness between us and you.”’

> **22:31** Phinehas the son of Eleazar the priest said to the children of Reuben, to the children of Gad, and to the children of Manasseh, “Today we know that Yahweh is among us, because you have not committed this trespass against Yahweh. Now you have delivered the children of Israel out of Yahweh’s hand.”

> **22:34** The children of Reuben and the children of Gad named the altar “A Witness Between Us that Yahweh is God.”

- [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]] — named only as Phinehas's father, with the title *the priest* (13, 31).
- [[Reuben]], [[Gad]], and [[Manasseh]] — their descendants build the altar and answer the charge.

- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — not named anywhere in 10–34. The embassy, not the leader, settles the
  dispute.

## Notes

**A great altar to look at.** Verse 10 describes the altar by how it is seen. The WEB places it
*in the land of Canaan* (10) and *on the side that belongs to the children of Israel* (11). The
eastern tribes are on their way to Gilead, and the delegation has to travel *into the land of
Gilead* to find them (13, 15). Read in the WEB, the altar stands on the bank its builders are
leaving, facing the tribes they fear being cut off from. The text does not say why it was put
there. The WEB's location wording is followed here, and the wiki makes no claim about the
Hebrew prepositions of verse 11.

**Inquiry before the sword.** The congregation gathers *to go up against them to war* (12). It
does not march. It sends a priest and ten princes, one for each western tribe, to speak first.
This matches the order of Deuteronomy 13:12–15. When a city is reported to have turned to
other gods, the command comes before the sword: *you shall inquire, and make search, and ask
diligently* (14). Joshua 22 does not cite that law, and the altar was not charged with serving other gods. The resemblance is
procedural, and the wiki does not call it a fulfillment.

**The charge: one altar, one congregation.** The delegation's case rests on Deuteronomy 12. It
treats an altar *other than Yahweh our God's altar* (19) as rebellion against Yahweh and against
Israel. It also argues from corporate liability. Peor's iniquity is one *from which we have not
cleansed ourselves to this day* (17), and Achan *didn't perish alone in his iniquity* (20). If the
eastern tribes rebel *today*, Yahweh will be angry *tomorrow* with *the whole congregation* (18).
The same fear drove Moses' rebuke in Numbers 32:14–15, where turning away would *increase the
fierce anger of Yahweh toward Israel* and *destroy all these people*.

**A contradiction to keep in view: Peor still uncleansed?** In verse 17 the delegation says
Israel has *not cleansed* itself of Peor's iniquity *to this day*, *although there came a
plague*. Numbers 25:8, 11 says Phinehas' act *stayed* the plague and *turned [Yahweh's] wrath
away*, and Deuteronomy 4:4 tells the survivors that those faithful at Peor *are all alive
today*. These are different claims. Numbers and Deuteronomy describe the plague stopped and the
faithful spared, and Joshua 22:17 describes guilt still lingering. The text does not say what
uncleansed iniquity remains. The speaker is also the priest who ended the plague, so his own
reckoning keeps the guilt open. The wiki records both claims side by side and does not reconcile
them.

**The offer of verse 19.** *If the land of your possession is unclean* is the only place in this
pericope where the eastern land is treated as possibly less than the land of Yahweh's
possession. The offer is generous: the western tribes would make room *among us*. It also states
the very view the eastern tribes fear their grandchildren will hear. The wiki does not decide
whether the delegation actually held that view or only allowed for it.

**The defense: a copy, not a rival.** The answer begins with a doubled oath (22). The WEB renders
it as the same triple title, *The Mighty One, God, Yahweh*, twice. The wiki makes no claim about
the Hebrew form of the divine names here. The tribes say the altar is *the pattern of Yahweh's
altar* (28). It copies the one at the tabernacle so that it points to that one, and it is not
for sacrifice. Their fear concerns the next generations. Children might say *Yahweh has made the
Jordan a border*, and so make *our children cease from fearing Yahweh* (25). Joshua 4 set up
stones so that children would ask and hear what Yahweh did. This altar is built for a question
the builders are afraid of, and its answer is meant to keep their children inside the
congregation. See [[When Your Children Ask - Exodus, Deuteronomy, and Joshua]].

**Who speaks for Israel.** Joshua is not named in this unit. He dismissed the eastern tribes in
22:6, and the dispute is handled by the congregation and its delegation. Phinehas speaks the
verdict: *Today we know that Yahweh is among us* (31). He treats the absence of trespass as
evidence of Yahweh's presence, the reverse of Joshua 7, where defeat at Ai revealed a trespass in
the camp. *Now you have delivered the children of Israel out of Yahweh's hand* credits the
eastern tribes with averting wrath. At Peor, Phinehas averted it himself by an act of zeal (see
[[Two Priests Stop the Plague - Aaron and Phinehas]]). Here the wrath never comes, because there
was nothing to punish.

**The name, and who gives it.** Verse 34 names only Reuben and Gad as giving the name.
Manasseh's half-tribe, present everywhere else in the unit, is not mentioned. Verse 30 likewise
says *the children of Manasseh* where earlier verses say *the half-tribe*. The text does not
explain either variation. Translations differ in how they render the naming in verse 34. This
page follows the WEB wording, *A Witness Between Us that Yahweh is God*, and makes no claim about
the underlying Hebrew. The witness formula itself, *a witness between us and you*, is the
language of Laban and Jacob's heap at Galeed in Gilead (Genesis 31:44, 48). See
[[A Witness Between Us - Galeed, the Jordan Altar, and the Shechem Stone]].

**What the book leaves open.** The dispute ends in agreement, and this pericope says nothing
more about the altar. The question it raises, whether a tribe beyond the Jordan has a *portion
in Yahweh*, recurs in later books. See
[[The Two and a Half Tribes - Numbers 32, Deuteronomy 3, and Joshua 1, 4, 22]]. Joshua's
farewell (23:1–16) is the next unit. The planned boundary at 22:34 is kept.
