# The Whole Land Taken

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 11:16–23

## Summary

The narrator steps back from single battles and summarizes the conquest of the land west of
the Jordan. Joshua takes the hill country, the South, the land of Goshen, the lowland, the
Arabah, and the hill country of Israel, from Mount Halak, which goes up toward Seir, to Baal
Gad in the valley of Lebanon under Mount Hermon (16–17). He takes and kills their kings, and
the war lasts *a long time* (18).

No city makes peace with Israel except the Hivites of Gibeon; every other city is taken in
battle (19). The narrator gives the reason: Yahweh hardened their hearts to come against
Israel, so that they would be destroyed without favor, as Yahweh commanded Moses (20).

Joshua then cuts off the Anakim from Hebron, Debir, Anab, and the hill country of Judah and
of Israel, destroying them with their cities. None remain in Israel's land, except some in
Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod (21–22). The unit closes with a double verdict: Joshua took the whole
land as Yahweh spoke to Moses and gave it to Israel by tribes, and *the land had rest from war*
(23).

## Key verses

> **11:18** Joshua made war a long time with all those kings.

> **11:19** There was not a city that made peace with the children of Israel, except the Hivites, the inhabitants of Gibeon. They took all in battle.

> **11:20** For it was of Yahweh to harden their hearts, to come against Israel in battle, that he might utterly destroy them, that they might have no favor, but that he might destroy them, as Yahweh commanded Moses.

> **11:22** There were none of the Anakim left in the land of the children of Israel. Only in Gaza, in Gath, and in Ashdod, did some remain.

> **11:23** So Joshua took the whole land, according to all that Yahweh spoke to Moses; and Joshua gave it for an inheritance to Israel according to their divisions by their tribes. The land had rest from war.

## Notes

**A summary, not a new campaign.** The first half of Joshua has narrated battles one at a time.
Here the narrator draws them together and names the land's regions in a sweep: the southern
hill country and the South, the Goshen already named in 10:41, the lowland and the Arabah, and
the hill country of Israel. The pair of limits in verse 17 runs from Mount Halak in the south
to Baal Gad under Hermon in the north. Joshua 12:7 repeats the same pair in reverse order, and
13:5 places Baal Gad under Hermon again as the start of land still remaining. The summary's
northern limit is therefore also the edge of what 13:1–6 says has not yet been taken.

**"A long time."** Verse 18 corrects any impression, left by chapters 10–11, that the conquest
took a few days. The text gives no figure. Readers often infer about seven years by comparing
Caleb's ages in 14:7 and 14:10 — forty when sent from Kadesh Barnea, eighty-five *today*, after
*forty-five years* — and subtracting the wilderness years. That is an inference from a
chronology the text does not lay out, not a statement of any verse.

**Peace and hardening.** Verse 19 states that no city made peace except Gibeon, which made it
by deception (9:3–15). Verse 20 explains why, and the explanation is theological: it was *of
Yahweh to harden their hearts*. The kings' refusal belongs to them and to Yahweh at once, as with
Pharaoh in Exodus and Sihon in Deuteronomy 2:30. The purpose clause, *that they might have no
favor*, recalls Deuteronomy 7:2, *nor show mercy to them*; whether the Hebrew of the two verses
shares a root was not checked in this pass, and the wiki makes no claim about it. The text does
not say whether a Canaanite city that sought peace would have received it. Deuteronomy 20:10–18
reserves the offer of peace for cities *very far off*, which is the category Gibeon pretended to
belong to. See [[No City Made Peace - Deuteronomy 20 and Joshua 11.19-20]].

The verse makes divine agency the reason for destruction without favor. The wiki records it as
the text states it and does not soften the ethical weight of a hardening that leads to killing,
nor reduce it to foreknowledge.

**The Anakim.** The people whose size stopped Israel at Kadesh (Numbers 13:28, 33; Deuteronomy
1:28; 9:2) are now *cut off* from Hebron, Debir, and Anab — the place of the old fear. Two later
notices tell it differently: Caleb drives out the three sons of Anak at Hebron (15:13–14), and
Othniel takes Debir (15:15–17). Joshua 14:12 has Caleb still speak of the Anakim as *there*. The
three accounts are kept side by side; see
[[Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1]]. The remnant in Gaza, Gath, and
Ashdod ties the summary to the Philistine cities that 13:2–3 lists as remaining, and Gath is the
home of Goliath in 1 Samuel 17 (not yet ingested; reference only). See
[[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]].

**Judah and Israel.** Verse 21 distinguishes *the hill country of Judah* from *the hill country
of Israel*. In the narrative's time this is a regional description, and Judah is the tribe whose
allotment is described first (chapter 15). Some readers see the later division of the kingdom
reflected in the phrase; that is an interpretation of the book's composition, not something the
verse states.

**The double verdict.** Verse 23 is one of Joshua's strongest statements of completion: *the
whole land*, *according to all that Yahweh spoke to Moses*, and *rest from war*. The next words
of Yahweh, at 13:1, are *there remains yet very much land to be possessed*. The summary's
*whole land* and the later list of land remaining both stand in the book. Verse 23 already
looks forward: the inheritance by tribes is the work of chapters 13–21, and *the land had rest
from war* recurs at 14:15. The verb there is a different word from Deuteronomy's *rest from all
your enemies*; see
[[Rest from All Your Enemies - Deuteronomy 12, Joshua 1, 21-23, and Hebrews 4]].

**Boundary.** The planned 11:16–23 unit is retained. Chapter 12's list of kings has its own
opening formula (*Now these are the kings of the land*) and remains the next unit.

**Names and geography.** Mount Halak, Baal Gad, Anab, Gath, and Ashdod receive new place pages;
no modern site identification is asserted for any of them. *Seir* is linked to [[Edom]], whose
page carries Seir and Mount Seir as aliases; the verse names the direction of the ascent, not
Edomite territory taken. *The South* is linked to [[The Negev]], which carries *the South* as an
alias. The hill country, lowland, and the hill country of Judah and of Israel remain descriptive
terms without pages. The Hivites of Gibeon are treated on [[Gibeon]] and [[The Nations]].
