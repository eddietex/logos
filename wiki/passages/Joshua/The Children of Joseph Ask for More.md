# The Children of Joseph Ask for More

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 17:14–18

## Summary

The children of Joseph ask Joshua why their numerous, divinely blessed people have received
only one lot and portion. Joshua answers that their numbers equip them to clear forest in the
land of the Perizzites and Rephaim. They object that the hill country is insufficient and
that the Canaanites of Beth Shean and the Jezreel valley possess iron chariots.

Joshua addresses Ephraim and Manasseh together: their strength means the forested hill country
and its farthest extent can be theirs, and strong Canaanites can be driven out. The scene ends
with an assurance and a task, not a further allotment ceremony or a narrated victory.

## Key verses

Full pericope, copied from the stored WEB:

> **14** The children of Joseph spoke to Joshua, saying, “Why have you given me just one lot and one part for an inheritance, since we are a numerous people, because Yahweh has blessed us so far?”
> **15** Joshua said to them, “If you are a numerous people, go up to the forest, and clear land for yourself there in the land of the Perizzites and of the Rephaim; since the hill country of Ephraim is too narrow for you.”
> **16** The children of Joseph said, “The hill country is not enough for us. All the Canaanites who dwell in the land of the valley have chariots of iron, both those who are in Beth Shean and its towns, and those who are in the valley of Jezreel.”
> **17** Joshua spoke to the house of Joseph, that is, to Ephraim and to Manasseh, saying, “You are a numerous people, and have great power. You shall not have one lot only;
> **18** but the hill country shall be yours. Although it is a forest, you shall cut it down, and it’s farthest extent shall be yours; for you shall drive out the Canaanites, though they have chariots of iron, and though they are strong.”

## Notes

- **One lot, two tribes:** The complaint in verse 14 stands beside separate tribal inheritances
  in chapters 16–17 and the two-tribe explanation in 14:4. It voices the house’s assessment of
  inadequate room; it does not erase those distinctions or give the mechanics of the lots.
- **Promise and non-removal:** Verse 18’s assurance stands in tension with 17:12–13’s inability
  and subsequent forced labor. Neither statement should be rewritten as the other. No action
  following this conversation is supplied here.
- **Iron chariots:** The WEB identifies the equipment as iron chariots; it does not specify their
  construction, dimensions, tactics, or number. No archaeological reconstruction is assumed.
- **Geography and peoples:** Jezreel’s valley is distinct from the town named in Judah’s list
  (15:56). The forest is named by its peoples, without a mapped extent. The Rephaim designation
  does not itself say whether surviving members are encountered here.
- **Scope and sources:** Queue boundary unchanged. All quotations come directly from stored WEB
  Joshua 17:14–18; comparisons use stored Joshua 14:4, 6–15; 16:1–10; 17:1–13 and
  Deuteronomy 20:1–4. No new external quotation or source-language assertion is made.
