# The Second Circumcision at Gilgal

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 5:1–9

## Summary

The kings west of the Jordan hear that Yahweh dried the river for Israel and lose courage.
At this moment Yahweh orders Joshua to make flint knives and circumcise Israel again. Joshua
obeys at the hill of the foreskins. The narrator explains the command through the change of
generations: the males who left Egypt were circumcised, but those born on the wilderness
journey were not. The disobedient military generation died during the forty years, excluded
from the land that Yahweh had sworn to give the fathers.

Joshua circumcises their children, and the people remain in camp until healed. Yahweh declares
that he has rolled away Egypt’s reproach; the narrator connects that declaration with the
name Gilgal. The movement after crossing is first toward restored observance, not battle.

## Notes

**Second time, different recipients.** Verses 5 and 7 interpret verse 2: children who lacked the
sign receive it. This is not a repeated operation on the same bodies. Joshua’s attribution as
the one who circumcises need not be expanded into an unsupported account of assistants or
an assertion that he personally operated on every individual.

**The generation and its exceptions.** The sweeping language of verses 4–6 concerns the men
of war. Numbers 14:29–30 and 26:64–65 explicitly preserve Joshua and Caleb as exceptions.
Joshua himself is alive in this scene. Reading the summary as every exodus participant dying
would contradict those texts. The circumcised fathers’ disobedience also shows that having the
sign was no guarantee of entrance.

**An unexplained omission and reproach.** The text does not explain why circumcision went
unperformed during the journey. Nor does verse 9 define Egypt’s reproach precisely. Former
slavery or disgrace associated with wilderness failure are possible readings, not established
definitions. The reference to rolling away explains the name within the narrative; no
independently verified Hebrew etymology is asserted here.

**After crossing, before Passover.** Joshua 4:19 already locates the camp west of the Jordan.
The rite is not before geographic entry into the land. The following pericope,
[[Passover at Gilgal and the End of the Manna]] (5:10–12), narrates the feast and change of food. Verse 1 bridges the
crossing and the present pause; the planned 5:1–9 boundary is retained.

**Correction to earlier synthesis.** The former wording in [[Circumcision]] and
[[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]] described the exodus generation as uncircumcised
or put this rite before entry. Joshua 5:5–7 and 4:19 contradict that wording; those pages are
corrected in this pass, with the conflict recorded rather than silently overwritten.
