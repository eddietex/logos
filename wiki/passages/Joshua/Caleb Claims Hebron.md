# Caleb Claims Hebron

**Book:** [[Joshua]]
**Reference:** Joshua 14:6–15

## Summary

At Gilgal, Caleb recalls the promise made at Kadesh Barnea and asks Joshua for the hill country.
He was forty when sent to spy out the land and is now eighty-five, preserved by Yahweh for
forty-five years. The fortified cities and Anakim that frightened the other scouts become
the inheritance he requests. Joshua blesses him and grants Hebron; the narrator connects
the continuing grant with Caleb’s wholehearted loyalty and closes with rest from war.

## Key verses

Full pericope, WEB, copied from the stored source:

> **6** Then the children of Judah came near to Joshua in Gilgal. Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite said to him, “You know the thing that Yahweh spoke to Moses the man of God concerning me and concerning you in Kadesh Barnea.

> **7** I was forty years old when Moses the servant of Yahweh sent me from Kadesh Barnea to spy out the land. I brought him word again as it was in my heart.

> **8** Nevertheless, my brothers who went up with me made the heart of the people melt; but I wholly followed Yahweh my God.

> **9** Moses swore on that day, saying, ‘Surely the land where you walked shall be an inheritance to you and to your children forever, because you have wholly followed Yahweh my God.’

> **10** “Now, behold, Yahweh has kept me alive, as he spoke, these forty-five years, from the time that Yahweh spoke this word to Moses, while Israel walked in the wilderness. Now, behold, I am eighty-five years old, today.

> **11** As yet I am as strong today as I was in the day that Moses sent me. As my strength was then, even so is my strength now for war, to go out and to come in.

> **12** Now therefore give me this hill country, of which Yahweh spoke in that day; for you heard in that day how the Anakim were there, and great and fortified cities. It may be that Yahweh will be with me, and I shall drive them out, as Yahweh said.”

> **13** Joshua blessed him; and he gave Hebron to Caleb the son of Jephunneh for an inheritance.

> **14** Therefore Hebron became the inheritance of Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite to this day, because he followed Yahweh, the God of Israel wholeheartedly.

> **15** Now the name of Hebron before was Kiriath Arba, after the greatest man among the Anakim. Then the land had rest from war.

## People
- [[Caleb (son of Jephunneh)]] — At eighty-five Caleb asks for the hill country promised forty-five years earlier. He credits Yahweh with preserving his life, recalls his faithful report, and receives Hebron from Joshua (6–14). His claimed strength serves a still-future campaign; this scene grants land without narrating its capture.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — Caleb appeals to Joshua as a witness to the promise made through Moses. Joshua blesses him and grants Hebron (6, 13). The earlier removal of the Anakim credited to Joshua in 11:21–22 remains in tension with Caleb’s proposed expulsion in 14:12 and action in 15:14.
- [[Moses]] — Caleb recalls Moses sending him from Kadesh Barnea at forty and swearing that the land he walked would belong to him and his children (7–9). Moses acts through a remembered oath, not as a living participant.
- [[God]] — Caleb attributes his survival across forty-five years to Yahweh’s promise (10). He asks for the difficult hill country in dependence on Yahweh’s presence (12); the narrator confirms his wholehearted following (14). Preservation and promised inheritance accompany further action.
- [[Judah]] — The children of Judah approach Joshua at Gilgal with Caleb (6). This is the tribe descended from Judah, not the patriarch in person; Caleb’s individual inheritance stands within this tribal setting.
- [[The Ten Spies]] — Caleb remembers the fellow scouts who made the people’s heart melt (8), contrasting their discouragement with his own faithful report. They are recalled collectively and are not present at Gilgal; Joshua is Caleb’s addressee, not one of the opposing scouts.
- [[The Anakim]] — Caleb recalls their fortified hill country and proposes to drive them out (12). Verse 15 associates Hebron’s former name Kiriath Arba with their greatest man. Joshua 11:21–22 reports their prior removal from Hebron, while 15:14 narrates Caleb’s expulsion: the tension is retained rather than resolved by an invented return.

## Places
- [[Gilgal]] — The children of Judah approach Joshua here, and Caleb presents his claim (6). This is the meeting’s location, distinct from Hebron, the inheritance requested and granted.
- [[Kadesh]] — Kadesh Barnea is recalled as the place of Caleb’s commission and the promise concerning Caleb and Joshua (6–7). The present meeting occurs at Gilgal, forty-five years after the remembered promise.
- [[Hebron]] — Joshua gives Hebron to Caleb and blesses him (13–14). Its former name Kiriath Arba recalls the greatest man among the Anakim (15). The grant is narrated here; Caleb’s expulsion follows in 15:14. Earlier conquest reports in 10:36–37 and 11:21–22 remain in tension with the further campaign.

## Themes
- [[Faith]] — Caleb contrasts the discouraging report with his own loyalty and requests the very hill country remembered for formidable inhabitants (7–12). His confidence includes dependence on Yahweh’s presence rather than a claim that age or strength guarantees victory.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The promised land becomes Caleb’s named inheritance, Hebron, for him and his children (9, 13–14). Legal allocation and actual removal of inhabitants are distinguished: the latter is still proposed in verse 12.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Caleb’s wholehearted following is stated by Caleb, in Moses’ recalled oath, and by the narrator (8–9, 14). The passage confirms this assessment rather than leaving it solely as Caleb’s self-description.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — Moses’ remembered oath grounds Caleb’s request for the land he walked and an inheritance for his children (9). Joshua’s grant in verses 13–14 gives the old promise a named territorial expression.
- [[War]] — Caleb claims continuing strength for war and anticipates driving out the Anakim (11–12), yet verse 15 says the land had rest from war. Rest cannot mean that no local fighting remains; Joshua 15:14 will narrate the expulsion.
- [[Providence]] — Caleb attributes forty-five years of survival to Yahweh’s keeping him alive as promised (10). He treats preserved life as capacity for the remaining task, not merely as an occasion for retirement.
- [[Remembrance]] — Caleb’s appeal makes the commission, failed report, and oath at Kadesh govern the present allocation at Gilgal (6–10). Remembering here creates a claim on present leadership.
- [[Blessing]] — Joshua blesses Caleb and gives him Hebron (13). The narrator reports the blessing without preserving its words; the inheritance enacts the favorable recognition.
- [[Names and Naming]] — Verse 15 supplies Hebron’s former name, Kiriath Arba, and associates it with the greatest man among the Anakim. It does not narrate when the name changed or identify who changed it.

## Notes

**Promise, grant, and possession.** Numbers 14:24 and Deuteronomy 1:36 promise Caleb and his
offspring the land he entered or trod. Joshua 14:9 presents this as Moses’ oath, and 14:13–14
names Hebron as the grant. These are related presentations, not identical verbatim speeches.
The conquest itself is not narrated in this unit.

**Explicit narrative tension.** Joshua 11:21–22 reports Joshua cutting off the Anakim from
Hebron and leaving none in Israel’s land. Here Caleb asks to drive them out (14:12); 15:14
credits him with doing so. A general summary followed by a local account is a possible
reading, but the text does not explain the chronology or describe a return of the Anakim.
Likewise the rest from war in 14:15 stands beside fighting still anticipated. Neither claim
is erased.

**Ages and attribution.** Forty, forty-five, and eighty-five are Caleb’s own figures (7, 10).
They do not supply an absolute date or independently date every campaign. His request
acknowledges dependence on Yahweh even while asserting strength. His loyalty receives the
narrator’s confirmation in verse 14.

**Names and groups.** Jephunneh is a patronymic, not an actor in this scene. The Kenizzite
designation is retained without inferring an otherwise unstated genealogy. Arba remains
covered on the Anakim page, where he is already included; Judah denotes descendants.
No modern site identification or Hebrew lexical claim is added.
