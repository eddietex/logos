# Clean and Unclean Animals

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 11:1–47

## Summary

Yahweh addresses Moses and Aaron together and commands them to teach Israel which creatures
may be eaten. Land animals require both a divided hoof and cud-chewing; aquatic creatures
require fins and scales. A list excludes particular birds, while specified hopping insects
are permitted. The chapter then regulates contact with carcasses, the contamination of
household objects and food, and exceptions for water sources and dry sowing seed.

Even an animal permitted for food can transmit uncleanness when it dies. Touching, carrying,
and eating carcasses are distinguished, with washing and uncleanness until evening specified
in the relevant cases. The conclusion grounds these distinctions in Yahweh's holiness and
his bringing Israel out of Egypt to be their God (44–47).

## Key verses

> **11:1** Yahweh spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying to them,

> **11:3** Whatever parts the hoof, and is cloven-footed, and chews the cud among the animals, that you may eat.

> **11:9** “‘These you may eat of all that are in the waters: whatever has fins and scales in the waters, in the seas, and in the rivers, that you may eat.

> **11:24** “‘By these you will become unclean: whoever touches their carcass shall be unclean until the evening.

> **11:36** Nevertheless a spring or a cistern in which water is gathered shall be clean: but that which touches their carcass shall be unclean.

> **11:39** “‘If any animal, of which you may eat, dies; he who touches its carcass shall be unclean until the evening.

> **11:40** He who eats of its carcass shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the evening. He also who carries its carcass shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the evening.

> **11:44** For I am Yahweh your God. Sanctify yourselves therefore, and be holy; for I am holy. You shall not defile yourselves with any kind of creeping thing that moves on the earth.

> **11:45** For I am Yahweh who brought you up out of the land of Egypt, to be your God. You shall therefore be holy, for I am holy.

> **11:46** “‘This is the law of the animal, and of the bird, and of every living creature that moves in the waters, and of every creature that creeps on the earth,

> **11:47** to make a distinction between the unclean and the clean, and between the living thing that may be eaten and the living thing that may not be eaten.’”

## Notes

### Food permission is not immunity from impurity

Verses 3–23 sort edible and prohibited creatures. Verses 24–40 regulate carcass contact
and its consequences. These axes overlap without being identical: verses 39–40 expressly
include a dead animal of a kind allowed for food. Carrying its carcass or eating from it
requires washing clothes and waiting until evening; touching it brings uncleanness until
evening. The chapter does not prescribe a sacrifice for these cases.

**Tension with Leviticus 5:2–6:** there, hidden contact with an unclean carcass leads to guilt,
confession, and an offering. Here, contact brings time-limited impurity and the specified
washing. Neither passage explains their full relation. This page retains both rules rather
than inserting an unstated sanctuary entry or assuming every contact requires sacrifice.
Ritual uncleanness is not a synonym for wrongdoing, but the commands about it still bind Israel.

### Households, vessels, and exceptions

Verses 29–38 extend the concern beyond meals. Dead creeping creatures can contaminate
wooden vessels, clothing, skin, sacks, pottery, food, and drink. Washable objects go into
water and remain unclean until evening; earthen vessels are broken, as are affected ovens
and ranges. Springs and cisterns remain clean, though contact with the carcass still
defiles (36). Dry sowing seed remains clean, whereas seed on which water has been put is
unclean after carcass contact (37–38). The text supplies concrete differences rather than
a rule that anything near death must be destroyed.

### Classification and explanation

The WEB calls the cony and hare cud-chewers (5–6), places the bat at the end of the bird
list (19), and describes winged creeping things with four-footed language (20–23).
Those descriptions are preserved as the source's classifications; this page neither
silently modernizes them nor supplies an unverified Hebrew explanation. The species
names are the WEB's renderings, not independently established zoological identifications.

The chapter states a theological rationale: Yahweh is holy and brought Israel out of Egypt
to be their God. It does not offer a medical explanation for each animal or prescribe
an allegory of its traits. Such explanations would require evidence beyond this passage.

### Distinctions within a delivered people

The closing purpose of distinguishing clean from unclean (47) follows Aaron's charge in
10:10–11. Moses and Aaron now teach the whole people. Holiness consequently reaches
ordinary eating and household handling as well as priestly service.

Genesis 9:3 grants Noah every moving living thing as food, with the blood restriction of
9:4; Leviticus 11 limits Israel's food by kind. The different addressees are explicit,
but the texts do not themselves explain the complete relationship of those permissions.
The connection page records this tension without erasing either statement.
