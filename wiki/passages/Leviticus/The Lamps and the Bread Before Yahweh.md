# The Lamps and the Bread Before Yahweh

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 24:1–9

## Summary

Yahweh directs Moses to command Israel to supply pure beaten olive oil. Aaron must tend
the lamps outside the veil in the Tent of Meeting, from evening to morning. The people
provide the material; the priest maintains its ordered use before Yahweh (1–4).

Twelve cakes of fine flour, each containing two tenths of an ephah, are arranged in two
rows of six on the gold table. Pure frankincense accompanies each row as the bread's
memorial offering. The bread is set in order every Sabbath as an everlasting covenant
on Israel's behalf, then belongs to Aaron and his sons to eat in a holy place (5–9).

After the annual calendar, the focus returns to recurring sanctuary service: nightly
light and weekly bread. These are commands for continuing practice, not an account of
a particular performance.

## Key verses

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **2** “Command the children of Israel, that they bring to you pure olive oil beaten for the light, to cause a lamp to burn continually.

> **3** Outside of the veil of the Testimony, in the Tent of Meeting, shall Aaron keep it in order from evening to morning before Yahweh continually: it shall be a statute forever throughout your generations.

> **4** He shall keep in order the lamps on the pure gold lamp stand before Yahweh continually.

> **5** “You shall take fine flour, and bake twelve cakes of it: two tenths of an ephah shall be in one cake.

> **6** You shall set them in two rows, six on a row, on the pure gold table before Yahweh.

> **7** You shall put pure frankincense on each row, that it may be to the bread for a memorial, even an offering made by fire to Yahweh.

> **8** Every Sabbath day he shall set it in order before Yahweh continually. It is an everlasting covenant on the behalf of the children of Israel.

> **9** It shall be for Aaron and his sons; and they shall eat it in a holy place: for it is most holy to him of the offerings of Yahweh made by fire by a perpetual statute.”

## Notes

**Continuity requires maintenance.** The continual lamp has an explicit evening-to-morning
schedule (3); the continual bread has an explicit Sabbath renewal (8). These English
formulations do not settle all questions about daytime burning. Exodus 30:7–8 distinguishes
morning tending from evening lighting; no new Hebrew lexical claim is required.

**The bread is eaten, not wholly burned.** Verse 7 assigns frankincense as its memorial
fire offering; verse 9 expressly gives the bread to the priests. Its classification
among offerings made by fire must be read together with that eating instruction.
The unit does not describe the precise timing or mechanics of burning the frankincense.

**Twelve invites a representative reading, but does not explain it.** The twelve cakes
can reasonably be read in relation to Israel's tribes, especially with the covenant
on Israel's behalf (8). The text does not explicitly assign one cake to each tribe.

**Sabbath rest and sanctuary service stand together.** Leviticus 23:3 prohibits work;
24:8 commands the bread's arrangement on that day. This is a qualification of any blanket
claim that Sabbath eliminates every activity, not a reason to erase either command.
Likewise, Exodus 27:21 assigns lamps to Aaron and his sons while 24:3 names Aaron;
the later wording does not expressly revoke the sons' role.

The planned boundary is retained: verses 1–9 complete the furnishing instructions before
the narrative at verse 10. Comparisons for this addition were read in stored WEB Exodus
25, 27, 30 and Leviticus 10, 23–24. Earlier external-source documentation on the existing
bread connection is preserved; this addition introduces no external quotations or
source-language assertions.
