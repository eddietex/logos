# Mildew in Garments

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 13:47–59

## Summary

The garment rules extend priestly examination to wool, linen, warp and woof, skins,
and objects made of skin. A greenish or reddish plague is shown to the priest and
isolated for seven days. If it spreads, the affected article is unclean and must burn.

If it has not spread, washing and a second seven-day isolation precede another examination.
Unchanged color still requires burning even without spread. A faded patch is torn out;
recurrence requires burning. When washing removes the plague, a second washing completes
the route to cleanness. The concluding formula identifies the purpose as pronouncing
the article clean or unclean.

## Key verses

> **13:49** if the plague is greenish or reddish in the garment, or in the skin, or in the warp, or in the woof, or in anything made of skin; it is the plague of leprosy, and shall be shown to the priest.

> **13:51** He shall examine the plague on the seventh day. If the plague has spread in the garment, either in the warp, or in the woof, or in the skin, whatever use the skin is used for, the plague is a destructive mildew. It is unclean.

> **13:54** then the priest shall command that they wash the thing in which the plague is, and he shall isolate it seven more days.

> **13:55** Then the priest shall examine it, after the plague is washed; and behold, if the plague hasn’t changed its color, and the plague hasn’t spread, it is unclean; you shall burn it in the fire. It is a mildewed spot, whether the bareness is inside or outside.

> **13:56** If the priest looks, and behold, the plague has faded after it is washed, then he shall tear it out of the garment, or out of the skin, or out of the warp, or out of the woof:

> **13:57** and if it appears again in the garment, either in the warp, or in the woof, or in anything of skin, it is spreading. You shall burn with fire that in which the plague is.

> **13:58** The garment, either the warp, or the woof, or whatever thing of skin it is, which you shall wash, if the plague has departed from them, then it shall be washed the second time, and it will be clean.”

> **13:59** This is the law of the plague of mildew in a garment of wool or linen, either in the warp, or the woof, or in anything of skin, to pronounce it clean, or to pronounce it unclean.

## Notes

### Reading the sequence

The two observation periods do not guarantee a clean result. Verse 55 expressly joins
unchanged color to a verdict of uncleanness even when the plague has not spread. Verses
56–58 then distinguish removing a faded patch, recurrence, and successful washing.
The law does not order every suspicious article destroyed at first sight.

The WEB itself moves between “leprosy” (47–49) and “mildew” (51–59) in this material.
The title follows its mildew wording and the existing queue. No identification of a
particular organism or modern diagnosis is asserted. These English renderings are not
used to establish an exact Hebrew lexical claim.

### Status and fault

The article is pronounced unclean; the owner is not charged with an offense in this unit.
No confession, offering, or atonement rite is prescribed. Burning disposes of the affected
material and is not described as an altar offering. The possibility of cleansing should
also be retained alongside the destructive outcome.
