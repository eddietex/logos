# Trumpets and the Day of Atonement

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 23:23–32

## Summary

Two divine speeches move the calendar into the seventh month. Yahweh tells [[Moses]]
to announce solemn rest, a memorial of trumpet blowing, and a holy convocation on the
first day. Israel must cease regular work and bring an offering made by fire (23–25).

On the tenth day comes the Day of Atonement: holy assembly, self-affliction, an offering
made by fire, and cessation of every kind of work. The stated reason for rest is that
atonement is made for Israel before Yahweh (26–28). Refusal of self-denial brings cutting
off; Yahweh declares that he will destroy the person who works (29–30). The prohibition
is perpetual throughout Israel's generations and dwellings (31).

The observance runs from the ninth day at evening until the following evening (32).
This explains how the tenth day's observance begins; it does not move the appointed
date to the ninth. The unit gives the community's duties rather than retelling the
priestly entry and two-goat rite of [[The Day of Atonement]], Leviticus 16.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Leviticus.md`. Complete unit:

> **23** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **24** “Speak to the children of Israel, saying, ‘In the seventh month, on the first day of the month, shall be a solemn rest to you, a memorial of blowing of trumpets, a holy convocation.

> **25** You shall do no regular work; and you shall offer an offering made by fire to Yahweh.’”

> **26** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **27** “However on the tenth day of this seventh month is the day of atonement: it shall be a holy convocation to you, and you shall afflict yourselves; and you shall offer an offering made by fire to Yahweh.

> **28** You shall do no kind of work in that same day; for it is a day of atonement, to make atonement for you before Yahweh your God.

> **29** For whoever it is who shall not deny himself in that same day; shall be cut off from his people.

> **30** Whoever it is who does any kind of work in that same day, that person I will destroy from among his people.

> **31** You shall do no kind of work: it is a statute forever throughout your generations in all your dwellings.

> **32** It shall be a Sabbath of solemn rest for you, and you shall deny yourselves. In the ninth day of the month at evening, from evening to evening, you shall keep your Sabbath.”

## Notes

**What the trumpet memorial commemorates is not specified.** The WEB describes trumpet
blowing (24) but supplies neither a remembered historical event nor an instrument's
material or detailed ritual. No later festival name, new-year identification, or
source-language claim is inferred from this English text.

**Self-denial is commanded without an explicit procedure here.** The WEB varies between
afflicting oneself and denying oneself (27, 29, 32). This page does not treat those English
renderings as proof of a particular Hebrew form, or identify a prescribed diet from these
verses alone. The existing connection preserves the same qualification for chapter 16.

**Rest and offering stand together.** The command to offer (27) accompanies the ban on all
work (28, 31). The passage does not explain a general taxonomy of allowed tasks. Its
wording must remain distinct from the prohibition on regular work at 25. Likewise, the
sanctions at 29–30 are not a narrated execution or an instruction to a human court.

**Complement, not contradiction.** Chapter 16:29 explicitly includes the resident foreigner;
chapter 23 does not repeat that phrase. Omission does not revoke the earlier inclusion.
Chapter 16 supplies the priestly procedure; chapter 23 supplies assembly, sanctions, and
an evening-to-evening boundary. Neither description should silently replace the other.

The planned boundary is retained: 23–25 and 26–32 are adjacent speeches about the first
two seventh-month observances; a new speech opens at 33. Quotations are copied from stored
WEB. Comparisons were checked in stored Leviticus 7, 16, and 23. No unverified external
quotation or new Hebrew/Greek assertion is introduced.
