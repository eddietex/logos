# Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 27:1–34

## Summary

Leviticus closes with a price list. A person may be vowed to Yahweh, and the vow is met by a
valuation in silver set by sex and age. A man of twenty to sixty is fifty shekels, a woman thirty,
and the scale falls for the young and the old (2–7). If the one who vowed cannot pay, the priest sets
a figure by what that person can afford (8).

Then the things a person might give. An animal fit for offering becomes holy, and it cannot be
swapped: try, and both animals are holy (9–10). An unclean animal, a house, or a field is valued by
the priest, and may be redeemed by adding a fifth (11–19). A field's value follows the seed it takes
and the years left to the Jubilee. An inherited field not redeemed goes to the priest at the Jubilee
(20–21). A bought field goes back to its original owner (22–24).

The last section lists what cannot be given because it is already Yahweh's, or cannot be taken back.
A firstborn animal cannot be dedicated (26–27). A devoted thing, *ḥērem*, cannot be sold or redeemed,
and a devoted human being is put to death (28–29). The tithe of the land is holy and may be redeemed
with a fifth; the tithe of the flock may not (30–33). Verse 34 closes the book at Mount Sinai.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Leviticus.md`. Complete unit:

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **2** “Speak to the children of Israel, and say to them, ‘When a man consecrates a person to Yahweh in a vow, according to your valuation,

> **3** your valuation of a male from twenty years old even to sixty years old shall be fifty shekels of silver, after the shekel of the sanctuary.

> **4** If she is a female, then your valuation shall be thirty shekels.

> **5** If the person is from five years old even to twenty years old, then your valuation shall be for a male twenty shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

> **6** If the person is from a month old even to five years old, then your valuation shall be for a male five shekels of silver, and for a female your valuation shall be three shekels of silver.

> **7** If the person is from sixty years old and upward; if he is a male, then your valuation shall be fifteen shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

> **8** But if he is poorer than your valuation, then he shall be set before the priest, and the priest shall assign a value to him. The priest shall assign a value according to his ability to pay.

> **9** “‘If it is an animal, of which men offer an offering to Yahweh, all that any man gives of such to Yahweh becomes holy.

> **10** He shall not alter it, nor change it, a good for a bad, or a bad for a good: and if he shall at all change animal for animal, then both it and that for which it is changed shall be holy.

> **11** If it is any unclean animal, of which they do not offer as an offering to Yahweh, then he shall set the animal before the priest;

> **12** and the priest shall value it, whether it is good or bad. As you the priest values it, so shall it be.

> **13** But if he will indeed redeem it, then he shall add the fifth part of it to its valuation.

> **14** “‘When a man dedicates his house to be holy to Yahweh, then the priest shall evaluate it, whether it is good or bad: as the priest shall evaluate it, so shall it stand.

> **15** If he who dedicates it will redeem his house, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of your valuation to it, and it shall be his.

> **16** “‘If a man dedicates to Yahweh part of the field of his possession, then your valuation shall be according to the seed for it. The sowing of a homer of barley shall be valued at fifty shekels of silver.

> **17** If he dedicates his field from the Year of Jubilee, according to your valuation it shall stand.

> **18** But if he dedicates his field after the Jubilee, then the priest shall reckon to him the money according to the years that remain to the Year of Jubilee; and an abatement shall be made from your valuation.

> **19** If he who dedicated the field will indeed redeem it, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of your valuation to it, and it shall remain his.

> **20** If he will not redeem the field, or if he has sold the field to another man, it shall not be redeemed any more;

> **21** but the field, when it goes out in the Jubilee, shall be holy to Yahweh, as a devoted field. It shall be owned by the priests.

> **22** “‘If he dedicates a field to Yahweh which he has bought, which is not of the field of his possession,

> **23** then the priest shall reckon to him the worth of your valuation up to the Year of Jubilee; and he shall give your valuation on that day, as a holy thing to Yahweh.

> **24** In the Year of Jubilee the field shall return to him from whom it was bought, even to him to whom the possession of the land belongs.

> **25** All your valuations shall be according to the shekel of the sanctuary: twenty gerahs to the shekel.

> **26** “‘Only the firstborn among animals, which is made Yahweh’s firstborn, no man may dedicate; whether an ox or a sheep. It is Yahweh’s.

> **27** If it is an unclean animal, then he shall buy it back according to your valuation, and shall add to it the fifth part of it; or if it isn’t redeemed, then it shall be sold according to your valuation.

> **28** “‘Notwithstanding, no devoted thing, that a man shall devote to Yahweh of all that he has, whether of man or animal, or of the field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed. Everything devoted to destruction is most holy to Yahweh.

> **29** “‘No one devoted, who shall be devoted from among men, shall be ransomed; he shall surely be put to death.

> **30** “‘All the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land or of the fruit of the trees, is Yahweh’s. It is holy to Yahweh.

> **31** If a man redeems anything of his tithe, he shall add a fifth part to it.

> **32** All the tithe of the herds or the flocks, whatever passes under the rod, the tenth shall be holy to Yahweh.

> **33** He shall not search whether it is good or bad, neither shall he change it. If he changes it at all, then both it and that for which it is changed shall be holy. It shall not be redeemed.’”

> **34** These are the commandments which Yahweh commanded Moses for the children of Israel on Mount Sinai.

## People

- [[God]] — sole speaker, and the one to whom everything here is given or already belongs.
- [[Moses]] — addressed (1) and named in the closing colophon (34).

The *priest* acts throughout, unnamed; [[Aaron]] is not mentioned.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — Mount Sinai, the last words of the book (34).
- [[Canaan]] — not named. The fields of possession and the Jubilee presume Israel settled there.

## Notes

**Boundary.** The plan stands: one unit for the chapter. The WLC has a setumah after verses 8 and 25
and none between 26 and 34, so it divides persons (2–8), property (9–25), and what cannot be vowed
(26–34). This page follows that outline.

**Why the book ends here.** Chapter 26 sounds like an ending, and 26:46 reads like one. Then comes a
chapter of prices. Many readers find it anticlimactic, and some treat it as an appendix. The text
gives no reason for the order. One thing it does show: after a speech about Israel's failure to keep
its word, the book's last unit asks what happens when an Israelite gives Yahweh his word freely, and
takes that seriously enough to price it. That is a reading.

**What the person's vow is.** Hebrew *yaplīʾ neder* (2) uses a verb WEB renders *consecrates* here and
*accomplish* at 22:21; its exact sense is not agreed. The unit does not say what vowing a person
means, nor describe any service. It also uses neither redemption verb in 2–8: *gāʾal* begins at 13
(WLC). So the common description, a schedule for redeeming vowed persons, is a reasonable inference
and not the wording. WEB's *your valuation* preserves a fixed form, *bəʿerkəkā*. *ʿErek* occurs 21
times in this chapter and three times elsewhere in Leviticus, all in the trespass laws (5:15, 18;
WEB 6:6; WLC lemma search).

**The table.**

| Age | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| 20–60 | 50 | 30 |
| 5–20 | 20 | 10 |
| 1 month–5 | 5 | 3 |
| 60 and up | 15 | 10 |

The text gives no reason for these numbers. They rise and fall with age, so the common reading is
that they track capacity for labor. That is an inference; verse 8 shows only that the scale was not
a measure of the vower's money. It should not be read as a statement of a person's worth before God,
since the chapter is pricing a vow, but neither should its plain inequality be hidden: a woman is
assessed below a man at every age. The gap also narrows with age: the man over sixty falls by seventy
percent from his top figure and the woman by two-thirds.

Three figures meet other texts. Twenty shekels is the sale price of [[Joseph]] at Genesis 37:28; see
[[Twenty Pieces of Silver - The Price of a Brother]]. Thirty is the price of a gored slave at Exodus 21:32. Five is the
redemption price of each surplus firstborn at Numbers 3:47, and the Levite count there also starts
at a month old (3:15; WEB, bible-api.com). No text connects any of these to this table.

**Poverty.** Verse 8's *mûk* occurs elsewhere in Leviticus only of the poor brother (25:25, 35, 39,
47; WLC lemma search), and *taśśîg yad*, what a hand can reach, is the idiom of the graded offerings
(5:11; 14:21–32). The law that sets a price immediately refuses to let the price exclude anyone. It
still does not say the vower may walk away from the vow.

**No exchange.** *Mûr*, *to exchange*, occurs in Leviticus only here (10, 33), three times in each
verse (WLC lemma search). The rule blocks even an upgrade: *a good for a bad, or a bad for a good*.
Holiness here spreads rather than transfers, and a substitute only doubles the debt. Psalm 15:4 uses
the same verb, *wəlōʾ yāmir*, of the one who swears to his own hurt *and doesn't change* (WLC). The
psalm does not cite this law; the shared verb is what can be shown.

**The added fifth.** A redeemer adds a fifth at 13, 15, 19, 27, and 31, as the wrongdoer does at
5:16, 6:5, and 22:14. Here no wrong has been done. The text does not say what the fifth is for. It
may deter casual reversal, but that is an inference.

**A field and its price.** The price depends on seed sown, not on current yield, and is prorated to
the Jubilee (16–18). Fifty shekels is usually taken as the figure for a full Jubilee cycle, about a
shekel a year, though the text does not do this arithmetic. Verses 20–21 are hard: how can someone
sell a field he has dedicated, and what exactly is forfeited? Ancient and modern interpreters differ,
and this page leaves it open. WEB's *the priests* at 21 is singular in the Hebrew, *lakkōhēn*.

**Three things that are already Yahweh's.** The verbal pattern changes at 26 with *ʾak*, *only*, and
at 28 with *ʾak* again. A firstborn, a devoted thing, and a tithe are not gifts. *It is Yahweh's* (26,
30). The chapter that lets people give to God ends by marking what they cannot give, because it never
stopped being his.

**Devoted things.** *Ḥērem* occurs in Leviticus only here (21, 28, 29), with the verb at 28–29. *Most
holy* (28) is the language of offerings at 2:3, but the verse does not call it an offering. The
private *ḥērem* of verse 28, and verse 29's human being, are never explained: who may be devoted,
by whom, and for what. Exodus 22:20 uses the verb of a death sentence for sacrificing to another god,
and Deuteronomy and Joshua use it of war. Those are the common explanations of verse 29, and neither
is stated here. Verse 29 is not a license to kill a person as a gift to God: 18:21 and 20:2–5 forbid
child sacrifice, and this verse uses neither *offer* nor *altar*. But the text does say *he shall
surely be put to death*, and that should stay on the page. See [[Devoted to Destruction - Leviticus 27.28-29, Deuteronomy 7, Joshua 6-7, and 1 Samuel 15]].

**Tithes.** Leviticus names no recipient, only Yahweh. The Levites are named at Numbers 18:21. The
land's tithe may be redeemed, and the flock's may not. The owner may not *search* for good or bad
(33): the tenth animal under the rod is the tenth animal. Compare 22:20's rule against defective
offerings: the unit does not say what happens when the tenth is defective. See [[Under the Rod - Leviticus 27.32, Jeremiah 33.13, and Ezekiel 20.37]].

**The second colophon.** 26:46 said *statutes, ordinances and laws*; 27:34 says *commandments*. The
book opens at the Tent of Meeting (1:1) and ends with the words *bəhar sînāy*, *on Mount Sinai*. It
has not moved geographically in twenty-seven chapters.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Land forever.** 25:23 says the land is not sold in perpetuity, and 25:28 returns it at the
   Jubilee. Verse 21 lets a field pass permanently to the priest. Numbers 18:20 says Aaron has no
   inheritance in the land, while 18:14 gives him everything devoted. Both claims stand; the
   exception may be a matter of what is already Yahweh's, but that is a reading.
2. **Unclean firstborn.** Verse 27 lets an unclean animal be bought back at valuation plus a fifth
   or sold. Exodus 13:13 has the firstborn donkey redeemed with a lamb or its neck broken, and
   Numbers 18:15 says it must be redeemed. Verse 27 may not refer to the same case, because its
   *unclean animal* is not called firstborn in that clause, though context suggests it.
3. **One word, two meanings.** *Ḥērem* in a field (21) is a change of ownership. *Ḥērem* in a
   person (29) is death. Nothing here explains how one word does both.
4. **Devoted persons and owners.** Verse 28's *of all that he has ... of man* may mean slaves or
   dependants, but Exodus 21:20 punishes killing a slave. The verse does not address this.
5. **Vows and rashness.** Nothing in this chapter calls a vow rash, and nothing cancels one. The
   wiki's earlier statement that it priced *things rashly dedicated* went beyond the text and has
   been corrected. Proverbs 20:25 and Ecclesiastes 5:5 warn of the danger; this unit only prices it.
