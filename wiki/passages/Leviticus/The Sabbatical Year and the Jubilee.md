# The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 25:1–22

## Summary

Yahweh speaks to [[Moses]] at Sinai about life in the land he will give Israel. Six years
of sowing and pruning must give way to a seventh-year Sabbath for the land. The ban on
ordinary cultivation and harvesting stands alongside permission for the produce to feed
owners, servants, hired workers, resident foreigners, livestock, and wild animals (1–7).

After seven cycles of seven years, Israel must sound the trumpet on the Day of Atonement
and make the fiftieth year holy. Jubilee proclaims liberty and return to family and property;
it also suspends sowing and harvesting while permitting food from the fields (8–13).
Transactions must reflect the number of crop years remaining before Jubilee. The buyer
purchases crops counted by years, and neither party may wrong the other (14–17).

The speech joins obedience to secure dwelling and sufficient food. It anticipates the
question of what people will eat without cultivation: Yahweh promises a sixth-year yield
for three years, with old stores supporting the people through resumed sowing in the eighth
year until the ninth year's harvest comes in (18–22). Land rest is paired with provision.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Leviticus.md`. Complete unit:

> **1** Yahweh said to Moses in Mount Sinai,

> **2** “Speak to the children of Israel, and tell them, ‘When you come into the land which I give you, then the land shall keep a Sabbath to Yahweh.

> **3** You shall sow your field six years, and you shall prune your vineyard six years, and gather in its fruits;

> **4** but in the seventh year there shall be a Sabbath of solemn rest for the land, a Sabbath to Yahweh. You shall not sow your field or prune your vineyard.

> **5** What grows of itself in your harvest you shall not reap, and you shall not gather the grapes of your undressed vine. It shall be a year of solemn rest for the land.

> **6** The Sabbath of the land shall be for food for you; for yourself, for your servant, for your maid, for your hired servant, and for your stranger, who lives as a foreigner with you.

> **7** For your livestock also, and for the animals that are in your land, shall all its increase be for food.

> **8** “‘You shall count off seven Sabbaths of years, seven times seven years; and there shall be to you the days of seven Sabbaths of years, even forty-nine years.

> **9** Then you shall sound the loud trumpet on the tenth day of the seventh month. On the Day of Atonement you shall sound the trumpet throughout all your land.

> **10** You shall make the fiftieth year holy, and proclaim liberty throughout the land to all its inhabitants. It shall be a jubilee to you; and each of you shall return to his own property, and each of you shall return to his family.

> **11** That fiftieth year shall be a jubilee to you. In it you shall not sow, neither reap that which grows of itself, nor gather from the undressed vines.

> **12** For it is a jubilee; it shall be holy to you. You shall eat of its increase out of the field.

> **13** “‘In this Year of Jubilee each of you shall return to his property.

> **14** “‘If you sell anything to your neighbor, or buy from your neighbor, you shall not wrong one another.

> **15** According to the number of years after the Jubilee you shall buy from your neighbor. According to the number of years of the crops he shall sell to you.

> **16** According to the length of the years you shall increase its price, and according to the shortness of the years you shall diminish its price; for he is selling the number of the crops to you.

> **17** You shall not wrong one another; but you shall fear your God: for I am Yahweh your God.

> **18** “‘Therefore you shall do my statutes, and keep my ordinances and do them; and you shall dwell in the land in safety.

> **19** The land shall yield its fruit, and you shall eat your fill, and dwell therein in safety.

> **20** If you said, “What shall we eat the seventh year? Behold, we shall not sow, nor gather in our increase”;

> **21** then I will command my blessing on you in the sixth year, and it shall bear fruit for the three years.

> **22** You shall sow the eighth year, and eat of the fruits, the old store; until the ninth year, until its fruits come in, you shall eat the old store.

## Themes

- [[Sabbath]] — The six-and-seven pattern governs years of cultivation; seven such cycles lead to a holy fiftieth year (1–12).
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The given land must rest, and Jubilee restores property and family connection (2, 10, 13).
- [[Work]] — Sowing, pruning, and reaping are interrupted by the land Sabbath and Jubilee (3–5, 11–12).
- [[Holiness]] — Israel must make the fiftieth year holy (10, 12).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Keeping the statutes is joined to dwelling securely and eating sufficiently (18–19).
- [[The Fear of God]] — The prohibition of wronging a neighbor is grounded in fearing God (17).
- [[Wealth and Possessions]] — The buyer pays for the number of crop years remaining (15–16), while property returns at Jubilee (13).
- [[Blessing]] — Yahweh promises to command a sixth-year blessing sufficient for three years (21–22).
- [[Famine and Plenty]] — The question of food during the fallow year is answered by sixth-year abundance and stored crops (20–22).
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — Servants and hired workers share the land’s food (6), and Jubilee proclaims liberty and return (10).
- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — Servants, hired workers, and resident foreigners have access to the land’s food (6–7).
- [[Redemption]] — Jubilee returns each person to property and family (10, 13).
- [[Faith]] — The question about food (20) meets a promise of sufficient old stores (21–22).
- [[Atonement]] — The Jubilee trumpet sounds on the Day of Atonement (9).
- [[Festivals and First Fruits]] — Seven Sabbaths of years and a fiftieth year (8–10) extend the calendar’s counting patterns.

## Notes

**Rest and food must both remain visible.** Verses 5 and 11 prohibit reaping spontaneous
produce, while 6–7 and 12 explicitly permit eating. The text distinguishes agricultural
exploitation from available food without supplying a complete manual of permitted collection.
Neither a ban on eating nor unrestricted commercial harvesting follows from these verses alone.

**Liberty has a context.** The fiftieth year restores family and property (10, 13).
The detailed laws that follow distinguish land, houses, and kinds of service. In particular,
25:44–46 permits inherited foreign slaves. That is a real limit on an unrestricted reading
of liberty for all inhabitants; neither statement should be silently erased. The later unit
will treat the service laws directly. A general cancellation of all monetary debts is not
specified in 1–22.

**The count is stated; its later implementation is not.** The WEB calls Jubilee the
fiftieth year after forty-nine counted years (8–11). Verses 20–22 answer the seventh-year
food question and describe sixth-, eighth-, and ninth-year provision. They do not explicitly
settle how the next seven-year cycle is aligned after Jubilee. No historical observance or
calendar harmonization is inferred here.

**Source and boundary.** Quotations are copied from stored WEB. Comparisons use stored
Exodus 16 and 23, Genesis 47, and Leviticus 23 and 25. No new source-language assertion or
external quotation is introduced. The planned 1–22 boundary is retained: 23 introduces
land ownership and specific redemption rules.
