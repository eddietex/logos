# Restitution for Fraud and False Oaths

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 6:1–7

## Summary

Yahweh speaks to Moses about wronging a neighbor through fraud over deposits or bargains,
robbery, oppression, dishonest treatment of lost property, and false swearing. Such conduct
is explicitly trespass against Yahweh, although the goods belong to another human being.

The guilty person must return the property in full, add a fifth, and give it to its owner
on the day of being found guilty. A ram without defect, according to valuation, is then
brought to the priest as a trespass offering. The priest makes atonement before Yahweh,
and the unit closes with forgiveness. Repair of the loss and the sacrificial remedy are
both required; the passage does not make them alternatives.

## Key verses

> **6:1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **6:2** “If anyone sins, and commits a trespass against Yahweh, and deals falsely with his neighbor in a matter of deposit, or of bargain, or of robbery, or has oppressed his neighbor,

> **6:3** or has found that which was lost, and dealt falsely therein, and swearing to a lie; in any of these things that a man does, sinning therein;

> **6:4** then it shall be, if he has sinned, and is guilty, he shall restore that which he took by robbery, or the thing which he has gotten by oppression, or the deposit which was committed to him, or the lost thing which he found,

> **6:5** or any thing about which he has sworn falsely; he shall restore it even in full, and shall add a fifth part more to it. He shall return it to him to whom it belongs in the day of his being found guilty.

> **6:6** He shall bring his trespass offering to Yahweh, a ram without defect from the flock, according to your estimation, for a trespass offering, to the priest.

> **6:7** The priest shall make atonement for him before Yahweh, and he will be forgiven concerning whatever he does to become guilty.”

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh calls fraud against a neighbor a trespass against himself (2), and prescribes restitution, an offering, and forgiveness (4–7). Human loss and divine accountability belong to the same case.
- [[Moses]] — Receives the new divine speech (1). The law specifies repayment to the owner and a valued ram brought to the priest; Moses does not adjudicate a narrated dispute here.
- [[Aaron]] — The priestly office established in Exodus 28:1 receives the ram and makes atonement (6–7). Aaron is contextual, not named as the officiant in these verses; repayment goes to the injured owner, not to the priest.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — The offering laws are located at Sinai by Leviticus 7:38. This unit names no geographical location; its restoration of the neighbor’s goods belongs within that wider Sinai instruction.

## Themes

- [[Sin]] — Fraud, robbery, oppression, dishonest handling of lost property, and false swearing are addressed as trespass against Yahweh (2–3). Unlike the preceding unknown-guilt case, these offenses involve deception; the offering laws cannot all be restricted to inadvertent acts.
- [[Guilt and Conscience]] — Guilt leads to returning the goods in full with an added fifth (4–5). The WEB speaks of the day of being found guilty; these verses do not specify whether discovery, confession, or a judicial verdict establishes that point.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The prescribed remedy joins full repayment plus a fifth to a ram without defect and priestly atonement (4–7). The law orders conduct toward the owner and approach to Yahweh together.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — The offender brings a ram without defect, according to valuation, as a trespass offering (6). No cheaper alternative, slaughter procedure, or blood rite is specified in this unit; the offering does not replace repayment.
- [[Atonement]] — The priest makes atonement before Yahweh after the law has required repayment to the injured owner (4–7). The ritual remedy addresses fraud and false swearing as well as the unwitting wrong of 5:15.
- [[Forgiveness]] — The closing promise of forgiveness (7) applies to the listed wrongs, including fraud and false swearing. Pardon is set beside required restitution, not permission to retain the neighbor’s goods.
- [[Priesthood]] — The ram is brought to the priest, who makes atonement before Yahweh (6–7). Unlike the holy-things case of 5:16, the repayment with its fifth goes to the owner (5), not to the priest.
- [[Theft and Restitution]] — Goods obtained through robbery, oppression, fraud over deposits, or dishonest retention of lost property must be restored in full with an added fifth (2–5). Tension with Exodus 22:1–9: that law requires multiplied repayment in theft and disputed-property cases. These texts state differing provisions; neither expressly explains how the penalties interact in an overlapping case.
- [[Deception]] — False dealings over entrusted or lost property and false swearing compound material injury (2–5). Correcting the falsehood includes returning what belongs to the neighbor; a verbal assurance alone is not the prescribed remedy.
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — False swearing about another’s property belongs to the wrong requiring repayment and an offering (3, 5–7). This differs from the rash oath of Leviticus 5:4 and exposes the abuse of the property oath accepted in Exodus 22:10–11.

## Notes

### The neighbor’s loss is a wrong against Yahweh

Verse 2 links the two explicitly. Its opening description prevents a division in which
ordinary property matters are irrelevant to worship. Yet the payment remains the owner’s:
it is not redirected to the sanctuary. The offering cannot discharge the material debt
by itself, and restitution is not presented as a replacement for priestly atonement.

### Deliberate deceit and the promise of forgiveness

The preceding unit treats unwitting wrong and wrongdoing without knowledge (5:15, 17–18).
This one names deceit, robbery, oppression, and false swearing. It therefore qualifies any
claim that every offense addressed by these offerings must be accidental. The closing
promise is broad within the listed cases, not an assertion that every possible offense
has this same remedy.

### Repayment, timing, and the limits of harmonization

An added fifth means the principal plus twenty percent. The WEB locates repayment on
“the day of his being found guilty” (5), but does not describe the process by which guilt
is established. This page does not turn that phrase into a requirement of either voluntary
confession or court conviction. Unlike 5:5, these verses contain no explicit confession
instruction in the WEB; this is an observation about the stored translation, not a claim
about Hebrew wording.

**Unresolved legal tension:** Exodus 22:1–9 specifies double, fourfold, or fivefold payment
in its theft and property-dispute cases, whereas Leviticus 6:1–5 specifies an added fifth
for its fraud and robbery cases. The situations are not identical, but they overlap. Neither
passage explicitly says whether a voluntary admission changes the penalty, or whether
these sums accumulate. Do not silently harmonize the rates by inventing that distinction.
See [[Restitution to the Neighbor and for Holy Things]].
