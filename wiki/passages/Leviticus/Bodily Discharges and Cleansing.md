# Bodily Discharges and Cleansing

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 15:1–33

## Summary

Yahweh instructs Moses and Aaron about bodily discharges and the uncleanness communicated
through contact. The first section treats a man’s discharge, its effects on beds, seats,
people, and vessels, and the seven-day count after it ceases, followed by eighth-day
bird offerings (1–15). Seminal emission, including intercourse with emission, instead
requires washing and uncleanness until evening (16–18).

Menstruation brings a seven-day period, with rules for contact and bedding (19–24).
Bleeding outside or beyond the usual period extends the condition; after cessation,
seven days precede eighth-day offerings (25–30). The chapter closes by explaining the
stakes: Israel must be separated from uncleanness so as not to defile the tabernacle
among them and die (31–33). Household bodily life and sanctuary access belong to the
same community, without every bodily process being named a moral transgression.

## Key verses

> **15:13** “‘When he who has a discharge is cleansed of his discharge, then he shall count to himself seven days for his cleansing, and wash his clothes; and he shall bathe his flesh in running water, and shall be clean.

> **15:15** and the priest shall offer them, the one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering. The priest shall make atonement for him before Yahweh for his discharge.

> **15:18** If a man lies with a woman and there is an emission of semen, they shall both bathe themselves in water, and be unclean until the evening.

> **15:28** “‘But if she is cleansed of her discharge, then she shall count to herself seven days, and after that she shall be clean.

> **15:30** The priest shall offer the one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering; and the priest shall make atonement for her before Yahweh for the uncleanness of her discharge.

> **15:31** “‘Thus you shall separate the children of Israel from their uncleanness, so they will not die in their uncleanness, when they defile my tabernacle that is among them.’”

## Notes

### Differences that must remain visible

The male discharge of verses 2–15 is distinguished from seminal emission in 16–18;
the text does not identify a modern disease. Menstruation in 19–24 is distinguished
from prolonged or out-of-period bleeding in 25–30. Only the first and last of these
sections prescribe paired bird offerings. Verse 24 assigns seven-day uncleanness to
the man upon whom the monthly flow comes; the until-evening rules of other contacts
must not replace that duration.

The man’s restoration explicitly includes washing clothes and bathing in running
water (13). The woman’s seven-day count in 28 does not repeat those instructions;
we should neither erase that difference nor silently expand the verse. Both bring
the same bird offerings on the eighth day.

### Uncleanness, fault, and sanctuary danger

**Qualification against conflation:** the sin-offering label does not establish a
personal offense causing the condition. Neither confession nor a forgiveness formula
appears in this chapter’s stored WEB. The explicit death warning concerns defilement
of the tabernacle (31). A reading that makes ordinary intercourse or menstruation
inherently culpable would exceed this passage, while a purely hygienic reading would
omit its stated sanctuary rationale.
