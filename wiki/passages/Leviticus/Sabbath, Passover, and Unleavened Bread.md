# Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 23:1–8

## Summary

Yahweh tells [[Moses]] to address Israel about his appointed feasts and holy convocations.
The calendar begins with the weekly [[Sabbath]]: six days of work and a seventh of solemn
rest, observed throughout the people's dwellings (1–3). A renewed introduction then turns
to festivals proclaimed at their appointed season (4).

Passover falls on the fourteenth day of the first month in the evening (5). Unleavened
Bread begins on the fifteenth and lasts seven days (6). Its first and seventh days are
holy convocations without regular work; offerings made by fire are required throughout
the seven days (7–8). The unit gives dates and communal obligations, without retelling
the departure from Egypt or specifying the offerings' animals and quantities.

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Leviticus.md`. The complete eight-verse unit:

> **1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **2** “Speak to the children of Israel, and tell them, ‘The set feasts of Yahweh, which you shall proclaim to be holy convocations, even these are my set feasts.

> **3** “‘Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day is a Sabbath of solemn rest, a holy convocation; you shall do no kind of work. It is a Sabbath to Yahweh in all your dwellings.

> **4** “‘These are the set feasts of Yahweh, even holy convocations, which you shall proclaim in their appointed season.

> **5** In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month in the evening, is Yahweh’s Passover.

> **6** On the fifteenth day of the same month is the feast of unleavened bread to Yahweh. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread.

> **7** In the first day you shall have a holy convocation. You shall do no regular work.

> **8** But you shall offer an offering made by fire to Yahweh seven days. In the seventh day is a holy convocation: you shall do no regular work.’”

## Notes

**Weekly and annual observances remain distinct.** Verse 3 calls the seventh day a holy
convocation. Verse 4 restarts the festival introduction before the dated observances.
The arrangement places both rhythms under Yahweh's authority without making Passover a
weekly Sabbath or saying that every day of Unleavened Bread forbids work.

**Keep the wording differences visible.** The stored WEB has “no kind of work” in verse 3
and “no regular work” in verses 7–8. Exodus 12:16 explicitly permits preparation of what
people must eat on the festival assembly days. Leviticus does not repeat that exception;
its silence alone neither revokes it nor defines every permitted task. This is a comparison
of the verified English texts, not a claim about unexamined Hebrew wording.

**Scope and source.** The existing 1–8 boundary is retained: a new divine speech begins at
verse 9. No new person or place page is needed for the collective audience or unnamed
habitations. Quotations come directly from the stored WEB; the Exodus comparison was checked
in `raw/bible/Exodus.md`, chapter 12. No external verses or source-language claims are quoted.
