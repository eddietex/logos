# Acceptable Victims and the Holy Name

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 22:17–33

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to speak to Aaron, his sons, and all the children of Israel (17–18). Anyone of
the house of Israel, or of the foreigners in Israel, who brings a vow or freewill burnt offering
must bring "a male without defect" from bulls, sheep, or goats, so that the offerer may be
accepted; whatever has a defect "shall not be acceptable for you" (18–20). A peace offering for a
vow or a freewill offering must likewise be "perfect to be accepted" (21). The text lists the
excluded conditions — blind, injured, maimed, a wart, festering, a running sore — and forbids
offering them by fire on the altar (22). One exception follows: a bull or lamb with a deformity
or lacking in its parts may be a freewill offering, but not a vow (23). Animals with bruised,
crushed, broken, or cut testicles are not to be offered, and "you must not do this in your
land" (24). Nor may Israel offer "the bread of your God" from a foreigner's hand as any of these,
because "there is a defect in them" (25).

A second speech (26–30) adds three timing rules. A newborn bull, sheep, or goat stays seven days
with its mother and is acceptable from the eighth day (27). A cow or ewe and its young are not to
be killed on the same day (28). A thanksgiving sacrifice is to be eaten on the day it is offered,
with nothing left until morning (29–30).

The closing exhortation (31–33) gathers chapters 21–22: keep the commandments, do not profane the
holy name, "but I will be made holy among the children of Israel"; Yahweh is the one who makes
Israel holy, who brought them out of Egypt to be their God.

The pericope turns the defect language of [[The Holiness of the Priests]] from the priest's body
to the animal. Chapter 21 barred a descendant of Aaron "who has a defect" from offering the bread of
his God (21:17); chapter 22 bars an animal with a defect from being that bread (25). The previous
unit, [[Eating the Holy Things]], regulated who eats; this one regulates what is offered.

## Key verses

> **22:19** that you may be accepted, you shall offer a male without defect, of the bulls, of the sheep, or of the goats.

> **22:20** But whatever has a defect, that you shall not offer; for it shall not be acceptable for you.

> **22:23** Either a bull or a lamb that has any deformity or lacking in his parts, that you may offer for a freewill offering; but for a vow it shall not be accepted.

> **22:27** “When a bull, or a sheep, or a goat, is born, then it shall remain seven days with its mother; and from the eighth day and thenceforth it shall be accepted for the offering of an offering made by fire to Yahweh.

> **22:32** You shall not profane my holy name, but I will be made holy among the children of Israel. I am Yahweh who makes you holy,

> **22:33** who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God. I am Yahweh.”

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh speaks both speeches (17, 26). Acceptance is acceptance by him (19–21, 27, 29), and the close names him as the one who makes Israel holy and brought them out of Egypt (32–33). "I will be made holy among the children of Israel" (32) makes his holiness something displayed among the people.
- [[Moses]] — Moses receives both speeches and is to speak to Aaron, his sons, and all Israel (17–18, 26). No delivery is narrated.
- [[Aaron]] — Aaron and his sons are addressed with all Israel (18). The rules concern what the offerer brings and the priest places on the altar (22); the unit does not narrate Aaron acting.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — The Sinai setting comes from the book's frame (Leviticus 7:38); this unit names no place. "The altar" (22) is the location of acceptance, and "your land" (24) extends one prohibition beyond it.
- [[Egypt]] — Recalled in the closing self-identification: Yahweh "brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God" (33). Egypt is the remembered place of deliverance, not a setting.

## Themes

- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — The burnt offering must be an unblemished male (18–19), as Leviticus 1:3 already required; the peace offering must be "perfect" without a stated sex (21), consistent with the "male or female" of Leviticus 3:1, 6. The freewill exception (23), the eighth-day minimum (27), and the same-day thanksgiving meal (29–30) refine the earlier offering laws.
- [[Holiness]] — The unit ends with the fullest statement of the refrain in chapters 21–22: Yahweh's name is not to be profaned, he "will be made holy among the children of Israel", and he is the one "who makes you holy" (32). Unacceptable offerings are framed as the way the name could be profaned.
- [[The Names of God]] — "My holy name" (32) and "I am Yahweh" (30, 31, 33) close the unit. The name is joined to two actions: making Israel holy and bringing them out of Egypt (32–33).
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — Vow and freewill offerings are named together (18, 21), and then distinguished: an animal with a deformity or lacking in its parts may be offered freely but not for a vow (23). The text gives the rule without stating its reason.
- [[The Nations]] — Foreigners in Israel may bring burnt offerings under the same rule (18), as in Leviticus 17:8. But Israel must not offer "the bread of your God from the hand of a foreigner as any of these", because "there is a defect in them" (25). The two verses together stop either inclusion or exclusion from being read as the whole picture.
- [[Priesthood]] — The priest's own defect excluded him from approach in chapter 21; here the animal's defect excludes it (20–22, 25). Both are called "the bread of your God" (21:17; 22:25), so the altar is guarded at the officiant and at the offering.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — "Therefore you shall keep my commandments, and do them" (31) summarizes the priestly holiness laws begun in chapter 21.
- [[Redemption]] — The exodus is the ground of the whole demand: "who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God" (33), echoing Leviticus 11:45.

## Notes

**Who is addressed.** Verse 18 widens the audience from Aaron and his sons, the addressees of
22:1–16, to "all the children of Israel". The offerer chooses the animal, so the rules bind the
worshipper as well as the officiating priest.

**Burnt and peace offerings differ on sex, not on defect.** Verse 19 requires a male for the
burnt offering, matching Leviticus 1:3. Verse 21 does not name a sex for the peace offering, and
Leviticus 3:1, 6 allowed male or female. Both must be without defect.

**The freewill exception.** Verse 23 permits a bull or lamb "that has any deformity or lacking in
his parts" as a freewill offering but not for a vow. The listed conditions of verse 22 (blindness,
injury, sores) are not included in the exception in the WEB wording; the deformity and lacking parts
of verse 23 are. The text does not explain why a vow requires the stricter standard. Explanations
— for instance, that a vow discharges an obligation already owed — are interpretations not stated
here.

**"You must not do this in your land" (24).** In the WEB this follows the prohibition on offering
animals with damaged testicles. The sentence could forbid the offering in the land or the practice
of castration itself; the WEB wording leaves that open, and this page makes no claim about the
Hebrew to decide it.

**The foreigner in verses 18 and 25.** Verse 18 lets foreigners in Israel bring burnt offerings.
Verse 25 forbids offering the bread of God "from the hand of a foreigner as any of these", giving
as the reason "their corruption is in them. There is a defect in them." In the WEB context the
reason is the animals' defect, which suggests the prohibition concerns blemished animals supplied
by a foreigner rather than all participation by foreigners; that is a reading of the English
sentence, not a source-language claim. The tension is recorded, not resolved.

**Mother and young.** Verse 27 parallels Exodus 22:30, where a firstborn animal stays seven days
with its mother and is given on the eighth; Leviticus extends the interval to every acceptable
newborn. Verse 28 forbids killing a cow or ewe and its young on the same day. Neither verse states a
reason. Readings of compassion, or of a link to the goat-and-mother's-milk rule, are interpretive
and are recorded on the connection pages as such.

**Thanksgiving restated.** Verses 29–30 repeat the same-day rule of Leviticus 7:15 and connect it
to acceptance ("so that you may be accepted"). The vow and freewill next-day allowance of 7:16 is
not repeated here.

**Boundary.** The planned 22:17–33 boundary is kept. The two speeches (17, 26) share the theme of
acceptability, and verses 31–33 close chapters 21–22. Chapter 23 opens a new speech on the
appointed feasts.
