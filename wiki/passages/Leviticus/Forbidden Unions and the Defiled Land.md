# Forbidden Unions and the Defiled Land

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 18:1–30

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to instruct Israel to follow his statutes rather than the practices of
Egypt or Canaan. Obedience is associated with life (1–5). Kinship prohibitions cover close
relatives and relationships by marriage, including taking two sisters while both live (6–18).

The speech prohibits intercourse during menstrual impurity, adultery, giving children to
Molech, male same-sex intercourse, and intercourse with animals (19–23). These practices
defile persons and land. The land vomits out its inhabitants, and Israel is warned that it
too can be expelled. Native-born people and resident foreigners share the obligation;
individual offenders face cutting off (24–30).

## Key verses

> **18:5** You shall therefore keep my statutes and my ordinances; which if a man does, he shall live in them. I am Yahweh.

> **18:18** “‘You shall not take a wife in addition to her sister, to be a rival, to uncover her nakedness, while her sister is still alive.

> **18:21** “‘You shall not give any of your children as a sacrifice to Molech. You shall not profane the name of your God. I am Yahweh.

> **18:26** You therefore shall keep my statutes and my ordinances, and shall not do any of these abominations; neither the native-born, nor the stranger who lives as a foreigner among you;

> **18:28** that the land not vomit you out also, when you defile it, as it vomited out the nation that was before you.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh commands Moses to address Israel and warns that Israel too can lose the land through defiling conduct (1–5, 24–30).
- [[Moses]] — Moses receives the charge to speak to Israel (1–2), now about household boundaries, forbidden worship, and the land.

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — Egypt is Israel’s former home, whose practices must not govern its life (3), rather than the setting of this speech.
- [[Canaan]] — Canaan is the anticipated destination (3), but the land’s expulsion of inhabitants warns Israel against the same conduct (24–28).
- [[Horeb]] — The Sinai context continues from Leviticus 7:38; chapter 18 does not name the mountain. Egypt and Canaan are the remembered origin and anticipated destination.

## Themes

- [[Sexual Sin]] — The chapter prohibits specified kinship unions (6–18), intercourse during menstrual impurity (19), adultery (20), male same-sex intercourse (22), and intercourse with animals (23). It describes acts, not modern categories of sexual orientation.
- [[Marriage]] — The half-sister, aunt, and simultaneous sister marriages excluded in 18:9, 12, 18 stand in tension with the unions reported in Genesis 20:12; 29:23–30; Exodus 6:20. Leviticus does not name those ancestors or pronounce a retrospective verdict.
- [[Nakedness and Covering]] — Uncovering nakedness organizes the kinship prohibitions (6–18); the father’s wife and brother’s wife are described through the corresponding male relative (8, 16). This observation concerns the stored WEB wording, not an exact Hebrew claim.
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — The earlier nonmoral definition needs qualification: Leviticus 12 and 15 describe bodily conditions without necessarily identifying an offense, while 18:20–30 expressly associates defilement with prohibited conduct and the land. Menstruation itself is not identified as sin; sexual approach during it is forbidden (19).
- [[Holiness]] — Yahweh’s self-identification frames household boundaries and rejection of neighboring customs (2–5, 30). The Molech prohibition is joined to not profaning God’s name (21).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — Yahweh’s statutes replace Egypt’s and Canaan’s practices (3–5); obedience is associated with life (5). Native-born people and resident foreigners alike must refrain from the prohibited acts (26).
- [[Judgment]] — The land vomits out its inhabitants for their conduct, and Israel faces the same warning (24–28). Individuals face cutting off (29); chapter 18 does not specify judicial execution procedures.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — The promised destination can expel Israel as well as its predecessors (3, 24–28). Native-born people and resident foreigners share the obligation (26); entry is not unconditional tenure.
- [[Idolatry]] — The stored WEB forbids giving children as a sacrifice to Molech and profaning God’s name (21). It specifies neither a ritual procedure nor that the children are firstborn. Molech is treated as an object of forbidden worship rather than a new human person.
- [[The Nations]] — Egypt and Canaan supply practices Israel must not imitate (3). The nations’ conduct explains expulsion, but Israel receives the same warning (24–28). This is the text’s theological portrayal, not independent ethnographic evidence about every inhabitant.
- [[Life and Death]] — Obedience is associated with life (5), child sacrifice is forbidden (21), and prohibited acts bring cutting off (29). The chapter does not define the life promise as immortality.
- [[Sin]] — Prohibited acts defile persons and land (24–30). Unlike the bodily conditions in chapters 12–15, these acts are expressly forbidden, and chapter 18 supplies no washing-and-waiting remedy.
- [[The Names of God]] — Yahweh’s self-identification frames the commands (2, 4–6, 21, 30). Giving children to Molech is joined to profaning God’s name (21).

## Notes

**Tensions retained.** Genesis 20:12; 29:23–30 and the stored WEB Exodus 6:20 report unions
within degrees this chapter prohibits. Leviticus does not name these ancestors or
retrospectively condemn their households. The brother’s-wife prohibition (16) also stands
beside the duty described in Genesis 38:8.

**Status and prohibition.** Leviticus 15:24 assigns an impurity consequence to intercourse
involving menstrual flow; 18:19 explicitly prohibits approach during menstrual impurity.
A status consequence is not permission, and the prohibition does not make menstruation
itself a moral offense.

**Verse 22.** The WEB prohibits male same-sex intercourse. It does not discuss sexual
orientation or female same-sex relations, nor state a restriction to coercion or cultic
activity. Later application requires interpretation beyond this description.

**Narrative time.** Entry is anticipated (3), while the land imagery describes previous
inhabitants as already expelled (25, 28). Both formulations are retained; this chapter does
not narrate the conquest itself.

The planned 18:1–30 boundary is unchanged.
