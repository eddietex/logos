# The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 6:24–7:10

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to instruct Aaron and his sons about most holy offerings. Priests eat
sin-offering flesh in the court of the Tent of Meeting, but may not eat an offering whose
blood enters the tent for atonement in the Holy Place. Contact with flesh, blood on garments,
and cooking vessels requires specified handling: washing in a holy place, breaking earthen
vessels, and scouring and rinsing bronze ones.

The trespass offering is killed at the burnt-offering site. Its blood is sprinkled around
the altar and its fat portions burned; male priests may eat it in a holy place. The unit
ends by assigning offerings to the officiating priest, including the burnt offering's skin
and prepared meal offerings, while grain mixed with oil or dry belongs to all Aaron's sons.

## Key verses

> **6:24** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **6:25** “Speak to Aaron and to his sons, saying, ‘This is the law of the sin offering: in the place where the burnt offering is killed, the sin offering shall be killed before Yahweh. It is most holy.

> **6:26** The priest who offers it for sin shall eat it. It shall be eaten in a holy place, in the court of the Tent of Meeting.

> **6:27** Whatever shall touch its flesh shall be holy. When there is any of its blood sprinkled on a garment, you shall wash that on which it was sprinkled in a holy place.

> **6:28** But the earthen vessel in which it is boiled shall be broken; and if it is boiled in a bronze vessel, it shall be scoured, and rinsed in water.

> **6:29** Every male among the priests shall eat of it: it is most holy.

> **6:30** No sin offering, of which any of the blood is brought into the Tent of Meeting to make atonement in the Holy Place, shall be eaten: it shall be burned with fire.

> **7:1** “‘This is the law of the trespass offering. It is most holy.

> **7:2** In the place where they kill the burnt offering, he shall kill the trespass offering; and its blood he shall sprinkle around on the altar.

> **7:3** He shall offer all of its fat: the fat tail, and the fat that covers the innards,

> **7:4** and the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them, which is by the loins, and the cover on the liver, with the kidneys, shall he take away;

> **7:5** and the priest shall burn them on the altar for an offering made by fire to Yahweh: it is a trespass offering.

> **7:6** Every male among the priests may eat of it. It shall be eaten in a holy place. It is most holy.

> **7:7** “‘As is the sin offering, so is the trespass offering; there is one law for them. The priest who makes atonement with them shall have it.

> **7:8** The priest who offers any man’s burnt offering, even the priest shall have for himself the skin of the burnt offering which he has offered.

> **7:9** Every meal offering that is baked in the oven, and all that is dressed in the pan, and on the griddle, shall be the priest’s who offers it.

> **7:10** Every meal offering, mixed with oil or dry, belongs to all the sons of Aaron, one as well as another.

## Notes

### Holy food and the exception

The permission to eat (6:26, 29) and prohibition (6:30) concern different cases. An
unqualified claim that all sin offerings are burned would contradict the eating rule;
an unqualified claim that all are eaten would contradict the sanctuary-blood exception.
Leviticus 4:5–12, 16–21 supplies the blood-and-burning pattern. Verse 6:30 itself commands
burning without repeating its location. The ruler and individual cases in 4:22–35 must
not inherit every detail of the preceding bulls' disposal.

### Contact and vessels

The WEB says, “Whatever shall touch its flesh shall be holy” (6:27). This is not a claim
that contact makes someone morally virtuous or permits anyone to eat. The same instructions
restrict eaters and location. Washing blood from a garment must happen in a holy place.
Earthenware is broken; bronze is scoured and rinsed. Porosity may explain the difference,
but the text does not give that explanation, or call the flesh unclean. No exact Hebrew
construction is asserted here.

### One law and distinct allocations

The shared rule for sin and trespass offerings (7:7) concerns the priest who makes
atonement receiving the portion. It does not erase the blood procedures already distinguished
in chapter 4 and 7:2. Eligibility of all male priests (6:29; 7:6) stands beside the
assignment to the officiant (6:26; 7:7); the text does not detail every act of distribution.

There is also a wording tension between the prepared offerings assigned to the officiant
(7:9) and every meal offering mixed with oil or dry assigned to all sons equally (7:10).
Reading the latter as unprepared grain fits the contrast, but the WEB does not explicitly
insert that qualification, and prepared offerings in 2:4–7 also contain oil. Both clauses
are retained without claiming a complete distribution system. The priest's own meal offering
remains wholly burned under 6:23. Finally, a burnt offering's burning does not mean its
skin must also burn: 1:6 directs flaying and 7:8 assigns that skin.
