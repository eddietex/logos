# Fat, Blood, and the Priests’ Share

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 7:22–38

## Summary

Two speeches to Moses close the offering legislation. The first prohibits eating the fat
of cattle, sheep, and goats and the blood of birds and animals. Fat from animals that die
or are torn may serve other uses, but cannot become food. Eating the prohibited fat or
blood carries the sanction of being cut off from one's people.

The second assigns the peace offering's portions. The offerer brings the fat and breast
with his own hands; the fat burns on the altar, the waved breast belongs to Aaron and his
sons, and the right thigh belongs to the son who offers the blood and fat. Yahweh describes
these priestly portions as his own gift from Israel's offerings. A concluding summary
locates the offering laws in Yahweh's commands to Moses at Sinai.

## Key verses

> **7:22** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **7:23** “Speak to the children of Israel, saying, ‘You shall eat no fat, of bull, or sheep, or goat.

> **7:24** The fat of that which dies of itself, and the fat of that which is torn of animals, may be used for any other service, but you shall in no way eat of it.

> **7:25** For whoever eats the fat of the animal, of which men offer an offering made by fire to Yahweh, even the soul who eats it shall be cut off from his people.

> **7:26** You shall not eat any blood, whether it is of bird or of animal, in any of your dwellings.

> **7:27** Whoever it is who eats any blood, that soul shall be cut off from his people.’”

> **7:28** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **7:29** “Speak to the children of Israel, saying, ‘He who offers the sacrifice of his peace offerings to Yahweh shall bring his offering to Yahweh out of the sacrifice of his peace offerings.

> **7:30** With his own hands he shall bring the offerings of Yahweh made by fire. He shall bring the fat with the breast, that the breast may be waved for a wave offering before Yahweh.

> **7:31** The priest shall burn the fat on the altar, but the breast shall be Aaron’s and his sons’.

> **7:32** The right thigh you shall give to the priest for a heave offering out of the sacrifices of your peace offerings.

> **7:33** He among the sons of Aaron who offers the blood of the peace offerings, and the fat, shall have the right thigh for a portion.

> **7:34** For the waved breast and the heaved thigh I have taken from the children of Israel out of the sacrifices of their peace offerings, and have given them to Aaron the priest and to his sons as their portion forever from the children of Israel.’”

> **7:35** This is the anointing portion of Aaron, and the anointing portion of his sons, out of the offerings of Yahweh made by fire, in the day when he presented them to minister to Yahweh in the priest’s office;

> **7:36** which Yahweh commanded to be given them of the children of Israel, in the day that he anointed them. It is their portion forever throughout their generations.

> **7:37** This is the law of the burnt offering, of the meal offering, and of the sin offering, and of the trespass offering, and of the consecration, and of the sacrifice of peace offerings;

> **7:38** which Yahweh commanded Moses in Mount Sinai, in the day that he commanded the children of Israel to offer their offerings to Yahweh, in the wilderness of Sinai.

## Notes

### Use is not the same as eating

Verse 23 names three kinds of livestock; verse 26 extends the blood restriction to birds
and animals in every dwelling. Verse 24 expressly permits other use of fat from animals
that die of themselves or are torn, while excluding eating it. Thus the fat prohibition
cannot be reduced to the handling of animals actually offered on the altar. The passage
does not enumerate permitted crafts or supply a hygienic rationale. Nor does its English
wording by itself settle modern anatomical questions about every kind of fat in meat.

The cutting-off formula in verses 25 and 27 does not identify an executor, a procedure,
or a time. As in 7:20–21, this page does not equate it without qualification with either
human expulsion or a specified form of execution.

### A gift brought by hand and assigned by God

The offerer's own hands bring the portions (30); the priest burns the fat (31). The breast
belongs to Aaron and his sons, while the thigh is specifically assigned to the officiant
who offers blood and fat (33). Neither the priest nor the donor may treat every part as
interchangeable. The WEB calls the breast waved and the thigh heaved; these labels alone
do not justify reconstructing a complete choreography of movement.

**Apparent tension:** 7:19 allows everyone clean to eat peace-offering meat, but 7:31–34
reserves breast and thigh to priests. These are complementary conditions: eligibility
for the shared meal does not confer a right to every portion. Likewise the right thigh
burned in the ordination rite (Exodus 29:22–25) and the thigh given to the officiant here
belong to different ritual settings; see the comparison page.

### The close of the laws and the coming ordination

Verses 35–36 associate the priestly portions with anointing and continuing generations.
Verses 37–38 summarize burnt, meal, sin, trespass, consecration, and peace offerings and
locate the commands at Sinai. This links the laws to the ordination narrative that follows;
it does not itself narrate that ceremony. The Sinai summary and the speech from the tent
in 1:1 name different scales of setting, without fixing a detailed chronology here.
