# The Trespass Offering for Holy Things and Unknown Guilt

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 5:14–19

## Summary

A new speech to Moses gives two trespass-offering cases. Someone who sins unwittingly in
Yahweh’s holy things brings an unblemished ram, valued in silver by the sanctuary shekel.
The person also restores what was wronged, adds a fifth, and gives it to the priest.
Priestly atonement with the ram concludes with the promise of forgiveness (14–16).

The second case concerns doing what Yahweh forbids without knowing it. The person is
nevertheless guilty and bears iniquity, but receives a prescribed remedy: an unblemished
ram according to valuation, brought to the priest for atonement and forgiveness (17–19).
The passage distinguishes liability from awareness without leaving the guilty person
without a way forward.

## Key verses

> **5:14** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **5:15** “If anyone commits a trespass, and sins unwittingly, in the holy things of Yahweh; then he shall bring his trespass offering to Yahweh, a ram without defect from the flock, according to your estimation in silver by shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary, for a trespass offering.

> **5:16** He shall make restitution for that which he has done wrong in the holy thing, and shall add a fifth part to it, and give it to the priest; and the priest shall make atonement for him with the ram of the trespass offering, and he will be forgiven.

> **5:17** “If anyone sins, and does any of the things which Yahweh has commanded not to be done; though he didn’t know it, yet he is guilty, and shall bear his iniquity.

> **5:18** He shall bring a ram without defect from of the flock, according to your estimation, for a trespass offering, to the priest; and the priest shall make atonement for him concerning the thing in which he sinned and didn’t know it, and he will be forgiven.

> **5:19** It is a trespass offering. He is certainly guilty before Yahweh.”

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh speaks to Moses, identifies liability even without knowledge, and provides a remedy ending in forgiveness (14–19).
- [[Moses]] — Receives the new speech at 5:14; the valuation language belongs to the instruction delivered to him, without a narrated act of appraisal.
- [[Aaron]] — Contextual priestly office established in Exodus 28:1: the priest receives restitution and makes atonement with the ram. Aaron is not named in Leviticus 5:14–19.
- The offerer and priest are legal roles, not new named individuals.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — The offering laws belong to the Sinai setting identified in Leviticus 7:38. Leviticus 5:14–19 itself names no mountain or new geographical location.

## Themes

- [[Sin]] — Unwitting wrongdoing against holy things (15) and prohibited acts done without knowledge (17–18) still incur liability. Lack of intent is not here a declaration that no wrong occurred.
- [[Guilt and Conscience]] — Verses 17–19 explicitly separate liability from knowledge. This is actual wrongdoing described by the law, not a declaration that every anxious suspicion proves an offense.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The remedy is prescribed as carefully as the prohibition: a ram without defect, valuation, and priestly atonement. For holy things, restitution plus a fifth is also required.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — Both cases prescribe a ram without defect as a trespass offering. Unlike 5:7–13, this unit supplies no bird or flour alternative; it does not repeat the slaughter or blood-handling directions.
- [[Atonement]] — The priest makes atonement with the ram and forgiveness is promised (16, 18). In the holy-things case, material restitution accompanies rather than replaces the offering.
- [[Forgiveness]] — Forgiveness is expressly promised in both cases (16, 18). The holy-things case joins pardon to restitution and priestly atonement; ignorance alone does not settle the liability.
- [[Priesthood]] — The priest receives the holy-things restitution with its added fifth and makes atonement with the ram (16); the second case also brings the ram to the priest (18). No priest is named here.
- [[Holiness]] — Wrong against Yahweh’s holy things requires repayment plus a fifth and a ram (15–16). Holy things are not interchangeable with ordinary possessions; the text does not enumerate the particular misuse.
- [[Theft and Restitution]] — Leviticus 5:15–16 extends the restitution theme to unwitting wrong in holy things: repayment plus one fifth, given to the priest, alongside a ram offering. This is distinct from the multiplied theft penalties of Exodus 22 and is not explicitly called deliberate theft. Verses 17–19 do not repeat the repayment rule.

## Notes

### Two cases, not one expanded penalty

The first case specifies holy things and adds a fifth to restitution. The second speaks
more generally of prohibited acts, repeats the ram, and does not explicitly repeat the
restitution clause. Importing that clause into every unknown offense would exceed these
verses. Likewise, the holy-things case does not list the particular goods or misuse.

The WEB’s valuation language does not supply a fixed numerical price for the ram. Its
reference to the sanctuary shekel specifies the valuation standard, not a modern currency
conversion. No additional ritual gestures or blood procedure should be invented from the
silence of this short unit; fuller priestly directions remain ahead in the queue.

### Liability without knowledge

Verses 17–18 describe wrongdoing committed without knowledge. They do not explain in
detail how the person comes to seek the offering, or whether the precise offense has
become identifiable by that point. The title’s unknown guilt follows this lack-of-knowledge
language; it should not be turned into a diagnosis of every troubled conscience.

There is a contrast with the affordability provisions in 5:7–13: this unit prescribes a
ram without offering cheaper alternatives. The text does not explain how inability to
provide that ram would be handled. Preserve that difference rather than silently extending
the flour concession to this rite.
