# The Burnt Offering

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 1:1–17

## Summary

Yahweh calls Moses from the Tent of Meeting and instructs him to teach Israel about offerings.
The burnt offering is described in three cases: cattle (3–9), sheep or goats (10–13), and
birds (14–17). The livestock offerer supplies and prepares the victim, while Aaron’s sons
handle its blood and burn the offering. The bird rite assigns the handling to the priest.
Each case ends with the offering described as a pleasant aroma to Yahweh.

The opening cattle case names the purpose of accepted approach and atonement (3–4).
The chapter gives a pattern of worship, not a narrative of a sacrifice being performed.
It continues the completed sanctuary story of Exodus by showing how speech and approach
operate at that dwelling.

## Key verses

> **1:1** Yahweh called to Moses, and spoke to him from the Tent of Meeting, saying,

> **1:2** “Speak to the children of Israel, and tell them, ‘When anyone of you offers an offering to Yahweh, you shall offer your offering of the livestock, from the herd and from the flock.

> **1:3** “‘If his offering is a burnt offering from the herd, he shall offer a male without defect. He shall offer it at the door of the Tent of Meeting, that he may be accepted before Yahweh.

> **1:4** He shall lay his hand on the head of the burnt offering, and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him.

> **1:5** He shall kill the bull before Yahweh. Aaron’s sons, the priests, shall present the blood and sprinkle the blood around on the altar that is at the door of the Tent of Meeting.

> **1:6** He shall flay the burnt offering, and cut it into pieces.

> **1:7** The sons of Aaron the priest shall put fire on the altar, and lay wood in order on the fire;

> **1:8** and Aaron’s sons, the priests, shall lay the pieces, the head, and the fat in order on the wood that is on the fire which is on the altar;

> **1:9** but its innards and its legs he shall wash with water. The priest shall burn all of it on the altar, for a burnt offering, an offering made by fire, of a pleasant aroma to Yahweh.

> **1:10** “‘If his offering is from the flock, from the sheep, or from the goats, for a burnt offering, he shall offer a male without defect.

> **1:11** He shall kill it on the north side of the altar before Yahweh. Aaron’s sons, the priests, shall sprinkle its blood around on the altar.

> **1:12** He shall cut it into its pieces, with its head and its fat. The priest shall lay them in order on the wood that is on the fire which is on the altar,

> **1:13** but the innards and the legs he shall wash with water. The priest shall offer the whole, and burn it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering made by fire, of a pleasant aroma to Yahweh.

> **1:14** “‘If his offering to Yahweh is a burnt offering of birds, then he shall offer his offering of turtledoves or of young pigeons.

> **1:15** The priest shall bring it to the altar, and wring off its head, and burn it on the altar; and its blood shall be drained out on the side of the altar;

> **1:16** and he shall take away its crop with its filth, and cast it beside the altar on the east part, in the place of the ashes.

> **1:17** He shall tear it by its wings, but shall not divide it apart. The priest shall burn it on the altar, on the wood that is on the fire. It is a burnt offering, an offering made by fire, of a pleasant aroma to Yahweh.

- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — The burnt offering has three forms: cattle, sheep or goats, and turtledoves or young pigeons.
- [[Atonement]] — Leviticus 1:4 explicitly connects the accepted burnt offering with atonement for its offerer.

- [[Priesthood]] — The regulated offering retains active lay participation: the livestock offerer brings, lays a hand on the cattle victim, kills, and prepares the animal; the priests handle blood and the altar.

## Notes

### One offering, three cases

The unit keeps the introductory call with the first offering law. Chapter 2 begins the
meal offering, making 1:1–17 a natural first unit. The cattle and flock must be male and
without defect; those requirements are not repeated for the birds. The north side is
specified for slaughter of the flock (11); the east side is where the bird’s discarded
material goes (16). Neither detail should be generalized into a uniform procedure.
The cattle are flayed (6), so “all” in verse 9 is read within the preparation already
specified. No destination for the hide is stated here. The bird is torn by its wings but
not divided apart (17). No symbolic meaning for each body part is supplied.

Birds broaden the available offerings, but chapter 1 does not explicitly make this option
conditional on poverty. That is different from the explicit affordability provision of
Leviticus 5:7 and 12:8. The bird case here does not prescribe a pair of birds. Those later
laws should not be imported into this paragraph.

### Acceptance without an invented explanation

Verses 3–4 join acceptance, hand-laying, and atonement. They do not name an offense,
require confession, or explicitly identify the hand as transferring sin. The burnt
offering therefore cannot simply be equated with the separate sin offering of chapter 4.
Nor should atonement be removed from its description merely because the unit gives no
specific sin. The identical pleasant-aroma ending belongs to cattle, flock, and birds;
the chapter does not rank divine acceptance by the size of the animal.

### Who does what

In the cattle and flock instructions the offerer kills and prepares, and the priests
handle the blood and altar. In the bird instructions the priest handles the victim
throughout the stated procedure. The contrast matters: regulated priesthood does not
remove the offerer’s participation. Aaron’s sons are collectively named; their individual
identities come from Exodus 28:1, not from four appearances in this chapter. Israel is the
people being addressed, not an appearance of Jacob.

### The voice from the dwelling

Exodus closes with Moses unable to enter the filled tent (40:35). Leviticus opens with
Yahweh calling and speaking from it. Speech answers the promise of Exodus 29:42 without
an explicit claim that Moses has entered or that the cloud has lifted. See
[[From the Filled Tent to the Speaking Tent - Exodus 40 and Leviticus 1]].
