# The Place of Sacrifice and the Life in the Blood

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 17:1–16

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to address Aaron, his sons, and all Israel. Killing cattle, sheep,
or goats without bringing them to the Tent of Meeting incurs blood liability and
cutting off. Field sacrifices are to become peace offerings to Yahweh, with blood
sprinkled and fat burned by the priest; sacrifice to goat idols must cease (1–7).
Israelites and resident foreigners must bring their offerings to the sanctuary (8–9).

The blood prohibition receives its rationale: life is in the blood, and God has given
blood on the altar for atonement (10–12). Hunters must pour out and cover the blood
of edible animals and birds (13–14). Eating animals that died or were torn brings
washing, bathing, and uncleanness until evening; failure to cleanse brings iniquity
(15–16). Altar use, hunting, and carcass eating remain distinct cases.

## Key verses

> **17:4** and hasn’t brought it to the door of the Tent of Meeting, to offer it as an offering to Yahweh before Yahweh’s tabernacle: blood shall be imputed to that man. He has shed blood; and that man shall be cut off from among his people.

> **17:7** They shall no more sacrifice their sacrifices to the goat idols, after which they play the prostitute. This shall be a statute forever to them throughout their generations.’

> **17:11** For the life of the flesh is in the blood; and I have given it to you on the altar to make atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that makes atonement by reason of the life.

> **17:13** “‘Whatever man there is of the children of Israel, or of the strangers who live as foreigners among them, who takes in hunting any animal or bird that may be eaten; he shall pour out its blood, and cover it with dust.

> **17:15** “‘Every person that eats what dies of itself, or that which is torn by animals, whether he is native-born or a foreigner, he shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the evening: then he shall be clean.

> **17:16** But if he doesn’t wash them, or bathe his flesh, then he shall bear his iniquity.’”

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh commands Moses to address priests and people (1–2), gives blood its altar function (11), and promises his own opposition to those who eat blood (10).
- [[Moses]] — Moses receives the speech and must transmit it to Aaron, his sons, and all Israel (1–2).
- [[Aaron]] — Aaron and his sons are named among the recipients (2).
- Aaron’s sons, Israelites, and foreigners are corporate recipients; no new individual is named.

## Places

- [[Horeb]] — continuing Sinai context (7:38); the mountain is not named here.
- The sanctuary, camp, and open field are the spatial distinctions; see [[The Dwelling of God]].

## Themes

- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — Livestock slaughter is brought to the Tent of Meeting as peace offerings (3–6); this redirects field sacrifices to Yahweh and excludes goat idols (7).
- [[Blood]] — Blood is excluded from food because of its relation to life and its God-given altar function (10–14).
- [[Atonement]] — Yahweh gives blood on the altar to make atonement by reason of the life (11).
- [[Priesthood]] — The priest sprinkles the peace offering’s blood and burns its fat at the sanctuary altar (6).
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — Eating an animal that died or was torn requires washing clothes, bathing, and waiting until evening (15).
- [[Holiness]] — Sacrificial allegiance and ordinary meals fall under the sanctuary’s ordering: field sacrifices must become offerings to Yahweh, blood is withheld from consumption, and carcass eating requires cleansing (3–16).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The rules distinguish livestock slaughter (3–7), offerings (8–9), blood eating (10–12), hunting (13–14), and carcass eating (15–16).
- [[Judgment]] — Unlawful slaughter incurs blood liability and cutting off (4); unauthorized offerings also incur cutting off (9).
- [[Life and Death]] — The life of flesh grounds the blood restriction (11, 14).
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — The door of the Tent of Meeting is the required place of sacrifice (4–9).
- [[Sin]] — The text assigns blood liability to prohibited slaughter (4), condemns sacrifice to goat idols as unfaithfulness (7), and assigns iniquity to neglect of cleansing after carcass eating (16).
- [[Idolatry]] — The purpose of redirecting field sacrifices is that Israel cease sacrificing to goat idols (5–7).
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Resident foreigners are expressly included in the offering and blood rules (8–14) and in carcass cleansing (15).

## Notes

### The extent of the slaughter rule

Verses 3–4 name killing cattle, sheep, or goats in or outside the camp, without
explicitly limiting the opening case to an already intended sacrifice. Verse 5
explains the redirection of field sacrifices into peace offerings. Reading the rule
as governing all slaughter of those animals in this camp setting follows the breadth
of its opening wording; limiting it to ritual slaughter is a narrower interpretation,
not a qualification printed in the stored WEB. Verse 13 separately permits hunting
edible animals and birds and tells the hunter what to do with their blood.

### Life, gift, and sanction

Verse 11 connects blood with life and with God’s gift for atonement. It does not give
a complete theory of every sacrifice. In particular, Leviticus 5:11–13 prescribes
flour for a poor person’s sin offering and promises atonement and forgiveness.
Those claims must stand together. Nor is every permissible handling of blood an
altar rite: the hunter covers it with dust.

The cutting-off clauses do not establish one human judicial mechanism. Verse 10
expressly names God as acting against the blood eater; verses 4 and 9 do not spell
out execution procedures. The distinction should remain visible.

### Prohibition and purification

**Apparent tension:** Exodus 22:31 prohibits eating torn flesh and assigns it to dogs;
Leviticus 17:15 prescribes cleansing after eating it, for native and foreigner alike.
A rule for what follows an act is not necessarily permission to perform it, but this
chapter does not restate the prohibition or explain the legal relationship. Leviticus
11:39–40 likewise regulates impurity from a dead edible animal. Chapter 17 explicitly
adds bathing and liability for failure to cleanse. See the connection page for both
claims without a silent harmonization.
