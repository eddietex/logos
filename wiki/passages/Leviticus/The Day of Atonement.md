# The Day of Atonement

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 16:1–34

## Summary

Yahweh speaks to Moses *"after the death of the two sons of Aaron"* (1) and turns that death
into a rule of entry: Aaron must not come at all times within the veil before the mercy seat,
lest he die, because Yahweh will appear in the cloud on it (2). Entry is possible, but only
in one prescribed way. Aaron bathes and puts on plain linen garments, called here *"the holy
garments"* (4). He brings a bull for his own sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, and
takes from the congregation two male goats for a sin offering and a ram (3, 5).

Lots divide the two goats: one for Yahweh, one *"for the scapegoat"* (8). Aaron kills his
bull, carries a censer of altar coals and beaten incense within the veil so that the cloud
covers the mercy seat, *"so that he will not die"* (12–13), and sprinkles the bull's blood on
and before the mercy seat (14). The people's goat follows the same path (15). This atones for
the Holy Place and the Tent of Meeting, which dwells with Israel *"in the middle of their
uncleanness"* (16), and then for the altar, on whose horns both bloods are put (18–19). No one
else may be in the tent while he does it (17).

Then the live goat: Aaron lays both hands on its head, confesses over it all Israel's
iniquities, transgressions, and sins, puts them on its head, and sends it by a man in
readiness into the wilderness, to *"a solitary land"* (20–22). Aaron removes the linen
garments in the tent, bathes, dresses again, and offers the two burnt offerings (23–25). The
goat's escort and the man who burns the carcasses outside the camp both wash before returning
(26–28).

The rite becomes a perpetual annual statute: on the tenth day of the seventh month Israel
afflicts its souls and does no work, native and resident foreigner alike (29). *"On this day
shall atonement be made for you, to cleanse you"* (30). The future anointed priest in his
father's place repeats it, once a year, for sanctuary, tent, altar, priests, and people
(32–34). The chapter closes: *"It was done as Yahweh commanded Moses"* (34).

## Key verses

> **16:2** and Yahweh said to Moses, “Tell Aaron your brother, not to come at all times into the Most Holy Place within the veil, before the mercy seat which is on the ark; lest he die: for I will appear in the cloud on the mercy seat.

> **16:16** He shall make atonement for the Holy Place, because of the uncleanness of the children of Israel, and because of their transgressions, even all their sins; and so he shall do for the Tent of Meeting, that dwells with them in the middle of their uncleanness.

> **16:21** Aaron shall lay both his hands on the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions, even all their sins; and he shall put them on the head of the goat, and shall send him away into the wilderness by the hand of a man who is in readiness.

> **16:22** The goat shall carry all their iniquities on himself to a solitary land, and he shall release the goat in the wilderness.

> **16:30** for on this day shall atonement be made for you, to cleanse you; from all your sins you shall be clean before Yahweh.

> **16:34** “This shall be an everlasting statute for you, to make atonement for the children of Israel once in the year because of all their sins.” It was done as Yahweh commanded Moses.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh speaks to Moses, will appear in the cloud on the mercy seat, and is the one before whom Israel is made clean (1–2, 30, 34).
- [[Moses]] — receives the speech and is charged to tell Aaron (1–2); the closing formula reports compliance with what Yahweh commanded him (34).
- [[Aaron]] — the sole officiant: bathing, linen garments, the bull for himself and his house, the lots, the blood within the veil, the confession over the live goat (3–25).
- [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]] and [[Abihu]] — not named, but the chapter is dated by their deaths (1).
- The man in readiness who leads the goat away, the one who burns the carcasses, and the future anointed priest *"in his father's place"* (32) are unnamed; none receives a page.

- [[Horeb]] — continuing Sinai context; not named in this chapter.

## Notes

### The chapter as an answer to Leviticus 10

The opening verse makes the deaths of Nadab and Abihu the occasion of the law. They *"came near
before Yahweh, and died"* (1); Aaron is now told he may not come at all times (2). The censer
recurs: theirs carried fire Yahweh had not commanded (10:1), Aaron's carries coals *"from off
the altar before Yahweh"* (12), and its incense cloud covers the mercy seat *"so that he will
not die"* (13). Leviticus 10:9 had already tied sober entry to the threat of death. **The
deaths are not reversed or explained further; the chapter answers them by prescribing the
one approach that is permitted.**

### Two goats, one sin offering

Verse 5 calls the pair together *"a sin offering"*. One is killed and its blood taken within
the veil (9, 15); the other is presented alive *"to make atonement for him"* (10) and sent away
bearing iniquities (21–22). The text does not explain why the task is divided. Readings that
call the first goat *propitiation* and the second *expiation*, or that identify a single
theory of substitution here, are interpretations; the verses themselves name sprinkling,
atoning, confessing, putting on the head, carrying, and releasing.

**On the word *"scapegoat"*:** the WEB renders the destination of the second lot as *"the
scapegoat"* (8, 10, 26). The underlying Hebrew is commonly transliterated *Azazel*, and whether
it names the goat, a place, a figure, or the act of removal is disputed. **This is not verified
here against a source-language edition**, and the wiki does not decide among the proposals; it
gives the term no page.

### Cleansing the sanctuary and cleansing the people

The sequence runs from the inside out: Most Holy Place, Tent of Meeting, altar (16–20). Verse 16
gives two grounds — Israel's *uncleanness* and their *transgressions* — so the rite gathers the
two categories that Leviticus 11–15 (uncleanness) and 4–6 (sin and trespass) had kept apart.
Verse 30 joins them again from the people's side: atonement is made *"to cleanse you; from all
your sins you shall be clean"*. The ordinary sin offerings of Leviticus 4 left the congregation's
blood before the veil (4:17); here, once a year, blood goes behind it.

**Uncertainty — which altar?** Verse 18 sends Aaron *"out to the altar that is before Yahweh"*,
and verse 12 takes coals *"from off the altar before Yahweh"*. Exodus 30:10 prescribes yearly
atonement on the horns of the incense altar, which stands inside the tent before the veil. The
stored WEB of Leviticus 16 does not say which altar is meant in 18; the incense altar and the
bronze altar of burnt offering are both proposed by interpreters. In its favor, Leviticus 4:18
uses nearly the same phrase — *"the altar which is before Yahweh, that is in the Tent of
Meeting"* — for the incense altar; but 16:18 has Aaron *"go out"* to it, and verse 17 speaks of
his coming out of the tent, so the phrase may mean leaving the tent or only the Most Holy Place.
The wiki records both readings and does not harmonize them.

### Garments

Aaron enters in linen coat, breeches, sash, and turban (4). Exodus 28 commands *"holy garments for
Aaron your brother, for glory and for beauty"* (28:2), including the ephod and breastplate, with
linen coat, turban, and sash among them (28:39) and linen breeches *"to cover the flesh of their
nakedness"* (28:42). Leviticus 16:4 calls the linen set alone *"the holy garments"*, and they stay
in the tent afterward (23). The chapter does not say why the ornate garments are not worn within
the veil.

### The people's part, and the stranger

Israel does not enter; it afflicts its souls and does no work (29, 31). The resident foreigner is
named alongside the native-born in the rest from work (29). Leviticus 23:27–32 restates the day
with *"deny yourselves"* / *"afflict yourselves"* and adds cutting-off and destruction for
those who do not; chapter 16 states no sanction. The difference is recorded in
[[The Day of Atonement Commanded Twice - Leviticus 16 and 23]].

### Once in the year

The rule of verse 2 is *not at all times*; verse 34 makes it *once in the year*. The provision
for a successor priest (32) makes the statute outlast Aaron himself. Hebrews 9:7 and 9:25 take
up this annual repetition — see [[The High Priest Once a Year - Leviticus 16 and Hebrews 9]].
