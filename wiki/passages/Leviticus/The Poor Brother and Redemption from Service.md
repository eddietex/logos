# The Poor Brother and Redemption from Service

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 25:35–55

## Summary

The chapter follows a brother down three steps of poverty. When he can no longer support
himself, Israel must uphold him and may take no interest or profit from him, in money or
food (35–38). When he sells himself to a fellow Israelite, he is not to be worked as a slave:
he serves as a hired servant until Jubilee, then goes back with his children to his family
and his fathers' possession, because Israelites are Yahweh's servants (39–43).

Between the Israelite buyer and the foreign one stands the law's sharpest line. Male and female
slaves may be bought from the surrounding nations and from the resident aliens' families,
held as property, and left to children *forever*; only brothers are shielded from harsh rule
(44–46). When a brother sells himself to a rich alien, he may be redeemed by a relative or by
himself at a price reckoned on the years left to Jubilee. He must be treated as a hired
servant, and if not redeemed he goes out at Jubilee with his children (47–54). The unit ends
on the reason it has already given: they are Yahweh's servants (55).

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Leviticus.md`. Complete unit:

> **35** “‘If your brother has become poor, and his hand can’t support himself among you; then you shall uphold him. He shall live with you like an alien and a temporary resident.

> **36** Take no interest from him or profit, but fear your God; that your brother may live among you.

> **37** You shall not lend him your money at interest, nor give him your food for profit.

> **38** I am Yahweh your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to give you the land of Canaan, and to be your God.

> **39** “‘If your brother has grown poor among you, and sells himself to you; you shall not make him to serve as a slave.

> **40** As a hired servant, and as a temporary resident, he shall be with you; he shall serve with you until the Year of Jubilee:

> **41** then he shall go out from you, he and his children with him, and shall return to his own family, and to the possession of his fathers.

> **42** For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt. They shall not be sold as slaves.

> **43** You shall not rule over him with harshness, but shall fear your God.

> **44** “‘As for your male and your female slaves, whom you may have; of the nations that are around you, from them you may buy male and female slaves.

> **45** Moreover of the children of the aliens who live among you, of them you may buy, and of their families who are with you, which they have conceived in your land; and they will be your property.

> **46** You may make them an inheritance for your children after you, to hold for a possession; of them may you take your slaves forever; but over your brothers the children of Israel you shall not rule, one over another, with harshness.

> **47** “‘If an alien or temporary resident with you becomes rich, and your brother beside him has grown poor, and sells himself to the stranger or foreigner living among you, or to a member of the stranger’s family;

> **48** after he is sold he may be redeemed. One of his brothers may redeem him;

> **49** or his uncle, or his uncle’s son, may redeem him, or any who is a close relative to him of his family may redeem him; or if he has grown rich, he may redeem himself.

> **50** He shall reckon with him who bought him from the year that he sold himself to him to the Year of Jubilee. The price of his sale shall be according to the number of years; he shall be with him according to the time of a hired servant.

> **51** If there are yet many years, according to them he shall give back the price of his redemption out of the money that he was bought for.

> **52** If there remain but a few years to the year of jubilee, then he shall reckon with him; according to his years of service he shall give back the price of his redemption.

> **53** As a servant hired year by year shall he be with him. He shall not rule with harshness over him in your sight.

> **54** If he isn’t redeemed by these means, then he shall be released in the Year of Jubilee, he, and his children with him.

> **55** For to me the children of Israel are servants; they are my servants whom I brought out of the land of Egypt. I am Yahweh your God.

## People

- [[God]] — speaker; he identifies himself by the exodus (38, 42, 55) and claims Israelites as his
  servants.
- [[Moses]] — recipient of the continuing speech, identified at 25:1.
- The poor brother, his buyer, the rich alien, and the redeeming relatives are legal roles; no
  named individual appears. The bought slaves of 44–46 are likewise unnamed.

## Places

- [[Egypt]] — the house Yahweh brought Israel out of (38, 42, 55), and the model of the rule
  Israelite masters may not exercise (43).
- [[Canaan]] — the land Yahweh gives (38); this is the one naming of it in the chapter.
- [[Horeb]] — Sinai, the setting supplied at 25:1, not repeated in this unit.

## Notes

**A descent in three steps.** The text sets up the stages with parallel openings: *wəkî-yāmûk
ʾāḥîkā*, *if your brother has grown poor* (35, 39), and *ûmāk ʾāḥîkā* (47). The field has already
gone at 25:25, which opens the same way. He next cannot support himself (35), then sells
himself to an Israelite (39), and lastly to an outsider (47). This is the reader's sequence of the
law's cases; the text does not say each man passes through all of them.

**Not literally "like" an alien?** WEB's *like an alien and a temporary resident* (35) has
no comparative word in the Hebrew: *gēr wətôšāb wāḥay ʿimmāk*, *a stranger and a resident, and he
shall live with you*. Verse 40, by contrast, has the particle twice: *kəśākîr kətôšāb*. Hence
*like* is a translator's supply in 35, and the wording may mean *live with you as* or describe
how he is to be upheld. The grammar is not settled here. Whatever the construction, the words
are the ones Yahweh applied to all Israel at 25:23: the poor man's new standing is everyone's.

**Two nouns and two uses.** Verse 36 forbids *nešek wətarbît*, verse 37 *nešek* for money and
*marbît* for food. Exodus 22:25 had only *nešek*. WEB's *interest* and *profit* separate them;
how the terms differed in practice is not stated in the passage, and no explanation is offered here.

**Fear of God where no one else is watching.** *Wəyārēʾtā mēʾĕlōheykā*, *fear your God*, closes
the commands at 36 and 43, as it did at 25:17, 19:14, and 19:32. Each concerns a wrong the
victim cannot easily answer: the deaf, the blind, the old, the buyer, the debtor, the sold man.
No human penalty is stated for any breach in 35–43. That last point is the text's silence, not a
finding that none existed. Verse 53 changes the check for the foreign master: *in your sight*.
He does not owe the fear of Israel's God in this text, so the community's eyes do the watching.

**"Sells himself."** WEB's *sells himself* (39, 47) renders the niphal *nimkar*, which may be
passive or reflexive, as WEB's own *after he is sold* at 48 shows with the same verb. Verse 50's
*himmākərô* is the same stem. The text therefore does not by itself decide whether he, his family,
or a creditor initiates the sale. Nor does it use the word *debt*; poverty is the only stated cause.

**The two prohibitions depend on *as*.** At 39 the brother is not to serve *ʿăbōdat ʿābed*,
the service of a slave. At 42 they shall not be sold *mimkeret ʿābed*, the sale of a slave. Yet
verses 39 and 47 assume sale, and verse 40 assumes labor. The law forbids a status rather than
the transaction. A brother can be sold; he cannot be sold *as a slave*, because he has an
owner already.

**Permanent slavery for others.** Verses 44–46 are an authorization, not a concession to be
softened: *you may buy* (*tiqnû*), *your property*, an *inheritance*, *forever*. Their force is
in the vocabulary. *ʾĂḥuzzâ*, the *possession* the brother returns to at 41 and the land held
under Jubilee (25:10, 13), is used of people at 45–46, and *ləʿōlām* (46) names the permanence
25:23 denies a seller of land, though that verse's word is *liṣmitut*. **Land may not be sold forever;
foreign persons may be held forever.** The *with harshness* ban is explicitly limited to brothers (46); the
text gives no such ruling for foreign slaves here, in either direction.

**Tensions left standing.**

1. *Liberty to all its inhabitants* (25:10) and *your slaves forever* (25:46) are in one chapter.
   The chapter's own later clause restricts the first in practice; it does not comment on the conflict.
2. The stranger is to be loved *as yourself* because Israel were foreigners in Egypt (19:33–34),
   and there is *one kind of law* for foreigner and native-born (24:22); yet 25:45 allows buying
   the children of resident aliens. The contexts differ, since 24:22 follows blasphemy and injury
   cases. But Egypt, the reason for kindness at 19:34, is used here to protect only Israelites.
3. Exodus 21:2 releases the Hebrew servant in the seventh year; this unit releases at Jubilee,
   which could be much later or sooner. Neither text refers to the other. See
   [[The Hebrew Servant Goes Free - Exodus 21, Deuteronomy 15, and Jeremiah 34]].

**Redemption is stated for one buyer.** Verses 48–52 provide redemption from the alien buyer.
Verses 39–43 give none for an Israelite buyer; the text states no reason, and silence need not
mean prohibition. The ladder runs brother, uncle, uncle's son, *šəʾēr bəśārô*, *flesh of his flesh
from his family*, then the man himself. WEB's *may redeem* matches the Hebrew imperfect's range;
*yigʾālennû* could be read as *may* or *shall*, and 48's *gəʾullâ tihyeh-lô* gives the sold man a
right. The noun *gōʾēl* itself does not occur in 47–55: the unit has the verb and *gəʾullâ*.

**A price that treats him as already partly free.** The reckoning runs from sale to Jubilee
(50), and the redemption money is proportional to the years left (51–52), the same logic as land
sales (25:15–16, 27). What was bought is remaining labor, not the man.

**Scope and sources.** This remains the Sinai address, with no narrated sale, redemption, or
Jubilee. WEB quoted as stored. Hebrew checked against Miqra according to the Masorah (Sefaria),
Leviticus 19:14, 32; 25:10, 13, 17, 23, 25, 35–55. Other comparisons use stored WEB. Boundary
retained as planned: 26:1 turns from cases to blessings.
