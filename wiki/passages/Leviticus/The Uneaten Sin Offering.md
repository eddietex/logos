# The Uneaten Sin Offering

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 10:12–20

## Summary

After the deaths of Nadab and Abihu, Moses turns to Aaron and to Eleazar and Ithamar, "his
sons who were left" (12), and directs them to finish the eighth-day meal. The meal offering
that remains is to be eaten without yeast beside the altar in a holy place, because it is most
holy. The waved breast and heaved thigh of the peace offerings are to be eaten in a clean
place by Aaron, his sons, and his daughters, as a portion forever.

Moses then inquires about the goat of the people's sin offering and finds it burned. He is
angry with Eleazar and Ithamar: its blood was not brought into the sanctuary, so it should
have been eaten there, as he had commanded. Aaron answers for them. The offerings were made
that day, "and such things as these have happened to me" (19); would eating the sin offering
have pleased Yahweh? Moses hears him, and it is pleasing in his sight (20).

## Key verses

> **10:12** Moses spoke to Aaron, and to Eleazar and to Ithamar, his sons who were left, “Take the meal offering that remains of the offerings of Yahweh made by fire, and eat it without yeast beside the altar; for it is most holy;

> **10:13** and you shall eat it in a holy place, because it is your portion, and your sons’ portion, of the offerings of Yahweh made by fire: for so I am commanded.

> **10:14** The waved breast and the heaved thigh you shall eat in a clean place, you, and your sons, and your daughters with you: for they are given as your portion, and your sons’ portion, out of the sacrifices of the peace offerings of the children of Israel.

> **10:15** The heaved thigh and the waved breast they shall bring with the offerings made by fire of the fat, to wave it for a wave offering before Yahweh: and it shall be yours, and your sons’ with you, as a portion forever; as Yahweh has commanded.”

> **10:16** Moses diligently inquired about the goat of the sin offering, and, behold, it was burned: and he was angry with Eleazar and with Ithamar, the sons of Aaron who were left, saying,

> **10:17** “Why haven’t you eaten the sin offering in the place of the sanctuary, since it is most holy, and he has given it to you to bear the iniquity of the congregation, to make atonement for them before Yahweh?

> **10:18** Behold, its blood was not brought into the inner part of the sanctuary: you certainly should have eaten it in the sanctuary, as I commanded.”

> **10:19** Aaron spoke to Moses, “Behold, today they have offered their sin offering and their burnt offering before Yahweh; and such things as these have happened to me. If I had eaten the sin offering today, would it have been pleasing in Yahweh’s sight?”

> **10:20** When Moses heard that, it was pleasing in his sight.

## Notes

### Finishing the eighth day

Every item Moses names comes from the inaugural service of chapter 9: the meal offering of
9:17, the breast and thigh waved in 9:21, and the goat for the people offered in 9:15. Unit
10:12–20 is therefore not a new rite but the completion of one that the deaths interrupted.
The instructions repeat earlier rules rather than introduce new ones: unleavened remnants in a
holy place (6:16) and the breast and thigh for Aaron and his sons (7:31–34).

One detail is newly explicit. Verse 14 includes Aaron's daughters among those who eat the
breast and thigh, in "a clean place". Leviticus 6:29 and 7:6 reserve the most holy sin and
trespass flesh for "every male among the priests". The meal offering here is likewise "most
holy" and is assigned to Aaron and his sons in a holy place (12–13); the daughters are named
only for the peace-offering portions. The text marks the difference by place — holy versus
clean — and by eaters; it does not state the reason.

### Moses' complaint

Moses' argument applies the rule of 6:26–30: a sin offering is eaten by the priest in a holy
place unless its blood is brought into the Tent of Meeting, in which case it is burned. The
goat's blood "was not brought into the inner part of the sanctuary" (18), so by that rule it
should have been eaten. Verse 17 gives a further reason: the offering was given to the priests
"to bear the iniquity of the congregation, to make atonement for them". The text joins eating
to that purpose without explaining how one serves the other; no claim about the underlying
Hebrew wording is made here.

There is a loose end in chapter 9 itself. Aaron's own sin-offering calf had its blood put on
the altar's horns, yet its meat and skin were burned outside the camp (9:9–11) — the pattern
of the priest's ordination bull (Exodus 29:14; Leviticus 8:17) rather than of 6:30. The
people's goat was then offered "for sin, like the first" (9:15). Whether "like the first"
extends to the burning is not said. Moses plainly treats the goat as food the priests should
have eaten. The question is recorded on
[[Eaten or Burned - The Sin Offering in Leviticus 6, 9, and 10]], not resolved.

### Aaron's answer and Moses' acceptance

Aaron answers though Moses addressed his sons, and he does not dispute the rule. He sets the
day's events beside it — "such things as these have happened to me" — and asks, "If I had eaten the
sin offering today, would it have been pleasing in Yahweh’s sight?" (19). The text does not say whether the
burning was a deliberate decision or by whom it was made, and it does not explain what made
eating unfitting: grief, defilement, fear after 10:1–2, or something else. Those remain
interpretations.

Verse 20 reports that the answer "was pleasing in his sight" — Moses' sight. No word from
Yahweh is reported, so the passage records Moses' acceptance, not a divine ruling. Read against
10:1–11, the contrast is pointed: an uncommanded offering brought death, while a withheld meal
is questioned and allowed. The difference the text supplies is that Aaron's answer appeals to
what would please Yahweh; it does not formulate a general principle from the case.
