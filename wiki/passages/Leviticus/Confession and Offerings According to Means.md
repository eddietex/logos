# Confession and Offerings According to Means

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 5:1–13

## Summary

Four cases lead into confession and an offering: withholding known testimony after an
adjuration, contact with an unclean carcass, contact with human uncleanness, and rash
swearing. Hiddenness and subsequent knowledge matter in the latter cases. The guilty person
must confess the particular sin and bring a female lamb or goat for a sin offering.

The required gift adjusts to means. Someone unable to afford a lamb brings two birds,
one for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering. Someone unable to afford the birds
brings a tenth of an ephah of fine flour, without oil or frankincense. The priest burns a
memorial handful and receives the remainder. The bird and flour procedures both explicitly
promise forgiveness; poverty changes the offering without removing the remedy.

## Key verses

> **5:1** “‘If anyone sins, in that he hears the voice of adjuration, he being a witness, whether he has seen or known, if he doesn’t report it, then he shall bear his iniquity.

> **5:2** “‘Or if anyone touches any unclean thing, whether it is the carcass of an unclean animal, or the carcass of unclean livestock, or the carcass of unclean creeping things, and it is hidden from him, and he is unclean, then he shall be guilty.

> **5:3** “‘Or if he touches the uncleanness of man, whatever his uncleanness is with which he is unclean, and it is hidden from him; when he knows of it, then he shall be guilty.

> **5:4** “‘Or if anyone swears rashly with his lips to do evil, or to do good, whatever it is that a man might utter rashly with an oath, and it is hidden from him; when he knows of it, then he shall be guilty of one of these.

> **5:5** It shall be, when he is guilty of one of these, he shall confess that in which he has sinned:

> **5:6** and he shall bring his trespass offering to Yahweh for his sin which he has sinned, a female from the flock, a lamb or a goat, for a sin offering; and the priest shall make atonement for him concerning his sin.

> **5:7** “‘If he can’t afford a lamb, then he shall bring his trespass offering for that in which he has sinned, two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, to Yahweh; one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering.

> **5:8** He shall bring them to the priest, who shall first offer the one which is for the sin offering, and wring off its head from its neck, but shall not sever it completely.

> **5:9** He shall sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar; and the rest of the blood shall be drained out at the base of the altar. It is a sin offering.

> **5:10** He shall offer the second for a burnt offering, according to the ordinance; and the priest shall make atonement for him concerning his sin which he has sinned, and he shall be forgiven.

> **5:11** “‘But if he can’t afford two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, then he shall bring his offering for that in which he has sinned, one tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a sin offering. He shall put no oil on it, and he shall not put any frankincense on it, for it is a sin offering.

> **5:12** He shall bring it to the priest, and the priest shall take his handful of it as the memorial portion, and burn it on the altar, on the offerings of Yahweh made by fire. It is a sin offering.

> **5:13** The priest shall make atonement for him concerning his sin that he has sinned in any of these things, and he will be forgiven; and the rest shall be the priest’s, as the meal offering.’”

## Themes

- [[Sin]] — Withheld testimony, impurity contact, and rash swearing are addressed together, without making every offense identical or every case inadvertent.
- [[Guilt and Conscience]] — Awareness of liability leads to confession of the particular sin (5), not merely an unspecified feeling of guilt.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The prescribed response includes acknowledgment and an offering whose cost is adjusted to the offerer’s means.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — A female flock animal, two birds with different functions, or fine flour serve the stated cases according to means (6–13).
- [[Atonement]] — The priest makes atonement even with the flour sin offering (11–13). Atonement here cannot be defined as invariably requiring this offerer to provide an animal.
- [[Forgiveness]] — Forgiveness is expressly promised after both the bird and flour procedures (10, 13); the less costly provision does not promise a lesser pardon.
- [[Priesthood]] — The priest offers the sin-offering bird first, then the burnt offering; with flour he burns a memorial handful and receives the remainder (8–13).
- [[Blood]] — The bird’s blood is sprinkled on the altar’s side and drained at its base (9). The flour procedure prescribes no blood from this offering yet promises atonement and forgiveness (11–13).
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — The ritual/moral distinction needs qualification: these particular hidden-contact cases incur guilt and require confession and a sin offering (2–6). This does not make every unclean condition a moral offense.
- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — Inability to afford a lamb, and then even two birds, receives explicit provision (7, 11). Access to the stated remedy is not restricted to livestock owners.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Speech can fail by omission of testimony or by rash oath; the corrective response itself includes specific confession (1, 4–5).
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — Hearing an adjuration and failing to testify (1) is distinct from uttering a rash oath (4). The remedy does not command carrying out an evil promise.

## Notes

### Confession and knowledge

Verse 5 requires confession of the particular sin. It does not specify the exact words,
audience, or an accompanying hand-laying formula. Verse 1 does not explicitly repeat the
hiddenness language of verses 2–4, so withholding testimony should not simply be renamed
an accidental offense. Likewise, the text does not explain the precise circumstances
that make the impurity contact culpable; a concealed sanctuary entry should not be
supplied as though it were narrated.

### Distinct rites and English labels

The WEB uses both trespass-offering and sin-offering language in verses 6–7. The procedures
here must be described from their actual provisions rather than conflated with the ram
and restitution rules beginning at 5:14. No claim about exact Hebrew terminology is made.
The birds are not two interchangeable sin offerings: the sin offering comes first, and
the second bird is a burnt offering. The first bird’s head is not completely severed (8).

The flour is expressly a sin offering. Its memorial is burned on Yahweh’s offerings made
by fire, but these verses do not explain that other offerings’ blood substitutes for the
flour offerer’s blood rite. Nor does the passage abandon the sacrificial system: it
prescribes priestly altar service within it. Any universal claim that each forgiven
person here must bring a blood-bearing victim conflicts with verses 11–13.
