# The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 6:8–23

## Summary

Yahweh directs Moses to command Aaron and his sons about the continuing work of the altar.
The burnt offering remains on its hearth overnight; the priest removes the ashes in linen,
changes garments to carry them outside the camp, and adds wood every morning. The fire must
not go out. The same altar receives the fat of peace offerings.

The meal offering's memorial is burned, while Aaron's male descendants eat the unleavened
remainder in the court of the Tent of Meeting. This is a most holy portion given by Yahweh.
A second divine speech prescribes the offering associated with Aaron's anointing and his
successor: fine flour with oil, divided between morning and evening. Every priest's meal
offering is wholly burned and may not be eaten.

## Key verses

> **6:8** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **6:9** “Command Aaron and his sons, saying, ‘This is the law of the burnt offering: the burnt offering shall be on the hearth on the altar all night until the morning; and the fire of the altar shall be kept burning on it.

> **6:10** The priest shall put on his linen garment, and he shall put on his linen breeches upon his body; and he shall remove the ashes from where the fire has consumed the burnt offering on the altar, and he shall put them beside the altar.

> **6:11** He shall take off his garments, and put on other garments, and carry the ashes outside the camp to a clean place.

> **6:12** The fire on the altar shall be kept burning on it, it shall not go out; and the priest shall burn wood on it every morning: and he shall lay the burnt offering in order upon it, and shall burn on it the fat of the peace offerings.

> **6:13** Fire shall be kept burning on the altar continually; it shall not go out.

> **6:14** “‘This is the law of the meal offering: the sons of Aaron shall offer it before Yahweh, before the altar.

> **6:15** He shall take from there his handful of the fine flour of the meal offering, and of its oil, and all the frankincense which is on the meal offering, and shall burn it on the altar for a pleasant aroma, as its memorial, to Yahweh.

> **6:16** That which is left of it Aaron and his sons shall eat. It shall be eaten without yeast in a holy place. They shall eat it in the court of the Tent of Meeting.

> **6:17** It shall not be baked with yeast. I have given it as their portion of my offerings made by fire. It is most holy, as the sin offering, and as the trespass offering.

> **6:18** Every male among the children of Aaron shall eat of it, as their portion forever throughout your generations, from the offerings of Yahweh made by fire. Whoever touches them shall be holy.’”

> **6:19** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **6:20** “This is the offering of Aaron and of his sons, which they shall offer to Yahweh in the day when he is anointed: one tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a meal offering perpetually, half of it in the morning, and half of it in the evening.

> **6:21** It shall be made with oil in a griddle. When it is soaked, you shall bring it in. You shall offer the meal offering in baked pieces for a pleasant aroma to Yahweh.

> **6:22** The anointed priest that will be in his place from among his sons shall offer it. By a statute forever, it shall be wholly burned to Yahweh.

> **6:23** Every meal offering of a priest shall be wholly burned. It shall not be eaten.”

## Notes

### Ordinary labor within holy service

The repeated command to keep the fire burning (9, 12, 13) includes practical tasks: clothing,
ash removal, and fresh wood. This passage does not describe the fire's first ignition or say
it perpetuates itself. A clean destination outside the camp is prescribed for the ashes;
being outside does not itself make a location unclean. No reason for the garment change is
stated, so explanations about protecting clothing or separating kinds of work remain inference.

### Eating is restricted, not opposed to holiness

Verses 16–18 specify the court, unleavened preparation, and male Aaronide eaters. Holy food
can be eaten by authorized people. The WEB's contact clause, “Whoever touches them shall be
holy” (18), should not be turned into a promise of automatic moral improvement or a license
for outsiders to eat; the preceding restriction remains. No claim about the exact Hebrew
construction is made here.

### A necessary qualification to the earlier meal-offering rule

Leviticus 2:3, 10 assigns the remainder to Aaron and his sons. Leviticus 6:23 explicitly
prohibits eating a priest's meal offering. An unqualified claim that *every* meal offering
feeds the priests would therefore contradict this verse: the offerer's identity matters.
This is a stated exception to the general presentation, not evidence that grain is unsuitable.

Verse 20 combines the day of anointing with a perpetual meal offering divided between morning
and evening; verse 22 extends the obligation to the anointed successor. The WEB wording leaves
a question about how inauguration and recurrence fit together. The perpetual language supports
ongoing service, but this page does not settle every priest's personal schedule or collapse the
whole prescription into a one-time ceremony. The broader rule in verse 23 is clear regardless:
no priest's meal offering is eaten.
