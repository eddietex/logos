# The Feast of Booths

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 23:33–44

## Summary

Yahweh tells [[Moses]] to announce a seven-day feast beginning on the fifteenth day of the
seventh month. The first day is a holy convocation without regular work; seven days of
fire offerings lead to an eighth-day solemn assembly, also with an offering and no regular
work (33–36). A calendar summary distinguishes appointed offerings from Sabbaths, gifts,
vows, and freewill offerings (37–38).

The instruction returns to the feast at the gathering of the land's produce. Israel takes
fruit and branches and rejoices before Yahweh for seven days (39–41). All native-born
Israelites must live in temporary shelters for seven days so later generations will know
that Yahweh made Israel live in shelters when he brought them out of Egypt (42–43).
Harvest abundance and remembered dependence belong to the same celebration.

Moses declares Yahweh's appointed feasts to Israel (44). This narrates transmission of the
calendar, not a performance of the feast. The chapter ends with the community receiving
its sacred times through Moses.

> **33** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **34** “Speak to the children of Israel, and say, ‘On the fifteenth day of this seventh month is the feast of tents for seven days to Yahweh.

> **37** “‘These are the appointed feasts of Yahweh, which you shall proclaim to be holy convocations, to offer an offering made by fire to Yahweh, a burnt offering, and a meal offering, a sacrifice, and drink offerings, each on its own day—

> **39** “‘So on the fifteenth day of the seventh month, when you have gathered in the fruits of the land, you shall keep the feast of Yahweh seven days: on the first day shall be a solemn rest, and on the eighth day shall be a solemn rest.

> **40** You shall take on the first day the fruit of goodly trees, branches of palm trees, and boughs of thick trees, and willows of the brook; and you shall rejoice before Yahweh your God seven days.

> **41** You shall keep it as a feast to Yahweh seven days in the year. It is a statute forever throughout your generations. You shall keep it in the seventh month.

> **42** You shall dwell in temporary shelters for seven days. All who are native-born in Israel shall dwell in temporary shelters,

> **44** Moses declared to the children of Israel the appointed feasts of Yahweh.

## Notes

**Seven days and an eighth day are distinguished.** The seven-day feast, rejoicing, and
shelter-dwelling are not extended to eight days by the added assembly (34, 36, 40–42).
The text supplies both durations; neither needs correction.

**Harvest and history explain the same feast.** Verse 39 situates the celebration after
gathering produce; verse 43 gives the shelters their teaching purpose. The WEB calls the
feast “tents” (34) and the dwellings “temporary shelters” (42–43); the conventional page
title “Booths” does not replace the source wording in the quotation.

**The plants are named, but their handling is not fully described.** Verse 40 commands
taking fruit and branches and rejoicing. It does not explicitly say here to build the
shelters from those materials, identify every botanical species, or describe later rituals.
No source-language or later-practice claim is inferred from this English text.

**Scope remains explicit.** Verse 42 names all native-born Israelites for shelter-dwelling.
That wording is retained without inferring that foreigners are prohibited from celebrating.
Verses 37–38 summarize the appointed offerings and distinguish additional obligations;
they do not authorize replacing vows with festival offerings.

The planned boundary is retained: the speech at 33 and Moses’ declaration at 44 complete
the chapter. Comparisons for this addition were checked in stored WEB Exodus 13, 23, 34
and Leviticus 23. No external quotation or new Hebrew/Greek claim is introduced.
