# The Blasphemer and One Law for All

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 24:10–23

## Summary

An unnamed son of an Israelite woman and an Egyptian man fights with an Israelite
in the camp, blasphemes the Name, and curses. His mother is Shelomith, daughter of
Dibri, of Dan’s tribe. He is brought to Moses and held until Yahweh’s will is declared
(10–12). The narrative preserves family identification but not the words spoken.

Yahweh commands that the offender be taken outside the camp, that those who heard
him lay hands on his head, and that the congregation stone him. The ensuing laws
apply the blasphemy penalty to foreigner and native-born alike, distinguish human
killing from animal loss, and prescribe corresponding bodily injury (13–22).
Moses speaks to Israel; the people carry out the execution as commanded (23).

> **18** He who strikes an animal mortally shall make it good, life for life.

> **19** If anyone injures his neighbor; as he has done, so shall it be done to him:

> **23** Moses spoke to the children of Israel; and they brought him who had cursed out of the camp, and stoned him with stones. The children of Israel did as Yahweh commanded Moses.

## Notes

**The family is identified; the accusation is not racial.** The text names the
mother and grandfather but leaves the offender, father, and opponent unnamed.
The rule concerns blasphemy regardless of native or foreign status (16). Neither
the cause of the fight nor a legal classification of the son is stated.

**Custody leaves a question pending.** Verse 12 does not specify whether the
uncertainty concerns the penalty, procedure, or application to this individual.
The prior prohibition in Exodus 22:28 means this need not be discovery that
blasphemy is wrong for the first time.

**Hand-laying is prescribed but not explained.** All who heard him must perform
it (14). Identifying the offender and affirming responsibility are possible
interpretations; a transfer of guilt or sacrificial equivalence is not stated.
Verse 23 reports removal and stoning, then summarizes obedience rather than
individually repeating each procedural detail.

**Equal law has a defined setting.** Leviticus 24:22 forbids a different standard
for foreigner and native-born here. It does not erase the distinct servant remedies
of Exodus 21:26–27. The corresponding-injury command is explicit; this unit does
not supply evidence that historical enforcement always substituted money.
Leviticus 19:18 prohibits private vengeance; the commanded communal sentence
must be read alongside that prohibition rather than silently replacing it.
