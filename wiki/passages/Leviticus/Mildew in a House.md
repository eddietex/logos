# Mildew in a House

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 14:33–57

## Summary

Yahweh addresses Moses and Aaron about houses in the land he will give Israel. The owner
reports a suspected plague; the priest orders the house emptied before inspection.
Greenish or reddish hollow streaks that appear deeper than the wall lead to seven days
of closure. Spread requires removing affected stones, scraping the interior, disposing
of material outside the city, and replacing stones and plaster.

If the plague returns and spreads after repair, the house must be demolished and its
materials removed to an unclean place outside the city. Entry during closure makes a
person unclean until evening; lying down or eating there requires washing clothes.
If examination finds no spread after replastering, the priest pronounces the house
clean and performs the bird rite. The conclusion gathers the skin, garment, and house
laws under their purpose of teaching clean and unclean.

## Key verses

> **14:34** “When you have come into the land of Canaan, which I give to you for a possession, and I put a spreading mildew in a house in the land of your possession,

> **14:36** The priest shall command that they empty the house, before the priest goes in to examine the plague, that all that is in the house not be made unclean. Afterward the priest shall go in to inspect the house.

> **14:45** He shall break down the house, its stones, and its timber, and all the house’s mortar. He shall carry them out of the city into an unclean place.

> **14:48** “If the priest shall come in, and examine it, and behold, the plague hasn’t spread in the house, after the house was plastered, then the priest shall pronounce the house clean, because the plague is healed.

> **14:53** but he shall let the living bird go out of the city into the open field. So shall he make atonement for the house; and it shall be clean.”

> **14:57** to teach when it is unclean, and when it is clean. This is the law of leprosy.

## Themes

- [[Clean and Unclean]] — A house can be unclean, repaired, condemned, or pronounced clean (37–48). Emptying it before inspection protects its contents from uncleanness (36). Entry during closure brings uncleanness until evening; lying down or eating also requires washing clothes (46–47). Cleanness after repair is followed by the prescribed bird rite (48–53).
- [[Priesthood]] — The owner reports a suspected plague; the priest examines and rules, orders seven-day closure, removal and replastering, and demolition if the plague returns and spreads (35–45). The conclusion frames these laws as teaching when something is unclean or clean (54–57), extending the commission of 10:10–11 to houses.
- [[Atonement]] — The WEB explicitly speaks of making atonement for the house (53), concluding the bird, blood, and water rite. This extends atonement beyond persons and sanctuary objects to an ordinary dwelling. **Qualification:** the personal forgiveness formulas of Leviticus 4–5 do not define every use: here the result is a clean house, with no confession or stated forgiveness of its owner.
- [[Blood]] — Blood from the slain bird, together with running water, cedar, scarlet, hyssop, and the living bird, is used to sprinkle the house seven times (49–53). The living bird is released outside the city. No altar application or bodily marking is prescribed in this house rite, unlike the eighth-day rites for a person in 14:14–29.
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The house procedure distinguishes reporting, emptying, inspection, closure, repair, recurrence, demolition, and cleansing (35–53). The concluding law groups skin, garments, and houses for instruction about clean and unclean (54–57), without making their individual remedies interchangeable.
- [[Holiness]] — The purity framework extends to ordinary dwellings in the promised land (34–53). The house is pronounced clean, not consecrated as a sanctuary. Clean status and holy status must remain distinct even where priestly cleansing uses blood.
- [[Sin]] — Yahweh says he puts the mildew in the house (34), but identifies no offense by its owner. **Qualification:** atonement for the house (53) cannot be treated as proof that the owner committed a particular sin. The named problem is the house’s uncleanness, not a narrated transgression or a confession.
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — One bird is killed over running water and the living bird is released after the cleansing rite (49–53). The house procedure names no sin, trespass, burnt, or meal offering and no altar service. Those offerings in 14:10–32 belong to the restored person’s distinct eighth-day procedure and must not be imported here.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Canaan is named as the possession Yahweh gives (34); the law anticipates settled houses and cities before entry is narrated. Possession does not exempt a dwelling from inspection, costly repair, or demolition. Gift of land and regulation of life in it stand together in this instruction.

## Notes

### Agency, diagnosis, and blame

Verse 34 combines the gift of land with Yahweh’s stated agency in the mildew. It does
not identify an owner’s sin or explain why a particular house is affected. Neither a
modern biological diagnosis nor an allegory of a sinful household is established by
this wording. The title follows the stored WEB’s house terminology.

### Repair before demolition; cleansing after repair

The law does not command destruction on the owner’s first report. Emptying the house
protects possessions before inspection; repair intervenes between the first finding
of spread and the final destructive verdict. Verse 48 attributes the clean ruling to
the healed condition. Verses 49–53 prescribe the cleansing rite rather than claiming
that sprinkling replaces the repair and examination.

The two birds, cedar, scarlet, hyssop, water, and sevenfold sprinkling repeat 14:4–7.
No symbolic explanation of those materials is supplied here, and the living bird is
not said to carry away sin. The house receives atonement without the person’s later
washing, shaving, blood-and-oil marking, or eighth-day offerings.
