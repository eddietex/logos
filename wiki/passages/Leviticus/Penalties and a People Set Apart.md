# Penalties and a People Set Apart

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 20:1–27

## Summary

Yahweh tells Moses to address Israel about giving offspring to Molech. The people must stone
the offender, and Yahweh also promises to cut him off. If the community hides its eyes,
God will still oppose the man, his family, and those following him in that worship (1–5).
Turning to mediums brings divine cutting off; holiness and obedience rest on Yahweh’s
sanctifying action (6–8).

The speech assigns penalties for cursing parents and for forbidden sexual relations
(9–21). These vary: death, burning, cutting off, bearing iniquity, and childlessness
must be distinguished. The closing exhortation joins obedience, continued residence in
the land, separation from other peoples, and clean/unclean animal distinctions (22–26).
A final provision prescribes stoning for a man or woman who is a medium or wizard (27).

## Key verses

> **20:3** I also will set my face against that person, and will cut him off from among his people because he has given of his offspring to Molech, to defile my sanctuary, and to profane my holy name.

> **20:8** You shall keep my statutes, and do them. I am Yahweh who sanctifies you.

> **20:21** “‘If a man takes his brother’s wife, it is an impurity. He has uncovered his brother’s nakedness. They shall be childless.

> **20:22** “‘You shall therefore keep all my statutes, and all my ordinances, and do them; that the land, where I am bringing you to dwell, may not vomit you out.

> **20:26** You shall be holy to me; for I, Yahweh, am holy, and have set you apart from the peoples, that you should be mine.

## People

- [[God]] — Yahweh commands penalties, opposes Molech worship even when the community conceals it, and identifies himself as the one who sanctifies Israel (1–8). His separation of Israel is for belonging to him (26).
- [[Moses]] — Moses receives the speech and must relay it to Israel (1–2). The chapter gives sanctions and the reason for national separation rather than narrating a trial.

## Places

- [[Canaan]] — The land promised as an inheritance flowing with milk and honey can also vomit Israel out (22–24). Canaan is identified by the context of 18:3; its name is not repeated here.
- [[Horeb]] — The Sinai setting continues from Leviticus 7:38; chapter 20 names no mountain. Its land inheritance remains prospective (22–24).

## Themes

- [[Holiness]] — The commands to sanctify themselves and keep the statutes stand beside Yahweh’s declaration that he sanctifies them (7–8). Separation from peoples and discrimination among animals culminate in belonging to God (24–26).
- [[Law and Obedience]] — The chapter distinguishes commanded communal execution from divine cutting off, bearing iniquity, and childlessness (2–21, 27). Keeping all the statutes is tied to remaining in the land (22); these sanctions must not be flattened into one procedure.
- [[Judgment]] — Stoning is specified for giving offspring to Molech and for mediums or wizards (2, 27); burning is specified in the wife-and-mother case (14). Other cases state death without naming a method. Divine opposition persists when the people conceal Molech worship (3–5).
- [[Idolatry]] — Giving offspring to Molech defiles the sanctuary and profanes the holy name (2–5). Concealment by the community does not prevent divine action. Consulting mediums brings divine cutting off (6), while being a medium or wizard carries stoning (27); these are distinct cases.
- [[Sexual Sin]] — Adultery and several prohibited unions carry death (10–16), while other cases name cutting off, bearing iniquity, or childlessness (17–21). Verse 13 addresses male same-sex intercourse and assigns death to both participants; it does not describe modern categories of orientation.
- [[Marriage]] — The brother’s-wife prohibition and childlessness sanction (21) remain in tension with Judah’s command to Onan to raise offspring for his dead brother in Genesis 38:8. Neither a living-brother restriction nor a widowhood exception is explicit in Leviticus 20:21.
- [[Nakedness and Covering]] — The stored WEB describes intercourse with the father’s, uncle’s, or brother’s wife as uncovering the corresponding man’s nakedness (11, 20–21). Sister and aunt cases also employ nakedness language (17, 19); this is an observation about the translation, not a new Hebrew claim.
- [[Clean and Unclean]] — The separation of clean and unclean animals is joined to Israel’s separation from the peoples (24–26). Intercourse during menstruation brings cutting off (18), beyond the bodily status in 15:24; menstruation itself is not declared an offense.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — Israel’s inheritance of the land (24) stands alongside the warning of expulsion (22). The Molech prohibition applies explicitly to resident foreigners as well as Israelites (2); separation does not exempt Israel from judgment.
- [[The Nations]] — Israel must not imitate the nation being expelled and is set apart to belong to Yahweh (23–26). This is the law’s theological account of the inhabitants’ conduct, not independent evidence about every person in that land.
- [[Life and Death]] — Death penalties, cutting off, and childlessness are differentiated (2–21, 27). The chapter gives sanctions, not a narrative of their execution or a description of the offenders’ afterlife.
- [[Sin]] — Some cases specify bearing iniquity or sin (17, 19–20); the chapter supplies no sacrificial remedy. Its account of prohibited conduct should not be generalized into a claim that every bodily impurity is sinful.
- [[The Names of God]] — Giving offspring to Molech profanes Yahweh’s holy name (3). Yahweh identifies himself as the one who sanctifies Israel (8) and separates the people to belong to him (26).
- [[Blood]] — The repeated formula assigning offenders’ blood to themselves accompanies death penalties (9, 11–13, 16, 27). This is liability language in the WEB, distinct from the menstrual blood in verse 18 and the altar blood of chapter 17.
- [[Barrenness]] — The uncle’s-wife and brother’s-wife cases specify dying childless or being childless (20–21). These particular sanctions do not establish that all infertility is punishment; Genesis 11:30 states Sarai’s barrenness without naming an offense.
- [[Call and Election]] — Yahweh has separated Israel from the peoples to belong to him (24, 26). That election includes obligations and the possibility of expulsion (22), rather than guaranteeing possession regardless of conduct.
- [[Language and Speech]] — Cursing father or mother carries death and personal liability (9). This strengthens the parent-respect command in 19:3 and shows that the chapter’s sanctions concern speech as well as sexual and cultic acts.
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — Giving offspring to Molech is said to defile God’s sanctuary (3), although no entry into it is described. Sanctuary protection here includes conduct beyond the sanctuary precinct.

## Notes

**Sanctions are not synonyms.** The WEB explicitly assigns stoning in verses 2 and 27,
and burning in 14. It does not assign a method to every death sentence. Cutting off is
explicitly God’s action in 3–6, while 17–18 do not specify its mechanism. Childlessness
in 20–21 is not a command to the community to execute the couple.

**Tensions retained.** The brother’s-wife prohibition in 21 stands beside the duty in
Genesis 38:8; a widowhood exception is not stated here. Leviticus 19:20 excludes execution
in its particular slave-status case, while 20:10 prescribes death for adultery. These
legal formulations are retained with their distinct case descriptions, not collapsed
into an identical penalty for every sexual offense.

**Scope.** Molech remains covered under Idolatry and the existing child-sacrifice
connection; no historical reconstruction of the ritual is supplied. The unnamed
parents, couples, practitioners, and foreigners are legal roles, not newly identified
individuals. The planned whole-chapter boundary is unchanged.
