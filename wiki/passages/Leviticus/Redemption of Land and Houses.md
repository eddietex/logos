# Redemption of Land and Houses

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 25:23–34

## Summary

Yahweh forbids permanent sale of the land because it belongs to him; Israel holds it as
strangers and foreigners with him (23–24). When poverty forces a sale, the nearest kinsman
must redeem the property. A seller who later acquires sufficient means may redeem it by
reckoning the elapsed years and returning the surplus. If neither remedy occurs, the
property returns at Jubilee (25–28).

Houses receive distinct rules. A house in a walled city has a one-year redemption period;
after that it belongs permanently to the purchaser and does not return at Jubilee.
Unwalled village houses count with the fields and do return. Levite city houses retain
redemption at any time and Jubilee release, while their surrounding fields may not be
sold at all, because they are a perpetual possession (29–34).

## Key verses

Source: stored WEB, `raw/bible/Leviticus.md`. Complete unit:

> **23** “‘The land shall not be sold in perpetuity, for the land is mine; for you are strangers and live as foreigners with me.

> **24** In all the land of your possession you shall grant a redemption for the land.

> **25** “‘If your brother becomes poor, and sells some of his possessions, then his kinsman who is next to him shall come, and redeem that which his brother has sold.

> **26** If a man has no one to redeem it, and he becomes prosperous and finds sufficient means to redeem it;

> **27** then let him reckon the years since its sale, and restore the surplus to the man to whom he sold it; and he shall return to his property.

> **28** But if he isn’t able to get it back for himself, then what he has sold shall remain in the hand of him who has bought it until the Year of Jubilee: and in the Jubilee it shall be released, and he shall return to his property.

> **29** “‘If a man sells a dwelling house in a walled city, then he may redeem it within a whole year after it has been sold. For a full year he shall have the right of redemption.

> **30** If it isn’t redeemed within the space of a full year, then the house that is in the walled city shall be made sure in perpetuity to him who bought it, throughout his generations. It shall not be released in the Jubilee.

> **31** But the houses of the villages which have no wall around them shall be accounted for with the fields of the country: they may be redeemed, and they shall be released in the Jubilee.

> **32** “‘Nevertheless the cities of the Levites, the houses in the cities of their possession, the Levites may redeem at any time.

> **33** The Levites may redeem the house that was sold, and the city of his possession, and it shall be released in the Jubilee; for the houses of the cities of the Levites are their possession among the children of Israel.

> **34** But the field of the suburbs of their cities may not be sold; for it is their perpetual possession.

## Notes

**A general rule with stated distinctions.** The prohibition on selling land in perpetuity
(23) must be read beside the explicitly permanent sale of a walled-city house after a year
(30). Verse 31 places village houses with fields; verses 32–34 protect Levite property.
The text itself differentiates the categories. It does not promise that every purchased
house returns at Jubilee or cancel every kind of debt.

**Abraham's words, said by the landowner.** Abraham called himself *gēr-wətôšāb* to the
Hittites (Genesis 23:4); here Yahweh calls Israel *gērîm wətôšābîm* *ʿimmādî*, *with me* (23).
Israel in its own land stands toward God where Abraham stood toward the Hittites. Hebrew
checked against Miqra according to the Masorah (Sefaria), Genesis 23:4 and Leviticus 25:23–34.

**The *gōʾēl* appears.** *Gōʾălô haqqārōb ʾēlāyw* (25), WEB's *his kinsman who is next to him*,
is the law that [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]] and [[Redemption]] had been reading
ahead to. Unlike Genesis 48:16 and Exodus 6:6, where the wiki first met the verb, its subject
here is an ordinary relative with money. Verse 26 envisages a man who has no *gōʾēl*.

**Redemption is not only release without payment.** A kinsman redeems; a recovered seller
settles with the purchaser according to elapsed years; Jubilee provides return when the
seller cannot recover the property (25–28). These protections address dispossession without
making every transaction invalid. Verse 27 supplies no worked numerical example.

**Levite possession is real.** The older connection's claim that Levites have no property
or fields conflicts with 32–34, which names houses and a field held perpetually. Absence of
a tribal territorial allotment in later passages must be distinguished from absence of
all real property. This correction is also recorded on the connection and theme pages.
Levites as a group are not simply equated with every individual serving as an Aaronic priest.
These two verses are the only place the stored WEB of Leviticus uses the word *Levites*.

**Scope and sources.** This is the continuing Sinai address, not a narrated sale or a report
that Jubilee was historically observed. WEB 25:33 is quoted as stored; no conclusion about
its underlying Hebrew syntax is asserted. At 25:30 the Hebrew has a *ketiv/qere*: written
*lōʾ* (not), read *lô* (to it), so *the city that has a wall*; WEB follows the reading.
Comparisons use stored Genesis 47 and Leviticus 25. The planned boundary is retained; the service laws beginning at 25:35 remain queued.
