# Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 26:14–46

## Summary

The other half of the *if* of 26:3 is now spoken: *But if you will not listen to me* (14). Yahweh
answers in rising stages. First come disease, lost harvests, and defeat (16–17). If Israel still
does not listen, he will chastise *seven times more*: a sky like iron, soil like brass, and a land
that yields nothing (18–20). Next, wild animals take children and livestock (21–22). Next, a sword
*that will execute the vengeance of the covenant*, siege, pestilence, and bread by weight (23–26).

The last stage is the longest (27–39). Parents eat their children. High places and idols fall, with
Israel's corpses thrown on them, and Yahweh says *my soul will abhor you*. Cities and sanctuaries
lie waste, and he will no longer take delight in the offerings. Israel is scattered among the
nations. The land lies empty and at last has the Sabbaths it was denied (34–35). Those left abroad
run from a driven leaf and pine away.

Then the speech turns, with no stage beyond exile (40–45). Israel confesses its own iniquity and
its fathers', its uncircumcised heart is humbled, and Yahweh remembers his covenant with Jacob,
Isaac, and Abraham, and the land. He will not reject them, nor abhor them to destroy them utterly,
nor break his covenant, *for I am Yahweh their God*. A colophon closes the unit: these are the
statutes given at Mount Sinai by Moses (46).

> **14** “‘But if you will not listen to me, and will not do all these commandments;

> **15** and if you shall reject my statutes, and if your soul abhors my ordinances, so that you will not do all my commandments, but break my covenant;

> **22** I will send the wild animals among you, which will rob you of your children, destroy your livestock, and make you few in number. Your roads will become desolate.

> **26** When I break your staff of bread, ten women shall bake your bread in one oven, and they shall deliver your bread again by weight. You shall eat, and not be satisfied.

> **27** “‘If you in spite of this won’t listen to me, but walk contrary to me;

> **29** You will eat the flesh of your sons, and you will eat the flesh of your daughters.

> **30** I will destroy your high places, and cut down your incense altars, and cast your dead bodies upon the bodies of your idols; and my soul will abhor you.

> **32** I will bring the land into desolation; and your enemies that dwell therein will be astonished at it.

> **33** I will scatter you among the nations, and I will draw out the sword after you. Your land will be a desolation, and your cities shall be a waste.

> **34** Then the land will enjoy its Sabbaths as long as it lies desolate and you are in your enemies’ land. Even then the land will rest and enjoy its Sabbaths.

> **35** As long as it lies desolate it shall have rest, even the rest which it didn’t have in your Sabbaths, when you lived on it.

> **36** “‘As for those of you who are left, I will send a faintness into their hearts in the lands of their enemies. The sound of a driven leaf will put them to flight; and they shall flee, as one flees from the sword. They will fall when no one pursues.

> **37** They will stumble over one another, as it were before the sword, when no one pursues. You will have no power to stand before your enemies.

> **38** You will perish among the nations. The land of your enemies will eat you up.

> **39** Those of you who are left will pine away in their iniquity in your enemies’ lands; and also in the iniquities of their fathers they shall pine away with them.

> **41** I also walked contrary to them, and brought them into the land of their enemies; if then their uncircumcised heart is humbled, and they then accept the punishment of their iniquity;

> **42** then I will remember my covenant with Jacob; and also my covenant with Isaac, and also my covenant with Abraham; and I will remember the land.

> **43** The land also will be left by them, and will enjoy its Sabbaths while it lies desolate without them: and they will accept the punishment of their iniquity; because, even because they rejected my ordinances, and their soul abhorred my statutes.

> **45** but I will for their sake remember the covenant of their ancestors, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt in the sight of the nations, that I might be their God. I am Yahweh.’”

> **46** These are the statutes, ordinances and laws, which Yahweh made between him and the children of Israel in Mount Sinai by Moses.

## People

- [[God]] — sole speaker, and the subject of nearly every verb of harm and of the final *I will
  remember*.
- [[Moses]] — named once, in the colophon: the law was given *by Moses* (46; Hebrew *bəyad-mōšeh*,
  *by the hand of Moses*).
- [[Jacob]], [[Isaac]], and [[Abraham]] — named as parties to the remembered covenant, in that
  reverse order (42). They do not act here.

## Places

- [[Canaan]] — not named. It is *your land* and *the land*, desolated and then remembered (42).
- [[Egypt]] — the exodus *in the sight of the nations* (45).
- [[Horeb]] — Mount Sinai, named in the colophon (46).

## Themes

- [[Curse]] — the content of the unit, though the word *curse* does not occur in it.
- [[Covenant]] — broken by Israel (15), avenged (25), remembered (42, 45), and not broken by Yahweh
  (44).
- [[Judgment]] — stage after stage, each one conditional on the last having failed.

- [[Guilt and Conscience]] — confession and accepting punishment (40–41, 43).

- [[War]] — defeat, siege, and flight with no pursuer (17, 25, 36–37).

- [[The Nations]] — the place of exile and the audience of the exodus (33, 38, 45).
- [[Walking with God]] — *qerî*, walking *contrary*, on both sides (21–28, 40–41).
- [[Vengeance]] — *the vengeance of the covenant* (25).
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — a refused *pleasant aroma* (31).
- [[The Dwelling of God]] — sanctuaries made desolate, in contrast with 26:11 (31).
- [[Pride and Self-Exaltation]] — *the pride of your power* broken (19).

## Notes

**Boundary.** The plan stands. The WLC marks a paragraph break before verse 14 and after verse 46,
and setumah breaks after verses 17 and 26. Verse 46 could be read as a separate colophon, but it is
kept here because nothing else closes the speech. It is a strange close: chapter 27 follows, with
its own closing at 27:34.

**Five stages, and what makes the next one come.** The turns are marked: 14, 18, 21, 23, 27. Each
one after the first says Israel has not listened or has not been *reformed* (*tiwwāsrû*, 23, the
verb of *chastise* at 18 and 28). So the unit presents the disasters as discipline meant to end
early. They are not a fixed sequence that must run its course. The word *seven* comes four times
(18, 21, 24, 28); the count is rhetorical escalation and is not arithmetic. No stage says how long it
lasts or what it would look like if Israel listened partway through, apart from verses 40–45.

**The curse as the blessing undone.** Nearly every gift of 26:4–13 returns reversed:

| Blessing | Warning |
|---|---|
| rains; land and trees *give* (4) | iron sky; land and trees will not *give* (19–20) |
| *you shall eat your bread to the full* (5) | *you shall eat, and not be satisfied* (26) |
| *no one will make you afraid* (6) | *terror*, *behālâ* (16); a driven leaf (36) |
| evil animals removed (6) | wild animals sent (22) |
| no sword through your land (6) | a sword brought, then drawn after you (25, 33) |
| five chase a hundred (8) | flee when no one pursues (17, 36–37) |
| *I will turn to you* (9, *ûpānîtî*) | *I will set my face against you* (17, *nātattî pānay*) |
| *my soul won't abhor you* (11) | *my soul will abhor you* (30) |
| *I will walk among you* (12) | *I will walk contrary to you* (24, 28) |
| I broke your yoke (13, *wāʾešbōr*) | I break your pride and staff of bread (19, 26; same verb) |
| *made you go upright* (13, *qôməmiyyût*) | *no power to stand* (37, *təqûmâ*) |

The last two nouns are each found only once in the Hebrew Bible (WLC lemma search). *Qôməmiyyût* and
*təqûmâ* are generally derived from the root *qûm*, *to stand*; that derivation is a lexical
judgment, not something the text says.

**Eating.** *ʾĀkal* runs through the whole speech. Israel eats old store (10). Then enemies eat
Israel's seed (16), Israel eats unsatisfied (26), Israel eats its own children (29), and finally
*the land of your enemies will eat you up* (38). The food has become the eater. Numbers 13:32 uses
a similar phrase of Canaan, in the spies' mouths.

**The word *give*.** The previous unit noted *nātan* as the verb of rain, peace, and dwelling. Here
the same verb sets Yahweh's face against Israel (17), makes the sky iron (19), lays corpses on idols
(30), and turns cities to waste (31); WEB has *set*, *make*, *cast*, and *lay*. And the land *will
not give* (20).

**Walking *qerî*.** The noun occurs seven times in the Hebrew Bible, all in this chapter (21, 23, 24,
27, 28, 40, 41; WLC lemma search). WEB's *contrary to* is an interpretation of a rare word; its
exact sense is uncertain, and it has often been taken as *hostility* or *casual encounter*. What
the chapter does show is the symmetry: Israel walks this way, then Yahweh does, then Israel confesses
that both happened. The promise of 26:12 was *wəhithallaktî*, *I will walk about among you*. Here
the verb is plain *hālak* and the walking is against.

**The vengeance of the covenant.** *Neqam bərît* (25) occurs only here (WLC lemma search), and it
assigns the sword a legal cause. The enemies who carry it are incidental to the sentence. Yahweh is
the one who brings it. Compare [[Vengeance]], which so far has been mostly a human matter or God's
protection of the wronged. Here God avenges his covenant on the people in it.

**Children.** Verse 22 has animals bereaving Israel of children (*šikkəlâ*), and verse 29 has the
parents eating them. The verse is stated in two flat clauses with no comment. See
[[The Staff of Bread - Leviticus 26.26-29, Deuteronomy 28, and Ezekiel 4-5]] for where this is repeated and where it is narrated.

**Land's Sabbaths.** See [[The Land Will Enjoy Its Sabbaths - Leviticus 26.34-35, 43 and 2 Chronicles 36.21]]. The law of 25:1–7 is treated as a debt. The land is the
creditor, Israel's absence is the payment, and verse 35 says so: *the rest which it didn't have in
your Sabbaths*. This presumes the failure it warns of, but it is a warning: the verse does not
report a Sabbath year missed. Verses 34–35 interrupt the sequence of harm with something the land
gains, and it is a strange kind of mercy, to the land.

**One verb for land and people.** WEB's *enjoy* (34, 43) and *accept* (41, 43) render a single verb,
*rāṣâ* (WLC: five times in 34, 41, 43). The land *rāṣâ*s its Sabbaths and the people *rāṣâ* their
iniquity. Commonly glossed *to be pleased with* or *accept*, it is often taken in these verses as
*to pay off, make good*, though lexicons differ on whether that is a separate sense. Either way the
Hebrew makes the land's rest and the people's punishment one transaction, which WEB's two English
verbs conceal. Isaiah 40:2 uses the same lemma of Jerusalem, whose *iniquity is pardoned* (*nirṣâ
ʿăwōnāh*; WEB, bible-api.com).

**WEB's *if* at verse 40 is an interpretation.** Verse 14 has *wəʾim*, and so do 18, 21, 23, and 27.
Verse 40 has no *ʾim*: it opens with a perfect and *wə-*, *wəhitwaddû* (WLC). That form can be
conditional, as WEB reads it, or predictive, *they will confess*. Verse 41's *ʾô ʾāz* (*or then*)
is also unusual, and WEB's *if then* smooths it. Verse 41's *ʾēlēk* is imperfect, and WEB's past *I
walked* reads it as the content of what is confessed. The difference matters: one reading sets a
condition for restoration, and the other foretells repentance. This page does not choose. Verse 44,
at least, is stated with no condition.

**What the restoration does and does not say.** Yahweh remembers the covenant and the land, and
refuses to reject or abhor or destroy. The unit does **not** say Israel returns to the land, or that
the exile ends, and it gives no time. The land's Sabbaths are still being enjoyed at verse 43, *after*
the remembering of verse 42. Deuteronomy 30:1–3 makes the gathering explicit
*(WEB, bible-api.com)*. See [[If They Confess Their Iniquity - Leviticus 26.40-45, 1 Kings 8, Daniel 9, and Nehemiah 9]].

**Two remembered covenants.** Verse 42 names *my covenant* three times, each with a patriarch, and
the Hebrew has no *with*: *bərîtî yaʿăqôb*, literally *my covenant Jacob*. The order is the reverse
of Exodus 2:24, and of the usual sequence. Among verses naming all three, Genesis 35:27 is the only
other with this order (WLC lemma search), and that one is a travel note. No reason is given. Jacob
is also written with an added *waw* here, a spelling found five times in all (Leviticus 26:42;
Jeremiah 30:18; 33:26; 46:27; 51:19; WLC). Verse 45 then remembers *bərît riʾšōnîm*, the covenant of
*the first ones*, identified as those brought out of Egypt: that group is not the patriarchs. So the
speech appears to appeal to both the patriarchal covenant and the exodus generation's, and verse 15's
broken covenant is not obviously either. The text does not distinguish them.

**Uncircumcised heart.** *ʿārēl* of the heart appears here first in canonical order; earlier uses
are literal or of lips (Exodus 6:12). The verb *yikkānaʿ*, *is humbled*, is a passive-sense form, and
the text does not say who humbles it. Compare the command of Deuteronomy 10:16 and promise of 30:6 at
[[Circumcision of the Heart]].

**Tensions left standing.**

1. **Abhorring.** Verse 30 says *my soul will abhor you*. Verse 44 says *neither will I abhor them, to
   destroy them utterly*. Verse 11 had promised *won't abhor*. Taken flatly, 30 and 44 clash. The
   purpose clause at 44 (*lĕkallōtām*, *to destroy them*) may limit the denial to final destruction,
   but that is a reading. Both claims stand in one speech, in the same words.
2. **Fathers' iniquity.** Verses 39–40 have the survivors pine away and confess *the iniquities of
   their fathers*, with Exodus 20:5 behind it. Ezekiel 18 and Deuteronomy 24:16 press the other way.
   Verse 40 pairs *their iniquity* with their fathers', so the survivors are not presented as merely
   innocent heirs, but the unit does not explain how the two relate.
3. **Who reads this.** Set in the wilderness at Sinai (46), the passage speaks of *your sanctuaries*
   in the plural, of high places, and of a detailed exile. The text gives no date beyond that frame.
   Readers ancient and modern have taken it as prediction or as later reflection on the exile; the
   wiki records the frame the text gives and does not settle that question.
4. **The collective again.** As at [[Blessings for Obedience]], the *you* is the people. Job and
   Ecclesiastes stop this being read as a rule that an individual's disaster shows his guilt.

**High places.** The nouns of verse 30, *bāmôt*, *ḥammānîm*, and *gillûlîm*, recur in the oracle of
Ezekiel 6:3–6 (WLC), along with the sword and corpses laid before idols. *Pigrê gillûlêkem*, the
*bodies of your idols*, treats the idols as corpses too. WEB's *your dead bodies upon the bodies of
your idols* preserves the repeated noun, *peger*.
