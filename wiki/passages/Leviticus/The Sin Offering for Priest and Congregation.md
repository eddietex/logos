# The Sin Offering for Priest and Congregation

**Book:** [[Leviticus]]
**Reference:** Leviticus 4:1–21

## Summary

Yahweh gives Moses instructions for unintentional violations of his commandments. The first
case concerns the anointed priest whose sin brings guilt on the people; the second concerns
the whole congregation, whose wrongdoing was hidden but has become known. Each requires a
young bull. The priest lays his hand on his own offering; the congregation's elders lay
their hands on its bull.

Blood is brought into the Tent of Meeting, sprinkled seven times before the veil, and put
on the inner altar's horns; the remainder goes to the base of the outer burnt-offering
altar. Fat and specified organs burn on the altar, as with the peace offering. The rest of
the bull is burned outside the camp. The congregational case explicitly concludes with
atonement and forgiveness (20). These are prescribed cases, not reports that Aaron or
Israel has committed a particular offense.

## Key verses

> **4:1** Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

> **4:2** “Speak to the children of Israel, saying, ‘If anyone sins unintentionally, in any of the things which Yahweh has commanded not to be done, and does any one of them:

> **4:3** if the anointed priest sins so as to bring guilt on the people, then let him offer for his sin, which he has sinned, a young bull without defect to Yahweh for a sin offering.

> **4:4** He shall bring the bull to the door of the Tent of Meeting before Yahweh; and he shall lay his hand on the head of the bull, and kill the bull before Yahweh.

> **4:5** The anointed priest shall take some of the blood of the bull, and bring it to the Tent of Meeting.

> **4:6** The priest shall dip his finger in the blood, and sprinkle some of the blood seven times before Yahweh, before the veil of the sanctuary.

> **4:7** The priest shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar of sweet incense before Yahweh, which is in the Tent of Meeting; and he shall pour out all of rest of the blood of the bull at the base of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the door of the Tent of Meeting.

> **4:8** He shall take all the fat of the bull of the sin offering from it; the fat that covers the innards, and all the fat that is on the innards,

> **4:9** and the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them, which is by the loins, and the cover on the liver, with the kidneys, he shall take away,

> **4:10** as it is taken from the bull of the sacrifice of peace offerings. The priest shall burn them on the altar of burnt offering.

> **4:11** The bull’s skin, all its meat, with its head, and with its legs, its innards, and its dung,

> **4:12** he shall carry the whole bull outside of the camp to a clean place, where the ashes are poured out, and burn it on wood with fire. Where the ashes are poured out, it shall be burned.

> **4:13** “‘If the whole congregation of Israel sins, and the thing is hidden from the eyes of the assembly, and they have done any of the things which Yahweh has commanded not to be done, and are guilty;

> **4:14** when the sin in which they have sinned is known, then the assembly shall offer a young bull for a sin offering, and bring it before the Tent of Meeting.

> **4:15** The elders of the congregation shall lay their hands on the head of the bull before Yahweh; and the bull shall be killed before Yahweh.

> **4:16** The anointed priest shall bring some of the blood of the bull to the Tent of Meeting:

> **4:17** and the priest shall dip his finger in the blood, and sprinkle it seven times before Yahweh, before the veil.

> **4:18** He shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar which is before Yahweh, that is in the Tent of Meeting; and the rest of the blood he shall pour out at the base of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the door of the Tent of Meeting.

> **4:19** All its fat he shall take from it, and burn it on the altar.

> **4:20** Thus shall he do with the bull; as he did with the bull of the sin offering, so shall he do with this; and the priest shall make atonement for them, and they shall be forgiven.

> **4:21** He shall carry the bull outside the camp, and burn it as he burned the first bull. It is the sin offering for the assembly.

## Notes

### Shared rite, different responsibility

Verses 3–12 address the anointed priest; verses 13–21 address the congregation. The opening
condition of unintentional sin governs the instructions. The priest's offense can bring
guilt on the people (3), but the text does not explain the mechanism or name a particular
transgression. In the assembly's case, the elders represent the group in hand-laying (15).
No confession formula or spoken transfer of guilt is prescribed here; a theory of what
hand-laying signifies should be distinguished from what these verses actually state.

Verse 20 explicitly promises forgiveness to the congregation after priestly atonement.
The priest's case does not repeat that concluding formula. Its absence is not a statement
that the priest cannot be forgiven: the two procedures are expressly connected (20).

### Blood inside, burning outside

The seven sprinklings are before the veil, not behind it (6, 17). Blood touches the horns
of the incense altar inside; the remainder is poured at the outer altar's base (7, 18).
Only the assigned fat portions burn on the altar. The carcass goes to the clean ash site
outside (11–12, 21). Outside the camp therefore does not automatically mean an unclean
place, and disposal should not be called ordinary refuse without qualification.

The WEB names this a sin offering. Describing its blood rite as sanctuary purification
is an interpretation of its ritual action, not an additional statement that these verses
make about cleansing the veil. No Hebrew lexical claim is needed to distinguish it from
the whole burnt offering or the peace offering.

### Scope of the comparison

Hebrews 13:10–13 connects sanctuary blood and burning outside with Jesus' suffering outside
the gate and the readers' bearing reproach. This is a later typological reading; Leviticus
4 does not itself predict a crucifixion. Nor does this unit describe the annual Day of
Atonement: the priest does not pass behind the veil. The following unit treats ruler
and individual cases (4:22–35); see [[The Sin Offering for Ruler and Individual]].
