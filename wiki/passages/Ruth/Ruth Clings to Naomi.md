# Ruth Clings to Naomi

**Book:** [[Ruth]]
**Reference:** Ruth 1:6–18

## Summary

> **6** Then she arose with her daughters-in-law, that she might return from the country of Moab; for she
> had heard in the country of Moab how Yahweh had visited his people in giving them bread.
> **7** She went out of the place where she was, and her two daughters-in-law with her. They went on the
> way to return to the land of Judah.
> **8** Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May Yahweh
> deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead, and with me.
> **9** May Yahweh grant you that you may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband." Then she
> kissed them, and they lifted up their voices, and wept.
> **10** They said to her, "No, but we will return with you to your people."
> **11** Naomi said, "Go back, my daughters. Why do you want to go with me? Do I still have sons in my
> womb, that they may be your husbands?
> **12** Go back, my daughters, go your way; for I am too old to have a husband. If I should say, 'I have
> hope,' if I should even have a husband tonight, and should also bear sons;
> **13** would you then wait until they were grown? Would you then refrain from having husbands? No, my
> daughters, for it grieves me seriously for your sakes, for Yahweh's hand has gone out against me."
> **14** They lifted up their voices, and wept again; then Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth joined
> with her.
> **15** She said, "Behold, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people, and to her god. Follow your
> sister-in-law."
> **16** Ruth said, "Don't urge me to leave you, and to return from following you, for where you go, I will
> go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God.
> **17** Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May Yahweh do so to me, and more also, if
> anything but death parts you and me."
> **18** When she saw that she was determined to go with her, she stopped urging her.

[[Naomi]] hears in Moab that the famine in Judah is over, *Yahweh had visited his people in giving them
bread*, and sets out for home with her two widowed daughters-in-law (6–7). On the road she tells them to
go back, each to her mother's house, and blesses them: may Yahweh show them the kindness they showed the
dead and her, and give them *rest* in a new husband's house (8–9). They weep and refuse (9–10). Naomi
argues it out: she has no more sons to give them, she is too old for a husband, and even if she bore sons
that night they could not wait for them to grow up; Yahweh's hand has gone out against her (11–13). They
weep again. [[Orpah]] kisses her mother-in-law and goes; [[Ruth (person)]] *joined with her* (14). Naomi
points to Orpah, *gone back to her people, and to her god*, and tells Ruth to follow (15). Ruth answers
with a vow: where Naomi goes, lodges, dies, and is buried, she will too; Naomi's people and God will be
hers; and she binds herself by an oath in Yahweh's name that only death will part them (16–17). Naomi
stops arguing (18).

**What is going on.** This is the book's first dialogue, and its first turn. In 1:1–5 the family went
out to Moab and lost everything; here the one survivor turns back. The narrator gives Naomi's reason in
one clause: she heard that Yahweh had *visited* his people with bread (6). Everything else is speech.
Naomi's speeches all push the young women away, and they are kind: she blesses them, kisses them, and
reasons with them, and her reasoning is sound in its own terms. A widow's future in that world was a
husband and sons, and Naomi has neither to offer. Orpah takes the sensible advice and is not blamed for
it; the narrator gives her a kiss, not a rebuke. Ruth refuses it, and her refusal is the centre of the
scene. She does not argue with Naomi's reasoning. She answers it with a commitment that covers place,
people, God, death, and burial, and seals it with an oath.

**What is notable.**

- **Return.** *Šûb*, *to return* or *turn back* (lemma 7725), is the key word of the chapter. In the WLC
  of Ruth 1 it occurs twelve times, in verses 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 15 (twice), 16, 21, and 22 (twice); nine
  of those occurrences fall in 1:6–16. Naomi *returns* from Moab (6, 7), tells the women to *return* (8,
  11, 12), and they offer to *return* with her (10). For Naomi it is a homecoming. For two Moabite women,
  going to Judah is not a return at all, and their offer in verse 10 uses the word anyway. Orpah *has gone
  back* (*šāḇâ*, 15), and Naomi tells Ruth *šûḇî*, *go back*, after her (15); Ruth refuses *to return from
  following you* (16). The word turns in Ruth's mouth: she is returning, but to Naomi's people, not to her
  own. Joshua 23:12 used the same two verbs, *go back* (*šûb*) and *hold fast* (*dābaq*), of Israel
  clinging to the nations; here a woman of the nations is choosing between them. See
  [[Go Back or Cling - Joshua 23.12 and Ruth 1.14-16]].
- **Yahweh visited his people.** *Kî pāqaḏ YHWH ʾeṯ-ʿammô lāṯēṯ lāhem lāḥem* (6, WLC). *Pāqaḏ*, *to
  visit* or *attend to*, is the verb of *Yahweh visited Sarah* (Genesis 21:1) and of Joseph's *God will
  surely visit you* (Genesis 50:24). The book's first report of God acting is that he ended the famine;
  the narrator does not say he caused it. The end of the clause is a sound play, *lāhem lāḥem*, *to them
  bread*, and the bread comes back to *Bêt Leḥem*, the house of bread. See [[Famine and Plenty]].
- **Her mother's house.** *Ləḇêṯ ʾimmāh* (8, WLC). A widow would ordinarily return to her *father's*
  house, as Tamar did (*Remain a widow in your father's house*, Genesis 38:11). Naomi says *mother's
  house*, a rare phrase whose other uses gather around women and marriage. See
  [[Into My Mother's House - Song of Solomon 3.4, 8.2, Genesis 24, and Ruth 1.8]].
- **Kindness.** *Yaʿaś YHWH ʿimmāḵem ḥeseḏ*, *May Yahweh deal kindly with you* (8, WLC; the written
  consonants read *yʿśh*, the margin reads *yaʿaś*). *Ḥeseḏ* is loyal kindness, the faithfulness that goes
  beyond what is owed. In the WLC of Ruth it occurs three times: here, of Orpah and Ruth toward *the dead*
  and Naomi; at 2:20, of Yahweh, or of Boaz (the Hebrew is ambiguous; not yet ingested); and at 3:10, of
  Ruth's *latter kindness* (not yet ingested). Naomi asks Yahweh to repay the women in kind. In the rest of
  the book Boaz is the one who does it. See [[Love]].
- **Rest.** *Mənûḥâ*, *rest* (9), the settled place of a married woman. In 3:1 Naomi will set out to
  *seek rest for you*, with the related noun *mānôaḥ* (not yet ingested). The wish of 1:9 becomes Naomi's
  own plan in chapter 3.
- **Naomi's argument is the levirate law, turned inside out.** *Do I still have sons in my womb, that they
  may be your husbands?* (11). The custom behind it is that a brother marries his dead brother's childless
  widow (Deuteronomy 25:5–6; Genesis 38:8). Naomi carries the custom to its limit: there is no brother,
  there will be no brother, and even an impossible brother born tonight would come too late (12–13).
  *Tēʿāgēnâ*, *refrain*, *keep yourselves shut up* (13), occurs only here in the WLC files the wiki has read
  (Genesis, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel); the gloss is a lexical convention, not verified
  against a lexicon here. Judah told Tamar to wait for a son to grow up (Genesis 38:11); Naomi tells Ruth
  and Orpah not to wait. See [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].
- **Sister-in-law.** *Yəḇimtēḵ*, *your sister-in-law* (15, twice, WLC), is formed from the levirate root
  *ybm*. In the WLC files read, the noun *yəḇēmeṯ* (lemma 2994) occurs only here and at Deuteronomy 25:7, 9,
  the brother's widow in the levirate law. Naomi's word for Orpah is the law's word for the widow whose
  claim has failed.
- **It is bitter for me.** *Kî-mar-lî məʾōḏ mikkem* (13, WLC). The WEB has *it grieves me seriously for your
  sakes*. The phrase can also be read *it is far more bitter for me than for you*, because *min* can mark a
  comparison; translations differ, and the wiki does not decide between them. Either way, *mar*, *bitter*,
  prepares the name she will take in 1:20: *Call me Mara; for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me*.
- **Yahweh's hand has gone out against me.** *Yāṣəʾâ ḇî yaḏ-YHWH* (13). This is Naomi's reading of her
  losses. The narrator never gives a cause for the deaths of 1:3–5. The page keeps the two voices apart: the
  only thing the narrator says Yahweh did in Ruth 1 is give bread (6). See [[Suffering]].
- **Weeping, twice.** *They lifted up their voices, and wept* (9, 14). It is the first mourning in the book,
  and it comes on the road, at the parting, not at the graves. See [[Mourning and Lament]].
- **Orpah kissed; Ruth clung.** *Wattiššaq ʿorpâ laḥămôṯāh wərûṯ dāḇəqâ bāh* (14, WLC). The verb is
  *dābaq*, *to cling* or *hold fast*, the verb of Genesis 2:24 (*a man will leave his father and his
  mother, and will join with his wife*) and of Deuteronomy's command to *cling* to Yahweh (10:20; 30:20).
  Boaz will later say Ruth *left your father and your mother* (2:11; not yet ingested). The two clauses
  are set side by side with no verdict on Orpah. In the WLC of Ruth, *dābaq* occurs here and three times
  in chapter 2 (2:8, 21, 23), always of Ruth.
- **Her people, and her god.** *ʾEl-ʿammāh wəʾel-ʾĕlōheyhā* (15, WLC). The noun is the plural form
  *ʾĕlōhîm*, which can mean *gods* or one god; the WEB has *her god*. Moab's god is called Chemosh in
  Numbers 21:29 and Judges 11:24, but Ruth names no god of Moab. Naomi links people and god as one thing,
  and Ruth's answer keeps them linked: *Your people will be my people, and your God my God* (16). See
  [[Chemosh]] and [[The Nations]].
- **Ruth's vow.** *Where you go, I will go; and where you stay, I will stay* (16). The verbs are *hālaḵ*,
  *to go*, and *lîn*, *to lodge* or *spend the night*. The vow moves from the road (*go*) to the night's
  shelter (*lodge*), to belonging (*people*, *God*), to death and the grave (*die*, *be buried*, 17). It
  asks nothing back. Ruth calls Naomi's God by his name, *Yahweh*, in the oath that follows. This is the
  first speech the book gives her, apart from the joint reply of verse 10.
- **May Yahweh do so to me, and more also.** *Kōh yaʿăśeh YHWH lî wəḵōh yōsîp* (17, WLC). It is a
  self-curse: the speaker invites a penalty on herself if she breaks her word, without naming it. In the
  WLC files read, the full formula with *wəḵōh yōsîp* occurs here and in 1 Samuel 3:17; 14:44; 20:13;
  25:22, in the mouths of Eli, Saul, Jonathan, and David. Ruth is the only woman among them, and she and
  Jonathan the only two who call on *Yahweh* by name rather than *God*. See
  [[God Do So to Me - The Self-Curse Oath in Ruth and 1 Samuel]] and [[Oaths and Vows]].
- **Only death.** *Kî hammāweṯ yaprîḏ bênî ûḇênēḵ*, literally *for death will part between me and you*
  (17, WLC). The WEB's *if anything but death parts you and me* follows one reading of the particle *kî*;
  the sense is not in doubt. *Pāraḏ*, *to part*, is the verb of Abram and Lot: *Please separate yourself from
  me* and *they separated themselves the one from the other* (Genesis 13:9, 11). Lot chose the plain, and
  his daughter's son was Moab, *the father of the Moabites* (Genesis 19:37). A Moabite now refuses to
  separate.
- **There I will be buried.** Ruth asks to be buried in Naomi's land, as Jacob and Joseph asked to be
  buried in Canaan (Genesis 47:29–30; 50:25). The book does not say where she was buried. See
  [[Burial and the Grave]].
- **Determined.** *Mitʾammeṣeṯ*, *making herself strong* (18, WLC). The verb *ʾāmaṣ* (lemma 553) is the
  *courageous* of Moses' and Yahweh's charge to Joshua, *Be strong and courageous* (Deuteronomy 31:6–7;
  Joshua 1:6–9). In the WLC files read, Ruth 1:18 is its only reflexive (hitpael) form. Naomi *stopped
  urging her*, literally *stopped speaking to her*: she has no answer, and she gives none.

**How it connects.** The scene turns on a word the earlier books used of Israel. Deuteronomy commands Israel
to *cling* to Yahweh (10:20; 30:20), and Joshua warns Israel against *going back* to *cling* to the nations
and marry them (23:12). A Moabite widow now clings to Israel, by its God's name. That is the whole problem
of [[The Nations]] in this book, and the book does not argue it; it narrates it. Behind the scene stands the
levirate law that Naomi uses to send the women away, which Boaz will fulfil in another form (4:5, 10). Ahead
of it is Naomi's arrival in Bethlehem *empty* (1:21), with Ruth beside her and not yet counted.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope boundary is 1:6–18 as planned: from Naomi's rising to return to her
silence, before the arrival in Bethlehem (1:19). Chemosh is listed under People with a note that the passage
does not name him; *her god* (15) is the only reference. No place page was created for *the land of Judah*
(7), since the wiki has no territory page for Judah and the destination is [[Bethlehem]].

**On Orpah.** Later readers often contrast Orpah unfavourably with Ruth, and a rabbinic tradition makes her
an ancestor of Goliath (reported, not verified). The text does neither: Orpah does what Naomi tells her to
do, three times over, and leaves with a kiss. The wiki keeps the contrast the narrator draws (*kissed …
clung*) and does not add a judgment the narrator withholds.

**On Ruth's vow as conversion.** Ruth 1:16 is often read as a conversion to Israel's faith, and later
Jewish tradition treats it as the model of one (reported, not verified here). The text records a vow of
belonging to a people and its God, sealed by an oath in Yahweh's name; it does not use any word for
conversion and describes no rite.
