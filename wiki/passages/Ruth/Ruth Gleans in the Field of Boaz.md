# Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz

**Book:** [[Ruth]]
**Reference:** Ruth 2:1–23

## Summary

> **1** Naomi had a relative of her husband's, a mighty man of wealth, of the family of Elimelech, and his
> name was Boaz.
> **2** Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, "Let me now go to the field, and glean among the ears of grain
> after him in whose sight I find favor." She said to her, "Go, my daughter."
> **3** She went, and came and gleaned in the field after the reapers; and she happened to come to the
> portion of the field belonging to Boaz, who was of the family of Elimelech.
> **4** Behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem, and said to the reapers, "May Yahweh be with you." They answered
> him, "May Yahweh bless you."
> **5** Then Boaz said to his servant who was set over the reapers, "Whose young lady is this?"
> **6** The servant who was set over the reapers answered, "It is the Moabite lady who came back with Naomi
> out of the country of Moab.
> **7** She said, 'Please let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves.' So she came, and has
> continued even from the morning until now, except that she rested a little in the house."
> **8** Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen, my daughter. Don't go to glean in another field, and don't go from
> here, but stay here close to my maidens.
> **9** Let your eyes be on the field that they reap, and go after them. Haven't I commanded the young men not
> to touch you? When you are thirsty, go to the vessels, and drink from that which the young men have
> drawn."
> **10** Then she fell on her face, and bowed herself to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favor
> in your sight, that you should take knowledge of me, since I am a foreigner?"
> **11** Boaz answered her, "I have been told all about what you have done for your mother-in-law since the
> death of your husband, and how you have left your father and your mother, and the land of your birth,
> and have come to a people that you didn't know before.
> **12** May Yahweh repay your work, and a full reward be given to you from Yahweh, the God of Israel, under
> whose wings you have come to take refuge."
> **13** Then she said, "Let me find favor in your sight, my lord, because you have comforted me, and because
> you have spoken kindly to your servant, though I am not as one of your servants."
> **14** At meal time Boaz said to her, "Come here, and eat some bread, and dip your morsel in the vinegar."
> She sat beside the reapers, and they passed her parched grain, and she ate, and was satisfied, and left
> some of it.
> **15** When she had risen up to glean, Boaz commanded his young men, saying, "Let her glean even among the
> sheaves, and don't reproach her.
> **16** Also pull out some for her from the bundles, and leave it. Let her glean, and don't rebuke her."
> **17** So she gleaned in the field until evening; and she beat out that which she had gleaned, and it was
> about an ephah of barley.
> **18** She took it up, and went into the city. Then her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned; and she
> brought out and gave to her that which she had left after she had enough.
> **19** Her mother-in-law said to her, "Where have you gleaned today? Where have you worked? Blessed be he
> who noticed you." She told her mother-in-law with whom she had worked, "The man's name with whom I worked
> today is Boaz."
> **20** Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "May he be blessed by Yahweh, who has not abandoned his kindness
> to the living and to the dead." Naomi said to her, "The man is a close relative to us, one of our near
> kinsmen."
> **21** Ruth the Moabitess said, "Yes, he said to me, 'You shall stay close to my young men, until they have
> finished all my harvest.'"
> **22** Naomi said to Ruth her daughter-in-law, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his maidens,
> and that they not meet you in any other field."
> **23** So she stayed close to the maidens of Boaz, to glean to the end of barley harvest and of wheat
> harvest; and she lived with her mother-in-law.

The narrator introduces [[Boaz]] before any character knows about him: a relative of [[Elimelech]]'s, *a
mighty man of wealth* (1). [[Ruth (person)]] asks [[Naomi]] to let her glean *after him in whose sight I
find favor* (2), and she *happened to come* to Boaz's part of the field (3). Boaz arrives from
[[Bethlehem]], greets his reapers in Yahweh's name, and asks his overseer whose young woman this is; the
overseer tells him, *the Moabite lady who came back with Naomi* (4–7). Boaz tells Ruth to stay in his
field near his young women, says he has ordered the young men not to touch her, and offers her the water
they draw (8–9). She bows to the ground and asks why a *foreigner* has found favor (10). He answers that
he has heard what she did for her mother-in-law and how she left father, mother, and the land of her
birth, and he blesses her by *Yahweh, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge*
(11–12). At the meal he gives her bread and parched grain until she is satisfied, and he tells his men to
let her glean among the sheaves and to pull out grain for her on purpose (14–16). By evening she has
about an ephah of barley (17). At home she gives Naomi the grain and the food she saved; Naomi blesses the
man before she hears his name, and when she hears it she blesses him by Yahweh and says he is *one of our
near kinsmen* (18–20). Ruth stays with Boaz's young women through the barley and wheat harvests, and lives
with her mother-in-law (21–23).

**What is going on.** Chapter 1 ended with Naomi saying she came home *empty* and the narrator dating the
arrival to *the beginning of barley harvest* (1:21–22). Chapter 2 is the harvest, and the chapter fills
what Naomi called empty. It is built as a single day (3–18), framed by two conversations between Ruth and
Naomi (2, 19–22) and closed by a summary covering about two months (23). The reader knows from verse 1
what neither woman knows: that the owner of the field is family. Ruth goes out to find favor in someone's
eyes, and the word *favor*, *ḥēn*, runs through the chapter (2, 10, 13). Boaz finds out who she is from
his overseer and says he already knows her story; Naomi finds out who he is from Ruth, and knows at once
what it means. The chapter ends with the key word of the rest of the book on Naomi's lips: *gōʾēl*,
*near kinsman*, *redeemer* (20).

**What is notable.**

- **A relative, a mighty man of wealth.** The written Hebrew of verse 1 is *myd*ʿ (*mᵊyuddāʿ*, *an
  acquaintance*, tagged by the WLC as a participle of *yāḏaʿ*, lemma 3045), and the reading tradition says
  to read *mōḏāʿ*, *kinsman* (lemma 4129). In the WLC files read (Genesis–Ruth, 1 Samuel, Job, Psalms,
  Proverbs, Ecclesiastes), *mōḏāʿ* occurs only here and at Proverbs 7:4, *Call understanding your
  relative*. Naomi uses a related noun at 3:2, *Boaz our kinsman*. He is *ʾîš gibbôr ḥayil* (WLC). The
  phrase *gibbôr ḥayil* is used of soldiers at Joshua 1:14 and 6:2, of Gideon at Judges 6:12 (*Yahweh is
  with you, you mighty man of valor!*), of Jephthah at Judges 11:1, and of Saul's father Kish at 1 Samuel
  9:1. *Ḥayil* is strength, wealth, or worth; the WEB reads *wealth* here and *valor* at Judges 6:12. At
  3:11 Boaz calls Ruth *a worthy woman*, *ʾēšeṯ ḥayil*, the same noun. See
  [[A Worthy Woman - Proverbs 12.4, 31.10, and Ruth 3.11]] and [[Wealth and Possessions]].
- **Gleaning.** Ruth's request (2) describes the poor's right in Leviticus 19:9–10 and 23:22, and the
  foreigner's and widow's in Deuteronomy 24:19–22, but she does not cite the law and asks to glean *after
  him in whose sight I find favor*. The verb *lāqaṭ*, *glean* (lemma 3950, piel), occurs twelve times in
  the chapter (2, 3, 7, 8, 15 twice, 16, 17 twice, 18, 19, 23; WLC). Boaz goes past the law: the corners and
  the forgotten sheaf were left by rule, and he has grain *pulled out* of the bundles and left for her on
  purpose (16). See [[The Forgotten Sheaf - Gleaning in Leviticus 19 and 23, Deuteronomy 24, and Ruth 2]]
  and [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]].
- **She happened to come.** *Wayyiqer miqrehā*, literally *her chance chanced* (3, WLC): a verb and its
  own noun, *miqreh* (lemma 4745). In the WLC files read, *miqreh* occurs at 1 Samuel 6:9 (*it was a
  chance that happened to us*) and 20:26, and seven times in Ecclesiastes (2:14, 15; 3:19; 9:2, 3), where
  it is the *event* that happens to the wise and the fool alike. The narrator says *chance*, and then
  writes *Behold, Boaz* (4): the next sentence shows the owner arriving at that moment. The book never
  says God arranged it; Naomi will say Yahweh *has not abandoned his kindness* (20). See [[Providence]].
- **The greetings.** *YHWH ʿimmāḵem*, *Yahweh be with you*, and *yəḇāreḵḵā YHWH*, *May Yahweh bless you*
  (4, WLC). The second is the first two words of the priestly blessing, *Yahweh bless you* (Numbers 6:24);
  the first is the angel's word to Gideon, *Yahweh is with you* (Judges 6:12), in the verse that also
  calls him *gibbôr heḥāyil*. Here they are an employer's greeting and the workers' reply. See
  [[Blessing]] and [[Let Your Face Shine - Numbers 6 in Psalms 4, 67, 80, and 119]].
- **Whose young lady is this?** Boaz's first question is about whom she belongs to, *ləmî*, *to whom*
  (5). The overseer answers with her nation twice, *naʿărâ môʾăḇiyyâ*, *a Moabite young woman*, *who came
  back with Naomi out of the country of Moab* (6, WLC), and with a report that she asked permission and
  has worked since morning. Verse 7's last clause is hard Hebrew; word for word it is *this her sitting
  (or dwelling) the house a little*, and the WEB's *except that she rested a little in the house* is one
  way of making sense of it (the difficulty is visible in the WLC; proposed solutions are not assessed
  here).
- **Stay close.** *Tiḏbāqîn*, *you shall cling* (8, 21), and *wattiḏbaq*, *so she clung* (23): the verb
  *dāḇaq* of Ruth 1:14 (*Ruth joined with her*) comes back three times, now for staying with Boaz's young
  women. In 2:21 Ruth reports Boaz as saying *my young men*; in 2:8 he said *my maidens*, and Naomi
  steers her back to *his maidens* (22). Boaz has *commanded the young men not to touch you* (9), and Naomi
  says she should not be *met* in another field (22, *pāḡaʿ*, a verb that can mean to fall upon). Both
  imply that a woman gleaning alone was at risk; the text does not spell it out. See
  [[Go Back or Cling - Joshua 23.12 and Ruth 1.14-16]].
- **The water.** *Go to the vessels, and drink from that which the young men have drawn* (9). In the
  betrothal stories of Genesis 24 and 29 and Exodus 2 a woman draws water for a stranger or a man waters
  a woman's flock. Here the young men have drawn it, and the owner offers it to the foreign woman. See
  [[The Betrothal at the Well]].
- **Take knowledge of me … a foreigner.** *Ləhakkîrēnî wəʾānōḵî noḵriyyâ* (10, WLC): *to recognize me*
  (hiphil of *nāḵar*, lemma 5234 a) and *a foreigner* (*noḵrî*, lemma 5237) sound alike, and Ruth puts
  them side by side. Naomi uses the same verb in 19, *Blessed be he who noticed you*, *makkîrēḵ*. In the
  WLC files read, the feminine singular *noḵriyyâ* is otherwise Exodus' word for Moses' *foreign land*
  (2:22; 18:3) and Proverbs' word for the *strange woman* who leads a young man to ruin (2:16; 5:20; 6:24;
  7:5; 20:16 as read; 23:27; 27:13); Rachel and Leah use the plural of themselves, *Aren't we considered as
  foreigners by him?* (Genesis 31:15). Ruth uses it of herself. See [[The Nations]] and
  [[Two Voices in the Street - The Strange Woman and Wisdom in Proverbs 7-9]].
- **You left your father and your mother.** *Wattaʿazḇî ʾāḇîḵ wəʾimmēḵ wəʾereṣ môlaḏtēḵ* (11, WLC). The
  verb *ʿāzaḇ* with *father and mother* is Genesis 2:24's *a man will leave his father and his mother*,
  and *ʾereṣ* with *môleḏeṯ* is the pair of Yahweh's command to Abram, *Leave your country, and your
  relatives* (*mēʾarṣəḵā ûmimmôlaḏtəḵā*, Genesis 12:1). In the WLC files read, *môleḏeṯ* occurs nine
  times in Genesis and once in Ruth. Boaz's summary puts Ruth's departure in the terms of the first
  marriage and the first call; the text does not say he is quoting either. See
  [[Leave Your Country - The Two Lek-Lekha Commands]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].
- **Under whose wings.** *Lahăsôṯ taḥaṯ kənāpāyw*, *to take refuge under his wings* (12, WLC). *Ḥāsâ*,
  *take refuge* (lemma 2620), occurs in the WLC files read at Deuteronomy 32:37, Judges 9:15 (Jotham's
  bramble: *come and take refuge in my shade*), here, twice in Proverbs, and twenty-five times in Psalms;
  with *wings* it is a Psalms image (36:7; 57:1; 61:4; 91:4), and *taḥaṯ kənāpāyw* exactly recurs only at
  Psalm 91:4, *Under his wings you will take refuge*. At the threshing floor Ruth will ask Boaz, *spread
  the corner of your garment over your servant* (3:9), and *corner* is *kənāp̄əḵā*, the same noun. See
  [[Under His Wings - Ruth 2.12, 3.9, and the Refuge of the Psalms]].
- **A full reward.** *Maśkurtēḵ šəlēmâ* (12). *Maśkōreṯ*, *wages*, occurs in the WLC files read only
  here and in the Jacob–Laban story (Genesis 29:15; 31:7, 41), where Laban changed Jacob's wages ten times.
- **You have spoken kindly.** *Niḥamtānî … dibbartā ʿal-lēḇ*, *you have comforted me … you have spoken to
  the heart* (13, WLC). Joseph does both for his brothers: *He comforted them, and spoke kindly to them*
  (Genesis 50:21). *Speak to the heart* is also Shechem to Dinah (Genesis 34:3) and the Levite going after
  his concubine (Judges 19:3). See [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] and
  [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]].
- **Not as one of your servants.** Ruth calls herself *your servant*, *šip̄ḥāṯəḵā*, and then says she is
  not like one of his *šəp̄āḥōṯ* (13). At 3:9 she will use the other word, *ʾămāṯəḵā*, *your servant*
  (WLC). Whether the change of word means a change of standing is debated (reported, not assessed here).
- **She ate, and was satisfied, and left some of it.** *Wattōʾḵal wattiśbaʿ wattōṯar* (14). In verse 18
  she brings out *that which she had left after she had enough* (*hôṯirâ miśśāḇʿāh*) and gives it to
  Naomi. The woman who said she came home empty is fed by the foreigner she did not count. See
  [[Famine and Plenty]] and [[Hospitality]].
- **About an ephah of barley.** By Exodus 16:36, *an omer is one tenth of an ephah*, and the manna ration
  was *an omer a head* a day (16:16). On that reckoning one day's gleaning was ten days' manna for one
  person. This is the reader's arithmetic, not the narrator's; the narrator says only *about an ephah*.
- **Blessed be he who noticed you.** Naomi blesses the man before she knows his name (19), and Ruth puts
  his name last in her answer, *the man's name … is Boaz*.
- **Who has not abandoned his kindness.** *Bārûḵ hûʾ laYHWH ʾăšer lōʾ-ʿāzaḇ ḥasdô* (20, WLC). The
  relative clause can attach to *Yahweh* or to *he*, Boaz; the Hebrew does not decide, and the WEB's
  punctuation leaves it open. The same words, *ʾăšer lōʾ ʿāzaḇ ḥasdô*, are spoken by Abraham's servant
  when he finds Rebekah among his master's kin, *Blessed be Yahweh … who has not forsaken his loving
  kindness and his truth toward my master* (Genesis 24:27), and there they belong to Yahweh. In the WLC
  files read, *ʿāzaḇ* with *ḥeseḏ* occurs only in those two verses. Naomi's last speech about God was
  *Yahweh has testified against me* (1:21); this is her first blessing since the road (1:8–9). See
  [[Love]] and [[A Wife for Isaac]].
- **The living and the dead.** Naomi includes the dead, Elimelech, Mahlon, and Chilion, in the kindness.
  The book will name the purpose at 4:5, *to raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance* (not yet
  ingested). See [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].
- **One of our near kinsmen.** *Mig-gōʾălēnû hûʾ* (20, WLC), the participle of *gāʾal*, *redeem*. It is
  the first of twenty-two forms of the verb *gāʾal* in the book, all between 2:20 and 4:14
  (WLC; the noun *gəʾullâ* of 4:6–7 not counted). Naomi says *one of*:
  there are other redeemers, and one nearer than Boaz will appear at 3:12. Under Leviticus 25:25 the
  *gōʾēl* buys back land a poor relative has sold. See [[Redemption]] and
  [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]].
- **The end of barley harvest and of wheat harvest** (23). Barley comes first and wheat later; the WEB
  of Exodus 9:31–32 has the barley in the ear while the wheat is not yet up. The verse covers the whole
  grain season, and the next chapter opens at its end, with the barley winnowing (3:2). *She lived with
  her mother-in-law*: she is still not married, and the chapter has not said that anyone thought of it.

**How it connects.** The chapter turns Naomi's complaint into a question of whose favor and whose
kindness Ruth will find. Ruth hopes for a man's favor (2); Boaz invokes Yahweh's wings (12); Naomi
blesses Yahweh, or Boaz, for kindness to the living and the dead (20). The narrator credits nobody: the
field was *chance* (3). Boaz is drawn as the man the law hopes for. He follows the gleaning laws and goes
beyond them, protects the foreigner, feeds her at his own table, and speaks of Yahweh to his workers.
The words of the chapter point forward: *wings* to the threshing floor (3:9), *gōʾēl* to the gate
(4:1–10), *worth* (*ḥayil*) to *a worthy woman* (3:11), and *left over* to the six measures of barley
that will keep Ruth from going home *empty* (3:17).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The boundary 2:1–23 is kept as planned: one chapter, one day in the field framed by
Ruth's two conversations with Naomi, and a summary verse. A person page was created for [[Boaz]]. The
overseer of the reapers gets no page; he speaks once. A new connection page,
[[Under His Wings - Ruth 2.12, 3.9, and the Refuge of the Psalms]], records the *wings* of 2:12 and the
*corner* of 3:9 and the Psalms' refuge language; Psalms is not yet ingested, so its verses are quoted from
the WEB at bible-api.com and its passages are not linked.

**On *chance*.** The narrator's word is *miqreh*. The wiki's [[Providence]] page reads the chapter as the
method of Genesis 37–50 without the vocabulary; that is the reader's reading. The text itself sets chance
(3) beside Naomi's blessing of a God who has not abandoned his kindness (20) and does not reconcile them.

**On 2:20.** Whose *ḥeseḏ*? Grammatically either Yahweh's or Boaz's. The verbal match with Genesis 24:27,
where it is Yahweh's, is recorded; it does not settle Ruth 2:20.

**On 2:1 and 2:7.** The written and read forms of verse 1 are from the WLC's ketiv/qere tagging. The
difficulty of verse 7's last clause is an observation from the WLC words; the page does not offer a
solution.
