# Famine and Death in Moab

**Book:** [[Ruth]]
**Reference:** Ruth 1:1–5

## Summary

> **1** In the days when the judges judged, there was a famine in the land. A certain man of Bethlehem
> Judah went to live in the country of Moab, he, and his wife, and his two sons.
> **2** The name of the man was Elimelech, and the name of his wife Naomi. The names of his two sons were
> Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah. They came into the country of Moab, and lived there.
> **3** Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died; and she was left with her two sons.
> **4** They took for themselves wives of the women of Moab. The name of the one was Orpah, and the name of
> the other was Ruth. They lived there about ten years.
> **5** Mahlon and Chilion both died, and the woman was bereaved of her two children and of her husband.

Five verses take a family of four out of Judah and leave one widow in Moab. A famine drives
[[Elimelech]] of [[Bethlehem]] to live as a sojourner in [[Moab (place)]] with his wife [[Naomi]] and their
sons [[Mahlon]] and [[Chilion]] (1–2). He dies (3). The sons marry Moabite women, [[Orpah]] and
[[Ruth (person)]], and the family stays about ten years (4). Then both sons die, and the woman is left
without husband or children (5).

**What is going on.** This is the book's setup, and the whole problem the rest of Ruth answers is laid out
in it: no food, no husband, no sons, no land worked, and a woman far from her people. Every loss is told in
one clause, without cause or comment. The narrator gives no reason for the deaths and no judgment on the
move or the marriages. The book will reverse each item: the famine ends (*Yahweh had visited his people in
giving them bread*, 1:6), Naomi comes home (1:22), food is gathered (2), a near kinsman is found (2:20;
3–4), the dead man's field and name are redeemed (4:9–10), and *a son is born to Naomi* (4:17).

**What is notable.**

- **In the days when the judges judged.** *Bîmê šəpōṭ haššōpəṭîm* (1, WLC). The book's first words set it in
  the period of [[Judges]], which in the English canon it follows; the Hebrew Bible's traditional order
  places Ruth among the Writings instead (canon order reported, not argued here). Judges closed on *In those
  days there was no king in Israel* (Judges 21:25). Ruth's closing word is *David* (4:22). The book never
  names which judge, and nothing in it dates the famine more precisely.
- **A famine in the land.** *Wayhî rāʿāb bāʾāreṣ* (1, WLC) is word for word the clause that opens Abram's
  descent to Egypt (Genesis 12:10) and Isaac's journey to Gerar (Genesis 26:1). In the WLC of Genesis,
  Judges, and Ruth, the exact clause occurs only in those three verses. Abram went *to live as a foreigner
  there*, *lāgûr* (Genesis 12:10); Elimelech went *to live*, *lāgûr*, in Moab (1). Jacob's sons say the same
  to Pharaoh: *We have come to live as foreigners in the land, for … the famine is severe* (Genesis 47:4).
  The book of Ruth opens on the patriarchs' road, pointed east instead of south. See
  [[Don't Go Down to Egypt - The Three Patriarchs and the Same Road]] and [[Famine and Plenty]].
- **Out of the house of bread.** *Bêt leḥem* is *house of bread*. The famine falls on the town whose name
  means food, and the family goes for bread to Moab, a people barred from Yahweh's assembly *because they
  didn't meet you with bread and with water on the way* (Deuteronomy 23:3–4). The pericope does not draw
  either irony. The wiki records it as a reading. See [[Bethlehem]].
- **A man of Bethlehem Judah went to sojourn.** *Wayyēleḵ ʾîš mibbêt leḥem yəhûdâ lāgûr* (1, WLC). Judges
  17:8 has almost the same words of Micah's Levite: *wayyēleḵ hāʾîš … mibbêt leḥem yəhûdâ lāgûr*, *The man
  departed … out of Bethlehem Judah, to live where he could find a place*. The two journeys share a town, a
  verb of going, and an infinitive of sojourning. The Levite's journey ends in a stolen idol and a
  tribe's shrine; this one in a widow and a grandson. See
  [[Went to Sojourn from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17.8 and Ruth 1.1]].
- **Ephrathites.** *ʾEprāṯîm* (2, WLC). The gentilic *ʾeprāṯî* (lemma 673) occurs in the WLC files of
  Genesis, Judges, Ruth, and 1 Samuel only at Judges 12:5, Ruth 1:2, 1 Samuel 1:1, and 1 Samuel 17:12. At
  Judges 12:5 and 1 Samuel 1:1 the WEB renders it *Ephraimite*; here and at 1 Samuel 17:12, where it
  belongs to *Bethlehem Judah*, it points to *Ephrath*, the older name Genesis 35:19 glosses as
  Bethlehem. 1 Samuel 17:12 introduces David's father the same way: *that Ephrathite of Bethlehem Judah,
  whose name was Jesse* (WEB, checked at bible-api.com). The family that leaves here is the clan David
  will come from.
- **She was left.** *Wattiššāʾēr hîʾ* (3), and again *wattiššāʾēr hāʾiššâ* (5, WLC). The WEB's *bereaved*
  in verse 5 is the same verb as *left* in verse 3: literally *the woman was left from her two children
  and from her husband*. In the WLC of Ruth, *šāʾar* (lemma 7604) occurs only in these two verses. The
  verb is the one of *Only Noah was left* (Genesis 7:23). Naomi is what remains.
- **The woman.** By verse 5 the narrator stops using her name: *hāʾiššâ*, *the woman*. She has been
  *his wife* (1), *Naomi* (2), *Naomi's husband*'s widow (3), and now only *the woman*. In 1:20 she will
  refuse her name herself.
- **Her two children.** Verse 5 calls the married sons *yəlādêhā*, *her children* (*yeleḏ*), not *bānêhā*,
  *her sons*, the word of verse 3. In the WLC of Ruth *yeleḏ* (lemma 3206) occurs twice: here, and at
  4:16, *Naomi took the child*, *hayyeleḏ*, Obed. The book loses two children in its fifth verse and puts
  one in Naomi's lap near its end. The word is in the text; the bracket is a reader's observation.
- **About ten years, and no child.** *Kəʿeśer šānîm* (4, WLC). The text reports no children from either
  marriage. That Ruth had none by Mahlon follows from 4:10 and 4:13, where she is *the wife of Mahlon* and
  only conceives by Boaz. Orpah's husband is not named; since 4:10 makes Ruth Mahlon's wife, Orpah was
  presumably Chilion's, an inference the text leaves the reader to make. The order of 1:2 (Mahlon,
  Chilion) and 1:4 (Orpah, Ruth) does not pair them.
- **Moabite wives, without comment.** *Nāšîm môʾăḇiyyôṯ* (4, WLC). Deuteronomy 7:1–3 forbids marriage with
  seven named nations, and Moab is not among them. Deuteronomy 23:3 bars *an Ammonite or a Moabite* from
  Yahweh's assembly *to the tenth generation*, which is a law about the assembly, not about marriage.
  Later readers applied it to marriage (Nehemiah 13:1–3, 23–27; not yet ingested, paraphrased). Ruth 1
  neither condemns nor justifies the marriages. The wiki records the tension and resolves nothing. See
  [[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]] and [[The Nations]].
- **The names.** The text explains none of the four names here, but 1:20 builds on one: *Don't call me
  Naomi. Call me Mara* (not yet ingested). *Elimelech* reads naturally as *ʾĕlî meleḵ*, *my God is king*,
  in a period the book's predecessor ends by calling kingless (Judges 21:25). *Naomi* is usually connected
  with *nʿm*, *pleasant*, which 1:20 contrasts with *bitter*. *Mahlon* and *Chilion* are often linked
  with roots meaning *sickness* and *failing*; those two etymologies are disputed and the wiki reports
  them, not builds on them. See [[Names and Naming]].

**How it connects.** The pericope stands at the junction of two books already in the wiki and one not
yet reached. Behind it are Judges' two journeys out of Bethlehem Judah (17:7–9; 19:1), the Moab of Lot's
daughters (Genesis 19:37) and of Eglon (Judges 3:12–30), and the levirate law that a dead man's name not
be *blotted out of Israel* (Deuteronomy 25:6). Ahead of it is the line *Perez … Boaz … Obed … Jesse …
David* (4:18–22) and Jesse *the Ephrathite of Bethlehem Judah* (1 Samuel 17:12). See
[[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]] and
[[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]].

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The pericope boundary is 1:1–5: the setting and the losses, before Naomi rises to
return (1:6). The book's eight-pericope plan treats 1:6–18 (the parting on the road) and 1:19–22 (the
arrival in Bethlehem) as separate units.

**On reading the deaths as judgment.** Some interpreters read Elimelech's and his sons' deaths as
punishment for leaving the land or for marrying Moabites (a tradition reported, not verified here). The
text gives no cause. Naomi will later say *Yahweh's hand has gone out against me* (1:13) and *the
Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me* (1:20), which is her reading, not the narrator's. The wiki keeps
the two apart.
