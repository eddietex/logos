# The Generations of Perez

**Book:** [[Ruth]]
**Reference:** Ruth 4:18–22

## Summary

> **18** Now this is the history of the generations of Perez: Perez became the father of Hezron,
> **19** and Hezron became the father of Ram, and Ram became the father of Amminadab,
> **20** and Amminadab became the father of Nahshon, and Nahshon became the father of Salmon,
> **21** and Salmon became the father of Boaz, and Boaz became the father of Obed,
> **22** and Obed became the father of Jesse, and Jesse became the father of David.

The book ends on a list. Under the heading *this is the history of the generations of Perez* (18), ten
names follow, father to son: [[Perez]], [[Hezron (son of Perez)]], [[Ram (son of Hezron)]],
[[Amminadab]], [[Nahshon]], [[Salmon]], [[Boaz]], [[Obed]], [[Jesse]], [[David]]. Each link is the same
clause, *X became the father of Y*, nine times. No woman is named, no place, no age, no event. The last
word of the book is *David*.

**What is going on.** Verse 17 already ended the story at *the father of Jesse, the father of David*. The
genealogy adds two things. It runs the line backward past Boaz to [[Perez]], the son [[Tamar]] bore to
[[Judah]] — the same Perez the elders named in their blessing a few verses earlier, *Let your house be like
the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah* (4:12). And it gives the line the heading Genesis uses for
its family histories, so that a short story set *in the days when the judges judged* (1:1) ends inside the
large frame of Genesis's *generations*. The private story of Naomi's family turns out to be one link in a
line that runs from the patriarchs to the king.

**What is notable.**

- **The heading.** *Wəʾēlleh tôlədôṯ pāreṣ* (18, WLC), *and these are the generations of Perez*. It is the
  formula of Genesis — *This is the history of the generations of Shem* (Genesis 11:10), and *the book of
  the generations of Adam* (5:1). By the WLC search recorded on
  [[The Generations of Aaron and Moses - Toledot Outside Genesis]], the headed form occurs outside Genesis
  only here, at Numbers 3:1, and (with a suffix) at 1 Chronicles 1:29. Ruth's is the one of those that
  opens a line and carries it somewhere.
- **One verb, nine times.** Every link is *hôlîḏ ʾeṯ*, the hiphil of *yālaḏ*, *became the father of*
  (WLC, all five verses). The WEB keeps the uniformity. The list has none of Genesis 5's or 11's ages and
  years; it is only the chain.
- **Ten names.** The lists of Genesis 5 (Adam to Noah) and Genesis 11:10–26 (Shem to Abram) each run ten
  names as well, and each ends on a man through whom the story turns. Ruth's runs from Perez to David. On
  that shape some readers also note that Boaz stands seventh, as Enoch does in Genesis 5. The count is in
  the text; any significance given to the seventh and tenth places is a reading, and Ruth does not mark
  either.
- **Salmah and Salmon.** The Hebrew spells the name two ways within two verses: *śalmâ* at the end of
  verse 20 and *śalmôn* at the start of 21 (WLC; Strong's 8009 and 8012, each occurring only in its one
  verse in the files read). 1 Chronicles 2:11 has a third spelling, *śalmāʾ* (WEB *Salma*). The WEB
  normalizes both of Ruth's to *Salmon*. See [[Salmon]].
- **The gaps.** Perez went down into Egypt with Jacob, and his sons Hezron and Hamul with him (Genesis
  46:12). Nahshon, three generations later, is the prince of Judah in the wilderness (Numbers 1:7; 2:3).
  Exodus 12:40 gives *four hundred thirty years* for Israel's time in Egypt, and only Hezron, Ram, and
  Amminadab stand between Perez and Nahshon. Salmon, Boaz, Obed, and Jesse then cover the conquest and the
  whole period of the judges. Many readers take the list as selective, naming representative links rather
  than every generation; the text itself does not say whether it is complete, and the page records the
  arithmetic without deciding it.
- **A prince of Judah in the line.** [[Nahshon]] is the one name between Perez and Boaz that the earlier
  books give a role: Judah's census head (Numbers 1:7), *prince of the children of Judah* at the head of
  the east camp (2:3), first to bring his offering at the altar's dedication (7:12), and over the army
  that marched first (10:14). Ruth gives him no title. 1 Chronicles 2:10 does: *Nahshon, prince of the
  children of Judah* (WEB at bible-api.com; *nəśîʾ bənê yəhûḏâ*, WLC). His sister [[Elisheba]] married
  [[Aaron]] (Exodus 6:23), so the line to David and the priestly house meet one generation above him.
- **Who is not there.** No mother is named, so neither Tamar nor [[Ruth (person)]] stands in the list that
  ends her book. Nor do [[Elimelech]] or [[Mahlon]]: the marriage at the gate was made *to raise up the
  name of the dead on his inheritance* (4:5, 10), and the women said *A son is born to Naomi* (4:17), but
  the genealogy gives the son as Boaz's — *Boaz became the father of Obed* (21). The book lets the three
  attributions stand side by side. See
  [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].
- **Boaz's place.** The man the book has followed for three chapters is one link among ten, with the same
  clause as Ram, of whom nothing is known. The genealogy places the story's hero in a line much larger
  than his story.
- **The last word.** *Dāwiḏ* (22). Judges ended *In those days there was no king in Israel* (Judges
  21:25); Ruth ends on the name of the king, without the word *king*. Matthew 1:6 supplies it: *Jesse
  became the father of King David* (WEB at bible-api.com). See [[Kingship and Empire]].

**How it connects.** The same line is given three more times. 1 Chronicles 2:9–15 runs it from Hezron's
three sons to *David the seventh* son of Jesse, with Salma for Salmon and Nahshon titled *prince*.
Matthew 1:3–6 runs it from Judah and adds four mothers — Tamar, Rahab as Salmon's wife, Ruth, and *her who
had been Uriah's wife* — and Luke 3:31–33 runs it in reverse with *Aram* for Ram (WEB). Joshua never links
[[Rahab]] to Salmon; that link is Matthew's alone (WLC search: neither spelling of the name occurs in
Joshua). See [[From Perez to David - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, Matthew 1, and Luke 3]] and
[[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]]. Within Ruth, the list answers the
elders' blessing of 4:12: Boaz's house did become *like the house of Perez*, because it is that house.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The planned boundary 4:18–22 is kept; it is the book's last unit and completes Ruth.
[[Ram (son of Hezron)]], [[Amminadab]], and [[Salmon]] get pages: each is a link in the line of David
carried by four books, and the wiki already names Amminadab on several pages without one. Ram takes a
qualifier because Elihu is *of the family of Ram* (Job 32:2) and 1 Chronicles 2:25, 27 has a Ram son of
Jerahmeel (WLC lemma 7410); Amminadab stays bare for now, though 1 Chronicles 6:7 and 15:10–11 name a
Levite Amminadab (WLC lemma 5992), and will need a qualifier when that one gets a page.

**On the gaps.** The page sets out the four-hundred-thirty-year arithmetic and the reading that the list
is selective. It does not adopt a chronology.

**On Luke 3:33.** The WEB reads *the son of Amminadab, the son of Aram*. Other translations and some
manuscripts read differently at this point (recalled, not verified against a Greek edition); the page
relies only on the WEB reading. No Greek text was consulted.

**On Rahab.** Matthew 1:5 names *Rahab* as Salmon's wife and does not say she is the woman of Jericho.
The identification is the usual reading and is assumed on [[Rahab]] with that qualification.
