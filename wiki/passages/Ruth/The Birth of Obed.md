# The Birth of Obed

**Book:** [[Ruth]]
**Reference:** Ruth 4:13–17

## Summary

> **13** So Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife; and he went in to her, and Yahweh enabled her to
> conceive, and she bore a son.
> **14** The women said to Naomi, "Blessed be Yahweh, who has not left you today without a near kinsman.
> Let his name be famous in Israel.
> **15** He shall be to you a restorer of life, and sustain you in your old age, for your daughter-in-law,
> who loves you, who is better to you than seven sons, has given birth to him."
> **16** Naomi took the child, and laid him in her bosom, and became nurse to it.
> **17** The women, her neighbors, gave him a name, saying, "A son is born to Naomi". They named him Obed.
> He is the father of Jesse, the father of David.

[[Boaz]] takes [[Ruth (person)]], she becomes his wife, he goes in to her, *and Yahweh enabled her to
conceive*, and she bears a son (13). The birth takes one verse. The rest of the scene belongs to [[Naomi]]
and the women of [[Bethlehem]]. They bless Yahweh, *who has not left you today without a near kinsman*,
and pray that the child's name be famous in Israel (14). He will be to her *a restorer of life* and will
*sustain you in your old age*, because Ruth bore him — *your daughter-in-law, who loves you, who is better
to you than seven sons* (15). Naomi takes the child, lays him in her bosom, and becomes his nurse (16).
The neighbor women name him, saying *A son is born to Naomi*, and call him [[Obed]]; the narrator adds that
he is the father of [[Jesse]], the father of [[David]] (17).

**What is going on.** The book ends where it began, with Naomi, and reverses what it took from her. In
[[Famine and Death in Moab]] she lost a husband and *two children* (1:5); here she holds *the child*. In
[[Naomi Returns to Bethlehem]] the women asked *Is this Naomi?* and she told them *Yahweh has brought me
home again empty* (1:19–21); here the women speak again, to her, and bless the God she accused. On the road
she argued that she had no more sons in her womb to give (1:11–12); here the women say a son is born to
her, and that the daughter-in-law she tried to send back is worth more than seven. The book gives the
redemption, the marriage, and the conception in one verse each, and spends four on an old woman's
consolation. Then, in the last six words of verse 17, it names David, and the story of one family's
survival becomes the beginning of Israel's kings. The genealogy that follows (4:18–22) spells the line out.

**What is notable.**

- **Yahweh enabled her to conceive.** *Wayyittēn YHWH lāh hērāyôn* (13, WLC), literally *Yahweh gave her
  conception*. It is one of only two places in the book where the narrator, not a character, makes Yahweh
  the subject of an act; the other is *Yahweh had visited his people in giving them bread* (1:6), and both
  use *nāṯan*, *give*. The noun *hērāyôn* occurs in the WLC files read (Genesis–Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel)
  only here and, in the form *hērōnēḵ*, at Genesis 3:16, *I will greatly multiply your pain in childbirth*
  (WEB *childbirth*; the Hebrew there is *your conception*). Ruth was married to Mahlon for some part of
  *about ten years* in Moab (1:4), and the book names no child of that marriage; it never calls her
  barren. See [[Barrenness]] and [[The Barren Matriarchs]].
- **The women said.** *Wattōʾmarnâ hannāšîm* (14), and at 1:19 the same verb form, *wattōʾmarnâ*, *they
  said* (feminine plural), asked *Is this Naomi?* (WLC). The town's women frame Naomi's return: they asked
  the question that drew her lament, and they answer it here. No man speaks after verse 12.
- **Who has not left you today without a near kinsman.** *Lōʾ hišbîṯ lāḵ gōʾēl hayyôm* (14). The *gōʾēl*
  here is the child, not Boaz: it is the newborn who *shall be to you a restorer of life* (15). The word
  that named Boaz at 2:20 and the nearer man at 3:12 and 4:1–8 now names a baby. With this verse the root
  *gāʾal* comes twenty-one times in Ruth (WLC; 4:6's written-and-read pair counted once, as on
  [[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]]), and this is the last. The blessing also answers Naomi's own: *who has not
  abandoned his kindness to the living and to the dead* (2:20). See [[Redemption]].
- **Let his name be famous in Israel.** *Wəyiqqārēʾ šəmô bəyiśrāʾēl* (14), *let his name be called*. It
  echoes the town's blessing on Boaz, *be famous in Bethlehem* (*qərāʾ-šēm*, 4:11), widened from the town to
  Israel; and it comes three verses after Boaz acted *that the name of the dead may not be cut off* (4:10).
  See [[Names and Naming]].
- **A restorer of life.** *Ləmēšîḇ nep̄eš* (15), *one who brings back the soul*. *Mēšîḇ* is the hiphil of
  *šûḇ*, *return*, the key word of chapter 1. In Ruth the root's only other hiphil is Naomi's complaint,
  *Yahweh has brought me home again empty* (*hĕšîḇanî*, 1:21, WLC). Yahweh *brought her back* empty; the
  child *brings back* her life. The pairing of the two verbs is in the text; that the narrator meant one to
  answer the other is a reader's step, though a short one. See [[Life and Death]].
- **Sustain you in your old age.** *Ûləḵalkēl ʾeṯ-śêḇāṯēḵ* (15). *Kilkēl*, *sustain, nourish*, occurs in
  the files read otherwise only of Joseph providing for his father's house in the famine: *There I will
  nourish you* (Genesis 45:11), *Joseph nourished his father* (47:12), *I will nourish you and your little
  ones* (50:21) (WLC; WEB *nourish*). *Śêḇâ*, *gray hairs, old age*, is Jacob's word for what his sons
  would bring *down … with sorrow to Sheol* (Genesis 42:38). A book that began in famine ends with a
  grandson promised to feed a grandmother. See
  [[Sustain Your Old Age - Joseph's Nourishing and Ruth 4.15]].
- **Your daughter-in-law, who loves you.** *ʾĂšer ʾăhēḇaṯeḵ* (15). It is the only place in Ruth that uses
  *ʾāhaḇ*, *love*, and it is said of Ruth toward Naomi, by the women, not by either of them. What the book
  showed as *ḥeseḏ* and *clinging* (1:8, 14; 3:10) is named love only at the end. See [[Love]].
- **Better to you than seven sons.** *Ṭôḇâ lāḵ miššiḇʿâ bānîm* (15). Seven sons is a full house. The same
  construction comes in Elkanah's words to Hannah, *Am I not better to you than ten sons?* (1 Samuel 1:8,
  *ṭôḇ lāḵ mēʿăśārâ bānîm*, WLC; WEB at bible-api.com), and Hannah's song says *the barren has borne seven*
  (2:5). Naomi lost two sons; the women value Ruth above seven. See
  [[Better Than Seven Sons - Ruth 4.15 and 1 Samuel 1.8]].
- **Laid him in her bosom, and became nurse to it.** *Wattəšiṯēhû ḇəḥêqāh wattəhî-lô ləʾōmeneṯ* (16).
  *ʾŌmeneṯ* is a carer or guardian, not necessarily a wet-nurse; the feminine participle occurs in the
  files read only here. Its masculine, *hāʾōmēn*, comes with *ḥêq*, *bosom*, in Moses' complaint: *Carry
  them in your bosom, as a nurse carries a nursing infant* (Numbers 11:12). Numbers 11:12 and Ruth 4:16 are
  the only verses in the files read where the two words stand together (WLC). Moses refused the role; Naomi
  takes it. See [[Carried as a Man Carries His Son - Numbers 11.12 and Deuteronomy 1.31]].
- **Is this an adoption?** Some read verse 16 as a formal act by which Naomi adopts the child, comparing
  Rachel's *that she may bear on my knees* (Genesis 30:3) and Machir's children *born on Joseph's knees*
  (50:23). Ruth 4:16 uses *bosom*, not *knees*, and names no legal act. The page records the reading and
  does not adopt it. See [[Adoption]].
- **A son is born to Naomi.** *Yullaḏ-bēn lənoʿŏmî* (17). The levirate purpose was a son for *the dead*
  (4:5, 10), Mahlon; the women credit the son to Naomi; the genealogy will give him as Boaz's (*Boaz became
  the father of Obed*, 4:21). The book lets all three stand. See
  [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].
- **The women, her neighbors, gave him a name.** *Wattiqreʾnâ lô haššəḵēnôṯ šēm* (17). In Genesis the
  mother, or the father, names the child: Leah and Rachel name Jacob's sons as they are born (Genesis 29–30), and
  Rachel's Benoni is renamed Benjamin by Jacob (35:18). In the narratives the wiki has read, this is the only child
  named by neighbors. *Šəḵēnôṯ*, *neighbor women*, occurs in the files read only here; the singular feminine is the
  Israelite woman who asks *of her neighbor* for silver and gold at the exodus (Exodus 3:22; WLC). See
  [[Names and Naming]].
- **Obed.** *ʿÔḇēḏ* (17), the participle of *ʿāḇaḏ*, *one who serves* or *works*. The book gives no reason
  for the name, unlike Genesis's naming speeches; the women's saying, *A son is born to Naomi*, stands
  where an explanation usually stands. Whether the name means service to God or to Naomi the book does not
  say.
- **The father of Jesse, the father of David.** (17). The book's first mention of David. Judges never
  names him (WLC lemma search), and it ends *In those days there was no king in Israel* (Judges 21:25);
  Ruth ends three generations short of the king. See [[Kingship and Empire]].

**How it connects.** The scene closes every thread the book opened. The *empty* woman of 1:21 is filled; the
*left* woman of 1:3, 5 is *not left … without a near kinsman*; the sons she said she could not bear (1:11–12)
are surpassed by the daughter-in-law she sent away; the bread of 1:6 becomes sustenance in old age; the
*name of the dead* (4:5, 10) becomes a name *famous in Israel*. The birth itself belongs with the barren
wives of Genesis and Judges, whose conceptions the narrator always gives to God, and with Hannah, the next
barren wife in the canon, whose son Samuel will anoint David (1 Samuel 1–2; 16, not yet ingested). The last word,
*David*, carries the book forward into Samuel and backward, through the genealogy of 4:18–22, to Judah
and Tamar.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The planned boundary 4:13–17 is kept: the birth and the women's blessing and naming,
ending at *the father of David*. The genealogy of 4:18–22 is a separate unit with its own heading (*this is
the history of the generations of Perez*) and stays the next pericope; it is cited by reference here and not linked
until its page exists. [[Obed]] and [[Jesse]] get pages: Obed is the subject of the pericope,
and Jesse is named here and will be a major figure in 1 Samuel. The women of Bethlehem get no page; they
are a group without a name, and [[Naomi]] and [[Bethlehem]] carry them.

**On the adoption reading.** Whether Naomi's taking the child into her bosom (16) is a legal adoption, a
grandmother's care, or a guardian's office (*ʾōmeneṯ*) is debated; the page reports the comparison with
Genesis 30:3 and 50:23 and the difference of wording, and does not decide.

**On whose son.** The three attributions — to the dead (4:5, 10), to Naomi (4:17), to Boaz (4:21) — are
recorded as the text gives them, not harmonized.

**On David in Judges.** The claim that the name David does not occur in Judges is from a lemma search of
the WLC `Judg` file (lemma 1732), not from reading.
