# Boaz Redeems at the Gate

**Book:** [[Ruth]]
**Reference:** Ruth 4:1–12

## Summary

> **1** Now Boaz went up to the gate, and sat down there. Behold, the near kinsman of whom Boaz spoke came
> by. He said to him, "Come over here, friend, and sit down!" He came over, and sat down.
> **2** He took ten men of the elders of the city, and said, "Sit down here," and they sat down.
> **3** He said to the near kinsman, "Naomi, who has come back out of the country of Moab, is selling the
> parcel of land, which was our brother Elimelech's.
> **4** I thought I should tell you, saying, 'Buy it before those who sit here, and before the elders of my
> people.' If you will redeem it, redeem it; but if you will not redeem it, then tell me, that I may know.
> For there is no one to redeem it besides you; and I am after you." He said, "I will redeem it."
> **5** Then Boaz said, "On the day you buy the field from the hand of Naomi, you must buy it also from Ruth
> the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance."
> **6** The near kinsman said, "I can't redeem it for myself, lest I endanger my own inheritance. Take my
> right of redemption for yourself; for I can't redeem it."
> **7** Now this was the custom in former time in Israel concerning redeeming and concerning exchanging, to
> confirm all things: a man took off his shoe, and gave it to his neighbor; and this was the way of
> formalizing transactions in Israel.
> **8** So the near kinsman said to Boaz, "Buy it for yourself," then he took off his shoe.
> **9** Boaz said to the elders, and to all the people, "You are witnesses today, that I have bought all that
> was Elimelech's, and all that was Chilion's and Mahlon's, from the hand of Naomi.
> **10** Moreover Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, I have purchased to be my wife, to raise up the
> name of the dead on his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his
> brothers, and from the gate of his place. You are witnesses today."
> **11** All the people who were in the gate, and the elders, said, "We are witnesses. May Yahweh make the
> woman who has come into your house like Rachel and like Leah, which both built the house of Israel; and
> treat you worthily in Ephrathah, and be famous in Bethlehem.
> **12** Let your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, of the offspring which Yahweh
> will give you by this young woman."

[[Boaz]] goes up to the gate of [[Bethlehem]] and sits down, and the kinsman he spoke of at the threshing
floor passes by. Boaz calls him over without naming him, *friend*, and seats ten of the town's elders
(1–2). He tells [[The Nearer Kinsman (Ruth)]] that [[Naomi]], back from Moab, is selling the parcel of
land that was *our brother* [[Elimelech]]'s, and puts the right to him in front of witnesses: redeem it,
or say so, *for there is no one to redeem it besides you; and I am after you*. The man says *I will
redeem it* (3–4). Then Boaz adds the condition: the day he buys the field from Naomi he acquires it also
from [[Ruth (person)]] the Moabitess, *the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead on his
inheritance* (5). The kinsman withdraws — *I can't redeem it for myself, lest I endanger my own
inheritance* — and hands Boaz the right (6). The narrator explains an old custom: to confirm a
redemption or exchange, a man drew off his shoe and gave it to the other (7). The kinsman says *Buy it
for yourself* and draws off his shoe (8). Boaz calls the elders and all the people to witness that he has
bought everything that was Elimelech's, [[Chilion]]'s, and [[Mahlon]]'s from Naomi, and that he has
acquired Ruth, Mahlon's wife, as his own wife, so that the dead man's name is not cut off from his
brothers and from the gate of his place (9–10). The people at the gate and the elders answer *We are
witnesses*, and bless the marriage: may Yahweh make her *like Rachel and like Leah*, may Boaz do
worthily in Ephrathah and be famous in Bethlehem, and may his house be *like the house of Perez, whom
Tamar bore to Judah* (11–12).

**What is going on.** Naomi's *the man will not rest until he has settled this today* (3:18) is kept the
same morning. The threshing floor was private and at night; the gate is public and in daylight, and the
chapter turns the night's promise into a legal act with witnesses. Boaz's oath at 3:13 was conditional
(*if he will perform for you the part of a kinsman, good*), and he keeps the condition: he gives the
nearer man the first right and lets him accept it. The case turns at verse 5. Offered land alone, the man
will redeem; offered land with a widow whose son would inherit it in the dead man's name, he will not.
What the man would lose is not stated beyond *my own inheritance* (6). The book closes the matter with
three acts that make it binding: the drawn-off shoe (7–8), Boaz's double declaration before witnesses
(9–10), and the town's answer and blessing (11–12). Ruth, whose request set all this going, does not
speak in the scene and is not said to be present.

**What is notable.**

- **Went up to the gate.** *Ûḇōʿaz ʿālâ haššaʿar* (1, WLC). The gate is where a town's elders sit and its
  business is witnessed. Deuteronomy's levirate law uses the same verb and noun for the widow whose
  brother-in-law refuses: *his brother's wife shall go up to the gate to the elders* (Deuteronomy 25:7;
  *wəʿālᵊṯâ … haššaʿrâ*, WLC). Here a kinsman goes up instead of the widow. Abraham bought the field of
  Machpelah *in the presence of the children of Heth, before all who went in at the gate of his city*
  (Genesis 23:18). See [[Bought Before All Who Went In at the Gate - Machpelah and the Field of Elimelech]].
- **Behold, the near kinsman … came by.** *Wəhinnēh hagōʾēl ʿōḇēr* (1). The *behold* gives the timing
  without comment, as *she happened to come* did in the field (2:3). See [[Providence]].
- **Friend.** *Pəlōnî ʾalmōnî* (1, WLC). The WEB's *friend* is one rendering; the phrase means *so-and-so*,
  a way of not naming. In the WLC files read (Genesis–Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel, Jeremiah) it occurs
  elsewhere only at 1 Samuel 21:2 (Hebrew 21:3), where David sends young men *to a certain place* (WEB at
  bible-api.com). Boaz surely knew his name; the narrator withholds it. The man who would not raise up
  the dead man's name is left without one of his own, and Deuteronomy's refuser likewise loses his name to
  a byword (*The house of him who had his shoe removed*, Deuteronomy 25:10). The second point is a reader's
  comparison, not the narrator's.
- **Ten men of the elders.** Ten *zəqēnîm* of the city (2). No law in the books read sets a number for
  such witnesses; the book does not explain the ten.
- **Sit down.** *Yāšaḇ*, *sit*, comes five times in verses 1–2 and once more in verse 4, *those who sit
  here* (WLC). Ruth was told to *wait* (3:18), and the word there is the same verb, *šəḇî*.
- **The parcel of land.** *Ḥelqaṯ haśśāḏeh* (3). This is the first the book says of any land belonging to
  Elimelech or Naomi. *Māḵərâ*, a perfect, is rendered *is selling* in the WEB; *has sold* is also
  possible, and the two give different pictures of what the redeemer buys back. Leviticus 25:25 gives the
  rule: *If your brother becomes poor, and sells some of his possessions, then his kinsman who is next to
  him shall come, and redeem that which his brother has sold*. How a widow came to hold land that the
  inheritance laws of Numbers 27 do not assign to widows the book does not explain (the question is
  reported, not resolved). *Our brother Elimelech*: *ʾāḥînû*, *brother* in the wide sense of kinsman.
  See [[Land and Sojourning]] and [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]].
- **I thought I should tell you.** *ʾEḡleh ʾozněḵā* (4), literally *I will uncover your ear*. *Gālâ* is the
  verb of *uncover his feet* (3:4, 7). In the files read the idiom *uncover the ear* otherwise occurs only
  in 1 Samuel (9:15; 20:2, 12, 13; 22:8, 17; WLC). The night's uncovering is answered by a public one.
- **If he will not redeem.** The Hebrew of 4:4 has *wəʾim-lōʾ yiḡʾal*, *if he will not redeem*, third
  person, between two second-person verbs (WLC); the WEB gives *you*. Some read Boaz as turning for a moment
  to the elders, others as a scribal slip (both reported, not assessed). *That I may know* is likewise a
  written form *wʾdʿ* read as *wəʾēḏəʿâ* (WLC ketiv and qere).
- **And from Ruth the Moabitess.** Verse 5 has a written form *qnyty*, *I acquire*, read as *qānîṯâ*, *you
  acquire* (WLC ketiv and qere). Read one way, Boaz tells the kinsman that buying the field means
  acquiring Ruth with it; read the other, Boaz announces that he himself acquires Ruth, so that any son
  would claim the land the kinsman paid for. *Ûmēʾēṯ rûṯ*, *and from Ruth*, is also read by many as *and
  Ruth* (*wəʾeṯ*). The WEB follows the read form, *you*, and *from Ruth*. The page records the forms; it
  does not settle which the narrator meant.
- **To raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance.** *Ləhāqîm šēm-hammēṯ ʿal-naḥălāṯô* (5, 10). This
  is the levirate law's own phrase: the refusing brother *refuses to raise up to his brother a name in
  Israel* (Deuteronomy 25:7; *ləhāqîm ləʾāḥîw šēm*, WLC). Deuteronomy binds a brother; Boaz and the
  nearer kinsman are not brothers, and Ruth 4 joins the levirate's purpose to the redemption of land, which
  Deuteronomy 25 does not mention. See
  [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]] and [[Names and Naming]].
- **Lest I endanger my own inheritance.** *Pen-ʾašḥîṯ ʾeṯ-naḥălāṯî* (6). *Šāḥaṯ* is *ruin* or *spoil*;
  the WEB's *endanger* is mild. It is the verb of Onan, who *spilled it on the ground, lest he should give
  offspring to his brother* (Genesis 38:9; *wəšiḥēṯ ʾarṣâ*, WLC). In Ruth it occurs only here (WLC). The
  two refusals use one verb: Onan ruins the seed to protect what would be his; the kinsman declines lest
  his own inheritance be ruined. Unlike Onan, he declines openly and is not condemned. The shared verb is
  in the text; reading it as an allusion is a reader's step.
- **Take my right of redemption.** *Gəʾal-ləḵā ʾattâ ʾeṯ-gəʾullāṯî* (6). The root *gāʾal* comes twelve times
  in verses 1–8 (WLC; 4:6's written-and-read pair counted once), more than in the rest of the book
  together. *Gəʾullâ*, *right of redemption*, otherwise occurs in the files read only in Leviticus 25
  (24, 26, 29, 31, 32, 48, 51, 52) and at Jeremiah 32:7–8 (WLC). See [[Redemption]].
- **The custom in former time.** *Ləp̄ānîm bəyiśrāʾēl* (7): the narrator explains a custom his readers no
  longer know, which dates the telling well after the events (the inference is commonly drawn; the text
  states only *in former time*). *Təmûrâ*, *exchanging*, otherwise occurs in the files read only at
  Leviticus 27:10, 33, of a substitute animal. *Təʿûḏâ*, *the way of formalizing* (attestation), occurs in
  the files read only here (WLC).
- **He took off his shoe.** *Šālap̄ ʾîš naʿălô* (7, 8). The shoe connects the scene with Deuteronomy
  25:9–10, where the widow *shall loose his shoe from off his foot, and spit in his face*. The differences
  are large: in Deuteronomy the widow pulls off the shoe (*ḥālaṣ*) as a public shaming; here the man draws
  off his own shoe (*šālap̄*) and gives it, the narrator calls it a custom of *redeeming and exchanging*,
  and there is no spitting and no byword. *Šālap̄* is elsewhere in the files read the verb of drawing a
  sword (Judges 8:20, *the youth didn't draw his sword*; also Numbers 22:23; Joshua 5:13; Judges 3:22;
  1 Samuel 17:51; WLC); in Ruth it occurs only in 4:7–8. Verse 8 does not say to whom the shoe was
  given; verse 7 implies it went to Boaz. See
  [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].
- **Buy it for yourself.** *Qənēh-lāḵ* (8). In the files read the same two words are said only by Hanamel to
  Jeremiah, of a field in Anathoth: *for the right of inheritance is yours, and the redemption is yours.
  Buy it for yourself* (Jeremiah 32:8, WEB at bible-api.com; *qənēh-lāḵ*, and *mišpaṭ haggəʾullâ* at 32:7,
  WLC). See [[Buy It for Yourself - Ruth 4 and Jeremiah 32]].
- **Bought … purchased.** *Qānâ*, *acquire*, occurs in Ruth only in this chapter (4:4, 5, 8, 9, 10; WLC).
  The same verb covers the field and the wife (9, 10). The book does not call Ruth property; the verb is
  the one the transaction uses for both.
- **Chilion's and Mahlon's.** Verse 9 names Chilion first; 1:2 and 1:5 named Mahlon first. Verse 10 is the
  first place the book says which son was Ruth's husband: *Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon*.
- **You are witnesses today.** *ʿĒḏîm ʾattem hayyôm* (9, 10), and the answer *ʿēḏîm*, *We are witnesses*
  (11). In the files read the exchange is otherwise found only at Shechem: *You are witnesses against
  yourselves that you have chosen Yahweh … They said, "We are witnesses"* (Joshua 24:22; WLC). See
  [[Bought Before All Who Went In at the Gate - Machpelah and the Field of Elimelech]].
- **Not be cut off … from the gate of his place.** *Wəlōʾ-yikkārēṯ šēm-hammēṯ* (10). Joshua feared the
  Canaanites would *cut off our name from the earth* (Joshua 7:9), the only other verse in the files read
  where *kāraṯ* and *name* stand together (WLC). The dead man's name is kept *from the gate of his place*,
  the place where it is now being spoken.
- **Like Rachel and like Leah, which both built the house of Israel.** *ʾĂšer bānû šəṯêhem ʾeṯ-bêṯ
  yiśrāʾēl* (11). *Bānâ*, *build*, with *house* is the verb of Deuteronomy 25:9: *the man who does not build
  up his brother's house* (*lōʾ-yiḇneh ʾeṯ-bêṯ ʾāḥîw*, WLC). Rachel, the younger, is named first. See
  [[Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel]], [[Rachel]], and [[Leah]].
- **Treat you worthily in Ephrathah.** *Waʿăśēh-ḥayil* (11), *do valiantly* or *get wealth*: *ḥayil* is
  the word of Boaz (*a mighty man of wealth*, 2:1) and of Ruth (*a worthy woman*, 3:11). *Ephrathah* is
  Bethlehem's clan name; the family were *Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah* (1:2), and Rachel was buried *on
  the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem)* (Genesis 35:19). *Be famous*, *qərāʾ-šēm*, is literally
  *call a name* — the chapter's word again. See
  [[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]].
- **Like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah.** (12). The town's blessing names [[Judah and Tamar]]
  itself, the Bible's other levirate marriage that a man had to be brought to perform. *Of the offspring
  which Yahweh will give you* uses *zeraʿ*, the word of Onan's refusal (*the offspring wouldn't be his*,
  Genesis 38:9). *This young woman*, *hannaʿărâ*; Boaz first asked *Whose young lady is this?* (2:5). See
  [[Perez]], [[Tamar]], and [[Tamar in the Genealogies - Ruth 4, 1 Chronicles 2, and Matthew 1]].

**How it connects.** The scene settles the two questions chapter 3 left open — whether the nearer kinsman
would act, and how Boaz could keep his oath — and it settles them by law in public. It gathers three
strands of the Torah that the earlier chapters had set out separately: the redemption of a poor
kinsman's land (Leviticus 25:25), the levirate duty to raise up a dead man's name (Deuteronomy 25:5–10),
and the gleaning and kindness of chapter 2. It reaches back to Genesis twice in the blessing, to the wives
of Jacob and to Tamar, and both women it names had children by unusual routes. The redemption scene
recalls Abraham's purchase at Hebron's gate and anticipates Jeremiah's at Anathoth. What it does not
contain is the outcome: the birth that will make the name live comes in the next verses (4:13–17).

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The planned boundary 4:1–12 is kept: the scene at the gate, from Boaz sitting down to
the town's blessing. Verse 13 begins the marriage and birth and goes with the next pericope. The nearer
kinsman now gets a page, [[The Nearer Kinsman (Ruth)]]: he speaks and acts in 4:1–8, and he has no name
in the text, so the page is named by his role and the book, following the pattern of pages such as
[[The Levite (husband of the concubine)]]. The elders get no page. Ephrathah gets no separate place page;
the book uses it as another name for Bethlehem's clan and town, and [[Bethlehem]] carries it.

**On 4:4–5.** The written and read forms are from the WLC's ketiv and qere tagging. The page reports the
two readings of 4:5 and their consequences and does not choose between them; the *from Ruth* /
*and Ruth* question is likewise reported, not assessed.

**On the shoe.** Whether the shoe ceremony of 4:7–8 is the same institution as Deuteronomy 25:9–10 in a
milder form, or a separate custom of transfer, is debated; the page records the differences in wording
and does not decide.
