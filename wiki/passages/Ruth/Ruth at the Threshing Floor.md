# Ruth at the Threshing Floor

**Book:** [[Ruth]]
**Reference:** Ruth 3:1–18

## Summary

> **1** Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, shall I not seek rest for you, that it may be
> well with you?
> **2** Now isn't Boaz our kinsman, with whose maidens you were? Behold, he will be winnowing barley tonight
> on the threshing floor.
> **3** Therefore wash yourself, anoint yourself, get dressed, and go down to the threshing floor, but don't
> make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking.
> **4** It shall be, when he lies down, that you shall note the place where he is lying. Then you shall go
> in, uncover his feet, and lay down. Then he will tell you what to do."
> **5** She said to her, "All that you say, I will do."
> **6** She went down to the threshing floor, and did everything that her mother-in-law told her.
> **7** When Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap
> of grain. She came softly, uncovered his feet, and laid down.
> **8** At midnight, the man was startled and turned himself; and behold, a woman lay at his feet.
> **9** He said, "Who are you?" She answered, "I am Ruth your servant. Therefore spread the corner of your
> garment over your servant; for you are a near kinsman."
> **10** He said, "You are blessed by Yahweh, my daughter. You have shown more kindness in the latter end
> than at the beginning, because you didn't follow young men, whether poor or rich.
> **11** Now, my daughter, don't be afraid. I will do to you all that you say; for all the city of my people
> knows that you are a worthy woman.
> **12** Now it is true that I am a near kinsman. However, there is a kinsman nearer than I.
> **13** Stay this night, and in the morning, if he will perform for you the part of a kinsman, good. Let him
> do the kinsman's duty. But if he will not do the duty of a kinsman for you, then I will do the duty of a
> kinsman for you, as Yahweh lives. Lie down until the morning."
> **14** She lay at his feet until the morning, then she rose up before one could discern another. For he
> said, "Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."
> **15** He said, "Bring the mantle that is on you, and hold it." She held it; and he measured six measures
> of barley, and laid it on her; then he went into the city.
> **16** When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, "How did it go, my daughter?" She told her all that
> the man had done for her.
> **17** She said, "He gave me these six measures of barley; for he said, 'Don't go empty to your
> mother-in-law.'"
> **18** Then she said, "Wait, my daughter, until you know what will happen; for the man will not rest until
> he has settled this today."

At the end of the harvest [[Naomi]] tells [[Ruth (person)]] that she means to *seek rest* for her, and
that [[Boaz]], *our kinsman*, is winnowing barley on the threshing floor tonight (1–2). She tells her to
wash, anoint herself, dress, and go down; to keep out of sight until he has eaten and drunk; to mark where
he lies down, uncover his feet, and lie down; *he will tell you what to do* (3–4). Ruth agrees and does it
(5–7). At midnight Boaz wakes with a start and finds a woman at his feet. Asked who she is, Ruth gives her
name and makes her own request: *spread the corner of your garment over your servant; for you are a near
kinsman* (8–9). Boaz blesses her by Yahweh, calls this kindness greater than the first, because she did
not go after young men, and promises to do all she asks, for the whole town knows she is *a worthy woman*
(10–11). But there is a kinsman nearer than he. In the morning that man may redeem her; if not, Boaz
swears by Yahweh's life that he will (12–13). She lies at his feet until before dawn and leaves before
anyone can recognize anyone; he wants it kept quiet that a woman came to the threshing floor (14). He
fills her mantle with six measures of barley and goes to the city (15). At home Naomi asks, *How did it
go, my daughter?*, hears all of it, including Boaz's words *Don't go empty to your mother-in-law*, and
tells her to wait: *the man will not rest until he has settled this today* (16–18).

**What is going on.** Chapter 2 ended with Ruth gleaning to the end of barley and wheat harvest and living
with her mother-in-law (2:23). Chapter 3 is one night at the end of that season, and it has the same shape
as chapter 2: a conversation between Naomi and Ruth (1–5), a scene with Boaz (6–15), and a second
conversation between the women (16–18). In chapter 2 Ruth took the initiative and Naomi learned of Boaz
afterward. Here Naomi makes the plan, and at the decisive moment Ruth does not follow it. Naomi said *he
will tell you what to do* (4); instead Ruth tells him (9). Her request joins two things that Naomi's plan
did not name: marriage, in the image of the spread garment, and redemption, in the word *gōʾēl*. Boaz
accepts both and introduces the obstacle the next chapter has to clear, a nearer kinsman (12). The chapter
closes with the women waiting and the man unable to rest.

**What is notable.**

- **Rest.** Naomi asks, *shall I not seek rest for you* (1). On the road she had prayed that Yahweh would
  grant each widow *rest … in the house of her husband* (1:9), *mənûḥâ* (lemma 4496); here she uses its
  masculine cousin *mānôaḥ* (lemma 4494), and now she acts on the prayer herself. In the WLC files read
  (Genesis–Ruth, 1 Samuel, Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon, Ezekiel), *mānôaḥ* occurs
  only three times: here, at Genesis 8:9 (*the dove found no place to rest her foot*), and at Deuteronomy
  28:65 (*there will be no rest for the sole of your foot*). Both of the other two are rest for a foot;
  Ruth will lie at a man's feet. That echo is the reader's observation, not the narrator's. *That it may be
  well with you*, *yîṭaḇ* (lemma 3190): the verb occurs in Ruth only in this chapter, three times — Naomi's
  hope (1), Boaz's merry heart (7), and Boaz's *you have shown more kindness*, literally *you have made
  your kindness good* (10) (WLC).
- **Our kinsman.** *Mōḏaʿtānû* (2, WLC), from a noun (lemma 4130) that in the files read occurs only here;
  compare the *relative* (*mōḏāʿ*, read form) of 2:1. Naomi said *one of our near kinsmen* at 2:20; now she
  names him as hers, *ours*.
- **The threshing floor.** *Gōren*, the place where grain is beaten and winnowed. It occurs four times in
  this chapter (2, 3, 6, 14); elsewhere in the files read it is Joseph's mourning place, the threshing floor
  of Atad (Genesis 50:10–11), the measure of offerings (Numbers 15:20; 18:27, 30), of the freed
  slave's provision (Deuteronomy 15:14), and of the Feast of Booths (16:13), Gideon's fleece (Judges 6:37), and the floors the Philistines rob
  at Keilah (1 Samuel 23:1). Winnowing happened at night or evening because of the wind (a commonly given
  explanation; not stated in the text). *Go down to the threshing floor* (3, 6) implies the floor lay
  below the town, and Boaz *went into the city* afterward (15).
- **Wash, anoint, dress.** Naomi's three commands (3) prepare Ruth to be seen, and the book does not say
  whether they also end a widow's mourning. [[Judah and Tamar]] is the comparison the book itself invites
  (4:12): a widow who *took off her the garments of her widowhood* and veiled herself to sit at a gate
  (Genesis 38:14). The written Hebrew of verse 3 has *śimlāṯēḵ*,
  *your garment*, singular, read as plural, and *wəyāraḏtî*, a form that looks like *I will go down*, read
  as *you will go down*; verse 4 likewise writes *wəšāḵāḇtî*, *I will lie down*, read as *you will lie
  down* (WLC ketiv and qere). The written forms are often explained as an older second-person feminine
  ending in *-tî*, not as Naomi saying she will go herself (the explanation is reported, not assessed
  here).
- **Lying down.** The verb *šāḵaḇ* occurs eight times in the chapter as read (3:4 three times, 7 twice,
  8, 13, 14), and nowhere else in the book (WLC; the written form of 3:4 counted once). It is the ordinary
  word for sleeping and the ordinary euphemism for sex, and the chapter uses it of both Boaz and Ruth
  without settling which a hearer should hear. Nothing in the narration says that anything but sleep
  happened, and Boaz's *Stay this night … Lie down until the morning* (13) and the morning departure (14)
  are told as care for her name, not as concealment of an act.
- **Uncover his feet.** *Wəḡillîṯ margəlōṯāyw* (4, 7). *Margəlōṯ* (lemma 4772) is *the place of the feet*;
  in the files read it occurs only in this chapter (4, 7, 8, 14). *Gālâ* in the piel, *uncover*, is the
  verb of the incest laws (Leviticus 18:6–19; 20:11–21, WLC); Deuteronomy 22:30 (23:1 in Hebrew) says a man *shall not uncover his father's skirt*, and
  Deuteronomy 27:20 curses one who does so (WEB, *dishonors his father's bed*), and in both the noun is
  *kənap̄*, *skirt* or *corner* (WLC) — the noun Ruth will use in verse 9. The chapter's vocabulary sits on
  the edge of the language for sexual sin and never crosses it. See [[Nakedness and Covering]].
- **His heart was merry.** *Wayyîṭaḇ libbô* (7, WLC), after eating and drinking. In the files read, *yāṭaḇ*
  with *heart* is used at Judges 19:6, the Levite's father-in-law urging *let your heart be merry*, and at
  19:22, *As they were making their hearts merry*, the moment the men of Gibeah surround the house (also
  of the Danites' priest, Judges 18:20, and in Proverbs and Ecclesiastes). The last night in Judges that
  began this way ended in rape and death; this one ends in a blessing and an oath. See
  [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]]. Abigail found Nabal's *heart was merry within him, for he was very
  drunk* (1 Samuel 25:36, WEB at bible-api.com), with a different Hebrew construction (*lēḇ … ṭôḇ*); Ruth
  3 says only that he had eaten and drunk.
- **She came softly.** *Ballāṭ* (7, lemma 3909): *secretly*. In the files read it is used of Saul's
  servants speaking to David *secretly* (1 Samuel 18:22) and of David, who *cut off the skirt of Saul's
  robe secretly* (1 Samuel 24:4, Hebrew 24:5) — *kənap̄*, again — and, in a plural, of the Egyptian
  magicians' secret arts (Exodus 7:22; 8:7, 18). Jael *went softly* to Sisera (Judges 4:21) with a
  different word, *ballāʾṭ* (lemma 3814).
- **A night with a man who had drunk.** The scene repeats the verbs of the night that produced Moab:
  Lot's daughters *made their father drink wine that night: and the firstborn went in, and lay with her
  father. He didn't know when she lay down, nor when she arose* (Genesis 19:33). Ruth *came* (*wattāḇōʾ*)
  and *lay down* (*wattiškāḇ*) (7), *rose up* (*wattāqom*) (14), and Boaz says *Let it not be known* (14) —
  *bôʾ*, *šāḵaḇ*, *qûm*, *yāḏaʿ*, the four verbs of Genesis 19:33 (WLC). The older story's daughters said
  *there is not a man in the earth to come in to us* (19:31); Boaz praises Ruth for not following *young
  men, whether poor or rich* (10). The book never mentions Lot. See
  [[At Night with a Man Who Had Drunk - Genesis 19.30-38 and Ruth 3]] and
  [[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].
- **Startled and turned.** *Wayyeḥĕraḏ … wayyillāp̄ēṯ* (8). *Ḥāraḏ* is the verb of Isaac's trembling when
  he learns he has blessed the wrong son (Genesis 27:33). *Lāp̄aṯ* is rare: in the files read it occurs only
  here, at Judges 16:29, where Samson *took hold of* the two pillars, and at Job 6:18. The WEB's *turned
  himself* is one rendering; *bent forward* or *groped* are others offered by translators (reported, not
  assessed).
- **Who are you?** *Mî ʾāt* (9). Naomi greets Ruth at dawn with the same words, *mî ʾat bittî* (16, WLC),
  which the WEB renders *How did it go, my daughter?*; word for word it is *Who are you, my daughter?* The
  Hebrew lets the question mean *in what state*; it also lets the chapter ask, twice, who Ruth now is.
- **I am Ruth your servant.** She gives her name and calls herself *ʾămāṯəḵā*, *your maidservant*, twice
  (9; WLC). At 2:13 she used *šip̄ḥâ* and said she was *not as one of your servants*. Whether *ʾāmâ* marks a
  woman who can be a wife is debated (reported, not assessed here); the change of word is in the text.
- **Spread the corner of your garment.** *Ûp̄āraśtā kənāp̄əḵā ʿal-ʾămāṯəḵā* (9). *Kānāp̄* is a *wing* and a
  garment's *corner*; at 2:12 Boaz blessed her as one who had come to take refuge *under his wings*, the
  wings of *Yahweh, the God of Israel*. In the files read, the verb *pāraś* with *kānāp̄* is used of the
  cherubim's wings over the mercy seat (Exodus 25:20; 37:9), the eagle's (Deuteronomy 32:11), the hawk's
  (Job 39:26), and of Yahweh to Jerusalem: *I spread my skirt over you, and covered your nakedness. Yes, I
  swore to you, and entered into a covenant with you … and you became mine* (Ezekiel 16:8, WEB at
  bible-api.com; *wāʾep̄rōś kənāp̄î*, WLC). There the act is marriage. Ruth asks Boaz to be the answer to
  his own blessing. See [[Under His Wings - Ruth 2.12, 3.9, and the Refuge of the Psalms]] and
  [[Marriage]].
- **For you are a near kinsman.** *Kî gōʾēl ʾāttâ* (9). Ruth gives the reason Naomi did not: the
  redeemer's duty. Leviticus 25:25 makes the *gōʾēl* the one who buys back a poor relative's land;
  Deuteronomy 25:5–10 makes a dead man's brother marry his widow. Nothing in the Torah joins the two, and
  Boaz is not a brother. Ruth's sentence joins them, and chapter 4 will carry it out: *to raise up the name
  of the dead on his inheritance* (4:5, 10; not yet ingested). The verb *gāʾal* occurs seven times in this
  chapter (9; 12 twice; 13 four times; WLC). See [[Redemption]],
  [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]], and
  [[Levirate Marriage - Genesis 38, Deuteronomy 25, and Ruth]].
- **More kindness in the latter end than at the beginning.** *Ḥasdēḵ*, *your kindness* (10). The book's
  third *ḥeseḏ*: Naomi's prayer that Yahweh show it to the widows (1:8), Naomi's blessing of the one who has
  not abandoned it (2:20), and now Boaz's word for what Ruth has done. The *beginning* is usually taken as
  her loyalty to Naomi, which Boaz praised at 2:11, and the *latter* as this request, which keeps her
  husband's family and name; she could have married whom she liked, *young men, whether poor or rich*. The
  text does not say which act was first; that reading is the usual one, not the only one. Boaz's *young
  men* and *my daughter* (10, 11) imply that he is older than she is; the book gives no age. See [[Love]].
- **Don't be afraid … all that you say.** *Kōl ʾăšer-tōʾmərî ʾeʿĕśeh-lāḵ* (11): Ruth said this to Naomi
  (5); now Boaz says it to Ruth.
- **All the city of my people.** *Kol-šaʿar ʿammî*, literally *all the gate of my people* (11, WLC). The
  gate is where the town's business and judgments are done, and Boaz will go up to it the next morning
  (4:1). See [[Bethlehem]].
- **A worthy woman.** *ʾĒšeṯ ḥayil* (11), the noun the narrator used of Boaz (*gibbôr ḥayil*, 2:1). See
  [[A Worthy Woman - Proverbs 12.4, 31.10, and Ruth 3.11]].
- **A kinsman nearer than I.** *Gōʾēl qārôḇ mimmennî* (12). Verse 12 opens with three particles, *wəʿattâ
  kî ʾomnām kî ʾim*, of which the WLC marks *ʾim* as written but not read. The man is not named here; at
  4:1 Boaz will call him *pəlōnî ʾalmōnî*, which the WEB renders *friend*.
- **Stay this night.** *Lînî* (13), *lodge*, the verb of Ruth's vow, *where you stay, I will stay* (1:16);
  in Ruth it occurs only in those two verses (three times in all; WLC).
- **As Yahweh lives.** *Ḥay-YHWH* (13), an oath; Gideon swears it at Judges 8:19. Ruth's oath at 1:17 was a
  self-curse; this is the book's second oath and the first by a man. See [[Oaths and Vows]].
- **Before one could discern another.** *Bəṭerem yakkîr ʾîš ʾeṯ-rēʿēhû* (14). *Hikkîr* is Ruth's verb at
  2:10 (*that you should take knowledge of me*) and Naomi's at 2:19 (*Blessed be he who noticed you*); in
  Ruth it occurs only in those three verses (WLC). The one who wanted to be recognized now leaves before
  she can be. *Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor*: the town's good opinion of
  her (11) is what Boaz protects.
- **Six measures of barley.** *Šēš śəʿōrîm* (15, 17): the Hebrew names no unit, and the WEB supplies
  *measures*. *Miṭpaḥaṯ*, *mantle* (lemma 4304), occurs only here in the files read. The amount is unknown;
  what she can carry. Boaz's words *Don't go empty to your mother-in-law* (17) are reported only by Ruth;
  the narrator did not give them at 15. *Rêqām* is the word of Naomi's *Yahweh has brought me home again
  empty* (1:21); in Ruth it occurs only in those two verses. See [[Famine and Plenty]] and
  [[You Shall Not Go Empty-Handed - The Plunder of Egypt]].
- **The man will not rest.** *Lōʾ yišqōṭ hāʾîš* (18). *Šāqaṭ* is the verb of the Judges formula *the
  land had rest forty years* (Judges 3:11; also 3:30; 5:31; 8:28; Joshua 11:23; 14:15); in Ruth it occurs
  only here (WLC). The chapter opened with Naomi seeking *rest* for Ruth and closes with a man who will
  have none until it is done. *Until he has settled this*, *killâ*, is the verb of verse 3, *until he has
  finished eating and drinking*: Boaz will finish the matter as he finished his meal. See
  [[The Land Had Rest - Joshua 11.23, 14.15, and the Judges Formula]].
- **How the matter will fall.** *ʾÊḵ yippōl dāḇār* (18, WLC), *how the word falls*. Naomi's last line in the
  chapter makes the outcome something that happens, not something she or Ruth can do further. See
  [[Providence]].

**How it connects.** Every thread of chapters 1–2 is pulled in this chapter. The *rest* Naomi prayed for
on the road (1:9) she now seeks; the *wings* Boaz blessed Ruth by (2:12) she asks him to spread; the
*gōʾēl* Naomi named (2:20) is spoken to his face; *ḥeseḏ* comes round a third time; the *worth* of 2:1 is
Ruth's too (3:11); and *empty* (1:21) is answered with barley. The scene is built on the edge of the
stories it recalls — Lot's daughters and their father, Tamar at the gate of Enaim, the night at Gibeah —
and each time the narration turns the other way: a man who had drunk is awake, blesses, swears, and waits
for the law. The obstacle he names, the nearer kinsman, turns the next chapter into a public hearing at
the gate.

## Notes

**Judgment calls.** The planned boundary 3:1–18 is kept: one night, framed by two conversations between
Naomi and Ruth. The nearer kinsman gets no page yet; he is only mentioned here and acts at 4:1–8. The
threshing floor gets no place page; it is unnamed. A new connection page,
[[At Night with a Man Who Had Drunk - Genesis 19.30-38 and Ruth 3]], records the shared verbs with
Genesis 19; the book of Ruth does not mention Lot, and the page says so.

**On what happened.** The chapter's language — *uncover*, *feet*, *lie down*, *came*, *know* — is the
language the Bible uses elsewhere for sex, and readers have long asked whether the narrator is hinting.
The page records the vocabulary and records that the narration says only that she lay at his feet until
morning (14) and that the marriage follows at the gate (4:10, 13). It does not decide what is implied.

**On *feet*.** *Reḡel*, *foot*, is sometimes a euphemism in Hebrew (reported; not checked here).
*Margəlōṯ* is a different noun, *the place at the feet*, and in the files read it occurs only in this
chapter.

**On 3:3–4, 12, and 14.** The written and read forms are from the WLC's ketiv and qere tagging. The
explanation of the *-tî* forms as an old second-person feminine ending is reported, not assessed.
