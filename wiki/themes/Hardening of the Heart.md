# Hardening of the Heart

## Definition / overview

**The heart made strong, heavy, or hard against God's word — most of all Pharaoh's, in Exodus 4–14,
where God says he will do it before Moses has met the king.** In Hebrew thought the *lēb*, *heart*,
is the seat of will and understanding rather than of feeling, so a hardened heart is a will that will
not yield and a mind that will not see. **The theme is introduced at Exodus 4:21** — *"I will harden
his heart and he will not let the people go"* — and runs through every plague. See
[[Moses Returns to Egypt]].

**Exodus uses three verbs, and the WEB renders all three *harden* or close to it:**

| verb | sense | where (English verse numbers) |
|---|---|---|
| ***ḥāzaq*** | to be or make *strong*, firm | 4:21; 7:13, 22; 8:19; 9:12, 35; 10:20, 27; 11:10; 14:4, 8, 17 |
| ***kābēd*** | to be or make *heavy* | 7:14 (*"stubborn"*); 8:15, 32; 9:7, 34; 10:1 |
| ***qāšâ*** | to be or make *hard* | 7:3 (of the heart); 13:15, of Pharaoh without *heart* |

**Kābēd is the word of Moses' *"heavy"* mouth** (*kəbad-peh*, 4:10) — the deliverer slow of speech
against a king slow of heart — and **it is also the word for *glory***: at 14:4 and 14:17 God says he
will *get honour* (*ʾikkābədâ*, the same root) over Pharaoh. **The heavy heart and the heavy glory are
one word in Hebrew.** And before either, Pharaoh's first order after Moses' request is *tikbad
hāʿăbōdâ*, *"let heavier work be laid on the men"* (5:9) — the same root, used of the service he
imposes. The *strong* of *ḥāzaq* has its own counterpart in Yahweh's *"by a strong hand"* (*bəyāḏ
ḥăzāqâ*, 3:19; 6:1). See [[Bricks Without Straw]]. The verbs were counted from the Westminster Leningrad Codex, whose chapter 8
runs four verses ahead of the English (Hebrew 8:11, 15, 28 = English 8:15, 19, 32).

## Development across Scripture

### Exodus — who hardens whom

**The order of agents is the old question, and the text sets it out in sequence:**

1. **God foretells that he will harden** — 4:21 (*ḥāzaq*), 7:3 (*qāšâ*).
2. **Pharaoh's heart *is* hardened, with no agent named, "as Yahweh had spoken"** — 7:13, 22; 8:19;
   9:7; and 7:14, *"Pharaoh's heart is stubborn"*.
3. **Pharaoh hardens his own heart** — 8:15, *"when Pharaoh saw that there was a respite, he
   hardened his heart"*; 8:32; 9:34, *"he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his
   servants"*.
4. **Yahweh hardens Pharaoh's heart, as narrated fact** — first at 9:12, the sixth plague, *"Yahweh
   hardened the heart of Pharaoh"*; then 10:1, 20, 27; 11:10; 14:8.

**From chapter 10 on, the agent is only Yahweh.** At 10:1 God takes Pharaoh's own verb — *hikbadtî*,
the hiphil of *kābēd* Pharaoh used at 8:15, 32 and 9:34 — and the narrator's two statements in the
chapter are both *wayḥazzēq YHWH* (10:20, 27). In Exodus the hiphil of *kābēd* takes Pharaoh as subject
three times and God once (WLC lemma search).

**Three standard readings, none adopted:**

- **Judicial hardening.** Pharaoh hardens himself first, and God confirms him in it — the narrated
  divine hardening begins only after Pharaoh's own (8:15, 32). The foretelling at 4:21 is
  foreknowledge of the outcome, not its cause.
- **Sovereign hardening.** God's word at 4:21 and 7:3 governs the whole sequence, and the verses in
  which Pharaoh hardens himself describe the same event from the human side. Paul reads it so:
  *"he has mercy on whom he desires, and he hardens whom he desires"* (Romans 9:18).
- **Both at once.** The text makes Pharaoh fully responsible (*"he sinned yet more"*, 9:34) and God
  fully in control, and does not resolve the two — as Joseph's *"you meant evil against me, but God
  meant it for good"* (Genesis 50:20) held a human and a divine intention in one act. See
  [[Providence]].

**The purpose is stated, and it is not Pharaoh's destruction alone.** *"That I may show these my
signs among them"* (10:1); *"the Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"* (14:4). **The hardening
lengthens the contest so that the signs are seen.** See [[Signs and Wonders]].

**The theme turns around in the rest of Scripture.** In Exodus it is said of Egypt's king; in the
Psalms, the prophets, and the New Testament it is said, more often, of Israel. This section will grow
as those passages are ingested.

## Key passages

- [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] — Exodus 4:21; **the first hardening statement**, made before Moses has
  met Pharaoh, together with the announcement of the death of the firstborn
- [[Bricks Without Straw]] — Exodus 5:9; 6:1; **the heavy service and the strong hand.** Pharaoh orders
  *tikbad hāʿăbōdâ*, *let the service be heavy* — the root *kbd* of his own heavy heart (7:14) and of
  God's being *made heavy*, glorified, over him (14:4). And God promises that *"by a strong hand"*,
  *bəyāḏ ḥăzāqâ* — *ḥāzāq*, the root of *ʾăḥazzēq* (4:21) — Pharaoh will let them go. **Neither link is
  drawn by the text; both are lexical, checked in the WLC**
- [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] — Exodus 7:3, 13; **the second forecast, and the first
  fulfilment.** God says *ʾaqšeh*, *I will make hard* — the hiphil of *qāšâ*, the only time in
  Genesis, Exodus, and Job that the verb takes *heart* as its object. The narrator then reports
  *wayyeḥĕzaq lēb parʿōh*, the qal of *ḥāzaq*, *Pharaoh's heart was strong* — **intransitive, with no
  agent** — *"as Yahweh had spoken"*. The fulfilment uses the verb of 4:21, not of 7:3 (WLC)
- [[Water Turned to Blood]] — Exodus 7:14, 22–23; **the first *kābēd*, and the first walk away.**
  God says *kābēd lēb parʿōh*, *Pharaoh's heart is heavy* — an adjective, the word of Moses' *"heavy"*
  mouth (4:10). After the plague the narrator returns to the qal of *ḥāzaq*, no agent named, *"as Yahweh
  had spoken"*, and adds a new idiom: ***"he didn't even take this to heart"***, *lōʾ-šāt libbô
  gam-lāzōʾt* — he did not *set his heart* to it (WLC)
- [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]] — Exodus 8:15, 19, 32; **Pharaoh the subject for the first time.**
  At the respite after the frogs, *wəhakbēd ʾet-libbô*, *he made his heart heavy* — the hiphil of the
  *kābēd* God used at 7:14, now with Pharaoh as its agent, though still *"as Yahweh had spoken"*. After
  the lice, the qal of *ḥāzaq* again, no agent, in the same verse as the magicians' *"This is God's
  finger"*. After the flies, *wayyakbēd parʿōh ʾet-libbô gam bappaʿam hazzōʾt*, *"this time also"* —
  and no *"as Yahweh had spoken"*. **Relief, not pressure, is what precedes each self-hardening** (WLC)
- [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]] — Exodus 9:7, 12, 34–35; **Yahweh named as the hardener for the
  first time.** After the pestilence, the qal of *kābēd*, no agent (9:7). **After the boils, *wayḥazzēq
  YHWH ʾet-lēb parʿōh***, *"Yahweh hardened the heart of Pharaoh"* (9:12): the first narrated divine
  hardening, at the sixth plague. Before the hail God tells Pharaoh, *"for this cause I have made you
  stand"* (9:16), the verse of Romans 9:17. After the hail, ***"he sinned yet more, and hardened his
  heart, he and his servants"*** (9:34): the hiphil of *kābēd* again, the verb *ḥāṭāʾ* of his own
  confession, and **the servants included for the first time**. Then *ḥāzaq* with no agent (9:35)
  (WLC) — [[For This Very Purpose - Exodus 9.16 in Romans 9.17]]
- [[Locusts and Darkness]] — Exodus 10:1, 20, 27; **every hardening in the chapter is Yahweh's.** God says
  ***hikbadtî ʾet-libbô***, *"I have hardened his heart"* — the hiphil of *kābēd* that Pharaoh had
  used of himself (8:15, 32; 9:34), now with God as subject, the only time in Exodus (WLC) — *"and the
  heart of his servants"*. The narrator then says *wayḥazzēq YHWH* twice (10:20, 27), and at 10:27
  adds that Pharaoh *"wouldn't"*, *lōʾ ʾābâ*: unwilling as well as hardened. **The purpose is widened
  to Israel's children**: *"that you may tell in the hearing of your son"* (10:2). **The servants, whose
  hearts God claims in 10:1, are the ones who plead with Pharaoh in 10:7**; the text gives both and
  does not reconcile them — [[Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)]]
- [[The Last Plague Announced]] — Exodus 11:9–10; **the cycle closed in the forecast's words.**
  *"Pharaoh won't listen to you, that my wonders may be multiplied"* (11:9) restates 7:3–4; *"Yahweh
  hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he didn't let the children of Israel go out of his land"* (11:10) —
  *wayəḥazzēq*, the piel, the third narrated hardening in a row with Yahweh as subject (10:20, 27;
  11:10; WLC)
