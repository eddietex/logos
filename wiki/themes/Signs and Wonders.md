# Signs and Wonders

## Definition / overview

**A sign (*ʾôt*) in Scripture is a visible thing that points beyond itself; a wonder (*môpēt*, or
*niplāʾôt*, *wonders*) is a thing that exceeds what is possible.** The two are paired so often —
*"signs and wonders"* — that they become a single formula for the exodus: *"Yahweh showed great and
awesome signs and wonders on Egypt, on Pharaoh, and on all his house, before our eyes"* (Deuteronomy
6:22). **Not every sign is a wonder.** Genesis uses *ʾôt* for the lights in the sky (1:14), the mark on
Cain (4:15), the rainbow (9:12–17), and circumcision (17:11), and none of them is miraculous in the way
the plagues are. **What makes a thing a sign is that God attaches a meaning to it.**

**In Genesis, signs are given to reassure; in Exodus, to persuade.** The rainbow and circumcision are
covenant tokens — they mark a promise for the parties to it. **The signs of Exodus 4 are given because
someone will not believe**: *"if they will not believe you or listen to the voice of the first sign,
… they will believe the voice of the latter sign"* (4:8). **A sign has a voice.** It speaks for the
messenger where his own speech fails, which is why the signs are given to the man who is about to say
he is *"not a man of words"* (4:10).

**And one sign comes afterwards.** *"This will be the token to you, that I have sent you: when you have
brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain"* (Exodus 3:12). **It can only be
checked by obeying.** It is the first sign in Scripture that confirms rather than persuades, and it
sets a pattern the prophets will use: a sign whose meaning lies in a fulfillment not yet seen.

**Signs do not compel belief.** The three signs of Exodus 4 work on the elders (*"The people believed"*,
4:31); the same signs, and ten wonders after them, do not work on Pharaoh, and some are matched by his
magicians (7:11–12, 22). **The New Testament keeps both truths**: John's Gospel is built on *signs*
written *"that you may believe"* (John 20:31), and Jesus refuses a sign to a generation that asks for
one.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 1:14; 4:15; 9:12–17; 17:11** — signs as marks and tokens, none of them displays of power.
  See [[The Sign and the Seal - Genesis 17 in Romans 4]].
- **Exodus 3:12** — the token in arrears: *"you shall serve God on this mountain"*.
- **Exodus 3:20** — *"all my wonders"*, *niplāʾōtay*: the plagues named in advance.
- **Exodus 4:1–9, 17** — three signs, each with a *voice*, and the rod *"with which you shall do the
  signs"*.
- **Exodus 7:3, 9–12** — *"multiply my signs and my wonders"*; the wonder Pharaoh asks for, matched
  by his magicians and swallowed. See [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]].
- **Exodus 7:14–8:32** — the first four plagues; the magicians match the blood and the frogs and fail
  at the lice, *"This is God's finger"*; the flies are the first sign that distinguishes Israel from
  Egypt. See [[Water Turned to Blood]] and [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]].
- *(ahead)* **Exodus 9–12** — the remaining plagues.
- *(ahead)* **Deuteronomy 4:34; 6:22** — *"by signs, by wonders, by war, by a mighty hand"*: the exodus
  remembered as the definitive sign.
- *(ahead)* **Deuteronomy 13:1–3** — a sign that comes true from a prophet who leads to other gods is
  not to be believed.
- *(ahead)* **Acts 2:22** — Jesus *"approved by God to you by mighty works and wonders and signs"*.
- *(ahead)* **John 20:30–31** — the Gospel's signs *"written, that you may believe"*.

## Key passages

- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:12, 20; 4:1–9, 17; **the vocabulary of the exodus introduced in one
  conversation.** One *token* that can only be checked afterwards; *"my wonders"* for the plagues; and
  three signs for the elders — **a rod that becomes a *nāḥāš***, the serpent of Eden's word, **a hand
  made leprous and restored, and river water turned to blood**, the first plague in a cup. **The signs
  are given because of an objection** (*"they will not believe me"*), and **God says they have a voice**
  (*qōl hāʾōt*, 4:8). The rod Moses already carries is made the instrument: *"with which you shall do
  the signs"* (4:17)
- [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] — Exodus 4:20–21, 30; ***môpətîm*, a third word**, for what Moses is to do before
  Pharaoh — and the staff renamed *"God's rod"*. The wonders are to be done to a king whose heart God
  will harden. Before the people, **Aaron** does the signs given into Moses' hand, and they believe
- [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] — Exodus 7:3, 9–12; **the signs multiplied, and the one wonder
  asked for.** *"I will … multiply my signs and my wonders"* — *hirbêtî*, the verb of Israel's
  multiplying in chapter 1 — bracketed by 11:9, *"that my wonders may be multiplied"*. Pharaoh asks
  *"Perform a miracle!"*, the only sign in the contest he requests; the magicians match it, and
  Aaron's rod swallows theirs. **The first sign before Pharaoh harms no one: credentials, not
  judgment**
- [[Water Turned to Blood]] — Exodus 7:14–25; **the first plague, and the third sign of the bush
  enlarged.** At 4:9 river water poured on dry ground was to become blood for the elders; here the
  whole river and every water in Egypt become blood before Pharaoh. *"In this you shall know that I am
  Yahweh"* (7:17) gives the sign its stated meaning; the magicians match it, and the matching only adds
  blood — [[Waters Turned to Blood - The First Plague in the Psalms and Revelation]]
- [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]] — Exodus 8:1–32; **the sign that cannot be copied.** The
  magicians match the frogs, and then fail at the lice and call it *"God's finger"* (8:18–19). **At the
  flies the sign becomes a distinction**: *"I will put a division between my people and your people: by
  tomorrow shall this sign be"* (8:23) — the first plague that falls on Egypt and not on Goshen, and the
  first done with no rod at all — [[The Finger of God - Exodus 8.19, Exodus 31.18, and Luke 11.20]]
- [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]] — Exodus 9:1–35; **the plagues reach life and body.** The pestilence
  needs no rod — *"Yahweh's hand"* (9:3) — and keeps the distinction to the last animal; the boils
  remove the magicians from the story (*"couldn't stand before Moses"*, 9:11); the hail is the first
  sign announced with a way of escape (9:19), and its purpose is the widest yet: *"that my name may be
  declared throughout all the earth"* (9:16)
