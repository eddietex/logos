# Folly

## Definition / overview

Folly in the Old Testament is wisdom's opposite, and it is a moral condition before it is an
intellectual one. The fool is not someone who lacks information. Proverbs introduces him in its
first antithesis as someone who has been offered wisdom and refuses it: *"The fear of Yahweh is the
beginning of knowledge; but the foolish despise wisdom and instruction"* (Proverbs 1:7). The
operative verb is *despise*. Folly is a stance toward instruction, and so — because instruction
begins in the fear of Yahweh — a stance toward God.

Hebrew has several words for the fool, and Proverbs uses them as a graded vocabulary rather than as
synonyms. The glosses below are standard lexical descriptions, offered as orientation rather than as
fixed definitions; the words overlap in use.

- **The simple** — *petî*, plural *pətāʾyim* (Proverbs 1:4, 22). The untaught and impressionable:
  not yet a fool, open to anything, and therefore the person the book is most anxious to reach.
  The preface promises *"prudence to the simple"* (1:4).
- **The fool** — *ʾĕwîl* (1:7) and *kəsîl* (1:22; 10:1). The man who has heard and does not
  listen; *"The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but he who is wise listens to counsel"*
  (12:15). *"A fool has no delight in understanding, but only in revealing his own opinion"* (18:2).
- **The mocker** — *lēṣ* (1:22). Folly hardened into contempt, who *"delight[s] … in mockery"*.
- **The *nābāl*** — the word for the fool of Psalm 14:1, *"The fool has said in his heart, 'There
  is no God'"*, and of a moral and religious senselessness that shades into outrage.

**The sharpest sign of folly in Proverbs is self-assurance.** The fool is right in his own eyes
(12:15), *"hot headed and reckless"* (14:16), and repeats his folly *"as a dog that returns to his
vomit"* (26:11). The book's dry joke on the subject is *"Even a fool, when he keeps silent, is counted
wise"* (17:28) — which the wiki's Job pages have already had occasion to quote against men who did
not keep silent.

## Development across Scripture

- **Proverbs 1:1–7** — [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]]: the book's first antithesis, in its motto.
  What verse 2 offers — *ḥokmâ ûmûsār*, wisdom and instruction — is what verse 7's fools despise. The
  contrast between wise and foolish is set up as a choice before a single saying has been read.
- **Proverbs 1:8–19** — [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]]: the first lecture is addressed to the simple at their weakest
  point. *"If sinners entice you"* — *yəpattûkā*, from *p-t-h*, the root that (on the standard lexical
  account) also gives *pətāʾyim*, *the simple*: the simple are the enticeable, and the first danger the
  book names is not stupidity but suggestibility (Hebrew checked against Sefaria's *Miqra according to
  the Masorah*).
- **Proverbs 1:22, 32** — [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]]: Wisdom crying out to the simple, the
  mockers, and the fools, each paired with its own vice — *pətāyim* who love *peṯî*, *lēṣîm* who
  delight in *lāṣôn*, *kəsîlîm* who hate knowledge — and their end: *"the backsliding of the simple
  will kill them. The careless ease of fools will destroy them."* *Məšûḇâ*, *backsliding*, is from
  *šûḇ*, the *"Turn"* of 1:23: folly here is a turning, the wrong way (Hebrew checked against
  Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*).
- **Proverbs 9:13–18** — [[The Two Feasts]]; Folly personified as a woman, *"loud, undisciplined, and
  knows nothing"* (9:13; *ʾēšeṯ kəsîlûṯ*, *a woman of folly*, and *pəṯayyûṯ*, *simpleness*, for
  *undisciplined*), whose feast competes with Wisdom's. She sits at her door in the same high places,
  calls the simple with Wisdom's own words (9:16 repeats 9:4), and offers *"Stolen water"* and *"Food
  eaten in secret"*; her guests are *"in the depths of Sheol"*. She takes the strange woman's features
  — *loud* (7:11), at her door (5:8), bound for Sheol — and the wiki leaves open whether she is that
  woman or a wider figure including her. The mocker (*lēṣ*) who answers reproof with hatred (9:7–8) is
  folly's other face.
- **Proverbs 26:4–5** *(not yet ingested)* — *"Don't answer a fool according to his folly"* beside
  *"Answer a fool according to his folly"*: the book's own admission that dealing with folly takes
  judgment, not a rule.
- **Job 2:10** — [[The Second Test]]: *"You speak as one of the foolish women would speak"*
  (*hannəbālôt*). Job does not call his wife a fool; he says she has spoken like one.
- **Job 5:2–3** — [[Eliphaz's First Speech]]: *"resentment kills the foolish man, and jealousy kills
  the simple … I have seen the foolish taking root"*. Eliphaz uses Proverbs' own vocabulary —
  *ʾĕwîl* and *pōteh* — and applies the portrait of the fool to Job.
- **Job 42:8** — [[The Restoration of Job]]: God tells the friends he will not *"deal with you
  according to your folly"* (*nəbālâ*). **The men who applied the fool's portrait to Job are the ones
  whose speech God calls folly.**
- **Psalm 14:1; 53:1** — *"The fool has said in his heart, 'There is no God.'"* Folly as practical
  atheism.
- **Matthew 7:26** — the *"foolish man, who built his house on the sand"*: the one who hears and does
  not do, which is Proverbs 1:7's fool exactly.
- **Luke 12:20** — *"You foolish one, tonight your soul is required of you"*: the rich man whose
  planning left God out.
- **1 Corinthians 1:18–25** — the cross as *"foolishness"* to the world, and *"the foolishness of God
  is wiser than men"* (1:25): the category turned inside out.

Scriptural quotations outside Genesis, Exodus, Job, and Proverbs above are WEB, checked against
bible-api.com (2026-10-25); the Hebrew words for *Proverbs 1:7, 1:22, Job 2:10, 5:2–3, 42:8*, and
*Psalm 14:1* were checked against Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

### The promotion that is shame — Proverbs 3:21–35

The final contrast of Proverbs 3:35 sets the glory inherited by the wise against the
shame that becomes the promotion of fools in the WEB. Verse 34 has already turned the
mockers' contempt back upon them. Folly is evaluated by its outcome before God, not by
the apparent advantage that makes the violent man enviable in verse 31.

See [[Do Not Withhold Good]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 3; comparisons as cited above.
The discussion added here makes no claim about exact source-language forms.

### The fool's late confession — Proverbs 5:11–14, 23

Proverbs 5 puts the fool's confession in his own mouth, spoken at the end: *"How I have hated
instruction, and my heart despised reproof; neither have I obeyed the voice of my teachers"*
(5:12–13). It is 1:7's fool, who despises instruction, and the refusers of 1:29–30, now
admitting it too late, *"at the brink of utter ruin, among the gathered assembly"* (5:14). He dies
*"for lack of instruction"* and goes astray *"in the greatness of his folly"* (5:23, *ʾiwwaltô*).
See [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 5; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

### Void of understanding — Proverbs 6:32

*"He who commits adultery with a woman is void of understanding"* (6:32): *ḥăsar-lēḇ*, *lacking
heart* — the phrase Proverbs 7:7 will give the young man who walks toward the strange woman's house.
The folly is a failure to reckon: he *"destroys his own soul"*, and the reproach *"will not be wiped
away"* (6:32–33). See [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 6; Hebrew read
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

The young man of Proverbs 7 is *ḥăsar-lēḇ*, *"void of understanding"* (7:7), the description 6:32
gave the adulterer and 9:16 will give Folly's guest. He is not called wicked: he is one of the
*simple* (*pəṯāʾyim*, 7:7), and his folly is that he walks the way to her house at nightfall,
follows her *"immediately"* (7:22), and *"doesn't know that it will cost his life"* (7:23) — the
words 9:18 repeats of the guest of Folly. 7:22b, *"as a fool stepping into a noose"*, is textually
difficult in the Hebrew; see the passage page. See [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 7; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Fools invited — Proverbs 8:5

*"You simple, understand prudence. You fools, be of an understanding heart"* (8:5). In 1:22 Wisdom
rebuked the simple, mockers, and fools; in 8:5 she invites them, offering the simple the *ʿormâ*,
*prudence*, the preface promised them (1:4). The fool, *kəsîl*, is not written off: he is told to
acquire *lēḇ*, a heart, the organ the young man of 7:7 lacked. See [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father — Proverbs 10:1, 8, 10, 13–14, 18, 21, 23

The foolish son grieves his mother; chatter, slander, refusal of understanding, and pleasure in evil
give folly a social cost. The repeated falling fool (8, 10) is contrasted with the person willing to
learn.

See [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]]. Source: Proverbs 10 and the cited Proverbs/Job comparisons
checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### A False Balance — Proverbs 11:12, 22, 29

Despising a neighbor shows lack of understanding. The gold ring in a pig’s snout exposes the
incongruity of beauty without discretion, and the household troubler inherits wind. The fool
becoming servant to the wise is a proverbial reversal, not an explanation of every enslaved person’s
circumstances.

See [[A False Balance]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 11; comparisons are documented on the passage
and connection pages.

### Correction, speech, and daily conduct — Proverbs 12:1, 11, 15–16, 23

Hating reproof, chasing fantasies, trusting one’s own verdict, and broadcasting annoyance or
foolishness give folly recognizable conduct. The opening insult targets refusal to learn, not
intellectual disability. The wise person’s willingness to listen supplies the counterexample.

See [[Whoever Loves Correction]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 12 and the comparisons documented on
that passage page.

### The fool's desire — Proverbs 13:16, 19–20

The fool *"exposes folly"* (16) and harms his companions (20). Verse 19 gives folly a motive: the fool also longs, but *"fools detest turning from evil"* — what he cannot give up is the evil itself.

See [[Hope Deferred]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 13.

### The house torn down and the credulous simple — Proverbs 14:1, 3, 7–9, 15–18, 24, 29

The foolish woman tears down her house *"with her own hands"* (1); the fool's talk earns the rod (3); folly is deceit (8), credulity (15), recklessness (16), and a quick temper (17, 29). The simple *"inherit folly"* (18), and folly is its own crown (24).

See [[A Way That Seems Right]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 14.

### The gushing mouth — Proverbs 15:2, 5, 7, 14, 20–21

The fool's mouth *gushes out* folly (2) and *feeds on* it (14); he despises his father's correction (5) and his mother (20); and folly *"is joy to one who is void of wisdom"* (21). Folly here is an appetite as well as an error.

See [[A Gentle Answer]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 15.

### Folly as its own punishment — Proverbs 16:22

*"The punishment of fools is their folly"* (22): the consequence is not added from outside but is the condition itself.

See [[The Plans of the Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 16.

### Worse than a bereaved bear — Proverbs 17:10, 12, 16, 21, 24–25, 28

A hundred lashes reach a fool less than a rebuke reaches the wise (10); better *"a bear robbed of her cubs"* than *"a fool in his folly"* (12); his money cannot buy wisdom (16); his eyes *"wander to the ends of the earth"* (24); his father has no joy and his mother bitterness (21, 25). Only silence makes him seem wise (28).

See [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 17.

### Airing his own opinion — Proverbs 18:2, 6–7, 13

*"A fool has no delight in understanding, but only in revealing his own opinion"* (2). His lips bring
strife and his mouth invites beatings (6); his mouth is *"his destruction"* (7); and answering before
hearing is *"folly and shame to him"* (13). See
[[He Who Answers Before He Hears - Proverbs 18.13, 17 and Elihu's Waiting]].

See [[A Strong Tower]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 18.
