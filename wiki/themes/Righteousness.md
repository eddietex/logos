# Righteousness

## Definition / overview

Being in the right — and in the Hebrew Bible that is a relational and forensic condition before it
is a moral one. To be *ṣaddîq* is to be the party who would be vindicated if the case were tried,
whether the case is a lawsuit, a covenant, or a life.

The root is **ṣ-d-q**, giving the adjective *ṣaddîq* (righteous), the noun *ṣedeq* / *ṣədāqâ*
(righteousness), and the verb *ṣādaq* (to be in the right; in the causative, to declare in the
right). Its natural home is the courtroom, which is why *ṣādaq* and *rāšaʿ* (to be wicked, to be
condemned) sit opposite each other as verdicts rather than as character sketches, and why the
Septuagint's choice of *dikaios* / *dikaiosynē* — the same forensic register in Greek — hands Paul
the vocabulary he builds justification out of.

**Its first occurrence in the Bible is Genesis 6:9**, in [[Noah and the Flood]]: *"Noah was a
righteous man, blameless among the people of his time. Noah walked with God."* God repeats it in
his own voice at 7:1 — *"I have seen your righteousness before me in this generation"* — and these
two verses set a pattern the theme never really leaves.

- **It is declared, not demonstrated.** The narrative gives Noah no righteous act to point at before
  calling him righteous. What follows the declaration is obedience (6:22, 7:5), not what precedes
  it.
- **It is comparative in its first instance.** *"Among the people of his time"*, *"in this
  generation"* — the standing is stated against a specific and very low benchmark, and whether that
  qualifies the praise is an old argument (see [[Noah and the Flood]]).
- **It comes second.** Genesis 6:8 said Noah *found favor*; 6:9 says he was *righteous*. Which
  produces which is the question the wiki has been carrying since
  [[The Sons of God and the Corruption of the Earth]], and Genesis puts them in that order without a
  connective. See [[Favor and Grace]].
- **It does not transfer.** Noah's righteousness saves his household because they are in his boat,
  and Ezekiel 14:14, 20 turns that into a principle by naming Noah first among three men who *"would
  deliver only their own souls by their righteousness"*.

The second great occurrence is Genesis 15:6 — *"He believed in Yahweh, who credited it to him for
righteousness"* — where the word attaches to trust rather than to conduct, and the verb is an
accounting term. Between 6:9 and 15:6 the Bible has given both of the models it will keep arguing
about.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 6:9; 7:1** — *ṣaddîq*'s first appearance, of Noah, declared before any act and repeated
  by God. See [[Noah and the Flood]].
- **Genesis 15:6** *(not yet ingested)* — Abram's faith *credited* to him as righteousness: the verse
  Romans 4 and Galatians 3 both argue the gospel from.
- **Genesis 18:23–25** *(not yet ingested)* — Abraham's *"Will you consume the righteous with the
  wicked? … Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?"* The category becomes the ground of an
  argument with God, which is exactly what Noah never makes.
- **Genesis 38:26** *(not yet ingested)* — Judah of Tamar: *"She is more righteous than I"*, the word
  used comparatively of a woman who has just deceived him, and a good demonstration that it means
  *in the right in this matter* rather than *virtuous*.
- **Deuteronomy 6:25; 24:13** — righteousness as covenant obedience; and **Deuteronomy 9:4–6**,
  Israel told bluntly that the land is *not* given for its righteousness.
- **Psalm 1; Psalm 15; Psalm 37** — the righteous as a standing class, contrasted with the wicked,
  and **Psalm 143:2** — *"in your sight no man living is righteous"*, which is the same tradition
  undercutting itself.
- **Isaiah 53:11** — *"my righteous servant will justify many; and he will bear their iniquities"*:
  righteousness that transfers, against Ezekiel's insistence that it does not.
- **Isaiah 64:6** — *"all our righteousness is like a polluted garment"*.
- **Habakkuk 2:4** — *"the righteous will live by his faith"*, three words that Romans 1:17,
  Galatians 3:11, and Hebrews 10:38 all build on.
- **Ezekiel 14:14, 20; 18:1–32** — righteousness as strictly personal and non-transferable, with
  Noah named as the first example.
- **Matthew 5:20; 6:33** — a righteousness that must *exceed* that of the scribes and Pharisees, and
  a kingdom-righteousness to be sought first.
- **Romans 1:17; 3:21–26; 4:1–25** — *the righteousness of God* revealed apart from law, and Genesis
  15:6 quoted as the proof. Whether *dikaiosynē theou* means God's own attribute, a status he
  confers, or his covenant faithfulness is the central exegetical dispute of Pauline studies.
- **2 Corinthians 5:21; Philippians 3:9** — righteousness as something received rather than
  achieved, and the Reformation's central texts.
- **James 2:21–24** — Genesis 15:6 quoted for the opposite conclusion: *"a man is justified by
  works, and not only by faith"*. The New Testament argues with itself over the verse, using the
  same verse.

## Key passages

- [[Noah and the Flood]] — *ṣaddîq*'s first occurrence in Scripture, declared of a man before he is
  shown doing anything, and restated by God at 7:1
- [[Job of Uz]] — Job 1:1's *tām wə-yāšār*, blameless and upright, declared of a man before he is
  shown doing anything; the same narrative move as Genesis 6:9, and the premise the entire book
  runs on
- [[Blameless Before Anyone Is Watching]] — Genesis 6:9 and Job 1:1 side by side, and what the
  verdict buys in each book
- [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]] — Job 15:14, *"What is he who is born of a woman, that he should be
  righteous?"*, asked about a man God has already twice called blameless (Job 1:8, 2:3)
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — Job 16:17, *"there is no violence in my hands,
  and my prayer is pure"*: the claim the friends hear as blasphemy and the narrator has certified in
  advance
