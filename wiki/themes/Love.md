# Love

## Definition / overview

The Hebrew is ***ʾāhab***, and it is the Bible's ordinary word for the whole range English calls love:
a father for a son, a man for a woman, a woman for her son, a man for grilled venison, and eventually
Israel for God and God for Israel. **It is not a specialised religious term** and the Bible never
defines it; what it does instead is state, flatly and in the narrator's own voice, **who loves whom
more than whom** — and then let the reader watch the households come apart.

**Its first occurrence in Scripture is Genesis 22:2**, and the wiki notes what the placement means:
the Bible's first *love* is in the sentence that asks for the loved one back. *"Take now your son,
your only son, **whom you love**, even Isaac"* — a word that becomes sayable only after the other son
has been sent away in the previous chapter. **The word enters the book at the moment it costs
something**, and it does not stop costing.

**The theme's other pole is *śānēʾ*, to hate**, which in Hebrew idiom is often not an emotion but a
rank: the un-preferred wife at Genesis 29:31 and Deuteronomy 21:15 is *hated*, and what the law means
by it is *loved less*. **Genesis's love is comparative before it is anything else.**

## Development across Scripture

**Genesis loves in a straight line, and every instance ranks somebody.**

| verse | who loves whom | what it produces |
|---|---|---|
| **22:2** | Abraham loves Isaac | the demand for him back — [[The Binding of Isaac]] |
| **24:67** | Isaac loves Rebekah, **after** she becomes his wife | comfort after his mother's death |
| **25:28** | Isaac loves Esau *because he ate his venison*; Rebekah loves Jacob, **no reason given** | the divided house that produces chapter 27 |
| **27:14** | savoury food *such as his father loved* | the disguise that works |
| **29:18, 20** | Jacob loves Rachel, **before** marrying her | seven years, then seven more |
| **29:30** | *"he loved also Rachel more than Leah"* | *"Yahweh saw that Leah was hated"* (29:31) |
| **34:3** | Shechem loves Dinah, **after** taking her | the massacre at Shechem |
| **37:3–4** | *"Israel loved Joseph more than all his children"* | *"they hated him"*, and the pit |

**Two observations the table makes and the wiki holds to.**

**First, the order matters.** Genesis says of Isaac that he took Rebekah, *"and she became his wife.
He loved her"* — **in that order** (24:67). Of Jacob it says *"Jacob loved Rachel"* first (29:18), and
the marriage is seven years away. **29:18 is therefore the first love in the Bible that precedes a
marriage rather than following one**, and Genesis measures it: the seven years *"seemed to him but a
few days, for the love he had for her."* See [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]].

**Second, every stated preference in the book detonates.** Isaac's for Esau and Rebekah's for Jacob
produce [[The Stolen Blessing]]; Jacob's for Rachel over Leah produces the rivalry of Genesis 29–30
and, through it, the twelve tribes; Jacob's for Joseph over his brothers produces the pit and the
descent into Egypt. **The narrator never once calls any of these preferences wrong.** He states them
and reports what followed, which is the register the whole book uses on this family. See
[[Envy]] and [[Loved and Hated - Genesis 29 and Deuteronomy 21.15-17]].

**Then the law legislates the wreckage.** Deuteronomy 21:15–17 takes exactly Jacob's household — *"if
a man has two wives, the one beloved, and the other hated"* — and forbids preferring the loved wife's
son over the hated wife's firstborn. **The Torah's rule is written against the patriarchs' practice**,
which is what [[Birthright and the Firstborn]] tracks across the whole of Genesis.

**And then the word changes register entirely.** Deuteronomy makes *ʾāhab* the central verb of the
covenant, in both directions: *"Yahweh didn't set his love on you, nor choose you, because you were
more in number … but because Yahweh loves you"* (7:7–8), and *"You shall love Yahweh your God with all
your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your might"* (6:5) — **the sentence Jesus calls the
first and greatest commandment** (Matthew 22:37). Leviticus 19:18 puts the neighbour beside it, and
Leviticus 19:34 extends it to the resident alien. **A verb used in Genesis only of preference becomes,
in Deuteronomy, the thing commanded.**

The prophets then take the marital sense of the word and use it on the covenant. Hosea, told to
marry a wife who leaves him, is the book-length version — *"I will betroth you to me forever"* (2:19)
— and Jeremiah 31:3 gives it its most quoted form: *"I have loved you with an everlasting love."*
Malachi 1:2–3 states the whole theme in the vocabulary of Genesis 29 and applies it to the two
brothers of Genesis 25: *"I loved Jacob; but Esau I hated"*, which Romans 9:13 then argues from. See
[[The Elder Will Serve the Younger - Genesis 25.23 in Malachi and Romans]].

**The Song of Songs is the outlier and the wiki notes it as one**: eight chapters of *ʾāhab* with no
covenant, no command, and no ranking, ending in the Old Testament's one attempt at a definition —
*"love is strong as death … Many waters can't quench love, neither can floods drown it"* (8:6–7).

**The New Testament's word is *agapē***, and its innovations are two: it makes love the summary of the
law (Matthew 22:37–40; Romans 13:10), and it makes it a description of God rather than only of an act
toward him — *"God is love"* (1 John 4:8, 16), with 1 John 4:19 giving the order the Old Testament
implied and never stated: *"We love him, because he first loved us."* 1 Corinthians 13 then does for
*agapē* what Song of Songs 8 did for *ʾāhab*, and John 15:13 fixes its measure at the point Genesis
22 had raised and not answered.

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:6

The command against rival worship sets love of God alongside keeping his commandments (20:6), in
contrast with hatred in 20:5. Love here is expressed in allegiance and obedience, not merely
preference among family members. Romans 13:8–10 later gathers several of these neighbor-directed
commands under love; see [[Coveting and Love - The Commandments in Romans 7 and 13]]. See
[[The Ten Commandments]].

### "I love my master" — Exodus 21:5

In the ordinances, the first quoted declaration of love is a servant's: *"I love my master, my wife,
and my children. I will not go out free"* (21:5; *ʾāhabtî*, WLC). **The law takes the statement at
its word**, and also leaves its pressure visible, since leaving would have meant leaving a wife and
children who stay with the master (21:4). Deuteronomy 15:16 gives the same motive: *"because he
loves you and your house"* (WEB). See [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

### Help across enmity — Exodus 23:1–19

Exodus 23:4–5 requires returning an enemy's animal and helping the donkey of someone who hates the
hearer. The WEB here commands assistance rather than using Matthew 5:44's explicit language of
enemy-love. The later comparison concerns an ethical continuity, not identical source-language
wording or a claim that Matthew directly quotes this law. See
[[Help for an Enemy - Exodus 23, Proverbs 25, and Matthew 5]].

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

Exodus 34:6–7 describes God as abundant in loving kindness and keeping it for thousands, with
forgiveness and judgment alongside. The WEB does not add generations after thousands. This
extends the theme beyond household affection into God's declared character toward a guilty
people; it makes no claim that the Hebrew term is identical to the ordinary verb for love
discussed above. See [[The Covenant Renewed]].

### Whom Yahweh loves, he reproves — Proverbs 3:12

*"For whom Yahweh loves (yeʾĕhaḇ), he reproves; even as a father reproves the son in whom he
delights"* (3:12). Love is given as the reason for correction, and the comparison is a father's
delight in a son. This is the first time Proverbs says Yahweh loves anyone. Hebrews 12:6 and
Revelation 3:19 (*"As many as I love, I reprove and chasten"*) carry the sentence into the New
Testament. See [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]]. Source: Proverbs 3 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Love wisdom and embrace her — Proverbs 4:1–9

The command to love wisdom (4:6) and embrace her (4:8) extends the theme beyond attachment
to another human being. Personification makes learning an affection and allegiance:
wisdom is cherished, not merely used, and her response is protection and honor. The
passage is not a report of marriage to an identifiable woman. The contrast is thematic;
no claim about an exact Hebrew verb is needed for this reading of the WEB.

See [[Get Wisdom]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### Be captivated with her love — Proverbs 5:19–20

*"Be captivated always with her love"* (5:19, *bəʾahăḇāṯāh tišgeh*). The verb *šāḡâ*, rendered
*"be captivated"*, is used again in 5:20 of the adulteress and in 5:23 of the fool who *"will go
astray"*; its sense of reeling or erring is the conventional lexical one, not checked against a
lexicon this pass. Married love is the right object of that intoxication, and the same staggering
directed elsewhere is ruin. See [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 5; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

In Proverbs 7:18 the strange woman says *"Come, let's take our fill of loving until the morning.
Let's solace ourselves with loving"* (*nirweh ḏōḏîm … bāʾŏhāḇîm*). *Nirweh*, *drink our fill*, is
the verb of 5:19, where the wife was to satisfy the son (*yərawwuḵā*), and *ḏōḏîm* is the Song of
Solomon's word for love (4:10). The father has given the son this language for his own wife; the
woman offers it for someone else's. See [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]] and
[[Myrrh, Aloes, and Cinnamon - Proverbs 7.17, Song of Solomon 4.14, and Psalm 45.8]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 7; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.
Song of Solomon 4:10 verified WEB at bible-api.com, not stored.

### I love those who love me — Proverbs 8:17, 21

*"I love those who love me"* (8:17, *ʾănî ʾōhăḇay ʾēhāḇ*); *"that I may give wealth to those who love
me"* (8:21). Wisdom speaks the love the father commanded at 4:6 (*"Love her, and she will keep
you"*), now as hers to return. The Masoretic text writes the first word of 8:17 in a form usually
read as *those who love her* and reads it *those who love me*; the WEB follows the reading. The
strange woman offered *ʾŏhāḇîm*, *loving*, until morning (7:18); Wisdom offers a love that answers
love. See [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

**Proverbs 8:36 — the love of death.** Wisdom began her promises with *"I love those who love me"*
(8:17) and ends with the opposite love: *"All those who hate me love death"* (*ʾāhăḇû māweṯ*). Love is
not neutral in Proverbs; what one loves is a direction. See [[Wisdom at the Creation]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father — Proverbs 10:12

Love covers wrongs where hatred keeps conflict active. The contrast concerns how people respond to
offenses, not denial that an offense occurred. Verse 18 condemns concealed hatred, and Proverbs 17:9
connects covering an offense with friendship. See
[[Love Covers Wrongs - Proverbs 10.12, 1 Peter 4.8, and James 5.20]].

See [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]]. Source: Proverbs 10 and the cited Proverbs/Job comparisons
checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### Love that disciplines — Proverbs 13:24

*"One who spares the rod hates his son, but one who loves him is careful to discipline him"* (24) defines a father's love partly by correction, following 3:11–12's picture of Yahweh reproving the son he loves. The text does not set limits on the discipline; see [[The Rod of Discipline - Proverbs 13.24 and the Father's Correction]].

See [[Hope Deferred]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 13.

### Love and faithfulness for those who plan good — Proverbs 14:22

*"Love and faithfulness belong to those who plan good"* (22), while those who plot evil go astray.

See [[A Way That Seems Right]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 14.

### A dinner of herbs where love is — Proverbs 15:9, 12, 17

Yahweh *"loves him who follows after righteousness"* (9); the scoffer *"doesn't love to be reproved"* (12); and *"Better is a dinner of herbs, where love is, than a fattened calf with hatred"* (17), which ranks love at the table above the quality of the meal.

See [[A Gentle Answer]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 15.

### A friend loves at all times — Proverbs 17:9, 17

Covering an offense *"promotes love"* — *seeks* it, *məbaqqēš ʾahăbâ* (9; Hebrew from Sefaria); and *"A friend loves at all times; and a brother is born for adversity"* (17). Love here is constancy, tested by time and by trouble. See [[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]] and [[Love Covers Wrongs - Proverbs 10.12, 1 Peter 4.8, and James 5.20]].

See [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 17.

### The friend who clings — Proverbs 18:21, 24

The tongue's lovers *"will eat its fruit"* (21); and against *"many companions"* stands one who loves
(*ʾōhēḇ*) and *"sticks closer than a brother"* (24) — *dāḇēq*, the clinging of Genesis 2:24 (Hebrew from
Sefaria). See [[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]].

See [[A Strong Tower]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 18.

### Integrity, counsel, and compassion — Proverbs 19:4, 6–8, 17, 22

Acquiring wisdom is love for one's own soul (8), and kindness makes a person desirable (22, WEB).
The friends attracted by money and gifts (4, 6) disappear with poverty (7), exposing a contrast with
17:17's constant friend. Pity for the poor (17) is the concrete alternative to company purchased by
advantage. See [[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]] .

See [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] . Source: stored WEB Proverbs 19 and the
comparisons documented on that passage page.

### Discernment and accountability — Proverbs 20:6, 28

Many people claim unfailing love, but the faithful person is hard to find (6). The question
tests professions against constancy without denying that loyalty exists. Verse 28 makes love
and faithfulness protective of the king and love sustaining of his throne, balancing the
chapter's royal terror and punishment. No particular relationship or covenant is identified.

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 20; supporting comparisons are documented on the
passage page.

### Training a child — Proverbs 22:6, 15

*"Train up a child in the way he should go"* (22:6) and *"the rod of discipline drives"* folly
*"far from him"* (22:15). The word *love* does not appear, but the pericope continues 13:24, where
the father who loves his son is careful to discipline him. Verse 6 is a saying about the long reach
of formation, not a guarantee; see
[[Train Up a Child - Proverbs 22.6, Genesis 18.19, and Deuteronomy 6.7]].

See [[A Good Name]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 22; no new source-language claim.

### A parent's gladness — Proverbs 23:15–16, 24–25

*"My son, if your heart is wise, then my heart will be glad, even mine: yes, my heart will rejoice,
when your lips speak what is right"* (23:15–16). The father's love is expressed as joy in the son's
wisdom, and the block ends that strand with both parents: *"Let your father and your mother be glad!
Let her who bore you rejoice!"* (23:25). The father then asks for the son's heart in return: *"My son,
give me your heart"* (23:26).

See [[Do Not Envy Sinners]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 23:12–24:2.

### Faithful wounds — Proverbs 27:5–6, 9–10, 17

Chapter 27 tests love by what it costs to show it. *"Better is open rebuke than hidden love"* (5): a
love that never uncovers itself does not help. *"Faithful are the wounds of a friend; although the
kisses of an enemy are profuse"* (6) — the kiss belongs to the *śônēʾ*, *one who hates*, the word of
26:24's disguised hater. The friend's counsel is sweet (9), the father's friend is not to be forsaken
(10), and *"Iron sharpens iron; so a man sharpens his friend's countenance"* (17): love as friction
that improves. See [[A Friend Loves at All Times - Proverbs 17.17 and Job 6.14]].

See [[Do Not Boast About Tomorrow]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 27; Hebrew from Sefaria.
