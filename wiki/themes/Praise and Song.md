# Praise and Song

## Definition / overview

Speech addressed to God, or about him before others, that celebrates what he is and has done — and the
music, singing, and dance that carry it. **Scripture is slow to arrive at it.** Genesis has poetry —
Lamech's boast (4:23–24), the blessings of Isaac and Jacob (27; 49) — but no one in Genesis is said to sing,
and the verb *šîr* does not occur there (WLC lemma search). **The only *songs* Genesis mentions are a
send-off that never happened**: Laban's *"I might have sent you away with mirth and with songs, with
tambourine and with harp"* (Genesis 31:27).

**The first song in Scripture is the Song of the Sea.** *"Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song
to Yahweh"* (Exodus 15:1) — after the prose has said *"the people feared Yahweh; and they believed in Yahweh,
and in his servant Moses"* (14:31). **Praise comes after deliverance and after faith, and its first content
is what God has just done**: *"The horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea."* See
[[The Song of the Sea]].

## Development across Scripture

- **Before the Song, the people's speech to God is a cry.** They *"sighed … and cried"* in bondage (2:23),
  *"bowed their heads and worshiped"* without words (4:31; 12:27), and *"cried out to Yahweh"* at the sea in
  terror (14:10). **No one in Exodus addresses God in the second person with praise until *"Your right hand,
  Yahweh, is glorious in power"*** (15:6).
- **The Song moves from the first person to the second.** *"I will sing … This is my God, and I will praise
  him"* (15:1–2) becomes *"Who is like you, Yahweh, among the gods?"* (15:11). ***Təhillâ*, *praise*, first
  occurs there — *"fearful in praises"*** (WLC lemma search). **Praise and fear are joined in its first use.**
  See [[The Fear of God]].
- **It is sung by a crowd in the singular.** *"Yah is my strength and song … my God … my father's God"*
  (15:2). **The line becomes a fixed form**: Psalm 118:14 and Isaiah 12:2 repeat it word for word in the
  Hebrew (WLC). See [[Yah Is My Strength and Song - Exodus 15.2 in Psalm 118 and Isaiah 12]].
- **Its music and dance are led by a woman.** *"Miriam the prophetess … took a tambourine in her hand; and
  all the women went out after her with tambourines and with dances"* (15:20). The *tōp* is Laban's word
  (Genesis 31:27; the Torah's only other use, WLC); **the dances (*məḥōlōt*) recur in the Torah only at the
  golden calf** (32:19; WLC). **The same music can celebrate Yahweh or an image.** See [[Miriam]].
- **It looks forward as well as back.** The Song sings the habitation, the mountain, and the sanctuary as if
  reached (15:13, 17) and ends *"Yahweh shall reign forever and ever"* (15:18). See [[Kingship and Empire]].
- **Ahead** *(not yet ingested)*: the song at the well, *"Then Israel sang this song"* (Numbers 21:17, the
  only other Torah song introduced in the same words; WLC); Moses' second song (Deuteronomy 31:30–32:43);
  the Psalms; and *"the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb"* (Revelation 15:3). See
  [[The Song of Moses and the Lamb - Exodus 15 in Revelation 15]].
