# Wisdom

## Definition / overview

*Ḥokmâ* — skill, first of all. The word's earliest uses are craft: the *"wise-hearted"* who weave
and cut stones and work gold for the tabernacle (Exodus 28:3, 31:3–6, 35:35). From there it widens
to the skill of living well — knowing how the world is arranged and how to move within it — and
only then to what English usually means by wisdom.

The Hebrew Bible's wisdom literature (Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes, and much of the Psalter)
is distinctive in two ways. It argues from **observation** rather than from revelation: Proverbs
watches the ant, the sluggard, the adulteress, and the merchant, and draws conclusions. And it is
**international** — Israel's wisdom sits inside a tradition shared with Egypt, Mesopotamia, and
Edom, which is why the book of Job can be set entirely outside Israel with a cast of foreigners and
still belong in the canon, and why [[Teman]] can be famous for counsel.

Its one theological axiom is *"the fear of Yahweh is the beginning of wisdom"* (Proverbs 9:10, Psalm
111:10; and, in the WEB's rendering of Job 28:28, *"the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom"*) — meaning that the enterprise is bounded, that creaturely knowledge stops
somewhere, and that knowing where it stops is the first competence.

**The book of Job is the tradition auditing itself.** Every character claims wisdom and the book
disqualifies each claim in turn.

- **The friends claim it by inheritance.** Eliphaz: *"With us are both the gray-headed and the very
  aged men, much elder than your father"* (15:10), and *"which wise men have told by their fathers …
  to whom alone the land was given, and no stranger passed among them"* (15:18–19) — a pedigree
  guaranteed by having heard nothing from outside. Bildad: *"inquire of past generations"* (8:8).
  God's verdict on the pedigree is 42:7.
- **Eliphaz also claims it by private revelation** (4:12–17), and drops the claim when it does not
  work.
- **Job denies them all of it** — *"as for you all, come on now again; I shall not find a wise man
  among you"* (17:10) — and does not claim it for himself. What he claims is a case.
- **[[Elihu the Buzite]] claims it by inspiration against age** — *"It is not the great who are wise, nor the aged
  who understand justice"* (32:9) — which is the exact inversion of Eliphaz at 15:10, and Elihu is
  answered by nobody.
- **Chapter 28 concludes that no one has it.** *"Where shall wisdom be found? … The deep says, 'It
  isn't in me.' The sea says, 'It isn't with me.' … God understands its way, and he knows its
  place."*
- **And God's own answer is a wisdom examination Job fails** — sixty-odd questions about the sea's
  doors, the storehouses of snow, the ostrich, the wild donkey, and the hawk, none of which Job can
  answer, and none of which is about his case.

The taunt at 15:7 belongs here too. *"Are you the first man who was born? Or were you brought out
before the hills?"* uses of Job the language Proverbs 8:25 uses of Wisdom herself, personified and
present at the founding of the world. Eliphaz means it as an absurdity. God will ask Job the same
question in earnest at 38:4 — and the fact that God is entitled to ask it and Eliphaz is not is
close to the book's whole point about who may speak for the tradition. See
[[The First Man and Wisdom Before the Hills]].

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 3:6** — *"a tree to be desired to make one wise"*: wisdom as the first thing wanted
  wrongly. See [[The Fall]].
- **Genesis 41:33–39** *(not yet ingested)* — Joseph as the wise administrator, the tradition's
  courtly form.
- **Exodus 31:1–6; 35:30–35** — the craft sense, filled with the Spirit *"in wisdom, and in
  understanding, and in knowledge, and in all kinds of workmanship"*.
- **Deuteronomy 4:5–8** — Israel's law as its wisdom *"in the sight of the peoples"*.
- **1 Kings 3:5–28; 4:29–34** — Solomon asking for a hearing heart, and wisdom as international
  reputation, *"greater than the wisdom of all the children of the east"* — the same phrase used of
  Job at Job 1:3.
- **Job 15:7–19** — [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]]: wisdom claimed as unmixed inheritance.
- **Job 17:10** — [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]]: the claim refused.
- **Job 28:1–28** — [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]]: mining the earth for silver and iron, and finding that wisdom is
  not among the things that can be mined.
- **Job 38–41** — the examination.
- **Proverbs 1:1–7** — [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]]: the book's preface surveys wisdom's whole field
  and closes on the fear of Yahweh as *"the beginning of knowledge"*.
- **Proverbs 1:20–33** — [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]]: Wisdom personified for the first time,
  shouting in the street and the city gates, refused, and pronouncing the consequence in a prophet's
  voice.
- **Proverbs 8:22–31; 9:10** *(9:10 not yet ingested)* — [[Wisdom at the Creation]]; Wisdom personified, brought forth before the hills
  and playing before God at the creation, and the motto repeated.
- **Ecclesiastes 1:12–18; 7:23–24; 9:13–18** — wisdom pursued honestly to the end of its reach:
  *"in much wisdom is much grief"*.
- **Jeremiah 8:8–9; 9:23–24** — the wise put to shame, and the only boast permitted.
- **Isaiah 29:14** — *"the wisdom of their wise men will perish"*, quoted at 1 Corinthians 1:19.
- **1 Corinthians 1:18–31; 3:18–20** — the wisdom of the world made foolish, Christ *"made to us
  wisdom from God"*, and Job 5:13 quoted as scripture: *"He has taken the wise in their craftiness"*.
  See [[He Takes the Wise in Their Craftiness - Job 5.13 in 1 Corinthians 3.19]].
- **Colossians 2:3** — *"in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden"*, which is
  the New Testament's answer to Job 28's question about where it is kept.
- **James 1:5; 3:13–18** — wisdom to be asked for, and a wisdom *"from above"* distinguished from
  one that is *"earthly, sensual, demonic"* — the closest the New Testament comes to describing
  what went wrong with Job's friends.

### The priestly garments — Exodus 28:1–43

The makers of the garments are *"wise-hearted"*, whom God says he has *"filled with the spirit of
wisdom"* (Exodus 28:3). Wisdom is here a craft skill given by God for sanctuary work; the
chapter names no craftsman. See [[The Priestly Garments]].

### Wisdom shared among the makers — Exodus 31

Exodus 31:3 names wisdom, understanding, and knowledge as dimensions of Bezalel's Spirit-given
craftsmanship. Verse 6 also attributes the wisdom of the whole wise-hearted company to God. Wisdom
here produces things that can be used in worship; it is not confined to interpreting dreams or
advising rulers. Exodus 28:3 had already described gifted garment makers, and chapter 31 names two
leaders without making them the only gifted workers. See [[A Man in Whom Is the Spirit of God]].

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

The wise-hearted are called to make the sanctuary's components (35:10), and women are expressly
called wise-hearted as they spin (35:25–26). Wisdom here takes the form of practical competence,
not merely advice or the ability of the named master craftsmen. Verse 21 describes the donor's
own willing spirit in the WEB; it should not be silently equated with the explicit filling with
the Spirit of God in 31:3.

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The makers and the surplus — Exodus 35:30–36:7

The filling described for Bezalel (35:31) is followed by the ability to teach attributed to
him and Oholiab (35:34). Wisdom and understanding also belong to the wider company (36:1–2).
Divine gifting and the transmission of skill appear together, without a narrative of training
or a claim that all workers have identical abilities.

See [[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]].

### The enclosure made — Exodus 36:8–38

The wise-hearted workers of 36:8 put the abilities attributed to God in 36:1–2 into curtains,
joined frames, and hangings. Competence is shown by the objects made and fitted together, not
only by a title or an assertion of inspiration. The passage gives neither a training history
nor an individual allocation of tasks among the named leaders and wider company.

See [[The Tabernacle Built]].

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The ability attributed to God in 35:30–36:2 is now visible in measured woodwork, cast rings,
beaten gold, integrated branches and ornament, and the perfumer's craft (37:1–29). This is
practical skill within the prior commission. Chapter 37 gives no new account of inspiration,
training, or teaching, and does not assign all tasks exclusively to one worker.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38:1–31

Oholiab is described as an engraver, skillful workman, and embroiderer (38:23). The ability
announced in 35:30–35 now appears beside the credit for work done. No training history or exclusive
assignment of particular objects is supplied.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

The skillful work of the ephod and breastplate, including gold cut into wires for the
textiles (39:3, 8), realizes the capacities described in 28:3 and 35:30–35. The account shows
what gifted competence produces without repeating the earlier account of its divine source.
It supplies neither a new craftsman’s name nor a claim that skill requires no learning.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The capacities and shared commission described in 35:30–36:2 culminate in finished
articles approved by Moses (39:43). The completion unit does not repeat the account of gifted
wisdom or name an individual craftsman; it shows the outcome of that earlier equipping.
Approval concerns conformity to the received command, not a claim that craftsmanship requires
no training or that every Israelite performed every task.

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The preface of Proverbs — Proverbs 1:1–7

Proverbs opens by defining its subject through accumulation: wisdom, instruction, understanding, wise
dealing, *"righteousness, justice, and equity"*, prudence, knowledge, discretion, learning, sound counsel
(1:2–5). Wisdom here is a field, not a faculty, and it is moral as well as practical. It is also
**received** — *lāqaḥat*, *to receive* (1:3), and *leqaḥ*, *learning* (1:5), share a root — and it is
offered to the simple and the young and to the already wise, who *"may hear, and increase in learning"*.
Verse 4's *ʿormâ*, *prudence*, is the root of the serpent's *ʿārûm* (Genesis 3:1) and of the
*"craftiness"* of Job 5:13: shrewdness claimed for wisdom. Verse 6 promises to teach the reading of
proverb, parable, and riddle. The motto follows (1:7). The book's heading attaches all of it to
[[Solomon]], whose wisdom 1 Kings 3–4 describes as asked for and given. Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*. See [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]] and
[[The Wisdom of Solomon - 1 Kings 3-4 and the Headings of Proverbs]].

### The first lecture — Proverbs 1:8–19

Wisdom's first concrete form in Proverbs is a parent's word: the father's *mûsār* and the mother's
*tôrâ* (1:8), worn as a garland and a necklace (1:9). Its first content is a refusal — *"if sinners
entice you, don't consent"* (1:10) — and its method is to let the son hear the gang's invitation in
its own attractive voice (1:11–14) before showing him where their road goes (1:15–19). The lecture
never names God; it argues from the shape of the act, by repeating the gang's own verbs of ambush
against them (1:11, 18). Wisdom here is foresight: seeing the net being spread (1:17). Hebrew checked
against Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*. See [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]].

### Hidden, and shouting in the street — Proverbs 1:20–33

Proverbs 1:20–21 places Wisdom in the most public places a city has — *baḥûṣ*, the street;
*birḥōḇôt*, the squares; *bərōʾš hōmiyyôt*, the head of the noisy streets; *bəpiṯḥê šəʿārîm*, the
gate-openings — and names her with the plural *ḥoḵmôt* and a singular verb. **Job 28 says the
opposite**: *"where shall wisdom be found?"* (28:12); *"it is hidden from the eyes of all living"*
(28:21, *neʿelmâ*). Both claims stand and the wiki does not merge them. Its reading is that they are
about different things — Job 28 about wisdom as the knowledge by which the world is made and run,
which no creature can mine; Proverbs 1 about wisdom as the summons to fear Yahweh, which no one can
claim not to have heard — and that the two meet at the fear of Yahweh (Job 28:28; Proverbs 1:7, 29).
That is a reading, not something either text says.

The speech makes Wisdom a speaker in **God's register without being God**: the prophetic *"because I
called"* (1:24; Isaiah 65:12), *"I will pour out my spirit"* (1:23; compare Joel 2:28), laughter in
Psalm 2:4's verbs (1:26), and a refusal to answer (1:28) — yet she names Yahweh in the third person
(1:29). The Gospels let the voice become Jesus': Luke 11:49's *"the wisdom of God said"* is Matthew
23:34's *"I send"*. See [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]],
[[Wisdom Sends Prophets - Proverbs 1.20-33, Luke 11.49, and Matthew 23.34-37]],
[[When I Called, You Didn't Answer - Proverbs 1.24, Isaiah 65.12, and Jeremiah 7.13]], and
[[I Will Pour Out My Spirit - Proverbs 1.23, Joel 2.28, and Acts 2.17]]. Source: Proverbs 1 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Sought as silver, given by Yahweh — Proverbs 2:1–22

The second lecture makes wisdom both a search and a gift in one sentence. The son is to receive,
store up, incline his ear, call out, and *"seek her as silver, and search for her as for hidden
treasures"* (2:1–4), and the result is explained by *"For Yahweh gives wisdom"* (2:6). **Seeking finds
here** (2:5, *timṣāʾ*), where in 1:28 it did not. And the miner's picture that Job 28 uses to show
where wisdom is *not* found (28:1–11, 21) is the picture Proverbs uses for the search that succeeds.
**Contradiction flagged, not resolved**: the wiki's reading is that Proverbs' search succeeds because
it looks for what God gives, Job 28's fails because it looks in the earth, and both end at the fear
of Yahweh (2:5; Job 28:28) — but Proverbs uses the image without Job's caveat. Wisdom then works as a
guard (2:11, the same verbs God performs in 2:8) and rescues from two dangers, perverse men and the
strange woman (2:12–19). See [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] and
[[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]]. Source: Proverbs 2 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Not leaning on one's own understanding — Proverbs 3:1–12

The third lecture does not name wisdom until its last verse is past (3:13). Its commands are about
Yahweh — trust him, know him, fear him, honour him, accept his reproof — and its one warning about the
mind is a limit: *"don't lean on your own understanding"* (3:5), *"Don't be wise in your own eyes"*
(3:7). **The book written *"to discern the words of understanding"* (1:2) tells the son not to rest
his weight on understanding.** The wisdom it means is the kind that knows it is not the ground. See [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]]. Source: Proverbs 3 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Wisdom found, retained, and at creation — Proverbs 3:13–20

The praise in [[Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth]] connects human wisdom to
the world's making. The finder is happy (3:13), and the one who retains wisdom is happy
(3:18); finding must become a continuing hold. Wisdom's value exceeds silver, gold, and
rubies, and she gives long life, riches, honor, pleasantness, and peace. The closing
verses place wisdom, understanding, and knowledge in Yahweh's founding of the earth and
ordering of the waters. This complicates a sharp division between Proverbs' accessible
wisdom and Job's inaccessible cosmic wisdom: Proverbs itself puts them next to one another.
Job 28:12–28 still distinguishes God's knowledge from the fear of the Lord given to humans;
the difference should remain visible rather than be harmonized away. See
[[By Wisdom Yahweh Founded the Earth - Proverbs 3 and Creation]].

Source: Proverbs 3 and the cited Genesis and Job comparisons checked in the stored WEB,
2026-10-25.

### Retaining wisdom in the neighborhood — Proverbs 3:21–35

The counsel of 3:21–35 joins retained wisdom and discretion to secure walking, sleep,
and prompt dealings with a neighbor. Wisdom is neither only cosmic knowledge nor only
self-protection: the protected person must not exploit someone else's trust (3:29).
Glory and shame close the contrast between the wise and fools (3:35).

See [[Do Not Withhold Good]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 3; comparisons as cited above.
The discussion added here makes no claim about exact source-language forms.

### Wisdom received, loved, and handed on — Proverbs 4:1–9

The teacher of 4:1–9 first remembers being taught himself. Wisdom is acquired through
attention and continuing fidelity, and personified as someone to love and embrace. She
protects and honors the learner; the remembered lesson makes its hearer a potential next
teacher. This qualifies an account of Proverbs as solely observational: here wisdom is
also inherited instruction. The crown of 4:9 stands in explicit tension with the upright
Job's lost crown in Job 19:9; see [[The Crown Given and Taken - Proverbs 4 and Job 19]].

See [[Get Wisdom]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The way of wisdom — Proverbs 4:10–19

Wisdom becomes a road: *"I have taught you in the way of wisdom"* (4:11). Instruction is held
like wisdom herself — *"Don't let her go. Keep her, for she is your life"* (4:13), the WEB
using the same pronoun as for wisdom in 4:6–8. The lecture's wisdom is mostly avoidance — knowing
which road not to set foot on (4:14–15).

See [[The Two Paths]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### Wisdom kept in the heart — Proverbs 4:20–27

The father's words are to stay before the son's eyes and *"in the center of your heart"* (4:21),
and the heart is to be kept *"with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring of life"*
(4:23). Wisdom here is not only acquired and held (4:5–8) but stored at the source from which
speech, sight, and walking proceed (4:24–27). See
[[Out of the Heart - Proverbs 4.23, Matthew 15, and Luke 6]].

See [[Keep Your Heart]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### Consider her ways — Proverbs 6:6

*"Go to the ant, you sluggard. Consider her ways, and be wise"* (6:6). Wisdom here is learned by
watching a creature, as Job 12:7 sent Job's friends to *"ask the animals"* and as Agur will count
the ants among four small, wise things (30:24–25). See
[[The Surety, the Sluggard, and the Seven Abominations]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 6; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

**In Proverbs 7 the strange woman imitates Wisdom.** She stands *"in the streets … in the squares"*
(7:12, *baḥûṣ … bārəḥōḇôṯ*), the two places where Wisdom cried out (1:20); she is *loud*
(*hōmiyyâ*, 7:11), and Wisdom called *"at the head of noisy places"* (1:21, *hōmiyyôṯ*); she has
*"diligently"* sought and found (7:15, *šiḥēr*, *māṣāʾ*), the pair Wisdom uses of those who seek her
(1:28; 8:17); and her persuasion is *leqaḥ*, *learning* (7:21; cf. 1:5; 4:2). Against her the son
is told to call wisdom *"my sister"* (7:4). Chapter 8 answers at once with Wisdom's own speech at the
gates. See [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 7; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Wisdom at the gates — Proverbs 8:1–21

Wisdom's second speech answers the strange woman of chapter 7 from the same public ground. She stands
on the heights, at the crossroads (*bêṯ nəṯîḇôṯ*, *the house of paths*), and at the city gates
(8:2–3), using the verbs of her first speech (*tiqrāʾ*, *tittēn qôlāh*, *tārōnnâ*; 1:20–21), and
calls *"the sons of mankind"* (8:4) — the simple and fools included. Her words have *"nothing crooked
or perverse in them"* (8:8). She repeats the father's valuation of herself — 8:11b is 3:15b almost
word for word — and then claims, in God's grammar, what Job 12:13 gave to God: *"Counsel and sound
knowledge are mine. I have understanding and power"* (8:14). Through her *"kings reign"* (8:15–16);
she loves those who love her and is found by those who seek her (8:17); she brings riches, honour,
and *ṣəḏāqâ* (8:18). **Job 28 still stands against 8:1's open call**, and the wiki keeps the reading
given under 1:20–33. See [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]],
[[Two Voices in the Street - The Strange Woman and Wisdom in Proverbs 7-9]], and
[[Counsel and Might - Job 12.13, Proverbs 8.14, and Isaiah 11.2]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

**Proverbs 8:22–36 — Wisdom before the world.** The speech's second half gives Wisdom an origin and
a place. *"Yahweh possessed me in the beginning of his work"* (8:22): *qānānî*, the verb the father
used for *"Get wisdom"* (4:7, *qənēh ḥoḵmâ*), so that what the son must acquire Yahweh had at the
beginning. She was *"set up from everlasting"* and *"born"* before depths and hills (8:23–25), was
*"there"* when the heavens were established and the sea bounded (8:27–29), and stood beside God as
*ʾāmôn* — *"craftsman"* in the WEB, *nursling* on another reading — delighting before him and with
*"the sons of men"* (8:30–31). Then she speaks as the father does, *"Now therefore, my sons, listen to
me"* (8:32; 5:7; 7:24), and closes: *"whoever finds me, finds life … All those who hate me love
death"* (8:35–36). **Job 28 and Proverbs 8 agree that wisdom was with God at creation** (Job 28:27);
they differ on what humans may have of it — Job 28:28 gives them the fear of the Lord, Proverbs 8:35
life to whoever finds her. Wisdom has an origin and was not one of the things made; the wiki reads
her still as God's wisdom personified. The New Testament's language for Christ as agent of creation
resembles the passage without citing it. See [[Wisdom at the Creation]] and
[[Possessed in the Beginning - Proverbs 8.22-31, John 1, and Colossians 1]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

  grain in a country whose whole economy is cattle and grain. **The failure is not of intelligence but of
  access**, which is the doctrine of 40:8 demonstrated with the professionals in the room. Then Joseph
  recommends ***ʾîš nābôn wəḥākām***, *a discreet and wise man* (41:33) — **the standard Israelite wisdom
  pair**, the same two words as Solomon's gift at 1 Kings 3:12 and Moses' requirement at
  Deuteronomy 1:13 — **as a qualification for running a country**, and **Pharaoh applies them back to
  him verbatim** (41:39). **Joseph is Scripture's first wise man at a foreign court and the pattern for
  every later one**; note that neither he nor Daniel wins by being untrained, and that the same word
  *ḥākām* describes both the guild that fails and the man who succeeds. **Wisdom here is entirely
  practical** — a forecast, a levy, a bureaucracy, and seven years of storage that work. See
  [[A Man in Whom Is the Spirit of God]]
- [[Joseph and the Famine]] — Genesis 47:13–26; ***ḥākām wənābôn*** **was Pharaoh's verdict at
  41:39, and this is what it looked like over seven years.** **The plan works completely**: nobody
  starves, the seed is issued, four parts in five are left to the farmer, and the beneficiaries thank
  him. **It also ends with a free peasantry holding no land and paying a fifth for ever** (47:20,
  26). **Genesis supplies no adverb for any of it** — **the book that says *"the thing which he did
  was evil in Yahweh's sight"* about a much smaller matter** (38:10) **reports this transaction, the
  people's gratitude, and stops.** **The wiki records the silence and does not fill it**
- [[Eliphaz's First Speech]] — Job 4–5, **all three of the wise man's warrants in one speech**:
  observation (*"According to what I have seen"*, 4:8), revelation (the night vision, 4:12–21), and
  consensus (*"we have searched it, so it is"*, 5:27). And 5:13, *"He takes the wise in their own
  craftiness"*, which Paul quotes as Scripture and which the book turns on its speaker. See
  [[He Takes the Wise in Their Craftiness - Job 5.13 in 1 Corinthians 3.19]]
- [[Bildad's First Speech]] — Job 8:8–10, **wisdom as inheritance, and the brevity of life as its
  warrant.** *"We are but of yesterday, and know nothing, because our days on earth are a shadow"*:
  one lifetime is too short to see the pattern, so ask the fathers, who saw more of it. Job shares the
  premise and draws the opposite conclusion (7:6–7), and the parenthesis is the speech's one
  accidentally true line — none of the men on the ash heap knows what was said in the council.
- [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]] — Job 9:4, *"wise in heart, and mighty in strength: who has hardened himself against him,
  and prospered?"* — God's wisdom cited not as a resource but as one more reason no man can win
  against him.
- [[Zophar's First Speech]] — Job 11:6–9; ***"the secrets of wisdom"*** and **wisdom given four dimensions** — higher
  than heaven, deeper than Sheol, longer than the earth, broader than the sea. Zophar's premise is
  God's own (38:16–18, with the same *ḥēqer*); his inference, that Job should be silent, is not. See
  [[Higher Than Heaven, Deeper Than Sheol - The Measure of the Unsearchable]]
- [[Though He Slay Me]] — Job 12:2–3, 12–13; 13:5; **a dispute about who owns it.** *"No doubt, but
  you are the people, and wisdom shall die with you"*; *"With aged men is wisdom"* — and then the
  correction, ***"With God is wisdom and might. He has counsel and understanding"*** — followed by a
  hymn in which God's wisdom strips counselors and makes judges fools (12:17). The friends' one wise
  act is named: *"Oh that you would be completely silent! Then you would be wise"* (13:5). See
  [[He Pours Contempt on Princes - Job 12.21-24 and Psalm 107.40]]
- [[Why Do the Wicked Live]] — Job 21:22, 29; **two sources of knowledge set against each other.**
  The friends' authority is the ancients — the fathers (8:8), the aged (15:10), what has been known
  *"since man was placed on earth"* (20:4). Job's is the *"wayfaring men"* (21:29), *ʿôbrê dārek*,
  who have been somewhere and seen. Depth of time against breadth of observation, and the observers
  win at 42:7. And 21:22's *"Shall any teach God knowledge?"* is Isaiah 40:14's question, aimed at the
  men who presume to know what God must do.
- [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]] — Job 26:3, 14; *"How have you counseled him who has no
  wisdom"* — sarcasm at Bildad's expense — and ***"Behold, these are but the outskirts of his ways. How
  small a whisper do we hear of him!"*** **The limit of knowledge, stated on the eve of the hymn to
  wisdom (Job 28).** The friends reasoned from God's character as if they had heard the thunder; Job
  says no one has
- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:1–28; **the theme's centre in Job, and the tradition's question asked of itself.**
  The miner can find silver, gold, iron, copper and sapphire anywhere in the earth and bring *"the
  thing that is hidden"* to light (28:11), but *"where shall wisdom be found? Where is the place of
  understanding?"* (28:12, 20). Not in the land of the living, not in the deep or the sea, not for gold
  of Ophir or topaz of Cush, and the underworld has only *"heard a rumor of it"* (28:22). **God alone
  *"understands its way"*** (28:23), having seen and searched it out when he measured the wind and the
  rain (28:25–27). What he gives man is not wisdom's location but its human form: *"the fear of the
  Lord, that is wisdom. To depart from evil is understanding"* (28:28) — Job 1:1's description of Job.
  The same verb, *ḥāqar*, is the miner's search (28:3) and God's (28:27). See
  [[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]] and
  [[Better Than Gold - The Price of Wisdom]]
- [[Elihu Speaks in Anger]] — Job 32:7–9; 33:33; **wisdom as gift against wisdom as age.** *"I said, 'Days should speak,
  and multitude of years should teach wisdom.' But there is a spirit in man, and the breath of the
  Almighty gives them understanding. It is not the great who are wise, nor the aged who understand
  justice."* Eliphaz's credential (15:10) and Job's half-concession (12:12) overturned by the youngest
  man present, who claims the breath of Genesis 2:7 as his authority and ends *"I will teach you
  wisdom"* (33:33). Half of chapter 28 — wisdom is given, not accumulated — without the other half,
  that it belongs to God and is found in fearing him.
- [[Elihu on God's Justice]] — Job 34:2, 34–35; 35:11; **the wise as a jury.** *"Hear my words, you wise
  men"* (34:2); *"every wise man who hears me"* will say *"His words are without wisdom"* (34:34–35).
  Elihu, who in chapter 32 denied that age made men wise, now calls on the wise to agree with him. And God
  *"makes us wiser than the birds of the sky"* (35:11) — Job's *"ask the animals … the birds of the sky"*
  (12:7) inverted.
- [[Elihu on God's Greatness]] — Job 36:4, 22; 37:16, 24; **the teacher and the self-wise.** Elihu calls
  himself *"perfect in knowledge"* (36:4) and gives God the same title (37:16); asks *"Who is a teacher
  like him?"* (36:22); and ends *"He doesn't regard any who are wise of heart"* (37:24) — Job's phrase for
  God at 9:4, and the second half of 28:28's motto set against the first. See
  [[The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom - Job 28.28 and the Wisdom Motto]].
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:36–37; 39:17, 26; **wisdom as God's to give and withhold.** *"Who has
  put wisdom in the inward parts?"* (38:36); *"Who can count the clouds by wisdom?"* (38:37); the
  ostrich, *"because God has deprived her of wisdom"* (39:17); *"Is it by your wisdom that the hawk
  soars?"* (39:26). The hymn of chapter 28 said only God knows wisdom's place; here God examines Job on
  it, and Job does not answer.
- [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]] — Job 42:2–3; **the limit of knowledge confessed.** *"I have uttered that which I did not
  understand, things too wonderful for me, which I didn't know"* (42:3) — *niplāʾôt*, the *p-l-ʾ* of Genesis
  18:14. Job concedes the charge of 38:2 and nothing else; what he did not know is God's purpose, which *"can[not]
  be restrained"* (42:2). See [[Words Without Knowledge - Job 34.35, 35.16, 38.2, and 42.3]].
- [[The Oppression in Egypt]] — Exodus 1:10; **wisdom as cunning.** *"Come, let us deal wisely with them"* — *ḥākam*,
  in the country where Joseph was called *"discreet and wise"* (Genesis 41:39), turned against his
  family
- [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] — Exodus 7:11; **Egypt's *ḥăkāmîm* again.** Pharaoh calls *"the
  wise men and the sorcerers"*, and the *ḥarṭummîm* of Genesis 41:8 match Aaron's sign — the guild that
  could not read a dream can copy a wonder, and loses its rods for it —
  [[The Magicians of Egypt - Hartummim in Genesis, Exodus, and Daniel]]
- [[Jethro's Counsel]] — Exodus 18:17–23; **wisdom as administration, from a foreigner.** *"The thing that you
  do is not good … I will give you counsel"* — *ʾîʿāṣəkā*, a verb the Torah uses only here and of Balaam (Numbers
  24:14; WLC). **Not a dream read or a wonder copied but a working plan**, conditional on *"and God commands you
  so"*, and adopted without any reported word from God. Deuteronomy's retelling asks the judges to be *"wise men"*
  (1:13) — [[Too Heavy for You Alone - Exodus 18, Numbers 11, and Deuteronomy 1]]
- [[The Priestly Garments]] — Exodus 28:1–43; the wise-hearted makers filled with *"the spirit of wisdom"*.
- [[The Priestly Garments Made]] — Exodus 39:1–31; Skill takes material form in metalwork, stone engraving, and textiles.

- [[The Work Completed]] — Exodus 39:32–43; Previously commissioned skill produces work that withstands review.

- [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] — Proverbs 1:8–19; the first lecture — a parent's word worn as ornament, and foresight about the gang's road
- [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]] — Proverbs 1:20–33; Wisdom personified, calling in public, refused, and speaking judgment in a prophet's voice; the tension with Job 28's hidden wisdom recorded
- [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] — Proverbs 2:1–22; sought as silver and given by Yahweh; the tension with Job 28's failed search recorded
- [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]] — Proverbs 3:1–12; trust, not one's own understanding; *"Don't be wise in your own eyes"*
- [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]] — Proverbs 7:4, 12, 15, 21; wisdom as sister, and the strange woman in Wisdom's
  places and words
- [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]] — Proverbs 8:1–21; Wisdom's second speech: at the gates and crossroads, straight words, better than silver, *"By me kings reign"*, found by those who seek her
- [[Wisdom at the Creation]] — Proverbs 8:22–36; Wisdom possessed by Yahweh at the beginning, present at creation as *ʾāmôn*, delighting in humanity; whoever finds her finds life
