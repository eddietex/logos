# Land and Sojourning

## Definition / overview

Going somewhere and not arriving; living in a place that belongs to someone else.

The theme begins in a travel notice. *"They went from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of
Canaan. They came to Haran and lived there"* (Genesis 11:31). It is the first time in the Bible
anyone sets out for [[Canaan]], and the sentence records both the intention and the failure in the
same breath. Two verses later the man who set out is dead in the wrong city. Then Genesis 12:1 —
*"Leave your country, and your relatives, and your father's house, and go to the land that I will
show you"* — turns the unfinished journey into a command, and the rest of the Torah is about
getting there.

Two Hebrew words carry it. ***ʾereṣ***, land or earth, which Genesis has used for the whole world
(1:1) and now begins using for one territory; and **_gûr_**, *to sojourn*, to live somewhere as a
resident alien with no title — the root behind *gēr*, the stranger whom Israelite law will protect
precisely because Israel was one (Exodus 22:21; Leviticus 19:34). The patriarchs are *gērîm* in the
land they are promised. Abraham owns nothing in Canaan until he buys a grave (Genesis 23), and the
one piece of the promise he holds in his hand is a field with a cave in it.

**The structure the theme creates is a permanent gap between promise and possession.** The land is
promised to Abraham and he is a sojourner in it; promised to Isaac and Jacob and they leave it for
famine and for exile; promised to Israel and they spend forty years outside it; given at last under
Joshua and lost again in 587 BC. Hebrews 11 reads the whole sequence as intentional: *"By faith he
lived as an alien in the land of promise, as in a land not his own … for he looked for the city which
has foundations, whose builder and maker is God"* (11:9–10) — which sets the patriarchs' tents
against the city of Genesis 11:4 that had none.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 11:31–32** — the first journey toward Canaan, undertaken by [[Terah]] with no command
  behind it, stopping at [[Haran (city)]]. He dies short of the land.
- **Genesis 12:1, 5–9** — the command, the arrival, and the first promise of the land itself. Three
  things are set here that hold for the rest of the Bible. The destination is **withheld** — *"the
  land that I will show you"*, where Terah's journey had named Canaan and stopped. The land is
  **occupied** when he gets there, and the narrator says so (12:6) before any promise about it is
  made. And the promise, when it comes, is **not to him**: *"I will give this land to your
  offspring"* (12:7). Abram crosses the whole country from Shechem to the Negev and ends the pericope
  *"still going on"*, having built two altars and pitched one tent. Acts 7:5 states the outcome
  flatly — *"he gave him no inheritance in it, no, not enough to set his foot on."* See
  [[The Call of Abram]].
- **Genesis 12:10–20** — the theme's first hard test, and it comes four verses after the promise.
  *"There was a famine in the land"*, and Abram *"went down into Egypt to live as a foreigner
  there"* — **_lāgûr_**, the theme's own verb, now applied to a country that was never promised him.
  The sequence is worth stating flatly: the land is promised at 12:7, and by 12:10 it cannot feed
  him. He is a *gēr* in Canaan because it belongs to somebody else, and a *gēr* in Egypt because he
  is starving; the difference between the two is a promise and nothing else. Genesis offers no
  comment on the descent, and Isaac will be explicitly forbidden the same move at 26:2. See
  [[Abram in Egypt]].
- **Genesis 15:13–16** — the promise given a delay built into it: four hundred years of servitude in
  a land not theirs, *"for the iniquity of the Amorite is not yet full."*
- **Genesis 23** — Abraham, *"a stranger and a foreigner"* among the Hittites, buys Machpelah. The
  only land he ever owns is a burial plot, and he pays full price for it.
- **Genesis 47:9** — Jacob to Pharaoh: *"The days of the years of my pilgrimage are one hundred
  thirty years."*
- **Exodus and Numbers** — the land as the destination of the exodus, and the wilderness generation
  that does not enter it; Moses sees it from Nebo and dies outside (Deuteronomy 34:1–5), the same
  ending as Terah's, at the other end of the Torah.
- **Leviticus 25:23** — the theological centre: *"The land shall not be sold in perpetuity, for the
  land is mine; for you are strangers and live as foreigners with me."* Israel in the land is still a
  sojourner, and the landlord is God.
- **Joshua 21:43–45; Judges 2** — the land given, and immediately not fully held.
- **2 Kings 25; Psalm 137** — the land lost, and sojourning resumed involuntarily.
- **1 Chronicles 29:15; Psalm 39:12** — Israel praying the patriarchs' status back onto itself: *"we
  are strangers before you, and foreigners, as all our fathers were."*
- **Hebrews 11:8–16** — the New Testament's reading: they *"confessed that they were strangers and
  pilgrims on the earth"* and sought *"a better country, that is, a heavenly one."*
- **1 Peter 1:1; 2:11** — the church addressed as *"sojourners of the Dispersion"* and urged to live
  *"as foreigners and pilgrims."*

**The pattern in one line:** in Scripture, the people who are promised land are almost never the
people who possess it, and the ones who possess it are told they hold it as tenants. Genesis 11:31 is
where that starts, in a sentence about a family that got halfway.

## Key passages

- [[From Shem to Abram]] — Genesis 11:31–32, the first journey toward Canaan and the first stop short
  of it
- [[The Call of Abram]] — Genesis 12:1–9: the land entered, crossed end to end, promised to somebody
  not yet born, and already somebody else's
- [[Abram in Egypt]] — Genesis 12:10–20: the promised land starves him out of itself four verses
  after the promise, and he sojourns in Egypt instead
- [[Abram and Lot Separate]] — Genesis 13:6, 14–18: the land cannot carry two of his households, and
  in the same chapter it is given to him and his offspring *forever*, in four directions, with an
  order to walk its length and breadth. Total title, zero possession — and he answers by pitching a
  tent
- Genesis 15:7–21 — the land promised by covenant, with four centuries of delay *(not yet ingested)*
- Genesis 23 — Machpelah, the only ground Abraham owns *(not yet ingested)*
- Leviticus 25:23 — *"the land is mine; for you are strangers"*
- Deuteronomy 34:1–5 — Moses outside the land
- Hebrews 11:8–16 — the patriarchs as pilgrims looking for a city
