# Priesthood

## Definition / overview

### The altar of witness — Joshua 22:10–34

Phinehas, son of Eleazar the priest, leads the delegation and declares its outcome (13, 30–31).
The one legitimate altar stands *before his tabernacle* (29). The eastern altar is denied any
priestly or sacrificial use.

See [[The Altar of Witness by the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 22:10–34; comparisons as cited.

### Levitical cities — Joshua 21:1–42

The list distinguishes Aaron’s thirteen priestly cities from the remaining Kohathites’ ten, Gershon’s thirteen, and Merari’s twelve (4–7, 19–40). Not every Levite is thereby called an Aaronic priest.

See [[The Cities of the Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:1–42.

### Refuge cities appointed — Joshua 20:1–9

Residence continues until the death of the high priest of those days (6). No particular priest is named, no death is narrated, and no sacrificial explanation for this release is supplied.

See [[The Cities of Refuge Appointed]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 20:1–9.

### Joshua’s own inheritance — Joshua 19:49–51

Eleazar’s priestly role includes shared responsibility for dividing the land with Joshua and the tribal household heads (51). Priestly participation does not make the grant a unilateral priestly decision.

See [[Joshua's Own Inheritance]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 19:49–51.

### The survey at Shiloh — Joshua 18:1–10

Yahweh’s priesthood is called the Levites’ inheritance instead of a territorial portion (7). The
broad tribal wording stands beside Numbers 18:1–7’s restricted altar priesthood; it does not
explicitly erase that distinction or the cities allowed in Joshua 14:4.

See [[The Land Surveyed at Shiloh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 18:1–10; comparisons as cited.

### The western division — Joshua 14:1–5

Eleazar the priest participates with Joshua and tribal heads in allocation (1). The Levites’ exclusion from tribal territory is paired with cities and land for livestock and property (3–4); the passage does not collapse the distinction between priests and other Levites.

See [[The Division of the Land Begins]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:1–5; comparisons as cited.


### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

Levi receives no territorial share: offerings made by fire are its inheritance (14), and Yahweh is its inheritance (33). Compare Deuteronomy 18:1–2; the collective wording does not resolve Numbers 18’s distinctions between priests and other Levites.

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### The law read at Ebal — Joshua 8:30–35

The Levitical priests carry the covenant ark before the assembled people (33). Joshua is the named reader (34–35); the passage does not individually name the priests or explicitly identify who performs each sacrifice.

See [[The Law Read at Mount Ebal]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 8:30–35.

### The fall of Jericho — Joshua 6:1–27

Seven unnamed priests sound rams’ horns before the ark, with armed men ahead and a rear guard behind (4–13). Priestly service accompanies the military procession; the account does not name these priests or identify their horns as the silver instruments of Numbers 10.

See [[The Fall of Jericho]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 6:1–27; comparisons identified above.


### The twelve stones — Joshua 4:1–24

The ark-bearing priests hold their position until the work is finished and leave at the transmitted
divine command (10–18). No individual priest is named.
See [[The Twelve Stones from the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 4:1–24.

### Crossing the Jordan — Joshua 3:1–17

Unnamed Levitical priests carry the covenant ark into the Jordan and stand until all Israel crosses (3, 17). Their service supports the entire people. This passage does not reconcile their ark-bearing role with the Kohathite transport regulations of Numbers 4.

See [[Crossing the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 3:1–17.


### Moses blesses the tribes — Deuteronomy 33:1–29

Levi’s blessing unites sacred discernment, teaching, incense, burnt offering, and covenant loyalty
(8–11); the godly one is not personally named. The tribe and its priestly functions are joined
without a detailed account of internal offices.

See [[Moses Blesses the Tribes]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 33:1–29; comparisons as cited.


### The book beside the ark — Deuteronomy 31:24–29

Ark-bearing Levites are entrusted with the book’s deposit (25–26). The functional description connects custody of covenant objects with custody of instruction without specifying internal Levitical divisions.

See [[The Book Beside the Ark]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:24–29.


### The responsive curses — Deuteronomy 27:11–26

The Levites must pronounce the curses loudly to all Israel (14). Levi also appears among the tribes on Gerizim (12); the text does not specify how the speaking group is positioned relative to that tribal assignment.

See [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:11–26.


### Stones and an altar — Deuteronomy 27:1–10

The Levitical priests speak with Moses to all Israel, calling for silence, listening,
and obedience grounded in belonging to Yahweh (9–10). This is a teaching role; the unit
does not name an individual priest or narrate a priest performing the future sacrifices.

See [[Stones and an Altar on Mount Ebal]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:1–10.

### Present and silent at the valley — Deuteronomy 21:5

*"The priests the sons of Levi shall come near"* (5), and the verse states why — chosen *"to minister to
him, and to bless in Yahweh's name"*, and by their word *"every controversy and every assault"* is
decided. It gives them nothing to do. The elders take, bring down, and kill the heifer, wash, and pray;
no priestly act is named. Their presence is that of the court of 17:8–9 and 19:17.

See [[An Unsolved Killing and the Broken-Necked Heifer]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 21:1–9.

### The priest before battle — Deuteronomy 20:2–4

*"When you draw near to the battle, … the priest shall approach and speak to the people"* (2). His
address opens *"Hear, Israel"* and forbids fear, because Yahweh *"goes with you, to fight for you"* (3–4).
The priest gives the theological word; the officers then handle the dismissals and the muster (5–9).
Compare Phinehas at the war with Midian, with *"the trumpets for the alarm in his hand"* (Numbers 31:6).

See [[Laws for Going to War]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 20:1–20; Numbers 31:6.

### Priests in the court — Deuteronomy 19:17

In a disputed testimony both parties *"stand before Yahweh, before the priests and the judges who shall
be in those days"* (17), the phrase of 17:9's court. Whether that means the central court at the chosen
place, the text does not say.

See [[Landmarks, Witnesses, and False Testimony]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 19:14–21.

### Renewed tablets and onward journey — Deuteronomy 10:1–11

Aaron’s death is followed by Eleazar’s ministry (6). Levi’s collective appointment includes carrying
the ark, ministering, and blessing (8–9). Numbers 18 distinguishes Aaron’s altar service from Levite
assistance; Deuteronomy’s collective wording should not silently erase that distinction.

See [[New Tablets and the Journey Onward]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 10:1–11.

### The calf and intercession — Deuteronomy 9:7–29

Aaron himself is threatened with destruction and receives Moses’ intercession (20). His office
does not exempt him from the judgment associated with the calf; no new ordination or replacement
is narrated.

See [[Remember the Calf and Moses' Intercession]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 9:7–29.


### Levitical cities and refuge — Numbers 35:1–34

Levites receive forty-eight cities with surrounding land, including six refuges (2–8). This confirms the correction to absolute propertylessness: Numbers 18 withholds a tribal inheritance, while Numbers 35 grants residential and pastoral holdings. The anointed high priest’s death ends the required refuge residence (25–28); the text does not explain the mechanism or call that death a sacrifice.

See [[The Levitical Cities and the Cities of Refuge]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 35; comparisons as cited.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

Eleazar the priest shares the allotment task with Joshua and tribal leaders (17–29). His commission concerns land administration; no new priestly rite is described.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

Eleazar shares the count once performed with Aaron. Levi is separately counted from one month upward
and explicitly excluded from the other tribes’ inheritance (57–64).

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


### Baal Peor — Numbers 25:1–18

The promise to Phinehas and his offspring confirms a future within Aaron’s line (7, 11–13). It
neither creates Aaron’s office for the first time nor states that Eleazar immediately ceases
serving.

See [[Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 25:1–18.


### The death of Aaron — Numbers 20:22–29

Aaron dies, but his garments pass to Eleazar by divine command (25–28). This enacts
the succession anticipated in Exodus 29:29–30. The short account does not narrate
every ordination rite. See [[Aaron's Garments Pass to Eleazar]] for the mourning-law question.

See [[The Death of Aaron]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 20:22–29.

### The red heifer — Numbers 19:1–22

Eleazar oversees the heifer and sprinkles its blood toward the Tent (3–4). Gathering
ashes and applying the water require clean persons, not explicitly priests (9, 18).
The officiating priest himself becomes temporarily unclean (7).

See [[The Red Heifer]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 19.

### The budding rod — Numbers 17:1–13

The chosen rod belongs to Aaron for Levi (3, 8). In the context of Numbers 16:9–10, 40, this
affirms Aaron’s priesthood without erasing the distinction between priests and other Levites.

See [[Aaron's Rod Buds]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 17.

### The rebellion of Korah — Numbers 16:1–40

Levite service is already a gift of nearness, yet Korah seeks priesthood also (9–10). The altar
covering explicitly warns anyone outside Aaron’s offspring against burning incense (40). The
distinction is between offices within Israel, not simply between Israel and foreigners.

See [[The Rebellion of Korah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:1–40; comparisons as cited.

### Defect in priest and offering — Leviticus 22:17–33

Chapter 21 excluded a priest with a defect from offering "the bread of his God" (21:17); chapter
22 excludes an animal with a defect from being "the bread of your God" (25). The altar is guarded
both at the officiant and at the offering, and the offerer, not only the priest, is addressed (18).

See [[Acceptable Victims and the Holy Name]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 22:17–33.



### The priest’s household table — Leviticus 22:1–16

The priest's portion is "his bread" (7), and it feeds a household: bought and house-born slaves
and a returned childless daughter eat; a stranger, a resident foreigner, a hired servant, and a
daughter married to an outsider do not (10–13). Uncleanness suspends a priest's own eating until
evening and bathing (4–7), and priests must not make Israel bear guilt through the holy things
(15–16).

See [[Eating the Holy Things]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 22:1–16.


### Priestly holiness — Leviticus 21

Ordinary priests may incur corpse defilement for specified close relatives (1–3), but the high
priest may not do so even for parents (10–12). Bodily defects bar descendants of Aaron from
altar and veil approach, yet not from eating holy and most holy food (17–23). Descent, service
eligibility, and food entitlement are distinct.

See [[The Holiness of the Priests]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 21.


Someone appointed to stand between God and people — to offer what people bring and to carry back
what God gives.

The Hebrew is *kōhēn*, and its first occurrence in the Bible is Genesis 14:18: *"Melchizedek king of
Salem brought out bread and wine: and he was priest of God Most High."* Everything Israel's
priesthood will later be defined by is absent from that verse. There is no sanctuary, no altar
mentioned, no sacrifice, no law, no tribe, no anointing, no genealogy, and no Israel — Levi's
great-grandfather is the man standing in front of him. The office arrives in Scripture attached to a
Canaanite king, and the Bible's two great arguments about priesthood (Psalm 110 and Hebrews 5–7)
both work from precisely that. See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] and [[Melchizedek]].

**The point of a priest is direction.** Sacrifice has been happening since Genesis 4 without one:
Cain and Abel bring offerings, Noah builds an altar, Job sacrifices for his children, and Abram
builds three altars — and not one of them is called a priest. What Melchizedek does that they do not
is move in both directions. He *brings out* to Abram, and he blesses Abram *of God Most High*; then
he blesses God on Abram's behalf; then he receives a tenth. Something goes up through him and
something comes down through him. That two-way motion is what the word adds. See
[[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

**The national vocation and the restricted office coexist in Exodus 19.** The whole assembly
is offered a priestly kingdom (19:6), while a group already called priests must prepare and
remain within the boundary (19:22, 24). The text does not identify those priests or explain
their appointment. It singles Aaron out to accompany Moses without narrating his ordination.
This prevents reading the national calling as universal permission to officiate or ascend.
See [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] and [[A Kingdom of Priests - Exodus 19 and First Peter 2]].

## Development across Scripture

### Reading to all Israel — Deuteronomy 31:9–13

Levitical priests and all the elders receive Moses’ written law (9). The ensuing command is addressed to them (10), without naming a single future reader or excluding the elders from responsibility. No personal priestly name is given.

See [[Read the Law to All Israel]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:9–13.

### The priests' due and the Levite from the towns — Deuteronomy 18:1–8

*"The priests the Levites, all the tribe of Levi"* have no portion nor inheritance with Israel and eat *"the offerings of Yahweh made by fire"* (1–2). The priest receives the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the inner parts of each ox or sheep sacrificed, and the first fruits of grain, wine, oil, and fleece (3–4), because Yahweh *"has chosen him out of all your tribes, to stand to minister in Yahweh's name, him and his sons for ever"* (5). A Levite from the towns who comes to the place *"shall minister … as all his brothers the Levites do"* and eats *"like portions"* (7–8). The cuts are not Leviticus 7's breast and right thigh. Whether 18:7 makes every Levite eligible for altar service, as against Numbers 18:1–7, is the long-standing question this passage raises; the passage page records three readings and adopts none, as does [[Yahweh as Levi’s Inheritance - Numbers 18 and Deuteronomy 10]]. See also [[The Custom of the Priests - Deuteronomy 18.3 and 1 Samuel 2.13-16]] and [[The Priests of the High Places - Deuteronomy 18.6-8 and 2 Kings 23.9]].

See [[The Portion of the Priests and Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 18:1–8.

### The priests the Levites as judges — Deuteronomy 17:8–12

The hard case goes to *"the priests who are Levites, and to the judge who shall be in those days"* (17:9), and refusing *"the priest who stands to minister there before Yahweh"* is a capital offence (12). *Hakkōhănîm halwiyyim* occurs in the Torah only in Deuteronomy (17:9, 18; 18:1; 24:8; 27:9; WLC, morphhb). How priest and judge divide the work is not stated.

See [[Judges, Worship, and Difficult Cases]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 16:18–17:13.

### The Levite's share in the tithe — Deuteronomy 14:22–29

The Levite *"has no portion nor inheritance"* and must not be forsaken (27); he joins the tithe meal and is first among the third-year recipients (29). Numbers 18:21–24 gives the Levites the whole tithe as their inheritance. Deuteronomy's arrangement is different and does not mention Numbers'. See [[Three Tithe Laws - Leviticus 27, Numbers 18, and Deuteronomy 14]].

See [[Tithes for Feasting and Provision]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 14:22–29.

### The Levite not forsaken — Deuteronomy 12:1–28

The Levite *"within your gates"*, who *"has no portion nor inheritance with you"*, eats at the household feast (12, 18), and Israel is warned, *"Be careful that you don't forsake the Levite"* (19). The passage says nothing about the Levite's duties at the place itself.

See [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 12:1–28.

### Joshua commissioned — Numbers 27:12–23

Eleazar becomes the means of guidance for the new leader: Joshua is to stand before him while he
*inquires for him by the judgment of the Urim before Yahweh* (21). Leadership and priestly inquiry
are separated in a way they were not under Moses, who spoke with Yahweh directly.

See [[Joshua Commissioned]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:12–23.


### Duties and portions — Numbers 18:1–32

Priestly service and Levite assistance are gifts with distinct boundaries (1–7).
Responsibility for the sanctuary accompanies support from Israel’s holy gifts (8–24).
The no-inheritance rule does not erase the city houses and fields of Leviticus 25:32–34;
older descriptions of absolute propertylessness are too broad.

See [[The Duties and Portions of Priests and Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 18.

### Between the dead and the living — Numbers 16:41–50

The defended office becomes a means of rescue: Aaron runs among the people who accuse him
and makes atonement (41, 46–48). Numbers 16:40 guards priestly access; 16:47 shows that service
benefiting the congregation rather than merely protecting the priest’s status.

See [[Aaron Stands Between the Dead and the Living]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:41–50; comparisons as cited.

### Vows, valuations, and devoted things — Leviticus 27:1–34

The priest sets values, and his word stands (8, 12, 14). A field not redeemed passes to him at the
Jubilee (21), which sits uneasily with Numbers 18:20; both are recorded at the passage page.

See [[Vows, Valuations, and Devoted Things]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 27.

### Land and house redemption — Leviticus 25:23–34

Leviticus 25:32–34 explicitly gives Levites houses with perpetual redemption rights and
unsaleable surrounding fields. This contradicts the older connection’s unrestricted
claim that they have no property or fields: lack of a tribal territorial allotment is
not lack of every holding. The group called Levites here is not simply synonymous with
all the Aaronic priestly roles regulated in chapters 21–22.

See [[Redemption of Land and Houses]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 25.

### Lamps and weekly bread — Leviticus 24:1–9

Aaron tends the lamps and Aaron with his sons receives the bread (3–4, 9). Priestly
service includes maintaining furnishings and eating assigned holy food, alongside
the slaughter and blood rites treated earlier.

See [[The Lamps and the Bread Before Yahweh]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 24:1–9.

### First fruits and harvest provision — Leviticus 23:9–22

The unnamed priest waves the sheaf for the people's acceptance (11), then waves the
first-fruits bread with the peace-offering lambs (20). Holy dedication includes a
priestly portion; it does not mean everything presented is burned.

See [[First Fruits and the Feast of Weeks]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:9–22; contextual references as cited.


### Field and community — Leviticus 19:19–37

An unnamed priest makes atonement for the man with the trespass-offering ram (22). No individual
priest is identified, and the priest’s service does not cancel verse 20’s punishment.

See [[Holy Living in Field and Community]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19.


### Sacrifice and blood — Leviticus 17:1–16

The priest sprinkles the peace offering’s blood and burns its fat at the sanctuary
altar (6). Aaron and his sons receive the instruction alongside the people (2); the
chapter names no individual son or particular performance.

See [[The Place of Sacrifice and the Life in the Blood]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 17; comparisons as cited.


### The high priest alone — Leviticus 16:1–34

The rite belongs to one man: no one else may be in the tent while he atones (17). He must
first atone for himself and his house (6, 11), and the office passes to *"the priest, who is
anointed and who is consecrated to be priest in his father's place"* (32). Hebrews 9:7 reads
this restriction closely; see [[The High Priest Once a Year - Leviticus 16 and Hebrews 9]].

See [[The Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 16; comparisons as cited.


### Bodily discharges — Leviticus 15:1–33

The priest receives two birds at the Tent of Meeting and offers one as a sin offering
and one as a burnt offering after the seven-day counts (14–15, 29–30). No repeated
priestly diagnostic inspection is prescribed here as in the skin laws of chapter 13;
much of the washing and waiting is assigned to the affected people themselves.

See [[Bodily Discharges and Cleansing]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 15; comparisons as cited.

### Houses in the promised land — Leviticus 14:33–57

The owner reports a suspected plague; the priest examines and rules, orders seven-day
closure, removal and replastering, and demolition if the plague returns and spreads
(35–45). The conclusion frames these laws as teaching when something is unclean or
clean (54–57), extending the commission of 10:10–11 to houses.

See [[Mildew in a House]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 14:33–57; contextual references as cited.

### Cleansing after healing — Leviticus 14:1–32

The priest goes outside the camp to inspect the healed person, pronounces cleanness, and later
presents him and his offerings at the Tent of Meeting (3, 7, 11). The office combines
examination and restoration; it is not credited with causing the healing.

See [[The Cleansing of the Leper]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 14:1–32; comparisons as cited.


### Mildew in garments — Leviticus 13:47–59

The priest examines affected articles, orders washing and isolation, and assesses
spread, persistence, fading, and recurrence. His discriminating role extends beyond
bodies to possessions; the unit prescribes no sacrifice or atonement rite.

See [[Mildew in Garments]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 13:47–59; contextual references as noted.

### Examining skin conditions — Leviticus 13:1–46

The priest examines depth, hair, raw flesh, and spread, sometimes after one or two
seven-day periods. He pronounces status and orders isolation; the unit gives him no
procedure for curing the disease. The teaching commission of 10:10–11 now includes
case-by-case judgment, not only communication of household rules.

See [[Examining Diseases of the Skin]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 13:1–46.

### Purification after childbirth — Leviticus 12:1–8

The mother brings the offerings to the priest at the Tent of Meeting's door (6). The
priest offers before Yahweh and makes atonement for her cleansing (7–8); no priest is named.

See [[Purification After Childbirth]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 12.


### Clean and unclean animals — Leviticus 11:1–47

Leviticus 11:1–2 addresses Moses and Aaron together to teach Israel. The charge to
distinguish and teach in 10:10–11 receives practical content, without requiring a priest
to perform every household washing or inspect every meal.

See [[Clean and Unclean Animals]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 11.

- **Before the law, whoever holds the office holds it without explanation.** Melchizedek (Genesis
  14:18); Egypt's priests, who keep their land through Joseph's famine reforms because they *"ate
  their portion which Pharaoh gave them"* (47:22, 26); and Jethro, *"the priest of Midian"*
  (Exodus 3:1), who sacrifices to God and gives Moses his judicial system in Exodus 18. In each case the priest is a
  foreigner, and in none of them does Scripture question the office.
- **At Sinai the national calling and particular priestly roles coexist.** Exodus 19:6 offers
  the whole people a priestly kingdom; 19:22, 24 also names a group of priests whose identity
  is not explained. **Correction to the former sequence on this page:** the instructions
  appointing Aaron and his sons already occur in Exodus 28:1 and 29, before the golden calf
  in Exodus 32. Their later consecration in Leviticus 8–9 and the Levites' appointment in
  Numbers 3 are separate stages (those books by reference). The narrative does not say the
  national vocation was revoked and transferred to a tribe because of the calf. See
  [[Israel at Mount Sinai]].
- **Kingship and priesthood are kept apart in Israel, and the two men who cross the line are
  punished.** Saul offers at Gilgal and loses his dynasty (1 Samuel 13:8–14); Uzziah burns incense
  in the temple and is struck with a skin disease (2 Chronicles 26:16–21). Melchizedek held both
  offices in the same city a thousand years earlier, which is exactly why Psalm 110 can hand a
  Davidic king a priesthood without invoking Aaron.
- **The prophets attack the priesthood without abolishing it.** *"My people are destroyed for lack
  of knowledge. Because you have rejected knowledge, I will also reject you, that you may be no
  priest to me"* (Hosea 4:6); Malachi 2:1–9 on a covenant with Levi that the priests have corrupted;
  Zechariah 3 on Joshua the high priest re-clothed. And Ezekiel 44 and Zechariah 6:11–13 both
  imagine the offices recombined.
- **Psalm 110:4 makes the anomaly a category.** *"You are a priest forever in the order of
  Melchizedek"* — sworn, permanent, and not Levitical, said to a king.
- **Hebrews builds its whole argument on that verse.** A priesthood older than Levi's, held by a man
  who blessed Abraham and was tithed by him, is the greater one (7:4–10); because Genesis gives him
  no genealogy, birth, or death, he stands as the image of a priest who is not replaced (7:3, 23–25);
  and the sacrifice offered *"once for all"* (7:27; 9:26; 10:10) closes the system rather than
  continuing it.
- **And the New Testament then gives the office back to everyone.** *"You are a chosen race, a royal
  priesthood, a holy nation"* (1 Peter 2:9, quoting Exodus 19:6); *"made us kings and priests to his
  God and Father"* (Revelation 1:6; 5:10; 20:6). No individual Christian is called *hiereus* in the
  New Testament epistles; the congregation is.

### Young men at the altar — Exodus 24:5

At the covenant ceremony *"young men of the children of Israel"* offer the sacrifices (24:5), and Moses
handles the blood. Aaron and his sons are present only among those who go up to see God (24:1, 9). The
priests mentioned at 19:22, 24 are not mentioned. The text does not explain why young men act; the wiki
does not supply a reason. See [[The Blood of the Covenant]] and [[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]].

### The altar, court, and maintained light — Exodus 27:1–21

Aaron and his sons are assigned the lamp outside the veil in Exodus 27:21. Israel supplies
the oil; this family tends it from evening to morning on the people's behalf. The instruction
precedes the explicit appointment in 28:1 and the consecration instructions in chapter 29.
It is not an account of ordination or a claim that every earlier priest was Aaronic. The
identity of the priests already mentioned in 19:22, 24 remains unexplained. See
[[The Altar and the Court]] and [[From Evening to Morning - The Lamp in Exodus 27 and Leviticus 24]].

### The priestly garments — Exodus 28:1–43

Exodus 28:1 is the first command in Exodus that gives *"the priest's office"* to named people:
Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar, brought near *"from among the children of Israel"*.
After a kingdom of priests (19:6) and sacrifices by young men (24:5), one family is now set
apart and dressed for service. The high priest's clothing carries the people's names, their
judgment, and the iniquity of their holy gifts. The office is defined by what the priest
*bears* before Yahweh. The ordination is still to come (chapter 29). See [[The Priestly Garments]] and [[Urim and Thummim - The Breastplate of Judgment and Later Inquiry]].

### The ordination of the priests — Exodus 29:1–46

Exodus 29 prescribes how the named family becomes priests: washing, clothing, anointing, three
offerings, blood on ear, thumb, and toe, a shared meal, and seven days at the tent's door. The
Hebrew idiom behind *"consecrate"* is *fill the hand* (*ûmillēʾtā yad*, 29:9, MAM). Moses performs the
rite before any priest exists. The office provides for its own succession, since Aaron's garments
pass to his sons (29:29–30), and it begins with a sin offering for the priests themselves. Its
first standing duty is the daily lamb, morning and evening (29:38–42). Leviticus 8 narrates the
performance. See [[The Ordination of the Priests]] and [[Ordination Commanded and Performed - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 8]].

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30:1–38

Exodus 30 adds the priests' inner duties. Aaron burns incense on the gold altar every morning and
evening, *"a perpetual incense before Yahweh"* (30:7–8), and makes atonement on its horns once a
year (30:10). Aaron and his sons wash hands and feet at the basin before entering the tent or
approaching the altar, *"that they not die"* (30:19–21). The holy oil anoints them (30:30), and its
recipe and the incense's are reserved: no one may copy them or put the oil *"on a stranger"*
(30:32–38). Who may burn incense becomes a recurring test of the office later in Scripture. See
[[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] and
[[Strange Incense - Exodus 30.9, Leviticus 10, Numbers 16, and Uzziah]].

### Garments made by people outside the priestly family — Exodus 31

Aaron and his sons receive garments made under the commission given to Bezalel of Judah, Oholiab of
Dan, and the wise-hearted workers (31:2, 6, 10). Making priestly clothing does not confer the
priestly office on the maker. The chapter assigns craftsmanship and anticipates priestly service
without narrating an ordination.

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Aaron makes the calf while the instructions for his appointment have already been given (28:1;
32:1–6). Levi’s descendants answer Moses and are called to consecrate themselves after the
killings (32:26–29). **Qualification of earlier wording here:** this passage links loyalty,
consecration, and blessing; it does not perform the prescribed Aaronic ordination or say the whole
nation’s vocation was transferred to Levi because of the calf. See [[The Golden Calf]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

The public list expressly includes holy garments for Aaron the priest and garments for his
sons (35:19). After Aaron's role in the calf, the narrative still names him for priestly service.
This is evidence that the assignment persists, not a separately narrated pardon or ordination.
The wider congregation supplies the materials; making or donating them does not confer that office.

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38:1–31

Ithamar, identified as Aaron the priest’s son, is named in the count for the Levites’ service
(38:21). The basin is made from the ministering women’s mirrors (38:8). Their ministry is affirmed
without defined duties or an assignment of Aaron’s priestly office. Accounting, making,
contribution, and ritual service should not be collapsed into one role.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

Exodus 39:1–31 makes the clothing prescribed in chapter 28 for Aaron and his sons.
Representation, service, and differentiated garments become material preparations. Calling them
holy does not mean the seven-day ordination of chapter 29 has occurred: no dressing, washing,
anointing, or sacrifice is performed in this manufacturing account.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The inventory includes holy garments for Aaron the priest and garments for his sons
(39:41). They are presented with the sanctuary equipment and approved as work. The blessing of
39:43 is Moses’ act toward the people, not Aaron’s priestly blessing. No washing, dressing,
anointing, or sacrificial ordination is performed in this unit; 40:12–15 still prescribes the
priests’ preparation.

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

Aaron and his sons are to be washed, clothed, and anointed, with priestly service continuing
through their generations (40:12–15). Verse 16 broadly affirms obedience, but 40:17–33
details erection and service rather than retelling the seven-day rite prescribed in
chapter 29. Moses burns incense and offers at the altar (40:27, 29); Moses,
Aaron, and the sons all wash at the basin (40:31). See
[[Ordination Commanded and Performed - Exodus 29 and Leviticus 8]].

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

### The burnt offering — Leviticus 1:1–17

The regulated offering retains active lay participation: the livestock offerer brings, lays a hand
on the cattle victim, kills, and prepares the animal; the priests handle blood and the altar. In the
bird case the priest performs the killing and preparation described (1:15–17). Priesthood does not
mean that every action in every sacrifice is exclusively priestly.

See [[The Burnt Offering]] . Source: stored WEB Leviticus 1; comparisons identified on the passage
page.

### The meal offering — Leviticus 2

The priest brings the gift to the altar and burns its memorial; Aaron and his sons receive the most holy remainder (2:2–3, 8–10). Priestly service includes receiving a sacred portion, alongside handling offerings. See [[The Meal Offering]].

### The peace offering — Leviticus 3

The offerer lays a hand on the victim and kills it; Aaron's sons handle its blood and the priest burns the specified portions (3:2–5, 8–11, 13–16). Shared participation does not erase priestly responsibility. The priestly meat shares are specified later in 7:28–34.

See [[The Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 3; contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Priest and congregation — Leviticus 4:1–21

The anointed priest is both potentially liable for sin affecting the people (3) and the minister
who makes atonement for the congregation (20). Office does not confer moral immunity. The elders
lay hands on the congregation’s bull (15), while the anointed priest handles its sanctuary blood
(16–18).

See [[The Sin Offering for Priest and Congregation]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:1–21;
contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Ruler and individual — Leviticus 4:22–35

The offerer lays a hand on the victim and slaughters it; the priest applies blood
to the burnt-offering altar and makes atonement (24–26, 29–31, 33–35). The priest is not
specified as the anointed priest in these cases, unlike the preceding bull offerings.

See [[The Sin Offering for Ruler and Individual]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:22–35; contextual
comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Confession and means — Leviticus 5:1–13

The priest offers the sin-offering bird first, then the burnt offering; with flour he burns a memorial handful and receives the remainder (8–13).

See [[Confession and Offerings According to Means]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 5:1–13.

### Holy things and unknown guilt — Leviticus 5:14–19

The priest receives the holy-things restitution with its added fifth and makes atonement with the ram (16); the second case also brings the ram to the priest (18). No priest is named here.

See [[The Trespass Offering for Holy Things and Unknown Guilt]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 5:14–19.

### Fraud, restitution, and forgiveness — Leviticus 6:1–7

The ram is brought to the priest, who makes atonement before Yahweh (6–7). Unlike the holy-things
case of 5:16, the repayment with its fifth goes to the owner (5), not to the priest.

See [[Restitution for Fraud and False Oaths]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:1–7; contextual comparisons documented there.

### Altar fire and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:8–23

Priestly work includes tending fire, moving ashes with a change of garments, and eating authorized portions in the court. Anointed succession carries an offering obligation; the priest’s own meal offering is not his food.

See [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:8–23; comparisons documented there.

### Holy flesh and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:24–7:10

Eligible male priests eat most holy flesh in a holy place; the officiant receives the assigned
offering and the burnt offering’s skin. Leviticus 7:9–10 juxtaposes prepared offerings for the
officiant with oil-mixed or dry grain for all sons equally. The exact distribution is not fully
explained.

See [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:24–7:10.

### Thanksgiving and the peace-offering meal — Leviticus 7:11–21

The priest who sprinkles peace-offering blood receives one from each accompanying
bread offering (7:14). Peace-offering meat is available to clean participants (19),
which differs from the male-priest rule for sin and trespass portions in 6:29; 7:6.
The broader permission does not cancel the restrictions on those other offerings.

See [[Thanksgiving, Vows, and the Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6–7.

### Fat, blood, and priestly shares — Leviticus 7:22–38

The breast belongs to Aaron and his sons; the right thigh belongs to the son who offers blood
and fat (31–34). These portions are Yahweh’s gift from Israel and are associated with anointing
and continuing generations (35–36), not unrestricted priestly ownership of the sacrifice.

See [[Fat, Blood, and the Priests’ Share]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 7:22–38.

### Aaron and his sons consecrated — Leviticus 8:1–36

The office requires public installation: washing, clothing, anointing, sacrifices, and seven
days at the tent’s door. Moses officiates while Aaron and his sons receive consecration. Their
own need for atonement (34) precedes their inaugural service in chapter 9.

See [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 8; Exodus 28:1; 29; Leviticus 7:38.

### The eighth-day service — Leviticus 9

After consecration, Aaron officiates and his sons deliver blood and portions. His own offering
precedes the people's; priestly office does not remove his need for atonement (9:7–21). See
[[The Eighth Day and the Appearing Glory]] .

### Strange fire and the surviving priests — Leviticus 10:1–11

Consecrated status does not authorize any offering a priest chooses (1–2). The office also requires sober entry, distinguishing holy from common and clean from unclean, and teaching Israel the statutes mediated by Moses (8–11).

See [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:1–11.

### Eating the portions and answering for them — Leviticus 10:12–20

The surviving priests must eat the most holy meal offering in a holy place and the breast and thigh in a clean place, with Aaron's daughters included for the latter (12–15). Moses treats the priests' eating of the people's sin offering as part of their service "to bear the iniquity of the congregation" (17), and holds them to it; Aaron's appeal to the day's events is accepted (19–20).

See [[The Uneaten Sin Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:12–20.

### The census — Numbers 1:1–54

The Levites are not counted with the fighters (47–49). They are appointed to the tabernacle (50), and Aaron is not called a priest in this unit. The difference between Levite and priest is drawn in chapters 3–4.

See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 1.

### The camp — Numbers 2:1–34

The Levites travel with the Tent, in the middle of the camps (17), and stay outside the count (33). Chapter 10 details the march differently. See [[As They Encamp, So They Set Out - Numbers 2.17 and 10.14-28]].

See [[The Camp Around the Tent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 2.

### The Levites given — Numbers 3:1–13

The line narrows to Eleazar and Ithamar (4). The Levites are placed under them to keep the Tent, but the priesthood itself stays with Aaron and his sons, and an outsider who comes near dies (10). Levites serve; they do not become priests. Verse 3 calls the sons *anointed*, and WEB's *consecrated* is the idiom *filled their hand* (WLC).

See [[The Generations of Aaron and the Levites Given]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 3.

### The Levites ranked — Numbers 3:14–51

Three Levite princes stand under Eleazar, *prince of the princes* (24, 30, 32, 35), and the east side belongs to Moses and the priests (38). The redemption money goes to Aaron and his sons (48, 51), the first income in Numbers to reach them. The order is now priest, Levite prince, clan.

See [[The Levites and the Firstborn Counted]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 3.

### The priests answer for the bearers — Numbers 4:1–49

Only Aaron and his sons handle the holy things, and they appoint every man (5, 19, 27). Eleazar has the oversight and Ithamar has two houses (16, 28, 33), so the Levites' work depends on the priests' work.

See [[The Service of Kohath, Gershon, and Merari]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 4.

### What is given to the priest — Numbers 5:5–10

A payment with no human recipient goes to the priest, along with the ram (8), and what is presented to him is his (9–10).

See [[The Unclean Sent Out and Wrongs Repaid]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 5.

### The priest at the rite — Numbers 5:11–31

The priest does every act: receives, sets her before Yahweh, mixes, loosens, swears, writes, blots, burns, makes her drink (15–26). He gives no ruling. The last verb of decision belongs to Yahweh (21).

See [[The Suspected Wife]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 5.

### A lay person under priestly rules — Numbers 6:1–21

The Nazirite is not a priest, but is held to wine, corpse, and head rules that run alongside priestly law, and the portions on the Nazirite's palms (19) recall Exodus 29:24 and Leviticus 8:27. The priest still does what only a priest does (10–11, 16–17, 20). See [[The Nazirite and the Priest - Numbers 6 and Leviticus 8, 10, and 21]].

See [[The Nazirite Vow]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 6.

### Words to say — Numbers 6:22–27

Aaron and his sons are given a blessing to speak. They do not bless on their own account: they put Yahweh's name on Israel, and he blesses (27). Exodus 28:12, 29 has Aaron carrying Israel's names before Yahweh, so the two passages can be read as the office running in both directions, though neither cites the other.

See [[Yahweh Bless You and Keep You]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 6.

### Silence at the altar — Numbers 7:8

The chapter is about an altar and names no priest at work; Aaron and Ithamar appear only in *Ithamar the son of Aaron the priest* (8). The initiative in it lies with the tribal heads. The text may assume the priests' work, since Leviticus 1–7 has set it out.

See [[The Offerings of the Twelve Princes]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 7.

### Two grades — Numbers 8:5–26

The Levites' rite differs from that of the priests at every point that matters: cleansing for sanctifying, washed clothes for new garments, no oil and no blood on the body. They are *given to Aaron and to his sons* and work *before* them (19, 22). See [[Cleansed, Not Anointed - The Levites of Numbers 8 and the Priests of Leviticus 8]].

See [[The Lamps and the Levites Cleansed]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 8.

### A statute for the instruments — Numbers 10:8

The trumpets go to *the sons of Aaron, the priests*, as a statute forever. The priest now gives Israel its signals as well as its offerings and blessing. Later texts do not all keep to this (2 Kings 11:14).

See [[The Two Silver Trumpets]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 10.

### Priests in the war — Numbers 31

Phinehas goes with the army holding the sanctuary vessels and the trumpets (6); Eleazar gives the
purification statute (21–24) and receives Yahweh's share of the plunder (29, 41). The Levites receive one
in fifty from the congregation's half (30, 47).

See [[The War Against Midian]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 31.

### The priests teach the leprosy law — Deuteronomy 24:8

*"Do according to all that the priests the Levites teach you. As I commanded them, so you shall observe
to do."* *Yôrû*, hiphil of *yārâ*, *teach*, is the verb of 17:11 (WLC). Deuteronomy assigns the case to
the priests without restating the procedure. See [[Pledges, Wages, Justice, and Gleaning]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 24:6–22.

  [[Melchizedek]], and a patriarch marries into it.** [[Potiphera]] is ***kōhēn ʾŌn*** — **and *kōhēn*

  does not reach for a pejorative**, and states the office flatly, twice, with no qualification. **[[On]]
