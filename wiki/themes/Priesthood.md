# Priesthood

## Definition / overview

Someone appointed to stand between God and people — to offer what people bring and to carry back
what God gives.

The Hebrew is *kōhēn*, and its first occurrence in the Bible is Genesis 14:18: *"Melchizedek king of
Salem brought out bread and wine: and he was priest of God Most High."* Everything Israel's
priesthood will later be defined by is absent from that verse. There is no sanctuary, no altar
mentioned, no sacrifice, no law, no tribe, no anointing, no genealogy, and no Israel — Levi's
great-grandfather is the man standing in front of him. The office arrives in Scripture attached to a
Canaanite king, and the Bible's two great arguments about priesthood (Psalm 110 and Hebrews 5–7)
both work from precisely that. See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] and [[Melchizedek]].

**The point of a priest is direction.** Sacrifice has been happening since Genesis 4 without one:
Cain and Abel bring offerings, Noah builds an altar, Job sacrifices for his children, and Abram
builds three altars — and not one of them is called a priest. What Melchizedek does that they do not
is move in both directions. He *brings out* to Abram, and he blesses Abram *of God Most High*; then
he blesses God on Abram's behalf; then he receives a tenth. Something goes up through him and
something comes down through him. That two-way motion is what the word adds. See
[[Sacrifice Before the Priesthood]] and [[Mediation and Advocacy]].

## Development across Scripture

- **Before the law, whoever holds the office holds it without explanation.** Melchizedek (Genesis
  14:18); Egypt's priests, who keep their land through Joseph's famine reforms because they *"ate
  their portion which Pharaoh gave them"* (47:22, 26); and Jethro, *"the priest of Midian"*
  (Exodus 3:1), who sacrifices to God and gives Moses his judicial system in Exodus 18. In each case the priest is a
  foreigner, and in none of them does Scripture question the office.
- **At Sinai the nation is offered it and then a tribe receives it.** *"You shall be to me a kingdom
  of priests and a holy nation"* (Exodus 19:6) precedes the golden calf; after it, Aaron and his
  sons are consecrated (Exodus 28–29; Leviticus 8–9) and Levi is set apart (Numbers 3:5–13). What
  the office now includes is stated: offering sacrifice, distinguishing holy from common and clean
  from unclean, teaching the law, carrying the Urim and Thummim, and pronouncing the blessing of
  Numbers 6:24–26. It is hereditary, tribal, and bounded by death — the point Hebrews 7:23 later
  presses.
- **Kingship and priesthood are kept apart in Israel, and the two men who cross the line are
  punished.** Saul offers at Gilgal and loses his dynasty (1 Samuel 13:8–14); Uzziah burns incense
  in the temple and is struck with a skin disease (2 Chronicles 26:16–21). Melchizedek held both
  offices in the same city a thousand years earlier, which is exactly why Psalm 110 can hand a
  Davidic king a priesthood without invoking Aaron.
- **The prophets attack the priesthood without abolishing it.** *"My people are destroyed for lack
  of knowledge. Because you have rejected knowledge, I will also reject you, that you may be no
  priest to me"* (Hosea 4:6); Malachi 2:1–9 on a covenant with Levi that the priests have corrupted;
  Zechariah 3 on Joshua the high priest re-clothed. And Ezekiel 44 and Zechariah 6:11–13 both
  imagine the offices recombined.
- **Psalm 110:4 makes the anomaly a category.** *"You are a priest forever in the order of
  Melchizedek"* — sworn, permanent, and not Levitical, said to a king.
- **Hebrews builds its whole argument on that verse.** A priesthood older than Levi's, held by a man
  who blessed Abraham and was tithed by him, is the greater one (7:4–10); because Genesis gives him
  no genealogy, birth, or death, he stands as the image of a priest who is not replaced (7:3, 23–25);
  and the sacrifice offered *"once for all"* (7:27; 9:26; 10:10) closes the system rather than
  continuing it.
- **And the New Testament then gives the office back to everyone.** *"You are a chosen race, a royal
  priesthood, a holy nation"* (1 Peter 2:9, quoting Exodus 19:6); *"made us kings and priests to his
  God and Father"* (Revelation 1:6; 5:10; 20:6). No individual Christian is called *hiereus* in the
  New Testament epistles; the congregation is.

## Key passages

- Genesis 14:18–20 — the first priest, [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]
- Exodus 19:6 — a kingdom of priests
- Exodus 28–29; Leviticus 8–9 — Aaron and his sons consecrated
- Numbers 3:5–13; Deuteronomy 33:8–11 — Levi set apart
- 1 Samuel 2:35 — *"I will raise up a faithful priest"*
- Psalm 110:4 — the order of Melchizedek
- Hosea 4:6; Malachi 2:1–9 — the indictment
- Hebrews 5:1–10; 7:1–28 — the argument from Genesis 14
- 1 Peter 2:5, 9; Revelation 1:6; 5:10 — the priesthood of the whole people
