# Names and Naming

## Definition / overview

**Genesis is a book that stops to explain names, and the explanations are almost never linguistics.**

Roughly forty times, the narrative pauses to say why somebody or somewhere is called what it is
called. *"He named him Noah, saying, 'This one will comfort us'"*; *"therefore its name was called
Babel"*; *"you shall call his name Ishmael, because Yahweh has heard your affliction"*; *"she named him
Judah"*. **The formula is standard — a name, then *kî*, *because*, then a clause containing a verb that
sounds like the name** — and what the clause supplies is not an etymology but **a verdict on the
moment.**

**The technical term is *paronomasia*, and the honest description is that the puns are frequently
loose.** *Bābel* is not from *bālal*, *confuse*; it is Akkadian *bāb-ili*, *gate of god*, which is
exactly what the builders were after and exactly what Genesis 11:9 declines to say. *Yiśḥāq* is *he
laughs*, but Genesis glosses it four different times with four different laughs (17:17; 18:12; 21:6),
and the one who does the laughing changes each time. **Genesis is not doing philology. It is doing
memory** — attaching a sentence to a name so that saying the name says the sentence.

**And the naming is an act, not a label.** The first thing the man does in the garden is name the
animals (2:19–20), which Genesis presents as authority exercised; God renames Abram, Sarai, and Jacob
at the three points where the covenant is restated; and the one thing [[Hagar]] does that nobody else in
the Bible does is name God back — *"You are a God who sees"* (16:13). **To name is to have standing over
what is named**, which is why it matters so much who does it, and why the exceptions are loud.

## Development across Scripture

### The three registers Genesis uses

**1. The name as a record of what happened.** Cain (*qānîtî*, *I have gotten*, 4:1); Seth (*šāt*, *he
has appointed*, 4:25); Noah (5:29, the one gloss in the whole of Genesis 5); Peleg (*"in his days the
earth was divided"*, 10:25); Babel (11:9); Ishmael (16:11); Isaac (21:6); Edom (25:30); Beersheba
(21:31; 26:33); Bethel (28:19); Peniel (32:30). **The name is a receipt.**

**2. The name as a claim on the future.** Abraham, *father of a multitude*, given thirteen years before
the son arrives (17:5); Israel, given at the ford before anything it names has happened (32:28); and
**[[Joseph]] — *may he add* — a name for a child his mother does not yet have** (30:24). **These are
names that outrun their evidence**, and the book tracks whether they come true.

**3. The name as an argument.** **This is the register Genesis 29:31–30:24 runs on**, and it is the
densest naming passage in the Bible: **twelve births, twelve namings, and every one spoken by a
woman**, each about her own standing in a household. *Now my husband will love me.* *Yahweh has heard
that I am hated.* *God has judged me.* *I have wrestled with my sister, and have prevailed.* *How
fortunate!* *The daughters will call me happy.* **The twelve tribes of Israel are named out of an
argument between two sisters**, and the nation carries the vocabulary of that argument for the rest of
Scripture. See [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]].

### Who is allowed to name

**Mothers do most of it in Genesis, and that is worth stating because it cuts against the book's usual
distribution of speech.** Eve names Cain and Seth; Lot's daughters name Moab and Ben-Ammi; Leah, Rachel,
and (through them) Bilhah's and Zilpah's sons account for twelve namings in twenty-six verses; Rachel
names Ben Oni with her last breath. **Fathers name less often and sometimes overrule** — Abraham names
Isaac on God's instruction (17:19; 21:3), and Jacob overrules Rachel at 35:18. **God names or renames
five times**, and each is a covenant hinge.

**And the anomalies are where the theme earns its keep:**

- **[[Levi]] (29:34)** — *"Therefore his name was called Levi"*, with **no stated subject**, where the
  other eleven have *she named him*. The Samaritan, Greek, and Latin traditions read *she called*; the
  Masoretic Text does not. **The one naming in the list with no agent belongs to the priestly tribe.**
- **[[Dinah]] (30:21)** — **named with no gloss at all**, the only birth in the passage without one, in
  a chapter that supplies eleven. Her name is the feminine of *Dan*, whose gloss the same passage gives.
- **[[Ishmael]] (16:11)** — **named by God before birth, through the angel, to a slave**, so that a boy
  carries a permanent record of God having heard an Egyptian woman's affliction.
- **[[Hagar]] (16:13)** — **the only person in Scripture who gives God a name**, and Genesis lets it
  stand.
- **[[Esau]] / Edom (25:25, 30; 36:1)** — a man named for how he looked and renamed for what he ate, and
  the second name becomes a nation.

### Outside Genesis

- **Exodus 3:13–15** — Moses asks for a name and gets a sentence, *"I AM WHO I AM"*, which is the theme
  meeting its limit. See [[The Names of God]].
- **The prophets name children as messages.** Isaiah's *Shear-Jashub* and *Maher-Shalal-Hash-Baz*
  (Isaiah 7:3; 8:1–4), and Hosea's *Jezreel*, *Lo-Ruhamah* and *Lo-Ammi* — *not pitied*, *not my
  people* — reversed at Hosea 2:23. **A child is used as a public sentence**, which is Genesis's
  register 1 applied to a nation.
- **Renaming as conquest.** Pharaoh Necho renames Eliakim *Jehoiakim* (2 Kings 23:34); Nebuchadnezzar's
  officer renames Daniel and his friends (Daniel 1:7); Pharaoh renames Joseph *Zaphenath-Paneah*
  (41:45). **Taking a name is what an empire does to a person.**
- **1 Samuel 25:25** — *"as his name is, so is he: Nabal is his name, and folly is with him"*, the
  Bible's most explicit statement that a name is a character.
- **The New Testament keeps register 2.** *"You shall call his name Jesus, for it is he who shall save
  his people from their sins"* (Matthew 1:21) — *Yēšûaʿ*, *Yahweh saves*, in the Genesis formula
  exactly. Simon becomes Peter (Matthew 16:18); Saul is Paul; and Revelation 2:17 promises *"a new name
  written, which no one knows but he who receives it"* — **a name that is not an argument with anyone.**

## Key passages

- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 29:31–30:24; **the densest naming passage in the Bible.**
  Twelve births, twelve names, eleven glosses, all spoken by women, and the tribal map of Israel laid
  down in the vocabulary of a household quarrel
- [[The Garden of Eden]] — Genesis 2:19–20, naming as the first human act and as authority exercised
- [[Cain and Abel]] — Genesis 4:1, *qānîtî*, and the first naming speech in Scripture
- [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]] — Genesis 4:25, *"God has appointed me another child"*
- [[From Adam to Noah]] — Genesis 5:29, **the one gloss in a chapter of ten generations**, and it is
  about the cursed ground
- [[The Tower of Babel]] — Genesis 11:9, a pun that contradicts the name's actual etymology, deliberately
- [[Hagar and Ishmael]] — Genesis 16:11, 13; **a name given by God to a slave's son, and a name given to
  God by the slave**
- [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]] — Genesis 17:5, 15, 19; two renamings and a name assigned
  before conception
- [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]] — Genesis 21:6, the fourth and last of Isaac's four
  laughs
- [[Jacob and Esau and the Sold Birthright]] — Genesis 25:25–26, 30; *Esau*, *Jacob*, and *Edom*, three
  glosses in two verses, and one of them is a prophecy of the plot
- [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]] — Genesis 28:19, a place renamed, and the name outliving what it meant
- [[Jacob's Flocks]] — Genesis 30:37; **the theme in its purely silent form: a pun Genesis makes and
  never glosses.** The verse that begins Laban's undoing contains the root ***l-b-n*** three times —
  ***libneh*** (the white poplar or storax), ***ləbānôt*** (the *white* streaks peeled into it), and
  ***hallābān*** (*the white* made to appear) — and ***Lābān*** is that adjective exactly. **A man
  strips bark to bare pale wood in order to strip *White* of a herd**, and the narrator says nothing.
  The same verse cuts rods of ***lûz***, *almond*, **which is the name Bethel went by before this man
  renamed it** (28:19). **Genesis glosses names when a mother speaks and withholds the gloss when the
  wordplay is its own**
- [[Lot and His Daughters]] — Genesis 19:37–38, Moab and Ben-Ammi, the two glosses Genesis gives without
  comment
- Genesis 32:28, 30 — **Israel** and **Peniel**, the two namings at the Jabbok *(not yet ingested)*
- Genesis 35:18 — Ben Oni renamed Benjamin: **the one naming in Genesis that is overruled** *(not yet
  ingested)*
- Genesis 41:45, 51–52 — an Egyptian name imposed, and two Hebrew names given in reply *(not yet
  ingested)*
- Genesis 49 — Jacob's blessing, which re-puns most of the twelve names given at 29:31–30:24 *(not yet
  ingested)*
- Exodus 3:13–15 — the name that is a sentence
- Hosea 1:4–9; 2:23; Isaiah 7:3; 8:1–4 — children named as public messages, and one reversal
- 1 Samuel 25:25 — *"as his name is, so is he"*
- Matthew 1:21; 16:18; Revelation 2:17; 3:12 — the formula kept, and a name promised that argues with
  nobody
