# Deception

## Definition / overview

Producing a false belief in someone else — and in Genesis, usually without saying anything false.

The theme's distinctive shape in this book is not the flat lie. It is the true statement aimed to
mislead, the withheld half of a fact, the substituted person, the borrowed clothing, the planted
object. *"Please say that you are my sister"* (12:13) is the founding case, and it is founding
precisely because Sarai **is** his sister — *"the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my
mother"* (20:12). Nothing untrue is uttered and everything about the situation is falsified. Genesis
returns to that structure again and again, and it is one of the more reliable signals that the book
knows exactly what it is doing with its patriarchs.

**Two Hebrew words carry it.** *Rāmâ*, *to deceive or beguile*, which is what Jacob accuses Laban of
after the wedding night (29:25) and what Jacob's own sons do to Shechem (34:13); and *mirmâ*,
*deceit*, the noun Isaac uses of Jacob at 27:35 — *"Your brother came with deceit, and has taken
away your blessing"* — and the noun attached to Shechem's murderers at 34:13. The same root tracks
the family for three generations, and the book applies it to the chosen line more often than to
anyone else.

**The question the deceived ask is the same question every time.** Pharaoh to Abram: *"What is this
that you have done to me?"* (12:18). Abimelech to Abraham: *"What have you done to us?"* (20:9), and
to Isaac: *"What is this you have done to us?"* (26:10). And Jacob to Laban, the morning after the
wedding: *"What is this you have done to me? … Why then have you deceived me?"* (29:25) — the
patriarch who has spent two chapters deceiving people asking it for the first time as the injured
party, in the exact words an Egyptian king used on his grandfather.

**And it starts before the family exists.** *"The serpent deceived me, and I ate"* (3:13), where the
verb is *nāšāʾ*, to beguile, and where the serpent's method was already the Genesis method: not a
false statement but a true one wrongly framed — *"you won't really die"* is, on the story's own
outcome, arguably accurate about the day in question and comprehensively false about everything
else. See [[The Fall]].

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 3:1–13** — the serpent's question, the amended prohibition, and Eve's *"the serpent
  deceived me"* — the first deception, worked by shading a true statement. See [[The Fall]].
- **Genesis 4:8–9** — Cain speaks to his brother, kills him in the field, and answers God's question
  with a question: *"I don't know. Am I my brother's keeper?"* The first lie told to God's face.
  See [[Cain and Abel]].
- **Genesis 12:10–20** — Abram's sister ruse in Egypt, and his first recorded speech in Scripture.
  The half-truth as policy: 20:13 has him admit it was a standing arrangement made at the start of
  the journey. See [[Abram in Egypt]].
- **Genesis 19:30–38** — the daughters in the cave, and the one deception in Genesis worked with no
  words spoken to the person deceived at all: *"Come, let's make our father drink wine"* is said to
  a sister, and Lot is told nothing on either night. *"He didn't know when she lay down, nor when
  she arose"*, twice. See [[Lot and His Daughters]].
- **Genesis 20:1–18** — the same ruse in Gerar, with Abraham's defence: *"she is indeed my sister"*,
  and *"Surely the fear of God is not in this place"*. Abimelech's reply is the one moral vocabulary
  in the episode: *"In the integrity of my heart and the innocence of my hands I have done this"*.
  **It is also where Genesis supplies the theme's own definition of the method**: the half was true,
  and God nonetheless treats what the king believed as false enough to kill him for — *"Behold, you
  are a dead man … For she is a man's wife"* (20:3). See [[Abraham and Abimelech]].
- **Genesis 26:6–11** — Isaac does it too, in the same town, and Abimelech catches him at a window.
  The trait is inherited before Jacob is old enough to demonstrate it *(not yet ingested)*.
- **Genesis 27** — Rebekah and Jacob, goatskins on the arms and Esau's clothes on the back: *"I am
  Esau your firstborn"*, which is the one place in the sequence where a flat lie is told, and the
  blind man's four senses are worked one at a time *(not yet ingested)*.
- **Genesis 29:21–27** — Laban substitutes Leah for Rachel in the dark, and the deceiver is deceived
  by the same device he used, an older sibling passed off as a younger one: *"It is not done so in
  our place, to give the younger before the firstborn"* *(not yet ingested)*.
- **Genesis 30:25–43; 31:20** — the peeled rods and the speckled flocks, and *"Jacob deceived Laban
  the Syrian, in that he didn't tell him that he was running away"*; then Rachel sitting on the
  household gods in the camel's saddle *(not yet ingested)*.
- **Genesis 34:13** — *"The sons of Jacob answered Shechem and Hamor his father with deceit"* — the
  covenant sign itself turned into an ambush. This is the point at which the family's habit becomes
  lethal *(not yet ingested)*.
- **Genesis 37:31–33** — the coat dipped in goat's blood and shown to Jacob, who is deceived by a
  garment and a goat exactly as he once deceived his father with a garment and a goat *(not yet
  ingested)*.
- **Genesis 38:13–26** — Tamar veiled at the roadside, and Judah's *"She is more righteous than I"*.
  The one deception in Genesis the text openly vindicates *(not yet ingested)*.
- **Genesis 42–44** — Joseph, unrecognised, running an elaborate deception on his brothers for three
  chapters, including a planted cup; the only sustained one performed by a character the book
  approves of, and it is aimed at producing repentance *(not yet ingested)*.
- **Exodus 1:15–21** — the Hebrew midwives lie to Pharaoh and *"God dealt well with the midwives"*,
  which is the Torah's clearest statement that the evaluation depends on who is being protected.
- **Joshua 2:4–6; 9:3–15** — Rahab hides the spies and misdirects the search; the Gibeonites arrive
  in worn-out shoes with mouldy bread. The first is commended in Hebrews 11:31 and James 2:25; the
  second sticks, because Israel *"didn't ask counsel from Yahweh's mouth"*.
- **Exodus 20:16; Leviticus 19:11** — the law arrives and names it: *"You shall not give false
  testimony against your neighbor"*; *"You shall not deal falsely, nor lie to one another."*
- **Proverbs 12:22; 6:16–19** — *"Lying lips are an abomination to Yahweh"*, and *"a false witness
  who utters lies"* among the seven things he hates.
- **John 8:44** — the serpent's descendant identified: *"he is a liar, and the father of lies."*
- **Acts 5:1–11; Colossians 3:9** — Ananias and Sapphira, and *"Don't lie to one another, seeing that
  you have put off the old man with his doings."*

**The pattern in one line:** Genesis is a book in which the chosen line lies its way through four
generations, is repeatedly caught by outsiders who behave better, and is not once disowned for it —
and the law that finally forbids the practice arrives only after the family it would have condemned
has become a nation.

## Key passages

- [[Abram in Egypt]] — Genesis 12:10–20, the first deceit by a patriarch, and the half-truth as its
  characteristic form
- [[The Fall]] — Genesis 3:1–13, where the method is introduced by the serpent
- [[Cain and Abel]] — Genesis 4:9, the first lie told directly to God
- [[Lot and His Daughters]] — Genesis 19:32–35, the Genesis shape at its barest: nothing false is
  said because nothing is said at all. The wine is administered on two consecutive nights, and the
  narrator records the result twice in almost identical words — *"He didn't know when she lay down,
  nor when she arose"*
- [[Abraham and Abimelech]] — Genesis 20:1–18, the ruse a second time and the only place Genesis
  lets the deceived party state the charge and the deceiver answer it. Abraham's three-part reply is
  the fullest defence of a deception anywhere in the book — a false premise about the place, a true
  technicality about the kinship, and the admission that it had been standing policy since 12:1
- [[The Treaty at Beersheba]] — Genesis 21:23, the theme named by its absence and by a first
  occurrence. *"swear to me here by God that you will not deal falsely with me"* — *šāqar*, the
  verb's first appearance in Scripture, and the root behind the ninth commandment's *false witness*
  (Exodus 20:16) and Leviticus 19:11's *"you shall not deal falsely"*. **The man who asks for the
  oath is the man who was deceived one chapter earlier, and the oath is what he now needs instead of
  trust.** Genesis does not connect the two scenes out loud; it puts them eleven verses apart
- Genesis 26:6–11 — the ruse a third time, inherited *(not yet ingested)*
- Genesis 27:1–45 — the stolen blessing, and the one flat lie in the sequence *(not yet ingested)*
- Genesis 29:21–27 — Laban's substitution, and the deceiver deceived *(not yet ingested)*
- Genesis 37:31–33 — the coat, the goat, and the father deceived by his own devices *(not yet
  ingested)*
- Exodus 1:15–21 — the midwives, and God dealing well with them
- John 8:44 — *"the father of lies"*

## Related themes

- [[Language and Speech]] — words as the medium of blessing, curse, and creation, and here of
  falsification; Genesis's interest in what speech can do does not stop at the good things
- [[Sin]] — though the connection is looser than it looks, because Genesis so rarely calls a
  deception a sin; Abimelech is the one who supplies the word (20:9)
- [[The Fear of God]] — Abraham's own stated reason for the ruse (20:11), and his misjudgment of two
  foreign kings
- [[Marriage]] — the relationship the wife-sister ruse falsifies, three times
- [[Blessing]] — which in Genesis is twice obtained by deceit and never revoked for it
- [[Judgment]] — what falls on the deceived party at 12:17, and on nobody else

## Related people

- [[The Serpent]] — the method's originator
- [[Abraham]] — the first patriarch to use it, and the one who admits at 20:13 that it was policy
- [[Sarah]] — the instrument of it, without a recorded word of consent
- [[Cain]] — the first to lie to God
- [[Pharaoh (of Abram's time)]] — the first person deceived by a member of the chosen family, and the
  first to say so
- [[Abimelech (of Abraham's time)]] — the second, and the only one who gets an answer; he also
  supplies the theme's one Genesis verdict, *"You have done deeds to me that ought not to be done!"*
