# Curse

## Definition / overview

The counter-word to [[Blessing]], and its exact structural opposite: where blessing is a
performative word conferring the power to flourish, a curse is a performative word withdrawing it.
Both are spoken by God in Genesis, and the first three blessings (1:22, 1:28, 2:3) all land before
the first curse does.

**Genesis 3 is where cursing enters Scripture, and its aim is more precise than it is usually
remembered.** The formula *"cursed are you"* (Hebrew *ʾārûr*) is pronounced exactly twice in
[[The Fall]]:

- **3:14, on the serpent** — *"you are cursed above all livestock, and above every animal of the
  field"*, sentencing it to the belly and to dust.
- **3:17, on the ground** — *"the ground is cursed for your sake"*, which is a curse on the man's
  *medium* rather than on the man. What it produces is futility: thorns, thistles, sweat, and a
  harvest that resists.

**Neither the man nor the woman is cursed.** Their sentences in 3:16 and 3:17–19 are pronounced
without the formula. This is easy to lose under the standing phrase "the curse on Adam and Eve",
and it matters: what falls on them is consequence, not status. God's last two acts in the chapter
are to clothe them and to bar the tree of life so that the condition is not permanent.

The pattern set here holds for most of the Bible's cursing. A curse is a **judicial word, not
magic** — it is spoken by an authority, for a stated cause, on a stated target, and it takes
effect because of who says it. It also travels: Genesis 4:11 curses Cain *from the ground* that
took his brother's blood, 5:29 names Noah in hope of relief *"because of the ground which Yahweh
has cursed"*, and 9:25 has Noah himself pronouncing one. Blessing and curse are then set side by
side as the two possible outcomes of covenant, most fully in Deuteronomy 27–28.

The theme closes where it opened. Galatians 3:13 has Christ *"becoming a curse for us"* by hanging
on a tree, and Revelation 22:3 says of the restored city: *"there will be no curse any more"* — in
the same verses that put the tree of life back in reach (see
[[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]]).

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 3:14, 3:17** — the serpent and the ground; the first two curses in Scripture.
- **Genesis 4:11; 5:29; 8:21** *(not yet ingested)* — Cain cursed from the ground; the hope of
  relief from the cursed ground; God's promise never again to curse it because of humanity.
- **Genesis 9:25; 12:3** *(not yet ingested)* — Noah's curse on Canaan, and the Abrahamic promise
  that pairs blessing and curse as the response to how the nations treat one family.
- Strands to trace as ingestion reaches them: Balaam, hired to curse and unable to (Numbers
  22–24); the covenant curses of Deuteronomy 27–28 and the ceremony at Ebal and Gerizim (Joshua
  8:30–35); Jeremiah 17:5–8, the cursed and blessed man side by side; Malachi 4:6, the last word
  of the Old Testament; Jesus reversing the direction (*"bless those who curse you"*, Luke 6:28);
  Galatians 3:10–14; Revelation 22:3.
