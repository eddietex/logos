# Healing

## Definition / overview

**God restoring a body, a household, or a place to soundness — and naming himself by it.** The Hebrew
verb is ***rāpāʾ***, *heal* (WLC). **Its first occurrence in Scripture is *"God healed Abimelech, and his
wife, and his female servants, and they bore children"*** (Genesis 20:17), after Abraham prayed. **Its
climax in the Torah is the name God gives himself at Marah**: ***"I am Yahweh who heals you"*** — *ʾănî YHWH
rōpəʾekā* (Exodus 15:26).

Proverbs 12:18 broadens this theme to the healing work of wise human speech, with a kind word
lightening anxiety in 12:25. This comparison follows the WEB’s English and makes no assertion about
the Hebrew forms in those two verses.

**Three features are already set by Exodus 15:**

1. **Healing comes with a condition.** *"If you will diligently listen to Yahweh your God's voice … I will put
   none of the diseases on you, which I have put on the Egyptians"* (15:26). **Health is promised as the
   fruit of hearing.**
2. **The healer and the one who strikes are the same.** The diseases on Egypt are those *"which I have put"*
   (*śamtî*, 15:26; WLC). Deuteronomy says it outright: *"I wound and I heal"* (32:39; WEB; *not yet
   ingested*).
3. **What is healed is not only bodies.** At Marah the first act is on water — *"the waters were made
   sweet"* (15:25) — and 2 Kings uses *rāpāʾ* of a spring: *"I have healed these waters"* (2 Kings 2:21;
   WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).

## Development across Scripture

**Genesis — healing after prayer, and physicians at a funeral.** *"Abraham prayed to God. God healed
Abimelech"* (Genesis 20:17): **the first healing in Scripture is of a foreign king's household, closing
wombs God himself had closed** (20:18). **The only other use in Genesis is a participle — *"the
physicians"*, *hārōpəʾîm*, who embalm Jacob in Egypt** (50:2; WLC lemma search). See
[[Abraham and Abimelech]] and [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]].

**Exodus 15 — the name.** **The participle Genesis gave Egypt's physicians becomes God's name for himself**:
*rōpəʾekā*, *your healer* (15:26; WLC). **Egypt's healers embalmed the dead; Israel's healer keeps Egypt's
diseases away.** The contrast is the wiki's; the form is the text's. **The occasion is water, and the
setting is the first statute and the first test** (15:25). The other Torah promise against *maḥălâ*,
*sickness*, joins the same two things: *"he will bless your bread and your water, and I will take sickness
away from among you"* (23:25; WLC; [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]). **Later piety turned *Yahweh rōpəʾekā* into a title,
*Yahweh Rophe*** (reported, not checked in this pass). See [[Bitter Water at Marah]].

**Ahead in the Torah** (*not yet ingested*): the priest's
verdict on skin disease *"healed"* (Leviticus 13–14); **Moses' cry for Miriam — *"Moses cried to Yahweh,
saying, 'Heal her, God, I beg you!'"*** (Numbers 12:13; WEB; WLC), the same pair, *ṣāʿaq* and *rāpāʾ*, as
Marah; **and the reversal of the promise**, *"He will bring on you again all the diseases of Egypt"*
(Deuteronomy 28:60; WEB) — with *madweh*, a different noun from Marah's *maḥălâ* (WLC).

**Beyond the Torah** (*not yet ingested*): Elisha's salt in the spring of Jericho (2 Kings 2:19–22); the
healing of the nations by the leaves of the tree of life — *"The leaves of the tree were for the healing of
the nations"* (Revelation 22:2; WEB). See
[[Bitter Water Made Sweet - Marah and Elisha's Spring]] and
[[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]].

### Violence, injury, and liability — Exodus 21:12–36

Exodus 21:18–19 assigns the assailant responsibility for the recovering victim's lost time and
healing until he is thoroughly healed. When the injured man can again walk with a staff, his
assailant is cleared of the fatal outcome at issue, but not of the duty to provide restoration.
Healing is now an obligation one human owes another, alongside the earlier accounts of God healing.
The verse does not prescribe a medical technique or make payment a substitute for recovery.
See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]].

### Sickness taken away — Exodus 23:20–33

*"I will take sickness away from among you"* (23:25). *Maḥălâ*, *sickness*, and *miqqirbekā*, *from
your midst* (MAM), sit in the same verse as the blessing of bread and water — the pair Marah joined
to God as healer (15:25–26). The promise is now attached to serving Yahweh rather than other gods,
and is followed at once by the end of miscarriage and barrenness (23:26). See
[[The Angel Sent Ahead]] and [[Bitter Water at Marah]].

### Health to the body — Proverbs 3:8

*"It will be health to your body, and nourishment to your bones"* (3:8). *Health* is *rip̄ʾûṯ*, from
*rāp̄āʾ*, the root of *"I am Yahweh who heals you"* at Marah (Exodus 15:26, *rōp̄əʾeḵā*), and the
condition is similar: there, listening and keeping his statutes; here, fearing Yahweh and departing
from evil. *Body* translates *šōr*, elsewhere *navel*; the sense of the Hebrew image is uncertain. See
[[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]] and [[Bitter Water at Marah]]. Source: Proverbs 3 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Health to the whole body — Proverbs 4:22

*"For they are life to those who find them, and health to their whole body"* (4:22). The noun is
*marpēʾ* (Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, read 2026-10-25), conventionally derived from
the same root *rāp̄āʾ* as 3:8's *rip̄ʾûṯ*; the root relation is the standard lexical one and was not
checked against a lexicon this pass. In 3:8 the fear of Yahweh gave health; here the father's words
kept in the heart do. Job is the recorded counter-case: his body was struck *"from the sole of his
foot to his head"* (Job 2:7) although he kept heart, eyes, and feet (Job 31:1, 7). See
[[Heart, Eyes, and Feet - Proverbs 4 and Job 31]].

See [[Keep Your Heart]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### Correction, speech, and daily conduct — Proverbs 12:18, 25

Wise speech heals where rash words wound, and a kind word gladdens an anxious heart. This broadens
the page’s emphasis on divine and bodily restoration to interpersonal care. The sayings name
beneficial speech without prescribing medical treatment or promising that words alone resolve every
illness or distress.

See [[Whoever Loves Correction]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 12 and the comparisons documented on
that passage page.

### The trustworthy envoy — Proverbs 13:17

*"A wicked messenger falls into trouble, but a trustworthy envoy gains healing"* (17): healing attaches to the faithful carrying of a message.

See [[Hope Deferred]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 13.
