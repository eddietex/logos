# Creation

## Definition / overview

That the world is made — not eternal, not accidental, not the residue of a battle among gods —
and made by a single God who stands outside it and pronounces it good. Everything the Bible
later says about creatures, land, work, rest, and redemption rests on this footing.

Genesis 1 sets four load-bearing claims:

1. **Creation is by word.** God speaks; it is so. This puts an unbridgeable distance between
   creator and creation — the world is not made *of* God, and no part of it is divine.
2. **Creation is ordering.** The starting state (1:2) is not nothing but *tohu wabohu* —
   formless and empty, dark, watery. The six days answer both problems: days 1–3 give form, days
   4–6 give fullness.
3. **Creation is good.** Seven times: *God saw that it was good*, and finally *very good*. Matter
   and bodies are not a fall from something better.
4. **Creation is for rest.** The account does not end at the sixth day with humanity, but at the
   seventh with a blessed and hallowed day — see [[Sabbath]].

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 1:1–2:3** — the cosmic account: God (Elohim) orders the whole world in six days and
  rests.
- **Genesis 2:4–25** — a second account at ground level, and a different picture of the same God.
  Creation here is not speech but craft: God *forms* the man like a potter working clay, *plants*
  a garden, *builds* a woman from the man's side. The starting problem is inverted (dryness, not
  water), the sequence differs (man before vegetation and animals, not after), and the name
  changes from Elohim to Yahweh Elohim. Two claims are added that chapter 1 does not make: life is
  God's own breath in dust, so creatures are contingent moment by moment; and creation is
  *entrusted* — the man is placed in the garden to cultivate and keep it. Whether the two accounts
  are complementary perspectives, joined sources, or a deliberate juxtaposition is the classic
  question — see [[Two Creation Accounts - Genesis 1 and Genesis 2]]. The canonical text preserves
  both without harmonizing them.
- **Genesis 5:1–2** — the first time Scripture quotes its own creation account. The register of
  Adam's generations does not resume from Genesis 4:26; it reaches back past the murder, past the
  fall, past the garden, and restates Genesis 1:27–28 in two verses: *"In the day that God created
  man, he made him in God's likeness. He created them male and female, and blessed them."* Creation
  becomes a *heading* — the thing every later generation is filed under — and the effect is to put
  the image and the blessing over a chapter otherwise made of deaths. See [[From Adam to Noah]].
- **Genesis 6:5–7** — creation quoted in order to be reversed. The verb of 6:5 is the verb of
  Genesis 1: seven times God *saw* what he had made and it was good, and here *"Yahweh saw that the
  wickedness of man was great in the earth"* — the same inspection, the opposite finding, set
  against 1:31's *"very good"*. Then the sentence lists what is to go: *"man, along with animals,
  creeping things, and birds of the sky"* — the populations of days six and five, named for erasure
  in something close to the order of their making, run backwards. The flood account will finish the
  reversal by reopening the waters that day two separated (7:11) and then redo it, with a *rûaḥ*
  passing over the waters and dry land reappearing (8:1–2). This is Genesis's strongest statement
  that the created order is not self-sustaining but held: what was spoken into being can be spoken
  out of it. See [[The Sons of God and the Corruption of the Earth]].
- **Genesis 7:11–24** — the reversal carried out, and it runs in order. *"All the fountains of the
  great deep burst open, and the sky's windows opened"* (7:11): *təhôm*, the deep of 1:2, returns
  from below while the waters held above come down, so the expanse of day two fails from both sides
  at once. *"All the high mountains that were under the whole sky were covered"* (7:19) and *"all on
  the dry land … died"* (7:22) undo day three. And 7:21's roll of the dead — *"birds, livestock,
  animals, every creeping thing … and every man"* — is the population of days five and six. What is
  left at 7:24 is a dark, water-covered earth with the breath gone out of it, which is a description
  of Genesis 1:2. Then 8:1–2 starts again with the same words in the forward order: a *rûaḥ* passes
  over the waters, the fountains close, and dry land appears. Genesis is telling the story of the
  world being made twice. See [[Noah and the Flood]] and [[The Flood as Un-Creation]].
- **Genesis 9:1–7** — Genesis 1 quoted a third time, and this time amended. The blessing of 1:28
  returns at 9:1 and 9:7; the diet of 1:29–30 is expanded at 9:3, in a sentence that cites it (*"as I
  gave you the green herb"*); the image of 1:26–27 is invoked at 9:6; and 1:14's vocabulary of *signs*
  supplies the *ʾôt* of 9:12. But *subdue* and *have dominion* are not reissued, and *"the fear of you
  and the dread of you"* (9:2) stands where they were. The world is made a second time out of the
  first world's own words, with every change running in the same direction: a concession to violence.
  See [[The Covenant with Noah]] and [[The Flood as Un-Creation]].
- **Job 38–39** — Creation described by its Creator, the longest passage in Scripture in which God
  describes it in his own voice. It is Genesis 1 told as interrogation: the foundation, the sea, the light,
  the stars, the creatures — and a world whose order is not organized around human beings. Rain falls
  where no one lives (38:26); the wild donkey is free of the city (39:7); the ostrich is made without wisdom
  (39:17); the eagle feeds its young on the slain (39:30). The friends read creation as a moral ledger;
  God presents it as his own, admired for itself, and Job's question is answered by enlarging the frame
  rather than by explaining his place in it. See [[Out of the Whirlwind]].
- Later strands to trace as ingestion reaches them: Psalm 8, Psalm 19, Psalm 104, Proverbs 8:22–31
  (wisdom present at creation), Isaiah 40–45 (the creator as the one who can therefore redeem),
  John 1:1–5, Colossians 1:15–20, Hebrews 11:3, Revelation 21–22 (creation renewed).

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:4, 11

The commandments draw two different uses from the created world. Heaven, earth, and water mark the
scope of the prohibition on images (20:4); God's six days of making and seventh-day rest ground the
weekly pattern of labor and Sabbath (20:11). The latter directly recalls Genesis 2:2–3 without
simply repeating its wording. See [[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]]. See
[[The Ten Commandments]].

### Creation remembered in the covenant sign — Exodus 31

Exodus 31:17 makes God's six days of making heaven and earth and seventh-day rest the rationale for
Israel's Sabbath sign. It adds *“and was refreshed”* to the account of rest; Genesis 2:2–3 does not
contain that phrase in the WEB. The text relates the sanctuary instructions to creation through the
final Sabbath command, but does not itself label the tabernacle a new creation. See
[[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]].

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

Genesis 1:31–2:3 and Exodus 39:32–43 both bring making to a close with inspection,
completion, and blessing. Their order and subjects differ: God sees creation and blesses the
seventh day; Moses sees Israel’s work and blesses the people. This supports a literary parallel,
not an explicit assertion that the tent is the cosmos or that it was made in six days.
See [[Finished Work, Seeing, and Blessing - Creation and the Tabernacle]].

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

Genesis 2:1–3 and Exodus 40:33 both speak of finished work in the stored WEB. Exodus
39:32–43 had already joined completion, seeing, and blessing, and 40:33 now completes
erection before the filling with glory. This supports a literary comparison with ordered
creation, but Exodus 40 does not assign sanctuary parts to creation days or report a
Sabbath at the erection. The first-month date in 40:17 is not a creation day count.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

### The Creator's wisdom — Proverbs 3:19–20

[[Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth]] grounds the praise of human wisdom in
Yahweh's founding of earth and establishing of heavens. Knowledge orders depths and dew.
This supplements Genesis 1's account of creation by speech with an explicit attribution of
wisdom to the Creator. The mention of broken depths can recall Genesis 7:11, but the verse
does not itself identify the flood: its setting is praise of wise creation. See
[[By Wisdom Yahweh Founded the Earth - Proverbs 3 and Creation]].

Source: Proverbs 3 and the cited Genesis and Job comparisons checked in the stored WEB,
2026-10-25.

**Proverbs 8:22–31 — creation with a witness.** Wisdom lists the world in the order it was not yet
there — depths, springs, mountains, hills, earth, fields, *"the beginning of the dust of the world"*
(8:24–26) — and then in the order it was made while she watched: heavens established, *"a circle on
the surface of the deep"* (*ḥûg ʿal-pənê ṯəhôm*, Genesis 1:2's phrase), clouds, springs, the sea given
*"its boundary, that the waters should not violate his commandment"* (literally *his mouth*), and the
earth's foundations (8:27–29). The answer to Job 38:4's *"Where were you?"* is, for Wisdom, *"I was
there"* (8:27). Creation is accompanied by delight — *"rejoicing in his whole world"* (8:30–31) —
which gives Genesis 1:31's *"very good"* a voice. See [[Wisdom at the Creation]] and
[[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### The poor man's Maker — Proverbs 14:31

Proverbs 14:31 calls God the poor man's *Maker* and makes oppression of the poor contempt for him. The ethics of poverty are grounded in creation, as Job grounded his servants' rights in their making *"in the womb"* (Job 31:15). See [[Contempt for His Maker - Proverbs 14.31 and Job 31.13-15]].

See [[A Way That Seems Right]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 14.

### Discernment and accountability — Proverbs 20:12

Yahweh's making is applied to the hearing ear and seeing eye (12). Exodus 4:11 similarly
attributes human faculties to him, but also includes deafness and blindness in the claim.
Proverbs praises the capacities without explaining their absence, and neither passage makes
disability a verdict on personal sin. See [[The Hearing Ear and the Seeing Eye - Proverbs 20.12 and Exodus 4.11]].

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 20; supporting comparisons are documented on the
passage page.

### The maker of them all — Proverbs 22:2

Proverbs 22:2 makes creation the common ground of rich and poor: *"Yahweh is the maker of them
all."* As at 14:31 and Job 31:15, creation grounds a social claim — no one stands outside the
Maker's ownership — without invoking the image of God.

See [[A Good Name]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 22; no new source-language claim.

### The house built as the world was — Proverbs 24:3–4

*"Through wisdom a house is built; by understanding it is established; by knowledge the rooms are
filled"* repeats the three nouns of 3:19–20, *"By wisdom Yahweh founded the earth. By understanding,
he established the heavens. By his knowledge, the depths were broken up"*, in the same order and with
the same verb *kûn* for *established* (Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, read 2026-10-26). A
household is built with the gifts that built the cosmos. See
[[Wisdom, Understanding, and Knowledge - The Builder's Gifts in Proverbs, Exodus, and Kings]].

See [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 24:3–22.

### Correction and public justice — Proverbs 29

Yahweh gives sight to the poor and the oppressor alike. Read beside 22:2’s shared Maker, the
saying places both parties under divine provision without granting equal moral approval to their
actions.

See [[Where There Is No Vision]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 29:13; the passage page records the
cross-reference sources and interpretive limits.

### Agur’s words — Proverbs 30:4

Wind, waters, and the ends of the earth become questions about the identity and power of their
governor. The imagery resembles Job 28:23–27 and 38:4–18 without repeating Genesis’s sequence of
creation. The question about a son supplies no name or account of a second creator.

See [[The Words of Agur]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 30:1–14 and the comparisons documented there.

### Agur's creatures — Proverbs 30:15–31

The lists draw on the natural world for most of their items: the leech, the earth that is not
satisfied with water, fire, the eagle, the serpent, the sea, ants, conies, locusts, the lizard, the
lion, and the goat. Creation is a source of wonder (18–19) and of lessons (24–28), but it is not
sentimentalized: ravens and young eagles eat the mocker's eye (17). Several animal names are
uncertain in translation; see the passage notes.

See [[Four Things]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 30:15–33 and the comparisons documented there.

### The opening of Ecclesiastes — 1:1–11

The world of Ecclesiastes 1:4–7 persists while generations pass. Sun, wind, and rivers repeat
their movements without a final arrival. Genesis 8:22 presents recurring seasons as assurance
after the flood; here persistence exposes the worker's transience. Genesis 1:31–2:3's good
creation and completed work remain beside this weary observation, rather than being silently
replaced by it. The opening poem does not give a creation account or deny that God made the
world. See [[The Earth Remains - Genesis and Ecclesiastes 1]].

See [[Vanity of Vanities]]. Sources: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:1–11 and the comparison texts
verified on that passage page.
