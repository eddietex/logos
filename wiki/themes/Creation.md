# Creation

## Definition / overview

That the world is made — not eternal, not accidental, not the residue of a battle among gods —
and made by a single God who stands outside it and pronounces it good. Everything the Bible
later says about creatures, land, work, rest, and redemption rests on this footing.

Genesis 1 sets four load-bearing claims:

1. **Creation is by word.** God speaks; it is so. This puts an unbridgeable distance between
   creator and creation — the world is not made *of* God, and no part of it is divine.
2. **Creation is ordering.** The starting state (1:2) is not nothing but *tohu wabohu* —
   formless and empty, dark, watery. The six days answer both problems: days 1–3 give form, days
   4–6 give fullness.
3. **Creation is good.** Seven times: *God saw that it was good*, and finally *very good*. Matter
   and bodies are not a fall from something better.
4. **Creation is for rest.** The account does not end at the sixth day with humanity, but at the
   seventh with a blessed and hallowed day — see [[Sabbath]].

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 1:1–2:3** — the cosmic account: God (Elohim) orders the whole world in six days and
  rests.
- **Genesis 2:4–25** — a second account at ground level, and a different picture of the same God.
  Creation here is not speech but craft: God *forms* the man like a potter working clay, *plants*
  a garden, *builds* a woman from the man's side. The starting problem is inverted (dryness, not
  water), the sequence differs (man before vegetation and animals, not after), and the name
  changes from Elohim to Yahweh Elohim. Two claims are added that chapter 1 does not make: life is
  God's own breath in dust, so creatures are contingent moment by moment; and creation is
  *entrusted* — the man is placed in the garden to cultivate and keep it. Whether the two accounts
  are complementary perspectives, joined sources, or a deliberate juxtaposition is the classic
  question — see [[Two Creation Accounts - Genesis 1 and Genesis 2]]. The canonical text preserves
  both without harmonizing them.
- **Genesis 5:1–2** — the first time Scripture quotes its own creation account. The register of
  Adam's generations does not resume from Genesis 4:26; it reaches back past the murder, past the
  fall, past the garden, and restates Genesis 1:27–28 in two verses: *"In the day that God created
  man, he made him in God's likeness. He created them male and female, and blessed them."* Creation
  becomes a *heading* — the thing every later generation is filed under — and the effect is to put
  the image and the blessing over a chapter otherwise made of deaths. See [[From Adam to Noah]].
- **Genesis 6:5–7** — creation quoted in order to be reversed. The verb of 6:5 is the verb of
  Genesis 1: seven times God *saw* what he had made and it was good, and here *"Yahweh saw that the
  wickedness of man was great in the earth"* — the same inspection, the opposite finding, set
  against 1:31's *"very good"*. Then the sentence lists what is to go: *"man, along with animals,
  creeping things, and birds of the sky"* — the populations of days six and five, named for erasure
  in something close to the order of their making, run backwards. The flood account will finish the
  reversal by reopening the waters that day two separated (7:11) and then redo it, with a *rûaḥ*
  passing over the waters and dry land reappearing (8:1–2). This is Genesis's strongest statement
  that the created order is not self-sustaining but held: what was spoken into being can be spoken
  out of it. See [[The Sons of God and the Corruption of the Earth]].
- **Genesis 7:11–24** — the reversal carried out, and it runs in order. *"All the fountains of the
  great deep burst open, and the sky's windows opened"* (7:11): *təhôm*, the deep of 1:2, returns
  from below while the waters held above come down, so the expanse of day two fails from both sides
  at once. *"All the high mountains that were under the whole sky were covered"* (7:19) and *"all on
  the dry land … died"* (7:22) undo day three. And 7:21's roll of the dead — *"birds, livestock,
  animals, every creeping thing … and every man"* — is the population of days five and six. What is
  left at 7:24 is a dark, water-covered earth with the breath gone out of it, which is a description
  of Genesis 1:2. Then 8:1–2 starts again with the same words in the forward order: a *rûaḥ* passes
  over the waters, the fountains close, and dry land appears. Genesis is telling the story of the
  world being made twice. See [[Noah and the Flood]] and [[The Flood as Un-Creation]].
- **Genesis 9:1–7** — Genesis 1 quoted a third time, and this time amended. The blessing of 1:28
  returns at 9:1 and 9:7; the diet of 1:29–30 is expanded at 9:3, in a sentence that cites it (*"as I
  gave you the green herb"*); the image of 1:26–27 is invoked at 9:6; and 1:14's vocabulary of *signs*
  supplies the *ʾôt* of 9:12. But *subdue* and *have dominion* are not reissued, and *"the fear of you
  and the dread of you"* (9:2) stands where they were. The world is made a second time out of the
  first world's own words, with every change running in the same direction: a concession to violence.
  See [[The Covenant with Noah]] and [[The Flood as Un-Creation]].
- **Job 38–39** — Creation described by its Creator, the longest passage in Scripture in which God
  describes it in his own voice. It is Genesis 1 told as interrogation: the foundation, the sea, the light,
  the stars, the creatures — and a world whose order is not organized around human beings. Rain falls
  where no one lives (38:26); the wild donkey is free of the city (39:7); the ostrich is made without wisdom
  (39:17); the eagle feeds its young on the slain (39:30). The friends read creation as a moral ledger;
  God presents it as his own, admired for itself, and Job's question is answered by enlarging the frame
  rather than by explaining his place in it. See [[Out of the Whirlwind]].
- Later strands to trace as ingestion reaches them: Psalm 8, Psalm 19, Psalm 104, Proverbs 8:22–31
  (wisdom present at creation), Isaiah 40–45 (the creator as the one who can therefore redeem),
  John 1:1–5, Colossians 1:15–20, Hebrews 11:3, Revelation 21–22 (creation renewed).

- [[From Adam to Noah]] — 5:1–2, Genesis 1 quoted back as the frame for a genealogy
- [[Noah and the Flood]] — the reversal executed: the deep, the windows, the dry land, and the
  creatures of days five and six
- [[The Waters Recede]] — the re-creation itself: the *rûaḥ* over the waters as in 1:2, the deep
  closed, dry land back, and the creatures released under the blessing of 1:22
- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — Genesis 1's blessing, diet, image, and *signs* reissued with
  amendments, and the dominion language quietly dropped
- [[The Flood as Un-Creation]] — the correspondences traced verse by verse, and the re-creation that
  answers them
- [[The Dove and the Spirit]] — the hovering of 1:2 and the bird over the water in Genesis 8, and
  where the Gospels put both
- [[The Sons of God and the Corruption of the Earth]] — 6:5–7, Genesis 1 quoted back in order to be
  undone

- [[Let the Day Perish]] — Job 3:1–26; **Genesis 1 spoken backwards by a man with no power to
  perform it.** *"Let that day be darkness"* (3:4) is *yəhî ḥōšek* against *yəhî ʾôr*, and the
  chapter runs on through the division of light from darkness, the calendar function of 1:14, the
  blessing of fertility, and the bounded deep — asking for each to be undone for the space of one
  day and one night. **The wiki's other un-creation, [[The Flood as Un-Creation]], is one God
  performs**; this is one a man requests and does not get. See
  [[Let That Day Be Darkness - Job 3 as Counter-Creation]] and
  [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]], where the same inventory comes back in God's mouth
- [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]] — Job 9:5–10 and 10:8–12; **creation praised twice, and both times turned into an
  accusation.** The cosmic hymn (mountains, pillars, sun, stars, the sea trodden, the Bear and
  Orion) is power that unmakes, and Job quotes Eliphaz's doxology (5:9 → 9:10) as a threat. The
  personal hymn — milk, cheese, skin, flesh, bones, sinews, *ḥesed*, *pəquddâ* — is the Bible's most
  detailed account of formation in the womb, and ends *"Yet you hid these things in your heart"*
  (10:13). See [[The Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades]] and
  [[You Have Fashioned Me as Clay - Job 10.8-12, Psalm 139, and the Potter]].
- [[Though He Slay Me]] — Job 12:7–10, 15, 22–24; 14:15; **creation as witness and creation
  undone.** The beasts, birds, earth and fish know that *"Yahweh's hand has done this, in whose hand is
  the life of every living thing, and the breath of all mankind"* (12:9–10, Genesis 2:7's *nepeš* and
  6:17's *rûaḥ*). Then the waters withheld and sent to overturn the earth (12:15), and the chiefs led
  into *tōhû* (12:24), Genesis 1:2's formless waste. And *"the work of your hands"* (14:15)
- [[Bildad's Third Speech]] — Job 25:2–3, 5–6; **Psalm 8's order of creation, inverted.** Moon and
  stars, then *man*, then *son of man* — the psalm's sequence (8:3–4) — ending not in a crown but in a
  worm. *"He makes peace in his high places"* (25:2) presupposes the cosmic conflict Job's poetry knows
  (Rahab, 9:13; 26:12). The whirlwind will give the stars another character: they *sang* (38:7). See
  [[What Is Man That You Should Visit Him - Job 7.17-18 and Psalm 8]].
- [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]] — Job 26:7–13; 27:3; **Job's cosmos, from the shades to the
  heavens.** The north stretched over *tōhû* — Genesis 1:2's *formless* — and the earth hung *"on
  nothing"* (26:7); the waters bound in the clouds (26:8); a circle drawn on the waters *"to the
  confines of light and darkness"* (26:10; compare Proverbs 8:27); the sea stirred, Rahab struck, the
  fleeing serpent pierced (26:12–13). Creation as combat and as order, ending in *"these are but the
  outskirts of his ways"* (26:14). And Genesis 2:7 in Job's oath: *"the spirit of God is in my
  nostrils"* (27:3)
- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:14, 24–27; **wisdom present at the measuring of the world.** God weighed the
  wind, measured the waters, *"made a decree for the rain, and a way for the lightning of the thunder"*,
  and *"then he saw it, and declared it. He established it, yes, and searched it out"* — Proverbs
  8:22–31's scene without Proverbs 8's personification. The deep, *təhôm*, Genesis 1:2's word, says *"It
  isn't in me"* (28:14). 28:26b and 38:25b are the same Hebrew, *wəderek laḥăzîz qōlôt*: the hymn
  anticipates the whirlwind's catalogue
- [[Elihu on God's Justice]] — Job 34:13–15, 19; 35:5, 10–11; **creation as the ground of justice.** God is
  appointed over the earth by no one (34:13), holds the breath of all flesh (34:14–15), and the rich and
  poor *"all are the work of his hands"* (34:19) — so he favours neither. *"Look to the heavens, and see"*
  (35:5). And *"God my Maker"* (35:10) *"teaches us more than the animals of the earth, and makes us wiser
  than the birds of the sky"* (35:11) — Job's *"ask the animals"* (12:7) reversed.
- [[Elihu on God's Greatness]] — Job 36:27–37:18; **God in the weather.** Water drawn up and distilled
  *"from his vapor"* (36:27) — *ʾēd*, the *mist* of Genesis 2:6 and nowhere else; the snow commanded,
  *"Fall on the earth"* (37:6); ice *"By the breath of God"* (37:10); and the sky spread out (*rāqaʿ*, the
  verb of the *rāqîaʿ* of Genesis 1:6) *"strong as a cast metal mirror"* (37:18). The rain serves
  *"correction, or … his land, or … loving kindness"* (37:13) — the same weather, three purposes. God's
  speech in chapter 38 takes the same subjects; see
  [[From Elihu's Storm to the Whirlwind - Job 36.27-37.24 and 38.1]].
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:4–38; **creation in God's own voice, and the human removed from its
  centre.** The earth as a building with foundations, measures, line, and cornerstone, dedicated while
  *"the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy"* (38:4–7); the sea born
  from the womb, swaddled in cloud, and given a bound (38:8–11); light and darkness each with a house
  (38:19–20); rain *"on a land where no man is"* (38:26); the stars under *"the laws of the heavens"*
  (38:31–33). Every element of Genesis 1 — the deep, the gathered waters, the lights — is here, and the
  one thing Genesis 1 builds toward, the human being, is the one asked where he was. See
  [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]].
- [[Behemoth and Leviathan]] — Job 40:15–41:34; **two creatures as the speeches' last word on creation.**
  Behemoth, *bəhēmôt*, the plural of Genesis 1:24's *livestock*, *"which I made as well as you"* — made with
  Job, on his day — and *"the chief of the ways of God"* (40:15, 19), Wisdom's title in Proverbs 8:22. Leviathan,
  outside the dominion of Genesis 1:26, whose wake turns *təhôm*, the deep of Genesis 1:2, white (41:32). No
  combat: the chaos-monster is a creature, admired. See
  [[The First of the Ways of God - Job 40.19 and Proverbs 8.22]].
- [[The Birth of Moses]] — Exodus 2:2–3; ***"she saw that he was a fine child"*** — *wattēreʾ ʾōtô
  kî-ṭôb hûʾ*, the sentence of Genesis 1:4 — and **the child laid in a *tēbâ*, the word used
  otherwise only of Noah's ark.** A new beginning marked in the creation week's vocabulary and the
  flood's. See [[The Two Arks - Noah's Ship and Moses' Basket]]
- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 4:11; ***"Who made man's mouth? Or who makes one mute, or deaf, or seeing, or blind?
  Isn't it I, Yahweh?"*** **The creator claims the disabilities with the faculties**, as the ground of
  the commission. *Mî śām*, *who set*, is the verb of the Hebrew's taunt at 2:14, *"Who made you a prince
  and a judge?"*. And the third sign pours river water *"on the dry land"* — the *yabbāšâ* of Genesis
  1:9
- [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] — Exodus 7:9–12; ***tannîn***, the word of Genesis 1:21's
  *"large sea creatures"*, the first creatures said to be *created* after the heavens and the earth.
  **In Pharaoh's court a rod becomes one.** Later Scripture makes the *tannîn* an emblem of Egypt
  (Ezekiel 29:3, WEB; Hebrew not checked) — [[Am I a Sea or a Sea Monster - The Guard on the Waters]]
