# Murmuring

## Definition / overview

### The waters of Meribah — Numbers 20:1–13

The lack of water is real (2), but the people turn it into an accusation against the
exodus and its leaders (3–5). Verse 13 identifies their quarrel as contention with Yahweh.
The warning intended to end complaints in Numbers 17:10 has not ended resistance.

See [[The Waters of Meribah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 20:1–13.

### The budding rod — Numbers 17:1–13

The sign is intended to end complaints against Moses and Aaron, which Yahweh treats as directed
against himself (5, 10). The closing cry is alarm about death (12–13), not a narrated declaration
that all future resistance has ended.

See [[Aaron's Rod Buds]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 17.

### The rebellion of Korah — Numbers 16:1–40

Moses identifies the complaint against Aaron as gathering against Yahweh (11). Dathan and Abiram
accuse Moses of domination and failed land promises (12–14). Here the dispute concerns authority and
priestly access as well as wilderness deprivation.

See [[The Rebellion of Korah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:1–40; comparisons as cited.

**The wilderness generation's complaint against its leaders, and through them against God.** The Hebrew is
***lûn*** in the niphal and hiphil, *murmur*, *grumble* (WLC), and its noun ***təlunnôt***, *murmurings*
(Exodus 16:7–12; Numbers 14:27; 17:20, 25 Hebrew; WLC lemma search). **It is a wilderness word.** In
the Torah the verb in this sense occurs only in Exodus 15–17 and Numbers 14–17 (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy),
and **its first occurrence is at Marah**: *"The people murmured against Moses, saying, 'What shall we
drink?'"* (Exodus 15:24).

**Three features hold across the cycle**, and Marah sets the first two:

1. **In the narrator's reports the object is Moses — or Moses and Aaron — and never God.** *"Against
   Moses"* (15:24; 17:3); *"against Moses and against Aaron"* (16:2; Numbers 14:2; WLC). **It is Moses and
   God who say where it really goes** (16:8; Numbers 14:27).
2. **The complaint is about need — water, bread, meat — and soon about Egypt.** At Marah there is no Egypt
   in it; by the manna there is: *"We wish that we had died by Yahweh's hand in the land of Egypt, when we
   sat by the meat pots"* (16:3).
3. **Moses redirects it to God.** *"Your murmurings are not against us, but against Yahweh"* (16:8). **The
   people complain sideways; the text insists the complaint goes up.**

**The WLC files the verb under the same lemma as *lûn*, *lodge*, *stay the night*** — Lot's *"stay all
night"* (Genesis 19:2), Jacob at Bethel, *"he stayed there all night"* (28:11) — with a sense letter
separating the two. **Whether they are one root or two is debated** (reported, not checked in this pass).

## Development across Scripture

### Between the dead and the living — Numbers 16:41–50

The next day’s complaint recasts the preceding judgment as killing by Moses and Aaron (41).
The narrator’s account in 16:28–35 attributes the extraordinary judgment to Yahweh; the
accusation contradicts that account and must not be adopted as the narrative verdict.

See [[Aaron Stands Between the Dead and the Living]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:41–50; comparisons as cited.

**Before the word: the complaint at the sea.** The first time the people turn on Moses the verb is not
used: *"Because there were no graves in Egypt, have you taken us away to die in the wilderness?"* (14:11).
**It is the complaint without the name**, and its answer was *"Stand still, and see the salvation of
Yahweh"* (14:13). See [[The Crossing of the Sea]].

**Marah — the mildest instance, and the first.** *"What shall we drink?"* (15:24), two words in Hebrew
(*mah-nništeh*, WLC). **No anger of God is reported, no one is punished, and Moses does not rebuke the
people**; he cries to Yahweh, and the water is made sweet. **What follows is a statute and a test** (15:25)
— the complaint is answered with provision and then with a condition. See [[Bitter Water at Marah]].

**The manna — the complaint with Egypt in it, and the noun.** *"The whole congregation … murmured against
Moses and against Aaron"* (16:2), **Aaron's first time as an object**, and the complaint is now a wish and a
charge: *"We wish that we had died by Yahweh's hand in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the meat pots … for
you have brought us out into this wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger"* (16:3). **The noun
*təlunnōt* appears for the first time, five times in six verses** (16:7–12; WLC lemma search). **Moses
redirects it** — *"Who are we? Your murmurings are not against us, but against Yahweh"* (16:8) — **and God
answers it with food**: *"I have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel … At evening you shall eat
meat, and in the morning you shall be filled with bread"* (16:12). **Still no punishment**; the only anger in
the chapter is Moses', at those who hoarded the bread (16:20). **What is added is a test** — *"whether they
will walk in my law, or not"* (16:4) — **and the glory**: *"Yahweh's glory appeared in the cloud"* (16:10),
shown to the murmurers. See [[Manna and Quail]].

**John 6 repeats the scene with the word**: *"The Jews therefore murmured concerning him, because he said, 'I
am the bread which came down out of heaven'"*, and *"Don't murmur among yourselves"* (John 6:41, 43; WEB; *not
yet ingested*). See [[Bread from Heaven - The Manna and John 6]].

**At Rephidim the murmuring becomes a quarrel.** *"The people quarreled (wayyāreb) with Moses"* (17:2) — *rîb*,
the verb of a dispute or a lawsuit — and *"murmured against Moses"*, the third *lûn* of the wilderness (17:3;
WLC). **The charge changes its verb**: at Sin *"you have brought us out"* (16:3), at Rephidim *"Why have you
brought us up (heʿĕlîtānû)"*, God's verb at the bush (3:8; WLC). **The danger is new**: *"They are almost
ready to stone me"* (17:4). **And the narrator names the sin as a test of God** — *"Is Yahweh among us, or
not?"* (17:7) — and names the place for it, *Massah* and *Meribah*. **Still no punishment in Exodus.** **The
cycle hardens as it repeats**: from a question of need to a wish for Egypt to a question about God's
presence. See [[Water from the Rock]].

**Taberah — after Sinai, a different verb, and the first penalty.** Numbers 11:1 does not use *lûn*. It has *mitʾōnənîm*,
by the tags a verb found only here and at Lamentations 3:39, and the people are *as* complainers, with *raʿ*, *evil*, which WEB
leaves untranslated (WLC). **All three features above bend here.** No object is named, and the complaint is placed straight
in Yahweh's ears; no need is stated; and Moses does not redirect anything, since Yahweh has already heard. **The word differs,
but the wiki files it here because the event belongs to the same series**, and Rahlfs's Greek uses *γογγύζω*. **The
answer has changed too**: fire, then Moses' prayer. Every complaint in Exodus before Sinai was answered with no penalty. See
[[The Fire at Taberah]].

**Kibroth Hattaavah — complaint with food in hand.** Numbers 11:4–35 also has no *lûn*. Its words are *hitʾawwâ*, *crave*, and *bākâ*,
*weep* (WLC). Unlike Marah and the wilderness of Sin there is no lack, since the manna is falling. The memory of Egypt from Exodus 16:3
comes back larger, and Yahweh calls it rejection of himself (11:20). For the first time **Moses joins the complaint**, against Yahweh, and
is not punished for it. The people are given what they asked for, and the graves follow. See [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]].

### The spies — Numbers 13

Still no *lûn*. The report itself is not a murmuring: the verb arrives at 14:2 and 14:36, where the men who brought the *dibbâ* are said to have *made all the congregation to murmur*. So the chapter is the cause. See [[The Twelve Spies]].

**Ahead in Numbers** (by reference, *not yet ingested*): after Korah, the murmuring ends in plague
(Numbers 16–17); and Aaron's budding rod is kept *"that you may make an end of their murmurings against
me"* (17:10 WEB; 17:25 Hebrew). **The first murmuring was met with sweet water; the last are met with judgment.**

**Later Scripture remembers the wilderness complaints as the pattern of unbelief.** Psalm 81:7 has God
recall *"I tested you at the waters of Meribah"*, and Psalm 95:8 warns *"Don't harden your heart, as at
Meribah"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*; see
[[Do Not Test Yahweh - Massah in Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and Hebrews]]); Paul warns the Corinthians not
to grumble *"as some of them also grumbled, and perished by the destroyer"* (1 Corinthians 10:10; WEB;
*not yet ingested*).

### Speaking against — Numbers 12

No *lûn* here. The verb is *dibbēr bə-* (1), as at Numbers 21:5, 7 and Psalm 78:19 (WLC). The complaint now comes from the leaders. Its object is Moses alone, and Yahweh replies that it is about his servant, *against my servant, against Moses* (8).

See [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 12. Word locations from WLC (morphhb) tags; Greek from Rahlfs 1935.

### The refusal — Numbers 14

*Lûn* returns at 14:2, and for the first time the people's complaint names Yahweh itself: *Why does
Yahweh bring us to this land?* (3). Yahweh takes it as his: *the murmurings of the children of
Israel, which they murmur against me* (27), and the noun *təlunnôt* is back. He says *I have heard*,
as at Exodus 16:12, but where that sentence went on to meat, this one goes on to *your dead bodies
shall fall*. So the first feature above is closed from Yahweh's side, and the third no longer
applies, since Moses does not redirect anyone. Verse 36 adds a new form, a leader *causing* a
murmur, *wayyallînû*, the *qərê* (WLC). See [[The People Refuse the Land]].
