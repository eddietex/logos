# Atonement

## Definition / overview

Atonement is what the Hebrew root *kpr* does in the sanctuary: it deals with whatever stands
between a holy God and a people, a priest, or a thing, so that approach is possible and death or
plague does not follow. The root supplies the verb *kipper*, *to make atonement*; the noun
*kippurîm*, *atonements*; *kappōret*, the gold lid of the ark the WEB calls the *"mercy seat"*; and
*kōper*, the payment that buys a forfeit life, the WEB's *"ransom"*. This page traces the root and
the practice it names. [[Redemption]] covers the wider language of buying back and delivering, and
[[Sacrifice and Offering]] the offerings themselves.

The texts do not give one theory of how atonement works. They say who makes it, with what, for whom
or for what, and with what result. Theories built from those facts — covering, wiping away,
ransom, substitution — are interpretations, and this page names them as such where they appear.

## Development across Scripture

### Before the sanctuary — Genesis

The root appears before any rite. Noah is told to *"seal"* the ship *"with pitch"* (Genesis 6:14),
in MAM *wəkāpartā … bakkōper*, the verb and the noun of the root used in a plain, physical sense.
Jacob sends a gift ahead to Esau, saying *"I will appease him with the present"* (Genesis 32:20,
WEB; 32:21 in MAM), *ʾăkappərâ pānāyw*, literally *I will cover his face*. See [[Noah and the Flood]]
and [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]]. Whether these ordinary senses are the origin of the ritual sense is
a question of etymology the texts do not settle, and the wiki records the uses without deciding it.

### Ransom in the law — Exodus 21

A man whose bull kills, after warning, may have *"a ransom"* (*kōper*) laid on him, and pays *"for
the redemption of his life"* (Exodus 21:30, MAM). The payment buys back a life the law could
claim. See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]] and
[[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]].

### The mercy seat — Exodus 25

The ark's lid is the *kappōret* (25:17, MAM), the place from which God says *"There I will meet with
you"* (25:22). The noun's relation to the verb is debated, and the WEB's *"mercy seat"* follows the
older English tradition. See [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] and
[[The Mercy Seat - Kapporet and Hilasterion from Leviticus 16 to Romans 3]].

### For the priests and the altar — Exodus 29

The priests eat *"those things with which atonement was made"* (*kuppar*, 29:33, MAM) at their
ordination, and for seven days Moses makes atonement for the bronze altar until it is *"most
holy"* (29:36–37). Atonement here is made for people and for an object, before either serves.
In the stored WEB, 29:33 is the first *"atonement"* in Genesis and Exodus. See
[[The Ordination of the Priests]].

### The incense altar and the census — Exodus 30

Exodus 30 uses the root more than any chapter so far. Aaron makes atonement on the incense altar's
horns *"once in the year"* with *"the blood of the sin offering of atonement"* (30:10). Every man
counted in a census pays *kōper napšô*, *"a ransom for his soul"*, half a shekel, *"that there be no
plague among them"* (30:12). The silver is *"atonement money"*, *kesep hakkippurîm*, given *"to make
atonement for your souls"* (30:15–16, MAM). Rich and poor pay the same sum (30:15). In one chapter
atonement is made with blood on an altar and with silver for persons. The form *hakkippurîm* of 30:10
and 30:16 is the one in *yôm hakkippurîm*, *"the day of atonement"*, at Leviticus 23:27 (MAM; verified
WEB). See [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] and
[[The Half Shekel - Census, Ransom, and the Temple Tax]].

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Moses says that perhaps he will make atonement for the people’s sin (32:30). What follows is
confession, a request for forgiveness, and the conditional request to be erased (32:31–32); no new
sacrificial rite is performed. God does not declare that atonement has been achieved and retains
judgment (32:33–35). The uncertain attempt must not be rewritten as a completed exchange of Moses’
life for Israel’s. See [[The Golden Calf]].

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The mercy seat with its cherubim is made in 37:6–9, and the incense altar with its horns in
37:25–28. The latter is the altar for which 30:10 prescribes annual atonement. Chapter 37
reports manufacture, not a blood rite, forgiveness, or a new theory of atonement. The later
ritual and canonical readings of the mercy seat remain distinct from its making.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38:1–31

The silver from the half-shekel census payment becomes a hundred sockets and the pillars’ fittings
(38:25–28). Its description as atonement money comes from 30:15–16, not a new ritual explanation in
chapter 38. The account shows a commanded payment used for sanctuary service; it does not make
freely donated wealth a general purchase of forgiveness.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### Fools mock at atonement — Proverbs 14:9

*"Fools mock at making atonement for sins, but among the upright there is good will"* (9). This is the WEB's rendering of a line that English versions translate differently; no claim is made here about the Hebrew or about whether a sacrificial rite is in view.

See [[A Way That Seems Right]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 14.

  incense altar, and the half-shekel ransom as atonement money.
