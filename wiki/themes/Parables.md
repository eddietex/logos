# Parables

## Definition / overview

A story or picture told so that the hearer will judge something real by it. The Hebrew *māšāl* is
usually said to cover the proverb, the taunt, and the parable, and the Gospels' *parabolē* to name
Jesus' stories (both reported, not checked against a lexicon; Judges 9 itself uses neither word).
This page collects the told stories: the fable, in which plants or animals speak, and the parable,
which tells a story of people. Proverbs that are only sayings belong to [[Wisdom]].

The thing a parable does is make the hearer pass a verdict before they know it is about them. Sometimes
the teller says so outright. Sometimes, as with Jotham, only one figure is decoded and the rest is left
to the hearer.

### The trees seek a king — Judges 9:7–21

The Bible's first fable. Jotham tells *the men of Shechem* that *the trees set out to anoint a king
over themselves* (9:8). The olive, the fig, and the vine refuse. Each would have to give up its oil,
fruit, or wine *to wave back and forth over the trees*. The bramble accepts: *If in truth you anoint
me king over you, then come and take refuge in my shade; and if not, let fire come out of the bramble,
and devour the cedars of Lebanon* (9:15). Jotham's application does not decode the good trees. It takes
up only the bramble's *if … in truth* and *if not … fire*, and applies them to Abimelech and Shechem
(9:16–20). The verdict is left for Shechem to reach.

## Development across Scripture

The second plant fable is Jehoash's to Amaziah: *The thistle that was in Lebanon sent to the cedar that
was in Lebanon* (2 Kings 14:9, WEB checked at bible-api.com; not yet ingested). Nathan's parable of the
poor man's lamb makes David judge himself (2 Samuel 12); it is already on file in
[[Fourfold Restitution - Exodus 22, Nathan's Parable, and Zacchaeus]]. The prophets use the form, and
the Gospels make it Jesus' common way of teaching. Those will be written up here as they are ingested.
