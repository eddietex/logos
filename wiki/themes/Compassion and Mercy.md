# Compassion and Mercy

## Definition / overview

**The Hebrew word is ***raḥămîm***, and it is a plural noun built on ***reḥem***, *womb*.** Whatever
else it means, it begins as a body word: **the feeling a mother has for what came out of her**,
extended to anyone who feels that way about anyone. It is not pity from above and it is not
forgiveness; **it is the involuntary turn of the insides toward someone in trouble.**

Scripture pairs it constantly with ***ḥānan***, *to be gracious* — **the two together are how God
names himself at Exodus 34:6**, *raḥûm wəḥannûn*, *merciful and gracious*. The distinction that
usually holds: ***ḥēn*** is favour freely given by a superior to an inferior (and is what Joseph
finds in Potiphar's sight at 39:4 and the jailer's at 39:21); ***raḥămîm*** is kinship felt as
physical distress. **[[Favor and Grace]] covers the first; this page covers the second.**

**Genesis's densest use of both is [[The Second Journey]]**, Genesis 43 — **a chapter in which
nobody forgives anybody.**

## Development across Scripture

### Genesis 43 — asked for in Canaan, kindled in Egypt

**The theme enters Genesis as a petition.** [[Jacob]], sending ten sons and [[Benjamin]] to a man he
believes may destroy them:

> **14** May God Almighty give you mercy before the man, that he may release to you your other
> brother and Benjamin. If I am bereaved of my children, I am bereaved. (43:14)

***Wəyittēn lākem raḥămîm*** — **he asks God to put compassion into a stranger.** It is the only
prayer in Genesis that asks God to change a third party's feelings, and **it is asked by a man with
no idea that the stranger is his son.**

Sixteen verses later:

> **30** Joseph hurried, for his heart yearned over his brother; and he sought a place to weep. He
> entered into his room, and wept there. (43:30)

***Nikmərû raḥămāyw*** — **literally *his compassions grew hot*.** The verb ***kāmar*** is rare and
suggests heat or agitation. **Jacob's noun and Joseph's noun are the same noun.** Genesis never says
so; it simply places the request and the granting sixteen verses and one border apart, **and the man
who prayed it never hears that it was answered.** See [[Providence]].

**Between them sits the other root.** *"God be gracious to you, my son"* — ***ʾĕlōhîm yoḥnəkā***
(43:29), ***ḥānan***, **said by [[Joseph]] to the brother he cannot claim.** The two halves of
Exodus 34:6 are in two mouths that cannot hear each other.

### The idiom elsewhere

***Nikmərû raḥămîm*** of a human being occurs **once more in Scripture, and it is the same
situation**: 1 Kings 3:26, where the true mother before Solomon *"yearned over her son"* and gives
the child up rather than see it divided. **Both cases are a person who must conceal what they feel
in order to save the one they feel it for**, and in both the concealment is what proves the claim.

Of God, the vocabulary runs through the whole canon: **Exodus 34:6** (the self-naming); **Deuteronomy
13:17**; **Psalm 103:13**, *"Like a father has compassion on his children, so Yahweh has compassion on
those who fear him"*; **Isaiah 49:15**, which goes back to the root's origin — *"Can a woman forget
her nursing child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb?"*; **Hosea 2:23**;
**Lamentations 3:22**. The Greek of the New Testament renders the feeling with ***splanchnizomai***,
*to be moved in the bowels* — **another body word** — used of Jesus at Matthew 9:36 and Mark 6:34,
**and of the father in Luke 15:20 who sees his son a long way off and runs.**

### In Genesis before 43

**The noun itself is nearly absent.** The book's mercy before this chapter is narrated as action
rather than named as feeling:

- ***"Yahweh shut him in"*** (7:16) and ***"God remembered Noah"*** (8:1) — see [[Noah]]
- **The men grab [[Lot]]'s hand**, *"Yahweh being merciful to him"* (19:16) — ***ḥemlâ***, pity, **the
  noun's only use of God in Genesis**
- **God hears [[Ishmael]]** in the wilderness (21:17) and opens [[Hagar]]'s eyes
- **[[Esau]] runs, embraces, and weeps** (33:4) — **the only unambiguous human mercy in Genesis
  before 43**, and it is extended by the brother who had sworn to kill

**What 43 adds is the vocabulary.** Genesis had shown mercy happening; **this chapter gives it a
name, and gives the name to a father who cannot see it work.**

### After 43

- **45:1–5** — *wayyitʾappaq* fails, and the compassion held in at 43:31 becomes the disclosure.
  *"Now don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that you sold me here"* is mercy spoken aloud for
  the first time in the cycle
- **50:19–21** — **the theme's settlement.** *"As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it
  for good"*, and then *"Now therefore don't be afraid. I will nourish you and your little ones."*
  **The same *don't be afraid* the steward used at 43:23**, now from the man himself
- **Exodus 2:6** — **Pharaoh's daughter sees the child and *"had compassion on him"***
  ([[The Birth of Moses]]). **The verb is *ḥāmal*, to pity or spare — not the Joseph cycle's
  *raḥămîm*.** This line formerly called it *"the Joseph cycle's characteristic verb"*; corrected on
  ingesting Exodus 2. *Ḥāmal* is the sparing of one who has the power to kill (1 Samuel 15:9;
  2 Samuel 12:6), and the princess has her father's decree in her hands

**The pledge law of Exodus 22:26–27 gives compassion as a legal reason.** The cloak must go back
before sunset because *"it is his garment for his skin. What would he sleep in?"*, and God adds,
*"I will hear, for I am gracious"* — *ḥannûn*, the second word of the pair *raḥûm wəḥannûn* that
34:6 will make God's name. **The adjective is first heard as the reason a creditor returns a
coat.** See [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].

### Help, food, and refreshment — Exodus 23:1–19

Mercy becomes a positive duty toward an enemy's wandering or overburdened animal (23:4–5). The alien
is protected because Israel knows alien life (23:9); the poor eat from the seventh year's
uncultivated land (23:11); and dependents and working animals receive rest (23:12). These provisions
do not wait for friendship or a request for pity. See
[[Help for an Enemy - Exodus 23, Proverbs 25, and Matthew 5]].

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

The people are spared the announced destruction through Moses’ appeal to God’s deliverance and
promise (32:11–14). This is mercy in narrative outcome, not a claim that this chapter uses the
Hebrew terms discussed above. Mercy does not remove the chapter’s remaining judgment (32:28,
34–35). See [[The Golden Calf]].

### Presence after the calf — Exodus 33

God joins the promised revelation of goodness to his freedom to be gracious and show mercy (33:19).
This broadens the page’s emphasis on bodily compassion in Genesis 43: here mercy is a divine
commitment stated in an encounter after communal sin, without any description of bodily feeling.
Romans 9:15–16 cites this speech to Moses in its argument about God’s mercy; see
[[For This Very Purpose - Exodus 9.16 in Romans 9.17]]. See [[Show Me Your Glory]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

The promise of mercy in 33:19 becomes a proclamation of character in 34:6–7. Mercy, grace,
patience, and abundant loving kindness stand alongside forgiveness and refusal to clear the
guilty. Neither half should displace the other. Moses answers by asking for the people to be
pardoned and taken as God's inheritance (34:8–9). The later uses in Numbers 14 and Jonah 4 are
collected in [[Merciful and Gracious - Exodus 34, Numbers 14, and Jonah 4]]. See [[The Covenant Renewed]].

### Help that must not wait — Proverbs 3:21–35

The command not to withhold good or postpone available help (3:27–28) makes concern for
another practical and timely. No emotion of pity is described; this is a thematic extension
of mercy as action, not an assertion about Hebrew vocabulary. Compare Exodus 22:26–27's
return of the pledged garment before night and Job 31:16–23's denial of withheld aid.

See [[Do Not Withhold Good]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 3; comparisons as cited above.
The discussion added here makes no claim about exact source-language forms.

### A False Balance — Proverbs 11:17, 24–26

Mercy benefits the giver, while cruelty harms the cruel person. Generosity and refreshment return
good to the one who gives; withholding grain is cursed, whereas making it available for sale is
blessed. The chapter commends practical access to resources as well as outright giving.

See [[A False Balance]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 11; comparisons are documented on the passage
and connection pages.

### Correction, speech, and daily conduct — Proverbs 12:10, 18, 25

The righteous person respects an animal’s life, while even the wicked person’s tender mercies are
cruel. Compassion is evaluated by its effect on the dependent creature, not by its kindly label.
Healing speech and a word that lightens anxiety extend this attention to human hurt. See
[[Care for the Animal - Proverbs 12.10 and Exodus 23]].

See [[Whoever Loves Correction]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 12 and the comparisons documented on
that passage page.

### Pity for the poor honours the Maker — Proverbs 14:21, 31

*"Blessed is he who has pity on the poor"* (21), and kindness to the needy *honors* the Maker (31), so mercy toward the poor is counted as an act toward God. Verse 29's *"slow to anger"* is, in the WEB, the phrase of Exodus 34:6; see [[Slow to Anger - Proverbs 14.29 and Exodus 34.6]].

See [[A Way That Seems Right]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 14.

### Mercy and truth — Proverbs 16:6

*"By mercy and truth iniquity is atoned for"* — the *ḥesed we'ĕmet* bound round the son's neck at 3:3 and proclaimed of Yahweh at Exodus 34:6 (Hebrew from Sefaria; the WEB's English differs in each). The verse leaves open whether the mercy is God's or the penitent's.

See [[The Plans of the Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 16.

### Integrity, counsel, and compassion — Proverbs 19:11, 17, 22

Discretion restrains anger and overlooks an offense (11); pity for the poor is an act Yahweh
undertakes to reward (17); kindness makes a person desirable (22, following the WEB). Compassion is
not limited to people who can return the favor. See
[[Lends to Yahweh - Proverbs 19.17, Exodus 22, and Job 31]] . This comparison makes no claim about
shared Hebrew vocabulary.

See [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] . Source: stored WEB Proverbs 19 and the
comparisons documented on that passage page.

### Mercy refused and kindness pursued — Proverbs 21:10, 13, 21, 26

The wicked soul shows the neighbor no mercy (10); stopping one's ears to the poor brings an unheard
cry (13); kindness pursued with righteousness finds life (21); the righteous give and do not
withhold (26).

See [[The King's Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 21; supporting comparisons are documented on
the passage page.

### The generous eye — Proverbs 22:9

*"He who has a generous eye will be blessed; for he shares his food with the poor"* (22:9). Mercy
here is a disposition (the eye) proven by a concrete act (food shared).

See [[A Good Name]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 22; no new source-language claim.

### Rescue those led away to death — Proverbs 24:10–12

*"Rescue those who are being led away to death! Indeed, hold back those who are staggering to the
slaughter!"* (24:11). The text does not say who they are; it says the bystander's excuse — *"we didn't
know this"* — is answered by *"he who weighs the hearts"* (12). Compassion here is a duty with a
witness, and the *"time of trouble"* that tests strength (10) is someone else's. See
[[According to His Work - Proverbs 24.12, Psalm 62.12, and Romans 2.6]].

See [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 24:3–22.

### Bread and water for an enemy — Proverbs 25:20–22

*"If your enemy is hungry, give him food to eat. If he is thirsty, give him water to drink"*
(25:21). The need governs, as in Exodus 23:4–5; the reason given is *"coals of fire on his head"*,
unexplained, and *"Yahweh will reward you"* (25:22). Just before, 25:20 names mercy's failure in
small things: songs sung *"to a heavy heart"*, like taking a coat away in the cold. See
[[Help for an Enemy - Exodus 23, Proverbs 25, and Matthew 5]].

See [[The Glory of Kings]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 25.

### Integrity, law, and public life — Proverbs 28:8, 13, 27

Wealth accumulated by excessive interest is destined for someone who pities the poor (8); mercy
meets confessed and renounced sin (13); giving to the poor is set against deliberately closed eyes
(27). The chapter broadens this page’s emphasis on inward compassion: mercy here also describes what
the wrongdoer receives, though the giver is not specified in verse 13.

See [[The Wicked Flee When No One Pursues]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 28 and the comparisons
documented on the passage and connection pages.

### Lemuel’s mother — Proverbs 31:6–9

The instruction acknowledges the person near death and the person bitter in soul, then commands
judgment for the poor. Its concern can be described as compassion, though this passage uses no
explicit mercy term in the WEB. The relief described in 6–7 does not cancel the obligation to
act in 8–9; reducing suffering to a reason for silencing sufferers would miss that ending.

See [[The Words of King Lemuel]] . Source: stored WEB Proverbs 31:1–9 and the comparisons documented
there.

- [[Seven Days of Silence]] — Job 2:11–13; **the theme's high-water mark in Scripture, performed by
  the three men who will become its standing counter-example.** Three men in three countries hear,
  send word to each other, fix a meeting, and travel; they weep at the sight of him, mourn him with
  the rites for the dead, and sit on the ground in his ashes for seven days and seven nights without
  speaking. **The stated verbs are *nûd* and *nāḥam*, to condole and to comfort**, and 42:11 will use
  the identical pair of the brothers and sisters who finally get it right. Everything Romans 12:15
  and Galatians 6:2 ask of a comforter is done here and undone from chapter 4 onward — **not by
  losing the compassion but by adding an explanation to it.** See
  [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]]

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — Genesis 45:1, 5, 9–11; **the mercy in this chapter is structural rather
  than stated.** *"Cause everyone to go out from me!"* (45:1) **empties an Egyptian court before
  [[Joseph]] says *"whom you sold into Egypt"*** — **a confession of their crime made where no Egyptian
  can hear the charge**, and the exact reverse of the instinct at 42:7. The rest follows the same shape:
  *"don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves"* (45:5), a message dictated for an old man who will
  not believe it, provisions for the road, and ***"there I will nourish you"*** (45:11). **Nobody in
  the chapter names any of it as mercy**, and the word [[Jacob]] prayed for at 43:14 — ***raḥămîm*** —
  does not appear
- **Genesis 50:21 — *wayḏabbēr ʿal-libbām*, *he spoke upon their heart***; the idiom's only previous
  use in Genesis is [[Shechem (son of Hamor)]]'s at 34:3 — [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]
- [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] — Job 22:6–9; **the duties of mercy as a charge sheet.** The widow, the
  fatherless, the hungry, the thirsty, the naked, the debtor whose cloak is his only covering — the
  people the law and the prophets put in the righteous man's care, named as the people Job
  neglected. Job answers by giving the list a real defendant (24:2–12), by recalling his own record
  (29:12–17), and by an oath (31:16–22). See
  [[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]]
- [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]] — Job 24:3–12; **the classes of mercy described from
  their own side.** The fatherless whose donkey is driven off, the widow whose ox is pledged, the
  needy pushed off the road, the child taken from the breast for a debt, the naked in the mountain
  rain, the harvesters without bread and the winepress-treaders without drink — the exact list
  Eliphaz charged Job with neglecting (22:6–9), given real oppressors. See
  [[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]].
- [[The Months of Old]] — Job 29:12–17; **the classes of mercy described from the giver's side**,
  as chapter 24 described them from the victim's: *"I delivered the poor who cried, and the fatherless
  also"*, *"I caused the widow's heart to sing for joy"*, *"I was eyes to the blind, and feet to the
  lame. I was a father to the needy"* — Psalm 68:5's title for God, practised by a man. It adds
  **force** to the list (*"I broke the jaws of the unrighteous"*, 29:17) and ends with Job *"as one who
  comforts the mourners"* (29:25). See
  [[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]].
- [[Now They Mock Me]] — Job 30:25; ***"Didn't I weep for him who was in trouble? Wasn't my soul grieved
  for the needy?"*** — the *ʾebyôn* of 29:16 — asked by a man for whom nobody in the chapter weeps.
  **And a tension in the same speech**: at 30:1–8 Job describes an outcast wilderness people with
  contempt, their fathers unfit to run with his sheep dogs, in terms close to those in which he pitied
  the wilderness poor at 24:4–8. The passage page records two readings (an outlaw class distinguished
  from the oppressed poor; or the old hierarchy surfacing in a man stripped of it) and does not choose.
- [[Job's Oath of Innocence]] — Job 31:16–23; the poor, the widow, the fatherless and the naked, sworn to under curse: *"if
  he hasn't been warmed with my sheep's fleece … then let my shoulder fall from the shoulder blade"*
  (31:20–22). The record of 29:12–17 put under oath, and Eliphaz's 22:6–9 denied. 31:18 goes further
  than either — the orphan raised *"as with a father"* in his own house. See
  [[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]].
- [[Elihu on God's Greatness]] — Job 36:5; 37:13; God *"doesn't despise anyone"* (36:5), and sends the
  rain *"for loving kindness"* (*ḥesed*, 37:13) — the covenant word, in a book set outside the covenant,

  say which it is.
- [[The Birth of Moses]] — Exodus 2:6; **Pharaoh's daughter *"had compassion on him"*** — *ḥāmal*,
  to pity or spare, the verb of one with the power to kill who does not (1 Samuel 15:9; 2 Samuel
  12:6). **Not the Joseph cycle's *raḥămîm***; the pity comes before her recognition that he is a
  Hebrew, and survives it
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:26–27; *"What would he sleep in? … for I am gracious"*

## Related themes

- [[Favor and Grace]] — ***ḥēn*** and ***ḥānan***, the other half of the pair, **and the theme Joseph's
  own career runs on** (39:4, 21)
- [[Providence]] — **43:14 and 43:30 are its least-argued instance in Genesis**: a prayer and its
  answer with no narrator between them
- [[Brotherhood]] — **the object of the compassion in both of the chapter's occurrences is a brother**
- [[Suffering]] — what compassion is a response to; *ṣārat napšô* at 42:21 is what Joseph's
  compassions are hot about
- [[The Names of God]] — *raḥûm wəḥannûn* (Exodus 34:6) **is built from this chapter's two roots**
- [[Strife and Peacemaking]] — [[Esau]] at 33:4, and the steward's *šālôm lākem* at 43:23
- **Forgiveness has no page of its own yet and does not need one**: Genesis's only explicit
  forgiveness is 50:17–21, and until that pericope is ingested the material belongs here
- [[Forgiveness]] — the word Genesis withholds until its last chapter
