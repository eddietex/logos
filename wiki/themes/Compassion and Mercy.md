# Compassion and Mercy

## Definition / overview

**The Hebrew word is ***raḥămîm***, and it is a plural noun built on ***reḥem***, *womb*.** Whatever
else it means, it begins as a body word: **the feeling a mother has for what came out of her**,
extended to anyone who feels that way about anyone. It is not pity from above and it is not
forgiveness; **it is the involuntary turn of the insides toward someone in trouble.**

Scripture pairs it constantly with ***ḥānan***, *to be gracious* — **the two together are how God
names himself at Exodus 34:6**, *raḥûm wəḥannûn*, *merciful and gracious*. The distinction that
usually holds: ***ḥēn*** is favour freely given by a superior to an inferior (and is what Joseph
finds in Potiphar's sight at 39:4 and the jailer's at 39:21); ***raḥămîm*** is kinship felt as
physical distress. **[[Favor and Grace]] covers the first; this page covers the second.**

**Genesis's densest use of both is [[The Second Journey]]**, Genesis 43 — **a chapter in which
nobody forgives anybody.**

## Development across Scripture

### Genesis 43 — asked for in Canaan, kindled in Egypt

**The theme enters Genesis as a petition.** [[Jacob]], sending ten sons and [[Benjamin]] to a man he
believes may destroy them:

> **14** May God Almighty give you mercy before the man, that he may release to you your other
> brother and Benjamin. If I am bereaved of my children, I am bereaved. (43:14)

***Wəyittēn lākem raḥămîm*** — **he asks God to put compassion into a stranger.** It is the only
prayer in Genesis that asks God to change a third party's feelings, and **it is asked by a man with
no idea that the stranger is his son.**

Sixteen verses later:

> **30** Joseph hurried, for his heart yearned over his brother; and he sought a place to weep. He
> entered into his room, and wept there. (43:30)

***Nikmərû raḥămāyw*** — **literally *his compassions grew hot*.** The verb ***kāmar*** is rare and
suggests heat or agitation. **Jacob's noun and Joseph's noun are the same noun.** Genesis never says
so; it simply places the request and the granting sixteen verses and one border apart, **and the man
who prayed it never hears that it was answered.** See [[Providence]].

**Between them sits the other root.** *"God be gracious to you, my son"* — ***ʾĕlōhîm yoḥnəkā***
(43:29), ***ḥānan***, **said by [[Joseph]] to the brother he cannot claim.** The two halves of
Exodus 34:6 are in two mouths that cannot hear each other.

### The idiom elsewhere

***Nikmərû raḥămîm*** of a human being occurs **once more in Scripture, and it is the same
situation**: 1 Kings 3:26, where the true mother before Solomon *"yearned over her son"* and gives
the child up rather than see it divided. **Both cases are a person who must conceal what they feel
in order to save the one they feel it for**, and in both the concealment is what proves the claim.

Of God, the vocabulary runs through the whole canon: **Exodus 34:6** (the self-naming); **Deuteronomy
13:17**; **Psalm 103:13**, *"Like a father has compassion on his children, so Yahweh has compassion on
those who fear him"*; **Isaiah 49:15**, which goes back to the root's origin — *"Can a woman forget
her nursing child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb?"*; **Hosea 2:23**;
**Lamentations 3:22**. The Greek of the New Testament renders the feeling with ***splanchnizomai***,
*to be moved in the bowels* — **another body word** — used of Jesus at Matthew 9:36 and Mark 6:34,
**and of the father in Luke 15:20 who sees his son a long way off and runs.**

### In Genesis before 43

**The noun itself is nearly absent.** The book's mercy before this chapter is narrated as action
rather than named as feeling:

- ***"Yahweh shut him in"*** (7:16) and ***"God remembered Noah"*** (8:1) — see [[Noah]]
- **The men grab [[Lot]]'s hand**, *"Yahweh being merciful to him"* (19:16) — ***ḥemlâ***, pity, **the
  noun's only use of God in Genesis**
- **God hears [[Ishmael]]** in the wilderness (21:17) and opens [[Hagar]]'s eyes
- **[[Esau]] runs, embraces, and weeps** (33:4) — **the only unambiguous human mercy in Genesis
  before 43**, and it is extended by the brother who had sworn to kill

**What 43 adds is the vocabulary.** Genesis had shown mercy happening; **this chapter gives it a
name, and gives the name to a father who cannot see it work.**

### After 43

- **45:1–5** — *wayyitʾappaq* fails, and the compassion held in at 43:31 becomes the disclosure.
  *"Now don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that you sold me here"* is mercy spoken aloud for
  the first time in the cycle
- **50:19–21** — **the theme's settlement.** *"As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it
  for good"*, and then *"Now therefore don't be afraid. I will nourish you and your little ones."*
  **The same *don't be afraid* the steward used at 43:23**, now from the man himself
- **Exodus 2:6** — **Pharaoh's daughter sees the child and *"had compassion on him"***; the Joseph
  cycle's characteristic verb reappears at the opening of the book that undoes his settlement

## Key passages

- **Genesis 43:14** — [[The Second Journey]]; *raḥămîm* asked for
- **Genesis 43:29** — [[The Second Journey]]; *ḥānan* spoken over Benjamin
- **Genesis 43:30** — [[The Second Journey]]; *nikmərû raḥămāyw*, and the second of Joseph's seven
  weepings
- **Genesis 33:4** — [[The Reconciliation with Esau]]; **the mercy Genesis shows without naming**
- **Genesis 19:16** — [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]]; *"Yahweh being merciful to him"*
- **Genesis 21:17** — [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]]
- **Genesis 50:19–21**, [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] — the cycle's answer
- **Exodus 34:6; Psalm 103:13; Isaiah 49:15; Luke 15:20**
- [[Seven Days of Silence]] — Job 2:11–13; **the theme's high-water mark in Scripture, performed by
  the three men who will become its standing counter-example.** Three men in three countries hear,
  send word to each other, fix a meeting, and travel; they weep at the sight of him, mourn him with
  the rites for the dead, and sit on the ground in his ashes for seven days and seven nights without
  speaking. **The stated verbs are *nûd* and *nāḥam*, to condole and to comfort**, and 42:11 will use
  the identical pair of the brothers and sisters who finally get it right. Everything Romans 12:15
  and Galatians 6:2 ask of a comforter is done here and undone from chapter 4 onward — **not by
  losing the compassion but by adding an explanation to it.** See
  [[Comforters Who Do Not Comfort]]

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — Genesis 45:1, 5, 9–11; **the mercy in this chapter is structural rather
  than stated.** *"Cause everyone to go out from me!"* (45:1) **empties an Egyptian court before
  [[Joseph]] says *"whom you sold into Egypt"*** — **a confession of their crime made where no Egyptian
  can hear the charge**, and the exact reverse of the instinct at 42:7. The rest follows the same shape:
  *"don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves"* (45:5), a message dictated for an old man who will
  not believe it, provisions for the road, and ***"there I will nourish you"*** (45:11). **Nobody in
  the chapter names any of it as mercy**, and the word [[Jacob]] prayed for at 43:14 — ***raḥămîm*** —
  does not appear
- **Genesis 50:21 — *wayḏabbēr ʿal-libbām*, *he spoke upon their heart***; the idiom's only previous
  use in Genesis is [[Shechem (son of Hamor)]]'s at 34:3 — [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]

## Related themes

- [[Favor and Grace]] — ***ḥēn*** and ***ḥānan***, the other half of the pair, **and the theme Joseph's
  own career runs on** (39:4, 21)
- [[Providence]] — **43:14 and 43:30 are its least-argued instance in Genesis**: a prayer and its
  answer with no narrator between them
- [[Brotherhood]] — **the object of the compassion in both of the chapter's occurrences is a brother**
- [[Suffering]] — what compassion is a response to; *ṣārat napšô* at 42:21 is what Joseph's
  compassions are hot about
- [[The Names of God]] — *raḥûm wəḥannûn* (Exodus 34:6) **is built from this chapter's two roots**
- [[Strife and Peacemaking]] — [[Esau]] at 33:4, and the steward's *šālôm lākem* at 43:23
- **Forgiveness has no page of its own yet and does not need one**: Genesis's only explicit
  forgiveness is 50:17–21, and until that pericope is ingested the material belongs here
- [[Forgiveness]] — the word Genesis withholds until its last chapter

## Related people

- [[Jacob]] — **asks for it** (43:14), in the only prayer he offers in the Joseph cycle
- [[Joseph]] — **feels it and hides it** (43:30), then cannot (45:1–2)
- [[Benjamin]] — **its object**, twice, without knowing either time
- [[Esau]] — **Genesis's first man to extend it** (33:4)
- [[God]] — **the one Jacob asks to supply it**, and the one the narrator will not credit with having
  done so
- [[Joseph's Steward]] — *"Peace be to you. Don't be afraid"* (43:23), **the only comfort anyone
  offers the brothers in two chapters**
