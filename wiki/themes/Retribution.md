# Retribution

## Definition / overview

The conviction that God so orders the world that righteousness is rewarded and wickedness punished
*within this life*, and therefore that a person's circumstances are a readable index of their
standing with God. Prosperity implies favour; catastrophe implies guilt; the size of the
catastrophe implies the size of the guilt.

Two things have to be said about it before anything else, because most treatments drop one or the
other.

**It is not a heresy invented by Job's friends.** It is the working assumption of Deuteronomy
28's blessings and curses, of the framework of Judges and Kings, and of a great deal of Proverbs —
*"No mischief shall happen to the righteous, but the wicked shall be filled with evil"* (Proverbs
12:21). When [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] says *"the wicked man writhes in pain all his days"* (15:20),
he is not saying anything a devout reader of the tradition would flinch at.

**And it is the doctrine the book of Job exists to break.** Not to deny — the epilogue restores
Job's fortunes, which is retribution's own ending — but to break as a *diagnostic*, as a rule that
can be run backwards from a man's condition to a verdict on his life. The friends run it backwards.
God tells them at 42:7 they did not speak rightly.

There is a second failure mode, visible in the second cycle, and it is self-inflicted. Under
pressure the claim retreats: it starts as *the wicked suffer* and becomes *the wicked suffer
eventually*. Zophar makes the retreat explicit — *"the triumphing of the wicked is short, the joy of
the godless but for a moment"* (20:5) — and the retreat is fatal, because a doctrine about timing
cannot be run backwards from a man's present condition. Job takes exactly that ground in chapter
21, where the wicked grow old, keep their children, and are buried with honours. See
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

The first failure mode is worth stating exactly, because the doctrine is not falsified by a single
hard case: it is made **unfalsifiable** by its defenders. When Job produces evidence against it, the
friends do not weigh the evidence; they treat his production of it as further evidence for the
verdict. *"Your own mouth condemns you, and not I"* (15:6). *"You have shriveled me up. This is a
witness against me"* (16:8) is Job seeing exactly what has happened to him: the punishment is being
read as the confession. Once that move is available, no innocent sufferer can ever be identified,
and the doctrine has stopped being a claim about the world and become a machine for producing
verdicts.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 3–4** — the pattern's origin, and its most defensible form: specific acts, specific
  sentences, spoken by God directly. See [[The Fall]] and [[Cain and Abel]]. What Job's friends do
  is run this in reverse, without the speaking part.
- **Genesis 6:5–8; 18:23–25** *(18 not yet ingested)* — a flood proportioned to universal
  wickedness, and then Abraham's protest at the possibility of the righteous being swept away with
  the wicked, which is the first time anyone in Scripture argues against the collateral form of the
  doctrine. See [[The Sons of God and the Corruption of the Earth]].
- **Deuteronomy 28:1–68** — blessings and curses laid out at length and addressed to the nation.
  Note the addressee: the covenant sanctions are corporate and conditional, and the friends' error
  includes applying them to an individual, and to a non-Israelite individual at that.
- **Proverbs 3:33; 10:2–3; 11:8; 12:21; 13:21** — the doctrine as proverb. Its own genre limits it:
  proverbs state what generally holds, not what always holds, and Proverbs itself knows exceptions
  (13:23, 16:8).
- **Job 4:7–9; 8:3–7, 20; 11:13–20; 15:17–35; 18:5–21; 20:4–29; 22:4–11** — the friends' cumulative
  case, sharpening across the three cycles from advice to insinuation to fabricated charges. See
  [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]].
- **Job 16:8; 17:4–10; 21:7–34** — Job's answer. Chapter 21 is the frontal assault: *"Why do the
  wicked live, become old, yes, and grow mighty in power? … Their houses are safe from fear."* He
  argues from observation, and he is right.
- **Job 42:7–9** — the verdict, and the only place in Scripture where God evaluates a theological
  argument by name.
- **Psalm 37; Psalm 73** — the same problem worked twice. Psalm 37 counsels patience (*"they will
  soon be cut down like the grass"*); Psalm 73 admits the doctrine nearly failed — *"my feet were
  almost gone … For I was envious of the arrogant"* — and resolves it not by denying the wicked
  prosper but by relocating the accounting: *"until I went into God's sanctuary, and considered
  their latter end."*
- **Ecclesiastes 7:15; 8:14; 9:2** — the flat empirical denial: *"There is a righteous man who
  perishes in his righteousness, and there is a wicked man who lives long in his evildoing."*
- **Ezekiel 18:1–32** — *"The soul who sins, he shall die"*, aimed at the proverb about fathers
  eating sour grapes. Ezekiel tightens retribution to the individual and thereby sharpens Job's
  problem rather than easing it.
- **Isaiah 53:4–11** — a sufferer *"stricken by God, and afflicted"* whose suffering is for others'
  transgressions. The passage assumes the retributive reading is what onlookers made, and says it
  was wrong.
- **Luke 13:1–5** — Jesus on the Galileans Pilate killed and the eighteen crushed at Siloam: *"Do
  you think that these Galileans were worse sinners…? I tell you, no."* The clearest New Testament
  refusal of the diagnostic.
- **John 9:1–3** — *"Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" Jesus
  answered, "Neither…"* — the disciples' question shows the doctrine still fully operative, and
  the answer refuses both of its available verdicts.
- **Galatians 6:7; Romans 2:6–8; 2 Corinthians 5:10** — retribution reaffirmed, and relocated to a
  final judgment. This is the New Testament's characteristic move: not that the accounting is
  fictional, but that it does not close here.

## Key passages

- [[The Stolen Blessing]] — Genesis 27:1–45, and **the theme in the one form Genesis will actually
  narrate: measure for measure, unannounced.** Jacob passes himself off as the firstborn to a father
  who cannot see, and twenty years later an elder sister is passed off as a younger one to him in the
  dark (29:23–26); he takes a blessing with a garment and a goat, and his sons take his favourite son
  from him with a garment and a goat (37:31–33). **Genesis states none of it.** There is no narrator's
  aside, no divine speech, no remark from Jacob linking the scenes — **the only verbal link is Laban
  saying, without irony, *"It is not done so in our place, to give the younger before the firstborn"***
  (29:26), in the feminine of the two words the whole cycle runs on. **The wiki records the pattern as
  a pattern and marks the moral inference as the reader's, not the book's.** See
  [[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]] and
  [[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]]
- [[Job of Uz]] — the doctrine's premises assembled without being stated: a blameless man, an
  itemised fortune, and the correlation between them that the friends will read backwards once the
  fortune is gone
- [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]] — the doctrine's fullest statement in the book, and 15:6's method for
  making it immune to counter-evidence
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — 16:8, the sufferer watching his own body
  being entered as testimony against him
- [[Bildad's Second Speech]] — the doctrine with everything else stripped off: no advice, no call
  to repent, no promise of restoration, and no offence named in twenty-one verses. Retribution
  stated as physics, with 18:8's *"by his own feet"* keeping God's hands clean
- [[Zophar's Second Speech]] — the doctrine conceding the case it needs. *"The triumphing of the
  wicked is short"* (20:5) grants that they triumph, which moves the claim to *eventually* and
  destroys the inference it was supposed to license
- [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] — the doctrine not attacked but bypassed: Job's answer to a
  portrait of the wicked man's ruin is to display his own and ask what it proves

- [[Righteousness]] — the status the doctrine claims to be able to infer from circumstances
- [[Wisdom]] — the tradition retribution belongs to, and which Job and Ecclesiastes stress-test
  from inside it
- [[Curse]] — the covenantal machinery of Deuteronomy 28, which the friends borrow without the
  covenant
- [[Sin]] — which the doctrine requires to be present wherever suffering is

  can tell devotion from investment, which is the accuser's whole case at 1:9
