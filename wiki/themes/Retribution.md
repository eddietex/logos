# Retribution

## Definition / overview

The conviction that God so orders the world that righteousness is rewarded and wickedness punished
*within this life*, and therefore that a person's circumstances are a readable index of their
standing with God. Prosperity implies favour; catastrophe implies guilt; the size of the
catastrophe implies the size of the guilt.

Two things have to be said about it before anything else, because most treatments drop one or the
other.

**It is not a heresy invented by Job's friends.** It is the working assumption of Deuteronomy
28's blessings and curses, of the framework of Judges and Kings, and of a great deal of Proverbs —
*"No mischief shall happen to the righteous, but the wicked shall be filled with evil"* (Proverbs
12:21). When [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] says *"the wicked man writhes in pain all his days"* (15:20),
he is not saying anything a devout reader of the tradition would flinch at.

**And it is the doctrine the book of Job exists to break.** Not to deny — the epilogue restores
Job's fortunes, which is retribution's own ending — but to break as a *diagnostic*, as a rule that
can be run backwards from a man's condition to a verdict on his life. The friends run it backwards.
God tells them at 42:7 they did not speak rightly.

There is a second failure mode, visible in the second cycle, and it is self-inflicted. Under
pressure the claim retreats: it starts as *the wicked suffer* and becomes *the wicked suffer
eventually*. Zophar makes the retreat explicit — *"the triumphing of the wicked is short, the joy of
the godless but for a moment"* (20:5) — and the retreat is fatal, because a doctrine about timing
cannot be run backwards from a man's present condition. Job takes exactly that ground in chapter
21, where the wicked grow old, keep their children, and are buried with honours. See
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

The first failure mode is worth stating exactly, because the doctrine is not falsified by a single
hard case: it is made **unfalsifiable** by its defenders. When Job produces evidence against it, the
friends do not weigh the evidence; they treat his production of it as further evidence for the
verdict. *"Your own mouth condemns you, and not I"* (15:6). *"You have shriveled me up. This is a
witness against me"* (16:8) is Job seeing exactly what has happened to him: the punishment is being
read as the confession. Once that move is available, no innocent sufferer can ever be identified,
and the doctrine has stopped being a claim about the world and become a machine for producing
verdicts.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 3–4** — the pattern's origin, and its most defensible form: specific acts, specific
  sentences, spoken by God directly. See [[The Fall]] and [[Cain and Abel]]. What Job's friends do
  is run this in reverse, without the speaking part.
- **Genesis 6:5–8; 18:23–25** *(18 not yet ingested)* — a flood proportioned to universal
  wickedness, and then Abraham's protest at the possibility of the righteous being swept away with
  the wicked, which is the first time anyone in Scripture argues against the collateral form of the
  doctrine. See [[The Sons of God and the Corruption of the Earth]].
- **Deuteronomy 28:1–68** — blessings and curses laid out at length and addressed to the nation.
  Note the addressee: the covenant sanctions are corporate and conditional, and the friends' error
  includes applying them to an individual, and to a non-Israelite individual at that.
- **Proverbs 3:33; 10:2–3; 11:8; 12:21; 13:21** — the doctrine as proverb. Its own genre limits it:
  proverbs state what generally holds, not what always holds, and Proverbs itself knows exceptions
  (13:23, 16:8).
- **Job 4:7–9; 8:3–7, 20; 11:13–20; 15:17–35; 18:5–21; 20:4–29; 22:4–11** — the friends' cumulative
  case, sharpening across the three cycles from advice to insinuation to fabricated charges. See
  [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]].
- **Job 16:8; 17:4–10; 21:7–34** — Job's answer. Chapter 21 is the frontal assault: *"Why do the
  wicked live, become old, yes, and grow mighty in power? … Their houses are safe from fear."* He
  argues from observation, and he is right.
- **Job 42:7–9** — the verdict, and the only place in Scripture where God evaluates a theological
  argument by name — [[The Restoration of Job]].
- **Psalm 37; Psalm 73** — the same problem worked twice. Psalm 37 counsels patience (*"they will
  soon be cut down like the grass"*); Psalm 73 admits the doctrine nearly failed — *"my feet were
  almost gone … For I was envious of the arrogant"* — and resolves it not by denying the wicked
  prosper but by relocating the accounting: *"until I went into God's sanctuary, and considered
  their latter end."*
- **Ecclesiastes 7:15; 8:14; 9:2** — the flat empirical denial: *"There is a righteous man who
  perishes in his righteousness, and there is a wicked man who lives long in his evildoing."*
- **Ezekiel 18:1–32** — *"The soul who sins, he shall die"*, aimed at the proverb about fathers
  eating sour grapes. Ezekiel tightens retribution to the individual and thereby sharpens Job's
  problem rather than easing it.
- **Isaiah 53:4–11** — a sufferer *"stricken by God, and afflicted"* whose suffering is for others'
  transgressions. The passage assumes the retributive reading is what onlookers made, and says it
  was wrong.
- **Luke 13:1–5** — Jesus on the Galileans Pilate killed and the eighteen crushed at Siloam: *"Do
  you think that these Galileans were worse sinners…? I tell you, no."* The clearest New Testament
  refusal of the diagnostic.
- **John 9:1–3** — *"Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" Jesus
  answered, "Neither…"* — the disciples' question shows the doctrine still fully operative, and
  the answer refuses both of its available verdicts.
- **Galatians 6:7; Romans 2:6–8; 2 Corinthians 5:10** — retribution reaffirmed, and relocated to a
  final judgment. This is the New Testament's characteristic move: not that the accounting is
  fictional, but that it does not close here.

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:5–6, 12

Exodus 20:5–6 attaches judgment and loving kindness to hatred or love of God, while 20:12 associates
honoring parents with long life in the given land. These are real sanctions and promises, but the
passage does not authorize a diagnosis of every sufferer's personal guilt. **The tension already
raised by Job is sharpened:** a warning of iniquity reaching children stands beside Job 21:19–21's
demand that the wrongdoer himself pay. Ezekiel 18:19–23 further insists on the righteous son's life
and the wicked person's possibility of turning. See [[The Ten Commandments]].

### Violence, injury, and liability — Exodus 21:12–36

The scale of life, eye, tooth, and wound corresponds to the harm done (21:23–25). It is a rule for
responding to an injury, not a rule for inferring a sufferer's past sin from that injury. The
surrounding remedies include lost time and healing costs, freedom for an injured servant, and
payments for damage. The pregnant bystander is injured by other people's fight (21:22): the case
itself gives no ground for treating her suffering as deserved.
See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]].
### Multiplied repayment — Exodus 22:1–9

The matching scale of 21:23–25 is followed by a different principle for theft: repayment of
more than was taken. Double for an animal found alive or a deposit stolen (22:4, 7), four or five
times for an animal slaughtered or sold (22:1), and double from anyone *"God condemns"* in a
disputed claim (22:9) — so a false accusation of theft costs what the theft would have. See
[[Theft and Restitution]] and
[[Fourfold Restitution - Exodus 22, Nathan's Parable, and Zacchaeus]].
See [[Laws of Property and Restitution]].

**Exodus 22:24 measures the penalty on the offence.** Those who take advantage of the widow and
the fatherless will be killed *"with the sword; and your wives shall be widows, and your children
fatherless"*. **The punishment makes the oppressor's household into the people he oppressed.** See
[[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] and [[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]].

### The ambush turned round — Proverbs 1:17–19

The first lecture of Proverbs states retribution in its self-acting form. The gang says *"Let's lay in
wait for blood. Let's lurk secretly"* (*neʾerbâ … niṣpənâ*, 1:11); the father answers *"these lay wait
for their own blood. They lurk secretly for their own lives"* (*yeʾĕrōbû … yiṣpənû*, 1:18) — their own
verbs, with the possessive changed. No judge appears and God is not named in 1:8–19; the consequence is
the shape of the act, as in Psalm 7:15–16 and Proverbs 26:27 (*"Whoever digs a pit shall fall into
it"*).

**Contradiction recorded, not resolved.** Proverbs 1:18–19 says those who ambush the innocent for gain
ambush themselves. Job says the opposite of what he has seen: *"The tents of robbers prosper"* (Job
12:6, [[Though He Slay Me]]), and *"The murderer rises with the light. He kills the poor and needy"*
(24:14, [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]) with no reckoning narrated. The wiki reads the
difference as one of direction — the father speaks forward, to a son deciding whether to take a road
(*"So are the ways"*, 1:19), and Job speaks of men already on it — but both claims stand in the text,
and Genesis 42:21–22 shows the reckoning arriving as conscience rather than as a trap; see
[[What Profit Is It - Joseph's Brothers and the Ambush of Proverbs 1]] and [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]].

### Eating the fruit of one's own way — Proverbs 1:20–33

Wisdom's first speech states the self-acting form again: *"they will eat of the fruit of their own
way, and be filled with their own schemes"* (1:31). The *schemes* are *mōʿăṣōṯêhem*, their own
*counsels*, from the root of the *ʿēṣâ*, *counsel*, they refused (1:25, 30): **refusing her counsel,
they are filled with their own.** The *backsliding* that kills (1:32, *məšûḇâ*) is from the *šûḇ* of
the call to *"Turn"* (1:23). The consequences are named as *paḥad*, *dread* (1:26–27), which the
listener is spared (1:33). Galatians 6:7 has the same logic (*"whatever a man sows, that he will also
reap"*, WEB, checked against bible-api.com).

**Two tensions recorded, not resolved.** (1) Wisdom *laughs* at the refusers' calamity (1:26, *ʾelʿaḡ*);
Job accuses God of the same mockery directed at the innocent (*"he will mock at the trial of the
innocent"*, Job 9:23, *yilʿāḡ*). The object differs, and Proverbs never makes calamity as such the
object of Wisdom's laughter; but the two verses use the same verb. (2) Those who seek after the
calamity *"will not find me"* (1:28), against Deuteronomy 4:29 and Jeremiah 29:13, where Israel seeks
after disaster *"and you shall find him"*. See [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]]. Source: Proverbs 1 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### The rule stated without exception — Proverbs 2:7, 21–22

The second lecture ends on the rule in its plainest form: God *"is a shield to those who walk in
integrity"* (2:7), *"the upright will dwell in the land … the wicked will be cut off from the land"*
(2:21–22). **Tension flagged, not resolved**: the words are Job's pair — *tām* and *yāšār*, Job 1:1 —
and Job, both, lost land and household. Psalm 37 meets the same problem by counselling patience
(*"Don't fret because of evildoers"*, 37:1, WEB, checked against bible-api.com); Proverbs 2 does not
raise it. The punishment is also self-acting in the chapter's middle: the strange woman's house sinks
of its own weight to death (2:18). See [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] and
[[The Upright Will Dwell in the Land - Proverbs 2.21-22, Psalm 37, and Matthew 5.5]]. Source: Proverbs 2 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Five promises and a reproof — Proverbs 3:1–12

The lecture states the doctrine in its plainest form — keep the teaching and gain long life (3:2);
fear Yahweh and gain health (3:7–8); honour him with first fruits and your barns will be full (3:9–10)
— and then, after a paragraph marker in the Masoretic layout, says Yahweh reproves the son he loves
(3:11–12). **The qualification is inside Proverbs, not only in Job.** Job, who *"feared God, and turned
away from evil"* (Job 1:1) — the two verbs of 3:7 — was struck with sores (2:7) and lost his flocks
(1:13–17). The wiki records both: the promises as Proverbs states them, and their limits as the next
verses and the book of Job show them. See [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]]. Source: Proverbs 3 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Riches and peace in Wisdom's hands — Proverbs 3:16–17

[[Wisdom's Worth and the Founding of the Earth]] associates wisdom with long life,
riches, honor, and peace. **Tension retained:** Job 1:1 and 1:13–19 describe a righteous,
God-fearing man deprived of wealth and children, and 2:7 records his bodily suffering.
Proverbs 3:11–12 also recognizes the reproof of the son God loves. Reading 3:16–17 as
praise of wisdom's life-giving direction rather than an exceptionless diagnosis of
individual fortunes is the wiki's interpretation; neither the promise nor Job's
counterexample is removed.

### Secure sleep and the two households — Proverbs 3:21–35

Proverbs 3:23–26 promises secure steps and sweet sleep; 3:32–35 distinguishes the
upright, blessed, humble, and wise from the perverse, cursed, mocking, and foolish.
**Contradiction if universalized:** Job 1:1, 13–19 and 7:3–4, 13–14 present uprightness
with ruined household and terrified nights, while Job 21:7–9 presents secure wicked
households. These sources must remain side by side. Reading Proverbs as instruction
about the course to choose, rather than a complete description of every present outcome,
is an interpretation, not a qualification explicitly supplied by Proverbs 3:33 itself.

### Wisdom crowns; Job loses his crown — Proverbs 4:1–9

Proverbs 4:6, 8–9 promises preservation and honor to the person who loves wisdom.
Job 19:9 describes glory stripped and a crown removed, while Job 1:1; 2:3 affirms the
sufferer's uprightness and continuing integrity. **These claims conflict if the promise
is made an exceptionless account of present circumstances.** The wiki reads the promise
as formative counsel about wisdom's worth, not a rule for judging sufferers; that reading
is an interpretation and leaves the tension explicit. See
[[The Crown Given and Taken - Proverbs 4 and Job 19]].

### Stumbling in the dark — Proverbs 4:19

*"The way of the wicked is like darkness. They don't know what they stumble over"* (4:19). The
consequence comes from the road itself rather than from a visible blow. Job 24:13–17 describes the
same rebels against the light but asks why the Almighty keeps no visible times (24:1) and says God
*"doesn't regard the folly"* (24:12). **The two passages conflict if Proverbs 4:19 is read as a
timetable.** The wiki reads it as a description of where the road leads, and records Job's
testimony that the fall is not always seen. See
[[Rebels Against the Light - Proverbs 4 and Job 24]].

See [[The Two Paths]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The cords of his own sin — Proverbs 5:22

*"The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him. The cords of his sin hold him firmly"* (5:22). The
self-catching trap of 1:17–18 is applied to the adulterer, and Job 31:12 calls the same sin *"a
fire that consumes to destruction"*. **Bildad used this picture against Job** — *"he is cast into
a net by his own feet"* (Job 18:8) — of a man God had called blameless (Job 1:8). The wiki keeps
the two uses apart: Proverbs 5 says what sin does to a man shown sinning; it does not license
reading a man's trouble as proof of his sin. See [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]],
[[Bildad's Second Speech]], and [[The Neighbor's Door - Proverbs 5 and Job 31.9-12]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 5; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

### Without remedy — Proverbs 6:11, 15

Two of the four units in Proverbs 6:1–19 end in sudden ruin: poverty arriving *"as a robber"*
(6:11), and the worthless man *"broken suddenly, and that without remedy"* (6:15, *ʾên marpēʾ* —
*marpēʾ* is the *"health"* of 4:22). The sayings are general, like the rest of the book's
retribution statements, and the wiki reads them alongside Job as it has throughout. See
[[The Surety, the Sluggard, and the Seven Abominations]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 6; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Touched back — Proverbs 6:29, 33

*"Whoever touches her will not be unpunished"* (6:29, *hannōḡēaʿ*); *"He will get wounds and
dishonor"* (6:33, *negaʿ*, a stroke, from the same root). The consequence takes the shape of the
act, as the fire burns the one who carries it (6:27–28). The enforcer is the husband, not a court,
and the wiki reads 6:29–35 as describing what adultery brings, not as a claim that every disgraced
man was an adulterer. See [[The Lamp of the Commandment]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 6; Hebrew
read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Riches to those who love her — Proverbs 8:18–21

Wisdom promises *"riches, honor, enduring wealth"* and full treasuries to those who love her
(8:18, 21), without qualification. **Tension flagged with Job:** Job did what 8:13 calls the fear of
Yahweh — he *"feared God, and turned away from evil"* (Job 1:1) — and lost riches (1:13–17), glory
(19:9), and children (1:18–19). The wiki reads Proverbs' promise as the ordinary course, not a verdict
on any one sufferer, as on earlier pages; the text itself does not add that limit. See
[[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

**Proverbs 8:35 — life to the finder, read after Job.** *"Whoever finds me, finds life, and will
obtain favor from Yahweh."* Job 28:27–28 agrees that wisdom was with God at creation, but gives
humans only the fear of the Lord and turning from evil as their share of it, and Job feared God and
lost nearly everything. Proverbs 8 states the promise without exceptions; Job remains the case where
it did not hold in the ordinary way. See [[Wisdom at the Creation]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 8; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father — Proverbs 10:2–3, 22, 24–30

The promises are categorical: provision for the righteous, realized fear for the wicked, life
prolonged by fear of Yahweh, and secure dwelling. Job 3:25 reports realized fear in an upright
sufferer; Job 21:7–13 reports the wicked growing old. The contradiction in outcomes remains visible.
Treating these proverbs as general instruction is an interpretive qualification, not a restriction
stated in their wording.

### A False Balance — Proverbs 11:4–8, 18–21, 23, 27–28, 31

Deliverance for the righteous, punishment for evil, and repayment in the earth make the chapter’s
moral accounting explicit. The contradiction in outcomes must remain visible: Proverbs 11:21
promises deliverance to righteous offspring, but Job 1:18–22 records the death of the upright man’s
children; Job 21:7–13 depicts secure wicked offspring. Job 2:3 rules out explaining his ruin by
hidden guilt. General instruction is the wiki’s reading of these proverbs, not an exception clause
written into the verses.

### Repaid for his ways — and the way that seems right — Proverbs 14:11, 14, 19, 22, 32

### The wicked for the day of evil — Proverbs 16:4–5, 7, 18, 31

### Unpunished, and the bribe that prospers — Proverbs 17:5, 8, 11, 13, 20, 26

### Sowing and reaping — Proverbs 22:4, 8, 16

*"He who sows wickedness reaps trouble"* (22:8) is the harvest form of the retribution principle;

the same image to read Job's disaster backward (Job 4:8). The wiki keeps both: the saying looks
forward from deed to outcome and gives no license to look back from suffering to sin. See

### The latter end — Proverbs 23:18, 21, 32

The block measures conduct by where it ends: hope that *"will not be cut off"* for the God-fearer
(23:18), poverty and rags for the drunkard and the glutton (23:21), a snakebite *"In the end"* for the
drinker (23:32). In Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah* (read 2026-10-26) 23:18's *future* and
23:32's *end* are one noun, *ʾaḥărît*. Job 21:13's wicked who *"spend their days in prosperity"* and go
down to Sheol *"In an instant"* are the standing challenge to the rule; see
[[The Prosperity of the Wicked]].

See [[Do Not Envy Sinners]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 23:12–24:2.

### According to his work — Proverbs 24:12, 16, 20, 22

The block states retribution four ways: God *"render[s] to every man according to his work"* (12);
the righteous *"falls seven times, and rises up again; but the wicked are overthrown by calamity"*
(16); *"there will be no reward to the evil man"* (20); the rebels' *"calamity will rise suddenly"*
(22). 24:16 is the qualification that matters beside Job: the righteous do fall, repeatedly. And
24:29, in the next block, forbids a man to say of himself the very phrase 24:12 gives to God. See
[[According to His Work - Proverbs 24.12, Psalm 62.12, and Romans 2.6]].

See [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 24:3–22.

### Rendering refused to the wronged — Proverbs 24:24–25, 29, 34

The appendix gives retribution its human channels and limits. The partial judge is cursed by the
peoples, and those who convict the guilty are blessed (24:24–25); but the wronged man may not say *"I
will render to the man according to his work"* (24:29) — 24:12's phrase for God. The sluggard's
poverty arriving *"as a robber"* (24:34) is consequence, not sentence. See
[[As He Has Done to Me - Proverbs 24.29 and the Law of Retaliation]].

See [[The Field of the Sluggard]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 24:23–34.

- [[The Purpose of the Proverbs]] — Proverbs 1:1–7; the preface of the book where the doctrine's classic
  sentences live. It promises to teach wisdom, not to explain every case; read after Job, its sayings
  describe the ordinary course of things rather than pass verdicts on a particular sufferer

- [[The Stolen Blessing]] — Genesis 27:1–45, and **the theme in the one form Genesis will actually
  narrate: measure for measure, unannounced.** Jacob passes himself off as the firstborn to a father
  who cannot see, and twenty years later an elder sister is passed off as a younger one to him in the
  dark (29:23–26); he takes a blessing with a garment and a goat, and his sons take his favourite son
  from him with a garment and a goat (37:31–33). **Genesis states none of it.** There is no narrator's
  aside, no divine speech, no remark from Jacob linking the scenes — **the only verbal link is Laban
  saying, without irony, *"It is not done so in our place, to give the younger before the firstborn"***
  (29:26), in the feminine of the two words the whole cycle runs on. **The wiki records the pattern as
  a pattern and marks the moral inference as the reader's, not the book's.** See
  [[The Younger Before the Firstborn - Jacob and Laban]] and
  [[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]]
- [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]] — Genesis 29:23–26; **the other half of that scene, now ingested.** The
  substitution happens, Jacob discovers it in the morning, and **Genesis supplies no comment of any
  kind** — no narrator's aside, no divine speech, and not a word from Jacob connecting his wedding
  night to his father's bedside. **The one voice that speaks in the passage is Laban's, and Laban is
  explaining a custom.** The measure-for-measure reading is the reader's inference and the book
  permits it; the wiki files it as an inference
- [[Job of Uz]] — the doctrine's premises assembled without being stated: a blameless man, an
  itemised fortune, and the correlation between them that the friends will read backwards once the
  fortune is gone
- [[The Wager in Heaven and the Four Messengers]] — **the doctrine stated aloud, in heaven, by its
  sharpest critic.** *"Haven't you made a hedge around him… You have blessed the work of his hands"*
  (1:10). The accuser and the three friends hold **the identical theory of the world** — prosperity
  tracks righteousness — and differ only in the direction they run it: the friends infer sin from
  ruin, the accuser infers hire from prosperity. **The premise is discredited in chapter 1, before a
  friend has opened his mouth**, and the reader spends twenty-five chapters watching four men reason
  from it anyway
- [[Seven Days of Silence]] — Job 2:11–13; **the doctrine's three spokesmen introduced before they
  hold it.** Nothing in the passage is doctrinal. Three men travel, weep, mourn, and sit in silence
  for a week, and every one of them behaves impeccably. **This is what makes the theme's case study
  uncomfortable rather than simple**: the friends are not callous, not lazy, and not insincere, and
  the wiki reads their collapse as caused by the doctrine rather than revealed by it. **The turn
  happens at 4:1, when someone starts explaining what he saw at 2:12** — a man marked with the boil
  of Deuteronomy 28, which under every rule they have means exactly one thing. See
  [[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]]
- [[The Second Test]] — **the doctrine arrives as a disease, and makes the friends' case before they
  speak.** *Šəḥîn rāʿ*, a malignant boil *"from the sole of his foot to his head"* (2:7), is the
  affliction of Deuteronomy 28:35's curse list, word for word and limit for limit. **The men who
  arrive four verses later are not being obtuse: they are reading a symptom the Torah designates as
  the mark of a covenant-breaker, and the rules return a false verdict.** This is why 42:7 is hard —
  God does not rebuke them for stupidity. See [[The Boil of Deuteronomy 28 on a Blameless Man]]
- [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]] — the doctrine's fullest statement in the book, and 15:6's method for
  making it immune to counter-evidence
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — 16:8, the sufferer watching his own body
  being entered as testimony against him
- [[Bildad's Second Speech]] — the doctrine with everything else stripped off: no advice, no call
  to repent, no promise of restoration, and no offence named in twenty-one verses. Retribution
  stated as physics, with 18:8's *"by his own feet"* keeping God's hands clean
- [[Zophar's Second Speech]] — the doctrine conceding the case it needs. *"The triumphing of the
  wicked is short"* (20:5) grants that they triumph, which moves the claim to *eventually* and
  destroys the inference it was supposed to license
- [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] — the doctrine not attacked but bypassed: Job's answer to a
  portrait of the wicked man's ruin is to display his own and ask what it proves
- [[Judah and Tamar]] — Genesis 38:7, 10: *"Yahweh killed him"*, and *"he killed him also."* **The only
  two occasions in Genesis where the narrator says Yahweh killed a named individual**, three verses
  apart, and they are the two men standing between [[Tamar]] and an heir. **The asymmetry between them
  is the point**: [[Onan]]'s offence is stated inside the same sentence as his act — *"lest he should
  give offspring to his brother"* — while **[[Er]] is *"wicked in Yahweh's sight"* and no charge is
  ever specified**, here or at 1 Chronicles 2:3. **The book that supplies a reason for the flood, for
  Sodom, and for Babel supplies none for its first individual execution.** The wiki records this as a
  fact about the text and **does not argue from it**
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:21–22; **the doctrine put into the mouths of the guilty, about themselves, and
  the reader knows it is wrong.** *"Therefore this distress has come upon us"* — ***ṣārâ*** for their
  punishment and ***ṣārâ*** for the anguish they caused, in a single verse. **They read an Egyptian
  interrogation as a divine reckoning**; it is in fact their living victim running a test. [[Reuben]]
  raises it to covenant law with *"his blood is required"* (9:5). **Genesis prints the theory, lets it
  produce a true confession from a false premise, and never endorses it**

- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:16; **the brothers read a planted cup as an
  accounting**, exactly as they read the returned money at 42:28 and the prison at 42:21. ***"God has
  found out the iniquity of your servants"*** **treats an entirely staged event as a reckoning coming
  due** — and they are right that one is in progress and wrong about every particular of it. **The
  chapter's opening line is the canon's first *"Why have you rewarded evil for good?"*** (44:4),
  **false as charged and exact as stated**; Proverbs 17:13 supplies the only consequence clause the
  idiom ever gets, *evil will not depart from his house*. See
  [[Rewarding Evil for Good - A False Charge That Is Exactly True]]
- [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]] — Genesis 49:3–7; **three sons are told what their own acts cost them,
  decades after the acts, by a father who said nothing at the time.** **[[Reuben]] for 35:22** — whose
  entire narrative record is *"and Israel heard of it"*, followed by a break in the verse — **and
  [[Simeon]] and [[Levi]] for 34:25–29**, where Jacob's only objection was that the massacre had made
  him *"a stench to the inhabitants of the land"* (34:30). **The deathbed supplies the moral term both
  chapters withheld**, fourteen and fifteen chapters late, **and in Simeon and Levi's case adds a
  charge the narrative never made** — *"in their self-will they hamstrung cattle."* **The sentences
  are carried out in the tribal history and never revisited in Genesis**
- **Genesis 50:15, 19–20 — the payment expected and refused**: *wəhāšēb yāšîb*, *he will fully pay us
  back*, answered with *"am I in the place of God?"* — [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]

- [[Let the Day Perish]] — Job 3:1–26; **the doctrine conspicuously absent from the speech that
  provokes it.** Job does not argue that he is innocent, does not ask what he has done, does not
  accuse God of injustice, and makes no claim about how the world is governed. **The doctrine enters
  the book with Eliphaz at 4:7, not here** — which means the friends spend twenty-two chapters
  answering a case that had not been made, and forced Job into making it. **They cannot hear a
  lament as a lament, because their theology has no category for suffering that is not evidence**
- [[Eliphaz's First Speech]] — Job 4:7–8, **the doctrine's first statement in the dialogue, and it is
  offered as an observation**: *"who ever perished, being innocent? … According to what I have seen,
  those who plow iniquity, and sow trouble, reap the same."* The reader has the counter-example on the
  page already — ten children and a household of servants, belonging to a man God called blameless.
  **The same speech then promises the doctrine's reward** (5:19–26), and the epilogue pays it in
  full (42:10–17) — **to the man who refused the doctrine, after God has said its spokesman was
  wrong.** See [[A Shock of Grain in Its Season - Eliphaz's Promise and the Epilogue]]
- [[The Arrows of the Almighty]] — Job 6:21, ***"You see a terror, and are afraid."*** **The
  doctrine diagnosed as the friends' own protection.** If Job is innocent and suffering anyway,
  nothing keeps it from happening to them; the doctrine is a wall around the men who hold it as much
  as an explanation of the man it is applied to. Job asks to be shown his fault (6:24) and nobody
  shows him one
- [[Bildad's First Speech]] — Job 8:3–7, 20; **the doctrine as a five-verse syllogism.** God does
  not pervert justice (8:3); therefore the dead children sinned (8:4); therefore a pure man who seeks
  God will be restored (8:5–7). **The children are its first casualties in the dialogue**, named as
  proof by the first friend willing to name them. And its promise comes true verbatim — *"your latter
  end would greatly increase"* (8:7) against *"Yahweh blessed the latter end of Job more than his
  beginning"* (42:12) — for a reason the doctrine cannot supply.
- [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]] — Job 9:22–24; **the doctrine denied in its own vocabulary.** *"He destroys the
  blameless and the wicked"* answers Bildad's *"God will not cast away a blameless man"* (8:20),
  and *"the earth is given into the hand of the wicked"* turns the friends' whole picture over.
  *"If not he, then who is it?"* — the prologue's answer is two-sided and Job never hears it.
- [[Zophar's First Speech]] — Job 11:6, ***"God exacts of you less than your iniquity deserves."*** **The doctrine at
  its most severe and least falsifiable**: Eliphaz and Bildad read suffering as the measure of sin;
  Zophar says it *under*measures it, so no loss can ever be too large to count as deserved. The
  epilogue answers at both ends — Job was wounded *"without cause"* (2:3) and is repaid *"twice as
  much"* (42:10).
- [[Though He Slay Me]] — Job 12:6, 17–25; 13:7–10; **refuted by observation, by doxology, and by
  turning it on the friends.** *"The tents of robbers prosper"* (12:6); a nine-verse hymn in which God
  humbles counselors, judges, kings and priests with no word that they deserved it (12:17–25) — the
  reversal-of-fortunes hymn of Psalm 107:40–41 with the reversal removed; and the friends' lies on
  God's behalf named as the thing God will punish (13:10). See
  [[He Pours Contempt on Princes - Job 12.21-24 and Psalm 107.40]]
- [[Why Do the Wicked Live]] — Job 21:1–34; **the frontal assault, and the first argued on the
  doctrine's own ground.** The friends' three portraits (15:20–35; 18:5–21; 20:4–29) inverted line by
  line: the wicked grow old, keep their children, live *"safe from fear"*, and go down to Sheol
  *"in an instant"* (21:7–13). Bildad's lamp put to a count — *"How often…?"* (21:17). The fallback
  that iniquity is stored up for the children refused as both unjust and useless, since the dead
  father will not know (21:19–21). Two deaths, one whole and one bitter, and the same worm (21:23–26);
  the wicked man's honoured burial (21:32–33). **Job does not claim the wicked are never punished,
  only that it happens too seldom to read a life by** — which is all he needs to remove the inference
  from his ruin to his guilt. See [[The Prosperity of the Wicked]] and
  [[Visiting the Iniquity of the Fathers - Job 21.19, Jeremiah 31, and Ezekiel 18]].
- [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] — Job 22:1–30; **the doctrine generating its own evidence.**
  *"Therefore snares are around you"* (22:10) runs the inference backwards from suffering to sin,
  and 22:5–9 supplies the sins: the canonical list of mercy negated clause by clause, attached to
  Job by name, and unsupported by anything in the book. The speech then offers the doctrine's reward
  on its usual condition (22:21–30), and every reward is delivered in chapter 42 without the
  condition. See
  [[Bread to the Hungry and Clothing to the Naked - Job 22.6-9, Job 31, and Matthew 25]].
- [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]] — Job 24:1–24; **the doctrine tested on everyone but Job,
  and then apparently restated.** *"Why aren't times laid up by the Almighty?"* (24:1): the
  oppressors of 24:2–11 and the night's criminals of 24:13–17 go unpunished, and *"God doesn't
  regard the folly"* (24:12). Then 24:18–20 describes the wicked swept away and swallowed by Sheol,
  in the friends' own idiom. The wiki reads those verses as a citation of the friends or as Job's
  imprecation, answered by 24:22–24 — God gives the mighty security, and they die *"as all others"*.
  Other readings, including a displaced fragment of Zophar, are recorded on the passage page.
- [[Bildad's Third Speech]] — Job 25:1–6; **the doctrine abandoned for a broader one.** No wicked
  man, no portrait, no particular guilt — only the claim that no human being can be clean before God.
  It answers Job's dare (24:25) by making his particular case irrelevant, and in doing so gives up the
  ground the friends had held since chapter 4: retribution needed Job to be guilty of something;
  universal impurity does not need anything.
- [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]] — Job 27:13–23; **the doctrine in Job's mouth, and a flagged
  contradiction.** *"This is the portion of a wicked man with God"* (27:13) repeats Zophar's 20:29,
  and the portrait that follows contradicts Job's chapter 21 point by point — children *"for the
  sword"* (27:14) against children *"established"* (21:8); *"A storm steals him away in the night"*
  (27:20) against *"How often is it that they are … as chaff that the storm carries away?"* (21:18),
  the same Hebrew phrase. **Both claims stand in the received text under Job's name.** Readings
  (Zophar's lost speech; imprecation on the false accuser, 27:7; concession of the end without the
  inference; citation) are on the passage page and at
  [[The Portion of a Wicked Man - Job 20.29 and Job 27.13]]; the wiki does not choose between them
- [[Now They Mock Me]] — Job 30; **the friends' portrait of the wicked man, worn by Job.** Bildad's
  *terrors* that *"chase him at his heels"* (18:11) — *"Terrors have turned on me. They chase my honor as
  the wind"* (30:15); the wicked man's lost name (18:17) — given, as *bəlî-šēm*, to the men who mock Job
  (30:8); the darkness (30:26), the ruin and the jackals (30:14, 29). The retribution reading of the
  chapter is available to anyone who wants it, and Job states every fact it needs without once
  conceding the inference.
- [[Job's Oath of Innocence]] — Job 31:2–3; *"Is it not calamity to the unrighteous, and disaster to the workers of
  iniquity?"* **Job still holds the doctrine.** He has argued since chapter 21 that it does not describe
  the world, and here he stakes his life on it as a rule for himself: every clause invokes a penalty that
  only a retributive God could execute. The oath is the friends' theology turned into a dare.
- [[Elihu Speaks in Anger]] — Job 32:2–3; 33:12–30; **the doctrine revised, not dropped.** Elihu is angry at the friends
  for condemning Job *"and yet"* having *"found no answer"* (32:3), and replaces the friends' sequence —
  sin, then suffering as payment — with his own: suffering first, as a warning, so that the man turns
  before the sin is done (33:16–18). But the sequence still ends in *"I have sinned, and perverted that
  which was right"* (33:27), and its premise is still that the sufferer needs correcting.
- [[Elihu on God's Justice]] — Job 34:11; 35:6–8; **the doctrine in its plainest words.** *"For the work of a
  man he will render to him, and cause every man to find according to his ways"* (34:11) — the formula of
  Psalm 62:12 and Proverbs 24:12, which Romans 2:6 quotes. Elihu grounds it not in a ledger but in God's
  position: appointed by no one (34:13), he has no motive to be unjust. Chapter 35 then removes God's
  stake: *"If you are righteous, what do you give him?"* (35:7). The doctrine survives, and the reason for
  God to care about Job's case is gone with it — the one thing the prologue (1:8; 2:3) shows is false.
- [[Elihu on God's Greatness]] — Job 36:6–14; **the doctrine restated against Job's chapter 21.** *"He
  doesn't preserve the life of the wicked, but gives to the afflicted their right"* (36:6) — *lōʾ-yəḥayyeh*,
  the verb of Job's *"Why do the wicked live?"* (21:7), contradicted. The righteous sit *"with kings on the
  throne"* (36:7); the listening prosper and the deaf *"perish by the sword"* (36:11–12); the godless *"die in
  youth"* (36:14). Elihu's addition is the middle term — affliction as instruction (36:15) — between the
  deed and the outcome.
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:13–15, 26–27; **the doctrine neither affirmed nor applied.** The
  wicked appear once: the dawn shakes them out and *"their light is withheld"* — a daily judgment by
  daybreak, not a verdict on a case. And rain falls *"on a land where no man is"* (38:26), weather that
  is no one's reward or punishment. The friends' system is not refuted by argument; the world God
  describes simply does not run on it.
- [[Behemoth and Leviathan]] — Job 40:11–14; 41:11; **the ledger closed.** God does not claim the wicked are
  always crushed; he offers Job the task (40:11–13). And *"Who has first given to me, that I should repay
  him?"* (41:11) removes the premise of retribution and of Job's suit alike: God is in no one's debt. See
  [[Who Has First Given to Me - Job 41.11 in Romans 11.35]].
- [[The Restoration of Job]] — Job 42:7–17; **the outcome delivered, the mechanism refused.** The friends' scheme promised
  restoration in return for confession. The epilogue gives the restoration — doubled flocks, a family at the table,
  a long life — but after a verdict that the friends were wrong, and *"when he prayed for his friends"* (42:10), not
  when he confessed. Bildad's 8:7 comes true in its own nouns (42:12); its condition was met at 1:1. The children
  are not doubled, and no reason is given. See [[A Shock of Grain in Its Season - Eliphaz's Promise and the Epilogue]]
  and [[Yahweh Turned the Captivity of Job - Restoration and the Double]].
- [[The Oppression in Egypt]] — Exodus 1:22; **the river turned back.** What Egypt does to Israel's sons in the water,
  water will do to Egypt — the Nile to blood (7:20), and the sea where Pharaoh's army is cast (15:4)
- [[The Birth of Moses]] — Exodus 2:3; **the *sûp*.** The infant is laid *"in the reeds"* — *bassûp*
  — and the sea where *"Pharaoh's chariots and his army"* are cast is *yam sûp* (15:4). The water
  meant for Hebrew sons receives Egypt's soldiers
- [[Water Turned to Blood]] — Exodus 7:17–21; **the river of the decree turned to blood.** Exodus
  gives the plague's purpose as knowledge (*"In this you shall know"*) and does not connect it to 1:22.
  **Later readers did**: Wisdom of Solomon 11:6–7 calls it a rebuke of *"the decree for the slaying of
  babes"*, and Revelation 16:6 says of the waters turned to blood, *"they poured out the blood of the
  saints and prophets, and you have given them blood to drink"* (both WEB). The measure-for-measure
  reading is theirs — [[Waters Turned to Blood - The First Plague in the Psalms and Revelation]]
- [[The Last Plague Announced]] — Exodus 11:5–6; **the sons for the sons, if it is read that way.**
  The decree against Israel's boys (1:16, 22) is answered by the death of Egypt's firstborn — but
  the decree was [[Pharaoh (of the oppression)]]'s, and the blow falls on his successor's house and
  on the slave girl *"behind the mill"*. **The text draws no measure-for-measure line itself**; the
  cry of the oppressed answered by the oppressor's cry is written into law at 22:23–24 —
  [[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]]

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:5–6, 12; generational consequences and promised long life do
  not yield an infallible diagnosis

- [[Laws of Violence and Injury]] — Exodus 21:12–36; distinctions and remedies for injury and loss
- [[Laws of Property and Restitution]] — Exodus 22:1–9; multiplied repayment for theft and false claims
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:24; widows and orphans for those who wronged them

- [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]] — Proverbs 1:17–19; the ambush turned round by repeating its own verbs; the contradiction with Job 12:6 and 24:14 recorded
- [[Wisdom Calls in the Street]] — Proverbs 1:31–32; refusers filled with their own counsel; the tensions with Job 9:23 and Deuteronomy 4:29 recorded
- [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] — Proverbs 2:7, 21–22; the upright shielded and kept in the land, the wicked torn out; the tension with Job 1:1 recorded
- [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]] — Proverbs 3:1–12; long life, health, and full barns promised, and reproof for the son Yahweh loves
- [[The Lamp of the Commandment]] — Proverbs 6:27–35; fire that burns its carrier, and the toucher
  touched back
- [[Wisdom Calls at the Gates]] — Proverbs 8:18–21; riches and honour promised to Wisdom's lovers, set against Job 1
- [[Wisdom at the Creation]] — Proverbs 8:35–36; life and favour to whoever finds Wisdom, death loved by her haters, read after Job
- [[The Two Feasts]] — Proverbs 9:11–12; days multiplied by Wisdom, and *"If you mock, you alone will bear it"*: each carries his own choice; Job's case kept beside it
- [[A Good Name]] — Proverbs 22:4, 8, 16; Sowing and reaping, kept beside Eliphaz's misuse of the image.
- [[Do Not Envy Sinners]] — Proverbs 23:18, 21, 32; The latter end as the measure: hope, rags, or a snakebite.
- [[Through Wisdom a House Is Built]] — Proverbs 24:12, 16, 20, 22; Each rendered according to his work; the righteous fall and rise, the wicked are overthrown.
- [[The Field of the Sluggard]] — Proverbs 24:24–25, 29, 34; Rendering according to work belongs to God and the court, not the wronged man.
