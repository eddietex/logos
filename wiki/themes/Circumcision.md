# Circumcision

## Definition / overview

The removal of the foreskin, commanded at Genesis 17:10 as the sign of the covenant with Abraham,
performed on the eighth day of life, and carried on the body of every male of the household —
including those *"bought with money from any foreigner"*. It is the Bible's first and only physical
covenant mark, the oldest continuously observed Jewish rite, and the single practice the New
Testament spends the most words arguing about.

The Hebrew verb is **mûl**, to cut or cut off, and the noun for the rite in later Judaism is
*bərît mîlâ*, **the covenant of circumcision** — a phrase that treats the sign and the covenant as
one thing, exactly as Genesis 17:14 does when it calls omission of the sign *"He has broken my
covenant."*

**The rite is not unique to Israel and the Bible knows it.** Egyptian tomb reliefs at Saqqara show
the operation being performed; Jeremiah 9:25–26 lists Egypt, Judah, Edom, Ammon, Moab, and the
desert peoples together as *circumcised in the flesh*; and the Philistines are the Old Testament's
standing example of the *uncircumcised*, which is why the word functions as an insult from Judges
14:3 to 1 Samuel 17:26. **What Genesis 17 does that the neighbours did not is move the age.**
Elsewhere circumcision is a puberty or marriage rite, performed on someone old enough to have chosen
it or to have endured it; Genesis puts it on an infant of eight days, before any consent, merit, or
memory — which is the same logic the chapter applies to the covenant itself.

**And it makes the sign private.** The other covenant sign in Genesis is the rainbow, which is
public, universal, and God's to look at (9:16). Circumcision is on one household, in the flesh, on
the men, and visible to almost nobody. That is the feature every later argument about it works with:
a mark that can be perfectly kept and entirely missed.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 17:1–27** — the command, in full and in one chapter. Every male; on the eighth day;
  *"born in the house, or bought with money"*; a *token* (*ʾôt*) of the covenant; *"an everlasting
  covenant"* in the flesh; and *kārēt*, being *"cut off from his people"*, for the one who is not
  cut. Abraham complies the same day at ninety-nine, and Ishmael at thirteen. See
  [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]].
- **Genesis 21:4** — Isaac circumcised at eight days: the command's first ordinary performance.
- **Genesis 34:13–24** — the rite weaponized. Jacob's sons demand it of the men of Shechem as a
  marriage condition and kill them on the third day while they are in pain. Genesis calls the
  proposal *deceit* in the same verse it reports it, and the episode is the Bible's own warning that
  the sign can be detached from everything it means.
- **Exodus 4:24–26** — the strangest passage in the Torah. Yahweh meets Moses at a lodging place and
  seeks to kill him; Zipporah cuts her son's foreskin with a flint, touches Moses' feet with it, and
  says *"You are a bridegroom of blood to me."* Nothing about it is explained.
- **Exodus 12:43–49** — circumcision as the condition for eating the Passover, and therefore the
  door through which a foreigner enters Israel: *"when a stranger lives as a foreigner with you, and
  will keep the Passover to Yahweh, let all his males be circumcised … he shall be as one who is born
  in the land."* The rite that marks a boundary is also the only means of crossing it.
- **Leviticus 12:3** — the eighth day put into law, and the reason it overrides the Sabbath in later
  halakhah.
- **Joshua 5:2–9** — the exodus generation, uncircumcised through forty years in the wilderness, is
  circumcised at Gilgal with flint knives before the land is entered. *"Today I have rolled away the
  reproach of Egypt from you."*
- **Deuteronomy 10:16; 30:6** — the metaphor's first appearance, and then its reversal. *"Circumcise
  therefore the foreskin of your heart"* becomes *"Yahweh your God will circumcise your heart, and
  the heart of your offspring"* — a command that becomes a promise.
- **Jeremiah 4:4; 6:10; 9:25–26** — the prophet's sharpest use: ears uncircumcised, hearts
  uncircumcised, and a list in which Israel is grouped with the nations precisely *because* everyone
  on it is circumcised in the flesh only.
- **1 Maccabees 1:15, 48, 60–61** — under Antiochus IV the rite is banned on pain of death, Hellenized
  Jews surgically disguise it, and mothers are executed with their infants hung around their necks for
  keeping it. This is where circumcision becomes the thing worth dying for, and it stays that under
  Hadrian.
- **Acts 15:1–29** — the church's founding controversy, and the only one it settles by council:
  *"Unless you are circumcised after the custom of Moses, you can't be saved"* is put to Jerusalem and
  rejected. Gentile believers are not bound to Genesis 17:10. See
  [[The Noahic Commandments and the Council of Jerusalem]].
- **Romans 2:25–29** — *"he is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is that of the heart, in
  the spirit, not in the letter"*: Jeremiah's move, made the criterion.
- **Romans 4:9–12** — Paul's argument from the order of the Genesis chapters. Abraham was credited
  with righteousness at 15:6 and circumcised at 17:10, therefore *"he received the sign of
  circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while he was in
  uncircumcision"*, and therefore he is father of the uncircumcised who believe as well as of the
  circumcised. See [[The Sign and the Seal - Genesis 17 in Romans 4]].
- **1 Corinthians 7:18–19; Galatians 5:2–6; 6:15; Philippians 3:2–5; Colossians 2:11–12** — the five
  other verdicts. *"Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the
  commandments of God"*; *"neither circumcision is anything, nor uncircumcision, but a new creation"*;
  and, at Colossians 2:11, a *"circumcision not made with hands"* set beside baptism — the verse every
  later argument about infant baptism is fought over.

## Key passages

- [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]] — Genesis 17: the command, the age, the household scope,
  the sanction, and the same-day obedience
- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — the other covenant sign in Genesis, and the contrast that defines this
  one: public, universal, unconditioned, and kept by God rather than by the human party
- [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] — the covenant this one signs, sworn by God alone two chapters
  earlier with nothing asked of Abram
- [[Circumcision of the Heart]] — Deuteronomy, Jeremiah, and Paul on a sign that can be kept in the
  flesh and missed entirely
- [[The Sign and the Seal - Genesis 17 in Romans 4]] — the order of the chapters, and what Paul builds
  on it
- [[Hagar and Sarah in Galatians]] — the letter in which the whole question comes to a head
- [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]] — Genesis 21:4, *"Abraham circumcised his son,
  Isaac, when he was eight days old, as God had commanded him"*. **The first eighth-day circumcision
  in Scripture**, executing 17:12 exactly and establishing the age the law will fix at Leviticus 12:3.
  His father had been ninety-nine and his brother thirteen; Isaac is the first person to receive the
  sign before he can know what it is, which is the whole of what the wiki says about him elsewhere
