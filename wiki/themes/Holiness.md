# Holiness

## Definition / overview

**Holiness in Scripture is first a matter of belonging and distance, and only later of moral
character.** The Hebrew root ***q-d-š*** marks what is set apart for God and therefore not available
for common use — a day, a place, an object, a people. **The first holy thing in the Bible is a time**:
God *"blessed the seventh day, and made it holy"* (Genesis 2:3), *wayqaddēš*. **The second is a place**:
*"Take off your sandals, for the place you are standing on is holy ground"* (Exodus 3:5),
***ʾadmat-qōdeš*** — and that verse is the first occurrence of the noun *qōdeš* in Scripture.

**Between those two verses, Genesis does not call anything holy.** The patriarchs build altars, set up
pillars, and name places for what God did there — *"Surely Yahweh is in this place, and I didn't know
it"* (Genesis 28:16) — but the word is held back. **When it comes, it comes with a boundary**: *"Don't
come close"* (Exodus 3:5). The ground is not holy because of anything in the ground. It is holy because
God is speaking from it, and the holiness lasts as long as the presence does; nobody in Scripture comes
back to the bush.

**Holiness is dangerous before it is good.** Moses hides his face *"for he was afraid to look at God"*
(3:6); the mountain will be fenced on pain of death (19:12); and the tabernacle, priesthood, and purity
laws of Exodus 25–Leviticus are, read together, a structure for letting a holy God live in the middle
of an unholy camp without destroying it. **The burning bush is that problem solved once, in miniature**:
fire that dwells in a bush and does not consume it.

**And then it becomes a calling.** *"You shall be to me a kingdom of priests, and a holy nation"*
(Exodus 19:6); *"You shall be holy; for I, Yahweh your God, am holy"* (Leviticus 19:2). **What was a
property of ground becomes a demand on people**, and the demand rests on God's own character. The New
Testament quotes the Leviticus sentence as a rule of conduct: *"You shall be holy; for I am holy"*
(1 Peter 1:16).

**Exodus 19 brings the holy nation and the guarded mountain into one scene.** The vocation of
19:6 does not abolish distance: the people wash, Moses sanctifies them, the mountain is bounded,
and even priests must sanctify themselves and must not break through (19:10–24). Holiness is
both belonging and a limit on access. The danger includes animals, so it cannot be reduced to
a verdict on individual moral guilt. See [[Israel at Mount Sinai]].

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 2:3** — the seventh day *made holy*: the root's first appearance, and its only one in
  Genesis applied by God to anything. See [[Sabbath]].
- **Exodus 3:5** — *holy ground*; the noun's first appearance, and the first holy place. See
  [[The Burning Bush]].
- *(ahead)* **Exodus 15:11** — *"Who is like you, glorious in holiness?"* — the first time God himself
  is called holy, in the Song of the Sea.
- **Exodus 19** — the holy mountain, boundary, and holy nation; [[Israel at Mount Sinai]].
- *(ahead)* **Exodus 25–40; Leviticus** — the holy place and the most holy, holy garments, holy oil,
  *"HOLY TO YAHWEH"* on the high priest's forehead (Exodus 28:36), and the purity code.
- *(ahead)* **Joshua 5:15** — *"Take off your shoes; for the place on which you stand is holy"*: the
  bush's command repeated to Moses' successor, outside Jericho.
- *(ahead)* **Isaiah 6:3** — *"Holy, holy, holy, is Yahweh of Armies!"*, and the prophet's *"Woe is
  me"*.
- *(ahead)* **1 Peter 1:15–16** — Leviticus 19:2 applied to the church.

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:8–11

Keeping the Sabbath holy is now expressly commanded (20:8). The day belongs to Yahweh, and the
assembly's abstention from work follows God's own blessing and making holy of the seventh day
(20:10–11). The holiness encountered as dangerous nearness on the mountain also orders ordinary time
throughout the household. See [[The Ten Commandments]].

### An altar a tool profanes — Exodus 20:25–26

Profanation is named here of an object: a
tool lifted on the altar's stones has *"polluted"* it (*wattəḥalləlehā*, from *ḥālal*, WLC). **What
profanes the altar is human work done to it**, and its approach is shaped so that no nakedness is
exposed (20:26). Jeremiah 34:16 uses the same verb of Judah taking back freed servants: *"you ...
profaned my name"*. See [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

**Exodus 22:31 turns the promise of 19:6 into a rule.** *"A holy nation"* (*gôy qādôš*)
becomes *wəʾanšê-qōdeš tihyûn lî*, *you shall be men of holiness to me* (MAM 22:30), and the first
thing it requires is dietary: flesh *"torn by animals in the field"* is not eaten but *"cast … to the
dogs"*. **The ordinances' first statement of their own purpose is holiness, and it closes a unit
mostly about protecting the weak.** See [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].

### Three zones on the mountain — Exodus 24:1–2, 9–18

Exodus 24 grades access to God by place: the people at the foot, forbidden to go up; the seventy-four
leaders partway, told to *"worship from a distance"*; and Moses alone allowed to *"come near"* (24:1–2),
who then goes into the cloud (24:18). The same three-part pattern will shape the tabernacle — court,
holy place, and most holy place — though Exodus 24 does not draw the comparison. See [[The Blood of the Covenant]].

**There is a boundary within the sanctuary itself.** In Exodus 26:33 the veil separates the holy
place from the most holy. The text does not contrast a holy room with an unholy room: both belong
to the sanctuary, while the ark and mercy seat occupy the inner one. Leviticus 16:2 later forbids
unrestricted entry within the veil; that restriction is not yet stated in Exodus 26. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]] and [[The Veil - Separation in Exodus and Access in Hebrews]].

### The altar, court, and maintained light — Exodus 27:1–21

Exodus 27:9–21 adds a court with a defined entrance to the tent's two rooms. The lamp is
explicitly tended outside the veil before the testimony. These are spatial distinctions;
this chapter does not provide a complete list of who may enter each space. The embroidered
court gate is also distinct from the tent entrance screen and the inner veil of chapter 26.
See [[The Altar and the Court]].

### The priestly garments — Exodus 28:1–43

The garments are *"holy garments"* (28:2, 4), made *"to sanctify"* Aaron (28:3). A gold plate on
his forehead reads *"HOLY TO YAHWEH"*, and he bears *"the iniquity of the holy things"* Israel
consecrates (28:36–38). Holiness here is dangerous as well as honourable: the bells and the
breeches are both required so that the priest does *"not die"* (28:35, 43). See [[The Priestly Garments]].

### The ordination of the priests — Exodus 29:1–46

The rite is *"to make them holy"* (29:1). Priests and garments are made holy together (29:21). The
priests' food is holy and barred to a *"stranger"* (29:33–34). The altar, atoned for seven days,
becomes *"most holy"* (*qōdeš qodāšîm*, MAM), and *"whatever touches the altar shall be holy"* (29:37).
God then claims the sanctifying act for himself: *"I will sanctify the Tent of Meeting and the
altar: Aaron also and his sons I will sanctify"* (29:44). Holiness here spreads by contact, and
God is its source. See [[The Ordination of the Priests]].

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30:1–38

The phrase *"most holy"* (*qōdeš qodāšîm*, MAM) is used three times in the chapter: of the incense
altar (30:10), of the anointed tent and furniture (30:29), and of the incense (30:36). The anointed
things pass holiness on: *"Whatever touches them shall be holy"* (30:29), as with the altar at 29:37.
The oil and the incense are holy in a second sense. Their recipes belong to God: copied for private
use, they bring the penalty of being *"cut off from his people"* (30:32–33, 37–38). See
[[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]].

## Key passages

- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:5; ***ʾadmat-qōdeš***, **the first holiness of a place, and the
  first *qōdeš* in the Bible.** It comes with two commands — *"Don't come close"* and *"Take off your
  sandals"* — and no explanation of either. The holiness is God's presence and nothing else: Horeb is
  not called holy before the bush and is not called holy because of it afterwards. Moses' response is
  fear (*"afraid to look at God"*), not worship. Joshua 5:15 repeats the command almost word for word
- [[The Passover]] — Exodus 12:16; ***"a holy convocation"*** on the first and seventh days.
  **The first holy time in Exodus**; the only holy thing before it was the ground at the bush (3:5;
  WLC lemma search of *qōdeš*)
- [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] — Exodus 13:2; ***"Sanctify to me all the firstborn"*** — *qaddeš*,
  **the piel of *qādaš* for the first time since God *"made"* the seventh day *"holy"*** (Genesis 2:3; WLC
  lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). **The first holy thing Israel is told to make holy is a class of persons
  and animals**, defined by birth order and claimed with *"It is mine"*
- [[The Song of the Sea]] — Exodus 15:11, 13, 17; **holiness sung.** *"Glorious in holiness"*; *"your holy habitation"*;
  and ***miqdāš***, *"the sanctuary"*, its first occurrence in Scripture, ten chapters before *"Let them make
  me a sanctuary"* (25:8; WLC lemma search). **The holy place is praised before it is built.**
- [[Manna and Quail]] — Exodus 16:23; ***"a holy Sabbath to Yahweh"*** — **the holy thing is a day again**, as
  at Genesis 2:3, and this time one in every week; the first *šabbāt* in Scripture
- [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] — Exodus 19:6, 10–24; a holy people prepared for a bounded mountain

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:8–11; the holy day orders ordinary household time
- [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]] — Exodus 20:25–26; a tool profanes the altar, and its approach
  guards against exposure
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:31; *"You shall be holy men to me"*, and torn meat refused
- [[The Blood of the Covenant]] — Exodus 24:1–2; people, leaders, and Moses at three distances from God
- [[The Offering for the Sanctuary]] — Exodus 25:8; ***"Let them make me a sanctuary"*** — *miqdāš*, its second
  occurrence in Scripture after the Song of the Sea (15:17; MAM search, Genesis–Exodus 25). **The holy place
  sung about at the sea is now commissioned**, and it is to be built from a freewill offering
- [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] — Exodus 25:10–40; *"pure gold"* eight times, and an ark whose poles *"shall not be taken from
  it"* (25:15). It is carried, never handled

- [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]] — Exodus 26:1–37; The veil distinguishes the holy place from the most holy (26:33).

- [[The Altar and the Court]] — Exodus 27:1–21; The court and veil distinguish spaces; the lamp is tended outside the veil.
- [[The Priestly Garments]] — Exodus 28:1–43; holy garments, the plate *"HOLY TO YAHWEH"*, and death warnings at the holy place.
- [[The Ordination of the Priests]] — Exodus 29:1, 21, 33–37, 43–44; priests, garments, food, and altar made holy; holiness by touch.
- [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] — Exodus 30:10, 29, 32–38; *"most holy"* three times, holiness by touch, and recipes reserved to God.
