# Adoption

## Definition / overview

**Taking as one's own a child who was not born to one.** Genesis reaches the thing itself only once, at
**48:5**, where [[Jacob]] says of [[Joseph]]'s two Egyptian-born sons ***"they are mine … even as
[[Reuben]] and [[Simeon]], will be mine."*** **Everything else in the book that looks like adoption is
something else** — surrogacy through a slave, a foundling raised in a household, an heir named for want
of a son — **and the wiki keeps the distinctions, because the Torah has no adoption law and Genesis
supplies no vocabulary for it.**

**There is no Hebrew verb for *to adopt*.** What Scripture has instead is a set of gestures and
formulae, and Genesis 48 uses three of them in one scene:

| mechanism | verse | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **the declaration** | 48:5 | ***lî-hēm***, *they are mine*, said twice |
| **the knees** | 48:12 | *"brought them out from between his knees"* — cf. [[Rachel]] at 30:3 |
| **the name** | 48:16 | *"let my name be named on them, and the name of my fathers"* |

**Only the first is unambiguous.** The other two are read as adoption gestures on the strength of
parallels inside and outside Scripture, and the wiki says so where it uses them.

## Development across Scripture

### What Genesis has before chapter 48

**Three near-misses, all of them about getting an heir rather than about taking a child.**

- **The named steward.** [[Abraham]] at 15:2–3, *"one born in my house is my heir"* —
  [[Eliezer of Damascus]]. **God refuses the arrangement** (15:4). **It is inheritance by default, not
  adoption.**
- **Surrogacy through a slave.** [[Sarah]] and [[Hagar]] (16:2, *"it may be that I will obtain children
  by her"*), [[Rachel]] and [[Bilhah]] (30:3), [[Leah]] and [[Zilpah]] (30:9). **The child is the
  husband's by blood and the wife's by claim**, and Rachel's *"that she may bear on my knees"* is the
  gesture Genesis 48:12 reuses. **Four of the twelve tribes arrive this way.** See [[Barrenness]].
- **A daughter's household absorbed.** [[Lot]] under Abram, [[Jacob]]'s household under [[Laban]] — but
  Laban's *"the daughters are my daughters, and the children are my children"* (31:43) **is a property
  claim in a dispute, not a transfer.** See [[Brotherhood]].

### Genesis 48

**The only formal adoption in the book**, and the most consequential act of legal speech in it after
[[Circumcision]]. See [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]].

**Three features are worth naming.**

1. **It is a grandfather adopting grandsons**, which raises their generation by one and makes them
   tribes rather than clans. **The comparison, *"even as [[Reuben]] and [[Simeon]]"*, assigns rank as
   well as sonship** — first and second — **and the next chapter strips both of those positions from
   the men named** (49:3–7). 1 Chronicles 5:1–2 states the transfer outright.
2. **It is bounded.** *"Your issue, whom you become the father of after them, will be yours"* (48:6).
   **Two named persons, not a policy.** No further sons of Joseph are recorded, so the clause never
   operates.
3. **The adoptees are half-foreign.** Their mother is [[Asenath]], daughter of a priest of [[On]]
   (41:45). **The two largest tribes of the northern kingdom enter Israel by declaration rather than by
   descent.** See [[The Nations]].

### After Genesis

- **Moses** — drawn from the river and *"he became her son"* (Exodus 2:10). **Adoption into a foreign
  royal house, and the reverse direction of Genesis 48.**
- **Genubath** in Egypt (1 Kings 11:20), **Esther by Mordecai** — *"took her for his own daughter"*
  (Esther 2:7, 15) — **and Jehosheba hiding Joash** (2 Kings 11:2). **All outside the land or outside
  the law, and all narrated without legal machinery.**
- **The king as God's son** — *"You are my son. Today I have become your father"* (Psalm 2:7);
  *"I will be his father, and he will be my son"* (2 Samuel 7:14). **A declaratory formula of exactly
  the Genesis 48:5 shape**, applied to a dynasty.
- **Israel as God's firstborn** — *"Israel is my son, my firstborn"* (Exodus 4:22); *"When Israel was a
  child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt"* (Hosea 11:1). **The nation is related to God
  by declaration, and the declaration uses the birth-order word Genesis 48 had just finished
  rearranging.** See [[Birthright and the Firstborn]].
- **Paul's *huiothesia*** — Romans 8:15, 23; 9:4; Galatians 4:5; Ephesians 1:5. **The only word in
  Scripture that means *adoption* and it is Greek, drawn from Roman law rather than from Israel's.**
  **Romans 9:4 assigns it to Israel as a possession** — *"who are Israelites; whose is the adoption"* —
  **which is the New Testament reading back over Exodus 4:22 and, behind it, Genesis 48.**

### The pattern

**Every adoption in Scripture is accomplished by someone saying so.** There is no rite, no document,
and no law. **Jacob's *they are mine*, Pharaoh's daughter's *he became her son*, the psalm's *you are my
son*, and Paul's *spirit of adoption* are all speech acts**, and in each case the status conferred is
immediately full: [[Ephraim]] and [[Manasseh]] are not junior tribes, and the adopted son in Romans 8
is an heir. **The wiki notes the consistency and does not build a doctrine of adoption in the Old
Testament out of it**, since Genesis 48 is a single case and the Torah legislates nothing.

## Key passages

- [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]] — **Genesis 48:5–6, 12, 16.** The declaration, the knees, and
  the naming; **the Bible's first adoption and its only formula spoken by the adopting parent**
- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 30:3, 9. ***"That she may bear on my knees"*** —
  surrogacy, and the gesture Genesis 48:12 reuses
- [[Hagar and Ishmael]] — Genesis 16:2. *"Obtain children by her"*; **the first claim on a child not
  borne**
- [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] — Genesis 15:2–4. **[[Eliezer of Damascus]] as heir, and God's
  refusal of the arrangement**
- **Genesis 50:23 — the idiom's second and last use in Genesis**: [[Machir]]'s children *"born on
  Joseph's knees"*, the phrase [[Rachel]] used of [[Bilhah]] at 30:3 —
  [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]

- [[Blessing]] — the adoption of 48:5 is executed inside the blessing of 48:15–16

  adoption
- [[Covenant]] — **membership by declaration rather than by descent**, which is what [[Circumcision]]

- [[Call and Election]] — **both are God, or a patriarch, saying that someone belongs**
- [[The Nations]] — **the adoptees of Genesis 48 are half-Egyptian**, and Romans 9:4 makes adoption
  Israel's own privilege
