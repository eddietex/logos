# The Dwelling of God

## Definition / overview

**God living in the middle of his people — not only appearing to them, visiting them, or meeting them
at a mountain, but staying.** The key text is Exodus 25:8: *"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may
dwell among them"* — *wəšākantî bətôkām* (MAM). The verb is ***šākan***, *to dwell* or *settle*, and its
noun ***miškān***, *dwelling place*, is the Hebrew name of the tabernacle (25:9; MAM).

The theme is related to two others in the wiki, and distinct from both:

- [[The Glory of God]] is about God's presence **made visible** — the cloud, the fire, the glory that
  appears, settles, and fills.
- [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]] traces a **promise of accompaniment** made to
  individuals who travel: Jacob, Moses, Joshua, Gideon.
- This theme is about **residence**: where God lives, how he lives among people who are not holy, and
  what has to be built, given, and guarded for that to happen.

## Development across Scripture

**Before the tabernacle, God visits.** He walks in the garden (Genesis 3:8), comes down to see Babel
(11:5), appears to the patriarchs and leaves. Jacob names a place *"God's house"* after a dream there
(28:17–19), but no one lives in it. At Sinai the glory *"settled"* (*šākan*) on the mountain for six
days (Exodus 24:16; MAM) — the verb of dwelling, used of a mountain the people may not touch.

**Exodus 25 moves the dwelling into the camp.** The first thing God says to Moses in the cloud is to
collect an offering for a sanctuary, *"that I may dwell among them"* (25:8). It is the purpose of the
whole set of instructions (25–31) and, by 29:45–46, of the exodus itself: God *"brought them out of
the land of Egypt, that I might dwell among them"*. The book ends with the glory filling the
*miškān* (40:34–35). *(29:45–46 and 40:34–35 are not yet ingested.)*

**What fills the dwelling is designed first.** Before any tent, God describes the ark, the table, and the
lampstand (25:10–40). The ark is where he will *meet* Moses and *speak* (*wənôʿadtî*, *I will meet by
appointment*, 25:22; MAM), and the bread is *"before me always"* (25:30). **On this account, God dwelling
among his people means God present to speak, with bread and light kept before him.** Exodus says nothing
there about his being seen; the Septuagint has *I will be made known to you* at 25:22 (Rahlfs). The
cherubim over the ark are the second cherubim in Scripture. The first were stationed (*wayyaškēn*, the
hiphil of *šākan*; MAM) to guard Eden (Genesis 3:24). See [[Cherubim]].

**Ahead** (by reference, *not yet ingested*; quoted from the WEB as fetched individually):

- Leviticus 26:11–12 — *"I will set my tent among you … I will walk among you, and will be your God,
  and you will be my people"*; the Hebrew of 26:11 has *miškānî*, *my dwelling* (MAM).
- 1 Kings 8:10–11, 27 — the cloud fills Solomon's temple, and Solomon asks, *"will God in very deed
  dwell on the earth? Behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens can't contain you"*.
- John 1:14 — *"The Word became flesh, and lived among us"*. The Greek verb is ***eskēnōsen***
  (SBLGNT), from *skēnē*, *tent* — the word the Septuagint uses for the tabernacle (*tēs skēnēs*, Exodus
  25:9, Rahlfs).
- Revelation 21:3 — *"Behold, God's dwelling is with people, and he will dwell with them"*;
  *hē skēnē tou theou* and *skēnōsei* (SBLGNT).

**The Septuagint hesitates at the key verse.** At Exodus 25:8 Rahlfs' text reads *ophthēsomai en
hymin*, *I will be seen among you*, not *I will dwell*. Whether the translators meant to soften the idea
of God living in a place is a common explanation, not something the text states.

**The dwelling is one structure with differentiated spaces.** Exodus 26 joins many curtains into
one enclosure (26:6, 11), then divides its interior with the veil (26:33). The promise that God will
live among the people (25:8) is not a promise that every space becomes equally accessible. The ark
and mercy seat are within the veil, the table and lampstand outside it. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].

### The altar, court, and maintained light — Exodus 27:1–21

The sanctuary's design now includes a bounded court as well as an interior tent (Exodus
27:9–19). Its use requires an ongoing contribution and assigned care: Israel supplies oil,
and Aaron and his sons keep the lamp in order outside the veil (27:20–21). God's promised
dwelling among the people thus includes sustained service, not only the making of objects.
See [[The Altar and the Court]].

### The priestly garments — Exodus 28:1–43

The garments are designed for entering *"the holy place"* and *"the Tent of Meeting"* and for
approaching the altar (Exodus 28:29, 35, 43). The dwelling now has a dress code: the one who
goes in before Yahweh must be clothed as prescribed. See [[The Priestly Garments]].

### The ordination of the priests — Exodus 29:1–46

29:45–46 is the most explicit statement of the theme in Exodus so far: *"I will dwell among the
children of Israel, and will be their God"*. God brought Israel out of Egypt *"that I might dwell
among them"* (*wəšākantî*; *ləšokənî bətôkām*, MAM). The dwelling is tied to a daily offering at a
place of meeting (29:42–43). See [[The Ordination of the Priests]] and
[[That I May Dwell Among Them - Tabernacle, Temple, Flesh, and New Creation]].

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30:1–38

The incense altar stands *"before the veil … before the mercy seat … where I will meet with you"*
(30:6), and the incense is put *"before the testimony in the Tent of Meeting, where I will meet with
you"* (30:36). The place of meeting named at the mercy seat (25:22) and the tent door (29:42) is
named twice more. See [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]].

## Key passages

- [[The Blood of the Covenant]] — Exodus 24:16; ***"Yahweh's glory settled on Mount Sinai"***, *šākan*
  on the mountain before *šākan* among the people
- [[The Offering for the Sanctuary]] — Exodus 25:1–9; ***"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell
  among them"*** (25:8); the first *miškān* in Scripture (25:9); materials given only by the willing,
  and a pattern shown by God
- [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]] — Exodus 25:10–40; **the furniture of the dwelling, designed from the centre outward.** The ark
  holds *"the testimony"*; above it, *"There I will meet with you … from between the two cherubim"* (25:22);
  bread *"before me always"* (25:30); seven lamps. The first thing designed for God's house is a place for
  him to speak from

- [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]] — Exodus 26:1–37; The promised dwelling becomes an assembled tent with an inner boundary.

- [[The Altar and the Court]] — Exodus 27:1–21; The dwelling includes an outer court and a continuing service of light.
- [[The Priestly Garments]] — Exodus 28:1–43; the priest clothed for entering the holy place.
- [[The Ordination of the Priests]] — Exodus 29:42–46; *"I will dwell among the children of Israel"*, the purpose of the exodus.
- [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] — Exodus 30:6, 36; *"where I will meet with you"*, twice.
