# The Dwelling of God

## Definition / overview

### Covenant warnings and remembered promises — Leviticus 26:14–46

The tent of 26:11 is answered by *your sanctuaries* brought to desolation (31) and exile with no
mention of a dwelling. The presence of God is not said to go with the exiles.

See [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 26.

### Blessings for obedience — Leviticus 26:1–13

*I will set my tent among you* (11, *miškānî*) is promised when the tent already stands, so the
promise concerns residence continuing rather than a first arrival. It is the last gift in the list,
before the identification in verse 13.

See [[Blessings for Obedience]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 26.

### Priestly holiness — Leviticus 21

The high priest must not leave or profane the sanctuary in the context of the death and mourning
instructions (10–12). Descendants with defects may not approach the veil or altar (23), even
though their right to holy food is retained (22). Sanctuary access and household provision have
distinct boundaries.

See [[The Holiness of the Priests]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 21.


### Penalties and separation — Leviticus 20

Giving offspring to Molech is said to defile God’s sanctuary (3), although no entry into it is
described. Sanctuary protection here includes conduct beyond the sanctuary precinct.

See [[Penalties and a People Set Apart]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 20; comparisons as cited.


**God living in the middle of his people — not only appearing to them, visiting them, or meeting them
at a mountain, but staying.** The key text is Exodus 25:8: *"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may
dwell among them"* — *wəšākantî bətôkām* (MAM). The verb is ***šākan***, *to dwell* or *settle*, and its
noun ***miškān***, *dwelling place*, is the Hebrew name of the tabernacle (25:9; MAM).

The theme is related to two others in the wiki, and distinct from both:

- [[The Glory of God]] is about God's presence **made visible** — the cloud, the fire, the glory that
  appears, settles, and fills.
- [[I Will Be With You - The Promise of Presence]] traces a **promise of accompaniment** made to
  individuals who travel: Jacob, Moses, Joshua, Gideon.
- This theme is about **residence**: where God lives, how he lives among people who are not holy, and
  what has to be built, given, and guarded for that to happen.

## Development across Scripture

### Lamps and weekly bread — Leviticus 24:1–9

The lamps stand outside the veil in the Tent of Meeting (3), while bread is arranged
before Yahweh on the gold table (6). Divine dwelling calls for recurring care, not
only the construction of furniture; holy bread then sustains the priestly family.

See [[The Lamps and the Bread Before Yahweh]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 24:1–9.

### Field and community — Leviticus 19:19–37

The trespass offering is brought to the Tent of Meeting’s door (21), and reverence for the
sanctuary is commanded (30). The speech prescribes approach without narrating a new manifestation
of divine presence.

See [[Holy Living in Field and Community]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19.


### Sacrifice and blood — Leviticus 17:1–16

The door of the Tent of Meeting is the required place of sacrifice (4–9).
The altar before the tabernacle orders slaughter within and outside the camp, while
the hunting rule supplies a different treatment of blood (13).

See [[The Place of Sacrifice and the Life in the Blood]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 17; comparisons as cited.


### Presence in the middle of uncleanness — Leviticus 16:1–34

Yahweh will *"appear in the cloud on the mercy seat"* (2), and the Tent of Meeting *"dwells with
them in the middle of their uncleanness"* (16). The annual rite is what makes continued
dwelling possible: it cleanses sanctuary, tent, and altar rather than removing the presence.
Entry into the inner room remains closed except on this one day.

See [[The Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 16; comparisons as cited.


### Bodily discharges — Leviticus 15:1–33

The tabernacle is among Israel, and defiling it in uncleanness threatens death (31).
The Tent of Meeting’s door is also where the restored persons bring their birds
(14, 29). Divine presence explains both guarded access and the provision for return.

See [[Bodily Discharges and Cleansing]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 15; comparisons as cited.

### Cleansing after healing — Leviticus 14:1–32

Restoration moves from outside the camp to camp entry, then to presentation before Yahweh at the
Tent of Meeting’s door on the eighth day (3, 8, 11, 23). The text distinguishes a person’s own
tent from the sanctuary and does not authorize unrestricted sanctuary entry.

See [[The Cleansing of the Leper]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 14:1–32; comparisons as cited.


### Purification after childbirth — Leviticus 12:1–8

The sanctuary is temporarily closed to the mother during purification; afterward she
brings her offerings to the Tent of Meeting's door (4, 6). Divine dwelling includes ordered
access and a specified restoration, not unrestricted entry.

See [[Purification After Childbirth]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 12.


**Before the tabernacle, God visits.** He walks in the garden (Genesis 3:8), comes down to see Babel
(11:5), appears to the patriarchs and leaves. Jacob names a place *"God's house"* after a dream there
(28:17–19), but no one lives in it. At Sinai the glory *"settled"* (*šākan*) on the mountain for six
days (Exodus 24:16; MAM) — the verb of dwelling, used of a mountain the people may not touch.

**Exodus 25 moves the dwelling into the camp.** The first thing God says to Moses in the cloud is to
collect an offering for a sanctuary, *"that I may dwell among them"* (25:8). It is the purpose of the
whole set of instructions (25–31) and, by 29:45–46, of the exodus itself: God *"brought them out of
the land of Egypt, that I might dwell among them"*. The book ends with the glory filling the
*miškān* (40:34–35). See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]].

**What fills the dwelling is designed first.** Before any tent, God describes the ark, the table, and the
lampstand (25:10–40). The ark is where he will *meet* Moses and *speak* (*wənôʿadtî*, *I will meet by
appointment*, 25:22; MAM), and the bread is *"before me always"* (25:30). **On this account, God dwelling
among his people means God present to speak, with bread and light kept before him.** Exodus says nothing
there about his being seen; the Septuagint has *I will be made known to you* at 25:22 (Rahlfs). The
cherubim over the ark are the second cherubim in Scripture. The first were stationed (*wayyaškēn*, the
hiphil of *šākan*; MAM) to guard Eden (Genesis 3:24). See [[Cherubim]].

- Revelation 21:3 — *"Behold, God's dwelling is with people, and he will dwell with them"*;
  *hē skēnē tou theou* and *skēnōsei* (SBLGNT).

**The Septuagint hesitates at the key verse.** At Exodus 25:8 Rahlfs' text reads *ophthēsomai en
hymin*, *I will be seen among you*, not *I will dwell*. Whether the translators meant to soften the idea
of God living in a place is a common explanation, not something the text states.

**The dwelling is one structure with differentiated spaces.** Exodus 26 joins many curtains into
one enclosure (26:6, 11), then divides its interior with the veil (26:33). The promise that God will
live among the people (25:8) is not a promise that every space becomes equally accessible. The ark
and mercy seat are within the veil, the table and lampstand outside it. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].

### The altar, court, and maintained light — Exodus 27:1–21

The sanctuary's design now includes a bounded court as well as an interior tent (Exodus
27:9–19). Its use requires an ongoing contribution and assigned care: Israel supplies oil,
and Aaron and his sons keep the lamp in order outside the veil (27:20–21). God's promised
dwelling among the people thus includes sustained service, not only the making of objects.
See [[The Altar and the Court]].

### The priestly garments — Exodus 28:1–43

The garments are designed for entering *"the holy place"* and *"the Tent of Meeting"* and for
approaching the altar (Exodus 28:29, 35, 43). The dwelling now has a dress code: the one who
goes in before Yahweh must be clothed as prescribed. See [[The Priestly Garments]].

### The ordination of the priests — Exodus 29:1–46

29:45–46 is the most explicit statement of the theme in Exodus so far: *"I will dwell among the
children of Israel, and will be their God"*. God brought Israel out of Egypt *"that I might dwell
among them"* (*wəšākantî*; *ləšokənî bətôkām*, MAM). The dwelling is tied to a daily offering at a
place of meeting (29:42–43). See [[The Ordination of the Priests]] and
[[That I May Dwell Among Them - Tabernacle, Temple, Flesh, and New Creation]].

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30:1–38

The incense altar stands *"before the veil … before the mercy seat … where I will meet with you"*
(30:6), and the incense is put *"before the testimony in the Tent of Meeting, where I will meet with
you"* (30:36). The place of meeting named at the mercy seat (25:22) and the tent door (29:42) is
named twice more. See [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]].

### The appointed makers and their stopping point — Exodus 31

Exodus 31:1–11 joins divine design to divinely supplied human skill. The tent, furniture, priestly
garments, oil, and incense all fall within the workers' commission. God does not merely request a
dwelling: he provides a company to make it. The Sabbath command then sets a limit on the work;
neither a finished sanctuary nor an indwelling glory is reported here.

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### Presence after the calf — Exodus 33

The command to dwell among Israel (25:8; 29:45–46) meets an explicit contrary announcement: Yahweh
will not go among the people because they are stiff-necked (33:3–5). The outside tent makes distance
visible, though God still meets Moses there. The promise of presence in 33:14–17 answers the crisis;
it does not report that the prescribed tabernacle has already been built. See [[Show Me Your Glory]]
.

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

The plea that the Lord go among the people (34:9) carries forward the negotiations of chapter
33. Verse 26 also names the house of Yahweh as the destination of first fruits without
specifying its site. Neither statement reports the completed sanctuary or the glory filling it;
the building and indwelling remain later events. See [[The Covenant Renewed]].

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

Exodus 34:34–35 describes Moses going in before Yahweh and coming out to Israel. The
outside tent of 33:7–11 is a plausible narrative setting, but these verses name no tent,
ark, or room. They therefore cannot prove that the prescribed tabernacle is already complete.
See [[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]].

See [[The Shining Face of Moses]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

After covenant rupture and renewal, the people supply materials for the Tent of Meeting and
its service (35:21). The promised dwelling of 25:8 is being prepared; its construction and filling
are not yet narrated. The outside meeting tent of 33:7–11 does not become a completed tabernacle
by being called the Tent of Meeting. See [[The Tent Outside the Camp and the Promised Dwelling]].

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The makers and the surplus — Exodus 35:30–36:7

The commanded sanctuary has a summoned workforce and more than enough material (36:1–7).
The workers are engaged in their tasks (36:4), so preparation has become actual work. This is
neither the final completion report nor the arrival of the glory. Sufficient resources do not
by themselves mean that the promised dwelling has already been erected.

See [[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]].

### The enclosure made — Exodus 36:8–38

The promised dwelling now has its curtains, coverings, boards, bars, veil, and entrance screen
made (36:8–38). The joining of the curtain assemblies (36:13, 18) is actual construction,
but the raising of the tent and the filling with glory are separate events (40:17–35).
The enclosure's manufacture must not be treated as the whole dwelling promise fulfilled.

See [[The Tabernacle Built]].

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

After the enclosure, Exodus 37 reports the furnishings and the oil and incense for the
promised dwelling. The ark and mercy seat prepare the meeting place of 25:22. Their making
is distinct from placing the testimony, installing the furniture, and beginning its use
(40:20–27), and from the glory's arrival (40:34–35). A manufactured sanctuary is not yet
an account of God filling it.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38:1–31

The outer altar, basin, and court are now made and materials accounted for (38:1–31). The reference
to ministering women at the Tent of Meeting (38:8) does not by itself date the final sanctuary’s
erection. Exodus 33:7–11 already describes a meeting tent; 40:17, 34–35 separately narrates erection
and filling. The relationship is not expressly resolved here.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

The garments are made for ministering in the holy place (39:1, 26–27). Preparation for
God’s dwelling therefore includes the people’s clothing as well as the enclosure and furniture.
The statement of purpose does not put the priests into an already functioning sanctuary;
completion, erection, and filling remain separately narrated after this pericope.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

All the work of the tabernacle is declared finished (39:32) and its components are brought
to Moses (39:33–41). The dwelling’s readiness is therefore narrated before the raising and filling
of chapter 40. Human inspection and blessing do not themselves constitute a manifestation of
God’s presence. The finished-work declaration and Moses’ later completion in 40:33 describe
distinct stages, rather than requiring either report to be erased.

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

The purpose of Exodus 25:8 and 29:45–46 reaches its visible outcome in 40:34–35: the
cloud covers the erected tent and glory fills it. Residence does not mean geographical
immobility; the cloud determines Israel’s journeys (40:36–38). Nor does residence mean
unrestricted approach, since even Moses cannot enter at the filling. The prior refusal
to go among Israel (33:3) is answered through intercession and renewal, not erased.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

See [[Cloud and Fire - From the Sea to the Filled Tabernacle]] for the cloud’s continuing guidance and differing access.

### God’s house and God in heaven — Ecclesiastes 5:1–2

The worshipper goes *"to God’s house"* (1), yet is told that *"God is in heaven, and you on
earth"* (2). The house is where one draws near to listen; the distance is the reason for few
words. The unit names no building or city, and the wiki does not identify the house with a
particular sanctuary.

See [[Let Your Words Be Few]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 5:1–7; the passage page records the comparison sources and Hebrew checks.

### The burnt offering — Leviticus 1:1–17

Yahweh speaks from the Tent of Meeting (1:1), and the door and its altar become the place of
accepted approach (3–5). This follows the filling of Exodus 40 without narrating Moses’ entry. The
promise of meeting and speech at the tent in Exodus 29:42–46 now has a concrete continuation.

See [[The Burnt Offering]] . Source: stored WEB Leviticus 1; comparisons identified on the passage
page.

### The meal offering — Leviticus 2

The sanctuary altar of the continuing speech receives grain memorials as well as animal offerings (2:2, 8–9). Produce is brought to Yahweh through priestly service; a most holy remainder stays with the priests. See [[The Meal Offering]].

### The peace offering — Leviticus 3

The Tent of Meeting is the place of approach, slaughter, and priestly altar service (3:2, 8, 13). The sanctuary receives the assigned fat portions while the prohibition on eating fat and blood reaches all dwellings (17).

See [[The Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 3; contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Priest and congregation — Leviticus 4:1–21

The tent’s interior is involved in the remedy for priestly and communal sin: blood is brought
before the veil and to the inner altar (5–7, 16–18). The carcass leaves the camp for a clean ash
site. These spatial distinctions order approach to God without describing entry behind the veil.

See [[The Sin Offering for Priest and Congregation]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:1–21;
contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Ruler and individual — Leviticus 4:22–35

The ruler’s and ordinary person’s remedies take place at the burnt-offering altar
(25, 30, 34), without carrying blood into the tent. Sanctuary service is not reducible
to interior access: the outer altar is also a site of atonement and promised forgiveness.

See [[The Sin Offering for Ruler and Individual]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:22–35; contextual
comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Altar fire and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:8–23

The court of the Tent of Meeting is the prescribed place for eating the most holy remainder (16). The sanctuary orders nourishment as well as burning; the ashes have a separate clean destination outside the camp.

See [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:8–23; comparisons documented there.

### Holy flesh and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:24–7:10

The court is the authorized place to eat sin-offering flesh (6:26). Blood entering the Tent of
Meeting for atonement marks the exception requiring burning (6:30); different sanctuary spaces
carry different rules.

See [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:24–7:10.

### Aaron and his sons consecrated — Leviticus 8:1–36

The tabernacle, its contents, altar, and basin are anointed alongside the installation of
its servants (10–12). The doorway is both the public gathering point and the priests’
required place of eating and remaining (3–5, 31–35); no new glory appearance is narrated here.

See [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 8; Exodus 28:1; 29; Leviticus 7:38.

### The eighth-day service — Leviticus 9

Moses and Aaron enter the Tent of Meeting and emerge to bless (9:23). Unlike Moses' inability to
enter during the filling in Exodus 40:35, this scene reports entry; it does not explain their
activity inside. See [[The Eighth Day and the Appearing Glory]] .

### Strange fire and the surviving priests — Leviticus 10:1–11

The sanctuary is both the scene of fatal approach and the place where the surviving anointed priests must remain (1–7). Relatives carry the dead outside the camp; access and removal are assigned by command.

See [[Nadab and Abihu and the Strange Fire]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:1–11.

### Where holy food is eaten — Leviticus 10:12–20

The sanctuary's spaces govern the meal: most holy grain beside the altar in a holy place, the breast and thigh in a clean place, and the sin offering in the sanctuary itself (12–18).

See [[The Uneaten Sin Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 10:12–20.
