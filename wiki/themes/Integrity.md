# Integrity

## Definition / overview

**Being all of one piece — the same thing on every side, and the same thing whether or not anyone is
looking.** The Hebrew root is ***t-m-m***, whose plain sense is *complete, whole, finished, entire*,
and which is used of a full day and a sum that has been paid out. (An earlier claim that it is used
of an uncut stone is withdrawn; see the correction below.) It is a word about **wholeness before it
is a word about morality**, and every ethical use in Scripture carries that literal sense inside it.

Four forms of the root matter, and the wiki keeps them distinct:

| Form | Sense | First and defining uses |
|---|---|---|
| ***tāmîm*** | complete, unblemished, sound | Noah at Genesis 6:9; Abram commanded at 17:1; sacrificial animals *without blemish* |
| ***tām*** | whole, of one piece | Job at 1:1, 1:8, 2:3; **Jacob at Genesis 25:27**, where WEB renders it *quiet* |
| ***tōm*** | uprightness, guilelessness | Abimelech's *"integrity of my heart"* at Genesis 20:5–6 |
| ***tummâ*** | integrity held onto | **Job 2:3 and 2:9**, God and Job's wife, six verses apart; Job's oath at 27:5 |

**Integrity in Scripture is not sinlessness, and the texts are careful to say so.** Noah is *tāmîm*
in 6:9 and drunk and naked in 9:21, in the same book and with no comment on the contradiction. Job
is *tām* four times and spends twenty-eight chapters accusing God of hunting him, and at 42:7 is the
one commended. **The word describes a man without a seam — no hidden compartment, no second account
of himself — not a man without a fault.** Its opposite is not wickedness but duplicity: the divided
heart of Psalm 12:2, the double weights of Deuteronomy 25:13–15 (which the law calls, exactly,
*a whole and just weight*, *ʾeben šəlēmâ wāṣedeq*).

**And it is a thing one *holds onto*.** *Tummâ* takes the verb *ḥāzaq* in the hiphil — to grasp,
keep a grip on. That grammar is the theme's centre of gravity: **integrity in the book of Job is not
a possession but a hold, and the question the book asks is what it costs to keep hold of it when
letting go is the only relief on offer.**

## Development across Scripture

### Holiness and the neighbor — Leviticus 19:1–18

Truthfulness, fair wages, and impartial judgment (11–15) make the holiness command concrete in
dealings with neighbors. Worship and reliable conduct are required together.

See [[Be Holy and Love Your Neighbor]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19:1–18.


**Genesis 6:9 — the first *tāmîm* applied to a person.** *"Noah was a righteous man, blameless among
the people of his time."* The verdict is given before Noah does anything, is stated in the same
breath as *ṣaddîq*, righteous, and is immediately qualified by a phrase — *in his generations* —
that has been read for two thousand years as either a superlative or a limitation. See
[[Noah and the Flood]] and [[Blameless Before Anyone Is Watching]].

**Genesis 17:1 — the first *tāmîm* commanded.** *"Walk before me, and be blameless."* Not a
description but an imperative, attached to the covenant and to the sign of it, and addressed to a man
who has just fathered a son by a slave on his wife's advice. **Integrity enters Scripture as a
verdict and is then reissued as a demand.** See [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]].

**Genesis 20:5–6 — the only time God confirms a man's *tōm* to his face.** Abimelech, who has taken
Sarah on Abraham's word, protests *"in the integrity of my heart and the innocence of my hands I
have done this"*, and God answers *"Yes, I know that in the integrity of your heart you have done
this."* **The man whose integrity God personally certifies in Genesis is a Philistine king outside
the covenant**, and the man who deceived him is the patriarch. See [[Abraham and Abimelech]] and
[[Sinning Unwittingly - Integrity, Ignorance, and Guilt]].

**Genesis 25:27 — the wrinkle.** Jacob is *ʾîš tām*, and WEB translates it *"a quiet man, living in
tents."* It is the same adjective as Job 1:1. Whether it means *settled*, *wholesome*, or
*straightforward* is disputed, and it is applied to the man who will buy a birthright off his
starving brother and impersonate him to a blind father. **The wiki records the collision and does
not resolve it**; at minimum it is a warning that *tām* is a narrower word than English *blameless*
suggests. See [[Jacob and Esau and the Sold Birthright]].

**Job — the word made into a plot.** The book takes *tām* from the narrator's mouth in the first
verse, has God repeat it twice, and then subjects it to a test designed to find out whether it is
load-bearing or bought. At **2:3** God announces the result — *"He still maintains his integrity"* —
and at **2:9** Job's wife throws the identical phrase back as an objection: *"Do you still maintain
your integrity? Renounce God, and die."* **Six verses apart, in the only two mouths in the book that
use the word, as praise and as reproach.** See [[The Second Test]] and [[Job's Wife]].

**Job then makes it his oath** ([[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]]). *"Far be it from me that I should justify you. Until I die I will
not put away my integrity from me. I hold fast to my righteousness, and will not let it go"*
(27:5–6). This is the position he refuses to trade: the friends offer restoration in exchange for a
confession, and taking it would require him to say something about himself he believes false.
**What he will not surrender is not his innocence but his account of his innocence** — and the
book's verdict at 42:7 is that the refusal was the right speech.

**The Psalms make it a way of walking.** *"Yahweh, who shall dwell in your sanctuary? … He who walks
blamelessly"* (Psalm 15:1–2); *"I will walk within my house with a perfect heart"* (Psalm 101:2);
*"Let integrity and uprightness preserve me"* (Psalm 25:21). **Psalm 26 is the closest thing outside
Job to Job's posture**: *"Examine me, Yahweh, and prove me. Try my heart and my mind… I have walked
in my integrity."*

**Proverbs makes it a road.** *"He who walks in integrity walks surely, but he who perverts his ways
will be found out"* (10:9); *"Better is the poor who walks in his integrity than he who is perverse
in his lips and is a fool"* (19:1; 28:6 gives a related comparison with perverse ways and riches).
**The wisdom tradition's claim is that integrity is safe**, and Job is the counter-example that tradition had to absorb.

**The law makes it a physical property.** The sacrificial animal must be *tāmîm*, without blemish
(Leviticus 1:3, 22:19–21), and the altar's stones must be uncut (Exodus 20:25). **Correction:** this
page earlier implied that *tāmîm* also describes the uncut stones. It does not: Exodus 20:25
forbids *gāzît*, *hewn* stone, and Deuteronomy 27:6 calls the stones *šəlēmôt*, *whole*, under a
different lemma (WLC; Joshua 8:31 not checked in Hebrew). **The same word governs the beast on the
altar and the man before God**, and the New Testament reaches back through
that usage when it calls Christ a lamb *"without blemish and without spot"* (1 Peter 1:19).

**The New Testament keeps the wholeness sense and changes the vocabulary.** *Haplous*, single —
*"if therefore your eye is single, your whole body will be full of light"* (Matthew 6:22); *teleios*,
complete — *"you shall therefore be perfect, as your Father in heaven is perfect"* (Matthew 5:48),
which is the Septuagint's word for *tāmîm* and which means *finished, brought to its end*, not
*flawless*. **James 1:4's *"that you may be perfect and complete, lacking in nothing"* stands at the
end of a sentence about the testing of faith**, which is where Job's *tummâ* started.
### An oath for what no one saw — Exodus 22:7–13

The deposit and keeping cases depend on a person's honesty where nobody else can check it: the
question is *"if he hasn't put his hand to his neighbor's goods"* (22:8, 11), and the answer is
his oath. Where evidence exists, he must bring it (22:13). The law treats an honest keeper as
blameless for losses he could not prevent.
See [[Laws of Property and Restitution]].

### Against the crowd and the bribe — Exodus 23:1–19

A witness must refuse cooperation in a malicious case and resist pressure from a multitude (23:1–2).
The ban on bribes recognizes that those who can see can be blinded and righteous words distorted
(23:8). Integrity therefore requires refusing the inducement, not simply confidence that a good
character can accept it without consequence.

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38:1–31

The material count is made under Moses’ command through Ithamar, and the silver’s amount and uses
are stated (38:21, 25–28). This can be read as accountable stewardship. The chapter does not accuse
anyone of theft, narrate an independent audit, or use a moral verdict that this page may silently
substitute for the inventory.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### The victims *təmîmîm* — Proverbs 1:12

*"Let's swallow them up alive like Sheol, and whole, like those who go down into the pit."* *Whole* is
*təmîmîm*, plural of *tāmîm* — Noah's *"blameless"* (Genesis 6:9) and the family of Job's *tām*
(Job 1:1; 2:3). In this verse the sense is physical, *intact*, and the WEB renders it so; but the
gang's chosen victims are described with the Bible's word for integrity, and in the verse before they
are *nāqî*, *"innocent"*. Hebrew checked against Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*. See
[[Do Not Consent to Sinners]].

### Job's pair, promised the land — Proverbs 2:7, 21

*"He is a shield to those who walk in integrity"* (2:7, *tōm*); *"the upright (yəšārîm) will dwell in
the land. The perfect (təmîmîm) will remain in it"* (2:21). The two roots are the pair with which Job
is introduced — *"blameless and upright"*, *tām wəyāšār* (Job 1:1). **Tension flagged, not
resolved**: Proverbs 2 promises the *tām* and the *yāšār* a shield and the land without exception;
Job was both and lost everything, and his book refuses to explain it by his sin. The wiki reads
Proverbs as stating the ordinary rule and Job as the case that shows it is not a guarantee in any
one life (see the [[Proverbs]] book page), but neither text says so. See [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] and
[[The Upright Will Dwell in the Land - Proverbs 2.21-22, Psalm 37, and Matthew 5.5]]. Source: Proverbs 2 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father — Proverbs 10:9, 29

Blameless walking brings security, while a twisted course is exposed (9); Yahweh’s way is a
stronghold to the upright (29). Job 1:1, 13–22 supplies the counterexample to using present safety
as a test of integrity. The proverb describes the worth of a course of life, not a narrated trial of
Job.

See [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]]. Source: Proverbs 10 and the cited Proverbs/Job comparisons
checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### A False Balance — Proverbs 11:1, 3, 5–6, 13, 20

The trustworthy person keeps a confidence, and uprightness guides conduct. Accurate weights make
integrity concrete in trade, while blameless ways delight Yahweh (20). Job 31:5–6 uses an even
balance for the appraisal of his integrity; the same English image connects moral judgment to fair
measurement without requiring a lexical claim.

See [[A False Balance]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 11; comparisons are documented on the passage
and connection pages.

### Correction, speech, and daily conduct — Proverbs 12:5, 17, 19, 22

Truthfulness joins intention, testimony, and action: Yahweh delights in those who do the truth, not
merely those who sound persuasive. Honest witness protects the moral reliability of speech. The
comparison with Exodus 20:16 and 23:1–8 is ethical agreement, not a claim of direct quotation.

See [[Whoever Loves Correction]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 12 and the comparisons documented on
that passage page.

### The way guarded — Proverbs 13:6

*"Righteousness guards the way of integrity"* (6): integrity is pictured as a road that righteousness keeps, using the same verb *guard* as 13:3's guarded mouth in the WEB.

See [[Hope Deferred]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 13.

### Flogged for integrity — Proverbs 17:26

*"Also to punish the righteous is not good, nor to flog officials for their integrity."* The *integrity* is *yōšer*, uprightness (Hebrew from Sefaria): the saying admits that the upright may be punished for being upright, and calls it wrong rather than impossible.

See [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 17.

### Integrity, counsel, and compassion — Proverbs 19:1, 22

The opening comparison honors the poor person who walks in integrity (19:1); verse 22 again ranks a
poor man above a liar. Neither comparison claims that every poor person is upright, or promises that
integrity will make him rich. They rule out treating poverty itself as proof of moral failure. See
[[Better Is Little - The Better-Than Sayings of Proverbs]] .

See [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] . Source: stored WEB Proverbs 19 and the
comparisons documented on that passage page.

### Discernment and accountability — Proverbs 20:6–7, 9, 11

Integrity marks the righteous person's walk and is linked to blessing for his children (7);
even a child is known by conduct (11). Yet verse 9 challenges the claim to a self-purified,
sinless heart. Integrity and sinlessness are not treated as interchangeable. **Tension:** Job's
affirmed integrity coexists with the deaths of his children (Job 1:1, 18–19; 2:3),
contradicting an exceptionless reading of family security. See [[Blessed Are His Children - Proverbs 20.7 and the Losses of Job]].

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 20; supporting comparisons are documented on the
passage page.

### Integrity, law, and public life — Proverbs 28:6, 18

Verse 6 explicitly ranks a poor person with integrity above a rich person with perverse ways; it
resembles 19:1 but replaces crooked lips and folly with crooked ways and wealth. Verse 18 promises
safety to the blameless. **Tension:** Job retains integrity while ruined without cause (Job 2:3, 7);
integrity has worth even when its promised security is not experienced.

See [[The Wicked Flee When No One Pursues]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 28 and the comparisons
documented on the passage and connection pages.

### Correction and public justice — Proverbs 29

Integrity can attract murderous hostility rather than protection. Verse 10 explicitly makes the
upright a target in the WEB, qualifying any inference that good conduct always wins acceptance or
safety.

See [[Where There Is No Vision]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 29:10; the passage page records the
cross-reference sources and interpretive limits.

- [[The Arrows of the Almighty]] — Job 6:10, *"that I have not denied the words of the Holy One"*:

- [[Why Do the Wicked Live]] — Job 21:23; **the root of integrity used of a wicked man's body.**

- [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] — Job 22:3; ***tattēm dərākêkā***, *"that you make your ways perfect"*

## Related themes
- [[Theft and Restitution]] — Exodus 22:1–15

- [[Righteousness]] — the neighbouring word, and the one that sits beside it at Genesis 6:9 and Job
  1:1. **Righteousness is a verdict about conduct; integrity is a verdict about consistency**, and
  the Bible pairs them because neither implies the other
- [[Testing]] — what converts a stated integrity into a demonstrated one, and the only instrument
  that can tell the two apart
- [[The Fear of God]] — its companion in Job 1:1's fourfold description, and the thing the accuser
  attacks in place of attacking the integrity directly
- [[Deception]] — its exact opposite in the Genesis material, and the family trait of the house the
  *ʾîš tām* of 25:27 heads
- [[Guilt and Conscience]] — the internal register integrity is measured against, and Abimelech's
  ground of appeal at Genesis 20:5
- [[Oaths and Vows]] — the form Job's integrity finally takes at 27:5 and 31:1–40, where he stakes
  it under curse
- [[Suffering]] — the pressure under which integrity either holds or is revealed to have been a
  purchase
- [[Sacrifice and Offering]] — where *tāmîm* is a physical requirement, and the origin of the word's
  force
