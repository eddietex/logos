# Joy and Enjoyment

## Definition / overview

Mirth, pleasure, rejoicing, and the enjoyment of what one has and does. The page is opened with
Ecclesiastes, where enjoyment is tested, judged, and repeatedly commended, and where the
question is not whether joy is real but what it yields. Earlier material on gladness in Genesis,
Exodus, Job, and Proverbs has not been gathered here; it will be linked as passages are revisited
or as the theme recurs.

## Development across Scripture

### Warnings in Proverbs

Proverbs 14:13 says *"Even in laughter the heart may be sorrowful, and mirth may end in
heaviness"*; 21:17 says *"He who loves pleasure shall be a poor man"*; and 20:1 warns that whoever
is led astray by wine *"is not wise."* Pleasure is treated as a risk to the heart, to wealth, and to
judgment. See [[A Way That Seems Right]], [[The King's Heart]], and [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Source:
stored WEB Proverbs 14:13; 20:1; 21:17.

### The trial of pleasure — Ecclesiastes 2:1–11

In [[The Trial of Pleasure]] the Preacher tests mirth deliberately, with wisdom guiding him (2:3).
He withholds his heart from no joy, and *"my heart rejoiced because of all my labor, and this was
my portion from all my labor"* (2:10). The verdict is vanity and no profit (2:11). The joy is not
denied; what is denied is that it remains as gain. God is not mentioned in the unit. See
[[Mirth and Its Verdict - Proverbs 14.13, 21.17, and Ecclesiastes 2.1-11]]. Source: stored WEB
Ecclesiastes 2:1–11.

### Labor left to another — Ecclesiastes 2:18–26

Eating, drinking, and enjoying good in labor come from God's hand (2:24), and joy is one of
his gifts to the person who pleases him (2:26). In contrast to the experiment's lack of profit
(2:11), enjoyment can still be acknowledged as good. Receiving a gift rather than securing
lasting gain is a reading of this contrast, not a distinction the text formally defines.
The closing vanity verdict in 2:26 remains. See
[[Mirth and Its Verdict - Proverbs 14.13, 21.17, and Ecclesiastes 2.1-11]].

See [[The Labor Left to Another]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 2:18–26.

### A time for every purpose — Ecclesiastes 3:1–15

Weeping and laughter, mourning and dancing each have their time (4). The Preacher then
commends rejoicing and doing good during life (12), and eating, drinking, and enjoying good
in labor as God's gift (13). The claim stands beside the burden (10) and the limit on human
understanding (11). It develops 2:24 without retracting 2:11's denial of profit: joy can be
received within the limits that the inquiry exposes.

See [[A Time for Every Purpose]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 3:1–15.

### From dust to dust — Ecclesiastes 3:16–22

After corrupt justice and shared death, the Preacher commends rejoicing in one's works
as one's portion (22). The uncertainty about what follows gives this commendation its setting.
The hatred of life in 2:17 and the vanity of 3:19 are not retracted: enjoyment coexists with
those findings. Here the focus is works, rather than an explicit repetition of eating and
drinking or of the gift formula in 3:13.

See [[From Dust to Dust]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 3:16–22; comparisons as identified above.

### The tears of the oppressed — Ecclesiastes 4:1–3

The commendation of rejoicing in works (3:22) is followed immediately by tears without
comfort and the preference for the dead and never-existent (4:1–3). The tension cannot be
removed by treating enjoyment as everyone's current experience or by claiming the new
observation erases the earlier gift. The stated ground here is evil seen under the sun.
See [[Mirth and Its Verdict - Proverbs 14.13, 21.17, and Ecclesiastes 2.1-11]].

See [[The Tears of the Oppressed]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 3:16–4:3; the passage page
records the additional comparison sources.

### Two are better than one — Ecclesiastes 4:4–12

The man alone asks, *"For whom then, do I labor, and deprive my soul of enjoyment?"* (4:8). He is
the negative image of the enjoyment commended in 2:24 and 3:12–13, 22: the means are there, the
enjoyment is withheld by his own labor. After the preference for the dead in 4:1–3, verses 9–12
name a good among the living — companionship — without using the language of joy.

See [[Two Are Better Than One]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 4:4–12; the passage page
records the comparison sources and Hebrew checks.

### Wealth and enjoyment — Ecclesiastes 5:8–20

Eating, drinking, and enjoying labor during God's given days are good and proper (18).
Verse 19 distinguishes the wealth God gives from the power he gives to eat of it and rejoice.
Joy occupies the heart rather than frequent reflection on life's days (20). **Tension:**
these affirmations do not cancel harmful wealth (13–17), oppression (8), or 4:1–3's
uncomforted victims. The now-ingested 6:2, checked in raw, presents wealth given by God
without enjoyment. Joy here is a gift described, not an experience guaranteed to everyone.

See [[The Lover of Silver and the Gift of Enjoyment]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 5:8–20;
comparisons as identified on the passage page.

### Riches without enjoyment — Ecclesiastes 6:1–9

Ecclesiastes 5:19 describes the power to enjoy given with wealth; 6:2 describes wealth
given without that power. This is a contrast between cases, not a withdrawal of the first
gift, but it prevents treating enjoyment as everyone's experience. Even a hundred children
and two thousand years cannot substitute for good enjoyed (3–6). Verse 9's preference for
what the eyes see can be read as valuing available good, yet the vanity refrain remains.
See [[Wealth Given and Enjoyment Withheld - Proverbs 10.22 and Ecclesiastes 5.19-6.2]].

See [[Riches Without Enjoyment]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 6:1–9; comparison
sources are identified on the passage and connection pages.

### Who knows what is good — Ecclesiastes 6:10–12

The question of what is good during life (12) follows wealth and years without good
in 6:1–9. **Tension:** 3:12–13 and 5:18 commend rejoicing and enjoyment, while 6:12
asks who knows what is good. The present portion can be distinguished from a complete
account of a life's good, but that is an interpretive distinction, not an answer the
verse supplies. No new command to enjoy or prohibition of enjoyment is given here.

See [[Who Knows What Is Good]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 6:10–12; comparison sources identified on the passage page.
