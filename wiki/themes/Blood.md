# Blood

## Definition / overview

**Dām**, blood: in the Bible, not a fluid but a life. The identification is made explicitly and more
than once — *"flesh with its life, that is, its blood"* (Genesis 9:4), *"the life of the flesh is in
the blood"* (Leviticus 17:11), *"the blood is the life"* (Deuteronomy 12:23) — and almost everything
else the canon does with blood follows from it.

Three uses run the length of Scripture, and they are the same idea seen from three sides.

- **Blood shed is a life taken, and it makes a claim.** Abel's blood *"cries to me from the ground"*
  (Genesis 4:10) before anyone has legislated about murder. Blood is the Bible's standing metaphor
  for a debt that does not go quiet: it cries, it is required, it pollutes the land it soaks into,
  and it is *"covered"* only in the sense of being hidden from the redress it is owed — which is why
  Job demands *"Earth, don't cover my blood"* (16:18). See [[Do Not Cover My Blood]].
- **Blood is withheld from the human table.** The one dietary restriction God gives the new world at
  Genesis 9:4 is that blood may not be eaten, and the reason given is what blood is. The
  prohibition is repeated more often in the Torah than almost any other food rule (Leviticus 3:17;
  7:26–27; 17:10–14; 19:26; Deuteronomy 12:16, 23–25; 15:23) and is one of only four things the
  Jerusalem council asks of Gentile believers (Acts 15:20, 29).
- **Blood is what atones.** It is reserved from the table because it is claimed for the altar.
  Leviticus 17:11 gives the mechanism in one sentence — *"I have given it to you on the altar to
  make atonement for your souls; for it is the blood that makes atonement by reason of the life"* —
  and Hebrews 9:22 states the resulting rule: *"apart from shedding of blood there is no
  remission."*

The three are one theme because a life is what is at stake in all of them. A life taken wrongly
demands a life; a life taken for food may be eaten but its life may not; a life given on the altar
stands in for a life owed. The New Testament's central claim about the death of Jesus is made
entirely inside this vocabulary, and would be unintelligible without it.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 4:10–11** — the first *dām* in the Bible, and it speaks. *"The voice of your brother's
  blood cries to me from the ground"*, and the ground that *"opened its mouth to receive your
  brother's blood"* curses the man who spilled it. Before there is any law about killing, there is a
  killing that God hears. See [[Cain and Abel]].
- **Genesis 9:4** — the first legislation. Meat is permitted and blood is not, on the stated ground
  that blood *is* the life. The reservation is the whole of the food law given to the new world.
- **Genesis 9:5–6** — and the first homicide statute, in the same speech: *"I will surely require
  your life's blood… Whoever sheds man's blood, his blood will be shed by man, for God made man in
  his own image."* Blood for blood, one to one, grounded in the [[Image of God]] rather than in
  social order. See [[The Covenant with Noah]] and [[Whoever Sheds Man's Blood]].
- **Exodus 12:7, 13, 22–23** — Passover. Blood on the doorposts as a *sign*, and the plague passing
  over the houses that carry it. The first time blood protects rather than accuses.
- **Exodus 24:6–8** — Sinai ratified in blood: half thrown against the altar, half thrown on the
  people, with the words *"Behold the blood of the covenant."* Quoted directly at the last supper.
- **Leviticus 1–7; 16** — the sacrificial system, in which the priest's essential act is not the
  killing but the handling of the blood: dashed against the altar, smeared on its horns, sprinkled
  seven times, and on the Day of Atonement carried through the veil and put on the mercy seat.
- **Leviticus 17:10–14** — the theological centre. The prohibition of eating blood and the reason
  for it stated together, which is what makes 9:4 and the altar two sides of one rule.
- **Numbers 35:30–34** — *"blood pollutes the land. No expiation can be made for the land for the
  blood that is shed on it, but by the blood of him who shed it."* Unavenged blood is treated as a
  contaminant of the ground itself, which is Genesis 4:11 turned into land law. Ransom for a
  murderer is forbidden, where the law permits it for nearly everything else.
- **Deuteronomy 21:1–9** — the unsolved killing: the elders of the nearest town break a heifer's
  neck over running water and wash their hands, saying *"Our hands have not shed this blood."* A
  procedure for the case where blood cries and no one can be named.
- **Isaiah 1:11–15** — the prophetic reversal. God is sated with the blood of bulls, and the hands
  raised in prayer are *"full of blood"*. Sacrificial blood is worthless in a nation shedding the
  other kind.
- **Job 16:18** — *"Earth, don't cover my blood. Let my cry have no place to rest."* Job applies the
  Cain and Abel logic to himself while still alive, and asks the ground to keep his case open.
- **Ezekiel 24:6–8** — the city as a pot with rust in it, whose blood was poured *"on the bare
  rock"*, not on the ground where dust could cover it, *"that it might cause fury to come up to take
  vengeance."*
- **Psalm 9:12** — *dōrēš dāmîm*, *"he who avenges blood"*: the verb of Genesis 9:5 as a title for
  God.
- **Matthew 26:28; Mark 14:24; Luke 22:20; 1 Corinthians 11:25** — *"this is my blood of the new
  covenant, which is poured out for many for the remission of sins"*, said over a cup at a Passover
  meal: Exodus 24:8's covenant blood and Exodus 12's protecting blood claimed at once.
- **John 6:53–56** — *"unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you don't
  have life in yourselves"*, which to a Jewish audience is a deliberate scandal, because Genesis 9:4
  and Leviticus 17 forbid exactly that, and *"many of his disciples… walked no more with him."*
- **Acts 15:20, 29; 21:25** — the apostolic decree keeps the Noahic reservation for Gentile
  believers, alongside idol-food, strangled meat, and sexual immorality.
- **Romans 3:25; 5:9; Ephesians 1:7; 2:13; Colossians 1:20** — justification, redemption, nearness,
  and cosmic peace, each attributed to *his blood*.
- **Hebrews 9:7–28; 10:4; 12:24** — the sustained argument: blood is required for remission, the
  blood of bulls and goats cannot deliver it, and *"the blood of sprinkling that speaks better than
  that of Abel"* — a blood that asks for pardon where Abel's asked for redress.
- **1 Peter 1:18–19; 1 John 1:7; Revelation 1:5; 5:9; 7:14; 12:11** — ransom, cleansing, purchase,
  and victory, all in the same idiom; and **Revelation 6:9–10**, where the souls under the altar cry
  *"How long?"*, which is Abel's voice again at the end of the canon.

## Key passages

- [[Cain and Abel]] — blood's first appearance, and the first thing it does is speak
- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — blood withheld from the table and required for bloodshed, in one
  speech: the two halves of the theme legislated together
- [[The Waters Recede]] — the first altar and the first burnt offering, whose blood the text does
  not mention, in the pericope immediately before the rule that reserves it
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — Job 16:18, a living man asking the ground not
  to cover his blood
- [[Do Not Cover My Blood]] — the thread from Abel's cry through Job to the martyrs under the altar
- [[Whoever Sheds Man's Blood]] — Genesis 9:6 through the law of homicide and into the New Testament
- [[The Blood Is the Life]] — Genesis 9:4 through Leviticus 17, Acts 15, and John 6
