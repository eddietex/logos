# Blood

## Definition / overview

The Hebrew is **dām**, and it is one of the densest words in the canon: it occurs some three hundred
and sixty times, and the New Testament's *haima* another ninety-odd. What holds the uses together is
a single claim, stated as law in Genesis 9:4 and as explanation in Leviticus 17:11 — **the blood is
the life**. Not a symbol of life, not a vehicle for it. *"The life of the flesh is in the blood."*
The Hebrew word standing behind *life* in both verses is **nepeš**, the same word Genesis 2:7 uses
when the man becomes *"a living soul"*.

Everything the Bible does with blood follows from that equation, and it runs in two directions at
once:

- **Blood is not yours to consume.** It belongs to the God who gave it, which is why it is drained
  before meat is eaten (Genesis 9:4) and poured out at the base of the altar rather than burned with
  the rest (Leviticus 4:7). This is the oldest food law in Scripture and the only one addressed to
  all humanity.
- **Blood is not yours to spill.** Human blood shed unjustly cries out (Genesis 4:10), pollutes the
  land (Numbers 35:33), and is required back by God from whoever shed it (Genesis 9:5). Murder is
  wrong because of who the victim resembles, not because of what the killer disturbs.

And out of the same equation comes the third thing, which is the Bible's central mechanism of
atonement: because blood *is* life, a life laid on an altar can stand in for a life owed. *"It is
the blood that makes atonement by reason of the life"* (Leviticus 17:11). The sentence that forbids
eating blood is the sentence that explains why blood saves.

The two halves — blood as forbidden, blood as sacred — are not in tension. They are the same
proposition seen from either side: life is God's.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 4:10–11** — the word's first occurrence, and it is a murder. *"The voice of your
  brother's blood cries to me from the ground"*: Hebrew *dəmê*, a plural, which the rabbis read as
  the blood of Abel *and of all his unborn descendants*. Blood is introduced into the Bible as
  something that speaks, and what it says is an accusation. See [[Cain and Abel]] and
  [[Do Not Cover My Blood]].
- **Genesis 9:4–6** — the first legislation. Blood identified with *nepeš*, forbidden as food to all
  humanity, and required by God from any hand that spills a human's, on the ground that the victim
  bears God's image. Both of the Bible's blood trajectories start in these three verses. See
  [[The Covenant with Noah]] and [[Image of God]].
- **Exodus 4:9; 7:17–21** — the Nile turned to blood, the first plague and the first sign: Egypt's
  river, which the previous chapter's infanticide had already filled with Israelite children,
  becomes visibly what it was.
- **Exodus 12:7, 13, 22–23** — Passover. Blood on the doorposts, and *"when I see the blood, I will
  pass over you."* The first time blood functions to *avert* rather than to accuse, and, like the
  rainbow of Genesis 9:16, a sign whose stated audience is God.
- **Exodus 24:6–8** — the Sinai covenant sealed: half the blood on the altar, half thrown on the
  people, and *"Behold, the blood of the covenant."* Jesus quotes this phrase at the last supper.
- **Leviticus 17:10–14** — the theological centre. The prohibition of Genesis 9:4 restated for
  Israel with its reason given in full: the life is in the blood, God has given it *on the altar* to
  make atonement, and *"no person among you may eat blood."* The verse is doing double duty and
  knows it.
- **Leviticus 1–7; 16** — the sacrificial system in operation: blood dashed, sprinkled, smeared on
  horns, carried behind the veil once a year on the Day of Atonement.
- **Numbers 35:9–34** — homicide law. Cities of refuge, the avenger of blood (*gōʾēl haddām*), the
  requirement of more than one witness, and the principle that gives it all force: *"blood pollutes
  the land. No expiation can be made for the land for the blood that is shed in it, but by the blood
  of him who shed it."*
- **Deuteronomy 12:16, 23–25** — the blood prohibition repeated three times in one chapter as Israel
  is permitted to slaughter for food away from the sanctuary. *"Only be sure that you don't eat the
  blood; for the blood is the life."*
- **1 Samuel 14:31–35** — the rule enforced in the field. Saul's exhausted troops eat animals *"with
  the blood"* and he calls it sin and has a stone rolled out for proper slaughter.
- **2 Samuel 23:17** — David pours out the water three men risked their lives to fetch: *"Is this
  not the blood of the men who went in jeopardy of their lives?"* The equation applied
  metaphorically, and by a man who understood it literally.
- **Isaiah 1:11, 15** — sacrificial blood refused and bloodstained hands named in the same oracle:
  *"I have had enough of the burnt offerings… your hands are full of blood."* The prophets keep the
  two trajectories in one sentence.
- **Ezekiel 3:18; 33:6–8** — *"his blood I will require at your hand"*, Genesis 9:5's verb *dāraš*
  turned on the watchman who failed to warn.
- **Joel 2:30–31; Acts 2:19–20** — blood among the portents of the day of Yahweh.
- **Matthew 27:24–25** — Pilate washes his hands and the crowd answers *"May his blood be on us, and
  on our children"*, a formula from the homicide law of Deuteronomy 19:10 and 2 Samuel 1:16. A verse
  with an appalling history of Christian use against Jews, and its own genre is the point: it is
  courtroom language about liability, not a curse on a people.
- **Matthew 26:28; Mark 14:24; Luke 22:20; 1 Corinthians 11:25** — *"This is my blood of the new
  covenant"*, joining Exodus 24:8's *blood of the covenant* to Jeremiah 31:31's *new covenant*.
- **Acts 15:20, 29; 21:25** — the Jerusalem council keeps the Genesis 9:4 prohibition for Gentile
  believers when it keeps almost nothing else. See [[Life Is in the Blood]].
- **Romans 3:25; 5:9** — justification *"by his blood"*, and Christ set forth as a *hilastērion*,
  the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the mercy seat that was sprinkled on the Day of
  Atonement.
- **Hebrews 9:7–28** — the sustained argument: *"apart from shedding of blood there is no
  remission"* (9:22), and the high priest entering the true holy place with his own blood rather
  than that of goats and calves.
- **Hebrews 12:24** — the deliberate inversion of the Bible's first mention. Jesus' blood *"speaks
  better than that of Abel"*: the blood that cried for requital in Genesis 4 answered by blood that
  says something else.
- **1 Peter 1:19; 1 John 1:7; Revelation 1:5; 5:9; 7:14; 12:11** — the New Testament's settled
  idiom, in which blood cleanses, purchases, and conquers.

## Key passages

- [[Cain and Abel]] — the word's first occurrence, and blood as an accusation that speaks from the
  ground
- [[The Covenant with Noah]] — Genesis 9:4–6, blood identified with life, forbidden as food, and
  required from the hand that spills it
- [[Noah and the Flood]] — the earth *"filled with violence"* (6:11, 13), which is the condition
  9:5–6 is legislated against
- [[Do Not Cover My Blood]] — Job 16:18 taking up Abel's cry and refusing to let the ground absorb
  it
- [[Life Is in the Blood]] — the prohibition of Genesis 9:4 traced through Leviticus to Acts 15
- [[Whoever Sheds Man's Blood]] — Genesis 9:6, the image of God as the ground of the prohibition of
  murder
