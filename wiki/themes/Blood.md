# Blood

## Definition / overview

**Dām**, blood — in the Hebrew Bible not a substance but a claim. Three convictions run through
every use of the word, and all three are stated or assumed by the time Genesis 9 is finished:

- **The life is in the blood.** *"Flesh with its life, that is, its blood"* (Genesis 9:4). Leviticus
  17:11 makes it a formal principle: *"the life of the flesh is in the blood."* The Hebrew is
  *nephesh*, the same word for the *living soul* the man becomes when God breathes into him at 2:7.
  Blood is therefore not property, not food, and not disposable — it is the visible form of
  something lent.
- **Spilled blood speaks.** *"The voice of your brother's blood cries to me from the ground"*
  (Genesis 4:10 — the noun is plural in Hebrew, *dəmê*, bloods, and the verb is a shout). Blood that
  has been shed does not stay silent, and what it addresses is God. Job takes up the same idea —
  *"earth, don't cover my blood"* (16:18) — and Revelation 6:10 still has it shouting.
- **Blood is given for atonement.** Leviticus 17:11 completes its sentence: *"I have given it to you
  on the altar to make atonement for your souls."* The one thing that may not be eaten is the one
  thing that may be offered. The dietary prohibition and the sacrificial system rest on the same
  premise, and the New Testament's *"apart from shedding of blood there is no remission"* (Hebrews
  9:22) is a restatement of it.

The theme's first two rules arrive together, in the same divine speech, in
[[The Covenant with Noah]]: **do not eat blood** (9:4) and **God will require human blood from
whoever sheds it** (9:5–6). They are given before Sinai, before Israel, and before any court exists,
addressed to eight people and through them to everyone — the only body of law in the Hebrew Bible
with that scope.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 4:10–11** — the word's first appearance, and it is a victim's. Abel's blood cries from
  the ground and the ground is cursed for having opened *"to receive your brother's blood from your
  hand"*. Blood pollutes the land that takes it, which is the idea Numbers 35:33 will legislate. See
  [[Cain and Abel]] and [[Do Not Cover My Blood]].
- **Genesis 9:4** — the first dietary law in Scripture, and it is not about which animals but about
  what part. *"Flesh with its life, that is, its blood, you shall not eat."* Meat is permitted in
  the same breath (9:3); blood is withheld from the permission.
- **Genesis 9:5–6** — blood-guilt made a rule. *"I will surely require your life's blood"*, three
  times, and then the chiastic sentence that grounds the prohibition of homicide in the image of
  God. See [[Whoever Sheds Man's Blood]] and [[Image of God]].
- **Exodus 4:9; 7:17–21** — blood as a plague. The sign of water poured out as blood on dry ground,
  given at the bush, becomes the first blow on Egypt: the Nile and every water in the land turned to
  blood. Psalm 78:44 and 105:29 retell it; Revelation 8:8 and 16:3–6 extend it to the sea and the
  springs. See [[Water Turned to Blood]].
- **Exodus 12:7, 13, 22–23** — blood on the doorposts, and the sign God reads: *"When I see the
  blood, I will pass over you."* Blood becomes protective, and the covenant sign is again something
  God looks at rather than something the people carry.
- **Exodus 24:6–8** — the Sinai covenant sealed with blood thrown half on the altar and half on the
  people: *"Behold, the blood of the covenant, which Yahweh has made with you."* Quoted at Matthew
  26:28 and Hebrews 9:20.
- **Leviticus 1–7; 16** — the sacrificial system, in which blood is dashed, sprinkled, smeared on
  horns, and carried behind the veil once a year. The blood is never eaten and never wasted.
- **Leviticus 3:17; 7:26–27; 17:10–14; Deuteronomy 12:16, 23–25** — the Genesis 9:4 prohibition
  legislated for Israel, with the reason attached and the penalty of being cut off. Deuteronomy's
  phrasing is the blunt one: *"the blood is the life, and you shall not eat the life with the
  flesh."*
- **Leviticus 17:11** — the theme's hinge verse, and the one that ties its three strands into one
  sentence: the life is in the blood, God gave it for the altar, and it atones *by reason of the
  life*.
- **Numbers 35:30–34** — blood pollutes the land, and *"no expiation can be made for the land… but
  by the blood of him who shed it."* The cities of refuge and the requirement of two witnesses are
  the procedure Genesis 9:6 lacks.
- **Deuteronomy 21:1–9** — the unsolved murder: the elders break a heifer's neck over running water
  and wash their hands, declaring *"Our hands have not shed this blood."* Blood-guilt is a liability
  the community carries until it is discharged.
- **1 Samuel 14:32–34** — the one narrative in Scripture where Genesis 9:4 is broken. Saul's
  famished army *"flew on the plunder, and took sheep, cattle, and calves, and killed them on the
  ground; and the people ate them with the blood"*, and Saul is told the people are sinning against
  Yahweh.
- **2 Samuel 23:17** — David pours out on the ground the water three men risked their lives for:
  *"Is this not the blood of the men who went in jeopardy of their lives?"* Blood as a figure for a
  life spent.
- **Psalm 72:14; Isaiah 1:15; Ezekiel 22:1–13** — the prophets' charge sheet, on which *"your hands
  are full of blood"* is the summary of a society's failure.
- **Isaiah 63:1–6; Joel 3:21** — the divine warrior's garments stained, and blood finally requited.
- **Matthew 26:28; Mark 14:24; Luke 22:20; 1 Corinthians 11:25** — *"this is my blood of the new
  covenant"*, Exodus 24:8 and Jeremiah 31:31 claimed together at a table.
- **John 6:53–56** — *"unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you don't
  have life in yourselves"*, a sentence that is shocking in exact proportion to how absolute Genesis
  9:4 and Leviticus 17 are.
- **Acts 15:20, 29; 21:25** — the Jerusalem council's four abstentions, two of which (*"things
  strangled"* and *"blood"*) are the Genesis 9:4 prohibition. See
  [[The Noahic Commandments and the Council of Jerusalem]].
- **Hebrews 9:11–28** — the sustained argument that blood is what makes a covenant take effect, and
  that the blood of goats and bulls sanctifies the flesh while another blood cleanses the
  conscience.
- **Hebrews 12:24** — *"the blood of sprinkling that speaks better than that of Abel"*: Genesis
  4:10's crying blood answered by a blood that says something else.
- **1 John 1:7; Revelation 1:5; 5:9; 7:14; 12:11** — blood as what cleanses, purchases, and
  overcomes; and **Revelation 6:9–10**, where the souls under the altar are still asking the
  question Abel's blood asked.
### Guilt of bloodshed — Exodus 22:2–3

The WEB's *"guilt of bloodshed"* renders the plural *dāmîm*, *bloods* (MAM, 22:1–2 Hebrew): *ʾên
lô dāmîm* for a thief killed while breaking in, and *dāmîm lô* once the sun has risen on him
(22:2–3). Blood here is a legal status — whether a death is counted against the one who caused
it — and the same killing can carry it or not depending on the hour. See
[[Laws of Property and Restitution]].

### Sacrificial blood and leaven — Exodus 23:1–19

Exodus 23:18 prohibits offering sacrificial blood with leavened bread and leaving festival fat
overnight. It regulates the circumstances of an offering; the verse does not explain the rule as a
prohibition of eating blood or state a theory of atonement. Exodus 34:25 repeats the
blood-and-leaven rule and specifically names the Passover sacrifice in its overnight instruction
(checked in raw).

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The blood of the covenant — Exodus 24:6–8

**One blood, divided between two parties.** Moses puts half the blood of the covenant sacrifices in
basins and throws half on the altar; after the reading and the people's assent he throws the rest on
the people: *"Look, this is the blood of the covenant"* (24:6–8). The verb for both is *zāraq*, *dash*
(MAM), the priests' verb for blood on the altar in Leviticus 1:5 (MAM). The Passover blood was put on
doorposts for God to see (12:13); here, for the only time in the Torah narrative so far, blood is put
on the whole people. Exodus does not explain the rite; self-curse, kinship, and consecration are all
proposed, and the wiki records them as readings. See [[The Blood of the Covenant]] and
[[The Blood of the Covenant - Exodus 24, the Last Supper, and Hebrews 9]].

### The ordination of the priests — Exodus 29:1–46

At Exodus 24:8 Moses sprinkled blood on the people. In Exodus 29 blood is applied to the priests.
The bull's blood goes on the altar's horns and at its base (29:12). The ordination ram's blood is
put on each priest's right ear, thumb, and big toe and sprinkled on the altar (29:20). Blood from
the altar is then mixed with anointing oil and sprinkled on the priests and their garments (29:21).
The chapter gives no explanation of the three bodily places. See [[The Ordination of the Priests]] and
[[The Blood of the Covenant - Exodus 24, the Last Supper, and Hebrews 9]].

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30:1–38

Once a year Aaron puts the blood of the sin offering on the horns of the gold incense altar
(30:10). This is the only blood that touches the altar of incense, on which no animal is offered
(30:9). See [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

The prohibition on offering sacrificial blood with leavened bread repeats 23:18, but the next
sentence specifically names the Passover sacrifice as what must not remain until morning
(34:25). Exodus 23:18 instead names festival fat. The shared context does not justify replacing
one text's object with the other's. This is legislation about an offering, not a second
blood-sprinkling ceremony like 24:8. See [[The Covenant Renewed]].

### Lying in wait for blood — Proverbs 1:11–18

The gang's plan opens *"Let's lay in wait for blood"* (*neʾerbâ lədām*, 1:11); the father says their
feet *"hurry to shed blood"* (*lišpok-dām*, 1:16, nearly verbatim in Isaiah 59:7); and the lecture
turns on *"these lay wait for their own blood"* (*lədāmām yeʾĕrōbû*, 1:18) — the same verb, the
possessive changed. Reuben's *"Shed no blood"* (Genesis 37:22) uses the same verb and noun; see
[[What Profit Is It - Joseph's Brothers and the Ambush of Proverbs 1]]. Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*. See [[Do Not Consent to Sinners]].

### Innocent blood — Proverbs 6:17

*"Hands that shed innocent blood"* (6:17, *dām-nāqî*) stand third among the things Yahweh hates,
and *"feet that are swift in running to mischief"* (6:18) repeat the two verbs of the gang that
*"hurry to shed blood"* (1:16). Exodus 23:7 forbids killing *"the innocent and righteous"*. See
[[The Surety, the Sluggard, and the Seven Abominations]] and
[[Their Feet Run to Evil - Proverbs 1.16, Isaiah 59.7, and Romans 3.15]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 6; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Integrity, law, and public life — Proverbs 28:17

The man tormented by life blood remains a fugitive until death, without support, in the WEB’s
rendering. The saying supplies neither the circumstances of the killing nor an adjudication
procedure. It must not erase Exodus 21:12–14’s distinction between intentional and unintentional
killing. The comparison concerns liability and refuge, not a claim about an identical
source-language expression.

See [[The Wicked Flee When No One Pursues]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 28 and the comparisons
documented on the passage and connection pages.
