# Nakedness and Covering

## Definition / overview

Genesis takes an interest in who is naked, who sees it, and who covers it, and the interest is
structural rather than incidental: the primeval history's two worst episodes both turn on exposure,
and both are resolved by someone putting a garment on someone else.

**Two Hebrew words carry the theme and they are not the same word.** *ʿĀrôm* is the ordinary
adjective — *naked* — used of the man and his wife in [[The Garden of Eden]] and [[The Fall]], where
the point is a change in how they experience their own bodies. *ʿErwâ* is the noun *nakedness* in the
sense of shameful exposure, and it is the word in Genesis 9:22–23; it is also the word Leviticus 18
and 20 build an entire body of sexual law around, in the fixed idiom *"uncover the nakedness of"*
(*gillâ ʿerwat*). The distinction is why a sexual reading of Genesis 9 is even available and why it
is not compelled: the noun is Leviticus's, but the verb in Genesis 9:22 is *saw*, not *uncovered*.

**The pattern the theme establishes:**

1. Nakedness without shame is the created state — *"The man and his wife were both naked, and they
   were not ashamed"* (2:25), the last verse before the serpent.
2. Knowledge produces shame, and the shamed party covers itself, inadequately. *"Their eyes were
   opened, and they both knew that they were naked. They sewed fig leaves together, and made
   coverings for themselves"* (3:7) — and it is not enough, because at 3:10 the man is still afraid
   *"because I was naked"* while wearing them.
3. **In these two rescue scenes, the adequate covering is given by someone else.** *"Yahweh God made coats of animal
   skins for Adam and for his wife, and clothed them"* (3:21) is God's last recorded act in the
   garden. At 9:23 it is Shem and Japheth with a garment on their shoulders. In both cases the person
   exposed does not fix it, and in both cases the covering costs the coverer something — a skin, or a
   walk backwards into a tent.
4. Seeing is the offence. Genesis 9 spends four clauses on the brothers *not* seeing, and the whole
   contrast between Ham and his brothers is built out of the verb *rāʾâ*.

That covering is an act of mercy rather than of concealment is the theme's theological load, and it
is why the New Testament can end where it does: white garments given *"that the shame of your
nakedness may not be revealed"* (Revelation 3:18).

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 2:25** — *"both naked, and they were not ashamed"*: the state the fall interrupts, placed
  deliberately as the last line before Genesis 3. See [[The Garden of Eden]].
- **Genesis 3:7, 3:10–11, 3:21** — the eyes opened, the fig leaves, the hiding, and God's question
  *"Who told you that you were naked?"*; then the skins. The first thing the knowledge of good and
  evil produces is self-consciousness about the body, and the first thing God does after sentencing
  is clothe them. See [[The Fall]].
- **Genesis 9:20–23** — Noah uncovers himself (the verb is reflexive), Ham sees and tells, and Shem
  and Japheth walk in backwards with a garment. The Genesis 3 sequence run again with human beings in
  every role: the exposure is self-inflicted, the seeing is the sin, and the covering is done by
  brothers. See [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]] and
  [[Naked and Ashamed - Genesis 3 and Genesis 9]].
- **Exodus 20:26; 28:42** — the theme legislated: no steps up to the altar *"that your nakedness may
  not be exposed to it"*, and linen breeches for the priests *"to cover the flesh of their
  nakedness"* (quotations corrected to the WEB in `raw/bible/Exodus.md`). Exposure
  is a cultic problem before it is a moral one.
- **Leviticus 18:6–19; 20:11, 17–21** — *gillâ ʿerwat*, "uncover the nakedness of", as the Torah's
  standing idiom for illicit intercourse, listed relation by relation. Leviticus 18:8's *"the
  nakedness of your father's wife… it is your father's nakedness"* is the verse a sexual reading of
  Genesis 9:22 leans on.
- **Habakkuk 2:15–16; Lamentations 4:21; Nahum 3:5** — the prophets fuse drink and exposure into a
  single image of humiliation, and in Habakkuk it is the one who makes his neighbour drunk *"that you
  may gaze at their naked bodies"* who is condemned. That is Genesis 9 read with the blame placed
  where the narrative's own structure places it.
- **Ezekiel 16:8** — *"I spread my skirt over you, and covered your nakedness"*: covering as the
  gesture of covenant and marriage, God doing for Jerusalem what he did for the couple in the garden.
- **Isaiah 61:10; Zechariah 3:3–5** — garments of salvation, and filthy clothes exchanged for clean
  ones on the high priest: the covering given from outside, now as an image of standing rather than
  of modesty.
- **Matthew 25:36, 43** — *"naked, and you clothed me"*: covering the exposed as one of the six acts
  the judgment turns on.
- **2 Corinthians 5:2–4** — the longing *"to be clothed with our habitation which is from heaven"* so
  as not to be found naked: the theme applied to the body itself.
- **Revelation 3:17–18; 16:15** — the church that does not know it is *"wretched, miserable, poor,
  blind, and naked"*, told to buy white garments; and the blessing on the one who *"keeps his clothes,
  so that he doesn't walk naked"*. The canon closes with the image it opened with, and with the
  covering still coming from elsewhere.

### The altar without steps — Exodus 20:26

*"You shall not go up by steps to my altar, that your nakedness may not be exposed to it"* (20:26).
The noun is *ʿerwâ* (WLC), the word of Noah's nakedness (Genesis 9:22–23), and within Exodus it
recurs only at the priests' linen breeches (28:42). **Two solutions to one problem stand in the same
book**: no steps here, a garment there. See [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

**The pledged cloak of Exodus 22:26–27 is now ingested.** *"That is his only covering, it is his
garment for his skin. What would he sleep in?"* The garment is *śimlâ*, the word for the cloak Shem
and Japheth laid over their father (Genesis 9:23, MAM), and the law lets the lender hold it only in
daylight. See [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].

### The priestly garments — Exodus 28:1–43

Linen breeches are made *"to cover the flesh of their nakedness"* from waist to thigh, worn
when the priests approach the altar or enter the tent, *"that they don't bear iniquity, and
die"* (Exodus 28:42–43). Exodus 20:26 had forbidden steps to the altar for the same exposure;
the text does not link the two rules itself. See [[The Priestly Garments]] and [[An Altar of Uncut Stones - Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 27, and Joshua 8]].

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

Moses covers his face after speaking and removes the veil before Yahweh (34:33–35).
This covering concerns the radiance of an encounter, not nakedness, sexual shame, or a garment
given by another. The earlier pattern of mercifully clothing an exposed person therefore
cannot be made a universal account of biblical covering. No textile, color, or method of
fastening is specified. See [[The Shining Face and the Veil - Exodus 34 and 2 Corinthians 3]].

See [[The Shining Face of Moses]].

## Key passages

- [[The Shining Face of Moses]] — Exodus 34:29–35; The face veil covers radiance rather than shameful exposure.

- [[The Garden of Eden]] — 2:25, nakedness without shame
- [[The Wager in Heaven and the Four Messengers]] — Job 1:21's *ʿārōm*, **the theme's one use that
  has nothing to do with shame**: *"Naked I came out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return
  there."* Here the word marks the two ends of a life and the symmetry between what a person brings
  and what a person takes; Genesis 3:19's *"to dust you shall return"* stands behind it. See
  [[Naked I Came - Job 1.21 and the Empty Hands of Ecclesiastes and Paul]]
- [[The Fall]] — 3:7 and 3:21: the inadequate covering and the given one
- [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]] — 9:20–23, the whole sequence repeated among human beings
- [[Naked and Ashamed - Genesis 3 and Genesis 9]] — the two scenes set against each other
- [[The Stolen Blessing]] — Genesis 27:15–16; **clothing turned from covering into disguise.** The
  first garments in Scripture are God's, made of animal skins to cover a shame the couple had already
  failed to cover (3:21); here a mother puts her elder son's *"good clothes"* on her younger and ties
  goat skins to his hands and neck, and **the skins are used to make a man be someone else rather
  than to make him decent.** From this point almost every significant garment in Genesis produces a
  false belief: Joseph's coat in blood (37:31), Tamar's veil in place of widow's clothes (38:14),
  Joseph's cloak in Potiphar's wife's hand (39:12). See
  [[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]]
- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:3, 23, 31–33; **a garment given, stripped, dipped, and
  delivered — and it does all the work the chapter needs.** ***Kətōnet passîm***: the noun is **God's own
  word for what he made Adam and Eve** (3:21) and **the priest's tunic** (Exodus 28:4), and *passîm* occurs
  in this sense in only one other place in the Bible — **on Tamar at 2 Samuel 13:18–19, who tears hers after
  Amnon rapes her.** *"They stripped Joseph of his coat"* is the first thing the brothers do, **before the
  pit**; the coat is the cause at 37:3–4 and the instrument at 37:31–33; and the boy goes to Egypt without
  it. See [[A Garment and a Goat - Jacob Deceives and Is Deceived]]
- [[Judah and Tamar]] — Genesis 38:14, 19; **the garments of widowhood put off and put back on within
  six verses**, with the whole plot in between. What goes on over them is the ***ṣāʿîp***, **a veil the
  Hebrew Bible mentions in only one other place — [[Rebekah]]'s at 24:65**, where she covers herself on
  first seeing the man she is to marry. **The same rare word does bridal modesty in one chapter and
  roadside anonymity in another**, and both times it is a woman putting cloth between herself and a man
  she is about to be joined to. **What the veil does here is the opposite of what tradition says it
  does**: Genesis never claims a veil marked a prostitute — it says [[Judah]] *"thought that she was a
  prostitute, **for she had covered her face**."* **The inference is his, and the narrator gives the
  grounds so the reader can see how thin they are**
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:9, 12; ***ʿerwat hāʾāreṣ***, *"the nakedness of the land"* — **Noah's word
  (9:22–23) applied to a country's undefended border.** It makes the espionage charge an accusation of
  shameful exposure, **levelled by the one man in the room with something to hide**, at the ten men who
  once uncovered a brother and left him in a pit
- [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] — Job 22:6; ***"stripped the naked of their clothing"*** — the pledged
  cloak of Exodus 22:26–27, taken from a man with nothing else to sleep in. Job will answer that the
  naked were *"warmed with my sheep's fleece"* (31:20) and that he himself *"put on righteousness,
  and it clothed me"* (29:14)
- [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]] — Job 24:7–10; *"They lie all night naked without
  clothing, and have no covering in the cold … So that they go around naked without clothing."* The
  pledged cloak of Exodus 22:26–27, not returned at nightfall, and the poor who *"embrace the rock for
  lack of a shelter"* — the nakedness Eliphaz falsely laid at Job's door (22:6).
- [[The Months of Old]] — Job 29:14; ***"I put on righteousness, and it clothed me"***: the man
  accused of stripping the naked (22:6) remembers himself clothed in justice, robe and turban, the
  dress that 19:9's *"He has stripped me of my glory, and taken the crown from my head"* took off.
  See [[I Put On Righteousness - Job 29.14 and the Garments of Justice]].
- [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]] — Exodus 20:26; no steps to the altar, *"that your nakedness
  may not be exposed to it"*
- [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] — Exodus 22:26–27; *"his garment for his skin"*, returned before sunset
- [[The Priestly Garments]] — Exodus 28:1–43; linen breeches for the priests, under threat of death.
