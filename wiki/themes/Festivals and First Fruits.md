# Festivals and First Fruits

## Definition / overview

Recurring worship ordered around remembered deliverance and the produce of the land. A festival sets
aside time; first fruits dedicate the beginning of the harvest. Exodus 23:14–19 brings these
practices together without treating every offering or every holy day as the same observance.

## Development across Scripture

**The departure becomes a memorial.** [[The Passover]] gives a continuing feast and seven days of
Unleavened Bread (Exodus 12:14–20). [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] connects the observance
with Abib, the departure, and instruction to children (13:3–10). The firstborn claim is related to
these observances but concerns sons and animals, not a synonym for first fruits.

**Produce must not be withheld.** [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] requires offerings from
harvest and presses without delay (22:29). [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] places the first fruits
within the annual cycle: Unleavened Bread, Harvest, and Ingathering, with three male appearances and
the first of the first fruits brought to Yahweh's house (23:14–19). A named house is not yet an
identified sanctuary site.

**The calendar is repeated with differences.** Exodus 34:18–26, now in [[The Covenant Renewed]],
adds a promise of security during the appearances. Deuteronomy 16:1–17, verified WEB but not yet
ingested, specifies Weeks and Tents, the chosen place, giving according to blessing, and rejoicing
shared with dependents and vulnerable residents. Its explicit inclusion of women in rejoicing
prevents reading the male-appearance requirement as an exclusion of women. See
[[Three Times a Year - Exodus 23, Exodus 34, and Deuteronomy 16]].

The household rite of Exodus 12 and Deuteronomy's chosen-place Passover are not identical location
instructions; [[The Passover]] records the tension. This theme preserves both. Source texts were
checked in local Exodus and through bible-api.com's WEB for Deuteronomy 16:1–17 on 2026-10-23.

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Aaron proclaims a feast to Yahweh and the people sacrifice and celebrate before the calf (32:5–6).
The use of the divine name and familiar festival practices does not authorize the image. This is
an improvised feast in the narrative, not another commanded annual festival. See [[The Golden Calf]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

Unleavened Bread remembers the departure from Egypt in Abib (34:18); Weeks concerns wheat first
fruits, and the year-end feast is called harvest in this WEB text (34:22). The seasonal
descriptions distinguish the observances without changing the translation to match 23:16. The
three annual male appearances receive a promise that no one will desire Israel's land during the
journey (34:23–24). Passover and first-fruits rules follow (34:25–26). See [[Three Times a Year - Exodus 23, Exodus 34, and Deuteronomy 16]]. See
[[The Covenant Renewed]].

### First fruits as honour — Proverbs 3:9–10

*"Honor Yahweh with your substance, with the first fruits of all your increase: so your barns will
be filled with plenty"* (3:9–10). *Rēʾšîṯ* is the first fruits of Exodus 23:19 and 34:26, and the verb
is the fifth commandment's *kabbēḏ*, *honour*. The father widens the harvest offering to *hôn*, wealth
of any kind, and promises Deuteronomy's blessing *"in your barns"* (Deuteronomy 28:8). See [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]] and
[[Honor Yahweh with Your Substance - First Fruits in Exodus 23, Proverbs 3.9-10, and Malachi 3.10]]. Source: Proverbs 3 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.
