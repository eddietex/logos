# Festivals and First Fruits

## Definition / overview

Recurring worship ordered around remembered deliverance and the produce of the land. A festival sets
aside time; first fruits dedicate the beginning of the harvest. Exodus 23:14–19 brings these
practices together without treating every offering or every holy day as the same observance.

## Development across Scripture

### Reading to all Israel — Deuteronomy 31:9–13

The feast of tents in the year of release becomes the occasion for reading the law at the chosen place (10–11). The express summons includes men, women, little ones, and resident foreigners (12). Deuteronomy 16:16’s male pilgrimage requirement is not a prohibition on these others attending.

See [[Read the Law to All Israel]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:9–13.

### First fruits to the priest — Deuteronomy 18:4

*"The first fruits of your grain, of your new wine, and of your oil, and the first of the fleece of your sheep, you shall give him"* (4). Numbers 18:12 gives Aaron the first fruits of the same three crops. The fleece is new: *gēz* occurs nowhere else in the Torah (WLC).

See [[The Portion of the Priests and Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 18:1–8.

### Three feasts at the place — Deuteronomy 16:1–17

Deuteronomy's calendar keeps the three feasts of Exodus 23 and 34 and names them unleavened bread, weeks, and tents (16:16). It gives no day of the month for any of them: Passover is kept in *"the month of Abib"* (1), weeks is counted from the first cutting of the grain (9), and tents follows the gathering from threshing floor and wine press (13). It has no sheaf or loaves, no living in shelters, and no eighth day (compare Leviticus 23). What it adds is the chosen place for all three, including the Passover, which *"You may not sacrifice … within any of your gates"* (5); the household and its dependants rejoicing; and gifts *"according to Yahweh your God's blessing"* (17). The seventh day of unleavened bread is an *ʿăṣeret* (8), the word Leviticus 23:36 and Numbers 29:35 use only for the eighth day after booths (WLC lemma search, morphhb).

See [[Passover, Weeks, and Booths]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 16:1–17.

### Duties and portions — Numbers 18:1–32

The best oil, vintage, grain, and first-ripe fruit brought to Yahweh become
Aaron’s household provision (12–13). The passage specifies recipients and purity,
without adding a festival date.

See [[The Duties and Portions of Priests and Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 18.

### Land rest and Jubilee — Leviticus 25:1–22

Seven Sabbaths of years and a fiftieth year (8–10) extend the calendar’s counting patterns. Compare the seven Sabbaths and fifty days of 23:15–16: the structures resemble one another, but their units and prescribed acts differ.

See [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 25:1–22; contextual verses as cited.


### Booths and remembered deliverance — Leviticus 23:33–44

The seventh month’s fifteenth day begins seven days of harvest celebration, with
an eighth-day assembly (34–41). Shelters give the harvest feast an explicit exodus
memory (42–43); Moses’ declaration closes the calendar (44).

See [[The Feast of Booths]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:33–44; comparisons as cited.

### Trumpets and atonement — Leviticus 23:23–32

The first day of the seventh month brings a trumpet memorial, rest, and assembly
(24–25); the tenth brings atonement, self-denial, and complete rest (27–32). Not every
appointed assembly is a harvest celebration. No remembered event is specified for the memorial.

See [[Trumpets and the Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:23–32; contextual references as cited.

### First fruits and harvest provision — Leviticus 23:9–22

The sheaf precedes eating the harvest; seven Sabbaths and fifty days lead to two
leavened first-fruits loaves, accompanying sacrifices, and an assembly (10–21). The
unit ends with gleanings for the poor and foreigner (22), joining dedication to provision.

See [[First Fruits and the Feast of Weeks]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:9–22; contextual references as cited.


### Sabbath and the opening feasts — Leviticus 23:1–8

Passover is dated to the first month’s fourteenth evening, with Unleavened Bread beginning
on the fifteenth for seven days (5–6). First and seventh days have assemblies and work
restrictions, and all seven have fire offerings (7–8). The earlier departure narrative
is assumed rather than retold. See [[Passover and Unleavened Bread - Exodus 12 and Leviticus 23]].

See [[Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:1–8; Exodus 12:16 where cited.


### Field and community — Leviticus 19:19–37

New fruit trees have three years of forbidden fruit, a fourth year holy for praise, and a fifth
year for eating (23–25). This is an orchard timetable, not the announcement of another annual
festival.

See [[Holy Living in Field and Community]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19.


**The departure becomes a memorial.** [[The Passover]] gives a continuing feast and seven days of
Unleavened Bread (Exodus 12:14–20). [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] connects the observance
with Abib, the departure, and instruction to children (13:3–10). The firstborn claim is related to
these observances but concerns sons and animals, not a synonym for first fruits.

**Produce must not be withheld.** [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] requires offerings from
harvest and presses without delay (22:29). [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] places the first fruits
within the annual cycle: Unleavened Bread, Harvest, and Ingathering, with three male appearances and
the first of the first fruits brought to Yahweh's house (23:14–19). A named house is not yet an
identified sanctuary site.

**The calendar is repeated with differences.** Exodus 34:18–26, now in [[The Covenant Renewed]],
adds a promise of security during the appearances. Deuteronomy 16:1–17, verified WEB but not yet
ingested, specifies Weeks and Tents, the chosen place, giving according to blessing, and rejoicing
shared with dependents and vulnerable residents. Its explicit inclusion of women in rejoicing
prevents reading the male-appearance requirement as an exclusion of women. See
[[Three Times a Year - Exodus 23, Exodus 34, and Deuteronomy 16]].

The household rite of Exodus 12 and Deuteronomy's chosen-place Passover are not identical location
instructions; [[The Passover]] records the tension. This theme preserves both. Source texts were
checked in local Exodus and through bible-api.com's WEB for Deuteronomy 16:1–17 on 2026-10-23.

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Aaron proclaims a feast to Yahweh and the people sacrifice and celebrate before the calf (32:5–6).
The use of the divine name and familiar festival practices does not authorize the image. This is
an improvised feast in the narrative, not another commanded annual festival. See [[The Golden Calf]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

Unleavened Bread remembers the departure from Egypt in Abib (34:18); Weeks concerns wheat first
fruits, and the year-end feast is called harvest in this WEB text (34:22). The seasonal
descriptions distinguish the observances without changing the translation to match 23:16. The
three annual male appearances receive a promise that no one will desire Israel's land during the
journey (34:23–24). Passover and first-fruits rules follow (34:25–26). See [[Three Times a Year - Exodus 23, Exodus 34, and Deuteronomy 16]]. See
[[The Covenant Renewed]].

### First fruits as honour — Proverbs 3:9–10

*"Honor Yahweh with your substance, with the first fruits of all your increase: so your barns will
be filled with plenty"* (3:9–10). *Rēʾšîṯ* is the first fruits of Exodus 23:19 and 34:26, and the verb
is the fifth commandment's *kabbēḏ*, *honour*. The father widens the harvest offering to *hôn*, wealth
of any kind, and promises Deuteronomy's blessing *"in your barns"* (Deuteronomy 28:8). See [[Trust in Yahweh with All Your Heart]] and
[[Honor Yahweh with Your Substance - First Fruits in Exodus 23, Proverbs 3.9-10, and Malachi 3.10]]. Source: Proverbs 3 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### The meal offering — Leviticus 2

Yeast and honey may be offered as first fruits but not burned for a pleasant aroma (2:11–12); parched first-fruits grain has its own burned memorial (14–16). First fruits are not a single uniform altar preparation. See [[The Meal Offering]].

### The tenth day of the seventh month — Leviticus 16:1–34

Chapter 16 fixes its rite to a calendar date (29) and makes it annual (34). It is a day of
affliction and rest rather than of feasting or first fruits, and its rite is given here,
before the festival calendar of chapter 23 restates the date. See
[[The Day of Atonement Commanded Twice - Leviticus 16 and 23]].

See [[The Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 16; comparisons as cited.

### A second date — Numbers 9:1–14

The first feast the Bible narrates after Egypt is kept at Sinai on its date (5). A second date, the fourteenth of the second month, is then set for those unclean by a dead body or far away (10–11). The feast stays fixed, and access to it is made wider. The clean man who stays away is cut off (13).

See [[The Passover in the Second Year]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 9.

### Sound at the feasts — Numbers 10:10

The set feasts and the first of each month are marked by trumpets over the offerings. Psalm 81:3 has the *šôpār* at the new moon, not this instrument (WLC), and neither text mentions the other.

See [[The Two Silver Trumpets]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 10.

### The spies — Numbers 13

*The time of the first-ripe grapes*, *bikkûrê ʿănābîm* (20, WLC), gives the mission its date. The
men bring the land's first fruit before they own it, and they do so as evidence and not as an
offering.

See [[The Twelve Spies]].

### The new moon — Numbers 28:11–15

The beginnings of months, marked by trumpets in Numbers 10:10, receive their own offerings: two young
bulls, one ram, seven lambs, and a male goat for a sin offering. The same animal set recurs for
Unleavened Bread and Weeks in the next unit (28:19–22, 27–30). See
[[Sabbath and New Moon - Numbers 28, Ezekiel 46, Isaiah 66, and Colossians 2]].

See [[Daily, Sabbath, and New Moon Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 28:1–15.

### The annual feasts numbered — Numbers 28:16–29:40

Numbers gives the animals for every day Leviticus 23 dated: two bulls, a ram, seven lambs, and a goat
for each day of Unleavened Bread and for the first fruits; one bull for the first and tenth of the
seventh month and the eighth day; and for the seven days of tents a descending series of bulls from
thirteen to seven, seventy in all. On the first fruits it disagrees with Leviticus 23:18 (two bulls and
one ram against one bull and two rams); both are recorded. Ezra 3:4 later describes the feast of tents
kept *by number, according to the ordinance*. See
[[The Feast Offerings Numbered - Leviticus 23, Numbers 28-29, Ezra 3, and Ezekiel 45]].

See [[The Offerings of the Appointed Feasts]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 28:16–29:40.

### The basket and the confession — Deuteronomy 26:1–11

The first of the ground's fruit goes in a basket to the chosen place, and the farmer recites over it
the history from *"My father was a Syrian ready to perish"* to *"a land flowing with milk and honey"*,
and ends *"Now, behold, I have brought the first of the fruit of the ground, which you, Yahweh, have
given me"* (5–10). Deuteronomy's word is *rēʾšît*, *first*; it never uses *bikkûrîm* (WLC lemma
search). The rite ends in worship, the only verse in Deuteronomy where *hištaḥăwâ* is directed to
Yahweh, and in rejoicing with the Levite and the foreigner (10–11). See
[[First Fruits and the Tithe Declaration]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 26:1–11.
