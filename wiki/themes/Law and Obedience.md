# Law and Obedience

## Definition / overview

**God's commands to his people, and their hearing and keeping of them.** The Torah's vocabulary is
already plural before Sinai:

- ***ḥōq*** / ***ḥuqqâ***, *statute* — something engraved or fixed
- ***mišpāṭ***, *ordinance*, *judgment*, *justice*
- ***miṣwâ***, *commandment*
- ***tôrâ***, *law*, *instruction*

and its verbs of response are ***šāmaʿ***, *hear*, *obey*, ***šāmar***, *keep*, and ***ʿāśâ***, *do*
(all WLC).

**The theme existed in the wiki before it had a page.** The material sat on [[Testing]] and [[Covenant]],
and on the connection [[Because Abraham Obeyed My Voice - Law Before Sinai]], until Marah. **It gets its own
page at the first statute given to Israel after the sea**: *"There he made a statute and an ordinance for
them, and there he tested them"* (Exodus 15:25).

**Two things about the theme are set before Sinai and stay:**

1. **The Sinai covenant demand follows deliverance; this is not a claim about every law.** The
   earlier wording here, “never precedes it,” contradicted the Passover commands given before
   departure (Exodus 12:1–28). Marah follows the sea and Sinai recalls rescue in Exodus 19:4–5;
   the Ten Commandments open *"I am Yahweh your God, who brought
   you out of the land of Egypt"* (20:2; [[The Ten Commandments]]).
2. **Obedience is described as hearing.** At Marah the four conditions are *listen*, *do*, *pay attention*,
   *keep* (15:26) — **three of the four are verbs of attention, not of action.**

**At Sinai obedience is promised before the detailed covenant laws are spoken.** Yahweh calls
for obedience and covenant keeping (19:5), and the people assent together (19:8). Their rescue
is already the stated ground of the address (19:4); their promise is not evidence that every
later command has already been performed. See [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] and
[[Because Abraham Obeyed My Voice - Law Before Sinai]].

## Development across Scripture

**Genesis — a command, and then commandments no narrative gives.** The first command is in the garden
(Genesis 2:16–17). **Genesis 26:5 says Abraham *"obeyed my voice, and kept my requirements, my
commandments, my statutes, and my laws"*** — the first *miṣwâ*, the first *ḥuqqâ*, and the first plural
*tôrôt* in Scripture (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus), for a man to whom no such list was ever given. See
[[Because Abraham Obeyed My Voice - Law Before Sinai]] and [[Isaac in Gerar]]. **The first *ḥōq* is
Egypt's**: *"Joseph made it a statute concerning the land of Egypt to this day, that Pharaoh should have the
fifth"* (47:26). See [[Joseph and the Famine]].

**Exodus 5 — the statute of Egypt.** Pharaoh's foremen are beaten and asked *"Why haven't you fulfilled
your quota (ḥoqkem)"* (5:14; WLC). **Israel's first *ḥōq* in Exodus is a brick quota.** See
[[Bricks Without Straw]].

**Exodus 12–13 — law on the night of leaving.** *"An ordinance forever"* (*ḥuqqat ʿôlām*, 12:14, 17; WLC);
*"One law (tôrâ) shall be to him who is born at home, and to the stranger"* (12:49) — the first singular
*tôrâ* in Scripture; *"that Yahweh's law may be in your mouth"* (13:9). See [[The Passover]],
[[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]], and [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]].

**Exodus 15 — the first statute after the sea.** At Marah, *"a statute and an ordinance"* — *ḥōq ûmišpāṭ*,
the pair's first occurrence (WLC) — **made with the same verb, *śîm*, as Joseph's statute for Pharaoh**
(Genesis 47:26). **Its content is not given**; what is given is a condition in the vocabulary of Genesis
26:5: *"If you will diligently listen to Yahweh your God's voice, and will do that which is right in his
eyes, and will pay attention to his commandments, and keep all his statutes"* (15:26). **God *"showed"*
Moses the tree with *yārâ*, the verb of teaching, from which *tôrâ* is usually derived** (WLC for the verb;
the etymology reported, not checked in this pass). **And the infinitive-absolute *"if you will diligently
listen"* (*ʾim-šāmôaʿ tišmaʿ*) is the form that opens the covenant at Sinai** (19:5; WLC). See
[[Bitter Water at Marah]].

**Exodus 16 — law as a rule about bread.** God's first *"my law"* in Exodus — *"that I may test them,
whether they will walk in my law (bətôrātî), or not"* (16:4; WLC) — **is a rule about gathering food**: a
day's portion each day, nothing kept overnight, a double portion on the sixth day. **It is disobeyed twice,
and the second time God asks *"How long do you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?"*** (16:28) —
***miṣwōtay wətôrōtāy***, the two words of Genesis 26:5 in the same forms and order (WLC), **and the
refusal verb of Pharaoh** (*māʾēn*, 10:3). **The law in question is the Sabbath, named a week before** and
not yet written (16:23). See [[Manna and Quail]] and
[[Because Abraham Obeyed My Voice - Law Before Sinai]].

**Ahead** (written before Sinai was ingested): the Book of the Covenant (20:22–23:33), whose
*mišpāṭîm* begin at 21:1 and which is now ingested through [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]; the tablets *"that you may teach them"* (24:12, *yārâ* again); and Deuteronomy's *"statutes and
ordinances"*, its refrain (4:1, 5; 5:1).

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:1–21

God publicly speaks the commandments after naming himself as Israel's deliverer (20:1–2). The
instructions join worship, time, family, neighbors, and desire; obedience cannot be reduced to
ritual or visible acts alone. The people's request for Moses to speak includes a promise to listen
(20:19), not a reported rejection of the commands.
[[The Ten Commandments Retold - Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5]] preserves the differences in the later
account. See [[The Ten Commandments]].

### The ordinances begin — Exodus 21:1

*"Now these are the ordinances which you shall set before them"* (21:1). **After the commandments
spoken to all, the case law begins, given to Moses to set before the people.** Its form changes from
*you shall* to *if* (21:2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11), from command to case. Exodus 24:3 reports that
Moses *"told the people all Yahweh's words, and all the ordinances"*, and the people promise to
obey. See [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]] and [[The Blood of the Covenant]].

### Violence, injury, and liability — Exodus 21:12–36

The cases of 21:12–36 distinguish intent, recovery, a victim's status, and prior warning. Murder
receives death, an unintentional killer receives refuge, an injured neighbor receives lost time and
healing, and negligent ownership can require replacement or expose the owner to death. **Case law
requires judgment about facts; it is not one penalty applied to every loss.** The collection still
assumes slavery while restricting some acts against servants.
See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]].
### Property, deposit, and restitution — Exodus 22:1–15

The eighth commandment receives its cases. Theft is repaid double, or fourfold and fivefold
when the animal is gone; a thief who cannot pay is sold; loss through grazing or fire is made good;
and a keeper's liability turns on what he could have prevented (22:1–15). The law here regulates
loss as well as wrongdoing, and it trusts an oath where there is no witness (22:10–11).
See [[Laws of Property and Restitution]].

**At 22:16–31 the ordinances change form.** After one more casuistic case, the seduced virgin's
bride-price, the law shifts to short absolute sentences — death for the sorceress, for bestiality,
and for sacrifice to other gods — and then to God speaking in the first person about whom he will
protect: *"they cry at all to me, I will surely hear"* (22:23). **The section ends by naming the
purpose of the whole: *"You shall be holy men to me"*** (22:31). See
[[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].

### One address for court, field, and feast — Exodus 23:1–19

The ordinances require truthful testimony, impartial judgment, aid to an enemy, protection of the
alien, interrupted cultivation, and shared rest before prescribing the festivals (23:1–19). The
requirement to attend to all God's words (23:13) holds these subjects together. The passage reports
legislation, not its observance, and does not permit poverty or majority opinion to substitute for a
just verdict.

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The law ends in a promise with a condition — Exodus 23:20–33

The Book of the Covenant closes with *"if you indeed listen to his voice, and do all that I speak,
then I will be an enemy to your enemies"* (23:22). The construction *šāmōaʿ tišmaʿ bəqōlô*, *if you
indeed listen to his voice* (MAM), is the one already used at Marah (15:26) and in the covenant offer
(19:5). The promised outcomes — protection, blessing, health, and land — follow from listening, and
the danger named is serving other gods (23:24–25, 33). The whole Book of the Covenant, 20:22–23:33,
is now ingested. See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]].

### "We will do, and we will hear" — Exodus 24:3–12

The people assent twice. After hearing the words told, *naʿăśeh*, *we will do* (24:3); after hearing them
read from *"the book of the covenant"*, *naʿăśeh wənišmāʿ*, *we will do and we will hear* — WEB *"will we
do, and be obedient"* (24:7; MAM). **Writing enters the law here**: Moses writes the words (24:4), and God
promises *"the stone tablets with the law and the commands that I have written, that you may teach
them"* (24:12) — *hattôrâ wəhammiṣwâ* (MAM), *tôrâ* now a written thing given in order to be taught. See
[[The Blood of the Covenant]].

**Obedience includes how things are made and placed.** Exodus 26:30 requires Moses to erect the
tabernacle according to what he was shown on the mountain. Dimensions, joins, and the placement of
the veil and furniture all belong to the instruction. Exodus 36:8–38 later reports the making of
the enclosure, while 40:18–28 reports its erection and arrangement; those later units remain
un-ingested. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]] and [[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

### The altar, court, and maintained light — Exodus 27:1–21

Exodus 27:8 repeats the requirement to follow the design shown on the mountain. Verse 21
then makes lamp service an enduring statute through Israel's generations. Obedience includes
both a particular act of construction and recurring maintenance. Exodus 38:1–7, 9–20 later
reports the altar and court made, and 40:24–33 records their use and arrangement; those units
remain un-ingested. See [[The Altar and the Court]] and
[[As God Commanded, So He Did - The Obedience Formula]].

### Commanded work, commanded rest, written testimony — Exodus 31

The makers are to do all that God commanded Moses (31:6, 11). The obedience required includes
stopping work for the Sabbath (31:13–17); a sacred project is not given leave to disregard sacred
time. The tablets promised in 24:12 are delivered in 31:18 as God's own writing. This verse does not
transcribe their contents or report Israel's compliance.

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

Exodus 32:8 calls the calf a quick departure from the commanded way. Israel has both heard the
commands and pledged obedience (20:3–5; 24:3–8). Moses breaks the tablets God wrote (32:15–19), so
the narrative makes the material signs of the covenant share the crisis without declaring the
commands themselves revoked. See [[The Golden Calf]].
