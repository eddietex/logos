# Tithing

## Definition / overview

Giving a tenth — and in the Bible, a practice that exists for two full generations before anybody
is told to do it.

*"Abram gave him a tenth of all"* (Genesis 14:20). The Hebrew is *maʿăśēr*, from *ʿeśer*, ten. It is
voluntary, it is out of war spoil rather than out of harvest or herd, it is paid to a foreign priest,
and no command, covenant, or law lies behind it. The same is true of the second instance: Jacob
vows one at Bethel — *"Of all that you will give me I will surely give a tenth to you"* (28:22) — as
part of a bargain he proposes himself. Only at Leviticus 27:30 does a tenth become an obligation.
See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] and [[The Tithe Before the Law]].

**The grammar of the first one is genuinely ambiguous, and its meaning is not.**
*Wayyitten-lô maʿăśēr mikkōl* has no expressed subject and no expressed object: *and he gave him a
tenth of everything*. Both men are in the room, so the verse can be read either way, and a minority
Jewish reading takes Melchizedek as the giver. Hebrews 7:2, 4 is the earliest surviving explicit
reading — *"to whom even Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth out of the best plunder"* — and every
major tradition, and the WEB, follows it. This wiki notes the grammar and follows the consensus.

**A tenth was not a Hebrew invention.** Tithes to temples and to kings are attested across the
ancient Near East, in Ugarit, Mesopotamia, and among the Phoenicians and Greeks; 1 Samuel 8:15, 17
warns Israel that a king will take a tenth of their grain and their flocks exactly as a temple
would. What is distinctive in Genesis is not the fraction but that it is offered without being
asked for.

## Development across Scripture

- **Voluntary, twice, and both times at a hinge.** Abram after the rescue (14:20), in the same
  scene in which he refuses to take a thread from the king of Sodom; Jacob at Bethel (28:20–22),
  fleeing his brother with nothing. One gives from more than he can carry, the other from nothing at
  all.
- **Then commanded, and in more than one form.** Leviticus 27:30–33 makes a tenth of seed, fruit,
  and herd *"holy to Yahweh"*, redeemable at a fifth above value. Numbers 18:21–32 assigns it to
  Levi in place of a land inheritance, and requires Levi to tithe the tithe to the priests.
  Deuteronomy 14:22–29 has the giver eat it at the sanctuary — converting it to silver if the
  journey is long, and spending it on *"whatever your soul desires"* — with every third year's tithe
  stored locally for the Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless, and the widow. Reconciling these
  into one, two, or three distinct tithes is an old problem; rabbinic tradition settled on multiple
  tithes, and modern scholarship generally reads them as successive legislations.
- **Reformers restore it, prophets weaponize it.** Hezekiah's and Nehemiah's reforms both turn on
  getting the tithe flowing again so the Levites can stay at their posts (2 Chronicles 31:4–12;
  Nehemiah 10:37–39; 13:10–12). Malachi 3:8–10 makes withholding it robbery — *"Bring the whole
  tithe into the storehouse"* — and attaches the Bible's one explicit invitation to test God.
  Amos 4:4 does the opposite, mocking scrupulous tithing at a corrupt shrine.
- **Jesus mentions it twice, and neither time is it the subject.** *"You tithe mint, dill, and cumin,
  and have left undone the weightier matters of the law: justice, mercy, and faith. But you ought to
  have done these, and not to have left the other undone"* (Matthew 23:23; Luke 11:42) — an
  affirmation folded inside a rebuke about proportion. And the Pharisee in the temple who *"gives
  tithes of all that I get"* (Luke 18:12) goes home not justified.
- **The epistles never command it.** The New Testament's giving language is proportional and
  voluntary — *"as he may prosper"* (1 Corinthians 16:2), *"not grudgingly, or under compulsion, for
  God loves a cheerful giver"* (2 Corinthians 9:7) — and the one extended treatment of Genesis
  14:20, Hebrews 7:1–10, uses the tithe as evidence about Melchizedek's rank rather than as
  instruction. Whether the tenth binds Christians has been argued from that silence in both
  directions since the patristic period, and the wiki records the argument without settling it.

## Key passages

- Genesis 14:18–20 — the first tithe, [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]
- Genesis 28:20–22 — Jacob's vow at Bethel
- Leviticus 27:30–33 — a tenth is holy to Yahweh
- Numbers 18:21–32 — Levi's inheritance, and the tithe of the tithe
- Deuteronomy 14:22–29; 26:12–15 — the eaten tithe and the third-year tithe
- 2 Chronicles 31:4–12; Nehemiah 10:37–39; 13:10–12 — the reforms
- Malachi 3:8–10 — *"Will a man rob God?"*
- Matthew 23:23; Luke 11:42; 18:12 — mint, dill, and cumin
- Hebrews 7:1–10 — Levi tithed in Abraham's body
