# Redemption

## Definition / overview

Getting back, by right or by price, what belongs to you and has passed out of your hands — a person, a
field, a life. **The Torah uses two verbs for it, and the wiki has now met both.** (Both checked in the WLC.)

- ***Gāʾal*** — the act of the ***gōʾēl***, the kinsman whose duty is to buy back family land, redeem a
  relative sold to a foreign buyer, or avenge his blood (Leviticus 25; Numbers 35 by
  reference, *not yet ingested*). **Its first use is Jacob's**: *"the angel who has redeemed me from all evil"* (Genesis
  48:16). **Its second is God's promise in Egypt**: *"I will redeem you with an outstretched arm, and with
  great judgments"* (Exodus 6:6). **Its third says the promise is kept**: *"the people that you have
  redeemed"* (15:13) (WLC lemma search).
- ***Pādâ*** — *ransom*, buying a life free by payment or substitute. **It first occurs in the law of the
  firstborn**: *"Every firstborn of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb … and you shall redeem all the
  firstborn of man among your sons"* (13:13; WLC lemma search; Genesis has none). See
  [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]].

**The Song of the Sea adds a third word beside them, *qānâ*, *acquire, buy***: *"until the people you have
purchased pass over"* (15:16). **In Genesis that verb is Joseph's, buying Egypt's people for Pharaoh** —
*"I have bought you and your land today for Pharaoh"* (Genesis 47:23). **The exodus reverses the transaction:
Israel passes out of Pharaoh's ownership into God's.** See [[The Song of the Sea]] and
[[Joseph and the Famine]].

**Sinai states rescue's destination as belonging to the rescuer.** Yahweh recalls carrying Israel
out of its former situation and bringing the people to himself (19:4); the covenant vocation
follows (19:5–6). No ransom price is described here. The eagle image presents deliverance as
care and transport, extending the sea's account of the redeemed people. See
[[Israel at Mount Sinai]] and [[On Eagles Wings - Exodus 19 and Deuteronomy 32]].

## Development across Scripture

### Poor brother and service — Leviticus 25:35–55

*Gāʾal* now applies to a person. A sold brother may be redeemed by brother, uncle, cousin, close
relative, or himself (48–49), at a price proportional to the years left to Jubilee (50–52). The
noun *gōʾēl* is absent from these verses; verb and *gəʾullâ* carry it (Miqra according to the
Masorah, Sefaria). The law states this only for an alien buyer.

See [[The Poor Brother and Redemption from Service]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 25.

### Land and house redemption — Leviticus 25:23–34

A nearest kinsman redeems land sold through poverty (25). A seller with recovered means
can settle with the purchaser by elapsed years (26–27); inability leaves Jubilee return
as the remedy (28). Walled-city houses have a one-year window, but Levite houses retain
redemption at any time (29–33). Redemption here is a concrete property right.

See [[Redemption of Land and Houses]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 25.

### Land rest and Jubilee — Leviticus 25:1–22

Jubilee returns each person to property and family (10, 13). This scheduled restoration must be distinguished from the purchase-back procedures beginning at 25:23–34, which remain the next pericope.

See [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 25:1–22; contextual verses as cited.


### Brought out to be your God — Leviticus 22:17–33

The priestly holiness laws end on the exodus: "who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be
your God" (33). Deliverance is stated as the purpose of belonging, as at Leviticus 11:45.

See [[Acceptable Victims and the Holy Name]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 22:17–33.



### Field and community — Leviticus 19:19–37

The slave woman has not been ransomed or freed (20); those absent changes of status matter to the
penalty. The man’s ram is an offering for his sin, not a payment said to purchase her freedom.

See [[Holy Living in Field and Community]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19.


### Clean and unclean animals — Leviticus 11:1–47

Leviticus 11:45 recalls Yahweh bringing Israel out of Egypt to be their God. Deliverance
grounds the demand for holiness and dietary distinction; no ransom payment or
source-language claim is implied by this thematic connection.

See [[Clean and Unclean Animals]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 11.

- **A personal confession first.** Jacob, blessing Joseph's sons, calls God *"the God who has fed me"* and
  *"the angel who has redeemed me from all evil"* (Genesis 48:15–16) — **redemption as one man's life,
  looked back on.** See [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]].
- **Then a promise to a people.** *"I will bring you out … I will rid you … I will redeem you … I will take
  you to me for a people"* (Exodus 6:6–7) — **redemption as a family duty God takes on for Israel.** See
  [[I Am Yahweh]].
- **Then a law for every household.** The firstborn of man and donkey *must* be redeemed (*pādâ*), and the
  father tells his son why: *"Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt … but all the firstborn of
  my sons I redeem"* (13:15). **Each family re-enacts the exodus's price.**
- **Then praise.** *"You, in your loving kindness, have led the people that you have redeemed"* (15:13);
  *"the people you have purchased"* (15:16). **Moses' second song uses *qānâ* again**: *"Isn't he your
  father who has bought you?"* (Deuteronomy 32:6; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).
- **Ahead** *(not yet ingested)*: the *gōʾēl* law of blood (Numbers
  35); Deuteronomy's *pādâ* for the exodus (7:8); Boaz; the prophets' *"your Redeemer"*; and Micah's
  *"redeemed you out of the house of bondage"* (6:4, WEB).

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:2

The opening of the commandments identifies God through a completed rescue from the house of bondage
(20:2). The commands therefore address people already brought out, rather than offering release as
wages for keeping this list. The speech also assumes continuing household servitude (20:10, 17);
Israel's liberation is not an abolition of slavery throughout its society. See
[[The Ten Commandments]].

### A female servant who must be redeemable — Exodus 21:8

*"He shall let her be redeemed"* (21:8). The verb is *pādâ* (WLC), the verb of the firstborn's
redemption (13:13, 15). **Redemption here is a legal right protecting a woman whose master is
displeased with her**, and he may not sell her abroad instead. See
[[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

### Violence, injury, and liability — Exodus 21:12–36

A bull owner warned of its dangerous habit who nevertheless fails to restrain it may be liable
to death when it kills; if a ransom is imposed, he must pay the amount laid on him (21:29–30).
This is a conditional legal provision, not a general permission to purchase release from murder.
Numbers 35:31 forbids ransom for a murderer and 35:32 forbids payment for an early departure from
the required refuge residence. These WEB comparisons were verified at `bible-api.com` on
2026-10-23. See [[A Place to Flee - Exodus 21 and Numbers 35]] and
[[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]].
See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]].

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30:1–38

At every census each man counted gives *kōper napšô*, *"a ransom for his soul"* (30:12, MAM), half a
shekel by the sanctuary standard. The same amount is required of rich and poor (30:15). The money is
called *"atonement money"* and is given to *"the service of the Tent of Meeting"* (30:16), where
Exodus 38:25–28 (local WEB) uses it for the tabernacle's silver sockets. See
[[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] and
[[The Half Shekel - Census, Ransom, and the Temple Tax]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

Exodus 34:20 repeats redemption of the firstborn donkey with a lamb and redemption of all
firstborn sons. The neck-breaking alternative belongs to the donkey only. No price for a son is
specified here. The command follows the claim of divine ownership in 34:19, so redemption
answers that claim rather than denying it. See [[Child Sacrifice and the Redemption of the Firstborn]]. See [[The Covenant Renewed]].

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38:1–31

The census payment prescribed as ransom in 30:12 is now counted at half a shekel for each of 603,550
men aged twenty and upward (38:25–26). Its material destination is recorded in 38:27–28. This is a
fixed payment, distinct from the willingness and variable gifts of 35:21–29.

See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### The ransom refused — Proverbs 6:35

Exodus 21:30 lets a negligent ox owner pay *kōp̄er* for a forfeited life, and Numbers 35:31 refuses
it for a murderer. Proverbs 6:35 shows *kōp̄er* offered privately and refused: the wronged husband
*"won't regard any ransom, neither will he rest content, though you give many gifts"*. The adulterer
has no price to pay. See [[The Lamp of the Commandment]],
[[No Ransom for the Adulterer - Proverbs 6.29-35, Leviticus 20.10, and Deuteronomy 22.22]], and
[[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 6;
Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### A ransom for the righteous — Proverbs 21:18

*"The wicked is a ransom for the righteous"*: a reversal of places like 11:8, where the wicked takes
the delivered righteous man's place. The saying names no payer, payee, or procedure, and the wiki
does not develop it into a theory of substitution. Its Hebrew wording has not been checked in this
pass. See [[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]].

See [[The King's Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 21; supporting comparisons are documented on
the passage page.

### The fatherless's gōʾēl — Proverbs 23:11

*"Don't encroach on the fields of the fatherless: for their Defender is strong"* (23:10–11). The WEB's
*Defender* renders *gōʾălām*, *their gōʾēl* (Sefaria, *Miqra according to the Masorah*, read
2026-10-26). The kinsman's duty to buy back a relative's land (Leviticus 25:25) becomes here God's
defence of land belonging to children with no kinsman to defend it. The verse adds the courtroom
role: the Redeemer *"will plead their case against you"*.

See [[Thirty Excellent Things]] and [[The Redeemer and the Avenger of Blood]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 22:17–23:11.
