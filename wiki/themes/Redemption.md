# Redemption

## Definition / overview

Getting back, by right or by price, what belongs to you and has passed out of your hands — a person, a
field, a life. **The Torah uses two verbs for it, and the wiki has now met both.** (Both checked in the WLC.)

- ***Gāʾal*** — the act of the ***gōʾēl***, the kinsman whose duty is to buy back family land, redeem a
  relative sold into slavery, or avenge his blood (Leviticus 25; Numbers 35; by reference, *not yet
  ingested*). **Its first use is Jacob's**: *"the angel who has redeemed me from all evil"* (Genesis
  48:16). **Its second is God's promise in Egypt**: *"I will redeem you with an outstretched arm, and with
  great judgments"* (Exodus 6:6). **Its third says the promise is kept**: *"the people that you have
  redeemed"* (15:13) (WLC lemma search).
- ***Pādâ*** — *ransom*, buying a life free by payment or substitute. **It first occurs in the law of the
  firstborn**: *"Every firstborn of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb … and you shall redeem all the
  firstborn of man among your sons"* (13:13; WLC lemma search; Genesis has none). See
  [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]].

**The Song of the Sea adds a third word beside them, *qānâ*, *acquire, buy***: *"until the people you have
purchased pass over"* (15:16). **In Genesis that verb is Joseph's, buying Egypt's people for Pharaoh** —
*"I have bought you and your land today for Pharaoh"* (Genesis 47:23). **The exodus reverses the transaction:
Israel passes out of Pharaoh's ownership into God's.** See [[The Song of the Sea]] and
[[Joseph and the Famine]].

**Sinai states rescue's destination as belonging to the rescuer.** Yahweh recalls carrying Israel
out of its former situation and bringing the people to himself (19:4); the covenant vocation
follows (19:5–6). No ransom price is described here. The eagle image presents deliverance as
care and transport, extending the sea's account of the redeemed people. See
[[Israel at Mount Sinai]] and [[On Eagles Wings - Exodus 19 and Deuteronomy 32]].

## Development across Scripture

- **A personal confession first.** Jacob, blessing Joseph's sons, calls God *"the God who has fed me"* and
  *"the angel who has redeemed me from all evil"* (Genesis 48:15–16) — **redemption as one man's life,
  looked back on.** See [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]].
- **Then a promise to a people.** *"I will bring you out … I will rid you … I will redeem you … I will take
  you to me for a people"* (Exodus 6:6–7) — **redemption as a family duty God takes on for Israel.** See
  [[I Am Yahweh]].
- **Then a law for every household.** The firstborn of man and donkey *must* be redeemed (*pādâ*), and the
  father tells his son why: *"Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt … but all the firstborn of
  my sons I redeem"* (13:15). **Each family re-enacts the exodus's price.**
- **Then praise.** *"You, in your loving kindness, have led the people that you have redeemed"* (15:13);
  *"the people you have purchased"* (15:16). **Moses' second song uses *qānâ* again**: *"Isn't he your
  father who has bought you?"* (Deuteronomy 32:6; WEB; WLC; *not yet ingested*).
- **Ahead** *(not yet ingested)*: the *gōʾēl* laws of land and slaves (Leviticus 25) and of blood (Numbers
  35); Deuteronomy's *pādâ* for the exodus (7:8); Boaz; the prophets' *"your Redeemer"*; and Micah's
  *"redeemed you out of the house of bondage"* (6:4, WEB).

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:2

The opening of the commandments identifies God through a completed rescue from the house of bondage
(20:2). The commands therefore address people already brought out, rather than offering release as
wages for keeping this list. The speech also assumes continuing household servitude (20:10, 17);
Israel's liberation is not an abolition of slavery throughout its society. See
[[The Ten Commandments]].

### A female servant who must be redeemable — Exodus 21:8

*"He shall let her be redeemed"* (21:8). The verb is *pādâ* (WLC), the verb of the firstborn's
redemption (13:13, 15). **Redemption here is a legal right protecting a woman whose master is
displeased with her**, and he may not sell her abroad instead. See
[[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

### Violence, injury, and liability — Exodus 21:12–36

A bull owner warned of its dangerous habit who nevertheless fails to restrain it may be liable
to death when it kills; if a ransom is imposed, he must pay the amount laid on him (21:29–30).
This is a conditional legal provision, not a general permission to purchase release from murder.
Numbers 35:31 forbids ransom for a murderer and 35:32 forbids payment for an early departure from
the required refuge residence. These WEB comparisons were verified at `bible-api.com` on
2026-10-23. See [[A Place to Flee - Exodus 21 and Numbers 35]] and
[[I Have Found a Ransom - Job 33.24, Psalm 49, and Mark 10.45]].
See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]].
