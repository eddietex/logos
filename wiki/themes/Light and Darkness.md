# Light and Darkness

## Definition / overview

**The Bible's first recorded act is the making of light, and its first recorded act of ordering is
the separation of light from darkness.** Everything the pair does afterwards runs off those two
verses. *"God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light. God saw the light, and saw that it was
good. God divided the light from the darkness"* (Genesis 1:3–4).

Three things in that opening set the terms, and all three are easy to get wrong.

**Darkness is there first, and it is not created.** Genesis 1:2 has darkness *"on the surface of the
deep"* before God speaks. The text does not say God made it; light is what gets made, and darkness is
what light is separated from. **The Hebrew Bible never treats darkness as a rival power**, but it
also never quite explains it, and the reticence is deliberate enough to be worth marking.

**Both are named, and the naming is the point.** *"God called the light 'day', and the darkness he
called 'night'"* (1:5). **Darkness is not abolished; it is given a name, a boundary, and a shift.**
This is the pattern for the whole canon: the Bible's characteristic move against darkness is not
destruction but limitation — until Revelation 21:25 and 22:5, where the limitation becomes
abolition and there is no night.

**Light is the Bible's most flexible metaphor and its least stable.** It stands for life (Job 3:20's
*"why is light given to him who is in misery"* means *why are the miserable kept alive*), for
knowledge, for favour (the face that shines on someone), for moral good, and for God himself.
Darkness carries the matching set, and adds two of its own: **ignorance**, and **the grave**. A
reader who fixes on one of these will misread the others, and Job is where the ambiguity is worked
hardest.

## Development across Scripture

**Genesis establishes the order and then uses darkness for judgment.** After 1:3–5, the pair goes
quiet for a long stretch, surfacing at the covenant between the pieces, where *"terror and great
darkness fell on"* Abram and, *"when the sun went down, and it was dark"*, a smoking furnace and a
flaming torch pass between the pieces (15:12, 17) — see [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] and
[[Smoking Furnace and Flaming Torch - Fire as Theophany]]. **Sodom's men are struck with blindness at
the door** (19:11), which is darkness used as a weapon against people already in the dark, in a
chapter where *"the sun had risen on the earth when Lot came to Zoar"* (19:23) and the destruction
follows — see [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]].

**Job is the Old Testament's densest treatment, and it is a hostile one.**

At **3:3–10** Job asks that one day be handed back to darkness, in the grammar of Genesis 1 and
against it: *"Let that day be darkness."* Five terms for darkness in three verses, and the created
order's opening word reversed. See [[Let the Day Perish]] and
[[Let That Day Be Darkness - Job 3 as Counter-Creation]].

At **3:20 and 3:23** the metaphor changes under the reader's feet. *"Why is light given to him who
is in misery, life to the bitter in soul… why is light given to a man whose way is hidden?"*
**Light here is not illumination but existence itself**, and the complaint is that it is being
issued to people who do not want it. The chapter that spends eight verses trying to extinguish a day
spends four more asking why the light is still being handed out.

At **10:21–22** Job describes where he is going as *"the land of darkness and of the shadow of
death"* — see [[Sheol]], where darkness is the realm's leading property.

At **18:5–6** [[Bildad the Shuhite]] turns the image into a doctrine: *"the light of the wicked shall
be put out… his lamp above him shall be put out."* **Darkness as a sentence, four chapters after Job
asked for it as a mercy**, and the same physical fact carrying opposite freight depending on who is
describing it. See [[Bildad's Second Speech]] and [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]].

At **19:8** Job says God *"has set darkness in my paths"* — the enclosure of 3:23 with the darkness
added to it; see [[The Hedge Around Him - Job 1.10 and Job 3.23]].

And at **38:2, 38:19 and 41:18** the whirlwind gives all of it back. God's first words accuse Job of
*darkening counsel*; then *"as for darkness, where is its place?"* treats darkness as a thing with an
address that Job cannot find; then the eyelids of the morning, which Job wanted a night never to see
(3:9), turn out to describe Leviathan's eyes. See [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]].

**The rest of the canon fills out both halves.** The ninth plague's darkness *"which may be felt"*;
the pillar of fire that is light to Israel and darkness to Egypt; *"Yahweh is my light and my
salvation"* (Psalm 27:1) and *"your word is a lamp to my feet"* (Psalm 119:105); the priestly
blessing's shining face; Isaiah 9:2's people walking in darkness who see a great light, and Isaiah
45:7, where God says he forms light and creates darkness — **the one verse that declines the
reticence of Genesis 1:2** and attributes both. Amos 5:18–20 makes the day of Yahweh *"darkness, and
not light"*, inverting the expectation.

**The New Testament makes the pair a moral and personal category.** John 1:5 has the light shining in
the darkness with the darkness not overcoming it, and John 8:12 has Jesus say *I am* the light;
1 John 1:5 states flatly that God is light and in him is no darkness. Paul's *"children of light"*
(1 Thessalonians 5:5) and *"the armor of light"* (Romans 13:12) move the pair into ethics. **And the
crucifixion is dark from the sixth hour to the ninth** (Matthew 27:45), which is the canon's last
major use of darkness as judgment before Revelation removes night from the city.

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:21

Moses approaches the thick darkness where God is (20:21). Darkness here is neither abandonment nor a
rival evil power; it conceals the divine presence that Moses approaches while the people stand back.
The contrast is one of access within the same encounter, not a struggle between light and darkness.
See [[The Ten Commandments]].
### The sunrise clause — Exodus 22:2–3

In the theft law, daylight changes a killing's legal status. A thief struck dead while breaking in
brings no guilt of bloodshed, but *"If the sun has risen on him, guilt of bloodshed shall be for
him"* (22:2–3). The text gives the sunrise and not the reason; the usual reading is that darkness
hides what an intruder intends. Matthew 24:43 later puts the thief *"in what watch of the night"*
the householder does not know. See [[Laws of Property and Restitution]] and
[[The Thief Breaking In - Exodus 22 and Matthew 24]].

**The lampstand's location limits what can be claimed about its light.** Exodus 26:35 places it
on the south side, opposite the table, outside the veil. It belongs to the outer holy room; the
chapter does not put a lamp beside the ark or specify the lighting conditions within the veil.
The spatial arrangement supports the lamp's practical function in 25:37 without supplying a new
symbolism for light. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].

### The altar, court, and maintained light — Exodus 27:1–21

The gold lampstand's light now receives a supply rule and named attendants (Exodus 27:20–21).
The WEB places *continually* beside *from evening to morning*: the service is enduring and
regular, while its expressly stated period is the night. These verses alone do not settle
whether a lamp also remained lit by day. They assign no symbolism to the oil. Leviticus 24:1–4
closely repeats the rule and expressly names the lamps on the pure gold lampstand. See
[[The Altar and the Court]] and [[From Evening to Morning - The Lamp in Exodus 27 and Leviticus 24]].

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

The skin of Moses' face shines because of his conversation with Yahweh (34:29). This is
neither the supplied light of the lampstand nor a general claim that every divine encounter
produces brightness. The text gives no color, shape of rays, or rate of fading. The later
mountain accounts compare readily, but their details differ; see
[[Shining on the Mountain - Moses and the Transfiguration]].

See [[The Shining Face of Moses]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

Exodus 35:8, 14, 28 connects the lamp's equipment and oil to the people's offerings. The rulers
bring oil among their contributions. This supplies the light commanded in 27:20–21; it does not
report the lamps already lit or assign a symbolic meaning to each lamp. The Sabbath fire rule
in 35:3 is stated separately, without an explicit treatment here of ongoing sanctuary service.

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The lampstand and its seven lamps are actually made in 37:17–24. It is one stand with six
branches and seven lamps, not seven separate stands. The almond-blossom forms are described
without a symbolic explanation. Their lighting is narrated later in 40:25; the manufacture
here should not be treated as an already operating lamp service.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

The cloud by day and fire in it by night close Exodus (40:38), recalling the guidance
after departure in 13:21–22. The lamps Moses lights inside the sanctuary (40:24–25) are
a distinct provision from the fire in the cloud visible to the people. The closing verse
does not describe the night fire as darkness inflicted on an enemy; its emphasis is
continuing public presence throughout the journeys.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

See [[Cloud and Fire - From the Sea to the Filled Tabernacle]] for the cloud’s continuing guidance and differing access.

### The ways of darkness — Proverbs 2:13

The perverse men *"forsake the paths of uprightness, to walk in the ways of darkness"* (2:13, *darḵê
ḥōšeḵ*). Darkness is a road here, not a condition, and Job's description of the realm of the dead —
*"the land of darkness and of the shadow of death"* (Job 10:21) — is where the chapter's other road,
the strange woman's, ends (2:18). See [[If You Seek Her as Silver]]. Source: Proverbs 2 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### The dawning path and the dark way — Proverbs 4:18–19

*"The path of the righteous is like the dawning light, that shines more and more until the
perfect day. The way of the wicked is like darkness. They don't know what they stumble over"*
(4:18–19). Light here is growth along a road; darkness is ignorance of one's own footing, the
consequence of 2:13's *"ways of darkness"*. Job 24:13–17 draws the same people as *"those who rebel
against the light"*, but without the fall; see
[[Rebels Against the Light - Proverbs 4 and Job 24]] and [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]].

See [[The Two Paths]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The commandment as a lamp — Proverbs 6:23

*"For the commandment is a lamp, and the law is light"* (6:23, *nēr miṣwâ wəṯôrâ ʾôr*) — the pair of
Psalm 119:105, *"Your word is a lamp to my feet, and a light for my path"*, in the same order. The
dawning path of 4:18 was light on the road; this is a lamp carried by the walker, and it is given
for one danger in the dark, the married woman of 6:24. See [[The Lamp of the Commandment]] and
[[A Lamp and a Light - Proverbs 6.23 and Psalm 119.105]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 6; Hebrew read
from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

Proverbs 7:9 sets the strange woman's scene in four darkening times — *"in the twilight, in the
evening of the day, in the middle of the night and in the darkness"* (*bəneš ep̄-bəʿereḇ yôm bəʾîšôn
laylâ waʾăp̄ēlâ*). It is the hour Job 24:15 gave the adulterer, who *"waits for the twilight"*
(*nešep̄*), and the darkness against which 6:23's lamp was given. See [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 7; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### The lamp snuffed out — Proverbs 13:9

*"The light of the righteous shines brightly, but the lamp of the wicked is snuffed out"* (9) — the proverb behind Job 18:5–6 and Job 21:17's *"How often"*. See [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]].

See [[Hope Deferred]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 13.

### Discernment and accountability — Proverbs 20:20, 27

The parent's curser loses his lamp in blackness (20), while the human spirit is Yahweh's lamp
searching the inward person (27). The first image represents ruin and the second disclosure.
Verse 20 does not expressly define the loss as absence of descendants; that possibility comes
from comparison with other lamp passages, not from the saying alone.

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 20; supporting comparisons are documented on the
passage page.

### The lamp of the wicked — Proverbs 21:4

Pride is called *"the lamp of the wicked"*, the lamp that 13:9 and 20:20 say will be put out. See
[[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]].

See [[The King's Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 21; supporting comparisons are documented on
the passage page.

  3:19–20's *"everyone who does evil hates the light"* in advance. Job himself, at 23:17, is in the
  *ḥōšek* and *ʾōpel* — cut off by it, or not silenced by it, depending on the reading.
- [[Bildad's Third Speech]] — Job 25:3, 5; *"On whom does his light not arise?"* and *"even the moon
  has no brightness"*. **Light as universal and as relative**, offered in reply to a speech about

  their victims (24:13–17). Compare Matthew 5:45, the sun rising on evil and good, used there as a
  reason for mercy.
- [[I Will Not Put Away My Integrity]] — Job 26:10; 27:20; *"He has described a boundary on the

- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:3, 11; ***"Man sets an end to darkness"*** and ***"The thing that is hidden he

  *"hidden from the eyes of all living"* (28:21)
- [[The Months of Old]] — Job 29:3, 24; ***"when his lamp shone on my head, and by his light I walked

- [[Now They Mock Me]] — Job 30:26, 28; ***"When I looked for good, then evil came; When I waited for

- [[Creation]] — where the pair begins, and what Job 3 attacks.
- [[Life and Death]] — because *light* in Job 3:20 and 3:23 simply means *alive*, and the whole
  chapter turns on that equivalence.

- [[Judgment]] — darkness as sentence, from Sodom's blindness to Amos 5:18–20.
