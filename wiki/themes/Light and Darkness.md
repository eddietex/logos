# Light and Darkness

## Definition / overview

**The Bible's first recorded act is the making of light, and its first recorded act of ordering is
the separation of light from darkness.** Everything the pair does afterwards runs off those two
verses. *"God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light. God saw the light, and saw that it was
good. God divided the light from the darkness"* (Genesis 1:3–4).

Three things in that opening set the terms, and all three are easy to get wrong.

**Darkness is there first, and it is not created.** Genesis 1:2 has darkness *"on the surface of the
deep"* before God speaks. The text does not say God made it; light is what gets made, and darkness is
what light is separated from. **The Hebrew Bible never treats darkness as a rival power**, but it
also never quite explains it, and the reticence is deliberate enough to be worth marking.

**Both are named, and the naming is the point.** *"God called the light 'day', and the darkness he
called 'night'"* (1:5). **Darkness is not abolished; it is given a name, a boundary, and a shift.**
This is the pattern for the whole canon: the Bible's characteristic move against darkness is not
destruction but limitation — until Revelation 21:25 and 22:5, where the limitation becomes
abolition and there is no night.

**Light is the Bible's most flexible metaphor and its least stable.** It stands for life (Job 3:20's
*"why is light given to him who is in misery"* means *why are the miserable kept alive*), for
knowledge, for favour (the face that shines on someone), for moral good, and for God himself.
Darkness carries the matching set, and adds two of its own: **ignorance**, and **the grave**. A
reader who fixes on one of these will misread the others, and Job is where the ambiguity is worked
hardest.

## Development across Scripture

**Genesis establishes the order and then uses darkness for judgment.** After 1:3–5, the pair goes
quiet for a long stretch, surfacing at the covenant between the pieces, where *"terror and great
darkness fell on"* Abram and, *"when the sun went down, and it was dark"*, a smoking furnace and a
flaming torch pass between the pieces (15:12, 17) — see [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] and
[[Smoking Furnace and Flaming Torch - Fire as Theophany]]. **Sodom's men are struck with blindness at
the door** (19:11), which is darkness used as a weapon against people already in the dark, in a
chapter where *"the sun had risen on the earth when Lot came to Zoar"* (19:23) and the destruction
follows — see [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]].

**Job is the Old Testament's densest treatment, and it is a hostile one.**

At **3:3–10** Job asks that one day be handed back to darkness, in the grammar of Genesis 1 and
against it: *"Let that day be darkness."* Five terms for darkness in three verses, and the created
order's opening word reversed. See [[Let the Day Perish]] and
[[Let That Day Be Darkness - Job 3 as Counter-Creation]].

At **3:20 and 3:23** the metaphor changes under the reader's feet. *"Why is light given to him who
is in misery, life to the bitter in soul… why is light given to a man whose way is hidden?"*
**Light here is not illumination but existence itself**, and the complaint is that it is being
issued to people who do not want it. The chapter that spends eight verses trying to extinguish a day
spends four more asking why the light is still being handed out.

At **10:21–22** Job describes where he is going as *"the land of darkness and of the shadow of
death"* — see [[Sheol]], where darkness is the realm's leading property.

At **18:5–6** [[Bildad the Shuhite]] turns the image into a doctrine: *"the light of the wicked shall
be put out… his lamp above him shall be put out."* **Darkness as a sentence, four chapters after Job
asked for it as a mercy**, and the same physical fact carrying opposite freight depending on who is
describing it. See [[Bildad's Second Speech]] and [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]].

At **19:8** Job says God *"has set darkness in my paths"* — the enclosure of 3:23 with the darkness
added to it; see [[The Hedge Around Him - Job 1.10 and Job 3.23]].

And at **38:2, 38:19 and 41:18** the whirlwind gives all of it back. God's first words accuse Job of
*darkening counsel*; then *"as for darkness, where is its place?"* treats darkness as a thing with an
address that Job cannot find; then the eyelids of the morning, which Job wanted a night never to see
(3:9), turn out to describe Leviathan's eyes. See [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]].

**The rest of the canon fills out both halves.** The ninth plague's darkness *"which may be felt"*;
the pillar of fire that is light to Israel and darkness to Egypt; *"Yahweh is my light and my
salvation"* (Psalm 27:1) and *"your word is a lamp to my feet"* (Psalm 119:105); the priestly
blessing's shining face; Isaiah 9:2's people walking in darkness who see a great light, and Isaiah
45:7, where God says he forms light and creates darkness — **the one verse that declines the
reticence of Genesis 1:2** and attributes both. Amos 5:18–20 makes the day of Yahweh *"darkness, and
not light"*, inverting the expectation.

**The New Testament makes the pair a moral and personal category.** John 1:5 has the light shining in
the darkness with the darkness not overcoming it, and John 8:12 has Jesus say *I am* the light;
1 John 1:5 states flatly that God is light and in him is no darkness. Paul's *"children of light"*
(1 Thessalonians 5:5) and *"the armor of light"* (Romans 13:12) move the pair into ethics. **And the
crucifixion is dark from the sixth hour to the ninth** (Matthew 27:45), which is the canon's last
major use of darkness as judgment before Revelation removes night from the city.

*(Outside Genesis and Job, the citations above are references rather than quotations — those books
are not in `raw/bible/`, and the wiki does not quote WEB text from memory. Job 3:20, 3:23, 10:21–22,
18:5–6, 19:8, 38:2, 38:19 and 41:18, and Genesis 1:3–4, 15:12, 19:11 and 19:23, are quoted from the
raw text.)*

## Key passages

- [[The Creation of the World]] — Genesis 1:3–5: light made, seen to be good, divided from darkness,
  and both halves named. **Everything else on this page is downstream of three verses.**
- [[Let the Day Perish]] — Job 3: the canon's most sustained attack on the first day's work, and the
  place where *light* starts meaning *life*.
- [[Let That Day Be Darkness - Job 3 as Counter-Creation]] — the correspondences set out in order.
- [[The Whirlwind Answers Chapter 3]] — 38:2's *darkens counsel* and 38:19's *where is its place*.
- [[Bildad's Second Speech]] — Job 18:5–6, darkness as the wicked man's sentence.
- [[The Lamp of the Wicked Put Out]] — the image's career from Job 18 through Proverbs.
- [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] — Job 19:8, darkness set in the paths.
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — Job 17:12–13, the friends changing night into
  day, and Job's couch spread in the darkness.
- [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] — Genesis 15:12, 17: the deep sleep, the horror of great
  darkness, and fire moving between the pieces after sundown.
- [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]] — Genesis 19:11, 23: blindness at the door, and the sun
  risen over Zoar.
- [[The Flood as Un-Creation]] — the other place Genesis 1's order is run backwards.
- [[In the Beginning - Genesis 1 and John 1]] — where the canon takes Genesis 1:3–5 in the opposite
  direction from Job 3.
