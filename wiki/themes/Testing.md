# Testing

## Definition / overview

**God putting a person in a situation whose outcome will show what was in them, and telling the
reader what he is doing while telling the person nothing.**

The Hebrew is ***nissâ*** (נִסָּה), to test, try, put to the proof — the same root as *nēs*, a
standard or banner, something raised so it can be seen. **Its first occurrence in Scripture is
Genesis 22:1**: *"After these things, God tested Abraham."* The verb is not about tempting toward
evil and not about punishment; it is about producing evidence.

**Three features hold across every biblical instance, and all three are set in Genesis 22.**

1. **The test is disclosed to the reader and withheld from the tested.** Genesis tells us in the
   first verse; Abraham is never told at all, not even after the angel speaks. Job's prologue does
   the same thing and Job never learns it either.
2. **The test is real — the outcome is treated as open.** *"For now I know that you fear God"*
   (22:12) is unintelligible if nothing was being found out. Whatever one makes of divine
   foreknowledge, the narrative grammar of a test requires a result.
3. **What the test measures is not belief but what a person does when the reasons have run out.**
   Abraham is given no argument, no justification he could offer anyone else, and three days to
   think about it.

**Testing is not temptation, and the Bible is careful about the difference.** *"Let no man say when
he is tempted, 'I am tempted by God,' for God can't be tempted by evil, and he himself tempts no
one"* (James 1:13) — in Greek the same verb *peirazō* does both jobs, and James separates them by
intent: a test is aimed at proving something sound, a temptation at making it fail. The Septuagint
uses *peirazō* at Genesis 22:1, which is why the distinction has to be made by context in the New
Testament and cannot be made by vocabulary.

**And it runs in both directions.** Israel tests God — *"Is Yahweh among us, or not?"* (Exodus 17:7),
at a place named **Massah**, from the same root — and that is always the forbidden direction:
*"You shall not tempt Yahweh your God"* (Deuteronomy 6:16), quoted by Jesus in the wilderness. **The
same verb is faith when God is the subject and unbelief when a human being is.**

## Development across Scripture

**Genesis 22 — the pattern case.** God tests Abraham by asking for the son the whole promise runs
through. The reader is told; the man is not. The test's object is named at its close: *"now I know
that you fear God"*. What it proves is not that Abraham believes a proposition but that he will act
against his own interest, against his own future, and without an explanation. See
[[The Binding of Isaac]].

**Job — the same structure, argued out.** The book of Job takes the mechanism of Genesis 22 and makes
it the subject. The reader gets a heavenly scene the sufferer never sees; the question is explicitly
about motive — *"Does Job fear God for nothing?"* (Job 1:9) — and the answer requires taking
everything away. **Job is the Akedah told from inside, over forty chapters instead of nineteen
verses, and with the man given his voice back.** The two share the phrase *fears God* and the reader's
unfair advantage. See [[Job of Uz]] and [[The Fear of God]].

**Exodus and the wilderness — testing as a forty-year instrument.** The manna is given *"that I may
test them, whether they will walk in my law or not"* (Exodus 16:4). The whole journey is retrospectively
described the same way: *"You shall remember all the way which Yahweh your God has led you these forty
years in the wilderness, that he might humble you, testing you, to know what was in your heart"*
(Deuteronomy 8:2). **Here the test is prolonged rather than acute, and its content is ordinary —
hunger, thirst, and instructions — rather than extreme.**

**Deuteronomy also legislates for a test that comes wearing the wrong clothes**: a prophet whose sign
comes true but who says *"let us go after other gods"* is *"Yahweh your God testing you, to know
whether you love Yahweh your God with all your heart"* (13:1–3). The category is broad enough to
include a false prophet succeeding.

**The prophets and the Psalms make it metallurgical.** *"Behold, I have refined you, but not as
silver. I have chosen you in the furnace of affliction"* (Isaiah 48:10); *"you, God, have tested us.
You have refined us, as silver is refined"* (Psalm 66:10); *"the crucible is for silver, and the
furnace for gold, but Yahweh tests the hearts"* (Proverbs 17:3). **The image shifts from a court's
proof to a smelter's**, and with it the emphasis moves from demonstrating what is there to removing
what is not.

**The New Testament keeps both halves.** Jesus in the wilderness is *led up by the Spirit to be
tempted* (Matthew 4:1) and answers the third temptation with Deuteronomy's rule against testing God.
James makes endurance the point — *"the testing of your faith produces endurance"* (1:3) — and then
draws the line at 1:13 quoted above. 1 Peter 1:6–7 returns to the furnace: faith *"more precious than
gold which perishes even though it is tested by fire"*. And Hebrews 11:17 uses the Genesis word of
Abraham outright: *"By faith, Abraham, being tested, offered up Isaac."*

**What the Bible never does is explain a test to the person inside it while it is running.** Job gets
a whirlwind and no answer; Abraham gets a ram and a promise; Israel gets forty years and a sermon
afterwards. **The explanation, when it comes, always comes to the reader.**

## Key passages

- [[The Binding of Isaac]] — Genesis 22:1–19; the first *nissâ* in Scripture, and the one every later
  instance is measured against
- [[Job of Uz]] — Job 1–2; the prologue that gives the reader the frame and the sufferer nothing
- [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] — Job 16–17; what being tested looks like from
  inside, with the cause invisible
- [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] — Job 19; the answer a tested man arrives at without being told
  anything
- **Genesis 22:12** — *"For now I know that you fear God"*: the stated object of the test
- **Exodus 15:25; 16:4; 17:2, 7; 20:20** — the wilderness tests, in both directions
- **Deuteronomy 8:2, 16; 13:1–3** — testing as pedagogy, and a false prophet as an instrument of it
- **Psalm 66:10; Proverbs 17:3; Isaiah 48:10; Zechariah 13:9; Malachi 3:2–3** — the furnace
- **Matthew 4:1–11; Luke 22:31–32** — Jesus tested, and Simon sifted like wheat
- **James 1:2–4, 12–15; 1 Peter 1:6–7; Hebrews 11:17** — endurance, the crown, and the line between
  test and temptation
