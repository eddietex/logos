# Sabbath

## Definition / overview

The seventh day, on which God rested from the work of creation, blessed the day, and made it
holy (Genesis 2:2–3). Everything the Bible later builds on Sabbath — commandment, sign, sabbath
year, jubilee, and the rest promised in Hebrews — is anchored to these two verses.

Four things the creation account establishes, before any law exists:

- **The seventh day is unlike the six.** It has no *"God said"*, no act of making, no *"evening
  and there was morning"*. The pattern that governed the account simply stops.
- **Rest is completion, not exhaustion.** *"On the seventh day God finished his work"* — the
  resting is itself the finishing. Creation is not complete at the sixth day with humanity in
  place; it is complete at the seventh with the day hallowed.
- **The first holy thing in the Bible is a time.** No place, object, person, or people is called
  holy before this day is.
- **It precedes Israel.** The seventh day is blessed at creation, for the world, long before
  Sinai gives a command about it.

The word *shabbat* itself does not appear here — only the verb *shabat*, "he rested" — but the
day being counted, blessed, and hallowed is what Exodus 20:11 will point back to.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 2:2–3** — God rests, blesses the seventh day, makes it holy.
- **Exodus 16:22–30** — the first *šabbāt* and the first *šabbātôn* in Scripture (WLC lemma search,
  Genesis–Deuteronomy), **given as a fact about bread before it is given as a command.** *"Tomorrow is a
  solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to Yahweh"* (16:23); *"Six days you shall gather it, but on the seventh day is
  the Sabbath. In it there shall be none"* (16:26); ***"Yahweh has given you the Sabbath"*** (16:29), with the
  verb of the bread *"which Yahweh has given you"* (16:15); and ***"So the people rested (wayyišbətû) on the
  seventh day"*** (16:30) — **the first time people are the subject of *šābat*** (WLC lemma search,
  Genesis–Exodus). **The first Sabbath-breakers are not punished; they find nothing** (16:27), and God asks
  *"How long do you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?"* (16:28). See [[Manna and Quail]].
- To trace as ingestion reaches them: Deuteronomy 5:12–15 (the same commandment
  grounded instead in the exodus from Egypt — two different rationales for one command, worth
  holding side by side), Leviticus 25 (sabbath year
  and jubilee), Isaiah 58:13–14, Mark 2:23–3:6 (*"The Sabbath was made for man"*), and
  Hebrews 3:7–4:11 (a sabbath rest still remaining).

**Before Sinai, the first rest in Israel's story is the one Pharaoh forbids.** When Moses asks for
three days to worship, Pharaoh hears a stoppage: *"you make them rest from their burdens"* —
*wəhišbattem*, the causative of *šābat* (Exodus 5:5). The Sabbath law will later make rest a
command and give it to slaves and animals by name (23:12; Deuteronomy 5:14–15). See
[[Bricks Without Straw]].

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:8–11

The Sabbath practiced around the manna becomes part of the public covenant speech (20:8–11). Rest is
required not only of the hearer but of sons, daughters, male and female servants, livestock, and the
stranger within the gates. The reason stated here is God's six-day creation and seventh-day rest,
blessing, and sanctification. Deuteronomy 5:14–15 retains the command and grounds it in remembered
bondage and shared rest; both rationales remain distinct in
[[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]]. See [[The Ten Commandments]].

### Six years and the seventh — Exodus 21:2

The servant *"shall serve six years and in the seventh he shall go out free"* (21:2). **The week of
20:9–10 becomes a measure of years**, and release takes the place of rest. The verse does not call
the seventh year a Sabbath, nor say whether it counts from the purchase or is common to all.
Leviticus 25 develops a sabbatical year for the land and the Jubilee separately; this page does not
merge them. See [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

### Rest for the field and refreshment for dependents — Exodus 23:1–19

The six-and-seven pattern now governs cultivation and the working week side by side (23:10–12). The
seventh year's produce feeds the poor, then the animals; the seventh day refreshes working animals,
the servant's son, and the alien. This social purpose complements the creation rationale in 20:11.
The cultivation cycle is not expressly synchronized with the individual servant's release in 21:2.
See [[The Seventh Year's Food - Exodus 23 and Leviticus 25]].

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The covenant sign and its sanction — Exodus 31

Exodus 31:13–17 calls Sabbath the sign between Yahweh and Israel, the people whom he sanctifies.
Israel is to observe it as a perpetual covenant. Verses 14–15 prescribe death for profanation and
work; verse 14 also says the worker is cut off. The relation between these formulations is not
explained. This is stronger than the unpunished failure to find manna in 16:27–30, and is
legislation rather than a narrated execution.

**A tension with this page's opening synthesis must remain visible:** the earlier phrase “rest is
completion, not exhaustion” interprets Genesis 2:2–3, but Exodus 31:17 actually adds *“and was
refreshed.”* Isaiah 40:28 says the Creator *“isn’t weary”* (WEB verified from bible-api.com,
2026-10-24). Reading the refreshment as human language applied to God is a theological
interpretation; neither wording should be erased. See
[[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]] and
[[The Covenant Signs - Rainbow, Circumcision, and Sabbath]].

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

## Key passages

- [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]] — Exodus 31:1–18; the covenant sign and its sanction.

- [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]] — Exodus 23:1–19; rest for the field and refreshment for dependents.

- [[The Creation of the World]]
- [[The Waters Recede]] — the flood's one surviving human rhythm: *"He waited yet another seven
  days"*, twice (8:10, 8:12), a man measuring a new world by the week

- [[Let the Day Perish]] — Job 3:1–26, obliquely and by way of one word. ***Nûaḥ*, the verb under
  the Sabbath command and under Noah's name, occurs three times in the chapter and every one of them
  is in the grave**: *"I should have slept, then I would have been at rest"* (3:13), *"there the
  weary are at rest"* (3:17), and then the last line of the speech, *"neither have I rest"* (3:26).
  **The chapter opens a place where rest exists, spends seven verses furnishing it, and closes by
  saying he is not in it** — which is the Sabbath's promise stated as its absence
- [[Bricks Without Straw]] — Exodus 5:5; ***"you make them rest"*** — ***wəhišbattem***, the causative
  of *šābat*. **The first time in canonical order that *šābat* is used of people being made to rest**
  — before this only of God (Genesis 2:2–3) and of day and night (8:22) — **and the speaker is
  Pharaoh, making it an accusation.** The rest Israel is denied in Egypt is commanded in the
  wilderness (16:30) and grounded in mercy to servants (23:12). Checked in the WLC of Genesis, Exodus,
  and Job
- [[The Passover]] — Exodus 12:15–16; **a week appointed to be kept, with work forbidden on its first
  and last days** — the first such days since Genesis 2:2–3. The Sabbath itself is not named in Exodus
  until the manna (16:23); the penalty *"that soul shall be cut off"* will be used of the
  Sabbath-breaker at 31:14
- [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]] — Exodus 13:3, 6; ***"Remember this day"*** — *zākôr*, **the same command
  form as *"Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy"*** (20:8), and its first use (WLC lemma search). The
  feast is seven days with the seventh *"a feast to Yahweh"*, and its law opens with the first *qaddeš*
  since God made the seventh day holy (Genesis 2:3)
- [[Manna and Quail]] — Exodus 16:22–30; **the first *Sabbath* by name.** *"A solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to
  Yahweh"*; *"Yahweh has given you the Sabbath"*; *"Everyone stay in his place"*; *"So the people rested on the
  seventh day"* — **a gift before a command**, and the first time people are said to rest

- [[The Ten Commandments]] — Exodus 20:8–11; creation-grounded rest required for the whole household
- [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]] — Exodus 21:2; six years of service and release in the seventh
