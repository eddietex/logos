# Sabbath

## Definition / overview

The seventh day, on which God rested from the work of creation, blessed the day, and made it
holy (Genesis 2:2–3). Everything the Bible later builds on Sabbath — commandment, sign, sabbath
year, jubilee, and the rest promised in Hebrews — is anchored to these two verses.

Four things the creation account establishes, before any law exists:

- **The seventh day is unlike the six.** It has no *"God said"*, no act of making, no *"evening
  and there was morning"*. The pattern that governed the account simply stops.
- **Rest is completion, not exhaustion.** *"On the seventh day God finished his work"* — the
  resting is itself the finishing. Creation is not complete at the sixth day with humanity in
  place; it is complete at the seventh with the day hallowed.
- **The first holy thing in the Bible is a time.** No place, object, person, or people is called
  holy before this day is.
- **It precedes Israel.** The seventh day is blessed at creation, for the world, long before
  Sinai gives a command about it.

The word *shabbat* itself does not appear here — only the verb *shabat*, "he rested" — but the
day being counted, blessed, and hallowed is what Exodus 20:11 will point back to.

## Development across Scripture

### Covenant warnings and remembered promises — Leviticus 26:14–46

The land's Sabbaths return as a debt. The land *will enjoy its Sabbaths* while desolate (34–35, 43),
*the rest which it didn't have in your Sabbaths*. 2 Chronicles 36:21 reports this in the same words.
See [[The Land Will Enjoy Its Sabbaths - Leviticus 26.34-35, 43 and 2 Chronicles 36.21]].

See [[Covenant Warnings and Remembered Promises]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 26.

### Blessings for obedience — Leviticus 26:1–13

Verse 2 repeats 19:30 word for word in the Hebrew (MAM). Placed before the promises, *my Sabbaths* will
return as the land's Sabbaths in the warnings (26:34–35, 43).

See [[Blessings for Obedience]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 26.

### Land rest and Jubilee — Leviticus 25:1–22

The six-and-seven pattern governs years of cultivation; seven such cycles lead to a holy fiftieth year (1–12). Land rest permits eating its produce (6–7), so it cannot be summarized as a prohibition of all access to food.

See [[The Sabbatical Year and the Jubilee]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 25:1–22; contextual verses as cited.


### Lamps and weekly bread — Leviticus 24:1–9

Bread is arranged before Yahweh every Sabbath (8). This stands beside the prohibition
of work in 23:3: sanctuary service is commanded on the rest day. Any synthesis making
Sabbath an end to every activity must retain both instructions rather than suppress
the explicit weekly duty.

See [[The Lamps and the Bread Before Yahweh]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 24:1–9.

### Booths and remembered deliverance — Leviticus 23:33–44

The first and eighth days are solemn rests (39), with regular work forbidden
(35–36). The calendar summary distinguishes the appointed offerings from Yahweh’s
Sabbaths (37–38); annual rest does not cancel the weekly rhythm.

See [[The Feast of Booths]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:33–44; comparisons as cited.

### Trumpets and atonement — Leviticus 23:23–32

The tenth day of the seventh month is a Sabbath of solemn rest observed from the
ninth evening to the following evening (27, 32). The evening boundary explains the
observance of the tenth day, rather than contradicting its stated date.

See [[Trumpets and the Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:23–32; contextual references as cited.

### First fruits and harvest provision — Leviticus 23:9–22

Seven Sabbaths structure the fifty-day count from the day after the Sabbath (11, 15–16).
The resulting assembly prohibits regular work (21). The precise starting Sabbath is
not independently identified here; this page assigns no fixed festival date.

See [[First Fruits and the Feast of Weeks]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:9–22; contextual references as cited.


### Sabbath and the opening feasts — Leviticus 23:1–8

The weekly seventh day is both solemn rest and a holy convocation throughout the dwellings
(3). The annual calendar follows a repeated introduction (4). The WEB distinguishes the
Sabbath’s prohibition of any kind of work from the festival days’ prohibition of regular work
(7–8); the unit gives no new creation rationale.

See [[Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:1–8; Exodus 12:16 where cited.


### Field and community — Leviticus 19:19–37

Keeping Yahweh’s Sabbaths is paired with reverence for his sanctuary (30). Sacred time and sacred
space remain part of the same community instruction as care for elders and foreigners.

See [[Holy Living in Field and Community]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19.


### Holiness and the neighbor — Leviticus 19:1–18

Keeping Yahweh’s Sabbaths stands beside respect for mother and father (3). The address to the
whole congregation brings sacred time into the same instruction as everyday treatment of
neighbors.

See [[Be Holy and Love Your Neighbor]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19:1–18.


- **Genesis 2:2–3** — God rests, blesses the seventh day, makes it holy.
- **Exodus 16:22–30** — the first *šabbāt* and the first *šabbātôn* in Scripture (WLC lemma search,
  Genesis–Deuteronomy), **given as a fact about bread before it is given as a command.** *"Tomorrow is a
  solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to Yahweh"* (16:23); *"Six days you shall gather it, but on the seventh day is
  the Sabbath. In it there shall be none"* (16:26); ***"Yahweh has given you the Sabbath"*** (16:29), with the
  verb of the bread *"which Yahweh has given you"* (16:15); and ***"So the people rested (wayyišbətû) on the
  seventh day"*** (16:30) — **the first time people are the subject of *šābat*** (WLC lemma search,
  Genesis–Exodus). **The first Sabbath-breakers are not punished; they find nothing** (16:27), and God asks
  *"How long do you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?"* (16:28). See [[Manna and Quail]].
- To trace as ingestion reaches them: Deuteronomy 5:12–15 (the same commandment
  grounded instead in the exodus from Egypt — two different rationales for one command, worth
  holding side by side), Isaiah 58:13–14, Mark 2:23–3:6 (*"The Sabbath was made for man"*), and
  Hebrews 3:7–4:11 (a sabbath rest still remaining).

**Before Sinai, the first rest in Israel's story is the one Pharaoh forbids.** When Moses asks for
three days to worship, Pharaoh hears a stoppage: *"you make them rest from their burdens"* —
*wəhišbattem*, the causative of *šābat* (Exodus 5:5). The Sabbath law will later make rest a
command and give it to slaves and animals by name (23:12; Deuteronomy 5:14–15). See
[[Bricks Without Straw]].

### The commandments at Sinai — Exodus 20:8–11

The Sabbath practiced around the manna becomes part of the public covenant speech (20:8–11). Rest is
required not only of the hearer but of sons, daughters, male and female servants, livestock, and the
stranger within the gates. The reason stated here is God's six-day creation and seventh-day rest,
blessing, and sanctification. Deuteronomy 5:14–15 retains the command and grounds it in remembered
bondage and shared rest; both rationales remain distinct in
[[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]]. See [[The Ten Commandments]].

### Six years and the seventh — Exodus 21:2

The servant *"shall serve six years and in the seventh he shall go out free"* (21:2). **The week of
20:9–10 becomes a measure of years**, and release takes the place of rest. The verse does not call
the seventh year a Sabbath, nor say whether it counts from the purchase or is common to all.
Leviticus 25 develops a sabbatical year for the land and the Jubilee separately; this page does not
merge them. See [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

### Rest for the field and refreshment for dependents — Exodus 23:1–19

The six-and-seven pattern now governs cultivation and the working week side by side (23:10–12). The
seventh year's produce feeds the poor, then the animals; the seventh day refreshes working animals,
the servant's son, and the alien. This social purpose complements the creation rationale in 20:11.
The cultivation cycle is not expressly synchronized with the individual servant's release in 21:2.
See [[The Seventh Year's Food - Exodus 23 and Leviticus 25]].

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The covenant sign and its sanction — Exodus 31

Exodus 31:13–17 calls Sabbath the sign between Yahweh and Israel, the people whom he sanctifies.
Israel is to observe it as a perpetual covenant. Verses 14–15 prescribe death for profanation and
work; verse 14 also says the worker is cut off. The relation between these formulations is not
explained. This is stronger than the unpunished failure to find manna in 16:27–30, and is
legislation rather than a narrated execution.

**A tension with this page's opening synthesis must remain visible:** the earlier phrase “rest is
completion, not exhaustion” interprets Genesis 2:2–3, but Exodus 31:17 actually adds *“and was
refreshed.”* Isaiah 40:28 says the Creator *“isn’t weary”* (WEB verified from bible-api.com,
2026-10-24). Reading the refreshment as human language applied to God is a theological
interpretation; neither wording should be erased. See
[[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]] and
[[The Covenant Signs - Rainbow, Circumcision, and Sabbath]].

See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

The six-day work pattern and seventh-day rest are repeated with an explicit seasonal limit:
plowing and harvest do not excuse continued work (34:21). This applies the command to
agricultural urgency. Unlike 20:11 and 31:17, this verse does not restate a creation rationale;
the omission should not be filled with an invented quotation. See [[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]]. See [[The Covenant Renewed]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

Moses places the Sabbath command before the sanctuary appeal (35:1–3). The six-day pattern and
death sanction repeat 31:15, while 35:3 expressly forbids kindling fire throughout the people's
habitations. Sacred work receives no stated exemption. The wording is broader than a ban only on
craft furnaces; the passage does not give a detailed account of existing fires or exceptional cases.
Exodus 16:23's preparation of food beforehand is a useful earlier comparison, not an explanation
explicitly attached to the fire rule. See [[Creation as the Ground of the Sabbath Command]].

See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The completed-work and blessing report (39:32–43) can be read beside Genesis 2:1–3,
where God finishes, rests, and blesses the seventh day. Exodus’s construction sequence began
under the Sabbath rule (35:1–3), but 39:32–43 supplies neither a day count nor a new report
of Sabbath observance. The comparison must not invent a six-day manufacturing schedule.
See [[Finished Work, Seeing, and Blessing - Creation and the Tabernacle]].

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### A Sabbath of solemn rest — Leviticus 16:1–34

The Day of Atonement is called *"a Sabbath of solemn rest"* (31) on the tenth day of the
seventh month — an annual date, not the seventh day of the week. No work is done, by native-born or resident
foreigner (29). See [[The Day of Atonement Commanded Twice - Leviticus 16 and 23]].

See [[The Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 16; comparisons as cited.
