# War

## Definition / overview

Organized violence between peoples — which the Bible introduces late, reports without praising, and
then never stops narrating.

The word *milḥāmâ* first occurs at Genesis 14:2: *"they made war with Bera, king of Sodom."* Ten
chapters of Genesis have passed with a murder (4:8), a boast about killing (4:23–24), a world
*"filled with violence"* (6:11), and a *gibbōr* who founds kingdoms (10:8–10) — but no armies until
here. When war arrives it is between nine foreign kings over twelve years of unpaid tribute, and the
man the book is about is not in it. He joins only when it takes a member of his household. See
[[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

**Genesis is conspicuously restrained about it.** The battle of the four kings against the five gets
one verse (14:10). Abram's night attack gets one clause: *"He divided himself against them by night,
he and his servants, and struck them"* (14:15). The chapter then spends eight verses on what he does
with the proceeds. Nothing is said about whether he was right to go, whether God approved, or how
many died — and God does not speak in the chapter at all. The evaluation the reader wants is exactly
what is withheld, which is Genesis's habit with its patriarchs.

## Development across Scripture

- **The one war in Genesis Abram fights is a rescue, and he keeps nothing from it.** No territory,
  no plunder, no captives, no vassals. Set against the imperial reprisal that opens the chapter —
  tribute, rebellion, punitive campaign — the contrast is the chapter's political argument: he beats
  four kings and refuses to become one. See [[Kingship and Empire]] and
  [[Not a Thread nor a Sandal Strap]].
- **The other Genesis violence is a massacre, and it is condemned.** Simeon and Levi at Shechem
  (34:25–30), which Jacob calls a thing that has *"troubled me, to make me odious to the inhabitants
  of the land"* and curses on his deathbed: *"Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce"* (49:5–7).
  Genesis has one war and one atrocity, and grades only the second.
- **At the Exodus the fighting is God's and Israel stands still.** *"Yahweh will fight for you, and
  you shall be still"* (Exodus 14:14); *"Yahweh is a man of war"* (15:3). The conquest then makes
  Israel the instrument, under the *ḥērem* — the devoting of a city to destruction (Deuteronomy
  7:1–5; 20:16–18; Joshua 6–11) — which is the hardest material in the Old Testament and which
  Deuteronomy itself justifies not by Israel's righteousness but by the Amorites' iniquity
  (Deuteronomy 9:4–5; compare Genesis 15:16). The wiki records that this is contested ground and
  does not resolve it.
- **Even the conquest laws are bounded.** Deuteronomy 20 requires an offer of terms first, exempts
  the newly married, the new householder, the planter of a vineyard, and the afraid, and forbids
  cutting down fruit trees for siege works — *"for is the tree of the field man, that it should be
  besieged by you?"* (20:19).
- **The kings are told not to trust it.** *"A king is not saved by the multitude of an army"*
  (Psalm 33:16); *"Some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we trust in the name of Yahweh our
  God"* (Psalm 20:7); the law's king may not multiply horses (Deuteronomy 17:16). David, the Bible's
  most successful soldier, is refused the temple for exactly this: *"You have shed blood abundantly,
  and have made great wars"* (1 Chronicles 22:8).
- **The prophets make the end of war the shape of the future.** *"They shall beat their swords into
  plowshares … neither shall they learn war any more"* (Isaiah 2:4; Micah 4:3); the bow cut off and
  peace spoken to the nations (Zechariah 9:10); *"Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit"*
  (Zechariah 4:6).
- **And the New Testament reassigns the weapons.** *"All those who take the sword will die by the
  sword"* (Matthew 26:52); *"our wrestling is not against flesh and blood"* (Ephesians 6:12); *"the
  weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh"* (2 Corinthians 10:4). Revelation keeps the imagery of
  battle and gives the rider a sword that comes out of his mouth (19:15, 21).
- **In Exodus Israel's first war comes before its law, and it is fought with a raised rod.** At the sea God
  fought and Israel was *"still"* (14:14); at Rephidim Israel fights and the rod decides it (17:8–13). The war is
  then made permanent and written down — *"from generation to generation"* (17:16) — and the next time *milḥāmâ*
  is heard in Exodus it is Joshua mistaking the calf's festival for *"the noise of war in the camp"* (32:17; WLC;
  *not yet ingested*). See [[The Battle with Amalek]].

### God fights ahead of Israel — Exodus 23:20–33

The conquest is promised almost entirely as God's work: *"I will be an enemy to your enemies"*
(23:22), his terror sent ahead, the peoples confused (*wəhammōtî*, the panic verb of 14:24; MAM),
their backs turned, and the hornet driving them out (23:27–28). Israel's own part is named at the end
of 23:31: *"you shall drive them out before you."* The pace is set for the land's sake — *"little by
little"*, lest it become desolate (23:29–30). Joshua 24:12 later looks back on the hornet *"not with
your sword, nor with your bow"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*). See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]] and
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].

## Key passages

- Genesis 14:1–17 — the first war, [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]
- [[The Treaty at Beersheba]] — Genesis 21:22, 32, the theme's negative case. **Phicol, *the captain
  of his army*, is named twice and does nothing**; he is the standing reminder of what the king could
  do instead, and the entire function of his rank in the scene is that it is not used. A seized well,
  a formal complaint, and a general in the room — settled with sheep, seven lambs, and an oath
- [[Dinah and the Massacre at Shechem]] — Genesis 34:25–30; **the first time Israelites take a city,
  four centuries before the conquest.** It is fought by two men, not an army; the weapon is a treaty
  and a covenant rite rather than a siege; the city is taken ***beṭaḥ***, *unsuspecting*, in the state
  the treaty produced; and the spoil is inventoried in the vocabulary the conquest narratives will use
  — flocks, herds, donkeys, wealth, wives and little ones (34:28–29). **Genesis 34 also contains the
  Bible's first taking of captives.** Against Genesis 14, where Abram fights a coalition to recover a
  kinsman and refuses every thread of the spoil, **this is a raid that keeps everything**, and the
  book's verdict on it comes from Jacob's deathbed at 49:5–7: *"their swords are weapons of
  violence"*. See [[Simeon and Levi - The Sword at Shechem and the Deathbed Curse]]
- [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]] — Exodus 6:26; ***ʿal-ṣibʾōtām***, *"according to their armies"* — **the first time
  Israel is called *ṣəbāʾôt***. Genesis used the word for the host of heaven and earth (2:1) and for
  Phicol's army (21:22); from here Exodus uses it of Israel (7:4; 12:17, 41, 51). **The slaves of
  chapter 1 are listed as a muster**, before they have left Egypt or fought anyone
- Exodus 14:14; 15:3 — Yahweh fights
- Deuteronomy 20:1–20 — the laws of war, with their exemptions
- Deuteronomy 7:1–5; Joshua 6:17–21 — the *ḥērem*
- 1 Samuel 30:1–25 — David's rescue raid and his rule about the spoil
- Psalm 20:7; 33:16–17; 1 Chronicles 22:8 — the distrust of arms
- Isaiah 2:4; Micah 4:3; Zechariah 9:10 — the swords beaten down
- Matthew 26:52; 2 Corinthians 10:3–5; Ephesians 6:10–17 — the weapons reassigned
- [[Jacob Blesses His Sons]] — Genesis 49:5–27; **six of the twelve sayings are military, and the one
  that is not is about being conquered.** Swords called *weapons of violence* (49:5), a lion from the
  prey (49:9), a serpent that drops a rider (49:17), a raiding troop raided back (49:19), a bow that
  held against archers (49:23–24), and **a wolf dividing plunder** (49:27) — **against [[Issachar]]
  under forced labour** (49:15). **Genesis narrates none of it**: there is no battle of Jacob's, no
  archer in [[Joseph]]'s story, and the one massacre the book does narrate (34:25–29) is the thing
  49:5–7 curses. **The chapter's vocabulary is the conquest's, spoken in Egypt before any of it**
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:22–23; 39:19–25; **weapons that belong to God.** The hail kept
  *"against the day of battle and war"* (38:23), as at Joshua 10:11; and the war-horse that *"mocks at
  fear"* and smells the battle afar off (39:22, 25) — the army's animal, whose courage God asks whether
  Job gave.
- [[Behemoth and Leviathan]] — Job 41:8, 25–29; **the whole armoury useless.** *"Remember the battle, and do so
  no more"* (41:8); sword, spear, dart, pointed shaft, arrow, sling-stone, club and javelin listed and
  dismissed — *"He counts iron as straw; and brass as rotten wood"* (41:27), *"He laughs at the rushing of the
  javelin"* (41:29).
- [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]] — Exodus 7:4; ***ṣibʾōtay ʾet-ʿammî***, *"my armies, my
  people"* — **the first time God calls Israel *my armies***, before a single Israelite has borne arms.
  In Exodus *ṣābāʾ* occurs only at 6:26; 7:4; 12:17, 41, 51 (WLC)
- [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]] — Exodus 12:37, 41, 51; **a slave column counted and named as an army.** *"About six
  hundred thousand on foot"* — *raglî*, the foot-soldier's word, in the Torah only here and Numbers 11:21
  (WLC) — and *"all of Yahweh's armies went out"*, *ṣibʾôt YHWH*: the first time the narrator calls Israel
  Yahweh's armies (7:4 was God's *"my armies"*). **Israel carries plunder and dough, and fights no one**
- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 13:17; 14:14, 24–25; **the first war Yahweh fights.** God avoids the
  Philistine road *"Lest perhaps the people change their minds when they see war"* (13:17) — *milḥāmâ*,
  Pharaoh's word at 1:10 — and Moses says ***"Yahweh will fight for you, and you shall be still"*** (14:14),
  the first time Yahweh is the subject of *lāḥam* (WLC lemma search, Torah). **Israel does not fight**; the
  Egyptians confess *"Yahweh fights for them"* (14:25). The panic sent on the army, *hāmam* (14:24), is the
  verb of 23:27's promise, *"I will … confuse all the people to whom you come"* (WLC). Deuteronomy builds on
  14:14: *"he will fight for you, according to all that he did for you in Egypt"* (1:30, WEB; *not yet
  ingested*)
- [[The Song of the Sea]] — Exodus 15:3, 9; **the warrior named.** *"Yahweh is a man of war. Yahweh is his name"* — the
  *milḥāmâ* of Pharaoh's fear (1:10) and of the road God avoided (13:17) becomes a title. The enemy's six
  first-person verbs — *"I will pursue. I will overtake. I will divide the plunder … I will draw my sword"*
  (15:9) — are answered by one breath (15:10).
- [[The Battle with Amalek]] — Exodus 17:8–16; **the first war Israel fights.** *"Then Amalek came and fought
  with Israel"* — the first human enemy to *lāḥam* against Israel (WLC). Where the sea had *"Yahweh will fight for
  you, and you shall be still"* (14:14), Rephidim has *"Choose men for us, and go out, fight"* (17:9); the outcome
  follows *"God's rod"* in Moses' raised hand, and Joshua wins *"with the edge of the sword"*, the phrase of the
  massacre at Shechem (Genesis 34:26; WLC). **The war is not ended but declared permanent**: *"Yahweh will have
  war (milḥāmâ) with Amalek from generation to generation"* (17:16). The road God chose so Israel would not
  *"see war"* (13:17) did not avoid this one
- [[The Angel Sent Ahead]] — Exodus 23:20–33; God's terror, confusion, and hornet; a conquest *"little by little"*.
