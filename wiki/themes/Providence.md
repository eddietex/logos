# Providence

## Definition / overview

**Providence is Genesis's claim that God is running events he is not visibly present in.**

The book has two modes for divine action and they do not overlap. In the first, **God appears**: he
walks in a garden, shuts a door, speaks from a fire, wrestles a man at a ford, stops a knife. In the
second — **which is nearly the whole of the Joseph cycle** — **God does nothing on the page at all**,
and the narrative nonetheless arrives exactly where the promises said it would.

**The technical mark of providence in Genesis is therefore an absence, not a presence.** **Between
39:23 and 46:1 the narrator does not attribute a single event to God** — six chapters covering the
prison, the dreams, the rise, the famine, and the whole of the brothers' return. Across that stretch
every theological statement is **in a character's mouth**, and the characters are variously right,
wrong, self-serving and terrified. **The reader is left to do the attributing, which is the point.**
(The narration is not silent for the whole cycle: *"Yahweh killed him"* at 38:7 and 38:10, *"Yahweh
was with Joseph"* five times in chapter 39, and *"God spoke to Israel in the visions of the night"* at
46:2 are all the narrator's own. **They bracket the silence rather than break it.**) See
[[The Names of God]] for the parallel observation about which name is used where.

**Providence is also, in Genesis, almost always invisible to the person inside it.** That is what
distinguishes it from [[Blessing]], which is announced, and from [[Judgment]], which is explained. The
book's defining providence text is a man saying **the exact opposite of the truth** while surrounded by
evidence of it.

## Development across Scripture

### Before Joseph: providence with the machinery showing

Genesis's earlier books are not shy about the mechanism.

- **[[Abraham's Servant]] at the well** (24:12–27) — **the clearest providence scene in the book with
  God still openly credited.** The servant prays for a sign, gets it while he is still speaking, and
  says *"Yahweh has led me on the way"* (24:27). **The narrator does not contradict him.**
- **[[Joseph Sold into Slavery]]'s unnamed man** (37:15–17) — *"a certain man found him, and behold, he
  was wandering in the field"* — **three verses of a stranger who redirects Joseph to Dothan and is
  never identified.** Without him Joseph goes home. **Genesis puts the hinge of the entire book in the
  hands of a man with no name and no explanation.**
- **[[Joseph in Potiphar's House]]** (39:2, 3, 5, 21, 23) — **the last five times the narrator says
  *Yahweh* anywhere in Genesis.** *"Yahweh was with Joseph"* frames a chapter in which Joseph is enslaved,
  propositioned, falsely accused and imprisoned. **The formula is attached to the descent, not to a
  rescue.**

### Genesis 42 — the theme's hardest case

**The chapter is the Bible's most sustained portrait of providence misread, and it is misread in both
directions at once.**

> ***"What is this that God has done to us?"*** (42:28)

**The brothers' first mention of God in the entire cycle**, and it is about a bag of returned money.
**They read a kindness as a judgment.** Their brother has refused to take payment from his own family
and they experience it as a trap — which, given that they are foreign nationals accused of espionage,
is a reasonable reading of the facts and completely wrong about the cause.

> ***"All these things are against me."*** (42:36)

**[[Jacob]]'s verdict, and every clause of the evidence he is reciting is mercy.** Joseph is alive and
is the second man in Egypt; Simeon is fed and held by his own brother; the grain in the room came from
the son he is mourning. **Genesis prints the sentence and does not correct it for five chapters.**

**This is the theme's whole method.** The narrator will not tell the reader what is happening; he lets
a grieving man say the most false sentence in Genesis and moves on. See
[[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]].

### Genesis 45 — the doctrine stated aloud, by a character

**[[Joseph Reveals Himself]] is where the cycle finally says what it has been doing, and the manner of
the saying is as important as the content.**

**Genesis 45 names God four times in twenty-eight verses and every one of them is in Joseph's mouth**
(45:5, 7, 8, 9). **The narrator still says nothing** — the silence that began at 39:23 runs unbroken
through the disclosure, the reunion, Pharaoh's invitation and Jacob's revival, and does not end until
*"God spoke to Israel in the visions of the night"* at 46:2. **The book's most explicit statement of
providence is therefore testimony, not verdict.** Genesis reports that Joseph said it and declines to
say whether it is so.

**The speech works by swapping one verb for another in front of the reader.**

> **45:4** …I am Joseph, your brother, whom you **sold** into Egypt. **5** Now don't be grieved, nor
> angry with yourselves, that you **sold** me here, for **God sent me** before you to preserve life.
> … **7** **God sent me** before you to preserve for you a remnant… **8** So now **it wasn't you who
> sent me here, but God**…

***Mākar*** **twice, *šālaḥ* three times, same man as object.** **Joseph does not deny the sale** —
45:4 is the only accusation in the chapter and he makes it before he makes any of the consolation —
**but he stops using the verb after 45:5.** *"It wasn't you who sent me here"* read flatly contradicts
the verse before it; **read as the Hebrew comparative, or as deliberate overstatement aimed at eleven
men who *"couldn't answer him; for they were terrified at his presence"* (45:3), it does not.** See
[[God Sent Me Before You - The Verb That Replaces the Sale]].

**What the chapter also supplies is the theme's vocabulary.** ***Šəʾērît***, *a remnant in the earth*
(45:7), **is the word's first appearance in Scripture**, and ***pəlêṭâ gədōlâ***, *a great deliverance*,
stands beside it — **the survival of a household of seventy described in the terms the prophets will
use for the survival of a nation.** See
[[To Preserve a Remnant - Shearith from Genesis 45 to Romans 11]].

**And the chapter supplies the theme's limit.** **Joseph's reframing does not hold.** The brothers say
nothing at all between 45:3 and 45:15; their one recorded sentence to their father omits the sale
entirely (45:26); **and at 50:15, after Jacob dies, they are still afraid** — *"It may be that Joseph
will hate us, and will fully pay us back for all the evil which we did to him."* **A guilt disposed of
on someone's behalf does not stay disposed of**, which is why 50:19–20 has to say it again in a form
that concedes the brothers' intention rather than dissolving it. See [[Guilt and Conscience]].

### The correction, three times

**Joseph supplies the doctrine himself, in three statements that get progressively harder.**

| | text | what it claims |
|---|---|---|
| **45:5–8** | *"God sent me before you to preserve life … it wasn't you who sent me here, but God"* | **the same act had two agents and one of them did not know it** |
| **50:19–20** | *"You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good"* | ***ḥāšab* both times — they intended and he intended, of one event** |
| **45:7** | *"to save you alive by a great deliverance"* | **the purpose is national survival, not Joseph's career** |

***Ḥāšab***, *to reckon or intend*, is the verb of 50:20 **and the verb of 15:6**, where God *reckoned*
Abram's faith as righteousness. **Genesis uses one word for what a mind assigns to an act**, and 50:20
puts a human mind and the divine mind on the same act with opposite assignments — **without making the
human one innocent.** *"You meant evil"* stands. See
[[Am I in God's Place - Jacob at 30.2 and Joseph at 50.19]].

### After Genesis

- **Exodus 1–2** repeats the method exactly: a genocide, a basket, a princess, and **not one word from
  God until the bush.**
- **Ruth 2:3** — *"she happened to come to the portion of the field belonging to Boaz"* — **the Hebrew
  is emphatic about the chance**, and the book is about what the chance produced.
- **Esther** names God nowhere and is entirely about him; **"who knows whether you haven't come to the
  kingdom for such a time as this?"** (4:14) is 45:5 in a different accent.
- **Job 38–39** gives the doctrine its widest frame: God's care extends to creatures that are of no use to
  anyone, in places no one goes, and is described to a man who has seen none of it reach him. See
  [[Out of the Whirlwind]].
- **Psalm 105:17** reads the sale of Joseph as a sending: *"He sent a man before them; Joseph was sold
  for a servant."* See [[Sold for a Servant - Joseph's Sale in Psalm 105 and Acts 7]].
- **Acts 2:23** applies 50:20's double intention to the crucifixion — *"delivered up by the determined
  counsel and foreknowledge of God, you have crucified"* — **and Romans 8:28 states it as a rule.**

### Little by little — Exodus 23:20–33

God promises a deliberately slow conquest: *"I will not drive them out from before you in one year,
lest the land become desolate, and the animals of the field multiply against you. Little by little I
will drive them out"* (23:29–30). The delay is presented as care for the land and for Israel's ability
to fill it. Judges 2:1–3 later gives a different reason for peoples who remain: disobedience. The
wiki records both and does not merge them. See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]] and
[[Little by Little - The Hornet, the Snare, and the Land Not Taken]].

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

The closing movement rule is concrete: Israel travels when the cloud is taken up and
waits when it is not (40:36–37). Guidance includes remaining in place, rather than only
choosing a road. Verse 38 widens the horizon to all their journeys and the sight of all
Israel. This is the narrative’s public sign for this people, not a general promise that
every personal decision will receive a visible signal.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

See [[Cloud and Fire - From the Sea to the Filled Tabernacle]] for the cloud’s continuing guidance and differing access.

### Shield and guard — Proverbs 2:7–8, 11

*"He lays up sound wisdom for the upright. He is a shield to those who walk in integrity; that he may
guard the paths of justice, and preserve the way of his saints"* (2:7–8). Then the same work passes to
wisdom inside the son: *"Discretion will watch over you. Understanding will keep you"* (2:11) — the
verbs *nāṣar* and *šāmar* of 2:8 again. **God's protection arrives as wisdom internalized.** *Lays up*
is *ṣāp̄an*, the verb of the son's *store up* (2:1). See [[If You Seek Her as Silver]]. Source: Proverbs 2 checked in `raw/bible/Proverbs.md` (WEB); Hebrew checked against Sefaria's
*Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

### Man plans, Yahweh directs — Proverbs 16:1, 3–4, 9, 33

The chapter is framed by the division between plan and outcome: the heart's arrangements and the tongue's answer (1), the planned course and the directed steps (9), the cast lot and its decision (33). Verse 3 joins them — committed works make the plans stand — and verse 4 extends the ordering to everything, *"even the wicked for the day of evil"*. Joseph's *"God meant it for good"* (Genesis 50:20) is the narrative the sayings compress. See [[Man Plans, Yahweh Directs - Proverbs 16.1, 9 and 19.21]] and [[The Lot Is Cast into the Lap - Proverbs 16.33 and the Casting of Lots]].

See [[The Plans of the Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 16.

### The lot and the strong — Proverbs 18:18

*"The lot settles disputes, and keeps strong ones apart"* (18): the civic use of the lot whose every
decision, at 16:33, is *"from Yahweh"*. See
[[The Lot Is Cast into the Lap - Proverbs 16.33 and the Casting of Lots]].

See [[A Strong Tower]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 18.

### Integrity, counsel, and compassion — Proverbs 19:3, 14, 17, 20–21

The prudent wife comes from Yahweh (14), he rewards pity for the poor (17), and his counsel prevails
over the heart's many plans (21). Verse 20 has just commanded receiving counsel, so divine purpose
does not make deliberation pointless. Verse 3 also leaves folly responsible for its ruined course.
See [[Man Plans, Yahweh Directs - Proverbs 16.1, 9 and 19.21]] .

See [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] . Source: stored WEB Proverbs 19 and the
comparisons documented on that passage page.

### Discernment and accountability — Proverbs 20:18, 22, 24

The chapter joins established plans through advice (18) with waiting for Yahweh's salvation
(22) and steps that come from him (24). Its question about understanding one's way recognizes a
limit rather than explaining each event. The account neither makes planning pointless nor makes
every unexpected loss a legible moral judgment.

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 20; supporting comparisons are documented on the
passage page.

### The king's heart and the horse — Proverbs 21:1, 30–31

The king's heart is turned by Yahweh like irrigation water (1), no wisdom or counsel stands against
him (30), and the prepared horse does not decide the battle (31). The chapter does not explain how
the turning works, and Exodus, where Yahweh hardens Pharaoh's heart and Pharaoh hardens it himself,
keeps royal responsibility in place. See
[[The King's Heart in Yahweh's Hand - Proverbs 21.1, Exodus, and Ezra 6.22]] and
[[Victory Is with Yahweh - Proverbs 21.31, Exodus 15, and Psalm 20.7]].

See [[The King's Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 21; supporting comparisons are documented on
the passage page.

### You don't know what a day may bring — Proverbs 27:1

*"Don't boast about tomorrow; for you don't know what a day may bring"* (27:1). The Hebrew has the day
*give birth* (*yēleḏ*). The saying names no God — chapter 27 never does — but it stands in the same
collection as *"A man's heart plans his course, but Yahweh directs his steps"* (16:9): the future is
not owned by the one who plans it. James 4:15 supplies the conditional, *"If the Lord wills"*. See
[[Do Not Boast About Tomorrow - Proverbs 27.1, Luke 12.16-21, and James 4.13-16]].

See [[Do Not Boast About Tomorrow]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 16:9; 27:1; James 4:15 at bible-api.com.

### Agur’s words — Proverbs 30:7–9

Provision is explicitly requested from God, so this is prayer for care rather than a narrative of
hidden guidance. Agur asks for enough to live faithfully, recognizing possible dangers in both
abundance and lack. The request reports neither an answer nor a guarantee that all faithful people
will receive a fixed level of goods.

See [[The Words of Agur]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 30:1–14 and the comparisons documented there.

### A burden God has given — Ecclesiastes 1:13, 15

The first mention of God in Ecclesiastes assigns him the giving of the heavy task of searching
out all that is done (1:13). The next saying, *"That which is crooked can't be made straight"*
(1:15), does not name who made it crooked; Ecclesiastes 7:13, not yet ingested, asks *"who can
make that straight, which he has made crooked?"* and so attributes it to God's work. The later
attribution is noted here, not read back into 1:15. See [[The Burden of Wisdom]]. Source: stored WEB
Ecclesiastes 1:13–15; 7:13.

### Labor left to another — Ecclesiastes 2:18–26

Where 1:13 assigned the burdensome inquiry to God, 2:24 assigns enjoyment in labor to his
hand. Verse 26 describes God giving wisdom, knowledge, and joy to the person who pleases him,
and the task of gathering for that person to the sinner. Divine giving includes contrasting
experiences; it does not restore the worker's control over succession in 2:18–21. The passage
ends in vanity even while naming God as giver.

See [[The Labor Left to Another]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 2:18–26.

### A time for every purpose — Ecclesiastes 3:1–15

God gives the burden (10), makes everything beautiful in its time (11), and gives enjoyment
in labor (13). His work endures without human addition or subtraction (14). Yet people cannot
find it out from beginning to end (11). The passage asserts order without granting observers
the ability to explain each event. Verse 15 attributes the return of what has passed to God;
the WEB does not explain how that seeking operates.

See [[A Time for Every Purpose]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 3:1–15.

### From dust to dust — Ecclesiastes 3:16–22

The language of a time for every purpose and work in 3:17 now accompanies the affirmation
that God judges, even while wickedness occupies the places of justice (16). Verse 18 also
attributes the disclosure of human animal-like mortality to God's testing. These are the
Preacher's reflections on what God does, not a new divine speech or an explanation of why
every particular injustice has been permitted.

See [[From Dust to Dust]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 3:16–22; comparisons as identified above.

### Wealth and enjoyment — Ecclesiastes 5:8–20

God gives life's days (18), wealth, and power to eat of it and rejoice (19), and occupies
the heart with joy (20). This is an explicit attribution of enjoyment, not an explanation
of how the misfortune of verse 14 was caused. **Tension:** 6:2, now ingested in
[[Riches Without Enjoyment]], describes God giving wealth without the power to enjoy it.
Possession alone therefore cannot be treated as evidence of a uniformly enjoyed gift.

See [[The Lover of Silver and the Gift of Enjoyment]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 5:8–20;
comparisons as identified on the passage page.

### Riches without enjoyment — Ecclesiastes 6:1–9

The Preacher expressly attributes the wealth to God and the absence of power to eat
of it to God's not giving that power (2). The page's emphasis on hidden direction in
Genesis therefore needs this further dimension: here divine giving is explicitly named
and experienced as deprivation. **Tension:** the enjoyed gift of 5:19 remains real, while
6:2 withholds any explanation for its absence. Neither the owner's guilt nor the alien's
righteousness is stated; 2:26's moral division cannot simply be imported into this case.

See [[Riches Without Enjoyment]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 6:1–9; comparison
sources are identified on the passage and connection pages.

### Who knows what is good — Ecclesiastes 6:10–12

Naming long ago and inability to contend with a mightier party set limits around
humanity (10). God is a plausible referent in light of 3:14 and 6:2, but is not named
in 6:10–12. The passage does not itself state a complete doctrine of determinism or
explain why good is withheld. The question of what follows remains open (12).
See [[Contending with the Mightier - Job 9 and Ecclesiastes 6.10]]: Job's recognition
of unequal power does not stop his complaint, and the tension must remain visible.

See [[Who Knows What Is Good]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 6:10–12; comparison sources identified on the passage page.

### The house of mourning — Ecclesiastes 7:1–14

*"Consider the work of God, for who can make that straight, which he has made crooked?"*
(7:13) takes up 1:15 and now names God as maker of the crooked. Prosperity and adversity are
made side by side, so that man should not find out anything after him (14). No reason for a
particular adversity is supplied, and the crooked is not called evil. See
[[Good and Adversity from God - Job 2.10 and Ecclesiastes 7.14]].

See [[The House of Mourning]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 7:1–14; comparison sources identified on the passage page.

### Times without possession of the future — Ecclesiastes 8:1–9

Every purpose has a time and procedure (6), yet the person does not know what will be
or how it will happen (7). This recalls 3:1, 11 and 6:12 without explicitly attributing a
particular ruler's wrongdoing to God. God is named in the oath of 8:2; no divine speech or
explanation of the harmful rule in 8:9 follows. Wisdom's discernment of timing in 8:5 must
remain beside the lack of foreknowledge in 8:7.

See [[The King and the Limits of Power]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 8:1–9; comparison sources
are identified on the passage and connection pages.

### God gives days without disclosing the whole — Ecclesiastes 8:10–17

God gives the days in which joy may accompany labor (15), but his work cannot be
fully found out by human searching (17). The preceding reversal of outcomes (14) is
not supplied with a hidden cause or justification. This extends 3:11's limit: divine
work is affirmed without claiming access to the reason for every event.

See [[The Wicked Buried and the Work Beyond Finding]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 8:10–17;
comparisons are identified on the passage page.

### One event comes to all — Ecclesiastes 9:1–12

The righteous, wise, and their works are in God's hand (1); God has already accepted the
addressee's works (7). Yet outcomes are unpredictable, and sudden evil overtakes people
(11–12). **Explicit tension:** divine keeping does not yield a readable distribution of
success. Time and chance are not defined here as an independent deity or as events outside
God's reach; neither is a hidden causal explanation supplied. See
[[Time and Chance and the Prepared Horse]].

See [[One Event Comes to All]]. Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 9:1–12; comparison sources
are identified on the passage and connection pages.

  two of them contradictory, and Genesis harmonises none of them.** See
  [[The God Who Has Fed Me - The First Time God Is a Shepherd]]
- **Genesis 50:20 — the book's summary sentence**: *"you meant evil against me, but God meant it for
  good"*, ***ḥāšab*** **in both clauses — one act, two intentions, neither cancelled** —
  [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] and
  [[You Meant Evil Against Me - The Two Intentions at Genesis 50.20]]
- [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]] — Job 23:13–14; *"What his soul desires, even that he does. For he
  performs that which is appointed for me. Many such things are with him."* **Providence as the
  ground of both hope and terror.** The *ḥōq* appointed for Job is carried out by a God who *"stands
  alone"*; three verses earlier the same sovereignty made Job sure he would come out like gold, and
  in the next verse it makes him *"terrified at his presence"* (23:15).
- [[Out of the Whirlwind]] — Job 38:26–27, 39–41; 39:1–4; **providence without a human beneficiary.** God
  sends rain *"on a land where no man is … to cause the tender grass to grow"*, hunts *"the prey for the
  lioness"*, provides *"for the raven his prey, when his young ones cry to God"*, and watches the births
  of the mountain goats that no one else sees. Nothing in the speech applies it to Job; the provision is
  shown, not promised. See [[Who Provides for the Raven - Job 38.41, Psalm 147.9, and Luke 12.24]].
- [[Behemoth and Leviathan]] — Job 40:20–22; 41:11; **a beast at ease, and everything owned.** The mountains
  bring Behemoth food *"where all the animals of the field play"*, and the lotus shades him (40:20–22);
  *"Everything under the heavens is mine"* (41:11).
- [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]] — Job 42:2; **purpose that cannot be stopped.** *"I know that you can do all things, and
  that no purpose of yours can be restrained"* — Babel's *"nothing will be withheld from them"* (Genesis 11:6),
  said of God. Job confesses the purpose without being told what it is. See
  [[No Purpose of Yours Can Be Restrained - Job 42.2 and Genesis 11.6]].
- [[The Oppression in Egypt]] — Exodus 1:1–22; **providence with God almost absent.** He is not mentioned in 1:1–16 and
  never speaks; the people multiply in the passive under every measure, and the one channel of his
  action is two women's fear. Psalm 105:25 attributes the Egyptians' hatred to God; Exodus 1 does
  not — recorded, not resolved
- [[The Birth of Moses]] — Exodus 2:1–10; **providence with God wholly absent.** No divine word or
  act in the whole rescue: a mother's improvised ark, a sister's watch, the timing of a princess's
  bath, her pity, and a sister's quick tongue. **The deliverer is preserved by the enemy's own
  household**, and the narrator leaves the reader to supply what Genesis 50:20 already said
- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — Exodus 2:15–25; **the fugitive placed where God will find him.** Moses' own attempt at
  deliverance fails and drives him into the wilderness; there he becomes a shepherd within reach of
  Horeb (3:1). Nothing in 2:11–22 names God; **the narrator withholds him until 2:23–25 and then names
  him five times in three verses**
- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:1; 4:2, 14; ***"What is that in your hand?"*** — **the instrument of the plagues is
  the shepherd's staff Moses already carries**, and the help he asks for is already travelling: *"behold,
  he comes out to meet you"* (4:14). The forty years in Midian turn out to have put him on the right
  mountain with the right tool
- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 13:17–18; 14:1–4; **the route is God's, and so is the trap.** He leads
  the long way round (*wayyassēb*) for a stated reason, the people's fear of war; then turns them back to the
  sea so that Pharaoh will think them *"entangled"*, and says what Pharaoh will do before Pharaoh does it.
  ***"They did so"*** (14:4): **the people obey a route they are not told the reason for**. *Nāḥâ*, *lead*
  (13:17, 21), is the verb of Abraham's servant, *"Yahweh has led me on the way"* (Genesis 24:27; WLC)
- [[The Song of the Sea]] — Exodus 15:13; **led and guided.** *Nāḥâ*, *led* (the verb of 13:17, 21), and *nāhal*, *guided* —
  the shepherds' verb of Jacob's *"I will lead on gently"* (Genesis 33:14) and Joseph feeding Egypt (47:17)
  (WLC lemma search). **The Song sings the guidance *"to your holy habitation"* as already done.**
- [[Bitter Water at Marah]] — Exodus 15:22–27; **guidance into thirst, then to twelve springs.** The Song sang
  *"You have guided them (nēhaltā)"* (15:13), the verb of leading to water; **the first stage after it is
  three days with *"no water"***, then a bitter well, then Elim's *"twelve springs of water, and seventy palm
  trees"*. The text reports the sequence without comment
- [[Manna and Quail]] — Exodus 16:4–35; **provision by the day, for forty years.** *"I will rain bread from the
  sky"*; *"a day's portion every day"*; *"he who gathered little had no lack"*; and the narrator's *"The
  children of Israel ate the manna forty years"* (16:35) — **provision answered to a complaint, not to
  faith**
- [[Water from the Rock]] — Exodus 17:1–6; **led to the lack.** The camp without water is reached *"according
  to Yahweh's commandment"* (*ʿal-pî YHWH*); the provision comes from the least likely source, a rock, and from
  the place God says he will stand. Psalm 78:15–20 and 114:8 sing it as power over nature
- [[The Angel Sent Ahead]] — Exodus 23:20–33; a conquest paced for the land's sake.

- [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]] — Exodus 40:1–38; The cloud determines both departure and waiting throughout Israel’s journeys.
- [[If You Seek Her as Silver]] — Proverbs 2:7–8, 11; God as shield and guard, his keeping carried out by wisdom in the son
- [[Do Not Boast About Tomorrow]] — Proverbs 27:1; *"You don't know what a day may bring"*: tomorrow is not owned.
