# The Wilderness

## Definition / overview

### The waters of Meribah — Numbers 20:1–13

Zin and Kadesh frame a fresh lack of water (1–5). The people contrast wilderness
conditions with cultivated life, but the lack is met without their entering the land.
The first month is specified; the year is not specified in this pericope.

See [[The Waters of Meribah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 20:1–13.

### The budding rod — Numbers 17:1–13

The sign takes place at the Tent in the continuing wilderness narrative, with no new campsite
or departure named (1–13). The immediate issue is how Israel can live near the sanctuary.

See [[Aaron's Rod Buds]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 17.

### The rebellion of Korah — Numbers 16:1–40

The confrontation occurs at the camp and the Tent of Meeting; death in the wilderness is part of the
opponents’ accusation (13). No named campsite or precise date is supplied in this unit.

See [[The Rebellion of Korah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:1–40; comparisons as cited.

*Midbār* is the uncultivated, thinly watered land outside settled life. It is a place of danger,
hunger, and exposure. It is also where the Pentateuch puts God's most direct meetings with people.
This page opened with Numbers, whose Hebrew name is the word itself.

## Development across Scripture

### Between the dead and the living — Numbers 16:41–50

The renewed confrontation takes place the next day in the same camp, between the Tent and
the assembly. No named campsite is given in 16:41–50; the broader wilderness setting does
not establish a precise location.

See [[Aaron Stands Between the Dead and the Living]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 16:41–50; comparisons as cited.

### The Sabbath breaker — Numbers 15:32–36

The narrator locates the case only in the wilderness (32), with execution outside the
camp (35–36). No named wilderness, station, or date is supplied; the setting should not be
converted into a precise geographical claim.

See [[The Sabbath Breaker]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 15:32–36; comparisons as cited.

### Hagar — Genesis 16 and 21

The first people in the wilderness in the Bible are the ones thrown out of a household. *Yahweh's
angel found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness* (Genesis 16:7), and after she is sent away
Hagar *wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba* (21:14). God finds her both times. See
[[Hagar and Ishmael]] and [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]].

### Exodus — the destination of the demand

Moses asks Pharaoh for a festival there: *Let my people go, that they may hold a feast to me in the
wilderness* (Exodus 5:1). Once it is reached, the same place produces the first complaint: *you have
brought us out into this wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger* (16:3). See
[[Manna and Quail]]. Sinai is reached in the third month (19:1).

### The camp in order — Numbers 1:1–54

Numbers begins *in the wilderness of Sinai* (1:1) and counts the people there (19). The wilderness
is not presented here as a punishment. That comes at the spies. At this point it is the road, and
Israel is being organized to walk it, with the sanctuary at the center.

See [[The Census in the Wilderness of Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 1.

### The camp around the Tent — Numbers 2:1–34

The place has no walls, roads, or settled order, so the order comes from the people's arrangement around the Tent. It moves with them: *As they encamp, so shall they set out* (17).

See [[The Camp Around the Tent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 2.

### Ahead

Numbers 26:65 reports the sentence carried out: *They shall surely die in the wilderness*. Acts 7:38
later calls Israel *the assembly in the wilderness*.

### A feast in the wilderness — Numbers 9:1–5

Israel keeps the Passover in the wilderness of Sinai, though Exodus 12:25 and 13:5 spoke of keeping it in the land. See [[Kept in the Wilderness - The Passovers of Egypt, Sinai, and Gilgal]].

See [[The Passover in the Second Year]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 9.

### A rule for moving — Numbers 9:15–23

With no land and no fixed camp, Israel gets a rule instead of a map. A stop may last a night or far longer, and the people may not know which.

See [[The Cloud over the Tabernacle]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 9.

### The first stage — Numbers 10:11–36

Israel moves into the wilderness for the first time since the camp was ordered, and the first thing Moses wants is someone who knows it (31). Verse 33 has the ark seeking *a resting place*, in a place that offers none.

See [[The March from Sinai]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 10.

### The first stop — Numbers 11:1–3

The first place named after Sinai is named for fire. Whether Israel camped there as a stage the text does not say, since Numbers 33:16 does not list it.

See [[The Fire at Taberah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 11.

### Meat in the wilderness — Numbers 11:4–35

The second stop on the road is named for those buried in it. The food of the wilderness is defended by the narrator (7–9) and refused by the people (6). Egypt is remembered as free food (5), and the wind brings a supply no camp could need.

See [[The Seventy Elders and the Quail]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 11.

### Paran reached — Numbers 12:16

After Taberah, Kibroth Hattaavah, and Hazeroth, the people camp in the wilderness of Paran, which 10:12 had named ahead of time. The next chapter sends the spies from it.

See [[Miriam and Aaron Against Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 12. Word locations from WLC (morphhb) tags; Greek from Rahlfs 1935.

### The spies — Numbers 13

The men go from the wilderness of Paran and come back to it (3, 26), and they begin the search at
the wilderness of Zin (21). The wilderness is now the place Israel looks out of. See
[[The Wilderness of Zin]].

See [[The Twelve Spies]].

### The sentence — Numbers 14

The wilderness was the road, and now it becomes a destination. *In this wilderness* comes again and
again (2, 29, 32, 33, 35), and the children will be *shepherds* there for forty years (33). Yahweh's
direction is back *by the way to the Red Sea* (25). See [[The People Refuse the Land]].
