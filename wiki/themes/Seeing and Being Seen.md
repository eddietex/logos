# Seeing and Being Seen

## Definition / overview

Sight is the Bible's most morally loaded sense. *Rāʾâ*, to see, is the verb of God's approval in
Genesis 1 (*"God saw that it was good"*, seven times), of the first temptation (*"the woman saw that
the tree was good for food"*, 3:6), of the two ways of looking at a country (13:10 against 13:14), and
of the one thing nobody may do and live (Exodus 33:20). And its passive side — being seen, being
looked at, being overlooked — is where the theme does its hardest work, because in Scripture *to be
seen by God* is not surveillance but rescue.

**The two halves rarely appear together, and Genesis 16 is where they do.** The chapter runs the root
through every register it has: Sarai says *"See now"* (16:2); Hagar *saw* that she had conceived, and
her mistress was slight *in her eyes* (16:4); Sarai says she was despised *in her eyes* (16:5); Abram
tells her to do what is good *in your eyes* (16:6) — three uses of *eyes* as the seat of judgment,
each of them a person weighing another and finding them light. Then a woman nobody in the household
looks at is found at a spring (*ʿayin*, the word for both *spring* and *eye*) and says
***ʾAttâ ʾĒl Rŏʾî*** — *You are a God who sees* — and asks whether she has survived seeing him. The
well takes the name *the Living One who sees me*.

Nothing else in the Bible is quite like it: **the only divine name given by a human being is a name
about being looked at, and it is given by a slave.**

**Public manifestation does not authorize every kind of looking.** Yahweh will descend in the
people's sight (19:11), yet they must not break through to gaze (19:21). The assigned place is
the mountain's foot; cloud, fire, and the audible exchange with Moses make the encounter public
without unrestricted access. See [[Israel at Mount Sinai]].

## Development across Scripture

**God sees, and seeing is acting.** *"Yahweh saw that the wickedness of man was great"* (6:5) is the
verdict that produces the flood. *"I have surely seen the affliction of my people who are in Egypt …
and I know their sorrows"* (Exodus 3:7) is the sentence the exodus comes out of, and it uses Genesis
16:11's noun, *ʿŏnî*. Hannah asks God to *"look on the affliction of your servant"* (1 Samuel 1:11) in
the same vocabulary; Mary's *"he has looked at the humble state of his servant"* (Luke 1:48) is the
same clause again at the other end of the canon. God's seeing in Scripture is never neutral
observation; it is the moment before an intervention.

**And God sees differently from people.** *"Man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh looks at
the heart"* (1 Samuel 16:7), said over the son nobody thought to call in from the sheep. The prophets
turn it into an indictment (Isaiah 29:15), and the psalms into a comfort (Psalm 33:13–15; 139:1–16).
The Bible's most sustained treatment of God as the one who sees everything is Psalm 139, and its
conclusion is a request for more of it, not less: *"Search me, God, and know my heart."*

**Seeing God is the danger.** *"You cannot see my face, for man may not see me and live"* (Exodus
33:20), with the exception granted immediately afterward — Moses sees his back. Manoah's *"We shall
surely die, because we have seen God"* (Judges 13:22) and Isaiah's *"Woe is me! For I am undone"*
(6:5) are the standard reaction; Hagar's *"Have I even stayed alive after seeing him?"* is the first
instance of it in Scripture, and the only one spoken by someone with no cultic standing at all.

**Not being seen is the wound.** Leah *"was hated"* and God *"saw"* it (29:31). The despised servant
of Isaiah 53:2–3 is the one *"we hid, as it were, our faces from"*. Job's complaint is partly that God
watches him too closely (7:17–20) and partly that no one else does. The Bible's poor and afflicted ask
to be *looked upon* far more often than they ask for anything else.

**Sight fulfilled.** *"Now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face"* (1 Corinthians 13:12);
*"Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God"* (Matthew 5:8); *"they will see his face"*
(Revelation 22:4). The canon that begins with a God who sees ends with the promise that the looking
will finally go both ways.

### They saw the God of Israel — Exodus 24:9–11

**The most direct statement in the Torah that people saw God, and the most reticent description.**
*"They saw the God of Israel"* (*wayyirʾû*), and again *"They saw God"* (*wayyeḥĕzû*, the visionary
verb; both MAM) — yet the text describes only what was *"under his feet"*, a pavement like sapphire
(24:10–11). The narrator adds that *"He didn't lay his hand on"* them, which assumes the danger 33:20
will state. The Septuagint has them see *the place* where God stood (Rahlfs). See [[The Blood of the Covenant]] and
[[They Saw God - Exodus 24, Exodus 33, and John 1.18]].

### Presence after the calf — Exodus 33

The narrator describes face-to-face speech like conversation with a friend (33:11), but God forbids
Moses to see his face and live (33:20). The comparison with friendship supports a distinction
between direct speech and unrestricted sight, but that is an interpretation, not an explanation
supplied by the narrator. The report that the elders saw God in 24:10–11 remains in tension with
this prohibition; neither statement should erase the other. See [[Show Me Your Glory]].

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

Moses initially does not know that his own face shines, while Aaron and Israel see it and
fear approaching (34:29–30). Once called, they draw near and hear the commands; they see his
face before he covers it again (34:31–35). Seeing Moses does not establish that he saw God's
face, nor does it remove the tension between Exodus 24:10–11 and 33:20. The visible mediator
and the withheld divine face remain distinct.

See [[The Shining Face of Moses]].

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

Moses sees all the work and recognizes that it has been made according to the command
(39:43). Here seeing is an act of assessment, followed by blessing. The resemblance to God’s
seeing everything made in Genesis 1:31 supports a literary comparison without identifying Moses
with God or claiming a new vision. See
[[Finished Work, Seeing, and Blessing - Creation and the Tabernacle]].

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### Eyes straight ahead — Proverbs 4:21, 25

The father's words must not *"depart from your eyes"* (4:21), and the eyes are to *"look straight
ahead"*, the gaze fixed *"directly before you"* (4:25). Sight is disciplined, not merely used: what
the eyes look at is a matter of the heart's keeping. Job swore the same discipline — *"I made a
covenant with my eyes"* (Job 31:1) — and named its failure as the heart walking *"after my eyes"*
(31:7). See [[Heart, Eyes, and Feet - Proverbs 4 and Job 31]].

See [[Keep Your Heart]]. Source: cited verses checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### Before Yahweh's eyes — Proverbs 5:21

*"For the ways of man are before Yahweh's eyes. He examines all his paths"* (5:21). The adulterer
of Job 24:15 waits for twilight saying *"No eye shall see me"*; Proverbs answers him as Job 24:23
does, *"His eyes are on their ways"*. The verb *"examines"* (*məpallēs*) is the one 5:6 uses of
the strange woman, who does not weigh the way of life (*təpallēs*), and 4:26 of the son's level
path (*pallēs*). See [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 5; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*.

### Her eyelids — Proverbs 6:25

*"Don't lust after her beauty in your heart, neither let her captivate you with her eyelids"*
(6:25). The seeing is the woman's as much as the son's, as in Genesis 39:7, where *"his master's
wife set her eyes on Joseph"*; the coveting is located in the heart. See
[[The Lamp of the Commandment]] and [[A Covenant with My Eyes - Job 31.1 and Matthew 5.28]]. Source:
stored WEB Proverbs 6; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

In Proverbs 7 the watcher is a father at a window: *"I looked out through my lattice. I saw among
the simple ones"* (7:6–7). The verb *šāqap̄* with a window is Abimelech's at Genesis 26:8 and
Sisera's mother's at Judges 5:28. The son is told to guard the teaching *"as the apple of your eye"*
(7:2, *kəʾîšôn ʿênêḵā*), the phrase Deuteronomy 32:10 uses of God's care for Israel. See
[[The Young Man Void of Understanding]], [[Through the Window - Proverbs 7.6, Genesis 26.8, and Judges 5.28]], and
[[The Apple of the Eye - Deuteronomy 32.10, Psalm 17.8, and Proverbs 7.2]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 7; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.

## Key passages

- [[The Strange Woman and the Wife of Your Youth]] — Proverbs 5:21; every way of man before Yahweh's eyes

- [[Keep Your Heart]] — Proverbs 4:21, 25; the words kept before the eyes, and eyes fixed straight ahead

- [[The Shining Face of Moses]] — Exodus 34:29–35; Israel sees Moses shining, not the divine face withheld in 33:20.

- [[Show Me Your Glory]] — Exodus 33:1–23; Face-to-face speech and the face that cannot be seen.

- [[The Creation of the World]] — Genesis 1, *"God saw that it was good"*, seven times: sight as
  approval and as verdict
- [[The Fall]] — Genesis 3:5–7, seeing that the tree was good, and eyes opened onto nakedness
- [[Abram and Lot Separate]] — Genesis 13:10 and 13:14, two men told to lift up their eyes, and the
  two different things they see. See [[Lift Up Your Eyes - Two Ways of Seeing the Land]]
- [[Hagar and Ishmael]] — Genesis 16, the theme's densest chapter: three judgments passed *in the
  eyes*, and then *ʾĒl Rŏʾî* and a well called *the Living One who sees me*
- [[The Three Visitors and Abraham's Intercession]] — Genesis 18:2, 16, 21: a man who *"lifted up his
  eyes and looked"* and does not recognize what he is looking at, a party that *"looked toward
  Sodom"*, and God declining to sentence a city on a report — *"I will go down now, and see"*. The
  theme's two halves in one chapter: human sight that misses who is at the door, and divine sight
  that is an inquiry rather than an observation
- [[Seven Days of Silence]] — Job 2:12–13; **two acts of sight in consecutive verses, going opposite
  ways.** The friends *"lifted up their eyes from a distance, and didn't recognize him"* — *nākar*
  negated, the verb Genesis reserves for identifying a man from a token, used here of the man himself
  and failing. Then *"they saw that his grief was very great"*, and that sight is accurate and buys a
  week of silence. **The first is the theme's clearest failure and the second its quietest success**,
  and the difference between them is that the second is looking rather than interpreting: from 4:1
  the same eyes read the same body as a verdict and are wrong for twenty-five chapters. Job ends the
  book at 42:5 with *"now my eye sees you."* See
  [[They Didn't Recognize Him - Marred Beyond Knowing]]
- Genesis 22:8, 14 — *"God will provide"*, which in Hebrew is *God will see for himself*, and
  *Yahweh Yireh* on the mountain *(not yet ingested)*
- [[Jacob, Leah, and Rachel]] — Genesis 29:2, 25; ***hinnēh*** three times, and the last one is the
  disaster. The word that opens the passage on a well — *behold, a well … behold, three flocks* — is
  the word that opens the morning after the wedding: ***"In the morning, behold, it was Leah!"***
  **Two chapters running now have turned on a man who could not see who he was dealing with** — a
  blind father blessing the wrong son, and a son marrying the wrong daughter in the dark — and
  Genesis draws the parallel with a pronoun and no commentary
- Genesis 29:31–32 — *"Yahweh saw that Leah was hated"*: the first thing God is said to see in the
  Jacob cycle, and it is the un-preferred wife *(not yet ingested)*
- Exodus 2:23–25; 3:7 — God sees the affliction and hears the cry
- Exodus 33:18–23 — the face nobody may see, and the back
- Judges 13:22 — *"We shall surely die, because we have seen God"*
- 1 Samuel 1:11; 16:7 — looking on affliction, and looking at the heart
- Psalm 139 — the fullest treatment, and it ends in an invitation
- Isaiah 6:1–5; 53:2–3 — seeing God and being unable to look at the servant
- Luke 1:48 — *"he has looked at the humble state of his servant"*, in Hagar's grammar
- 1 Corinthians 13:12; Revelation 22:4 — face to face

- [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]] — Genesis 19:17, 26, 28: a prohibition on looking, a
  woman who looks in the verb she was forbidden to look in, and Abraham looking at the same fire
  from forty miles off and learning nothing from it. See [[Remember Lot's Wife]]
- [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]] — Genesis 21:9, 16, 19. Sarah **sees** Ishmael
  *mocking* and the household breaks; Hagar asks the opposite — *"Don't let me see the death of the
  child"* (21:16) — and walks a bowshot off so that she will not; and the rescue is neither a well
  dug nor water made, but *"God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water"* (21:19). **The woman
  who named God *a God who sees* is the one person in Scripture whose own seeing God has to repair**,
  and the same mechanism saves the other son one chapter later, when Abraham *lifts up his eyes* and
  sees the ram (22:13)
- [[The Binding of Isaac]] — Genesis 22, **the theme's densest chapter and the one that turns it into
  a name.** *Rāʾâ* five times in fourteen verses: Abraham sees the place far off (22:4); *"God will
  provide himself the lamb"* — *yirʾeh-lô*, God will **see** to it (22:8); Abraham lifts his eyes and
  sees the ram (22:13); the place is called *YHWH yirʾeh*, **Yahweh sees**; and the proverb closes in
  the passive, *"On Yahweh's mountain, it will be provided"* — *yērāʾeh*, the same consonants,
  readable also as *he will appear*, which is the technical term for pilgrimage (Exodus 23:17).
  **Providing is what seeing means when God is the subject**, and English *provide* keeps the same
  metaphor (*pro-videre*). Nothing is created in the rescue: the ram was in the thicket, exactly as
  the well was in the desert. See [[Yahweh Will Provide - Moriah and the Temple Mount]]
- [[A Wife for Isaac]] — Genesis 24:21, 30, 63–65: the servant *looked steadfastly* at Rebekah,
  *remaining silent*, through the whole watering; Laban is introduced by *when he saw the ring*; Isaac
  and Rebekah *lift up their eyes* in consecutive verses, the only mutual instance of the formula in
  Genesis; and the chapter that worked hardest to make a woman visible ends with her behind a veil
- [[The Stolen Blessing]] — Genesis 27:1, 21–27: **the theme inverted, and the sense simply
  withdrawn.** *"His eyes were dim, so that he could not see"* — *wattikhênā ʿênāyw mērəʾōt* — makes
  Isaac **the first person in Scripture said to have lost his sight** (*kāhâ*, the verb used of Eli
  at 1 Samuel 3:2 and denied of Moses at Deuteronomy 34:7). Genesis has spent fourteen chapters on
  eyes that lift up, look steadfastly, and find God looking back; here sight is gone and **the other
  four senses are worked against their owner** one at a time — touch, taste, smell deceived, and
  hearing correct. *"The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau"* (27:22) is a
  true forensic report that its speaker states aloud and then overrules. **The irony is exact**: the
  man whose lie was exposed one chapter earlier because a king looked out of a window (26:8) is the
  man who cannot look. And Jacob will do the same thing in reverse at 48:10, *"the eyes of Israel
  were dim for age, so that he couldn't see"*, and cross his hands **on purpose**
- [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]] — Genesis 28:12, 16: **seeing with the eyes shut, and not knowing with
  them open.** Genesis has given visions, appearances, and lifted eyes; here it gives a **dream** —
  the first inside the covenant line — and the man does his seeing asleep. Then he wakes into the
  theme's sharpest sentence: ***"Surely Yahweh is in this place, and I didn't know it."* The reverse
  of Abram at 13:14**, who was told to lift his eyes and look in four directions; Jacob is told
  nothing, sees in the dark, and confesses the ignorance afterwards. **The man whose whole method has
  been knowing more than the person in front of him opens his account with God by saying he did not
  know**
- [[The Birth of Jacob's Children]] — Genesis 29:31; 30:1, 9. ***Rāʾâ*** three times, and **each
  occurrence starts a move.** *Yahweh saw that Leah was hated*, and a womb opens; *Rachel saw that she
  bore Jacob no children*, and a slave is produced; *Leah saw that she had finished bearing*, and a
  second slave is produced. **The narrator uses one verb for God's compassion and for two women's
  arithmetic**, and the first of the three is the first thing God is recorded seeing in the whole Jacob
  cycle — **after thirty verses in which he is not named at all.** The theme's other half is in the
  firstborn's name: ***Reuben***, *rəʾû bēn*, *see, a son*, glossed by his mother not with *bēn* but
  with *"Yahweh has looked at my affliction"* — **the same *ʿŏnî* Genesis 16:11 used of [[Hagar]] and
  Exodus 3:7 will use of a nation.** See [[Names and Naming]]
- [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]] — Genesis 31:2, 12, 42; **the chapter's theology is a verb of
  sight, twice, and both times about a person being used.** *"I have ***seen*** all that Laban does
  to you"* (31:12), from the angel in the dream; and *"God has ***seen*** my affliction and the labor
  of my hands"* (31:42), where ***ʿŏnî*** is the noun of Exodus 3:7. **The human seeing in the
  chapter is the opposite**: Jacob *sees the expression on Laban's face* (31:2), and Laban gropes
  through four tents and finds nothing (31:34–35)
- [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]] — Genesis 32:20, 30; ***pānîm***, *face*, driven through the chapter
  until the name absorbs it. Jacob plans three times over to *"cover his face"* with a gift, *"see his
  face"*, and have his own face *lifted* (32:20) — all about the brother he has not yet met — and then
  names the place of the wrestling **Peniel**, *face of God*, for a face he did not choose to see:
  *"I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved"* (32:30). **The word built into every
  calculation about managing Esau is the word the chapter ends on, applied to God instead**, and
  Genesis does not say whether that is correction, coincidence, or the same fear under two names
- [[The Reconciliation with Esau]] — Genesis 33:10; **the word Peniel was named for, spoken to the
  person it was managing.** *"I have seen your face, as one sees the face of God, and you were
  pleased with me"* answers 32:30 directly — not *cover my face so he cannot see it*, which is what
  the staged gift of chapter 32 assumed, but *your face is like the face I have already survived
  seeing.* The same formula recurs a third time in this family, at 46:30, when Jacob tells Joseph
  *"since I have seen your face, that you are still alive"* — see
  [[Fell on His Neck and Wept - The Running Embrace in Genesis and Luke]]
- [[Judah and Tamar]] — Genesis 38:14–15, 25–26; **a chapter built on what a man can and cannot make
  out.** [[Tamar]] sits at *petaḥ ʿênayim*, **the opening of [[Enaim]] — and *ʿênayim* is the Hebrew
  for *two eyes*** — and Judah *"thought that she was a prostitute, **for she had covered her face**."*
  **Genesis never says a veil marked a prostitute; it says he could not see her face**, and supplies
  the grounds of his inference so the reader can weigh them. **The chapter turns when he is finally
  shown something identifiable**: *hakker-nāʾ*, *"discern, please, whose are these"* — and the verb is
  ***nākar***, which governs the whole cycle. **[[Isaac]] fails to recognise Jacob (27:23); [[Jacob]]
  recognises the coat and concludes falsely (37:33); [[Judah]] recognises the pledge and concludes
  rightly (38:26); [[Joseph]] recognises his brothers while they do not recognise him (42:7–8).**
  Three of the four get it wrong, and the one who gets it right is the one being convicted. See
  [[Discern, Please - Hakker-na from Jacob's Coat to Judah's Pledge]]
- [[The Cupbearer and the Baker]] — Genesis 40:6–7, 16; **Joseph reads two faces before he reads two
  dreams.** *"He saw them, and saw that they were sad"* — *zōʿăpîm* — and then asks the chapter's only
  question, *"why are your faces evil today?"* **The man with the least standing in the room is the one
  who notices.** Then the seeing runs the other way and costs someone: *"when the chief baker **saw**
  that the interpretation was good"* (40:16) is why he volunteers. **And the man Joseph reads correctly
  does not see him at all** (40:23)
- [[Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise]] — Genesis 41:19, 21, 37, **the chapter's argument runs on verbs of
  perception.** Pharaoh's retelling turns on what he ***saw***: *"such as I never saw in all the land of
  Egypt for ugliness"* (41:19) — **a superlative the narrator's own account does not contain** — and on
  what could ***not be known***: *"it couldn't be known that they had eaten them"* (41:21), **the clause
  the interpretation turns on**, returned to him at 41:31. The verdict on Joseph's advice is visual —
  *"the thing was **good in the eyes** of Pharaoh, and in the eyes of all his servants"* (41:37) — and so
  is the confession that follows: *"Because God has ***shown*** you all of this"* (41:39), which is
  Joseph's own word at 41:25 and 41:28. **What God does in this chapter, on the chapter's own account, is
  show somebody something**
- [[The Brothers' First Journey to Egypt]] — Genesis 42:1, 7, 9, 21; **the chapter's sin is a thing that was watched.** Jacob *saw*
  there was grain (42:1); Joseph *saw* his brothers and made himself unseeable (42:7); the espionage
  charge is about *seeing* *"the nakedness of the land"* (42:9, 12); **and the confession is
  *"we saw the distress of his soul… and we wouldn't listen"*** (42:21). **Seeing without acting is
  what they finally name as the offence**
- [[The Second Journey]] — Genesis 43:16, 29, 33; **the chapter's sight is all one-way.** *"When Joseph
  **saw** Benjamin with them"* (43:16) — and he turns to a servant about lunch. *"He **lifted up his
  eyes**, and **saw** Benjamin, his brother, his mother's son"* (43:29) — and he leaves the room.
  **Meanwhile eleven men are seated in the exact order of their births by a foreigner who has never met
  them, and *"the men marveled one with another"*** (43:33): **they look straight at the evidence and
  cannot read it**

- [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] — Genesis 44:21–34; **five verbs of sight in seventeen verses,
  and the man being addressed is the one nobody has recognised.** *"That I may set my eyes on him"*
  (44:21); *"you will see my face no more"* (44:23); *"I haven't seen him since"* (44:28); *"when he
  sees that the boy is no more"* (44:31); *"lest I see the evil"* (44:34). **[[Judah]] is arguing
  entirely about what one absent old man will and will not see**, in front of the son that man has
  not seen in twenty-two years. **And the search at 44:12 is looking run as theatre** — *"beginning
  with the oldest, and ending at the youngest"* — **by the only person in the scene who knows what is
  in the sacks**

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — Genesis 45:12–13, 16, 27–28; **the chapter that ends the concealment is
  built out of eyes, and the decisive sight is of an object.** *"Your eyes see, and the eyes of my
  brother [[Benjamin]], that it is my mouth that speaks to you"* (45:12) **offers sight and hearing
  together as the proof of identity**; *"all that you have seen"* (45:13) is what [[Jacob]] is to be
  told; *"it pleased Pharaoh well"* (45:16) is literally *good in the eyes of*. **Then the words fail
  and the looking works**: *"when he saw the wagons… the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived"*
  (45:27). **The man who was convinced of a death by looking at a coat is convinced of a life by
  looking at a cart**, and what he asks for is more of the same: *"I will go and see him before I
  die"*
- [[Jacob's Family Settles in Egypt]] — Genesis 46:29–30; 47:2; ***"he presented himself to him"***
  (46:29) is ***wayyērāʾ ʾēlāyw***, **the Niphal of *rāʾâ* — the verb-form that in every other
  occurrence in Genesis has God as its subject** (12:7; 17:1; 18:1; 26:2, 24; 35:9; 48:3). **Here it is a son getting down from a chariot.** Then ***"since I have
  seen your face, that you are still alive"*** (46:30): **[[Jacob]] disbelieved the report at 45:26,
  was persuaded by the wagons at 45:27, and is released by the face here.** **Testimony is the one
  stage that failed.** See [[Now Let Me Die, Since I Have Seen - Jacob and Simeon]]
- [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]] — Genesis 48:8–11; **a blind man's chapter, and the three verses
  contradict each other on purpose.** *"**Israel saw** Joseph's sons, and said, 'Who are these?'"* (48:8);
  *"the eyes of Israel were dim for age, **so that he couldn't see**"* (48:10); **and between them**
  *"**God has let me see** your offspring also"* (48:11). **The question is not ignorance** — he has named
  both boys in 48:5 — **it is the formal identification of the parties**, which is why [[Joseph]] answers
  with a legal sentence rather than with names. ***"Who are these?"*** **is [[Esau]]'s question to Jacob
  about his children at 33:5**, answered then with the same theology from the other side of the family.
  **And what the eyes cannot do the hands do** — *"guiding his hands knowingly"* (48:14). See
  [[Two Blind Fathers - Genesis 27 and Genesis 48]]
- **Genesis 50:11 — the Canaanites watch the funeral of the man their land was promised to and see
  Egyptians**, and name the place for what they saw — [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]]
- [[The Arrows of the Almighty]] — Job 7:7–21, **a prayer made of eyes.** *"My eye shall no more see
  good"*; *"the eye of him who sees me shall see me no more. Your eyes shall be on me, but I shall
  not be"*; *"how long will you not look away from me?"* **Job is the one lamenter in Scripture who
  asks God to stop looking** — the reversal of *"how long will you hide your face?"* (Psalm 13:1).
  And his last line imagines God looking for him and finding nothing (7:21). See
  [[What Is Man That You Should Visit Him - Job 7.17-18 and Psalm 8]]
- [[There Is No Umpire Between Us]] — Job 9:11; 10:4, 18; **God unseen, God's sight questioned, and Job's wish to have
  been unseen.** *"He goes by me, and I don't see him"*; *"Do you have eyes of flesh? Or do you see
  as man sees?"*; *"I wish… no eye had seen me."* The resolution is 42:5. See
  [[He Treads on the Waves of the Sea - Job 9.8-11 and the Walking on the Water]].
- [[Zophar's First Speech]] — Job 11:11; ***"He sees iniquity also"***: Zophar's answer to Job's *"Do you have eyes
  of flesh?"* (10:4). God does not need to investigate; he sees at a glance — and what he sees,
  Zophar is sure, is Job's guilt
- [[Though He Slay Me]] — Job 13:24, 27; 14:3, 6, 16; **the face hidden and the eyes fixed.** *"Why
  hide you your face, and hold me for your enemy?"* (13:24), and in the same speech God *"mark[s] all
  my paths"* (13:27), *"open[s] your eyes on such a one"* (14:3), and *"count[s] my steps"* (14:16).
  Job asks to be looked away from — *"Look away from him, that he may rest"* (14:6) — in the same
  breath as he complains of God's hidden face
- [[Why Do the Wicked Live]] — Job 21:5, 8, 20, 29; **what the eyes are shown.** *"Look at me, and be
  astonished"* (21:5): Job as the evidence. The wicked keep their offspring *"in their sight … before
  their eyes"* (21:8) — the sight Job lost at 1:19. *"Let his own eyes see his destruction"* (21:20):
  punishment should be seen by the one punished. And the witnesses who have seen the world, the
  *"wayfaring men"* (21:29), against the friends who have only read about it.
- [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]] — Job 22:12–14; **a God who cannot see, put in the mouth of a man who
  complains that God never stops looking.** *"You say, 'What does God know? Can he judge through the
  thick darkness?'"* — the practical atheist's creed of Psalm 10:11, 73:11 and 94:7, attributed to
  the Job who called God *"you watcher of men"* (7:20) and asked *"Don't you watch over my sin?"*
  (14:16). The *ʿărāpel* is Sinai's cloud (Exodus 20:21), where God is present, not absent.
- [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]] — Job 23:8–10; 24:13–17, 23; **the hidden God who
  sees.** Job looks in every direction and *"can't catch a glimpse of him"* (23:9) — *"But he knows
  the way that I take"* (23:10). In chapter 24 the adulterer says *"No eye shall see me"* (24:15),
  and Job answers *"His eyes are on their ways"* (24:23): against Eliphaz's misquotation (22:13), Job
  insists God does see — and does nothing. Psalm 139's inescapable God, turned inside out. See
  [[Where Can I Go from Your Spirit - Job 23.8-10 and Psalm 139.7-12]].
- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:7, 10, 21, 24, 27; **a search conducted as a contest of eyes.** The falcon's
  eye has not seen the miner's path (28:7); the miner's eye *"sees every precious thing"* (28:10);
  wisdom is *"hidden from the eyes of all living"* (28:21); God *"looks to the ends of the earth, and
  sees under the whole sky"* (28:24), and at the creation *"saw it"* (28:27). Only one eye sees far
  enough
- [[Job's Oath of Innocence]] — Job 31:1, 4, 26; the eye under covenant (*"how then should I look"*), God's eye on Job
  (*"Doesn't he see my ways, and count all my steps?"*), and the sun and moon that must not be seen as
  gods. Job guards his own seeing because he knows he is seen.
- [[Elihu on God's Justice]] — Job 34:21–22, 29; 35:14; **no darkness to hide in.** *"For his eyes are on the
  ways of a man. He sees all his goings. There is no darkness, nor thick gloom, where the workers of
  iniquity may hide themselves"* — the answer to Eliphaz's caricature of Job (*"Can he judge through the
  thick darkness?"*, 22:13) and to Job's adulterer (*"No eye shall see me"*, 24:15). *"When he hides his
  face, who then can see him?"* (34:29) concedes Job's 13:24 and calls it sovereignty; *"you say you don't
  see him"* (35:14) is 9:11 and 23:8–9.
- [[Elihu on God's Greatness]] — Job 36:7, 25; 37:21, 24; *"He doesn't withdraw his eyes from the
  righteous"* (36:7); *"All men have looked on it. Man sees it afar off"* (36:25); *"Now men don't see the
  light which is bright in the skies"* (37:21); and the last line, God *"doesn't regard"* — *lōʾ-yirʾeh*,
  does not see — the wise of heart (37:24).
- [[I Repent in Dust and Ashes]] — Job 42:5; **the ear exchanged for the eye.** *"I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now
  my eye sees you."* The dialogue's *"I don't see him"* (9:11; 23:9) ends, and the hope of 19:26–27, *"My eyes
  shall see"*, is met before death. Nothing in chapters 38–41 is described as seen; what Job calls seeing is
  being addressed. See [[Now My Eye Sees You - Hearing and Seeing in Job]].
- [[The Restoration of Job]] — Job 42:16; **the second seeing.** *"Saw his sons, and his sons' sons, to four
  generations"* — *rāʾâ* again, after *"now my eye sees you"* (42:5). Job had said the dead man's *"sons come to honor,
  and he doesn't know it"* (14:21), and that it was the wicked whose children were *"established with them in their
  sight"* (21:8). He is given the sight.
- [[The Birth of Moses]] — Exodus 2:2–6; **a passage built of looking.** The mother *sees that he is
  good* (*kî-ṭôb*, Genesis 1:4's verdict); the sister stands far off *to know* what will be done;
  the princess *sees* the basket, opens it, *sees* the child — and then hears him. See
  [[Watching from Far Off - Hagar and Moses' Sister]]
- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — Exodus 2:11–12, 25; **the chapter's first and last seeing.** Moses *"looked at their
  burdens"* and *saw* an Egyptian strike a Hebrew, then *"looked this way and that way"* and saw *no
  man* — seeing used to make sure he is not seen. The chapter closes on ***"God saw the children of
  Israel"*** and ***God knew*** (2:25): the seeing that is not hidden, and that ends the bondage
- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:2–9; **the encounter waits on a man's curiosity.** *"I will turn aside and see this
  great sight"*; *"When Yahweh saw that he turned aside to see, God called"*. Then Moses hides his face,
  *"for he was afraid to look at God"*, and God says he has seen: *rāʾōh rāʾîtî*, *"I have surely seen
  the affliction of my people"* — the narrator's *"God saw"* of 2:25 in God's own mouth. **The one who
  may not be looked at is the one who looks**
- [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] — Exodus 4:31; ***"he had seen their affliction"*** — **3:7 in the third person, and
  now heard by the people.** God's seeing is the reason they bow and worship
- [[Bricks Without Straw]] — Exodus 5:21; 6:1; ***"May Yahweh look at you"*** and ***"Now you shall
  see"*** — **the officers ask God to see Moses; God tells Moses that he will see.** *ʿattâ tirʾeh*
  answers the complaint with a promise of sight
- [[Locusts and Darkness]] — Exodus 10:5, 23, 28–29; **a land that cannot be seen, people who cannot see each other,
  and a face no longer to be seen.** The locusts cover *"the eye of the land"* *"so that one won't be
  able to see the earth"* (10:5). In the darkness *"They didn't see one another"* (10:23). Then Pharaoh:
  *"Be careful to see my face no more; for in the day you see my face you shall die!"*, and Moses: *"I
  will see your face again no more"* (10:28–29). **Pharaoh claims for his face what God says of his
  own**, *"man may not see me and live"* (33:20), and Jacob at Peniel saw God's face and lived (Genesis
  32:30). The echo is verbal; whether it is meant is not shown
- [[The Crossing of the Sea]] — Exodus 13:17; 14:10, 13, 30–31; **the chapter turns on *rāʾâ*.** *"Lest …
  they see war"*; *"they lifted up their eyes, and behold"*; *"Stand still, and see the salvation of Yahweh …
  the Egyptians whom you have seen today, you shall never see them again"*; then *"Israel saw the Egyptians
  dead"* and *"Israel saw the great work"*. **They see them again, once, dead**; the text records both
  statements. And Yahweh *"looked out"* on the army from the pillar (*šāqap*, 14:24), the verb of the look
  toward Sodom (Genesis 18:16; 19:28; WLC)
- [[Israel at Mount Sinai]] — Exodus 19:11, 17–21; an announced public descent and a prohibited rush to gaze
- [[The Blood of the Covenant]] — Exodus 24:10–11; *"They saw the God of Israel"*, and ate and drank

- [[The Work Completed]] — Exodus 39:32–43; Moses’ seeing assesses conformity rather than recording a vision.
- [[The Lamp of the Commandment]] — Proverbs 6:25; beauty coveted in the heart, and eyelids that
  capture
- [[The Young Man Void of Understanding]] — Proverbs 7:2, 6–7; the father at the window, and the apple of the eye

## Related themes

- [[The Names of God]] — *ʾĒl Rŏʾî* is the only name in Scripture given to God by a human being, and
  it is a name about sight
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — *maḥăzeh* and *ḥāzôn*, the seer's side of the same root
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — the person the household will not look at is the one God finds
- [[Judgment]] — God's seeing is the first move of every judgment in Genesis
- [[Remembrance]] — its close partner: God remembers and God sees, and both mean he is about to act
- [[Suffering]] — being unseen is half of what the sufferers in Scripture complain of
