# Theft and Restitution

## Definition / overview

Taking what belongs to someone else, and what is owed for it afterward. The Hebrew verb is
***gānab***, *to steal*. It is the verb of the commandment *"You shall not steal"* (Exodus 20:15;
*lōʾ tignōb*, Hebrew 20:13), and its nouns name the *thief* (*gannāb*) and the *theft* (*gənēbâ*)
in Exodus 22:2–3 (all checked in the *Miqra according to the Masorah* edition via Sefaria).

**Scripture treats theft as two things at once: a wrong done to a neighbor and a debt.** The law
does not treat it as settled by punishing the thief alone. Restitution goes to the person robbed,
and for theft it is multiplied — double, or four or five times — rather than matched, as bodily
injury is in Exodus 21:23–25. The verb also reaches past property. Genesis uses it of a person
taken (Joseph, 40:15), of household gods (31:19, 30), and of a mind deceived (31:20, where the WEB's
*"Jacob deceived Laban"* renders *wayyignōb yaʿăqōb ʾet-lēb lābān*, literally *Jacob stole Laban's
heart*).

## Development across Scripture

### Genesis — the verb before the law

Theft in Genesis is almost always an accusation, often disputed, and seldom settled. Jacob offers
to let any plain-coloured animal in his flock *"be considered stolen"* (30:33, *gānûb*); Rachel
steals her father's teraphim (31:19); Jacob *steals Laban's heart* by leaving without telling him
(31:20); and Laban's *"why have you stolen my gods?"* (31:30) leads to a search and a rash oath
(31:32). Jacob's speech afterward lists what he paid for that was *"stolen by day or stolen by
night"* (31:39, *gənubtî*). See [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]] and
[[The Shepherd's Liability - Genesis 31.39 and the Law of Exodus 22]].

Joseph says *"I was stolen away out of the land of the Hebrews"* (40:15, *gunnōb gunnabtî*) — a
kidnapping described with the theft verb, which Exodus 21:16 makes a capital crime. See
[[Kidnapping and Sale - Joseph and the Law of Exodus 21]]. And the brothers, asked about a cup they
did not take, protest *"How then should we steal silver or gold out of your lord's house?"* (44:8)
and propose death for the one *"with whom it is found"* (44:9), which Joseph turns into slavery for
*"the man in whose hand the cup is found"* (44:17). See [[The Silver Cup and Judah's Plea]] and
[[Found in His Hand - The Silver Cup and the Theft Law of Exodus 22]].

### Exodus — a commandment, then its cases

The eighth commandment states the prohibition without a penalty (20:15). Its cases follow in
[[Laws of Property and Restitution]] (22:1–15): fivefold and fourfold repayment for a slaughtered
or sold animal; double for one found alive in the thief's hand; sale into servitude for a thief who
cannot pay; double from a thief caught with a deposit; and the same double payment for anyone
*"whom God condemns"* in a disputed claim (22:9). The cases also separate theft from loss without
dishonesty — grazing, fire, an animal's death, a predator's kill — where restitution is owed for
negligence or not at all. **Liability follows what a person could have prevented, not only what
they meant to do.**

A thief struck dead while breaking in brings no guilt of bloodshed, but *"if the sun has risen on
him"* it does (22:2–3). **On the usual reading, a thief's life is protected once he can be seen and
made to pay** — the text names only the sunrise, not the reason.

### Later Scripture — the fourfold rate remembered

The fourfold rate for a sheep comes back twice where no court applies it: David's verdict on
Nathan's rich man, *"He must restore the lamb fourfold"* (2 Samuel 12:6), and Zacchaeus' promise,
*"If I have wrongfully exacted anything of anyone, I restore four times as much"* (Luke 19:8).
Proverbs raises the figure to *"seven times"* and *"all the wealth of his house"* (Proverbs 6:31).
See [[Fourfold Restitution - Exodus 22, Nathan's Parable, and Zacchaeus]]. Jesus uses a thief
breaking into a house as an image for an unexpected coming (Matthew 24:43), and contrasts the thief
who *"only comes to steal, kill, and destroy"* with himself (John 10:10). See
[[The Thief Breaking In - Exodus 22 and Matthew 24]].

### The hungry thief — Proverbs 6:30–31

The context of Proverbs' sevenfold figure is now ingested. *"Men don't despise a thief, if he steals
to satisfy himself when he is hungry: but if he is found, he shall restore seven times. He shall
give all the wealth of his house"* (6:30–31). The verb is the law's, *yəšallēm*, but the figure
*šiḇʿāṯāyim* is not the rate of Exodus 22:1, 4, and the wiki reads it as the proverbial number of
full repayment. The thief is the lesser case in an argument from lesser to greater: even the most
excusable thief pays, and he *can* pay; the adulterer of 6:32–35 has taken what no payment returns.
See [[The Lamp of the Commandment]] and
[[Fourfold Restitution - Exodus 22, Nathan's Parable, and Zacchaeus]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs
6; Hebrew read from Sefaria's *Miqra according to the Masorah*, 2026-10-25.
