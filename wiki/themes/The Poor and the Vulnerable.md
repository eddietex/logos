# The Poor and the Vulnerable

## Definition / overview

**The alien, the widow, the fatherless, and the poor are the people with no one to protect
them.** The *gēr* (alien) has no land or kin in the place where he lives; the *ʾalmānâ* (widow) and
the *yātôm* (fatherless child) have lost the man whose household protected them; the *ʿānî* (poor)
has nothing to borrow against but the cloak he sleeps in. **The law groups them together because
they share one condition: if they are wronged, there is no one who will answer for them.**

**The Torah's answer is that God will.** The Book of the Covenant names them for the first time as
a group and puts God in the place of the missing protector: *"If you take advantage of them at all,
and they cry at all to me, I will surely hear their cry"* (Exodus 22:23). The page tracks that
answer and the vocabulary it is written in. Hospitality to strangers is treated separately at
[[Hospitality]]; the alien's legal standing at [[Land and Sojourning]] and
[[One Law for the Native and the Stranger]].

## Development across Scripture

### Genesis — the condition before the law

**Genesis shows the condition without naming it as a category.** Abram's offspring are told they
will be *gēr* in a land not theirs and afflicted there (Genesis 15:13); see
[[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]. [[Tamar]] is sent to *"Remain a widow in your father's house"*
(Genesis 38:11) and left there by the man responsible for her, until she takes off *"the garments
of her widowhood"* (38:14) and makes him answer; see [[Judah and Tamar]]. **No law protects either;
the narrative shows what their lack of protection costs.**

### Job — the righteous man's defence

**In Job the treatment of the widow and fatherless is the test of a righteous man**, argued from
both sides. [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] accuses Job: *"You have sent widows away empty, and the arms
of the fatherless have been broken"* (Job 22:9); see [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]]. Job describes the
wicked who *"drive away the donkey of the fatherless, and they take the widow's ox for a pledge"*
(24:3; [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]), recalls that he *"delivered the poor who cried,
and the fatherless also, who had no one to help him"* (29:12; [[The Months of Old]]), and swears
that he never let *"the eyes of the widow"* fail or lifted his hand *"against the fatherless"*
(31:16, 21; [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]). **Job 29:12's *"the poor who cried"* is the situation
Exodus 22:23 legislates for.**

### Exodus 22 — the group named, and God as its guarantor

**[[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] (Exodus 22:21–27) is the first place the law names them
together.** Three features define the theme from here on:

- **The motive is Israel's own history.** *"You shall not wrong an alien or oppress him, for you
  were aliens in the land of Egypt"* (22:21). The verbs forbidden — *lāḥaṣ*, *oppress*, and
  *ʿinnâ*, *afflict* — are the verbs of Egypt's oppression of Israel (Exodus 1:11; 3:9; Genesis
  15:13; MAM). **Israel is forbidden to become Egypt.**
- **God takes the protector's place.** The widow's or orphan's cry goes to God, who says he will
  hear it; the penalty is measured on the offence, *"your wives shall be widows, and your children
  fatherless"* (22:24). See
  [[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]].
- **The poor are God's people first.** The borrower is *"any of my people with you who is poor"*
  (22:25), lent to without interest, and his cloak, taken as a pledge, goes back before sunset,
  *"for I am gracious"* (22:27). See
  [[Lending Without Interest - Exodus 22.25 and the Poor Brother]].

### Justice and access to food — Exodus 23:1–19

The poor must neither be favored simply for being poor nor denied justice (23:3, 6). Their
protection therefore includes a truthful verdict as well as relief. The seventh year's fallow
produce is food for them, with the remainder for animals (23:10–11); the alien is protected by
remembered experience and refreshed by weekly rest (23:9, 12). Provision and impartiality are both
required.

See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30:1–38

The census ransom is the same for everyone: *"The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not
give less, than the half shekel"* (30:15). Where the law of Exodus 22–23 protects the poor from
exploitation, this law makes their life cost exactly what a rich man's costs before God. See
[[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]].

### Later law and the prophets (not yet ingested)

Deuteronomy restates the pledge law and calls its return *"righteousness to you before Yahweh"*
(Deuteronomy 24:13); Amos accuses those who *"lay themselves down beside every altar on clothes taken
in pledge"* (Amos 2:8). These are recorded from the WEB, fetched individually, and will be expanded
when the books are ingested.

### Due good must not be withheld — Proverbs 3:21–35

Proverbs 3:27–28 prohibits withholding good owed or delaying assistance already within
reach. The recipient is not explicitly identified as poor, so the command should not be
restricted to almsgiving. Applying it to vulnerable people is supported by comparison
with Exodus 22:26–27's pledged garment and Job 31:16–23's claim to have fed and clothed
the needy. The connection is thematic, not a claim of direct quotation.

See [[Do Not Withhold Good]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 3; comparisons as cited above.
The discussion added here makes no claim about exact source-language forms.

### A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father — Proverbs 10:4, 15

Verse 4 warns that laziness brings poverty; verse 15 recognizes poverty itself as destructive
without assigning blame. Job 24:5–11 explicitly depicts working people who go hungry. These claims
distinguish a warning against neglecting work from a verdict that every poor person has neglected
it.

See [[A Wise Son Makes a Glad Father]]. Source: Proverbs 10 and the cited Proverbs/Job comparisons
checked in stored WEB, 2026-10-25.

### A False Balance — Proverbs 11:24–26

Excessive withholding brings poverty, while generosity and the release of grain sustain others.
Verse 26 blesses selling grain, not only donating it, and does not explicitly name a famine. The
observation concerns access to necessities; it does not justify calling every poor person a hoarder.

See [[A False Balance]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 11; comparisons are documented on the passage
and connection pages.

### Correction, speech, and daily conduct — Proverbs 12:9, 11, 24

The low-status person with provision is preferred to someone who honors himself but lacks bread.
Diligence is commended, yet Job 24:5–11’s exploited workers prevent hunger from becoming proof of
laziness; Exodus 1:11–14 similarly prevents forced labor from becoming a verdict on its victims.
These are comparative limits, not an explicit discussion of every cause of poverty in Proverbs 12.

See [[Whoever Loves Correction]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 12 and the comparisons documented on
that passage page.

### Injustice sweeps it away — Proverbs 13:7–8, 18, 23

The poor *"hear no threats"* (8), and refusing discipline brings poverty (18). Proverbs 13:23 then names a different cause: an abundance of food *"in poor people’s fields"* swept away by *injustice*. This verse forbids reversing 13:18 into a diagnosis of every poor person.

See [[Hope Deferred]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 13.

### Shunned, pitied, and made by God — Proverbs 14:20–21, 31

Verse 20 observes that the poor are shunned even by neighbours; verse 21 rules that despising a neighbour is sin and pity for the poor is blessed. Verse 31 grounds the duty in creation: *"He who oppresses the poor shows contempt for his Maker, but he who is kind to the needy honors him."* See [[Contempt for His Maker - Proverbs 14.31 and Job 31.13-15]] and [[Shunned by His Neighbor - Proverbs 14.20 and Job 19.13-19]].

See [[A Way That Seems Right]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 14.

### The widow's borders — Proverbs 15:15, 25

*"Yahweh will uproot the house of the proud, but he will keep the widow's borders intact"* (25): the God who hears the widow's cry (Exodus 22:22–23) keeps her field's boundary, the thing Job 24:2–3 sees the powerful move. Verse 15's afflicted, whose days are *"wretched"*, is described without being blamed. See [[The Widow's Borders - Proverbs 15.25 and the Ancient Boundary Stone]].

See [[A Gentle Answer]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 15.

### Mocking the poor — Proverbs 17:5

*"Whoever mocks the poor reproaches his Maker."* 14:31 said it of oppression; 17:5 says it of mockery, with the same predicate, *ḥērēp ʿōśēhû* (Hebrew from Sefaria). See [[Contempt for His Maker - Proverbs 14.31 and Job 31.13-15]].

See [[A Dry Morsel with Quietness]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 17.

### The poor plead — Proverbs 18:23

*"The poor plead for mercy, but the rich answer harshly"* (23) records without approving. The
*pleas* are *taḥănûnîm*, the noun of Job 41:3's Leviathan, who will not *"make many petitions"* (Hebrew
from Sefaria); the rich man's *harshly* (*ʿazzôt*) belongs to the chapter's vocabulary of the strong
city (11, 19).

See [[A Strong Tower]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 18.

### Integrity, counsel, and compassion — Proverbs 19:1, 4, 7, 17, 22

Verses 4 and 7 describe the poor losing friends and relatives; verses 1 and 22 deny that their
poverty discredits their character. Verse 17 supplies the response: pity for the poor is lending to
Yahweh, who will reward the giver. Social abandonment is described, not prescribed. The chapter's
warning about idleness (15) cannot explain all the poverty it names. See
[[Shunned by His Neighbor - Proverbs 14.20 and Job 19.13-19]] and
[[Lends to Yahweh - Proverbs 19.17, Exodus 22, and Job 31]] .

See [[Better Is the Poor Who Walks in Integrity]] . Source: stored WEB Proverbs 19 and the
comparisons documented on that passage page.

### Discernment and accountability — Proverbs 20:4, 13, 16

Verses 4 and 13 locate poverty in neglected labor and sleep in the cases they describe.
Proverbs 13:23 locates lost food in injustice, so the cause cannot be generalized to all
poverty. Verse 16's seizure of a guarantor's garment must also remain beside Exodus 22:26–27's
protection of the debtor's necessary covering. No overnight retention is authorized by the
proverb's silence.

See [[Wine Is a Mocker]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 20; supporting comparisons are documented on the
passage page.

### The cry of the poor — Proverbs 21:13

Whoever stops his ears at the cry of the poor will cry and not be heard: measure for measure. The
saying warns the one who ignores the poor; it does not diagnose every unanswered cry, as Job's
unheard cry (Job 19:7; 30:20) shows. See
[[Lends to Yahweh - Proverbs 19.17, Exodus 22, and Job 31]].

See [[The King's Heart]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 21; supporting comparisons are documented on
the passage page.

### One Maker — Proverbs 22:2, 7, 9, 16

*"The rich and the poor have this in common: Yahweh is the maker of them all"* (22:2). The
collection's last word on the poor joins observation (the borrower serves the lender, 7) to
judgment: the generous eye that shares food with the poor is blessed (9), and whoever oppresses the
poor for gain comes to poverty (16). The common Maker is the ground of 14:31 and 17:5 stated
without its consequence.

See [[A Good Name]]. Source: stored WEB Proverbs 22; no new source-language claim.
