# The Poor and the Vulnerable

## Definition / overview

**The alien, the widow, the fatherless, and the poor are the people with no one to protect
them.** The *gēr* (alien) has no land or kin in the place where he lives; the *ʾalmānâ* (widow) and
the *yātôm* (fatherless child) have lost the man whose household protected them; the *ʿānî* (poor)
has nothing to borrow against but the cloak he sleeps in. **The law groups them together because
they share one condition: if they are wronged, there is no one who will answer for them.**

**The Torah's answer is that God will.** The Book of the Covenant names them for the first time as
a group and puts God in the place of the missing protector: *"If you take advantage of them at all,
and they cry at all to me, I will surely hear their cry"* (Exodus 22:23). The page tracks that
answer and the vocabulary it is written in. Hospitality to strangers is treated separately at
[[Hospitality]]; the alien's legal standing at [[Land and Sojourning]] and
[[One Law for the Native and the Stranger]].

## Development across Scripture

### Genesis — the condition before the law

**Genesis shows the condition without naming it as a category.** Abram's offspring are told they
will be *gēr* in a land not theirs and afflicted there (Genesis 15:13); see
[[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]. [[Tamar]] is sent to *"Remain a widow in your father's house"*
(Genesis 38:11) and left there by the man responsible for her, until she takes off *"the garments
of her widowhood"* (38:14) and makes him answer; see [[Judah and Tamar]]. **No law protects either;
the narrative shows what their lack of protection costs.**

### Job — the righteous man's defence

**In Job the treatment of the widow and fatherless is the test of a righteous man**, argued from
both sides. [[Eliphaz the Temanite]] accuses Job: *"You have sent widows away empty, and the arms
of the fatherless have been broken"* (Job 22:9); see [[Eliphaz's Third Speech]]. Job describes the
wicked who *"drive away the donkey of the fatherless, and they take the widow's ox for a pledge"*
(24:3; [[Oh That I Knew Where I Might Find Him]]), recalls that he *"delivered the poor who cried,
and the fatherless also, who had no one to help him"* (29:12; [[The Months of Old]]), and swears
that he never let *"the eyes of the widow"* fail or lifted his hand *"against the fatherless"*
(31:16, 21; [[Job's Oath of Innocence]]). **Job 29:12's *"the poor who cried"* is the situation
Exodus 22:23 legislates for.**

### Exodus 22 — the group named, and God as its guarantor

**[[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]] (Exodus 22:21–27) is the first place the law names them
together.** Three features define the theme from here on:

- **The motive is Israel's own history.** *"You shall not wrong an alien or oppress him, for you
  were aliens in the land of Egypt"* (22:21). The verbs forbidden — *lāḥaṣ*, *oppress*, and
  *ʿinnâ*, *afflict* — are the verbs of Egypt's oppression of Israel (Exodus 1:11; 3:9; Genesis
  15:13; MAM). **Israel is forbidden to become Egypt.**
- **God takes the protector's place.** The widow's or orphan's cry goes to God, who says he will
  hear it; the penalty is measured on the offence, *"your wives shall be widows, and your children
  fatherless"* (22:24). See
  [[A Great Cry - The Outcry of the Oppressed and the Cry of Egypt]].
- **The poor are God's people first.** The borrower is *"any of my people with you who is poor"*
  (22:25), lent to without interest, and his cloak, taken as a pledge, goes back before sunset,
  *"for I am gracious"* (22:27). See
  [[Lending Without Interest - Exodus 22.25 and the Poor Brother]].

The next unit continues the subject in the courtroom and the field: *"You shall not deny justice to
your poor people in their lawsuits"* (23:6); *"you know the heart of an alien"* (23:9); and the
seventh year's fallow land is left *"that the poor of your people may eat"* (23:11). Those verses
belong to the next ingest and are noted here so the thread is not lost.

### Later law and the prophets (not yet ingested)

Deuteronomy restates the pledge law and calls its return *"righteousness to you before Yahweh"*
(Deuteronomy 24:13); Amos accuses those who *"lay themselves down beside every altar on clothes taken
in pledge"* (Amos 2:8). These are recorded from the WEB, fetched individually, and will be expanded
when the books are ingested.
