# Image of God

## Definition / overview

*"God created man in his own image. In God’s image he created him; male and female he created
them."* (Genesis 1:27). The Hebrew *tselem* (image) and *demut* (likeness) name humanity's
defining status: of everything God makes, only humanity is said to resemble him.

The text does not define what the image consists of, and the major readings each pick up
something the passage does supply:

- **Functional** — the image is the *job*: image-bearers rule on God's behalf. In the ancient Near
  East a king set up his *tselem* in a province to represent his rule there, and 1:26 pairs the
  image directly with dominion. On this reading, humanity is God's representative presence in his
  world.
- **Relational** — the image is *male and female* together (1:27), and God speaks of himself in
  the plural in the same breath (1:26). Humanity images God in relationship, not in isolation.
- **Substantial** — the image is some capacity humans possess: reason, moral agency, spirit.
  The oldest and most common reading in the tradition, though it is the one the text says least
  about.

What the passage does state plainly: the image belongs to humanity as such, male and female
alike, before any distinction of nation, righteousness, or ability — and it is the ground of the
dominion given in 1:28, not a reward for exercising it.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 1:26–28** — the image granted, tied to dominion and to male-and-female.
- **Genesis 2:19–20** — the vocabulary is absent, but the *functional* reading gets its first
  demonstration: God brings the animals to the man *"to see what he would call them"*, and
  whatever he calls them is their name. Dominion looks like naming — knowing and ordering — and
  God defers to the result. The *relational* reading gets its counterpart in 2:18–24, where
  aloneness is the one thing in creation called "not good".
- To trace as ingestion reaches them: Genesis 5:1–3 (Adam fathers a son "in his own likeness,
  after his image" — the image passes down), Genesis 9:6 (the image as the reason murder is
  capital), Psalm 8:4–8, 1 Corinthians 11:7, Colossians 3:10, James 3:9, and the New Testament's
  turn to Christ as *the* image (2 Corinthians 4:4, Colossians 1:15, Hebrews 1:3) into which
  people are remade (Romans 8:29).
