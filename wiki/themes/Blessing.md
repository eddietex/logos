# Blessing

## Definition / overview

God's act of granting the power to flourish. In Genesis 1 blessing is not a wish or a mood but a
performative word that confers capacity: what God blesses becomes able to increase, to fill, and
to be fruitful.

The first three blessings in Scripture all fall in this opening pericope, and they set the range
the word will keep:

- **1:22** — sea creatures and birds: *"Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the
  seas."* Blessing is given to animals before it is given to people; it is not a human privilege.
- **1:28** — humanity: the same words, plus *subdue* and *have dominion*. Blessing here carries
  responsibility with it.
- **2:3** — the seventh day: God *blesses* it and *makes it holy*. A span of time can be blessed,
  and what a blessed day produces is [[Sabbath]] rest rather than offspring.

Genesis will spend the rest of its length on what happens to this blessing when it collides with
human failure — and on the counter-word, *curse*, which arrives in [[The Fall]] and now has its
own page: [[Curse]]. Worth noting how the two interact rather than simply cancel. The blessing of
1:28 is never revoked in Genesis 3: the man and woman still multiply, still work, still rule — but
each of those now runs through pain, sweat, and resistance. Blessing is not withdrawn; it is made
costly.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 1:22, 1:28, 2:3** — creation blessing: fruitfulness, dominion, holy rest.
- **Genesis 3:14–19** — the first curses, aimed at the serpent and the ground, running against the
  creation blessing without cancelling it.
- **Genesis 5:1–32** — the clearest demonstration that the blessing was not revoked. The register of
  Adam's generations opens by restating it (*"He created them male and female, and blessed them"*,
  5:2) and then spends thirty verses being it: nine men who *"became the father of other sons and
  daughters"*, generation after generation, is what *be fruitful and multiply* looks like from
  inside. That the same entries end in *"then he died"* is the point — 1:28 and 3:19 running at once,
  neither cancelling the other. See [[From Adam to Noah]].
- **Genesis 6:1** — and then the blessing becomes the setting for the disaster. *"When men began to
  multiply on the surface of the ground, and daughters were born to them"* is 1:28 succeeding at
  scale, in the same verb God commanded, and the sentence turns in its second half into the crossing
  of 6:2. Genesis is careful about what it does and does not say here: the multiplying is not the
  fault and is never criticized, and God's response in 6:3 and 6:7 is aimed at wickedness, not at
  fruitfulness. But the book has now shown the blessing being carried out (Genesis 5) and shown the
  world it produced being condemned (Genesis 6:5), four verses apart. See
  [[The Sons of God and the Corruption of the Earth]].
- To trace as ingestion reaches them: the blessing renewed to Noah after the flood (9:1), the
  narrowing of blessing to one family and its widening back out to *"all the families of the
  earth"* (12:1–3), the patriarchal blessings that drive the Jacob narrative (27, 48, 49), and
  the covenant blessings-and-curses of Deuteronomy 28.
