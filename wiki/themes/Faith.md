# Faith

## Definition / overview

Leaning your weight on someone and finding they hold.

The Hebrew root is **ʾ-m-n**, whose basic physical sense is *firmness* — a nursing father carrying a
child (Numbers 11:12), a doorpost (2 Kings 18:16), a peg driven into a sure place (Isaiah 22:23). Out
of it come *ʾĕmûnâ*, faithfulness or reliability; *ʾĕmet*, truth in the sense of *dependability*; the
hiphil verb *heʾĕmin*, to treat as reliable, to trust, to believe; and **amen**, which is not a
sign-off but a word meaning *that holds*. The vocabulary is about load-bearing before it is about
opinion, and this is the single most important thing to carry into the New Testament's use of it: to
believe God in Hebrew is not primarily to hold a proposition true but to put weight on a person.

**The verb's first occurrence in the Bible is Genesis 15:6** — *"He believed in Yahweh, who credited
it to him for righteousness"* — and everything about the setting is worth holding on to:

- **What is believed is a promise, not a doctrine.** Abram has been shown the stars and told his
  offspring will be like them. He is childless, seventy-five or older, and married to a woman Genesis
  11:30 has already called barren. Faith's founding instance is a man taking God's word for something
  the evidence is flatly against.
- **It is not certainty, and the chapter proves it four verses later.** *"Lord Yahweh, how will I know
  that I will inherit it?"* (15:8) — a request for proof from the man just credited with
  righteousness for believing. Genesis sets the two beside each other and does not reconcile them.
  Whatever faith is in Scripture, 15:6 and 15:8 are compatible.
- **It is credited, not achieved.** *Wayyaḥšəbehā llô ṣədāqâ* uses an accounting verb: something is
  entered in a column. See [[Righteousness]].
- **And it precedes everything.** No law exists, no circumcision (Genesis 17), no sacrifice, no
  temple, no Israel. Paul's whole argument in Romans 4 and Galatians 3 is built on that chronology
  rather than on the word's meaning.

**The theme was there before the word was.** Genesis has been narrating trust with verbs of obedience
since 6:22 — *"Thus Noah did. He did all that God commanded him"* — and 12:4's
*"So Abram went, as Yahweh had told him"* is faith with no interior described at all. Hebrews 11
reads the whole sequence back as faith and supplies the interior Genesis withholds. What Genesis 15:6
adds is not the behaviour but the **word for it**, and a verdict attached.

## Development across Scripture

- **Genesis 6:22; 7:5** — obedience narrated without a word for it: Noah builds. Hebrews 11:7 calls it
  faith. See [[Noah and the Flood]].
- **Genesis 12:1–4** — the command and *"So Abram went"*, with nothing said about what he thought.
  See [[The Call of Abram]].
- **Genesis 15:6** — *heʾĕmin*'s first occurrence, and the verse the New Testament argues from more
  than any other in the Torah. See [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] and
  [[Credited to Him for Righteousness - Genesis 15.6 in Paul and James]].
- **Genesis 15:8** — and a request for a sign, from the same man, in the same conversation.
- **Genesis 16:1–4; 17:17; 18:12** — what the same faith looks like from the other side: Hagar taken
  as a workaround, and both parents laughing at the promise. Genesis never treats 15:6 as settling
  Abram's behaviour for the rest of his life.
- **Exodus 4:31; 14:31** — Israel *believes* at the start and after the sea; **Numbers 14:11; 20:12**
  — and does not, twice, at the cost of a generation and of Moses's entry.
- **2 Chronicles 20:20** — a pun the English cannot carry: *"Believe in Yahweh your God, so you will
  be established"* is *haʾămînû … wə-tēʾāmēnû*, the same root twice. Trust and stability are one word.
- **Isaiah 7:9** — the same pun as a threat to Ahaz: *"If you will not believe, surely you will not be
  established."*
- **Habakkuk 2:4** — *"the righteous will live by his faith"*, where the Hebrew *ʾĕmûnâ* means the
  righteous man's **faithfulness** and the Greek *pistis* Paul quotes admits both senses. Romans 1:17,
  Galatians 3:11, and Hebrews 10:38 all build on it, and the shift of sense between the Hebrew and
  the Greek is the single most consequential translation event in the Bible.
- **Psalm 78:22, 32** — the wilderness generation's unbelief as the psalter's standing example.
- **Mark 9:24** — *"I believe. Help my unbelief!"*, the New Testament's most exact description of what
  Genesis 15:6 and 15:8 look like in one person.
- **John 3:16; 20:31** — believing as the Fourth Gospel's single verb for the response it wants, used
  about a hundred times and almost never as the noun.
- **Romans 4:1–25** — Genesis 15:6 quoted twice, with the chronology of Genesis 17 doing the work:
  Abraham was credited before circumcision, so he is father of the uncircumcised too.
- **Galatians 3:6–29** — 15:6 again, plus the argument that the promise cannot be annulled by a law
  four hundred thirty years later.
- **Hebrews 11** — faith defined as *"assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen"* and
  then illustrated by sixteen people who mostly did not see the thing.
- **James 2:14–26** — 15:6 quoted for the opposite conclusion, with Genesis 22 as the works that
  complete it. The New Testament argues with itself over one verse, using that verse.

## Key passages

- [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] — Genesis 15:6, the word's first occurrence and the theme's
  founding text; and 15:8, the request for a sign that the same chapter does not treat as its
  opposite
- [[The Call of Abram]] — Genesis 12:4: faith as a verb of motion, with no interior reported
- [[Hagar and Ishmael]] — Genesis 16, the counterweight, and the reason this theme cannot be read off
  15:6 alone. The man credited with righteousness for believing a promise about his own body spends
  the next chapter carrying out his wife's plan to secure it by other means, without one recorded
  word — and Genesis places the two chapters back to back and passes no comment on either. Whatever
  *heʾĕmin* meant at 15:6, it plainly did not mean a settled state that made 16:2 impossible.
  Hebrews 11:11–12 and Romans 4:19–21 tell the story with chapter 16 left out; Genesis does not
- [[Circumcision, the Sign of the Covenant]] — Genesis 17:17 and 17:23 in one chapter. The man
  credited with righteousness for believing laughs, face down, at the arithmetic of the same promise
  once it is given a number — and is not rebuked; then he circumcises his entire household and himself
  the same day, without a recorded word. Genesis puts the laugh and the obedience four verses apart
  and comments on neither
- [[Noah and the Flood]] — obedience before there is a word for it, and what Hebrews 11:7 calls it
- [[Credited to Him for Righteousness - Genesis 15.6 in Paul and James]] — the whole downstream
  argument, including the half of it that was already in Psalm 106:31
- [[Blameless Before Anyone Is Watching]] — Job 1:1 and Genesis 6:9, and what a verdict about a man
  is worth before he has been tested
