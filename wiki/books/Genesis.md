# Genesis

## Overview

Genesis ("beginning", from the Greek of 2:4) is the first book of the Torah and of the canon. It
moves from the origin of everything to the origin of one family: eleven chapters of primeval
history covering all humanity, then thirty-nine chapters following a single line — Abraham,
Isaac, Jacob, Joseph — chosen as the channel through which blessing returns to the nations it
lost. Its structural spine is the repeated formula *"These are the generations of…"* (Hebrew
*toledot*), which segments the book ten times and hands the narrative from one generation to the
next.

The book's central problem is set in its first chapters: a world made good, entrusted to
creatures who are God's image, and then fractured by their reach for autonomy. Everything after
Genesis 3 is the working-out of that fracture and of God's refusal to abandon it — through
judgment (flood, Babel), through covenant (Noah, Abraham), and through a providence that turns
even fratricidal betrayal into rescue (Joseph).

## Structure

- **1–11 Primeval history** — creation, fall, flood, and the scattering at Babel: all humanity.
- **12–25:18 Abraham** — the call, the covenant, and the long wait for a promised son.
- **25:19–36 Isaac and Jacob** — the promise carried through struggle, deception, and exile.
- **37–50 Joseph** — the family preserved in Egypt, setting up the bondage Exodus opens with.
