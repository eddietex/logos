# Ruth

## Overview

Ruth begins *In the days when the judges judged* (1:1) and ends with the name *David* (4:22). In the
English canon it stands between [[Judges]], which closes on *In those days there was no king in Israel*
(Judges 21:25), and 1 Samuel, where the king arrives; in the Hebrew Bible's traditional order it sits
among the Writings instead (reported, not argued). Its four chapters follow one family of Bethlehem Judah
from famine and death in Moab to a field, a threshing floor, a city gate, and a birth.

[[Famine and Death in Moab]] (1:1–5) sets out the problem in five verses. A famine in *the house of
bread* sends Elimelech, Naomi, and their two sons to sojourn in Moab, in the words of Abram's and Isaac's
famines (*There was a famine in the land*, Genesis 12:10; 26:1). Elimelech dies; the sons marry Moabite
women, Orpah and Ruth; after about ten years both sons die, and *the woman was left* without husband or
children. The narrator gives no cause and no judgment. Every loss in these verses is reversed by the end of
the book: bread returns to Bethlehem, a kinsman redeems the dead man's field and name, and Naomi takes
*the child* in her lap (4:16).

[[Ruth Clings to Naomi]] (1:6–18) is the first dialogue and the first turn. *Yahweh had visited his people in
giving them bread* (1:6), and Naomi sets out for home. On the road she sends her daughters-in-law back three
times, blessing them with *ḥeseḏ* and arguing from the levirate custom that she has no sons to give them.
Orpah *kissed her mother-in-law*; Ruth *joined with her* (1:14), *dābaq*, the verb of Genesis 2:24 and of
Deuteronomy's *cling to him*. Ruth's vow, *Your people will be my people, and your God my God* (1:16), ends
in a self-curse oath in Yahweh's name that only death will part them (1:17). The chapter's key word is
*šûb*, *return*, twelve times in the WLC of chapter 1.

[[Naomi Returns to Bethlehem]] (1:19–22) closes the first act. The town is stirred, *Is this Naomi?*, and
Naomi refuses her name: *Call me Mara; for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me* (1:20). *I went out
full, and Yahweh has brought me home again empty* (1:21). She names God as the subject of four verbs, using
*Shaddai*, the name Job will use, with the same verb, *hēmar* (Job 27:2). The narrator does not answer her.
It lists Ruth beside her, *Ruth the Moabitess … who returned*, and dates the arrival *in the beginning of
barley harvest* (1:22). The rest of the book answers the word *empty*: Boaz will not send Ruth back
*empty* (3:17), and the child will be *a restorer of life* (4:15).

[[Ruth Gleans in the Field of Boaz]] (2:1–23) is the harvest. The narrator tells the reader at once that
Naomi has a kinsman, Boaz, *a mighty man of wealth* (2:1), and then that Ruth *happened to come* to his field
(2:3), *miqreh*, *chance*. Boaz greets his reapers in Yahweh's name, protects and feeds the foreign gleaner,
has grain left for her on purpose, and blesses her as one who has come *under whose wings* to take refuge
(2:12). Ruth asks why she has found favor, *since I am a foreigner* (2:10). At evening Naomi blesses him by
Yahweh, *who has not abandoned his kindness to the living and to the dead*, and gives the book its key word:
he is *one of our near kinsmen*, a *gōʾēl* (2:20). The woman who came home *empty* is fed from what Ruth
*left* (2:14, 18).

[[Ruth at the Threshing Floor]] (3:1–18) is one night at the end of the harvest. Naomi, who prayed on the
road for *rest* for her daughters-in-law (1:9), now seeks it herself (3:1) and sends Ruth to Boaz on the
threshing floor, washed and dressed, to lie at his feet after he has eaten and drunk. At midnight Ruth does
not wait to be told what to do; she names herself and asks, *spread the corner of your garment over your
servant; for you are a near kinsman* (3:9), the *kānāp̄* of the *wings* Boaz blessed her by (2:12), joining
marriage to the redeemer's duty. Boaz blesses her, calls her *a worthy woman*, names a kinsman nearer than
he, and swears by Yahweh's life to redeem her if that man will not (3:10–13). The scene uses the verbs of
Lot's daughters' night (Genesis 19:33) and narrates only sleep. Ruth goes home before dawn with six measures
of barley — *Don't go empty to your mother-in-law* (3:17) — and Naomi tells her to wait: *the man will not
rest until he has settled this today* (3:18).

[[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]] (4:1–12) settles it the same morning, in public. Boaz sits at the gate, calls
the nearer kinsman over as *so-and-so* (*pəlōnî ʾalmōnî*, 4:1), seats ten elders, and offers him the
first right to Elimelech's land. The man will redeem the field; told that with it comes *Ruth the
Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance* (4:5), he declines,
*lest I endanger my own inheritance*, and draws off his shoe (4:6–8). Boaz takes the land and Ruth before
witnesses, *that the name of the dead may not be cut off* (4:10), and the town answers *We are witnesses*
and blesses the marriage by Rachel and Leah, *which both built the house of Israel*, and by *the house of
Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah* (4:11–12). Leviticus 25's redemption of land and Deuteronomy 25's
levirate name meet in one transaction, which the Torah nowhere joins.

[[The Birth of Obed]] (4:13–17) closes the story where it began, with Naomi. The marriage and the birth take
one verse: *Yahweh enabled her to conceive* (4:13), one of only two acts the narrator gives to Yahweh, the
other being the bread of 1:6. The women who asked *Is this Naomi?* (1:19) now bless Yahweh, *who has not left
you today without a near kinsman* — the *gōʾēl* is now the child — and promise that he will be *a restorer of
life* (*mēšîḇ*, from *šûḇ*, the verb of *brought me home again empty*, 1:21) and *sustain you in your old age*,
for Ruth, *who loves you, who is better to you than seven sons*, bore him (4:14–15). Naomi lays the child in
her bosom; the neighbor women name him Obed, *A son is born to Naomi*; and the last words are *He is the
father of Jesse, the father of David* (4:16–17).

## Structure

1. **Emptied** (1) — the losses in Moab, the parting on the road, and Naomi's return *empty*.
2. **The field** (2) — Ruth gleans in Boaz's field; Naomi names him a near kinsman.
3. **The threshing floor** (3) — Ruth asks Boaz to spread his garment over her; a nearer kinsman stands
   in the way.
4. **The gate** (4:1–12) — the redemption of the field and of Ruth before the elders.
5. **Filled** (4:13–22) — Obed's birth, the women's blessing of Naomi, and the generations of Perez to
   David.

- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — two widows without sons
- [[The Nations]] — a Moabite woman in the line of David
- [[Names and Naming]] — Naomi and Mara; *the name of the dead*
- [[Redemption]] — the near kinsman (2:20; 3–4)
- [[Marriage]] — levirate duty and redemption joined (3:9; 4:5, 10); *like Rachel and like Leah* (4:11)
- [[Love]] — *ḥeseḏ* (1:8; 2:20; 3:10) and Ruth's loyalty
