# Ruth

## Overview

Ruth begins *In the days when the judges judged* (1:1) and ends with the name *David* (4:22). In the
English canon it stands between [[Judges]], which closes on *In those days there was no king in Israel*
(Judges 21:25), and 1 Samuel, where the king arrives; in the Hebrew Bible's traditional order it sits
among the Writings instead (reported, not argued). Its four chapters follow one family of Bethlehem Judah
from famine and death in Moab to a field, a threshing floor, a city gate, and a birth.

[[Famine and Death in Moab]] (1:1–5) sets out the problem in five verses. A famine in *the house of
bread* sends Elimelech, Naomi, and their two sons to sojourn in Moab, in the words of Abram's and Isaac's
famines (*There was a famine in the land*, Genesis 12:10; 26:1). Elimelech dies; the sons marry Moabite
women, Orpah and Ruth; after about ten years both sons die, and *the woman was left* without husband or
children. The narrator gives no cause and no judgment. Every loss in these verses is reversed by the end of
the book: bread returns to Bethlehem, a kinsman redeems the dead man's field and name, and Naomi takes
*the child* in her lap (4:16).

[[Ruth Clings to Naomi]] (1:6–18) is the first dialogue and the first turn. *Yahweh had visited his people in
giving them bread* (1:6), and Naomi sets out for home. On the road she sends her daughters-in-law back three
times, blessing them with *ḥeseḏ* and arguing from the levirate custom that she has no sons to give them.
Orpah *kissed her mother-in-law*; Ruth *joined with her* (1:14), *dābaq*, the verb of Genesis 2:24 and of
Deuteronomy's *cling to him*. Ruth's vow, *Your people will be my people, and your God my God* (1:16), ends
in a self-curse oath in Yahweh's name that only death will part them (1:17). The chapter's key word is
*šûb*, *return*, twelve times in the WLC of chapter 1.

## Structure

1. **Emptied** (1) — the losses in Moab, the parting on the road, and Naomi's return *empty*.
2. **The field** (2) — Ruth gleans in Boaz's field; Naomi names him a near kinsman.
3. **The threshing floor** (3) — Ruth asks Boaz to spread his garment over her; a nearer kinsman stands
   in the way.
4. **The gate** (4:1–12) — the redemption of the field and of Ruth before the elders.
5. **Filled** (4:13–22) — Obed's birth, the women's blessing of Naomi, and the generations of Perez to
   David.

- [[The Poor and the Vulnerable]] — two widows without sons
