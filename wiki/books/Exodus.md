# Exodus

## Overview

Exodus is the book in which a family becomes a people, a slave population becomes a nation under
God, and the God who walked with patriarchs comes to live in a tent in the middle of a camp. It
begins with the sons of Israel *"who came into Egypt"* (1:1) and ends with the cloud covering the
Tent of Meeting and *"Yahweh's glory filled the tabernacle"* (40:34). Everything between is the
journey from the first sentence to the last: out of one house of service and into another.

**The book is built on a single Hebrew root, *ʿābad*, *to serve*.** In chapter 1 Egypt makes Israel
*serve* with *service* that is hard (1:13–14); the demand Moses carries to Pharaoh is *"Let my son
go, that he may serve me"* (4:23); and the first commandment opens by naming Yahweh as the God
*"who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage"* — *bêt ʿăbādîm*, the house
of servants (20:2). Exodus is not the story of Israel becoming free in the modern sense. It is the
story of Israel changing masters, and of what kind of master Yahweh turns out to be.

**It is also the sequel Genesis set up and did not deliver.** Genesis closes on a promise of land
unfulfilled and unrevoked, a coffin in Egypt, and Joseph's *"God will surely visit you"*
(Genesis 50:24–25). Abram was told his offspring would *"serve"* a foreign nation who would
*"afflict them four hundred years"*, and that God would judge that nation and bring them out *"with
great wealth"* (Genesis 15:13–14). Exodus opens by reprinting Genesis 46's list of the names and
the seventy souls, reports the multiplication in the creation blessing's own verbs, and then turns
the affliction loose — so the reader knows from the first chapter what is being kept and by whom.
See [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]] and
[[They Got Possessions and Multiplied - Genesis 47.27 and Exodus 1.7]].

The book divides roughly in half. **Chapters 1–18 are narrative**: oppression, Moses, the call,
the plagues, the Passover, the sea, and the wilderness as far as Sinai. **Chapters 19–40 are at the
mountain**: the covenant and its law, the instructions for the tabernacle, the golden calf and the
covenant's renewal, and the building of the tabernacle in nearly the same words as its instruction.
The first half answers the question *Will God deliver?*; the second answers *Can a holy God live
among this people?* — and the calf, at the hinge, makes the second question urgent.

The building section now begins in [[The Freewill Offering]] (35:1–29). After covenant renewal,
Moses assembles the people, places rest before sanctuary work, and receives willing gifts of
materials and skilled labor. The instructions of chapters 25–31 begin to become public action,
including women's spinning; the completed structure and indwelling glory still lie ahead.

## Structure

- **1 The oppression** — the sons of Israel listed and the seventy counted, Joseph's generation
  dead, the people *"fruitful"* until *"the land was filled with them"* (1:7). Then a king *"who
  didn't know Joseph"* (1:8), three escalating measures — forced labour on the store-cities of
  Pithom and Raamses, an order to the Hebrew midwives to kill the boys at birth, and finally a
  decree to the whole nation: *"You shall cast every son who is born into the river"* (1:22). Each
  measure fails in the blessing's own words, and the second fails because two women *"feared
  God"* (1:17). **God acts once in the chapter, and only for the midwives** (1:20–21). See
  [[The Oppression in Egypt]].
- **2 Moses' birth and flight** — the river decree defeated by a basket on the river: a Levite
  mother puts her son into the Nile as ordered, but in a *tēbâ*, the word for Noah's ark, and
  Pharaoh's own daughter draws him out, pays his mother to nurse him, adopts him, and names him
  *Moses* (2:1–10). **God is not mentioned once in the rescue.** See [[The Birth of Moses]]. Then a
  Hebrew raised as an Egyptian tries to deliver his brothers by his own hand — kills an Egyptian in
  secret, is refused (*"Who made you a prince and a judge over us?"*, 2:14), and flees to Midian,
  where he saves seven women at a well, is taken for an Egyptian, marries Zipporah, and names his son
  *Gershom*, *a sojourner there*. **Then, after twenty-two more verses without him, God is named five
  times in three**: he hears, remembers his covenant, sees, and knows (2:23–25). See
  [[Moses Flees to Midian]].
- **3–4 The call** — at Horeb, *"God's mountain"*, a bush burns and is not consumed; God calls Moses
  by name, makes the ground holy, names himself the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and says he has
  *come down to deliver*. **Moses objects five times** — *who am I*, *what is his name*, *they will not
  believe*, *I am not eloquent*, *send someone else* — and **every answer is something God will be or
  do**: *ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*, *"I will be with you"* (3:12); *"I AM WHO I AM"* and *Yahweh* (3:14–15); three
  signs; *"I will be with your mouth"*; and, in anger, Aaron as Moses' mouth (4:14–16). The refusal of
  Pharaoh and the plunder of Egypt are foretold before anything is asked. See [[The Burning Bush]].
  Then the return: Jethro's *"Go in peace"*; on the road, the end of the contest told in advance —
  *"I will harden his heart"*, and ***"Israel is my son, my firstborn … I will kill your son, your
  firstborn"*** (4:21–23), the tenth plague announced before the first; **then *"Yahweh met Moses and
  wanted to kill him"***, and Zipporah's flint and *"bridegroom of blood"* end it (4:24–26). Aaron meets
  Moses at the mountain with a kiss, speaks and does the signs, and *"the people believed"* (4:31) —
  the third objection answered at the first telling. See [[Moses Returns to Egypt]].
- **5–7:13 The first confrontation** — Moses and Aaron go in with *"Let my people go"*, and Pharaoh
  answers with the question the plagues will answer: ***"Who is Yahweh … ? I don't know Yahweh"***
  (5:2). He hears the request as idleness — *"you make them rest"*, the Sabbath verb (5:5) — withdraws
  the straw, keeps the quota, and the people are *"scattered abroad"* over Egypt for stubble in
  Babel's word (5:12). **The Israelite foremen are beaten, cry to Pharaoh as *"your servants"*, and
  curse Moses — *"May Yahweh look at you, and judge"* (5:21) — and Moses accuses God: *"Why is it that
  you have sent me? … you have not rescued your people at all"*.** God answers without rebuke: *"Now you
  shall see"*, *"by a strong hand"* (6:1). See [[Bricks Without Straw]]. **Then God answers the
  complaint with his name**: *"I am Yahweh"*, four times, framing a look back at the patriarchs, to
  whom he appeared *"as God Almighty"* but was *"not known"* by the name (6:3), and **seven promises**:
  *bring out*, *rescue* (the verb Moses said he had not kept), *redeem*, *take you to me for a
  people*, *be to you a God*, *bring in*, *give*. **Israel does not listen, *"for anguish of
  spirit"*** (6:9); Moses calls himself *"of uncircumcised lips"*; and God, without answering,
  commands Moses and Aaron together (6:13). See [[I Am Yahweh]]. **Then the story stops for a
  genealogy**: headed for all Israel's *"fathers' houses"*, it gives Reuben and Simeon a verse each,
  spends ten on Levi, follows Kohath's line to Amram, who married his aunt Jochebed, and to *"Aaron and
  Moses"*, and carries Aaron's line — a wife from Judah's leading house, four sons — to Phinehas. **Moses'
  own sons are not in it.** It closes by naming the brothers in both orders (6:26–27). See
  [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]]. **Then the objection again, and this time an answer**:
  *"I have made you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet"* (7:1). God
  forecasts the contest — hardening, multiplied signs, *"Pharaoh will not listen"*, and its purpose,
  ***"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"*** (7:5) — and Moses, eighty, and Aaron,
  eighty-three, obey. Pharaoh asks for a wonder; Aaron's rod becomes a *tannîn*; the magicians match
  it; **Aaron's rod swallows theirs**; and Pharaoh's heart is hardened *"as Yahweh had spoken"*
  (7:13). See [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]].
- **7:14–11 The plagues** — nine blows in three cycles of three, and the tenth announced. **The first
  falls on the river.** God calls Pharaoh's heart *kābēd*, *heavy* — the word of Moses' *"heavy"*
  mouth (4:10) — and sends Moses to meet him *"by the river's bank"*, where the basket was laid (2:3),
  with the sentence that names the purpose of every blow: ***"In this you shall know that I am
  Yahweh"*** (7:17), now said to Pharaoh's face. The Nile and every water in Egypt, to the stored jars,
  become blood; the fish die and the river stinks, in the words of the forecast. **The magicians do the
  same, which only adds blood**; Pharaoh *"didn't even take this to heart"* and goes home, while his
  people dig for water; and *"seven days were fulfilled, after Yahweh had struck the river"*
  (7:25). See [[Water Turned to Blood]]. **Then frogs, lice, and swarms, and in each something is
  conceded.** At the frogs Pharaoh asks for the first time — ***"Entreat Yahweh"*** — promises release,
  and is answered to the hour he chose: *"Yahweh did according to the word of Moses"* (8:8–13). At the
  lice the magicians fail and confess, ***"This is God's finger"*** (8:19). At the swarms God strikes
  without the rod and draws a line — *"I will set apart in that day the land of Goshen"* (8:22) — and
  Pharaoh bargains, *"in the land"*, then *"not very far away"*, against Moses' *"the abomination of the
  Egyptians"* (8:25–28). **Twice the respite is followed by Pharaoh hardening his own heart** (8:15,
  32), the first times Exodus makes him the subject. See [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]]. **Then the
  plagues reach the herds, the body, and the field.** The pestilence is *"Yahweh's hand"*, with no rod,
  and *"not one"* of Israel's animals dies (9:3–7). At the boils the magicians *"couldn't stand before
  Moses"* and leave the story, and **for the first time the narrator says *"Yahweh hardened the heart
  of Pharaoh"*** (9:12). Before the hail God tells Pharaoh why he is still alive — ***"for this cause
  I have made you stand: to show you my power, and that my name may be declared throughout all the
  earth"*** (9:16), quoted in Romans 9:17 — and gives the first warning with a way of escape, which
  some of Pharaoh's servants take (9:19–21). The hail spares Goshen; Pharaoh confesses, ***"I have
  sinned this time. Yahweh is righteous, and I and my people are wicked"*** (9:27), and when Moses has
  prayed it away he *"sinned yet more"* (9:34). See [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]. **Then the
  locusts and the darkness, and every hardening is God's.** *"I have hardened his heart, and the heart
  of his servants"* (10:1), and the plagues gain a second audience: ***"that you may tell in the
  hearing of your son, and of your son's son"*** (10:2). Moses asks *"How long will you refuse to humble
  yourself before me?"* and walks out; **Pharaoh's servants speak for the first time — *"Don't you yet
  know that Egypt is destroyed?"*** (10:7) — and he offers the men only. The east wind of Pharaoh's
  dream brings locusts until *"There remained nothing green"*; Pharaoh confesses again, *"please forgive
  my sin"*, and a west wind throws them into the *yam sûp*, named here for the first time (10:13–19).
  Then *"darkness which may be felt"* for three days, ***"but all the children of Israel had light in
  their dwellings"*** (10:21–23). The last offer keeps the herds; Moses: *"Not a hoof shall be left
  behind"*; Pharaoh: *"in the day you see my face you shall die!"* (10:24–29). See
  [[Locusts and Darkness]]. **Then the tenth announced, and the cycle closed.** *"Yet one plague more"*
  (11:1) — *negaʿ*, the word of the plagues on Abram's Pharaoh (Genesis 12:17). The people ask for
  silver and gold, and *"the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt"* (11:2–3). Moses, still
  before Pharaoh: ***"About midnight I will go out into the middle of Egypt, and all the firstborn in
  the land of Egypt shall die"***, from the throne to the mill, with *"a great cry"* — and against
  Israel *"a dog won't even bark or move its tongue"* (11:4–7). He goes out *"in hot anger"* (11:8).
  **The narrator closes the wonders in the words of 7:3–4** (11:9–10). See
  [[The Last Plague Announced]].
- **12–15:21 Passover and deliverance** — **the story stops on the eve of the blow and becomes law.**
  *"In the land of Egypt"*, God renumbers the year — *"This month shall be to you the beginning of
  months"* (12:2) — and gives the rite: a lamb *"without defect"* for each household, its blood on the
  doorposts and lintel, the meat roasted and eaten *"in haste"*, dressed to leave. ***"When I see the
  blood, I will pass over you"*** (12:13); *"Against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments"*
  (12:12). The day becomes *"a memorial"* and a feast *"forever"*, with seven days of unleavened bread
  (12:14–20). Moses relays it to the elders and adds the hyssop, the door no one may go out of, and
  *"the destroyer"* (12:21–23); the children will ask *"What do you mean by this service?"* — ***ʿăbōdâ***,
  the word of Egypt's bondage, now a rite — and be told that Yahweh *"spared our houses"* (12:26–27). The
  people worship and obey (12:27–28). See [[The Passover]]. **Then the blow, in one verse**: *"At
  midnight, Yahweh struck all the firstborn"*, from Pharaoh's heir to *"the captive who was in the
  dungeon"* (12:29), and *"a great cry in Egypt"*. **Pharaoh concedes everything he had bargained over** —
  *"Rise up, get out … as you have said!"* — and adds ***"bless me also!"*** (12:31–32). The Egyptians urge
  them out, *"We are all dead men"*; the dough goes unleavened in the troughs; *"They plundered the
  Egyptians"* (12:33–36). From Rameses to Succoth, six hundred thousand men on foot and *"a mixed
  multitude"*, after ***"four hundred thirty years, to the day"*** (12:37–41), on *"a night to be much
  observed"* (12:42). **Then the law again, on who may eat**: no foreigner, settler, or hired man; the
  circumcised slave and the circumcised stranger, under ***"One law"*** — the first singular *tôrâ* in
  Scripture (12:43–49). *"That same day, Yahweh brought the children of Israel out"* (12:51). See
  [[The Death of the Firstborn and the Departure]]. **Then the law a third time, a day out of Egypt.** God
  speaks two verses — *"Sanctify to me all the firstborn, whatever opens the womb … It is mine"* (13:2) —
  and Moses fourteen: *"Remember this day, in which you came out of Egypt, out of the house of
  bondage"* (13:3), the month named *Abib*, seven days of unleavened bread *"when Yahweh shall bring you
  into the land"*, and the father's words, ***"It is because of that which Yahweh did for me when I came out
  of Egypt"*** (13:8). Every firstling is Yahweh's, the donkey redeemed with a lamb, and ***"you shall redeem
  all the firstborn of man"*** (13:13) — the first *redeem* in Scripture. The son asks *"What is this?"* and
  is told *"Yahweh killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt … Therefore … all the firstborn of my sons I
  redeem"* (13:15). Both laws are a sign *"on your hand"* and *"between your eyes"*, *"that Yahweh's law may
  be in your mouth"* (13:9). See [[The Consecration of the Firstborn]]. **Then the march resumes, and God
  chooses the road.** Not the near way through the land of the Philistines, *"Lest perhaps the people change
  their minds when they see war"* (13:17), but the wilderness road by the sea; Moses carries Joseph's bones
  (13:19), and ***"Yahweh went before them by day in a pillar of cloud … and by night in a pillar of fire"***
  (13:21). God turns them back to camp by the sea as bait, hardens Pharaoh *"and the heart of Egypt"*, and
  says he will *"get honor"* — the *kbd* root of Pharaoh's heavy heart (14:4, 17). Pharaoh asks *"What is
  this we have done, that we have let Israel go from serving us?"* (14:5); the people, overtaken, ask
  Moses why he brought them out to die; Moses: ***"Stand still, and see the salvation of Yahweh … Yahweh will
  fight for you"*** (14:13–14). An east wind blows all night, the sea becomes *"dry land"* — the word of
  Genesis 1:9 — the waters stand *"a wall to them on their right hand, and on their left"*, and the army that
  follows is covered: *"There remained not so much as one of them"* (14:28). **The prose ends on the book's
  root**: *"they believed in Yahweh, and in his servant Moses"* (14:31). See [[The Crossing of the Sea]].
  **Then the first song in Scripture** — *"Then Moses and the children of Israel sang"* (15:1), the verb
  *šîr* absent from Genesis (WLC). It sings the sea with God's own hand and breath where the prose had
  Moses' hand and an east wind (15:6–12); names Yahweh *"a man of war"* and asks *"Who is like you, Yahweh,
  among the gods?"* (15:3, 11); and then sings ahead — the nations melting, Israel *"redeemed"* and
  *"purchased"*, *"planted"* on *"the mountain of your inheritance"* by a *"sanctuary"* (the word's first
  occurrence), and ***"Yahweh shall reign forever and ever"*** (15:13–18). **Miriam, named here for the
  first time and called *"the prophetess"*, answers with the refrain** (15:20–21). See
  [[The Song of the Sea]].
- **15:22–18 To the mountain** — Marah, manna and quail, water from the rock, Amalek, and Jethro. **Three days
  past the sea there is no water, and the first water is bitter.** At Marah — named for it — the people
  ***"murmured against Moses"***, the first *lûn* in Scripture, with a two-word question, *"What shall we
  drink?"*; Moses *"cried to Yahweh"*; Yahweh *"showed him a tree"* (*yārâ*, the verb of teaching), Moses threw
  it in, and *"the waters were made sweet"* (15:23–25). **No anger is reported and no one is punished.**
  Instead, ***"There he made a statute and an ordinance for them, and there he tested them"*** — the first
  *ḥōq* since Pharaoh's brick quota (5:14) and the first test of Israel (*nissâ*, the verb of Genesis 22:1) —
  and a condition in the words of Genesis 26:5: *"If you will diligently listen to Yahweh your God's voice …
  I will put none of the diseases on you, which I have put on the Egyptians; for **I am Yahweh who heals
  you**"* (15:26). Then Elim, *"twelve springs of water, and seventy palm trees"* (15:27). See
  [[Bitter Water at Marah]]. **A month out, the food runs out, and Egypt is remembered as *"the meat
  pots"*.** The whole congregation murmurs *"against Moses and against Aaron"* and wishes it had died *"by
  Yahweh's hand in the land of Egypt"* (16:3). God answers with ***"I will rain bread from the sky"*** — the
  verb of the rain on Sodom (Genesis 19:24) — and a test, *"whether they will walk in my law, or not"* (16:4).
  Moses: ***"Your murmurings are not against us, but against Yahweh"*** (16:8); and the first
  ***"Yahweh's glory"*** in Scripture *"appeared in the cloud"* (16:10). Quail one evening; every morning
  under the dew *"a small round thing"*, and the question that names it, ***"What is it?"*** (*mān hûʾ*,
  16:15). An omer a head, and *"he who gathered much had nothing over, and he who gathered little had no
  lack"* (16:18); kept overnight, it *"bred worms, and became foul"* — the verb of the Nile (7:21). **Then the
  first *Sabbath* in Scripture, as a fact about bread**: a double portion on the sixth day, *"a holy Sabbath
  to Yahweh"* (16:23), nothing on the seventh, and to those who go out anyway the question once put to
  Pharaoh — ***"How long do you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?"*** (16:28), the two words of
  Genesis 26:5. *"Yahweh has given you the Sabbath"*; *"So the people rested"* (16:29–30). An omer is laid up
  *"before the Testimony"* for the generations, and the narrator closes on forty years of manna (16:34–35),
  both told from after Sinai. See [[Manna and Quail]]. **At Rephidim there is no water again, and the
  complaint becomes a quarrel and a test.** *"Give us water to drink"*; Moses: ***"Why do you quarrel with
  me? Why do you test Yahweh?"*** (17:2) — **the first time in Scripture a human being tests God**, after three
  tests by God (Genesis 22:1; Exodus 15:25; 16:4; WLC lemma search). Moses cries, *"They are almost ready to
  stone me"*, and is sent ahead with the elders and *"the rod … with which you struck the Nile"* — said to
  Moses, though 7:19 gave the rod to Aaron — to the place where God says ***"I will stand before you there on
  the rock in Horeb"***. He strikes, *"in the sight of the elders"*, and the place is named for the people:
  ***Massah*, *testing*, and *Meribah*, *quarreling***, because they asked ***"Is Yahweh among us, or
  not?"*** (17:7). **Still no punishment**; Deuteronomy, the Psalms, Hebrews, and Jesus supply the verdict
  (Deuteronomy 6:16; Psalm 95:8; Hebrews 3:8; Matthew 4:7), and Numbers 20 tells a second Meribah that costs
  Moses the land. See [[Water from the Rock]]. **Then, in the same camp, the first battle**: *"Then Amalek came and
  fought with Israel in Rephidim"* (17:8). Joshua, unintroduced, fights below; Moses stands on the hill with
  *"God's rod"*, and ***"When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed"*** (17:11). His hands grow *heavy* — the
  word of Pharaoh's heart and Moses' own mouth — and Aaron and Hur hold them up until they are *ʾĕmûnâ*,
  *"steady"*, the word's first occurrence (17:12; WLC). **God then orders the first writing in Scripture** —
  *"Write this for a memorial in a book … that I will utterly blot out the memory of Amalek"* (17:14) — Moses
  builds the book's first altar, *YHWH nissî*, *"Yahweh our Banner"* (Hebrew *my banner*), and the war is
  declared *"from generation to generation"* (17:16), the measure of God's own name at the bush (3:15). See
  [[The Battle with Amalek]]. **Then a foreigner comes in peace.** *"Jethro, the priest of Midian … heard"* (18:1) and
  brings Zipporah — *"after he had sent her away"* — and her two sons to the camp *"at the Mountain of God"*, the second
  son named here for the first time, ***Eliezer***, *"My father's God was my help"* (18:4). Moses bows, kisses him, and
  tells him everything; Jethro blesses Yahweh *"who has delivered you"* — the words of the bush (3:8) — and confesses
  ***"Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all gods"*** (18:11), the answer to Pharaoh's *"I don't know Yahweh"*
  (5:2). He provides **the first burnt offering actually made in Exodus**, and Aaron and the elders eat bread with
  him *"before God"* (18:12). **The next day he watches Moses judge alone from morning to evening** — the *judge* of
  2:14's taunt — and tells him ***"The thing that you do is not good … too heavy for you. You are not able to perform
  it yourself alone"*** (18:17–18): *kābēd*, the word of Moses' mouth and hands. Moses keeps the place *"before God"*
  and the hard cases, and appoints *"able men which fear God: men of truth, hating unjust gain"* over thousands,
  hundreds, fifties, and tens — on Jethro's condition *"and God commands you so"* (18:23), **a command the text never
  reports**. *"Moses listened to the voice of his father-in-law"* (18:24), and Jethro goes home. **All three *šālôm*
  in Exodus are his.** Deuteronomy credits the judges to Moses and Numbers gives Moses' burden to seventy elders with
  the Spirit; Exodus places the visit before the arrival at Sinai (19:1). See [[Jethro's Counsel]].
- **19–24 The covenant at Sinai** — the theophany, the Ten Commandments, the Book of the Covenant
  (20:22–23:33), and the covenant sealed in blood (24). **The assembly arrives in the third
  month**, and Yahweh recalls carrying it to himself before offering a priestly, holy vocation
  conditional on obedience (19:1–8). The people assent, prepare, and stand below a bounded
  mountain as God descends in fire, cloud, and trumpet sound. Moses carries the words between
  God and the people; Aaron is named for an ascent, while priests and people are warned not
  to break through (19:9–25). See [[Israel at Mount Sinai]]. **God then speaks the commands as
  the deliverer from Egypt**, joining exclusive worship, the holy name and day, parents, and
  neighbors in one address. The Sabbath rests on creation and includes servants, animals, and
  the stranger; coveting makes desire answerable as well as action (20:1–17). The frightened
  people ask Moses to speak, and he explains the encounter as a test intended to restrain sin.
  They stand back while he approaches the thick darkness (20:18–21). See
  [[The Ten Commandments]]. **Then God gives Moses words for the people, and the first are about
  an altar**: no gods of silver or gold beside him, an altar of earth or of stones no tool has
  touched, no steps, and *"In every place where I record my name I will come to you and I will
  bless you"* (20:22–26). **The ordinances then begin with a bought Hebrew servant**: six years
  and release in the seventh, a master-given wife and children who stay, the ear bored at the
  doorpost of the man who chooses to remain, and a daughter sold as a female servant whose food,
  clothing, and marital rights are protected (21:1–11). The first law spoken to a people brought
  out of the house of bondage limits a master's hold over a brother. See
  [[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]]. **The next cases distinguish violence, injury, and
  negligence** (21:12–36): refuge for an unintentional killer but no altar shelter for planned
  murder; sanctions for attacking or cursing parents and for kidnapping; healing costs for an
  injured neighbor; matching injury; release for a servant's lost eye or tooth; and liability
  for dangerous bulls and uncovered pits. Protection of servants coexists with ownership and
  unequal remedies (21:20–21, 32). See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]]. **Property follows**
  (22:1–15): theft repaid double, or four and five times when the animal is gone; no bloodguilt for
  a thief killed breaking in, but guilt once the sun has risen; grazing and fire damage made good; and deposits,
  kept animals, and loans settled by an oath, a hearing *"before God"*, or the keeper's evidence.
  See [[Laws of Property and Restitution]].
  **Then the law turns from things to persons** (22:16–31): the bride-price for a seduced virgin;
  death for the sorceress, for bestiality, and for sacrifice to other gods; and, in God's first
  person, protection for the alien, the widow, the fatherless, and the poor borrower, forbidding
  Israel the very verbs Egypt used on it — *"for you were aliens in the land of Egypt"*, *"I will
  surely hear their cry"*, *"I am gracious"*. The unit ends on the firstborn given *"on the eighth
  day"* and *"You shall be holy men to me"*. See [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].
  **Justice and the calendar follow together** (23:1–19): truthful testimony without the crowd's
  pressure, bribes, favoritism, or denial of the poor person's rights; help for an enemy's animals;
  protection of the alien because Israel knows alien life; a seventh year that feeds the poor and a
  seventh day that refreshes dependents; and Unleavened Bread, Harvest, and Ingathering with their
  offerings. The goat-and-milk prohibition closes the unit without explanation. See
  [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]]. **The Book of the Covenant ends with a promise about the road**
  (23:20–33): an angel sent ahead *"to keep you by the way"*, to be heard and not provoked, *"for my
  name is in him"*. Then a choice of masters in the book's own verb, *ʿābad* — *"nor serve them"*,
  *"You shall serve Yahweh"*, *"if you serve their gods"* — with bread, water, health, and children
  promised to the second. God's terror and the hornet go ahead, the land is given *"little by
  little"* so it does not go wild, a border runs from the Red Sea to the River, and the peoples' gods
  are named as *"a snare"*. The angel *"before you"* returns after the calf, when God offers him
  instead of going up *"among you"* himself (33:2–3). See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]. **Then the
  covenant is made** (24:1–18). Moses tells the people the words and they answer *"with one voice"*,
  *"will we do"*; he writes them, builds an altar and twelve pillars, has young men offer the
  sacrifices, throws half the blood on the altar, reads *"the book of the covenant"*, hears *"will we do,
  and be obedient"*, and throws the rest on the people: ***"Look, this is the blood of the covenant"***
  (24:8). Then Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders go up, and ***"They saw the God of
  Israel"*** — only the sapphire pavement *"under his feet"* is described — and *"He didn't lay his hand
  on"* them; *"They saw God, and ate and drank"* (24:10–11). Moses goes on with Joshua, leaves Aaron and
  Hur in charge, and enters the cloud where *"Yahweh's glory settled"* — *šākan*, the verb of the
  coming *miškān* — and looked *"like devouring fire"*, for forty days and forty nights (24:12–18). See
  [[The Blood of the Covenant]].
- **25–31 The tabernacle instructions** — sanctuary, furnishings, priesthood, ordination, and the
  Sabbath sign. **God's first words in the cloud ask for an offering** (25:1–9): *"From everyone whose
  heart makes him willing you shall take my offering"* — no amount and no compulsion (*nādab*, MAM) —
  in gold, silver, brass, dyed yarn, linen, skins, acacia wood, oil, spices, and stones. Then the
  reason: ***"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them"*** (25:8), in the verb
  (*šākan*) just used of the glory on Sinai; and the standard, *"the pattern"* (*tabnît*) that God
  shows Moses, *"even so you shall make it"* (25:9). The Septuagint has *"I will be seen among you"*
  (Rahlfs). See [[The Offering for the Sanctuary]] and [[The Dwelling of God]]. **The design then
  starts at the centre** (25:10–40): the ark, a gold-covered chest for *"the testimony"*, with its gold
  *kappōret*, the *"mercy seat"*, and two cherubim, and God's promise *"There I will meet with you"*
  from between them (25:22); the table for *"bread of the presence … before me always"* (25:30); and a
  lampstand of hammered gold shaped like an almond tree with seven lamps. *"See that you make them
  after their pattern, which has been shown to you on the mountain"* (25:40) is the verse Hebrews 8:5
  quotes. See [[The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand]].
  **The enclosure follows** (26:1–37): ten linen curtains with cherubim, a larger goats’ hair
  covering and skins, gold-covered boards in silver sockets, and bars holding them together.
  A cherubim veil separates the holy place from the most holy; the ark stands within it, the table
  and lampstand outside. An embroidered screen closes the entrance. The promise of dwelling now
  includes an internal boundary. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].
- **32–34 The calf and the covenant renewed** — the breach, Moses' intercession, the glory passing
  by, the second tablets, and the shining face.
- **35–40 The tabernacle built** — the offering, the work, the inventory, and the glory filling the
  tent.

### The altar, court, and lamp service — Exodus 27

[[The Altar and the Court]] extends the design to a portable brass-covered altar and a court
one hundred by fifty cubits. The east gate is distinct from the tent entrance and the inner
veil. Israel must supply pure beaten olive oil; Aaron and his sons keep the lamp in order
outside the veil from evening to morning (27:20–21), anticipating the priestly instructions
that follow. The altar's relation to the earth-or-stone law of 20:24–26 remains unexplained
by Exodus itself. This chapter prescribes construction and service; it reports neither done.

### The priestly garments — Exodus 28

[[The Priestly Garments]] names Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar for *"the priest's
office"* (28:1) — the first appointment of a named priesthood in Exodus. Aaron's clothing
carries what he bears before Yahweh: the twelve names on two onyx shoulder stones and on the
twelve stones of the breastplate of judgment, the Urim and Thummim, and a gold plate reading
*"HOLY TO YAHWEH"* with *"the iniquity of the holy things"* (28:9–38). Bells on the blue robe and
linen breeches are both required *"that he not die"* (28:35, 43). The garments are commanded
here, made in chapter 39; ordination follows in chapter 29.

### The ordination of the priests — Exodus 29

[[The Ordination of the Priests]] prescribes the seven-day rite by which Moses makes Aaron and his
sons priests: washing, clothing, anointing, a sin offering, a burnt offering, and the ram of
consecration, whose blood is put on ear, thumb, and toe (29:1–35). The altar is atoned for until
it is *"most holy"* (29:37). The chapter ends with the daily lamb at the tent's door and the book's
plainest statement of purpose: God brought Israel out of Egypt *"that I might dwell among them"*
(29:46). The rite is performed in Leviticus 8.

### The incense altar, the ransom, and the holy oil — Exodus 30

[[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]] completes the furniture with a small gold altar
for incense *"before the veil … where I will meet with you"* (30:6), on which Aaron burns *"a
perpetual incense"* morning and evening and makes atonement once a year (30:7–10). Every man counted
in a census pays half a shekel as *"a ransom for his soul"*, the same for rich and poor, and the
*"atonement money"* goes to the tent's service (30:11–16). A brass basin is added for the priests'
washing, *"that they not die"* (30:17–21). The holy anointing oil and the incense are given exact
recipes and reserved to God on pain of being *"cut off"* (30:22–38). The chapter uses the root of
*atonement* more densely than any before it.

### The makers, Sabbath sign, and tablets — Exodus 31

[[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]] closes the mountain instructions. God calls Bezalel of
Judah, appoints Oholiab of Dan beside him, and gives wisdom to the workers for all the sanctuary's
equipment (31:1–11). A fresh speech requires Sabbath as the sign between Yahweh and Israel, a
perpetual covenant grounded in creation, with death for profanation and work (31:12–17). The divine
rest is described as refreshment, a wording retained alongside Isaiah 40:28's denial of divine
weariness. When the speaking ends, Moses receives the two stone tablets written with God's finger
(31:18). The work is assigned; nothing has yet been built.

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

[[The Golden Calf]] breaks the movement from sanctuary instructions to construction. Aaron makes
an image and announces a feast to Yahweh; Moses appeals to the patriarchal oath, and God relents
from total destruction (32:1–14). The divine tablets are broken, the calf destroyed, and about
three thousand die at the Levites’ hands (32:15–29). A second plea seeks atonement; God orders
onward travel with an angel, retains accountability for sin, and strikes the people (32:30–35).
The chapter leaves pardon and the terms of continued presence unsettled.

**Presence after the calf — Exodus 33.** In [[Show Me Your Glory]], the land oath survives but
Yahweh initially refuses to go among the people lest he consume them (33:1–6). Moses meets him
at an outside tent and appeals from personal favor to the need of the whole nation (33:7–17).
God grants presence and promises goodness, the proclamation of his name, and a sheltered glimpse
of his back (33:18–23). Direct, friendly speech does not cancel the stated limit on sight.
The chapter ends before the narrated passing and covenant renewal of chapter 34.

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

[[The Covenant Renewed]] supplies replacement tablets and the promised proclamation of the name.
Mercy, forgiveness, and judgment stand together (34:5–7); Moses asks for presence, pardon, and
belonging (34:8–9). God announces a covenant and marvels, rejects rival worship and cast idols,
and repeats the firstborn, Sabbath, feast, and offering laws (34:10–26). Moses stays forty days
and nights without bread or water. The writing notices in 34:1, 27–28 and their relation to
Deuteronomy remain explicit in [[The Replacement Tablets - Exodus 34 and Deuteronomy 10]].
The descent is narrated in [[The Shining Face of Moses]].

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

In [[The Shining Face of Moses]], Moses descends with the testimony tablets and a shining face
of which he is unaware (34:29). Aaron and Israel fear approaching, but his call brings the
leaders and then the people near to receive the commands (34:30–32). He veils himself after
speaking and removes the veil for each encounter before Yahweh (34:33–35). The repeated pattern
continues communication after renewal; the construction of the sanctuary has not yet begun.

## Key people

- [[Nun]] — named as Joshua’s father at 33:11; no independent action described.

- [[Bezalel]] and [[Oholiab]] — the appointed makers, from Judah and Dan (31:2–6).
- [[Uri]], [[Hur (grandfather of Bezalel)]], and [[Ahisamach]] — their named ancestry.

- [[God]]
- [[Jacob]] — *Israel*, whose sons open the book
- [[Joseph]] — dead in its sixth verse, and forgotten by its eighth
- [[Pharaoh (of the oppression)]] — the king *"who didn't know Joseph"*
- [[Shiphrah and Puah]] — the Hebrew midwives
- [[Moses]] — drawn out of the river, and named for what he will do
- [[Jochebed]] and [[Amram]] — his Levite parents, unnamed until 6:20
- [[Miriam]] — his sister, who watches from far off, and *"the prophetess"* who leads the women's song at
  the sea
- [[Pharaoh's Daughter (of Moses' time)]] — who spares, adopts, and names him
- [[Jethro]] — the priest of Midian, called Reuel at 2:18, who takes the fugitive in, and at the Mountain of God
  blesses Yahweh and teaches Moses to share the judging (18)
- [[Zipporah]] — his daughter, Moses' wife
- [[Gershom]] — their son, named for exile
- [[Eliezer (son of Moses)]] — their second son, named at 18:4 for the escape from Pharaoh's sword
- [[Aaron]] — Moses' brother, appointed to be his mouth
- [[Elisheba]] — Aaron's wife, sister of [[Nahshon]] of Judah
- [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]], [[Abihu]], [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]], [[Ithamar]] — Aaron's sons, and
  [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]] his grandson
- [[Korah (son of Izhar)]] — their cousin
- [[The Angel of Yahweh]] — in the flame of the bush
- [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] — the king Moses is sent to
- [[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]] — the court experts who match the first signs, fail at
  the lice — *"This is God's finger"* — and cannot stand before Moses at the boils
- [[Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)]] — the court, divided at the hail, hardened with their king,
  and the first to tell him *"Egypt is destroyed"* (10:7)
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — unintroduced at 17:9, Moses' commander against Amalek and later his servant on the
  mountain
- [[Hur (companion of Moses)]] — who holds up Moses' other hand with Aaron (17:12)
- [[Amalek]] — the people who attack at Rephidim, under sentence *"from generation to generation"* (17:16)
