# Exodus

## Overview

Exodus is the book in which a family becomes a people, a slave population becomes a nation under
God, and the God who walked with patriarchs comes to live in a tent in the middle of a camp. It
begins with the sons of Israel *"who came into Egypt"* (1:1) and ends with the cloud covering the
Tent of Meeting and *"Yahweh's glory filled the tabernacle"* (40:34). Everything between is the
journey from the first sentence to the last: out of one house of service and into another.

**The book is built on a single Hebrew root, *ʿābad*, *to serve*.** In chapter 1 Egypt makes Israel
*serve* with *service* that is hard (1:13–14); the demand Moses carries to Pharaoh is *"Let my son
go, that he may serve me"* (4:23); and the first commandment opens by naming Yahweh as the God
*"who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage"* — *bêt ʿăbādîm*, the house
of servants (20:2). Exodus is not the story of Israel becoming free in the modern sense. It is the
story of Israel changing masters, and of what kind of master Yahweh turns out to be.

**It is also the sequel Genesis set up and did not deliver.** Genesis closes on a promise of land
unfulfilled and unrevoked, a coffin in Egypt, and Joseph's *"God will surely visit you"*
(Genesis 50:24–25). Abram was told his offspring would *"serve"* a foreign nation who would
*"afflict them four hundred years"*, and that God would judge that nation and bring them out *"with
great wealth"* (Genesis 15:13–14). Exodus opens by reprinting Genesis 46's list of the names and
the seventy souls, reports the multiplication in the creation blessing's own verbs, and then turns
the affliction loose — so the reader knows from the first chapter what is being kept and by whom.
See [[Four Hundred Years - The Sojourn and the Exodus]] and
[[They Got Possessions and Multiplied - Genesis 47.27 and Exodus 1.7]].

The book divides roughly in half. **Chapters 1–18 are narrative**: oppression, Moses, the call,
the plagues, the Passover, the sea, and the wilderness as far as Sinai. **Chapters 19–40 are at the
mountain**: the covenant and its law, the instructions for the tabernacle, the golden calf and the
covenant's renewal, and the building of the tabernacle in nearly the same words as its instruction.
The first half answers the question *Will God deliver?*; the second answers *Can a holy God live
among this people?* — and the calf, at the hinge, makes the second question urgent.

## Structure

- **1 The oppression** — the sons of Israel listed and the seventy counted, Joseph's generation
  dead, the people *"fruitful"* until *"the land was filled with them"* (1:7). Then a king *"who
  didn't know Joseph"* (1:8), three escalating measures — forced labour on the store-cities of
  Pithom and Raamses, an order to the Hebrew midwives to kill the boys at birth, and finally a
  decree to the whole nation: *"You shall cast every son who is born into the river"* (1:22). Each
  measure fails in the blessing's own words, and the second fails because two women *"feared
  God"* (1:17). **God acts once in the chapter, and only for the midwives** (1:20–21). See
  [[The Oppression in Egypt]].
- **2 Moses' birth and flight** — the river decree defeated by a basket on the river: a Levite
  mother puts her son into the Nile as ordered, but in a *tēbâ*, the word for Noah's ark, and
  Pharaoh's own daughter draws him out, pays his mother to nurse him, adopts him, and names him
  *Moses* (2:1–10). **God is not mentioned once in the rescue.** See [[The Birth of Moses]]. Then a
  Hebrew raised as an Egyptian tries to deliver his brothers by his own hand — kills an Egyptian in
  secret, is refused (*"Who made you a prince and a judge over us?"*, 2:14), and flees to Midian,
  where he saves seven women at a well, is taken for an Egyptian, marries Zipporah, and names his son
  *Gershom*, *a sojourner there*. **Then, after twenty-two more verses without him, God is named five
  times in three**: he hears, remembers his covenant, sees, and knows (2:23–25). See
  [[Moses Flees to Midian]].
- **3–4 The call** — at Horeb, *"God's mountain"*, a bush burns and is not consumed; God calls Moses
  by name, makes the ground holy, names himself the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and says he has
  *come down to deliver*. **Moses objects five times** — *who am I*, *what is his name*, *they will not
  believe*, *I am not eloquent*, *send someone else* — and **every answer is something God will be or
  do**: *ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*, *"I will be with you"* (3:12); *"I AM WHO I AM"* and *Yahweh* (3:14–15); three
  signs; *"I will be with your mouth"*; and, in anger, Aaron as Moses' mouth (4:14–16). The refusal of
  Pharaoh and the plunder of Egypt are foretold before anything is asked. See [[The Burning Bush]].
  Then the return: Jethro's *"Go in peace"*; on the road, the end of the contest told in advance —
  *"I will harden his heart"*, and ***"Israel is my son, my firstborn … I will kill your son, your
  firstborn"*** (4:21–23), the tenth plague announced before the first; **then *"Yahweh met Moses and
  wanted to kill him"***, and Zipporah's flint and *"bridegroom of blood"* end it (4:24–26). Aaron meets
  Moses at the mountain with a kiss, speaks and does the signs, and *"the people believed"* (4:31) —
  the third objection answered at the first telling. See [[Moses Returns to Egypt]].
- **5–7:13 The first confrontation** — Moses and Aaron go in with *"Let my people go"*, and Pharaoh
  answers with the question the plagues will answer: ***"Who is Yahweh … ? I don't know Yahweh"***
  (5:2). He hears the request as idleness — *"you make them rest"*, the Sabbath verb (5:5) — withdraws
  the straw, keeps the quota, and the people are *"scattered abroad"* over Egypt for stubble in
  Babel's word (5:12). **The Israelite foremen are beaten, cry to Pharaoh as *"your servants"*, and
  curse Moses — *"May Yahweh look at you, and judge"* (5:21) — and Moses accuses God: *"Why is it that
  you have sent me? … you have not rescued your people at all"*.** God answers without rebuke: *"Now you
  shall see"*, *"by a strong hand"* (6:1). See [[Bricks Without Straw]]. **Then God answers the
  complaint with his name**: *"I am Yahweh"*, four times, framing a look back at the patriarchs, to
  whom he appeared *"as God Almighty"* but was *"not known"* by the name (6:3), and **seven promises**:
  *bring out*, *rescue* (the verb Moses said he had not kept), *redeem*, *take you to me for a
  people*, *be to you a God*, *bring in*, *give*. **Israel does not listen, *"for anguish of
  spirit"*** (6:9); Moses calls himself *"of uncircumcised lips"*; and God, without answering,
  commands Moses and Aaron together (6:13). See [[I Am Yahweh]]. **Then the story stops for a
  genealogy**: headed for all Israel's *"fathers' houses"*, it gives Reuben and Simeon a verse each,
  spends ten on Levi, follows Kohath's line to Amram, who married his aunt Jochebed, and to *"Aaron and
  Moses"*, and carries Aaron's line — a wife from Judah's leading house, four sons — to Phinehas. **Moses'
  own sons are not in it.** It closes by naming the brothers in both orders (6:26–27). See
  [[The Heads of the Fathers' Houses]]. **Then the objection again, and this time an answer**:
  *"I have made you as God to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet"* (7:1). God
  forecasts the contest — hardening, multiplied signs, *"Pharaoh will not listen"*, and its purpose,
  ***"The Egyptians shall know that I am Yahweh"*** (7:5) — and Moses, eighty, and Aaron,
  eighty-three, obey. Pharaoh asks for a wonder; Aaron's rod becomes a *tannîn*; the magicians match
  it; **Aaron's rod swallows theirs**; and Pharaoh's heart is hardened *"as Yahweh had spoken"*
  (7:13). See [[Aaron's Staff Before Pharaoh]].
- **7:14–11 The plagues** — nine blows in three cycles of three, and the tenth announced. **The first
  falls on the river.** God calls Pharaoh's heart *kābēd*, *heavy* — the word of Moses' *"heavy"*
  mouth (4:10) — and sends Moses to meet him *"by the river's bank"*, where the basket was laid (2:3),
  with the sentence that names the purpose of every blow: ***"In this you shall know that I am
  Yahweh"*** (7:17), now said to Pharaoh's face. The Nile and every water in Egypt, to the stored jars,
  become blood; the fish die and the river stinks, in the words of the forecast. **The magicians do the
  same, which only adds blood**; Pharaoh *"didn't even take this to heart"* and goes home, while his
  people dig for water; and *"seven days were fulfilled, after Yahweh had struck the river"*
  (7:25). See [[Water Turned to Blood]]. **Then frogs, lice, and swarms, and in each something is
  conceded.** At the frogs Pharaoh asks for the first time — ***"Entreat Yahweh"*** — promises release,
  and is answered to the hour he chose: *"Yahweh did according to the word of Moses"* (8:8–13). At the
  lice the magicians fail and confess, ***"This is God's finger"*** (8:19). At the swarms God strikes
  without the rod and draws a line — *"I will set apart in that day the land of Goshen"* (8:22) — and
  Pharaoh bargains, *"in the land"*, then *"not very far away"*, against Moses' *"the abomination of the
  Egyptians"* (8:25–28). **Twice the respite is followed by Pharaoh hardening his own heart** (8:15,
  32), the first times Exodus makes him the subject. See [[Frogs, Lice, and Flies]]. **Then the
  plagues reach the herds, the body, and the field.** The pestilence is *"Yahweh's hand"*, with no rod,
  and *"not one"* of Israel's animals dies (9:3–7). At the boils the magicians *"couldn't stand before
  Moses"* and leave the story, and **for the first time the narrator says *"Yahweh hardened the heart
  of Pharaoh"*** (9:12). Before the hail God tells Pharaoh why he is still alive — ***"for this cause
  I have made you stand: to show you my power, and that my name may be declared throughout all the
  earth"*** (9:16), quoted in Romans 9:17 — and gives the first warning with a way of escape, which
  some of Pharaoh's servants take (9:19–21). The hail spares Goshen; Pharaoh confesses, ***"I have
  sinned this time. Yahweh is righteous, and I and my people are wicked"*** (9:27), and when Moses has
  prayed it away he *"sinned yet more"* (9:34). See [[Livestock, Boils, and Hail]]. **Then the
  locusts and the darkness, and every hardening is God's.** *"I have hardened his heart, and the heart
  of his servants"* (10:1), and the plagues gain a second audience: ***"that you may tell in the
  hearing of your son, and of your son's son"*** (10:2). Moses asks *"How long will you refuse to humble
  yourself before me?"* and walks out; **Pharaoh's servants speak for the first time — *"Don't you yet
  know that Egypt is destroyed?"*** (10:7) — and he offers the men only. The east wind of Pharaoh's
  dream brings locusts until *"There remained nothing green"*; Pharaoh confesses again, *"please forgive
  my sin"*, and a west wind throws them into the *yam sûp*, named here for the first time (10:13–19).
  Then *"darkness which may be felt"* for three days, ***"but all the children of Israel had light in
  their dwellings"*** (10:21–23). The last offer keeps the herds; Moses: *"Not a hoof shall be left
  behind"*; Pharaoh: *"in the day you see my face you shall die!"* (10:24–29). See
  [[Locusts and Darkness]].
- **12–15:21 Passover and deliverance** — the Passover instituted, the firstborn struck, the
  departure, the consecration of the firstborn, the sea, and the song.
- **15:22–18 To the mountain** — Marah, manna and quail, water from the rock, Amalek, and Jethro.
- **19–24 The covenant at Sinai** — the theophany, the Ten Commandments, the Book of the Covenant
  (20:22–23:33), and the covenant sealed in blood (24).
- **25–31 The tabernacle instructions** — sanctuary, furnishings, priesthood, ordination, and the
  Sabbath sign.
- **32–34 The calf and the covenant renewed** — the breach, Moses' intercession, the glory passing
  by, the second tablets, and the shining face.
- **35–40 The tabernacle built** — the offering, the work, the inventory, and the glory filling the
  tent.

## Key people

- [[God]]
- [[Jacob]] — *Israel*, whose sons open the book
- [[Joseph]] — dead in its sixth verse, and forgotten by its eighth
- [[Pharaoh (of the oppression)]] — the king *"who didn't know Joseph"*
- [[Shiphrah and Puah]] — the Hebrew midwives
- [[Moses]] — drawn out of the river, and named for what he will do
- [[Jochebed]] and [[Amram]] — his Levite parents, unnamed until 6:20
- [[Miriam]] — his sister, who watches from far off
- [[Pharaoh's Daughter (of Moses' time)]] — who spares, adopts, and names him
- [[Jethro]] — the priest of Midian, called Reuel at 2:18, who takes the fugitive in
- [[Zipporah]] — his daughter, Moses' wife
- [[Gershom]] — their son, named for exile
- [[Aaron]] — Moses' brother, appointed to be his mouth
- [[Elisheba]] — Aaron's wife, sister of [[Nahshon]] of Judah
- [[Nadab (son of Aaron)]], [[Abihu]], [[Eleazar (son of Aaron)]], [[Ithamar]] — Aaron's sons, and
  [[Phinehas (son of Eleazar)]] his grandson
- [[Korah (son of Izhar)]] — their cousin
- [[The Angel of Yahweh]] — in the flame of the bush
- [[Pharaoh (of the Exodus)]] — the king Moses is sent to
- [[Pharaoh's Magicians (of the Exodus)]] — the court experts who match the first signs, fail at
  the lice — *"This is God's finger"* — and cannot stand before Moses at the boils
- [[Pharaoh's Servants (of the Exodus)]] — the court, divided at the hail, hardened with their king,
  and the first to tell him *"Egypt is destroyed"* (10:7)
