# Judges

## Overview

Judges begins where Joshua ends, with a death: *After the death of Joshua, the children of Israel
asked of Yahweh, saying, "Who should go up for us first against the Canaanites, to fight against
them?"* (1:1). Joshua closed on a land divided and a people sworn to serve Yahweh; Judges opens on
the same land still to be taken, tribe by tribe, and on the question of who will lead now that no
single leader stands in Joshua's place. Its answer, for most of the book, is a series of
deliverers raised up one crisis at a time, and its closing verdict is the refrain *In those days
there was no king in Israel. Everyone did that which was right in his own eyes* (21:25).

[[Judah Goes Up First]] (1:1–7) puts the question to Yahweh and receives a tribe, not a man:
*Judah shall go up*. Judah takes Simeon his brother with him, as the two lots of Joshua 19 were
already joined, and they defeat the Canaanites and Perizzites at Bezek. Their captive, Adoni-Bezek,
gives the book its first theological sentence from the mouth of a Canaanite king: *As I have done,
so God has done to me*. The same question, *who shall go up for us first*, and the same answer,
*Judah*, return at 20:18 — but there the enemy is Benjamin, a brother tribe. The book's first and
last wars are framed by one inquiry.

[[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]] (1:8–21) carries Judah through Jerusalem, which it takes
and burns, to Hebron, Debir, Zephath renamed Hormah, and three Philistine cities. It repeats from Joshua
15 Caleb's gift of Achsah to Othniel, the man who will be the first judge, and her request for springs.
Then the run stops at the valley, *because they had chariots of iron* (1:19), though *Yahweh was with
Judah*. And the pericope ends with the first *did not drive out*: the Jebusites stay in Jerusalem with
Benjamin *to this day* (1:21). The burned city of verse 8 is still unpossessed in verse 21. The rest of
chapter 1 becomes a list of such sentences.

[[The Tribes That Did Not Drive Out]] (1:22–36) is that list. The house of Joseph takes Bethel, once
Luz, through a man who shows its spies the way in and is spared, as Rahab was; he leaves and builds a
new Luz in the land of the Hittites. Then Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, and Naphtali each *did not
drive out* the Canaanites of their cities, who were *determined* to stay. Where Israel grew strong they
became forced laborers. In Asher and Naphtali the relation is reversed, and Israel *lived among the
Canaanites*. Last, the Amorites press Dan out of the valley. Where Joshua 17:12 said Manasseh *couldn't*,
Judges 1:27 says *did not*. The chapter ends without a verdict; Judges 2:1–5 supplies one.

[[The Angel at Bochim]] (2:1–5) is that verdict. Yahweh's angel comes up from Gilgal and speaks in
Yahweh's first person: *I brought you out of Egypt … I will never break my covenant with you*. Israel
was to make no covenant with the land's inhabitants and to break their altars, *but you have not
listened to my voice*. So Yahweh *will not drive them out*, and their gods will be *a snare*. The
verb is *gāraš*, the verb of his promise in Exodus 23, not the *yāraš* of the tribes in chapter 1.
The people weep, name the place *Bochim*, *weepers*, and sacrifice. The same idiom of weeping returns
at Bethel in the book's last chapter (21:2).

[[Another Generation Arose]] (2:6–23) starts the book a second time. Joshua sends the people home,
dies, and is buried, in words taken from Joshua 24:28–31, and then *another generation arose after
them, who didn't know Yahweh*. The verb Joshua 24:31 used of the elders, *known*, returns negated for
their children. What follows is the cycle in general terms before any judge is named: Israel serves
the Baals and the Ashtaroth; Yahweh *sold them* to their enemies; Yahweh, *grieved because of their
groaning*, raises up judges who save them; and when each judge dies they turn back, *more corruptly
than their fathers*. Their sin is described in the words of the golden calf, *they quickly turned
away from the way*. Yahweh's last word on it is the same sentence as Bochim's, *I also will no longer
drive out*, with a new purpose added: the nations stay *that by them I may test Israel*.

[[The Nations Left to Test Israel]] (3:1–6) closes the introduction. It names the nations left: *the
five lords of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites who lived on Mount
Lebanon*, the same open edges as Joshua 13:2–6. It gives the test again, *to know whether they would
listen to Yahweh's commandments*, and a new purpose, *to teach them war* to a generation that *had not
known all the wars of Canaan*. Then it reports the answer before any judge appears. Israel *lived
among* six peoples, the list of Exodus 3:8 in the same order. *They took their daughters to be their
wives, and gave their own daughters to their sons and served their gods* (3:6). The verbs are those of
Deuteronomy 7:3–4, which forbade exactly this.

[[Othniel the First Judge]] (3:7–11) is the cycle of 2:11–19 carried out once, with names. Israel
*forgot Yahweh* and served *the Baals and the Asheroth*; Yahweh *sold them* to Cushan Rishathaim, *king
of Mesopotamia*, for eight years; they *cried*, and Yahweh *raised up a savior*, Othniel, Caleb's
kinsman, already known from Kiriath Sepher. *Yahweh's Spirit came on him*, in the clause used of
Balaam (Numbers 24:2); *he judged Israel*, went to war, and *his hand prevailed*. *The land had rest
forty years*, in Joshua's word for rest from war, and then he died. Nobody speaks, and nothing goes
wrong. The judges after him each add something to the pattern.

[[Ehud and Eglon]] (3:12–30) is the first judge story told at length. Israel *again* does evil, and
this time Yahweh does not sell them but *strengthened Eglon the king of Moab*, with the verb of
Pharaoh's hardened heart. Moab, with Ammon and Amalek, takes *the city of palm trees* and rules
eighteen years. The savior is *Ehud the son of Gera, the Benjamite, a left-handed man*, a son of the
right hand who is restricted in his right hand. He carries Israel's tribute to a *very fat* king,
gains a private audience with *a secret message* and then *a message from God*, and kills him with a
hidden sword. The servants, finding the doors locked, assume the king is *covering his feet* and wait
*until they were ashamed*. Ehud blows the horn, Israel holds the fords of the Jordan, *no man escaped*,
and the land rests eighty years. No Spirit comes on him, he is never said to judge, and the narrator
neither praises nor blames the deception.

[[Shamgar and the Ox Goad]] (3:31) is a single verse: *After him was Shamgar the son of Anath, who
struck six hundred men of the Philistines with an ox goad. He also saved Israel.* Almost none of the
cycle is there. No sin, oppressor, cry, Spirit, judging, or rest is mentioned, and Yahweh is not
named. Only the verb *saved* remains, and *also* counts him with Othniel and Ehud. He is the first in
the book to fight the Philistines, the enemy Samson will only *begin* to save Israel from (13:5). The
next verse dates the relapse from *when Ehud was dead* (4:1). The Song of Deborah remembers *the days
of Shamgar* as a time of empty roads (5:6).

[[Deborah and Barak]] (4:1–16) dates the next relapse from Ehud's death. Yahweh *sold* Israel to
*Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor*, the name and city of the king Joshua killed, and his
captain Sisera's *nine hundred chariots of iron* oppress them twenty years. The deliverer is not raised
up after the cry; she is already there. *Deborah, a prophetess, … judged Israel at that time*, under
her palm between Ramah and Bethel, and Israel came to her for judgment. She sends Barak of Kedesh
Naphtali with Yahweh's command. He will go only if she goes, and she tells him *Yahweh will sell Sisera
into a woman's hand*. Heber the Kenite's tent is pitched near Kedesh. On her word Barak comes down
Mount Tabor, *Yahweh confused Sisera* and his chariots, the verb of the sea and of Gibeon, and not a man
of the army is left. Sisera escapes on foot.

[[Jael and Sisera]] (4:17–24) follows him to the tent of Jael, wife of Heber the Kenite, whose house is
at peace with Jabin. She welcomes him, *don't be afraid*, covers him, gives him milk, and while he sleeps
drives a tent peg through his temple. Barak arrives second and is shown the body: the *woman's hand* of
4:9 was Jael's, not Deborah's. The scene is told in the shape of Eglon's death, a lone killing found
afterwards, *behold*, dead. The narrator gives the victory to God, *God subdued Jabin the king of
Canaan*, and Israel's hand presses until Jabin is destroyed. The rest-formula waits until after the
Song.

[[The Song of Deborah]] (5:1–31) tells the same war again as a poem, *Then Deborah and Barak the son of
Abinoam sang on that day*, as Exodus sings the sea after narrating it. It begins with Yahweh marching
out of Seir while the earth trembles and Sinai quakes, and remembers *the days of Shamgar* as a time of
empty roads, until Deborah *arose a mother in Israel*. It names the tribes one by one: Ephraim, Benjamin,
Machir, Zebulun, Issachar, and Naphtali came; Reuben, Gilead, Dan, and Asher stayed, each asked *why*.
*The kings of Canaan fought at Taanach*, *the stars fought* from heaven, and *the river Kishon swept
them away*. Yahweh's angel curses Meroz for not coming; Jael is *blessed above women*, and Sisera falls
*at her feet*. The last scene is his mother at her lattice, imagining plunder. The Song never names
Jabin, and it lists six tribes where the prose had two; the wiki records those differences rather than
harmonizing them. *Then the land had rest forty years.*

## Structure

The book is usually read in three parts, and the pericope plan below follows them:

1. **A double introduction (1:1–3:6).** Chapter 1 surveys the tribes' campaigns and, more and more,
   the cities they *did not drive out*. Chapter 2 retells Joshua's death and lays out the cycle the
   rest of the book will follow: Israel serves the Baals, Yahweh gives them into the hands of
   raiders, he raises up judges, and after each judge dies they turn back (2:11–19). Then come
   the nations left *to test Israel* (3:1–6).
2. **The judges (3:7–16:31).** Othniel, Ehud, Shamgar, Deborah and Barak, Gideon, the
   anti-judge Abimelech, Tola and Jair, Jephthah, Ibzan, Elon and Abdon, and Samson. The cycle
   frays as it goes: the deliverers become more compromised and the tribes less united.
3. **A double epilogue (17:1–21:25).** No judge appears. Micah's shrine and the migration of Dan;
   the outrage at Gibeah and the war against Benjamin. The refrain about having no king frames
   these chapters.

## Key themes
- [[War]] — the tribes' campaigns, and at the end a war of Israel against one of its own tribes.
- [[Retribution]] — *As I have done, so God has done to me* (1:7), the first of the book's
  measure-for-measure reversals.
- [[Land and Sojourning]] — the land allotted in Joshua, still to be taken in Judges.
- [[Brotherhood]] — Judah and Simeon fight together (1:3); Judah leads against Benjamin (20:18).
- [[Slavery and Servitude]] — forced labor in place of driving out (1:28–35).
- [[The Nations]] — the Canaanites and Amorites who stay (1:27–36), and the nations left to test Israel (3:1–6).
- [[Testing]] — the nations left *to test Israel by them* (2:22; 3:1, 4).
- [[Marriage]] — daughters taken and given with the land's peoples (3:6).
- [[Prophecy and Vision]] — Deborah, *a prophetess* who judges Israel (4:4).

## Key people
- [[Judah]] — the tribe named first, at the book's beginning and near its end.
- [[Simeon]] — goes up with Judah into his lot.
- [[Adoni-Bezek]] — the first enemy king, and the first to confess God's repayment.
- [[Joshua (son of Nun)]] — dead before the book begins.
- [[Joseph]] — *the house of Joseph* takes Bethel (1:22–25).
- [[The Canaanites]] — not driven out (1:27–33).
- [[The Amorites]] — press Dan from the valley (1:34–36).
- [[The Philistines]] — their five lords head the nations left (3:3); Samson's enemies.
- [[The Hivites]] — on Mount Lebanon (3:3), and among the six Israel lives among (3:5).
- [[Deborah (prophetess)]] — prophetess and judge (4:4–14).
- [[Barak]] — commander against Sisera (4:6–16).
- [[Sisera]] and [[Jabin (of Deborah's time)]] — the oppressors with nine hundred chariots (4:2–3).

## Key places
- [[Bezek]] — the first battle.
- [[Jerusalem]] — where Adoni-Bezek is brought to die.
- [[Canaan]] — the land still being taken.
- [[Bethel]] — taken by the house of Joseph; once Luz (1:22–26).
- [[Lebanon]], [[Hermon]], [[Hamath]] — the northern edge of the nations left (3:3).
- [[Tabor]], [[Kishon]], [[Kedesh]], [[Harosheth of the Gentiles]] — the battle with Sisera (4:6–16).
