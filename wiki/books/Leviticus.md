# Leviticus

## Overview

Leviticus continues the sanctuary narrative of [[Exodus]] with Yahweh speaking to
[[Moses]] from the Tent of Meeting. It orders Israel’s approach through offerings and
priestly service, distinguishes clean and unclean, and extends holiness into households,
sexual relations, economic life, the calendar, and the land. Divine dwelling among the
people requires both ritual order and conduct toward the neighbor.

The book presents its instructions as divine speech mediated chiefly through Moses.
The priestly inauguration and the deaths of Nadab and Abihu interrupt the legal material
with narrative. The Sinai setting is explicitly recalled in 7:38; 25:1; 26:46; 27:34.
This overview and queue survey the stored book; only checked units below are ingested.

The first three ingested units distinguish whole burnt offerings, grain memorials with
most holy priestly remainders, and the peace offering’s assigned fat portions. Leviticus 2
names covenant salt; chapter 3 permits male or female livestock and closes with the
prohibition on eating fat and blood. The meat-sharing rules follow in chapter 7.

The next unit (4:1–21) addresses unintentional sin by the anointed priest and congregation.
Blood enters the tent, carcasses leave the camp, and the congregation is promised forgiveness
after atonement. This adds liability and remedy to the opening account of approach.

The ruler and ordinary-person cases (4:22–35) complete the chapter’s prescribed groups.
Their blood remains at the outer altar; male goat, female goat, and female lamb procedures
all conclude with forgiveness. Authority does not exempt the ruler from the divine commands.

Leviticus 5:1–13 adds specific confession and offerings adjusted to means: a flock
animal, two birds, or flour. Even the flour sin offering receives the explicit promise
of atonement and forgiveness; unclean contact and failures of speech enter the cases.

Leviticus 5:14–19 adds the ram trespass offering: restitution plus a fifth accompanies
the holy-things case, while the second case names liability without knowledge and a remedy
of priestly atonement and forgiveness without repeating the restitution clause.

Leviticus 6:1–7 joins fraud against the neighbor to trespass against Yahweh: the owner
receives full repayment plus a fifth, while the priest makes atonement with the ram.
The listed deception cases prevent restricting every offering law to accidental wrongdoing.

Leviticus 6:8–23 turns to priestly maintenance and portions: continual fire requires
daily tending, holy grain is eaten in the court, and the priest’s own meal offering
is wholly burned rather than eaten.

Leviticus 6:24–7:10 regulates holy flesh, garments, and vessels, then distinguishes
priestly portions. Sanctuary blood excludes eating; hides and grain have assigned recipients.
The wording tension between individual and shared grain portions is recorded on
[[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]].

Leviticus 7:11–21 distinguishes thanksgiving, vow, and freewill peace-offering meals,
assigns accompanying bread, and regulates eating by time and purity. Sacred sharing
does not remove the sanctions for prohibited eating. See [[Thanksgiving, Vows, and the Peace Offering]].

## Structure

- 1–7: offerings and priestly handling of them.
- 8–10: consecration, inaugural service, and the crisis of unauthorized fire.
- 11–15: clean and unclean creatures, bodies, garments, and houses.
- 16: the annual atonement rite and guarded entry.
- 17–22: sacrifice, blood, communal holiness, and priestly obligations.
- 23–25: sacred times, sanctuary service, the divine name, and land redemption.
- 26–27: covenant blessings and warnings; vows, valuations, and devoted things.
