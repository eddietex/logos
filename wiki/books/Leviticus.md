# Leviticus

## Overview

Leviticus continues the sanctuary narrative of [[Exodus]] with Yahweh speaking to
[[Moses]] from the Tent of Meeting. It orders Israel’s approach through offerings and
priestly service, distinguishes clean and unclean, and extends holiness into households,
sexual relations, economic life, the calendar, and the land. Divine dwelling among the
people requires both ritual order and conduct toward the neighbor.

The book presents its instructions as divine speech mediated chiefly through Moses.
The priestly inauguration and the deaths of Nadab and Abihu interrupt the legal material
with narrative. The Sinai setting is explicitly recalled in 7:38; 25:1; 26:46; 27:34.
This overview and queue survey the stored book; only checked units below are ingested.

The first three ingested units distinguish whole burnt offerings, grain memorials with
most holy priestly remainders, and the peace offering’s assigned fat portions. Leviticus 2
names covenant salt; chapter 3 permits male or female livestock and closes with the
prohibition on eating fat and blood. The meat-sharing rules follow in chapter 7.

The next unit (4:1–21) addresses unintentional sin by the anointed priest and congregation.
Blood enters the tent, carcasses leave the camp, and the congregation is promised forgiveness
after atonement. This adds liability and remedy to the opening account of approach.

## Structure

- 1–7: offerings and priestly handling of them.
- 8–10: consecration, inaugural service, and the crisis of unauthorized fire.
- 11–15: clean and unclean creatures, bodies, garments, and houses.
- 16: the annual atonement rite and guarded entry.
- 17–22: sacrifice, blood, communal holiness, and priestly obligations.
- 23–25: sacred times, sanctuary service, the divine name, and land redemption.
- 26–27: covenant blessings and warnings; vows, valuations, and devoted things.
