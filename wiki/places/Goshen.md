# Goshen

## Overview

**The district of Egypt that Israel lives in for four hundred years, named for the first time in
Scripture by [[Joseph]] in the message he sends to his father** — *"You shall dwell in the land of
Goshen, and you will be near to me"* (Genesis 45:10).

**Genesis introduces it without explaining it.** There is no formula of the kind the book uses for
[[Bethel]] or [[Beersheba]] — no naming speech, no etymology, no *"therefore the name of the place is
called."* **Joseph names it as though [[Jacob]] will know where it is**, and the narrator lets him.
**It is the only major Israelite address in the Torah that the Torah never explains.**

**Its location is not given.** What the text supplies is relational: **near to Joseph** (45:10),
**good pasture** (46:34; 47:6), **separate enough that shepherds can live there** in a country where
*"every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians"* (46:34), and **on the way in from [[Canaan]]**,
since Judah is sent ahead to it (46:28). **The standard identification is the eastern Nile delta**,
which fits all four conditions; **Genesis itself commits to none of them as geography.**

**Goshen's whole function in Scripture is separation.** It is where a family can be foreign and stay
foreign — **near the court and not of it** — and the arrangement that makes Israel survivable in Egypt
in Genesis is the same arrangement that makes it visible and enslaveable in Exodus. See
[[Land and Sojourning]] and [[The Nations]].

## Appears in

- [[Joseph Reveals Himself]] — Genesis 45:10; **the first mention in Scripture.** Joseph assigns it in
  the message dictated for his father, **before Pharaoh has offered anything**, and his stated reason
  is proximity: *"you will be near to me, you, your children, your children's children, your flocks,
  your herds, and all that you have."* **[[Pharaoh (of Joseph's time)]]'s counter-offer eight verses
  later is wider and vaguer** — *"the good of the land of Egypt"*, *"the fat of the land"* (45:18, 20)
  — **and Goshen is the one that actually happens** (47:6, *not yet ingested*). The chapter names it
  once and never again

## Related places

## Events here

- **Israel settles in Goshen and is given it by Pharaoh** — Genesis 46:28–47:6 *(not yet ingested)*
- **Jacob lives there seventeen years and dies there** — Genesis 47:28 *(not yet ingested)*
- **The plagues fall on Egypt and not on Goshen** — Exodus 8:22; 9:26 — *"I will set apart in that day
  the land of Goshen, in which my people dwell, that no swarms of flies shall be there"*
- **Israel leaves from Rameses, in the same district** — Exodus 12:37; Genesis 47:11 calls the
  settlement *"the land of Rameses"*
- **Joshua 10:41 and 11:16 name a different Goshen** — a region in the south of Canaan — **which is not
  this one**, and the wiki keeps them separate
