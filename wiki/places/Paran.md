# Paran

## Overview

The great desert south of Canaan where [[Ishmael]] grows up, and afterwards the country Israel spends
its wilderness years in. It is the one place in the Bible that is a home to the son who was sent away
and a wasteland to everyone else.

**Where it is.** The central and eastern Sinai and the desert running north toward
[[Kadesh]] — Numbers 13:26 puts Kadesh *"in the wilderness of Paran"*, and Deuteronomy 1:1 lists it
among the bearings for the plains east of the Jordan, which between them make it a large and
loosely-bounded region rather than a location. **No boundary for it is recoverable**, and no
settlement in it is named. Genesis mentions its southern extremity once before, at 14:6, as
*"El Paran, which is by the wilderness"* — the turning point of the four kings' campaign, usually
identified with the head of the Gulf of Aqaba.

**In Genesis it appears exactly twice**, at 14:6 and at 21:21: *"He lived in the wilderness of Paran.
His mother took a wife for him out of the land of Egypt."* That is the last the book says about
Ishmael's address until 25:18 puts his descendants *"from Havilah to Shur that is before Egypt"* — a
range that runs along the same desert. See [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]].

**What the rest of the Bible does with it.**

- **It is the wilderness of the wandering.** Israel's first camp after leaving Sinai is *"in the
  wilderness of Paran"* (Numbers 10:12; 12:16); the twelve spies are sent out from it (Numbers 13:3)
  and return to it (13:26); and the generation that refuses to go up dies in it.
- **It is a place God comes from.** Two of the oldest poems in the canon put him there: *"Yahweh came
  from Sinai, and rose from Seir to them. He shone from Mount Paran"* (Deuteronomy 33:2), and *"God
  came from Teman, the Holy One from Mount Paran"* (Habakkuk 3:3). Which mountain is meant is not
  known, and the poems are not trying to say.
- **It is a road out.** Hadad the Edomite flees through Paran on his way to Egypt (1 Kings 11:18) —
  the same direction Hagar took at 16:7 and the same frontier.

**A note on the name.** *Pāʾrān* has no agreed etymology; a connection with *pəʾēr*, to glorify or
adorn, is often proposed and is not more than a guess. The Islamic identification of Paran with the
region of Mecca is late reception built on Genesis 21:21 and is not a reading available from the
geography of the Torah, which puts Israel's spies in it.

## Appears in

- [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]] — Genesis 21:21; where Ishmael grows up an archer,
  and where his mother takes him an Egyptian wife
- [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] — Genesis 14:6, as *El Paran*, the far point of the four
  kings' sweep down the eastern frontier

## Related places

- [[Kadesh]] — the oasis Numbers 13:26 places inside it, and the wilderness generation's address
- [[Beersheba]] — the wilderness to its north, where the boy is laid under a bush before he reaches
  this one
- [[Shur]] — the desert on the Egyptian frontier, and the other half of Ishmael's later range
- [[Egypt]] — where his mother came from and where his wife is fetched from
- [[Beer Lahai Roi]] — the well of the first flight, in the same desert belt
- [[Teman]] — named beside Mount Paran in Habakkuk 3:3
- [[Canaan]] — the land it lies south of, and which Israel will fail to enter from it

## Events here

- Chedorlaomer's coalition turns at *El Paran, which is by the wilderness* — Genesis 14:6,
  [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]
- Ishmael grows up here an archer, and marries an Egyptian — Genesis 21:20–21,
  [[The Birth of Isaac and the Sending of Hagar]]
- Israel camps here after Sinai, and Miriam's exclusion ends here — Numbers 10:12; 12:16
- The twelve spies are sent from here and report back here — Numbers 13:3, 26
- *"He shone from Mount Paran"* — Deuteronomy 33:2; Habakkuk 3:3
- Hadad the Edomite passes through on his flight to Egypt — 1 Kings 11:18
