# Salem

## Overview

Melchizedek's city, named once in Genesis and almost certainly the first appearance of Jerusalem in
the Bible.

*"Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine: and he was priest of God Most High"*
(Genesis 14:18). That is the whole of what Genesis says about the place: it has a king, the king is
a priest of *ʾĒl ʿElyôn*, and it is near enough to the valley of Shaveh for him to come out to a
returning army. See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

**The identification with Jerusalem rests on one verse and a great deal of convergence.** Psalm 76:2
sets the two names in parallel — *"His tabernacle is in Salem. His dwelling place in Zion"* — which
is as close to an explicit equation as the Hebrew Bible gives. Around it: Joshua 10:1 has
*Adoni-Zedek king of Jerusalem*, a royal name formed exactly like Melchizedek's; the King's Valley
of Genesis 14:17 is placed near Jerusalem by 2 Samuel 18:18; the fourteenth-century Amarna letters
call the city *Urusalim*; and the Genesis Apocryphon from Qumran glosses the valley of Shaveh as
*"the Valley of Beth-hakkerem"*, on the Jerusalem side. The one serious alternative, a Salem near
Shechem, is argued from John 3:23's *Aenon near Salim* and from Genesis 33:18 in some readings, and
has never had many defenders.

**Its name is a sentence too.** *Shālēm* shares its three consonants with *shālôm*, **peace**, and
Hebrews 7:2 reads it that way: Melchizedek is *king of righteousness* by his name and *king of
peace* by his city. Whether the city's name originally invoked *Shalim*, a West Semitic deity of
dusk, is disputed and does not affect the wordplay the Bible builds on it.

**Why it matters that this is the first Jerusalem.** Genesis 14 puts a priest of God Most High on
that site four hundred years before Israel exists and roughly a thousand before David takes the city
(2 Samuel 5:6–9) and Solomon builds on it. Psalm 110 then makes that priesthood the pattern for the
Davidic king who reigns there, and Hebrews 5–7 makes it the pattern for a priesthood that outranks
Levi's. Everything the city becomes is prefigured by three verses in which a stranger brings out
bread and wine.

## Appears in

- [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] — Genesis 14:18; Melchizedek comes out from it

## Related places

- [[Sodom]] — the other king in the valley of Shaveh comes from there; the chapter sets the two
  cities' rulers side by side and lets the comparison stand
- [[Hebron]] — Abram's own address, some nineteen miles south
- [[Canaan]] — the land it sits in, and which Abram has been promised
- [[Moriah]] — the land of Genesis 22, which 2 Chronicles 3:1 puts in Jerusalem; if that
  identification holds, Abraham has been to this city before, at 14:18, and Genesis never connects
  the two scenes

## Events here

- Melchizedek comes out with bread and wine, blesses Abram, and is given a tenth of the spoil —
  Genesis 14:18–20, [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]]
- Named in parallel with Zion — Psalm 76:2
- Read as *king of peace* — Hebrews 7:2, [[Melchizedek in Psalm 110 and Hebrews]]
