# Adullam

## Overview

**The Canaanite town Judah goes down to when he leaves his brothers — and the first place any son of
Jacob lives apart from the household.**

> **1** At that time, Judah went down from his brothers, and visited a certain Adullamite, whose name
> was Hirah. (38:1)

**It is in the Shephelah**, the belt of low hills between the Judean highlands and the coastal plain,
about halfway between Hebron and the sea and roughly sixteen miles west-north-west of Hebron. **The
verb is *wayyēred*, *he went down*** — topographically correct, since the Shephelah is a thousand feet
below the ridge, **and the same verb Genesis is using in the same week for Joseph's descent to Egypt**
(37:25; 39:1). See [[Judah and Tamar]] and [[Land and Sojourning]].

**What it means for the chapter is that Judah has moved out of the covenant household and into
Canaanite country**, where his friend, his wife, and his sons' birthplaces all are. **The line of David
is conceived there**, and Genesis makes no remark about the geography at all.

## Later

- **Joshua 12:15** lists the king of Adullam among the thirty-one kings defeated in the conquest, and
  **Joshua 15:35** puts the town in the Shephelah district of Judah's inheritance — **the same district
  as Achzib**, which is very probably this chapter's [[Chezib]].
- **1 Samuel 22:1–2** — *"David departed from there, and escaped to the cave of Adullam"*, where the
  distressed, the indebted, and the discontented gather to him, four hundred men. **The place Judah
  went down to alone is where his most famous descendant hides**, and the wiki notes the coincidence
  without pressing it: 2 Samuel 23:13 puts *the cave of Adullam* in the same story again.
- **2 Chronicles 11:7** — fortified by Rehoboam; **Nehemiah 11:30** — resettled after the exile;
  **Micah 1:15** — *"he will come to Adullam, the glory of Israel"*, in the same oracle that puns on
  Achzib.

The site is generally identified with **Tell esh-Sheikh Madhkur**, near modern Khirbet ʿId el-Mâ,
which preserves the name.
