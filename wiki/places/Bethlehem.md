# Bethlehem

## Overview

### The neighbor women — Ruth 4:13–17

The town's women, who asked *Is this Naomi?* when she came home (1:19), speak again at the birth of Obed:
they bless Yahweh to Naomi and name the child, *A son is born to Naomi* (4:14–17). The blessing on Boaz at
the gate, *be famous in Bethlehem* (4:11), widens for the child to *let his name be famous in Israel* (4:14).
Bethlehem is now the town of Jesse and David's family (4:17). See [[The Birth of Obed]]. Source: stored WEB
Ruth 1:19; 4:11–17.

### The gate — Ruth 4:1–12

The town's gate is where Boaz settles the redemption: ten elders of the city sit as witnesses, and *all
the people who were in the gate* answer *We are witnesses* (4:1–2, 9–11). Boaz had already called the town
*all the gate of my people* (3:11, WLC). The dead man's name is kept *from the gate of his place* (4:10).
The blessing uses both names of the place, *Ephrathah* and *Bethlehem* (11); the family were *Ephrathites
of Bethlehem Judah* (1:2), and Rachel was buried *on the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem)* (Genesis
35:19). See [[Boaz Redeems at the Gate]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:2; 4:1–12; Genesis 35:19; Hebrew WLC,
2026-11-17.

### Bread again — Ruth 1:6–7

*Yahweh had visited his people in giving them bread* (1:6), *lāṯēṯ lāhem lāḥem* (WLC), *to give them
bread*: the bread returns to *Bêt Leḥem*, the house of bread. Naomi sets out *to return to the land of
Judah* (7); the town is not named again until 1:19. See [[Ruth Clings to Naomi]]. Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:6–7, 19; Hebrew WLC,
2026-11-17.

### Famine in the house of bread — Ruth 1:1–2

*In the days when the judges judged, there was a famine in the land. A certain man of Bethlehem Judah went
to live in the country of Moab* (Ruth 1:1). The town whose name means *house of bread* is the one that
runs out, and its people are *Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah* (1:2), the older name *Ephrath* of Genesis
35:19 surviving as a clan name. The departure repeats the words of the Levite's at Judges 17:8, *wayyēleḵ
… mibbêt leḥem yəhûdâ lāgûr* (WLC): this is the third time in two books someone leaves Bethlehem Judah. See
[[Famine and Death in Moab]] and [[Went to Sojourn from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17.8 and Ruth 1.1]].
Source: stored WEB Ruth 1:1–2; Genesis 35:19; Judges 17:8; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-17.

### The concubine's father's house — Judges 19:1–9

The second Levite story of the epilogue also turns on *Bethlehem Judah*: the Levite of the far hill
country of Ephraim takes a concubine from there, she returns to her father's house there, and her father
keeps him five days with food and drink (19:1–9). The road between Bethlehem and the hill country of
Ephraim is the same one Micah's Levite walked the other way (17:7–8). See [[The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah]],
[[The Levite's Father-in-Law]], and [[Two Levites from Bethlehem Judah - Judges 17 and 19]]. Source: stored
WEB Judges 17:7–8; 19:1–9, 18.

*Bêt Leḥem*, **house of bread** — and Genesis introduces it inside a parenthesis, at a graveside.

> **19** Rachel died, and was buried on the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem). (35:19)

**The name the town carries through the rest of Scripture is supplied here as a gloss on an older
one.** Genesis uses *Ephrath* for the place and *Bethlehem* for the reader, in the same aside-making
habit that gave us *"Luz (that is, Bethel)"* at 35:6 and *"Kiriath Arba (which is Hebron)"* at 35:27
— **three glosses in one chapter**, all of them the narrator translating an old map for a later
audience. See [[Names and Naming]].

***ʾEprāt*** / ***ʾEprātâ*** is probably **fruitful**, from *p-r-h*, the root of *pərû ûrəbû* five
verses earlier. The two names are used interchangeably afterwards and sometimes together: *"Bethlehem
Ephrathah"* (Micah 5:2), *"Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah"* (Ruth 1:2). **Both names mean abundance,
which is the one thing the town is never said to have.** Ruth opens with a famine in the house of
bread.

**Where it is.** Five or six miles south of Jerusalem on the ridge road, at about 2,500 feet, in the
territory of Judah — **which is the difficulty this page exists to record.** See below.

### A town built almost entirely out of other people's stories

- **Rachel's grave** (35:19–20; 48:7) — the first and only Genesis event here, and one of the most
  disputed sites in the Bible.
- **Ruth and Boaz** (Ruth 1:1–2; 4:11) — the book begins with a Bethlehem family leaving for Moab in
  a famine and ends with the elders at the gate blessing a bride *"like Rachel and like Leah, which
  two built the house of Israel"* — **an invocation of Rachel spoken in the town Genesis says she is
  buried outside.** See [[Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel]].
- **David** (1 Samuel 16:1–13; 17:12) — Jesse's house, where Samuel finds the eighth son keeping
  sheep; *"the well of Bethlehem, which is by the gate"* is the water three of his men break through a
  Philistine garrison to fetch, and which he pours out on the ground rather than drink (2 Samuel
  23:15–17).
- **Micah 5:2** — *"But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, being small among the clans of Judah, out of you one
  will come out to me that is to be ruler in Israel"* — **the verse that makes a village's smallness
  the point**, and the one Matthew 2:5–6 has the chief priests quote to Herod.
- **Matthew 2 and Luke 2** — the birth, the shepherds, the magi, and the killing of the infants, which
  Matthew reads through Jeremiah 31:15: *"A voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and great mourning,
  Rachel weeping for her children."* **The town's first grave and its most famous night are joined by
  a quotation.**

### The problem with the gloss

**Genesis puts Rachel's tomb near Bethlehem. Two other passages put it about ten miles north, on the
far side of Jerusalem, in Benjamin.**

- **1 Samuel 10:2** — Samuel tells Saul he will meet two men *"by Rachel's tomb, in the border of
  Benjamin at Zelzah"*, on an itinerary that is clearly running north from Ramah.
- **Jeremiah 31:15** — *"A voice is heard in **Ramah**, lamentation and bitter weeping, Rachel weeping
  for her children"* — Ramah being about five miles north of Jerusalem, and the staging point for the
  deportation to Babylon (Jeremiah 40:1).

**The northern location also fits the family better**: Benjamin is the son whose birth killed her, and
a mother buried on the border of her son's territory is a more legible arrangement than one buried in
Judah. **The wiki lays out both locations and adopts neither**; see
[[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]] for the full case and the readings that have been
offered.

**What is not in doubt is which identification the New Testament uses.** Matthew 2:18 applies
Jeremiah's Ramah verse to Bethlehem's infants, which only works if Rachel is understood to be
mourning at the southern site — **and by Matthew's day the traditional tomb on the Bethlehem road was
long established.** It is still shown there.

### Ibzan *of Bethlehem* — which Bethlehem? (Judges 12:8–10)

*After him Ibzan of Bethlehem judged Israel … Ibzan died, and was buried at Bethlehem* (12:8, 10). The
text gives no tribe. Everywhere else in Judges the town is *Bethlehem Judah* (17:7–9; 19:1–2, 18), and
in the WLC each of those occurrences has *yəhûdâ*; only Ibzan's two verses leave *bêt leḥem* bare.
Joshua 19:15 lists a Bethlehem in Zebulun, and the judge after Ibzan is a Zebulunite. The text supports
either town; the wiki records Ibzan on both pages and chooses neither. A later Jewish tradition
identifying Ibzan with Boaz of this Bethlehem is recalled (Bava Batra 91a), not verified. See
[[Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon]] and [[Bethlehem (Zebulun)]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 12:8–10; 17:7–9;
19:1–2, 18; Joshua 19:15; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-16.

### David anointed — 1 Samuel 16:1–13

Samuel comes to Bethlehem with a heifer and a horn of oil. *The elders of the city came to meet him trembling, and
said, "Do you come peaceably?"* (16:4). He sanctifies Jesse and his sons for the sacrifice, and anoints the youngest,
David, *in the middle of his brothers* (16:5, 13). Jesse is *the Bethlehemite*, *bêṯ hallaḥmî* (16:1, WLC). Ruth ended
with this family in this town (Ruth 4:17); the king comes from it here. See [[Samuel Anoints David]]. Source: stored
WEB Ruth 4:17; 1 Samuel 16:1–13; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-20.

### David sent from Bethlehem to Saul — 1 Samuel 16:18–20

Saul's servant identifies David by his town, *a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite* (16:18), and Jesse sends him from it to
Saul's court with bread, wine, and a young goat (16:20). 17:15 has him going *back and forth from Saul to feed his
father's sheep at Bethlehem* (stored WEB). See [[David Plays the Harp for Saul]] and [[David Comes to the Camp]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:14–23; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-21.

### Jesse's house sends David to the war — 1 Samuel 17:12–20

Chapter 17 names the town in Ruth's words: Jesse is *that Ephrathite of Bethlehem Judah* (17:12), as Elimelech's sons
were *Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah* (Ruth 1:2). David keeps his father's sheep *at Bethlehem* (17:15), and goes from
it to the valley of Elah with grain, loaves, and cheeses (17:17–20). Eliab places the flock *in the wilderness* (17:28).
See [[David Comes to the Camp]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 17:12–28; Ruth 1:2.

### *Jesse the Bethlehemite* — 1 Samuel 17:58

Asked whose son he is, David names his father and his town: *I am the son of your servant Jesse the Bethlehemite*
(17:58), *bêṯ hallaḥmî* (WLC), as at 16:1 and 16:18. Saul then keeps him from going *home to his father's house*
(18:2), and Bethlehem drops out of David's story for now. See [[Jonathan Loves David]]. Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 16:1, 18;
17:58; 18:2.

  chapter
