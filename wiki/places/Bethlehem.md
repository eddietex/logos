# Bethlehem

## Overview

*Bêt Leḥem*, **house of bread** — and Genesis introduces it inside a parenthesis, at a graveside.

> **19** Rachel died, and was buried on the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem). (35:19)

**The name the town carries through the rest of Scripture is supplied here as a gloss on an older
one.** Genesis uses *Ephrath* for the place and *Bethlehem* for the reader, in the same aside-making
habit that gave us *"Luz (that is, Bethel)"* at 35:6 and *"Kiriath Arba (which is Hebron)"* at 35:27
— **three glosses in one chapter**, all of them the narrator translating an old map for a later
audience. See [[Names and Naming]].

***ʾEprāt*** / ***ʾEprātâ*** is probably **fruitful**, from *p-r-h*, the root of *pərû ûrəbû* five
verses earlier. The two names are used interchangeably afterwards and sometimes together: *"Bethlehem
Ephrathah"* (Micah 5:2), *"Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah"* (Ruth 1:2). **Both names mean abundance,
which is the one thing the town is never said to have.** Ruth opens with a famine in the house of
bread.

**Where it is.** Five or six miles south of Jerusalem on the ridge road, at about 2,500 feet, in the
territory of Judah — **which is the difficulty this page exists to record.** See below.

### A town built almost entirely out of other people's stories

- **Rachel's grave** (35:19–20; 48:7) — the first and only Genesis event here, and one of the most
  disputed sites in the Bible.
- **Ruth and Boaz** (Ruth 1:1–2; 4:11) — the book begins with a Bethlehem family leaving for Moab in
  a famine and ends with the elders at the gate blessing a bride *"like Rachel and like Leah, which
  two built the house of Israel"* — **an invocation of Rachel spoken in the town Genesis says she is
  buried outside.** See [[Like Rachel and Like Leah - The Builders of the House of Israel]].
- **David** (1 Samuel 16:1–13; 17:12) — Jesse's house, where Samuel finds the eighth son keeping
  sheep; *"the well of Bethlehem, which is by the gate"* is the water three of his men break through a
  Philistine garrison to fetch, and which he pours out on the ground rather than drink (2 Samuel
  23:15–17).
- **Micah 5:2** — *"But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, being small among the clans of Judah, out of you one
  will come out to me that is to be ruler in Israel"* — **the verse that makes a village's smallness
  the point**, and the one Matthew 2:5–6 has the chief priests quote to Herod.
- **Matthew 2 and Luke 2** — the birth, the shepherds, the magi, and the killing of the infants, which
  Matthew reads through Jeremiah 31:15: *"A voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and great mourning,
  Rachel weeping for her children."* **The town's first grave and its most famous night are joined by
  a quotation.**

### The problem with the gloss

**Genesis puts Rachel's tomb near Bethlehem. Two other passages put it about ten miles north, on the
far side of Jerusalem, in Benjamin.**

- **1 Samuel 10:2** — Samuel tells Saul he will meet two men *"by Rachel's tomb, in the border of
  Benjamin at Zelzah"*, on an itinerary that is clearly running north from Ramah.
- **Jeremiah 31:15** — *"A voice is heard in **Ramah**, lamentation and bitter weeping, Rachel weeping
  for her children"* — Ramah being about five miles north of Jerusalem, and the staging point for the
  deportation to Babylon (Jeremiah 40:1).

**The northern location also fits the family better**: Benjamin is the son whose birth killed her, and
a mother buried on the border of her son's territory is a more legible arrangement than one buried in
Judah. **The wiki lays out both locations and adopts neither**; see
[[Rachel Weeping - Her Grave, Ramah, and Bethlehem]] for the full case and the readings that have been
offered.

**What is not in doubt is which identification the New Testament uses.** Matthew 2:18 applies
Jeremiah's Ramah verse to Bethlehem's infants, which only works if Rachel is understood to be
mourning at the southern site — **and by Matthew's day the traditional tomb on the Bethlehem road was
long established.** It is still shown there.

## Appears in

- [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]] — Genesis 35:19–20; **named only as a gloss on
  *Ephrath***, in the verse that buries [[Rachel]] on the road outside it and raises the last of
  [[Jacob]]'s four pillars over her. **The one matriarch not carried to [[Machpelah]]** is left here,
  and the narrator adds *"to this day"*
- [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]] — Genesis 48:7; **named the same way a second time, thirteen
  chapters later, in [[Jacob]]'s own voice** — *"on the way to Ephrath (also called Bethlehem)"* —
  **with *Ephrath* twice and the later name supplied in a parenthesis for the reader, exactly as at
  35:19.** **It is the only thing he says about [[Rachel]] in the chapter that adopts her two
  grandsons**, and the wiki reads the verse as the premise of that adoption rather than as a digression.
  **The man speaking has, four verses earlier, put [[Joseph]] under oath to bury him at [[Machpelah]]**
  (47:29–30), **where [[Leah]] is and she is not**

## Related places

- [[Machpelah]] — the family tomb at Hebron, twenty miles further south, where the other five
  patriarchal burials are and Rachel's is not
- [[Hebron]] — where Jacob is headed when he buries her, and where Isaac dies at the end of the same
  chapter
- [[Bethel]] — the town he has just left, fifteen miles north; **Rachel dies between the two
  sanctuaries of the Jacob cycle**
- [[The Tower of Eder]] — the next landmark named, just past the grave
- [[Canaan]] — the land the road runs through

## Events here

- Rachel dies on the road to Ephrath bearing [[Benjamin]], and is buried with a pillar over the grave
  — Genesis 35:16–20, [[Return to Bethel; the Deaths of Rachel and Isaac]]
- Jacob retells the burial to Joseph, using the same obscure distance-word — Genesis 48:7,
  [[Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh]]
- Naomi's family leaves in a famine; Ruth returns; the elders bless Boaz's bride at the gate —
  Ruth 1:1–2; 4:11
- Samuel anoints David at Jesse's house — 1 Samuel 16:1–13
- Three of David's men fetch water from the well by the gate, and he pours it out — 2 Samuel 23:15–17
- *"But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, being small among the clans of Judah…"* — Micah 5:2
- The birth of Jesus, the shepherds, and the killing of the infants — Matthew 2:1–18; Luke 2:1–20
