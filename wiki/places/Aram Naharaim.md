# Aram Naharaim

## Overview

The country the family came from and kept going back to — **the region that supplies the mothers of
Israel for three generations, and never receives a single promise.**

*"He arose, and went to Mesopotamia, to the city of Nahor"* (Genesis 24:10). The Hebrew is
***ʾĂram Naharayim***, **Aram of the two rivers**, which the WEB renders by the Greek name,
*Mesopotamia*, the same word meaning the same thing. The *naharayim* is a dual — two rivers — and
the region meant is the great bend of the upper Euphrates and its tributaries, particularly the
Balikh and the Khabur, in what is now northern Syria and southeastern Turkey. It is not lower
Mesopotamia; [[Ur of the Chaldees]] is hundreds of miles downstream, and Genesis treats the two as
different places.

**Genesis names the same region two ways and the wiki keeps them on one page.** *Aram Naharaim*
appears at 24:10; ***Paddan Aram*** — *the field* or *plain of Aram* — is the designation the Jacob
material uses throughout (25:20; 28:2, 5–7; 31:18; 33:18; 35:9, 26; 46:15). Deuteronomy 23:4 and
Judges 3:8 use *Aram Naharaim*; Psalm 60's title uses it for a place David fought. They are the same
country under two names, and the difference is one of narrative source rather than of geography.

### The city of Nahor

*"To the city of Nahor"* — *ʿîr Nāḥôr* — and Genesis never says which city that is. Two readings, both
respectable:

- **It is [[Haran (city)]]**, the town Terah's household stopped at and Terah died in (11:31–32),
  described by its most prominent resident: *Nahor's town*. Genesis 27:43 and 28:10 send Jacob to
  *Haran* for the same errand to the same family, which is the strongest argument.
- **It is a distinct town called Nahor.** A city *Naḫur* is well attested in the Mari archives of the
  eighteenth century BC, on the Balikh, within a short journey of Haran — and the same district
  supplies matches for [[Serug]], [[Terah]], and possibly [[Reu]]. On this reading Genesis is naming
  a real neighbouring town, and the family register is a map of the district.

**Which Nahor the city is named for is also ambiguous**, since Abraham's brother
[[Nahor (brother of Abram)]] carries his own grandfather's name (11:22–26). Genesis 31:53's *"the God
of Nahor"* has the same ambiguity.

### What happens here, and what does not

**Every wife in the covenant line for three generations comes out of this country.**

- [[Rebekah]] is fetched from it for [[Isaac]] (24:10, 67); 25:20 calls her *"the daughter of Bethuel
  the Syrian of Paddan Aram, the sister of Laban the Syrian"*.
- Leah and Rachel are married here, and eleven of the twelve tribes are fathered here (29–30); 35:26
  lists *"the sons of Jacob, who were born to him in Paddan Aram."*
- Jacob serves [[Laban]] twenty years in it and leaves under pursuit (31).

**And nothing is ever promised about it.** No altar is built here, no theophany occurs here, and the
one non-negotiable clause of the oath in Genesis 24 is that Isaac must never set foot in it: *"Beware
that you don't bring my son there again"* (24:6, 8). **The land the covenant marries into is the land
the covenant may not return to.** See [[Land and Sojourning]] and [[A Wife for Isaac]].

Israel's own creed keeps the memory and keeps it uncomfortable: *"A Syrian ready to perish was my
father, and he went down into Egypt"* (Deuteronomy 26:5). And Joshua 24:2 is blunter about what was
left behind: *"Your fathers lived of old time beyond the River … and they served other gods."*

## Appears in

- [[A Wife for Isaac]] — Genesis 24:10; the servant's destination, reached with ten camels, and the
  country Isaac is forbidden to enter
- [[Jacob and Esau and the Sold Birthright]] — Genesis 25:20, **the first of the ten occurrences of
  *Paddan Aram* in Genesis**, in a pedigree that repeats what chapter 24 already gave at length:
  *"Rebekah, the daughter of Bethuel the Syrian of Paddan Aram, the sister of Laban the Syrian"*. The
  *toledot* form files a generation with its parentage — and the verse also **plants [[Laban]]**, four
  chapters before he takes over the life of the son about to be born
- [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]] — Genesis 28:2, 5–7; **four occurrences of *Paddan Aram* in
  nine verses**, more than anywhere else in the book, and the region named as an instruction for the
  second time: *"Arise, go to Paddan Aram, to the house of Bethuel your mother's father."* What has
  changed since 24:10 is who travels. **Abraham's oath forbade Isaac ever to come here, twice over
  (24:6, 8); Isaac sends his own son, alone and carrying nothing the text names.** See
  [[Two Errands to Paddan Aram - Genesis 24 and Genesis 28]]
- [[Jacob's Ladder at Bethel]] — Genesis 28:10; **the seam between the region's two names.** 28:5 sent
  Jacob to *Paddan Aram*; five verses later he *"went toward Haran"*. The narrative register uses the
  town, the genealogical register uses the region, and Genesis puts them in adjacent verses without
  reconciling them

## Related places

- [[Haran (city)]] — the crossroads in this region where Terah's household settled, and the standing
  candidate for *the city of Nahor*
- [[The Euphrates]] — the *River* the region is named for and lies beyond
- [[Ur of the Chaldees]] — far downstream, and the family's earlier home; Genesis keeps them distinct
- [[Canaan]] — the promised country, four hundred miles south-west, and the one this region's
  daughters all move to
- [[Damascus]] — the other Aramean city Genesis names
- [[Shechem]] — where Jacob arrives *"in peace … when he came from Paddan Aram"* (33:18)

- Abraham's servant arrives at the well outside the city of Nahor and meets [[Rebekah]] —
  Genesis 24:10–27, [[A Wife for Isaac]]
- Rebekah's family agrees, blesses her, and sends her to Canaan — Genesis 24:50–61
- Jacob is sent here for a wife from the daughters of [[Laban]] — Genesis 28:2, 5,
  [[Isaac Sends Jacob to Paddan Aram]]
- Jacob arrives, serves fourteen years, and marries Leah and Rachel — Genesis 29 *(not yet ingested)*
- Eleven of Jacob's twelve sons are born — Genesis 29:31–30:24 *(not yet ingested)*
- Jacob leaves, is pursued by Laban, and swears the boundary oath — Genesis 31 *(not yet ingested)*
