# Naamah

## Overview

The implied homeland of [[Zophar the Naamathite]], and **the least locatable place in the Bible that
has a name at all.** The book of Job never mentions it. It exists entirely inside the gentilic
*haNNaʿămātî*, attached to Zophar four times (2:11, 11:1, 20:1, 42:9) and to no one else in
Scripture.

**There is a Naamah in the Hebrew Bible, and it is the wrong one.** Joshua 15:41 lists a Naamah among
the towns of the Judean Shephelah, in the lowlands west of Hebron. **That is inside Israel**, which
no one in this book is, and it is in the wrong direction from everything else the book names. Almost
no one takes it as Zophar's home, and the wiki does not.

**The Septuagint offers the most interesting alternative and the wiki records it without adopting
it.** Where the Hebrew has *Zophar the Naamathite*, the Greek of Job 2:11 has **Sophar the king of
the Minaeans** — the Minaeans being a real south-Arabian people of the Yemeni highlands, known from
their own inscriptions. The Greek translator did the same thing to the other two, making Eliphaz
*king of the Temanites* and Bildad *tyrant of the Shuhites*, so the royal titles are a systematic
embellishment rather than independent information; the Septuagint's appendix goes further still and
makes Job himself the Edomite king [[Jobab]]. See
[[Jobab of Bozrah and the Septuagint's Job]]. **What survives the discount is the direction**: the
Greek tradition also read the friends as coming from the Arabian south-east, which is where the
Hebrew's other two gentilics point.

**The remaining proposals are conjectural**: a site in northern Arabia or in Edom that has left no
other trace, or a reading of the name as a description rather than a toponym. **The honest position
is that the place is unknown**, and that it is the third of three names doing one job — putting
Job's three visitors outside Israel and into the international wisdom tradition the book belongs to.

## Related places

- [[Teman]] — Eliphaz's home: the one of the three that can be located, that is attested repeatedly
  in the prophets, and that is proverbial for the wisdom all three friends claim.
- [[Shuah]] — Bildad's, unlocated, with at least two serious candidates; Naamah has none.
- [[Uz]] — Job's land, itself only vaguely placed, and the destination of all three journeys.
- [[Edom]] — the region the other two gentilics keep pointing into, and the usual guess for this one
  by association.

## Events here

None. Naamah is never a setting, never described, and never mentioned outside Zophar's name.

## Notes

- **The name.** *Naʿămâ*, from *nāʿēm*, *to be pleasant, lovely*. It is a common enough Semitic
  element that its appearance as a place name in Judah, as a personal name in Genesis 4:22, and as a
  gentilic here need not be related at all.
- **Two namesakes, no collision — yet.** Scripture has two women called Naamah: **Tubal Cain's
  sister** at Genesis 4:22, named once and dropped (see
  [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]]), and **Naamah the Ammonitess**, mother of Rehoboam
  (1 Kings 14:21). Neither has a page, so the bare filename is currently unambiguous. **If either
  ever gets one**, `AGENTS.md`'s person/place rule applies and both pages take qualifiers —
  `Naamah (place)` and `Naamah (sister of Tubal Cain)` or the like — because the link checker
  resolves by basename and would pass two files called `Naamah.md` in silence.
