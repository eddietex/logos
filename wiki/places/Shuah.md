# Shuah

## Overview

The implied homeland of [[Bildad the Shuhite]], and **one of the two places in the book of Job that
cannot be found**. The book never says *the land of Shuah*; it gives Bildad a gentilic, *haššūḥî*,
and leaves the reader to supply the place from the name.

**The usual derivation is the Shuah of Genesis 25:2** — the sixth son borne to [[Abraham]] by
[[Keturah]], and one of the six whom Abraham *"sent away… eastward, to the east country"* with gifts
while he was still alive (25:6). That sending is the verse the identification rests on, and it does
real work: it puts the Shuhites in exactly the territory the rest of the book's cast occupies, makes
them a collateral branch of Abraham's family rather than Israelites, and matches the book's habit of
placing everyone east and south-east of Canaan without ever naming Canaan. His brother [[Midian]] is
in the same list and became a nation the wiki has a page for. See
[[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]].

**The other standing proposal is Assyrian Sūḫu**, a district on the middle Euphrates below the mouth
of the Khabur, well attested in cuneiform records as *Sūḫu* and governed by its own rulers in the
second and first millennia. It is a real, locatable place with a real name that matches; the
difficulty is that it is a very long way north-east of everywhere else in the book, and nothing in
Job suggests Bildad travelled further than the other two.

**Neither can be confirmed, and the wiki adopts neither.** What both have in common is the only thing
the book needs: **a direction.** Together with [[Teman]] and [[Naamah]], Shuah points the three
friends' origins away from Israel and into the eastern and Arabian wisdom lands, which is where
Job's own [[Uz]] also sits.

**It is worth noting what is not here.** Bildad's page records that he argues from inherited
tradition more baldly than anyone else in the book — *"inquire of past generations. Find out about
the learning of their fathers"* (8:8) — and **the book gives him no fathers.** Of the three friends,
only Eliphaz's home is identifiable, and only Eliphaz's is famous for the thing all three claim.

## Related places

- [[Teman]] — Eliphaz's home, the only one of the three that can be located, and the one with a
  reputation.
- [[Naamah]] — Zophar's, equally unlocated, and with a weaker claim to any identification at all.
- [[Uz]] — Job's own land, in the same vague eastern country, and the place all three travelled to.
- [[Midian]] — Shuah's brother in Genesis 25:2, and the wiki's evidence that the sons of Keturah are
  treated elsewhere as the ancestors of locatable peoples.
- [[The Euphrates]] — the river the Assyrian Sūḫu sits on, if that identification is the right one.

## Events here

None. Like every other place in the book except Job's own, Shuah exists only as somewhere Bildad
came from. The whole of Job happens where Job sits.

## Notes

- **The name.** *Šūaḥ*, usually connected with a root meaning *to sink down* or *depression*, which
  would make it a topographical name — a hollow or a low-lying district. Nothing in the book plays on
  it, and unlike *Teman* (*south*, *right hand*) the meaning carries no freight.
- **A distinction worth keeping.** *Shuah* the son of Keturah is a **person** in Genesis and has no
  page of his own; the page you are reading is the **place** his name is taken to stand behind.
  Should a person page for him ever be warranted, `AGENTS.md`'s person/place collision rule applies
  and both pages take qualifiers.
