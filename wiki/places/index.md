# Places Index

Catalog of every place page, updated on each ingest. Split further once this list passes ~150
entries (see growth triggers in `AGENTS.md`).

| Name | Summary | Key passages |
|---|---|---|
| [[Ai]] | "The ruin" — the town east of Abram's camp on the ridge, and later the site of Israel's one defeat in the conquest; the excavated mound has no city standing at any plausible date for Joshua | [[The Call of Abram]] · [[Abram and Lot Separate]] |
| [[Ararat]] | The mountain range where the ark comes to rest — a region (Urartu, around Lake Van), not a peak; the single mountain is a medieval identification | [[The Waters Recede]] |
| [[Assyria]] | Upper Mesopotamia, and in Genesis 10 both a country and a son of Shem; whether Nimrod or Asshur founded its cities is the chapter's most disputed verse | [[The Table of Nations]] |
| [[Babel]] | The Bible's first named capital, and the same Hebrew word as Babylon; it enters Scripture as one entry in a king's city list | [[The Table of Nations]] · [[The Tower of Babel]] |
| [[Bethel]] | *House of God* — the ridge Abram pitched on and Jacob's gate of heaven, and then the northern kingdom's calf shrine; called Luz until Genesis 28:19, so its name here is the narrator's | [[The Call of Abram]] · [[Abram and Lot Separate]] |
| [[Canaan]] | The land the Torah is aimed at, and the only place in the Table of Nations whose border is drawn; promised at 12:7 and starving by 12:10, and given its first actual border at 15:18; the man it is named for keeps a separate page | [[The Table of Nations]] · [[Noah's Sons and the Curse of Canaan]] · [[From Shem to Abram]] · [[The Call of Abram]] · [[Abram in Egypt]] · [[Abram and Lot Separate]] · [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] · [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] |
| [[Damascus]] | The great oasis city of the Syrian plateau, entering Scripture as a landmark for somewhere else — Hobah, north of it, where Abram's pursuit ends — and then, four verses of narrative later, as the home town of the servant who would have inherited everything | [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] · [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] |
| [[Dan (city)]] | The northern limit of Abram's pursuit, and Genesis's clearest anachronism: Laish until the tribe of Dan renames it in Judges 18 | [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Eden]] | The region east of which God planted the first garden; watered by a river dividing into four | [[The Garden of Eden]] · [[The Fall]] · [[Cain and Abel]] |
| [[Egypt]] | *Mizraim* — Ham's second son in the Table, and the Hebrew name of the country for the rest of the Bible; the land that has food when Canaan does not, and the place you are always going *down* to; unnamed but unmistakable in the four-hundred-year prophecy of 15:13 | [[The Table of Nations]] · [[Abram in Egypt]] · [[Abram and Lot Separate]] · [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] |
| [[Elam]] | The highland kingdom east of Babylonia and Chedorlaomer's seat; a son of Shem in the Table of Nations, though its language is related to nothing else known | [[The Table of Nations]] · [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Enoch (city)]] | The first city in the Bible, built by Cain under a sentence to wander and named for his son | [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]] |
| [[Gomorrah]] | Sodom's inseparable partner, named roughly two dozen times in Scripture and almost never alone; nobody in the Bible ever enters or leaves it | [[The Table of Nations]] · [[Abram and Lot Separate]] · [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Haran (city)]] | The caravan crossroads on the Balikh where the journey to Canaan stopped and Terah died; Abram is called out of it, and both Isaac's and Jacob's wives come from it | [[From Shem to Abram]] · [[The Call of Abram]] |
| [[Hebron]] | The dry hill town Abram moves to at the end of Genesis 13 and never really leaves — the patriarchs' address, their graveyard at Machpelah, and later David's first capital | [[Abram and Lot Separate]] · [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Mamre (place)]] | The oak grove at Hebron where Abram pitches and builds his third altar, and the surveyor's benchmark for Machpelah; the Amorite who shares the name keeps a separate page | [[Abram and Lot Separate]] · [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Nineveh]] | Assyria's great city, founded in a genealogy; the one place in the Bible that receives both a mission of mercy and an oracle of total destruction | [[The Table of Nations]] |
| [[Nod]] | The land east of Eden where Cain settles; its name means "wandering", and it appears nowhere else | [[Cain and Abel]] · [[The Line of Cain and the Birth of Seth]] |
| [[Salem]] | Melchizedek's city, and on Psalm 76:2's evidence the first appearance of Jerusalem in the Bible — with a priest of God Most High on the site a thousand years before Solomon | [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Shechem]] | The first place in the land anyone stops, at the oak of Moreh; where Abram builds his first altar, Joshua renews the covenant, and the kingdom later splits | [[The Call of Abram]] |
| [[Sheol]] | The undifferentiated realm of the dead — down, dark, dusty, and gated; no judgment in it and no reward, which is why Job's vindication must come before he dies | [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] · [[Bildad's Second Speech]] · [[I Know That My Redeemer Lives]] |
| [[Shinar]] | Southern Mesopotamia, where human political power begins in Genesis; everything that happens there in Scripture is about ambition | [[The Table of Nations]] · [[The Tower of Babel]] · [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Sodom]] | A city named four times before anything happens in it, and condemned by the narrator one verse after Lot chooses to live beside it; what its sin actually was is answered differently by Ezekiel, Isaiah, Luke, and Jude | [[The Table of Nations]] · [[Abram and Lot Separate]] · [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Teman]] | An Edomite town proverbial for wisdom, and the home of Eliphaz; it appears in Job only as the origin of its most confident traditionalist | [[Eliphaz's Second Speech]] · [[Miserable Comforters and the Witness in Heaven]] |
| [[The Euphrates]] | *The great river* — the fourth river out of Eden, the northern edge of the land promised at 15:18, and in the Bible the standing name for the far side of everything; *beyond the River* is where Abram's fathers served other gods | [[The Garden of Eden]] · [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] |
| [[The Negev]] | The dry country at the bottom of the land, rendered *the South* in the WEB; the last stage of Abram's transit of Canaan, the patriarchs' usual address, and the corridor to Egypt | [[The Call of Abram]] · [[Abram in Egypt]] · [[Abram and Lot Separate]] |
| [[The Plain of the Jordan]] | The *kikkar* — the round of the Jordan, the best-watered ground in sight, compared by the narrator to Eden and to Egypt, and destroyed six chapters later | [[Abram and Lot Separate]] |
| [[The Salt Sea]] | The valley of Siddim, glossed by the narrator as a sea; its bitumen pits swallow the armies of Sodom and Gomorrah, and the same word mortared Babel | [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]] |
| [[Ur of the Chaldees]] | The family's origin and the first city in Genesis that anyone leaves; whether it is the great Sumerian city in the south or a smaller Ur near Haran is genuinely open; at 15:7 God puts himself where 11:31 had put Terah | [[From Shem to Abram]] · [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] |
| [[Uz]] | Job's homeland, named in the book's first clause and never described; outside Israel on purpose, and probably in or beside Edom | [[Job of Uz]] · [[The Table of Nations]] |
