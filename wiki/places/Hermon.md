# Hermon

## Overview

### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

All Mount Hermon appears in the general description of the eastern inheritance (11).

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### The land that remains — Joshua 13:1–7

Mount Hermon locates Baal Gad, from which the description of remaining land runs toward the entrance of Hamath (5). The verse does not narrate a conquest of the mountain.

See [[The Land That Remains]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:1–7.

### The whole land taken — Joshua 11:16–23

Baal Gad *in the valley of Lebanon under Mount Hermon* marks the northern limit of the conquest summary (17). Joshua 13:5 uses Baal Gad under Hermon again as the start of land still remaining.

See [[The Whole Land Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:16–23.

### The northern coalition — Joshua 11:1–15

The Hivites under Hermon in the land of Mizpah belong to the coalition (3). The mountain locates an allied population; its summit is not a separately narrated battlefield.

See [[The Northern Kings at the Waters of Merom]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:1–15.

### Setting of the instruction — Deuteronomy 4:44–49

The stored WEB explicitly identifies Mount Sion with Hermon (48). Sion is therefore recorded as an alias here, not as Jerusalem’s Zion. This additional name does not resolve the already flagged tension between the identification of Senir with Hermon in Deuteronomy 3:9 and their pairing in Song 4:8.

See [[The Setting of the Covenant Instruction]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:44–49.

### Og’s defeat recalled — Deuteronomy 3:1–11

The mountain is the northern endpoint of the acquired territory (8). Verse 9 identifies Sirion as
the Sidonian name and Senir as the Amorite name. The tension with Song 4:8, which pairs Senir and
Hermon, remains explicit; distinguishing a summit from a wider range is a possible explanation, not
something these verses establish.

See [[The Defeat of Og Recounted]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:1–11.

The highest mountain of the region, at the southern end of the Anti-Lebanon range, north of the
Sea of Galilee. In the Old Testament it marks the northern limit of the land Israel took east of the
Jordan: Moses' conquest ran *"from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon"* (Deuteronomy 3:8).

**Its first appearance in the wiki is in [[A Garden Locked and Opened]].** The man calls the bride to
come with him from Lebanon and to look *"from the top of Senir and Hermon, from the lions' dens, from
the mountains of the leopards"* (Song of Solomon 4:8). Hermon is not a setting there. It stands with
Amana and Senir for heights that are far north, high, and wild, from which he calls her down.

**Senir.** Deuteronomy 3:9 says, *"The Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir"*
(checked at bible-api.com, `translation=web`, 2026-10-28; Hebrew *śǝnîr*, Sefaria). On that verse
Senir is simply another name for Hermon. **The Song and 1 Chronicles do not read that way.** Song 4:8
says *"Senir and Hermon"*, *śǝnîr wǝḥermôn* (Sefaria), and 1 Chronicles 5:23 lists *"Baal Hermon,
Senir, and Mount Hermon"* (checked at bible-api.com, 2026-10-28) as separate points. Both claims are
recorded. A common way to reconcile them is that Senir named part of the Hermon massif, or the
range it belongs to; that is an inference, not a statement of any verse. This page keeps Senir as an
alias and does not decide. Ezekiel 27:5 says Tyre's ship planks were made of *"cypress trees from
Senir"* (checked at bible-api.com, 2026-10-28; Hebrew *biršîm miśśǝnîr*, Sefaria), beside a cedar
from Lebanon for the mast.

Sources: Song of Solomon 4:8 from the stored WEB, with its Hebrew from Sefaria's *Miqra according
to the Masorah*. Deuteronomy 3:8–9, 1 Chronicles 5:23, and Ezekiel 27:5 were checked as
single-reference lookups at bible-api.com on 2026-10-28, without fetching the books; Hebrew of
Deuteronomy 3:9 and Ezekiel 27:5 from the same Sefaria edition. The location is the standard
identification and is not argued from the verses here.

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** Mount Hermon is the northern limit of the east-bank conquest (12:1) and part of Og's rule (5); Baal Gad, the western list's northern limit, lies in the valley of Lebanon below it (7; compare 11:17). See [[The Kings Struck Down]].
