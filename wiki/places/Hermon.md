# Hermon

## Overview

The highest mountain of the region, at the southern end of the Anti-Lebanon range, north of the
Sea of Galilee. In the Old Testament it marks the northern limit of the land Israel took east of the
Jordan: Moses' conquest ran *"from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon"* (Deuteronomy 3:8).

**Its first appearance in the wiki is in [[A Garden Locked and Opened]].** The man calls the bride to
come with him from Lebanon and to look *"from the top of Senir and Hermon, from the lions' dens, from
the mountains of the leopards"* (Song of Solomon 4:8). Hermon is not a setting there. It stands with
Amana and Senir for heights that are far north, high, and wild, from which he calls her down.

**Senir.** Deuteronomy 3:9 says, *"The Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir"*
(checked at bible-api.com, `translation=web`, 2026-10-28; Hebrew *śǝnîr*, Sefaria). On that verse
Senir is simply another name for Hermon. **The Song and 1 Chronicles do not read that way.** Song 4:8
says *"Senir and Hermon"*, *śǝnîr wǝḥermôn* (Sefaria), and 1 Chronicles 5:23 lists *"Baal Hermon,
Senir, and Mount Hermon"* (checked at bible-api.com, 2026-10-28) as separate points. Both claims are
recorded. A common way to reconcile them is that Senir named part of the Hermon massif, or the
range it belongs to; that is an inference, not a statement of any verse. This page keeps Senir as an
alias and does not decide. Ezekiel 27:5 says Tyre's ship planks were made of *"cypress trees from
Senir"* (checked at bible-api.com, 2026-10-28; Hebrew *biršîm miśśǝnîr*, Sefaria), beside a cedar
from Lebanon for the mast.

Sources: Song of Solomon 4:8 from the stored WEB, with its Hebrew from Sefaria's *Miqra according
to the Masorah*. Deuteronomy 3:8–9, 1 Chronicles 5:23, and Ezekiel 27:5 were checked as
single-reference lookups at bible-api.com on 2026-10-28, without fetching the books; Hebrew of
Deuteronomy 3:9 and Ezekiel 27:5 from the same Sefaria edition. The location is the standard
identification and is not argued from the verses here.
