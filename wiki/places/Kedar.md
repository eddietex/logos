# Kedar

## Overview

A tent-dwelling Arabian people and its territory. In Scripture they appear with flocks,
camels, archers, and villages on the desert edge. Genesis names Kedar as the second of
[[Ishmael]]'s twelve sons (25:13). That is the usual biblical pattern, in which a people
and its land share an ancestor's name. See [[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]].
**No separate person page is kept for Kedar son of Ishmael.** Like [[Tema]], he is a name
in a list, with no act and no word. If one is ever needed, the page must be
`Kedar (son of Ishmael)` and not a bare `Kedar.md`, because this place page holds the name.

**Its first appearance in a poem is in [[Dark and Lovely]].** The woman of the Song calls
herself *"dark, but lovely … like Kedar's tents, like Solomon's curtains"* (Song of Solomon
1:5). The pairing sets a desert people's tents beside a king's furnishings. The usual
explanation is dark goat-hair tents, which is probable but is not stated by the verse.
Kedar is an image here, and nothing happens there.

Elsewhere the prophets and a psalm treat Kedar as a real neighbor of Israel:

- The psalmist laments, *"Woe is me, that I live in Meshech, that I dwell among the tents of
  Kedar!"* (Psalm 120:5). Here the same tents stand for a hostile place of exile.
- Isaiah 21:16–17 foretells that *"all the glory of Kedar will fail"*, and that the mighty
  archers of its people will be few.
- Isaiah 42:11 summons *"the villages that Kedar inhabits"* to raise their voices.
- Isaiah 60:7 gathers *"the flocks of Kedar"* to Zion. See
  [[Abraham's Other Sons in Isaiah 60]].
- Jeremiah 49:28–29 sends Nebuchadnezzar's attackers against Kedar to take *"their tents and
  their flocks"* and *"their curtains"*. That verse lists tents and curtains together, the
  same pairing as Song 1:5.

The tradition is therefore mixed. Kedar can be a scene of harshness (Psalm 120), a target of
judgment (Isaiah 21; Jeremiah 49), a voice of praise (Isaiah 42), or a contributor to Zion's
wealth (Isaiah 60). The Song uses only its appearance.

Isaiah 60:7 is quoted as already verified on the Isaiah 60 connection page. None of those
