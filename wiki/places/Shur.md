# Shur

## Overview

The desert frontier between Canaan and Egypt, and in the Bible always a direction rather than a
destination — the place you are *on the way to* when you are leaving one country for the other.

*Šûr* is the Hebrew word for **wall**, and the most persuasive explanation of the name is the most
literal one: the line of Egyptian frontier fortifications across the eastern Delta, the *Walls of the
Ruler* known from Egyptian texts of the Middle Kingdom, which guarded the road from Asia and are
exactly what a traveller coming down from Canaan would meet. On that reading Shur is not a region
Israel named but a border Egypt built, remembered under a translated name.

Geographically it is the northwestern Sinai: the dry country between the Negev and the Delta, crossed
by the coastal road and by the desert tracks below it. Genesis 25:18 gives the outer limit of
Ishmaelite territory as *"from Havilah to Shur that is before Egypt, as you go toward Assyria"*, and
Genesis 20:1 puts Abraham living *"between Kadesh and Shur"* — both use it as the far edge of the
inhabited world in that direction.

**Its four appearances make one point between them.** Hagar flees toward it (16:7). Ishmael's sons
graze to it (25:18). Abraham lives beside it (20:1). And Israel, coming out of Egypt, crosses the sea
and walks straight into it: *"Moses led Israel onward from the Red Sea, and they went out into the
wilderness of Shur"* (Exodus 15:22), three waterless days before the bitter water at Marah. It is the
threshold of Egypt from either side.

### In this pericope — Genesis 16:1–16

*"Yahweh's angel found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness, by the fountain on the way to
Shur"* (16:7).

The direction is the chapter's unspoken answer. The angel asks Hagar *"where did you come from? Where
are you going?"* and she answers only the first half — but she is on the Shur road, at the last water
before the crossing, and the Shur road goes to Egypt. **She is going home.** An Egyptian slave in a
Hebrew household, afflicted with the verb Exodus will use of Egypt afflicting Israel, is walking back
to the country the covenant has just called the place of four hundred years of servitude (15:13). She
is stopped at the border and sent back.

Israel will make the same crossing in the other direction, and hit the same wilderness on the far
side of the sea.
