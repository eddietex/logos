# Haran (city)

## Overview

The crossroads the family stopped at, and the place they kept coming back to for three generations.

*"They went from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan. They came to Haran and lived
there … The days of Terah were two hundred five years. Terah died in Haran"* (Genesis 11:31–32). It
is introduced as an interruption: a destination is named, and this is not it.

**It is not named after Terah's son.** The son is *Hārān* (הָרָן, with *he*); the city is *Ḥārān*
(חָרָן, with *ḥet*) — a different word in Hebrew, identical in English. The city's name is Akkadian
*Ḥarrānu*, **"road", "route", "crossroads"**, after the caravan routes that met there, and it is
attested centuries before any date proposed for Abraham. The reading in which a grieving father
settles in a town named for his dead son exists only in translation. It is a common enough misreading
to be worth stating flatly; see [[Haran (son of Terah)]].

**What it was.** A major city on the Balikh, a tributary of the upper Euphrates, in what is now
southeastern Turkey, at the junction of the routes running east–west between Mesopotamia and Asia
Minor and south toward Canaan. It appears in the Mari archives and in Assyrian records, was the last
capital of the Assyrian empire after Nineveh fell in 612 BC, and was the site of the Roman defeat at
Carrhae in 53 BC. Like [[Ur of the Chaldees]], it was a principal cult centre of the moon-god **Sin**
— a temple that survived into the Islamic period. Ezekiel 27:23 lists it among Tyre's trading
partners, and 2 Kings 19:12 and Isaiah 37:12 name it among the cities Assyria had already destroyed,
in a taunt sent to Hezekiah.

**Four of the register's names are towns in its district.** [[Serug]] (*Sarugi*, modern Suruç),
[[Nahor (son of Serug)]] (*Naḫur*, in the Mari texts), [[Terah]] (often connected to *Til-ša-turaḫi*),
and less certainly [[Reu]] all correspond to places within a short journey of Haran. Whatever that
means — a genuine clan memory, or a shared local stock of names — the family settles in the middle of
its own genealogy.

### The family's second home

Genesis returns here three times, and each time it is where the wives come from.

- **Abram is called out of it.** *"Now Yahweh said to Abram, 'Leave your country, and your
  relatives, and your father's house'"* (12:1), and he leaves at seventy-five (12:4). Acts 7:2–4
  complicates this by putting the call in Mesopotamia *before* Haran, and the departure from Haran
  after Terah's death — see [[Out of Ur - Terah's Journey and Abram's Call]].
- **Isaac's wife is fetched from it.** Abraham sends his servant *"to my country and to my
  relatives"*, to *"the city of Nahor"* in Aram Naharaim (24:4, 10), and Rebekah comes back with him.
- **Jacob flees to it and stays twenty years.** Sent by Rebekah to Laban her brother (27:43; 28:10),
  he marries Leah and Rachel there and fathers eleven of the twelve tribes before leaving (29–31).

So the line that received the promise takes its wives, for three generations, from the branch that
stayed at the place the journey stopped. Deuteronomy 26:5 keeps the memory in Israel's own confession:
*"A Syrian ready to perish was my father."*
