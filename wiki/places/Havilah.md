# Havilah

## Overview

**A gold country at the edge of the map, named four times in Genesis and never once located.**

*Ḥăwîlâ* is most naturally read from *ḥôl*, **sand**, which would make it something like *the sandy
land* — a description rather than a name, which is consistent with how the book uses it. In all four
of its Genesis appearances it is a bearing: the land a river runs through, a name in a list, or the
far end of a range. **Nobody in the Bible ever goes there.**

### The four appearances, and they do not agree

| verse | what it is | line |
|---|---|---|
| **Genesis 2:11** | the land the Pishon flows through, *"where there is gold"* | primeval geography |
| **Genesis 10:7** | a son of Cush | Ham |
| **Genesis 10:29** | a son of Joktan | Shem |
| **Genesis 25:18** | the eastern limit of Ishmaelite range | Abraham, through Hagar |

**Genesis files the same name under two different fathers inside one chapter**, which
[[The Table of Nations]] already records as one of that chapter's two double entries — Sheba is the
other. **This is not carelessness about ancestry; it is what a genealogy of peoples looks like.**
Tribal and territorial names in Arabia attach to ground, and a list tracing one branch will pick up a
name that another branch has already claimed. See [[The Nations]].

### Where it might be

The proposals are all guesses and the wiki adopts none of them.

- **North-eastern or central Arabia**, which is what Genesis 25:18 requires: paired with [[Shur]] on
  the Egyptian frontier, it has to be the far end of a line drawn across the whole Arabian trade
  corridor *"as you go toward Assyria"*. The same pairing is used in 1 Samuel 15:7 of Saul's Amalekite
  campaign — *"from Havilah as you go to Shur"* — so the two names function together as a formula for
  the whole desert south.
- **South-western Arabia (Yemen)**, which suits 10:29, where Havilah sits among Joktan's sons beside
  Ophir and Sheba, names generally placed on the incense coast.
- **The African side of the Red Sea**, which suits 10:7, where it sits among Cush's sons.
- **Nowhere on any map**, which is the only honest answer for 2:11. The Pishon is unidentified, and a
  land defined by the course of an unidentified river is not recoverable.

**These are not necessarily rival answers to one question.** The likeliest reading is that *Havilah*
was a loose designation for sandy country and got attached in more than one place, which is why the
Bible can put it beside Cush, beside Joktan, and beside Shur without embarrassment.

### In this pericope — Genesis 25:18

> **18** They lived from Havilah to Shur that is before Egypt, as you go toward Assyria. He lived
> opposite all his relatives.

**It is one end of a range, and the range is the whole northern Arabian desert.** Shur is the Egyptian
frontier in the west; Havilah is the eastern term; [[Assyria]] is the bearing beyond it. The verse is
drawing a corridor several hundred miles wide and telling the reader that Ishmael's twelve princes
live in it *"by their villages, and by their encampments"* — unwalled settlements and camps, not
cities.

**And the gold of 2:11 is quietly still there in the background.** The first thing Genesis ever says
about Havilah is that it has gold, bdellium, and onyx stone; the last thing it says is that Abraham's
disinherited son's descendants range to it. Isaiah 60:6 will have camels coming from that country to
Zion carrying gold and frankincense. **Genesis does not connect the two, and neither does this wiki —
but the trade route is the same one.** See [[Abraham's Other Sons in Isaiah 60]].
