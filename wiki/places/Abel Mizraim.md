# Abel Mizraim

## Overview

**A threshing floor that got renamed because the people who lived there misread a funeral.**

> **10** They came to the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan, and there they
> lamented with a very great and severe lamentation. He mourned for his father seven days. **11** When
> the inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites, saw the mourning in the floor of Atad, they said,
> "This is a grievous mourning by the Egyptians." Therefore its name was called Abel Mizraim, which
> is beyond the Jordan. (50:10–11)

**It enters Scripture with two names in two verses and leaves it for good.** ***Gōren hāʾāṭād***,
**the threshing floor of Atad**, is what it was called; ***ʾĒbel Miṣrayim***, **mourning of Egypt**,
is what the Canaanites called it after watching [[Joseph]]'s cortege stop there for a week.
**Neither name is ever used again in the Bible, and the site has never been identified.**

***ʾĀṭād*** **is the bramble or boxthorn** — **the plant that is offered the kingship in Jotham's
fable and takes it** (Judges 9:14–15), and the plant Psalm 58:9 uses for something that burns fast
and is worth nothing. **The mourning for Israel is held on a floor named for a thorn**, which is
what the ground was cursed to produce at 3:18. See [[Curse]].

**The renaming turns on a pun the Hebrew makes and English cannot.** ***ʾĀbēl*** **is *meadow* —
the first element in a string of place names west of the Jordan (Abel Shittim, Abel Meholah, Abel
Beth Maacah).** ***ʾĒbel*** **is *mourning*.** **Same three consonants.** **The Canaanites give the
place a perfectly ordinary meadow-name and Genesis tells you they meant the other word.** See
[[Names and Naming]].

## Who names it, and what they get wrong

***"This is a grievous mourning by the Egyptians."*** **The inhabitants of the land look at the
funeral of the man to whom their land has been promised three times over** (12:7; 26:3; 28:13) —
**a man carried by his own twelve sons** (50:12) — **and see Egyptians.** They are not entirely
wrong: Pharaoh's whole court is in the procession, with chariots and horsemen (50:7–9). **But the
body is [[Jacob]]'s, the sons doing the carrying are the twelve tribes, and the destination is a
field in [[Canaan]] that the family holds by purchase.** **The one naming-event in Genesis performed
by Canaanites is a misidentification of who is standing in their country.** See
[[Seeing and Being Seen]].

**It is also the only place name in Genesis given by people who are neither in the covenant line nor
speaking to anyone in it.** They say it among themselves and the narrator overhears.

## The problem with *beyond the Jordan*

***Bəʿēber hayyardēn*** **is said twice in two verses**, once of the threshing floor and once of the
name, **and it does not fit the journey.** **The road from [[Egypt]] to [[Machpelah]] at [[Hebron]]
runs up the coast or through the Negev; it does not cross [[The Jordan]] at any point.** **A cortege
that mourns seven days east of the river before burying a man in the Judean hills has gone three
sides of a square.**

**Three readings are live and the wiki adopts none of them:**

1. **Loose usage** — *ʿēber hayyardēn* meaning the Jordan valley generally rather than the east bank
   strictly, in which case Atad is somewhere near Jericho and the detour is short.
2. **Standpoint** — the phrase written from an eastern viewpoint, so that *beyond* means the west
   bank. **This is the harder reading in Hebrew and the easier one geographically.**
3. **Deliberate route** — the cortege genuinely swung east and entered the land across the Jordan,
   **which is the road Israel takes in Numbers and Joshua and the one Moses is refused.** **On this
   reading Jacob's body makes the conquest's approach four hundred years early.**

**The third is the most interesting and the least demonstrable.** Genesis reports the location twice
and explains it never.

## Events here

- **Seven days of mourning for [[Jacob]]** — *"a very great and severe lamentation"* — **the Bible's
  first week of mourning**, and the length that becomes the standard *shivah* (compare the seven days
  the men of Jabesh Gilead fast for Saul, 1 Samuel 31:13)
- **The naming**: the Canaanites call it *ʾĒbel Miṣrayim* and the narrator records their words
