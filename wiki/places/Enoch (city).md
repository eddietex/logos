# Enoch (city)

The first city in the Bible. Named for [[Enoch (son of Cain)]], with whom it should not be
confused — the person and the settlement share a page name only because Genesis gives them the same
one.

## Overview

*"He built a city, and called the name of the city, after the name of his son, Enoch"* (4:17). That
is the entire record. Genesis never says where it was, how large it was, who else lived in it, or
what became of it, and never mentions it again.

Everything interesting about it is in its position in the narrative. Two verses earlier Cain was
sentenced to be *"a fugitive and a wanderer in the earth"* (4:12) and settled in a land whose name
means *wandering* (see [[Nod]]). Then he builds walls. Genesis sets the sentence and the city beside
each other and offers no reconciliation. The readings on offer are that the city is defiance — a
man manufacturing the security God removed; that it is simply what a sentence looks like once it is
lived in, since children have to sleep somewhere; or that the narrator inherited two traditions and
declined to smooth them. The first has the longest history in Christian reading — Augustine's
*City of God* takes Cain's city as the archetype of the earthly city, founded by a fratricide, the
way Rome was founded by one — but it is inference. The text says only that the man who lost the
ground built a town.

Two smaller points. The city is named for a **son**, not for its founder, which is the single soft
detail in the whole line and is left uncommented. And a minority reading of the Hebrew takes Enoch
rather than Cain as the builder, since the pronoun is not decisive and naming a city after one's
child is slightly odd; WEB and nearly every translation read Cain as the subject.

Whether it stood in Nod the text does not say. It says Cain settled in Nod (4:16) and then that he
built a city (4:17), and the sequence is the only evidence there is.

It is the first of the Bible's cities and the beginning of a strand that runs to Babel (11:1–9),
to Babylon, and finally to a city that comes down out of heaven rather than being built
(Revelation 21:2). See [[City and Civilization]].
