# Horeb

## Overview

### First fruits and harvest provision — Leviticus 23:9–22

The Sinai frame of Leviticus 7:38 remains the context; no mountain is named here.
The commanded harvest belongs to the future land rather than the present wilderness.

See [[First Fruits and the Feast of Weeks]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:9–22; contextual references as cited.


### Sabbath and the opening feasts — Leviticus 23:1–8

The Sinai context follows the book’s frame (7:38); the mountain is not named here.
The Sabbath applies throughout Israel’s dwellings (23:3), without naming a new location.

See [[Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 23:1–8; Exodus 12:16 where cited.


### Acceptable victims and the holy name — Leviticus 22:17–33

The Sinai setting is supplied by the book's frame (Leviticus 7:38); Leviticus 22:17–33 names no
place. "The altar" (22) is where acceptance is decided, and "your land" (24) extends one
prohibition beyond the sanctuary.

See [[Acceptable Victims and the Holy Name]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 22:17–33.



### Eating the holy things — Leviticus 22:1–16

The Sinai setting is supplied by the book's context (Leviticus 7:38); Leviticus 22:1–16 names no
place. Its spaces are the holy things and the priest's household, the "father's house" to which a
daughter may return (13).

See [[Eating the Holy Things]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 22:1–16.


### Priestly holiness — Leviticus 21

The Sinai setting is supplied by the book’s context (Leviticus 7:38), not a mountain named in
chapter 21. The sanctuary, veil, and altar are the locations explicitly relevant to the approach
restrictions (12, 23).

See [[The Holiness of the Priests]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 21.


### Penalties and separation — Leviticus 20

The Sinai setting continues from Leviticus 7:38; chapter 20 names no mountain. Its land
inheritance remains prospective (22–24).

See [[Penalties and a People Set Apart]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 20; comparisons as cited.


### Field and community — Leviticus 19:19–37

The Sinai setting follows the book’s context (7:38); no mountain is named in 19:19–37. Orchard
planting anticipates settled life without relocating the current address.

See [[Holy Living in Field and Community]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19.


### Holiness and the neighbor — Leviticus 19:1–18

The Sinai setting is contextual from Leviticus 7:38; 19:1–18 names no mountain or settlement. Its
fields and vineyards anticipate agricultural life without narrating a move from Sinai.

See [[Be Holy and Love Your Neighbor]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 19:1–18.


### Forbidden unions and the land — Leviticus 18:1–30

The Sinai context continues from Leviticus 7:38; chapter 18 does not name the mountain. Egypt and Canaan are the remembered origin and anticipated destination.

See [[Forbidden Unions and the Defiled Land]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 18.

### Sacrifice and blood — Leviticus 17:1–16

The Sinai setting continues from Leviticus 7:38; chapter 17 does not name the
mountain. It locates sacrifice at the tent’s door and distinguishes camp, open field,
and hunting outside the altar procedure (3–13).

See [[The Place of Sacrifice and the Life in the Blood]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 17; comparisons as cited.


### The Day of Atonement — Leviticus 16:1–34

The Sinai setting continues; the chapter does not name the mountain. The rite looks beyond the
camp at Sinai to a perpetual annual observance (29, 34), and sends the live goat away from the
camp into an unnamed wilderness (21–22).

See [[The Day of Atonement]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 16; comparisons as cited.


### Bodily discharges — Leviticus 15:1–33

The continuing Sinai setting is contextual (Leviticus 7:38); the mountain is not named
in chapter 15. The specified ritual location is the Tent of Meeting’s door (14, 29),
and the tabernacle among the people grounds the warning against defilement (31).

See [[Bodily Discharges and Cleansing]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 15; comparisons as cited.

### Houses in the promised land — Leviticus 14:33–57

The house law belongs to the broader Sinai setting of Leviticus (7:38), but looks ahead
to houses in Canaan (14:34). Sinai is not named in 14:33–57; the house under inspection
is not a building at the mountain.

See [[Mildew in a House]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 14:33–57; contextual references as cited.

### Cleansing after healing — Leviticus 14:1–32

The law belongs to the continuing Sinai setting (7:38), without naming the mountain here. Its
specific locations are outside the camp, inside the camp but outside the person’s tent, and the
Tent of Meeting’s door (3, 8, 11).

See [[The Cleansing of the Leper]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 14:1–32; comparisons as cited.


### Mildew in garments — Leviticus 13:47–59

The garment law belongs to the continuing Sinai setting recalled in 7:38. It names
no mountain, settlement, or place for isolating the article; its handling rules should
not be given a location the unit does not supply.

See [[Mildew in Garments]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 13:47–59; contextual references as noted.

### Examining skin conditions — Leviticus 13:1–46

The law belongs to Leviticus’s continuing Sinai setting (7:38), but names no mountain
or town. Its explicit boundary is the camp: the person declared leprous must dwell
alone outside it while the condition remains (13:46). See [[Examining Diseases of the Skin]].

### Purification after childbirth — Leviticus 12:1–8

The childbirth law belongs to Leviticus's continuing Sinai setting (7:38). Chapter 12
does not name the mountain; its specified ritual location is the Tent of Meeting's door.

See [[Purification After Childbirth]].


### Clean and unclean animals — Leviticus 11:1–47

In the book's continuing Sinai setting, Leviticus 11 addresses food and carcass distinctions
to Israel through Moses and Aaron. The mountain is not named in this chapter.

See [[Clean and Unclean Animals]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 11.

**The mountain where God speaks from the burning bush — named *"God's mountain"* by the narrator before
anything has happened on it** (Exodus 3:1). *Ḥōrēb* probably means *dry place* or *waste*, from
*ḥārēb*, *to be dry*; it lies *"at the back of the wilderness"* — *ʾaḥar hammidbār*, behind or west
of the grazing land — where Moses brings Jethro's flock. See [[The Burning Bush]].

**Horeb and Sinai.** Exodus calls the mountain of the covenant *Sinai* (19:11, 18, 20, and
throughout); Deuteronomy calls it *Horeb* (Deuteronomy 1:2, 6; 4:10, 15; 5:2); Exodus uses *Horeb* at
3:1 and twice more (17:6; 33:6). **Most readers take them as two names for one mountain**, or Horeb
for the region and Sinai for the peak, and the wiki follows that reading: Stephen places the bush *"in
the wilderness of Mount Sinai"* (Acts 7:30), and God's own token in Exodus 3:12 — *"you shall serve God
on this mountain"* — is fulfilled when Israel camps *"before the mountain"* in the wilderness of Sinai
(19:1–2). Source-critical scholarship assigns the two names to different traditions.

**Filing decision at Exodus 19:** this page continues to hold both Horeb and Sinai, including
the wilderness camp around the mountain. A separate Sinai page would duplicate the narrative
site already gathered here. This convention does not settle whether the names originally
designated precisely the same feature, or establish a modern location.

**Its location is not known.** The traditional site, Jebel Musa in the southern Sinai peninsula, is
attested from the fourth century AD; northern Sinai, the Negev, and north-western Arabia (Midian's
country, which Exodus 3:1 puts within reach) all have advocates. Paul's *"Mount Sinai in Arabia"*
(Galatians 4:25) is cited on the Arabian side; *Arabia* in Paul's usage can include the Sinai
peninsula. The wiki records the options and adopts none.

**The name *Sinai* first occurs at Exodus 16:1**, in a verse that places the wilderness of Sin *"between Elim
and Sinai"* (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Deuteronomy) — **the mountain is named as a point on the road a
month after the sea.** The wilderness and the mountain share the consonants *s-y-n*. See
[[The Wilderness of Sin]] and [[Manna and Quail]].

**The bush may have named the mountain.** *Sənê*, the *bush* of 3:2–4, is close in sound to *Sînay*,
and many commentators hear the pun. The etymology of *Sinai* is not known.

**The rock of the water is *"the rock in Horeb"*** (17:6) — **the second *Horeb* in Exodus**, while Israel is
still camped at Rephidim, which it leaves before it reaches the wilderness of Sinai (19:2). **The text does not
resolve the geography.** Readings reported, not adopted: *Horeb* as a region wider than the mountain, Rephidim
lying at its edge; Moses and the elders walking ahead (*"Walk on before the people"*, 17:5) as far as the
mountain; or the episode placed by theme rather than by stage. **God says he will *"stand"* there on the
rock**, and later, on the mountain, he will set Moses *"on the rock"* while his glory passes
(33:21–22; [[Show Me Your Glory]] ) — the first two scenes with *ṣûr*, *rock*, in Scripture both
have God and Moses at a rock of
this mountain or its region (WLC lemma search, Genesis–Exodus). See [[Water from the Rock]] and [[Rephidim]].

**Elijah comes back to it.** Fleeing Jezebel, he goes *"forty days and forty nights to Horeb, God's
Mountain"* (1 Kings 19:8) — and there God is not in the fire.

**Jethro finds Israel camped *"at the Mountain of God"*** (18:5, *har hāʾĕlōhîm*, the phrase of 3:1 and 4:27;
WLC) **before Exodus has them arrive at Sinai** (19:1–2). Readings reported, not adopted: the chapter is told out of
sequence, grouped by subject; or Rephidim lay within sight of the mountain, as *"the rock in Horeb"* (17:6) may
suggest. **Deuteronomy sets the appointment of judges at the departure from Horeb** (Deuteronomy 1:6–18, by
reference). Here the first burnt offering in Exodus is made — by the priest of Midian (18:12). See
[[Jethro's Counsel]].

**At the arrival narrated in Exodus 19, the mountain becomes the meeting place for the whole
people.** Israel leaves Rephidim and camps below it; Moses leads the people to meet God, who
descends in fire, cloud, and sound (19:1–2, 16–20). The summit and the camp are not interchangeable:
a boundary prohibits unauthorized ascent, even by priests, while Moses is summoned and Aaron
is to accompany him (19:21–24). This is the return promised at the bush (3:12). The tension with
18:5's earlier mountain camp remains recorded above, not resolved by the filing convention. See
[[Israel at Mount Sinai]].

**The public commandments and the request for mediation occur at this mountain.** The proper name is
not repeated in Exodus 20:1–21, but the thunder, trumpet, and smoking mountain continue the Sinai
scene (20:18). The people remain at a distance while Moses approaches the thick darkness where God
is (20:21). The contrast concerns access within one encounter, not a relocation of the camp. See
[[The Ten Commandments]].

**The altar law is given here, but it is not tied to here.** Moses receives the instructions of
20:22–21:11 while still at the darkness on the mountain, and the place goes unnamed. The promise
is for *"every place where I record my name"* (20:24), not for the mountain itself. See
[[The Altar and the Hebrew Slave]].

**The injury laws continue the Sinai address without naming another location** (21:12–36).
The promised place of flight in 21:13 is not identified with this mountain, and the altar of
21:14 does not create an exemption for a planned killing. See [[Laws of Violence and Injury]].
**The property laws continue the Sinai address with no place named** (22:1–15). Coming *"near to
God"* in 22:8 does not identify a location. See [[Laws of Property and Restitution]].

**The ordinances on the vulnerable and on holiness continue there with no place named**
(22:16–31). See [[Laws of Holiness and Compassion]].

**The instructions on justice, rest, and feasts continue the Sinai address** (23:1–19). The mountain
is not named in this unit. The fields and Yahweh's house anticipated in the commands are not
identified as features of this mountain camp. See [[Justice, Sabbaths, and Feasts]].

**The Book of the Covenant ends at the mountain with a promise about leaving it** (23:20–33). The
angel will bring Israel from here to *"the place which I have prepared"*. The mountain is not named in
the unit. See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]].

**The covenant is made at its foot and the glory settles on its top** (24:1–18). The mountain is
named three ways in one chapter — *"the mountain"*, *"God's Mountain"* (24:13), and *"Mount Sinai"*
(24:16) — and used in three zones: an altar and twelve pillars *"under the mountain"* (24:4); a height
where seventy-four leaders see God and eat (24:9–11); and the top, covered by cloud for six days, where
the glory looks *"like devouring fire"* and Moses stays forty days and forty nights (24:15–18). The
glory *"settled"* there (*šākan*, MAM), the verb from which the tabernacle takes its name. See
[[The Blood of the Covenant]] and
[[Forty Days and Forty Nights - The Flood, Sinai, Horeb, and the Wilderness]].

**On the mountain, inside the cloud, God gives the plan for a portable sanctuary** (25:1–9). The
mountain is not named in these verses; Moses is there by context (24:18). What God asks for is a place
where he can *"dwell among them"* (25:8) — the verb of the glory that *"settled"* on Sinai (24:16;
MAM). **The tabernacle is designed on Horeb to take the mountain's presence on the road.** See
[[The Offering for the Sanctuary]].

**The mountain remains the place of instruction when the tent is designed.** Exodus 26:30 recalls
what Moses was shown there and makes it the standard for erection. The dwelling will carry the
ordered approach to God's presence beyond the mountain, but no move or construction takes place
in this chapter. See [[The Tabernacle Curtains and Frames]].

**The altar too must follow what was shown on the mountain** (Exodus 27:8). The court and
lamp rule continue that address; they do not narrate a departure or a completed sanctuary
at Sinai. See [[The Altar and the Court]].

**The priests' garments are prescribed at the mountain as well** (Exodus 28:1–43). No place is
named in the chapter; Sinai is the continuing setting of God's address to Moses in chapters
24–31. See [[The Priestly Garments]].

**The ordination of the priests is prescribed at the mountain too** (Exodus 29:1–46). The rite is set
at *"the door of the Tent of Meeting"*, and Numbers 28:6 (verified WEB, not yet ingested) later
calls the daily offering of 29:38–42 *"a continual burnt offering, which was ordained in Mount
Sinai"*. See [[The Ordination of the Priests]].

Exodus 30 continues the instructions given on the mountain: the incense altar, the census
ransom, the basin, the anointing oil, and the incense. Everything is placed relative to the tent —
the altar *"before the veil"* (30:6), the basin *"between the Tent of Meeting and the altar"* (30:18).
See [[The Incense Altar, the Ransom, and the Holy Oil]].

### The speaking ends and the tablets are given — Exodus 31:18

The location is expressly Mount Sinai when God finishes speaking and gives Moses the two tablets.
The makers' commission and Sabbath sign belong to this final portion of the mountain instructions.
The tent described in them has not yet been built. See [[Bezalel, Oholiab, and the Sabbath Sign]].

### The golden calf — Exodus 32

The mountain and the camp below become the two settings of covenant rupture: Moses pleads above,
descends with the tablets, and breaks them below; the next day he returns to seek atonement
(32:7–35). The chapter calls it the mountain without repeating Sinai or Horeb. The calf is
destroyed here, not at Shechem or Bethel. See [[The Golden Calf]].

### Presence after the calf — Exodus 33

Horeb is named as the point from which Israel strips off jewelry (33:6). The meeting tent is outside
the camp, far away from it (33:7); the rock and cleft are specified in relation to God (33:21–22),
without an independently identifiable site. This does not establish that the cleft is the rock of
17:6. See [[Show Me Your Glory]].

### The covenant renewed — Exodus 34:1–28

Sinai is expressly named when Moses is summoned to the summit and ascends with two new stones
(34:2, 4). Companions and grazing animals are excluded from the mountain (34:3). Yahweh descends
in the cloud, and Moses remains forty days and nights (34:5, 28). The existing joint treatment
of Horeb and Sinai does not locate the mountain more precisely than this narrative permits. See
[[The Covenant Renewed]].

### The shining face — Exodus 34:29–35

Mount Sinai is named as the place from which Moses descends and where Yahweh spoke the
commands (34:29, 32). The mountain setting now leads to a return with intact tablets and a
changed face. The repeated entries before Yahweh in 34:34–35 do not name their location;
they should not be made repeated ascents of the summit without evidence.

See [[The Shining Face of Moses]].

### The freewill offering — Exodus 35:1–29

Moses assembles the congregation after descending Sinai (34:29; 35:1). The camp remains the
setting by continuity, although this pericope does not repeat the mountain's name. The people's
habitations are the scope of the Sabbath fire rule (35:3); no new settlement or location is
introduced. Materials for the prescribed sanctuary are now brought, without a report of its
erection. See [[The Freewill Offering]].

### The makers and the surplus — Exodus 35:30–36:7

The proclamation stopping gifts goes throughout the camp (36:6). The ongoing Sinai setting
follows the assembly after Moses' descent; this unit names no new location or departure.
The workers are engaged in sanctuary work (36:4), but neither its erection nor the filling
with glory is reported here. See [[Bezalel's Workmen and the Surplus]].

### The enclosure made — Exodus 36:8–38

The makers produce the sanctuary's curtains, supports, veil, and screen in the continuing
Sinai-camp setting. No place name or departure occurs in this unit; the setting follows the
assembly after Moses' descent (34:29; 35:1) and the camp proclamation (36:6). The final erection
and filling belong to chapter 40. See [[The Tabernacle Built]].

### The furnishings made — Exodus 37:1–29

The furnishing work continues in the Sinai-camp setting established by Moses' descent,
assembly, and the proclamation of 34:29–36:7. Chapter 37 names no new location or departure.
The ark, table, lampstand, and incense altar are made, but their installation in the raised
tabernacle belongs to chapter 40. A sanctuary object is not a newly named geographical place.

See [[The Furnishings Made]]. Source: the cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-24.

### The outer equipment and inventory — Exodus 38

The altar, basin, court, and accounting belong to the continuing Sinai construction setting. Exodus
38 itself names no mountain or new journey stop. Its Tent of Meeting reference at 38:8 does not
settle whether the women’s ministry belongs to the earlier outside tent of 33:7–11 or is described
retrospectively. See [[The Altar, the Basin, the Court, and the Inventory]].

### The priestly garments made — Exodus 39:1–31

The priestly garments are made within the continuing Sinai encampment of the building
narrative. Exodus 39:1–31 gives no new geographical name or departure. The holy place mentioned
in 39:1 is the intended sphere of service, not evidence that the completed sanctuary is already
erected. This remains contextual coverage under the existing Horeb/Sinai page.

See [[The Priestly Garments Made]]. Source: cited Exodus verses checked in local raw WEB, 2026-10-25.

### The work completed — Exodus 39:32–43

The completed sanctuary and equipment are brought to Moses within the continuing Sinai
camp narrative (39:33–43). The unit gives no new geographical location and narrates no departure.
The completed work is not yet the erected, cloud-filled sanctuary of chapter 40. Coverage here
is contextual under the existing Horeb/Sinai identification, rather than a claim that the name
Horeb occurs in these verses.

See [[The Work Completed]]. Source: cited passages checked in the stored WEB Genesis and Exodus
texts, 2026-10-25.

### The tabernacle raised and filled — Exodus 40:1–38

The sanctuary is raised while the narrative remains at the Sinai camp established in
Exodus 19:1–2. Chapter 40 supplies a date and sanctuary positions, but does not name
Sinai again or locate a new camp. Its final journey rule (40:36–38) looks beyond this
setting without narrating a dated departure. The location remains filed here under the
existing Horeb/Sinai convention, without adding a modern identification.

See [[The Tabernacle Raised and the Glory]]. Source: Exodus 40 checked in `raw/bible/Exodus.md` (WEB), 2026-10-25.

### The burnt offering — Leviticus 1:1–17

The tent and its altar are the immediate setting; the continuing Sinai setting is confirmed by
Leviticus 7:38. Leviticus 1 does not name the mountain or narrate a departure. This page retains the
wiki’s Horeb/Sinai grouping without supplying a modern location.

See [[The Burnt Offering]] . Source: stored WEB Leviticus 1; comparisons identified on the passage
page.

### Meal offerings at the sanctuary

The altar receives the grain memorial in the sanctuary setting continued from Leviticus 1:1. Sinai is contextual here and named retrospectively in 7:38, not in chapter 2 itself. See [[The Meal Offering]].

### The peace offering — Leviticus 3

The offering is brought to the Tent of Meeting, with slaughter before it and blood handled at its altar (3:2, 8, 13). Sinai is the broader setting identified in 7:38, not a mountain named anew in chapter 3.

See [[The Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 3; contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Priest and congregation — Leviticus 4:1–21

Within the Sinai setting established by Leviticus 7:38, the rite moves between the Tent of
Meeting, the inner altar, the outer altar, and a clean ash site outside the camp (4:5–12,
16–21). The mountain is not named in this unit; no new named geographical place is introduced.

See [[The Sin Offering for Priest and Congregation]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:1–21;
contextual comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Ruler and individual — Leviticus 4:22–35

Leviticus 4:22–35 continues the Sinai legislation (location supplied by 7:38), but names
no mountain. Its operative locations are the burnt-offering slaughter place and altar;
blood is not carried into the tent in these cases.

See [[The Sin Offering for Ruler and Individual]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 4:22–35; contextual
comparisons documented on the passage page.

### Confession and offerings according to means

Leviticus 7:38 locates the offering laws at Sinai. The confession and means provisions of 5:1–13 belong to that setting, but name no mountain or new geographical location.

See [[Confession and Offerings According to Means]]. Sources: stored WEB Leviticus 4–5; 7:38; Exodus 28:1.

### The trespass-offering laws

The offering laws belong to the Sinai setting identified in Leviticus 7:38. Leviticus 5:14–19 itself names no mountain or new geographical location.

See [[The Trespass Offering for Holy Things and Unknown Guilt]].

### Fraud, restitution, and forgiveness — Leviticus 6:1–7

The offering laws are located at Sinai by Leviticus 7:38. This unit names no geographical

See [[Restitution for Fraud and False Oaths]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:1–7; contextual comparisons documented there.

### Altar fire and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:8–23

The altar-fire and meal-offering laws belong to the Sinai instructions identified in 7:38. The mountain is not named in 6:8–23; its own spatial distinctions concern altar, court, and a clean place outside the camp.

See [[The Altar Fire and the Priests’ Meal Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:8–23; comparisons documented there.

### Holy flesh and priestly portions — Leviticus 6:24–7:10

Leviticus 7:38 locates these offering laws at Sinai. This unit does not name the mountain; its
spatial distinctions concern slaughter, altar, holy eating in the court, and blood brought into
the tent.

See [[The Priests’ Portions of Sin and Trespass Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6:24–7:10.

### Thanksgiving and peace-offering meals — Leviticus 7:11–21

The Sinai setting confirmed by Leviticus 7:38 includes the peace-offering laws of
7:11–21. The unit names no geographical site and does not repeat the court eating
restriction applied to sin offerings in 6:26; the broader Sinai context should not
be turned into an unstated location rule for this meal.

See [[Thanksgiving, Vows, and the Peace Offering]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 6–7.

### Fat, blood, and priestly shares — Leviticus 7:22–38

Leviticus 7:38 explicitly locates the offering commands at Mount Sinai and in the wilderness of
Sinai. This confirms the setting previously inferred for the offering units; it supplies no new
modern identification of the site.

See [[Fat, Blood, and the Priests’ Share]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 7:22–38.

### Aaron and his sons consecrated — Leviticus 8:1–36

The consecration takes place at the Tent of Meeting in the Sinai setting established by
Leviticus 7:38. Chapter 8 names no mountain; its spatial contrasts are the doorway, altar,
and outside of the camp. This is contextual placement, not a fresh named occurrence of Sinai.

See [[Aaron and His Sons Consecrated]]. Source: stored WEB Leviticus 8; Exodus 28:1; 29; Leviticus 7:38.

### The eighth-day service — Leviticus 9

The Sinai camp remains the narrative setting, inferred from 7:38; chapter 9 itself locates the
action at the tent and altar and outside the camp, without naming the mountain. See
[[The Eighth Day and the Appearing Glory]] .

### Judgment at the sanctuary — Leviticus 10:1–11
