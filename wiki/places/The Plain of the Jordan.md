# The Plain of the Jordan

## Overview

The best-watered ground in sight, and the one part of the land the reader is told will be destroyed
before anyone in the story chooses it.

**The Hebrew is *kikkar ha-Yardēn*, the *round* or *disk* of the Jordan.** *Kikkar* is the word for a
loaf, a talent-weight, a flat circular thing; used geographically it names the broad oval basin where
the Jordan valley opens out before the river reaches the Dead Sea. English versions render it
*plain*, *circle*, *valley*, or *district*. Genesis 14:3 gives it a second name for the battle in it —
*the Valley of Siddim, which is the Salt Sea*.

**Lot chooses it by looking at it.** *"Lot lifted up his eyes, and saw all the plain of the Jordan,
that it was well-watered everywhere, before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, like the garden of
Yahweh, like the land of Egypt, as you go to Zoar"* (Genesis 13:10). The verse is doing four things
at once, and only the first of them is geography:

- ***Well-watered everywhere*** — *kullāh mašqeh*, the honest reason. This is the Rift floor with
  perennial springs and a river through it, in a country where everything else depends on rain.
- ***Like the garden of Yahweh*** — Eden, which humanity was driven out of and barred from with a
  flaming sword (3:24).
- ***Like the land of Egypt*** — the irrigated Nile, and the country Abram was escorted out of eleven
  verses earlier (12:20). Deuteronomy 11:10–12 will make the contrast doctrinal: Canaan is *not* like
  Egypt, *"where you sowed your seed and watered it with your foot"*, but *"a land which Yahweh your
  God cares for … drinks water from the rain of the sky."* Lot picks the piece of Canaan that is most
  like the place they just left.
- ***Before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah*** — the narrator's aside, addressed to the reader
  over the head of the man doing the looking.

See [[Abram and Lot Separate]] and [[Lift Up Your Eyes - Two Ways of Seeing the Land]].

**It is also, by 13:12, outside the land.** *"Abram lived in the land of Canaan, and Lot lived in the
cities of the plain"* — the sentence puts the two men on opposite sides of a boundary that the offer
of 13:9 never mentioned. Whatever the writer's precise mental map, the plain is being treated as not
Canaan, which makes Lot's choice a departure from the promised territory as well as from his uncle.

### The ground itself

The plain sits in the Jordan Rift, part of the Dead Sea Transform: an active strike-slip fault
system, and the lowest land surface on earth — the Dead Sea shore is around 430 metres below sea
level and still dropping. Bitumen seeps in the basin are attested in antiquity and in Genesis itself
(*"the valley of Siddim was full of tar pits"*, 14:10), and the southwestern shore carries Mount
Sodom, a salt diapir several kilometres long. It is a landscape in which burning, subsidence, and
salt are all ordinary local facts, which is worth knowing when reading Genesis 19 whatever one
concludes about the event.

Where the five cities stood is unsettled: a **southern** proposal places them at the shallow southern
end of the Dead Sea, a **northern** one identifies Tall el-Hammam on the eastern plain north of it.
The wiki endorses neither; see [[Sodom]].
