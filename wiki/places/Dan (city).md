# Dan (city)

## Overview

The northern limit of Abram's pursuit, and the clearest anachronism in Genesis.

*"He led out his trained men, born in his house, three hundred and eighteen, and pursued as far as
Dan"* (Genesis 14:14). The town is called **Laish** at the time of the judges, and it is renamed only
when the migrating tribe of Dan captures it: *"They called the name of the city Dan, after the name
of Dan their father"* (Judges 18:29; the parallel at Joshua 19:47 has *Leshem*). Dan the man is
Jacob's son, five generations after Abram. See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

**This is a narrator's gloss, not an error in the story.** Genesis names places by the names its
readers use, openly and often — [[Bethel]] at 12:8 and 13:3, sixteen chapters before 28:19 gives it
that name; [[Hebron]] at 13:18, ten chapters before 23:2; *"the valley of Siddim (also called the
Salt Sea)"* in this very chapter. The alternative, a pre-Danite Dan unrelated to the tribe, is
available and unnecessary. What the gloss does establish is that whoever wrote the verse was writing
at a distance, for readers who needed the map translated. The parallel case fourteen verses earlier
is *"all the country of the Amalekites"* (14:7), Amalek being Esau's grandson at 36:12.

**The phrase is a formula.** *From Dan to Beersheba* is the Bible's standing expression for the
whole length of the land (Judges 20:1; 1 Samuel 3:20; 2 Samuel 3:10; 1 Kings 4:25), Dan being the
northern extremity and Beersheba the southern. Abram pursuing *as far as Dan* is therefore a way of
saying he chased the coalition to the top of the country — and then past it, since 14:15 has him
going on to Hobah *"on the left hand of Damascus"*.

**What it becomes.** Jeroboam sets one of his two golden calves here when the kingdom divides
(1 Kings 12:29–30), which makes Dan a byword for illegitimate worship in the northern kingdom
(Amos 8:14). The site, Tel Dan in the far north of modern Israel at the foot of Mount Hermon, has
produced a Middle Bronze mud-brick gateway and, in 1993, the Tel Dan Stele — an Aramaic victory
inscription that contains the earliest extrabiblical reference to the *bytdwd*, the House of David.
