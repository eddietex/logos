# Mahanaim

## Overview

*"Two camps."* ***Maḥănayim*** is the dual form of ***maḥăneh***, *camp* — a grammatical number
Hebrew reserves for things that naturally come in pairs (hands, eyes, gates), applied here to a place
name. **Jacob coins it on the spot** after angels meet him unsought, saying only *"this is God's
camp"* (32:2) — a single camp, in his own words, named with a dual noun. **The name anticipates a
division he has not yet made**: he does not split his own people into two companies until five verses
later, out of fear of Esau's four hundred men (32:7–8). Whether the dual counts *God's camp and
Jacob's camp*, or looks forward to the two companies Jacob is about to create, Genesis does not say;
this page adopts neither reading over the other. See [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]].

Later in the historical books it is a real administrative town east of the Jordan in Gilead — a
Levitical city (Joshua 21:38), the place Ish-bosheth is made king over Israel (2 Samuel 2:8–9), a
stop on David's flight from Absalom (2 Samuel 17:24, 27), and one of Solomon's twelve provincial
districts (1 Kings 4:14). Its exact site is not archaeologically fixed.
