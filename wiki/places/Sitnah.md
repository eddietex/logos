# Sitnah

## Overview

**The second well Isaac digs in the valley of Gerar, disputed like the first and given up like the
first — and the first occurrence in the Bible of the noun behind *śāṭān*.**

> **21** They dug another well, and they argued over that, also. He called its name Sitnah.

***Śiṭnâ*** is **hostility**, **enmity**, or **accusation**, from the root ***śāṭan***, to be
hostile, to oppose, to accuse. **Genesis gives no etymological gloss for this one** — 26:20 explained
*Esek* and 26:22 explains *Rehoboth*, but 26:21 just names it and moves on, because the sentence
before it has already said what happened.

**The root is the one the Hebrew Bible builds *śāṭān* on.** As a common noun it means an adversary,
and the Bible uses it that way of ordinary human and angelic opponents (1 Samuel 29:4; 1 Kings 5:4;
11:14, 23; Numbers 22:22). With the article it becomes a title, *haśśāṭān*, **the accuser** — the
figure in the heavenly court in Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3:1–2, whose function there is prosecutorial
rather than diabolic. The related noun *śiṭnâ* itself reappears in Ezra 4:6 as the technical term for
a written **accusation** lodged against the returning exiles. **Its first appearance in Scripture is
a hole in the ground in the valley of Gerar**, and the Bible attaches no theology to it here at all.
See [[Job of Uz]] and [[The Witness in Heaven]].

**Where it is.** Unlocated, like [[Esek]] and [[Rehoboth]]. All three are known only from Genesis
26:19–22, all three are in the wadi west of the [[Beersheba]] basin, and none has ever been
identified with a site.

**What it is doing in the chapter.** It is the second of two concessions. Isaac's servants dig, the
herdsmen of Gerar argue, and he leaves — for the second time, without a claim, an appeal to his
father's treaty, or a fight. **Two wells lost and a third kept is the shape of the sequence**, and
the wiki reads the doubling as deliberate: one concession is an incident, two is a method. See
[[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]] and [[Strife and Peacemaking]].
