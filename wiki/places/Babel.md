# Babel

## Overview

The Bible's first named capital, and the city whose name will end up standing for every human project
that sets itself against God. It enters Scripture in a list, without comment:

> **10** The beginning of his kingdom was Babel, Erech, Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar.

That is all [[The Table of Nations]] says about it. It is the first of [[Nimrod]]'s four cities in
[[Shinar]], the head of *rēʾšît mamlaktô*, *the beginning of his kingdom* — the first occurrence of
the word *kingdom* in the Bible. Twenty-four verses later the same name reappears at the head of the
next pericope, attached to a city, a tower, and an etymology, and from there it runs the length of the
canon.

**The founding story is nine verses long and names nobody.** In [[The Tower of Babel]] a migrating
people find a plain in Shinar, invent fired brick set in bitumen because there is no stone, and
resolve to build *"a city, and a tower whose top reaches to the sky, and let's make a name for
ourselves, lest we be scattered abroad on the surface of the whole earth"* (11:4). God comes down to
look, confuses their speech, and scatters them; the work stops. The narrative gives the city four
mentions and the tower two, and the name at 11:9 is the **city's** — Genesis is telling the founding
of Babylon, not the erection of a building.

**The tower is a ziggurat, and the joke is architectural.** Babylon's was *Etemenanki*, *the house of
the foundation of heaven and earth*: a mud-brick platform temple with a shrine on top, rebuilt by
Nabopolassar and Nebuchadnezzar II and described by Herodotus (*Histories* 1.181). A ziggurat is not
a stair for people climbing up; it is a platform for a god coming down. Genesis 11:5 has Yahweh come
down anyway — to *see* it.

**Babel is Babylon.** Hebrew makes no distinction: *bābel* is the word in Genesis 10:10, in Genesis
11:9, and in every one of the roughly 280 later occurrences that English Bibles render *Babylon* —
Isaiah's oracles, Jeremiah's, the exile, Daniel, and Revelation. The choice to translate the two
Genesis passages as *Babel* and everything after as *Babylon* is a convention of English versions, and
it hides the fact that the tower and the empire are one word.

**Two etymologies, and Genesis chooses the polemical one.** The Akkadian *bāb-ilim* means *gate of
the god*, which is what the Babylonians understood their city's name to mean and what the ziggurat
Etemenanki was built to embody. Genesis 11:9 instead derives it from *bālal*, **to confuse**:
*"Therefore its name was called Babel, because there Yahweh confused the language of all the earth."*
That is not a philological claim but a pun, and it is a pointed one — the gate of the god renamed
*babble* by the God who came down to look at it. The wordplay runs deeper than the name: *nilbənâ
ləbēnîm*, *let us brick bricks* (11:3), is the consonants of *nābəlâ*, *let us confuse* (11:7),
reversed.

**The Table gets there first.** Whatever Genesis 11 will say about the city, Genesis 10 has already
placed it: it is not a spontaneous human venture on an empty plain but the capital of a kingdom
founded by a *gibbōr* whose word links him to the pre-flood giants. The two chapters are usually read
as one story about pride, and the Table supplies its political setting before the story is told. Note
that Genesis 11 itself names no builder — the tradition that Nimrod raised the tower is an inference
from 10:10, not a statement of the text.

**Afterwards.** Babylon takes Judah into exile (2 Kings 25), and Isaiah 13–14 and Jeremiah 50–51 spend
four chapters on its fall. Psalm 137 is sung *"by the rivers of Babylon"*. Daniel is taken to
[[Shinar]] to serve in its court. And the New Testament makes the name a cipher — *"Babylon the great,
the mother of the prostitutes"* (Revelation 17:5), a city that is also an empire, falling in a single
hour (Revelation 18:2, 10, 21), with 1 Peter 5:13 apparently using it of Rome. The entire arc starts
as one entry in a king's city list. See [[Babel and Babylon the Great]].

## Appears in

- [[The Tower of Babel]] — Genesis 11:1–9: founded, abandoned, and named; the only narrative
  Scripture gives the city, and the source of everything the canon later does with the word

## Events here

- Named as *"the beginning of his kingdom"* under Nimrod — Genesis 10:10, [[The Table of Nations]]
- The city and tower are built, the language confused, and the builders scattered; the name is given
  its etymology — Genesis 11:1–9, [[The Tower of Babel]]
- Amraphel king of Shinar joins the coalition Abram defeats — Genesis 14:1, 9 *(not yet ingested)*
- Judah deported and the temple vessels carried here — 2 Kings 25; Daniel 1:1–2
