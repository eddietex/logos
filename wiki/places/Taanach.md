# Taanach

## Overview

### The kings fought at Taanach — Judges 5:19

*The kings came and fought, then the kings of Canaan fought at Taanach by the waters of Megiddo.
They took no plunder of silver* (5:19). The prose of Judges 4 placed the battle at the Kishon and
Tabor and did not name Taanach; the Song names it. Taanach is one of the towns whose Canaanites
Manasseh *did not drive out* (1:27), so the kings of Canaan fight from a city Israel had left in
their hands. See [[The Song of Deborah]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 1:27; 4:6–16; 5:19.

### Not driven out — Judges 1:27

One of Manasseh's five cities not driven out (27), as in Joshua 17:11–12; its king is counted in Joshua 12:21.

See [[The Tribes That Did Not Drive Out]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 1:22–36.

### Levitical cities — Joshua 21:1–42

Allocated with its suburbs from half-Manasseh to the remaining Kohathites (21:25).  Joshua 17:11–13 records non-removal and forced labor; the Levitical grant does not narrate a new conquest.

See [[The Cities of the Levites]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 21:1–42.

### Manasseh’s inheritance — Joshua 17:1–13

Named among Manasseh’s allotted cities with dependent towns (11), whose inhabitants are not driven
out (12). The later imposition of forced labor (13) qualifies any inference that the defeat of its
king in Joshua 12 meant complete occupation.

See [[The Inheritance of Manasseh]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 17:1–13.

Royal city whose king is counted in Joshua 12:21, paired with Megiddo. No battle is narrated. Joshua 17:11–12 places Taanach among the towns Manasseh held in Issachar and Asher and *couldn't drive out the inhabitants*; Judges 1:27 (WEB checked, not yet ingested) repeats that Manasseh *did not drive out* Taanach. Joshua 21:25 later gives it to the Levites. A king struck, a population not driven out: see [[Kings Struck and Cities Not Driven Out - Joshua 12, 15-17, and Judges 1]].
