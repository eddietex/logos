# Esek

## Overview

**The first of the three wells Isaac's servants dig in the valley of Gerar, and the first he gives
up.**

> **19** Isaac’s servants dug in the valley, and found there a well of springing water.
> **20** The herdsmen of Gerar argued with Isaac’s herdsmen, saying, “The water is ours.” He called
> the name of the well Esek, because they contended with him.

***ʿĒśeq*** means **contention** or **dispute**, and Genesis supplies the etymology in the same
sentence: *kî hitʿaśśəqû ʿimmô*, **because they contended with him** — the verb and the noun from one
root, which is the book's standard way of naming a place. **The name is not the water's; it is the
argument's.** Every body of water Genesis names in the patriarchal narratives is named for something
that happened at it rather than for anything about the water, and this is the clearest case: a well
of *living water* — a spring, not a cistern — called *quarrel*.

**Where it is.** *"in the valley"* (26:19), which 26:17 has already identified as **the valley of
Gerar** — the wadi running west from the [[Beersheba]] basin toward the coastal plain, where Isaac
encamped after [[Abimelech (of Isaac's time)]] expelled him from the town. **No site has been
identified**, and none of the three wells of 26:19–22 is locatable; they are known only from these
verses.

**What the dispute is about.** *"The water is ours"* — *lānû hammayim* — is the herdsmen of Gerar's
whole argument, and it is the counter-claim to the customary rule both Genesis 21 and Genesis 26
assume, that **labour confers the right**. Abraham had said *"that I have dug this well"* (21:30) and
paid seven ewe lambs to get it on the record. **Isaac's servants dig, and the herdsmen say the water
was theirs already**, and Isaac neither argues the point nor invokes his father's treaty. He digs
somewhere else. See [[The Wells of Abraham and the Wells of Isaac]] and [[Strife and Peacemaking]].

**It is the first of a sequence with an argument in it** — Esek, then [[Sitnah]], then
[[Rehoboth]] — and the sequence is the wiki's clearest statement of Isaac's method: **concede twice,
move further out, and credit the room to God.**
