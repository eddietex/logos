# The Euphrates

## Overview

The largest river in western Asia, and in the Bible the standing name for *far away in that
direction* — the northern edge of the promise, the boundary of empire, and the water Israel is
carried across into exile.

**Genesis names it twice, at the two ends of its own argument.** It is the fourth of the rivers that
go out of [[Eden]]: *"The fourth river is the Euphrates"* (2:14) — given without a description, where
the first three each get a clause, as though it needed none. And it is the far border of the land
sworn to Abram: *"from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates"* (15:18). A river
that watered the garden becomes the limit of a land grant. See
[[The Covenant Between the Pieces]].

*"The great river"* is the phrase the Torah keeps for it (Deuteronomy 1:7; Joshua 1:4), and from the
exile onward it is often just **the River** with no name attached — *ʿēber ha-nāhār*, *beyond the
River*, becomes the Persian empire's administrative name for the whole province west of it
(Ezra 4:10–11; Nehemiah 2:7), and Joshua 24:2–3 uses the same phrase for where Abram's fathers lived
before he was called: *"Your fathers lived of old time beyond the River."* The Euphrates is the line
Abram crossed to become, on one etymology, *the Hebrew* — *the one from the other side*. See
[[Abram the Hebrew - The First Use of the Name]].

**Geographically** it rises in the Armenian highlands, runs some 1,700 miles southeast through Syria
and Iraq, joins the Tigris, and empties into the Persian Gulf. Between the two rivers is Mesopotamia
— *the land between the rivers* — where [[Shinar]], [[Ur of the Chaldees]], Babylon, and
[[Haran (city)]] all sit, which is to say that every place Abram's family came from lies on or
beyond it.

**The boundary it marks is a claim Israel almost never holds.** *"From the river of Egypt to the
great river"* recurs at Exodus 23:31, Deuteronomy 1:7 and 11:24, and Joshua 1:4; the only period in
which anything like it is real is Solomon's, *"and he ruled over all the kings from the River even
to the land of the Philistines, and to the border of Egypt"* (1 Kings 4:21, 24; 2 Chronicles 9:26),
and briefly under David's Aramean campaigns (2 Samuel 8:3). Josiah dies trying to stop Neco at
Megiddo on his way to it (2 Kings 23:29); Nebuchadnezzar takes everything *"from the brook of Egypt
to the river Euphrates"* from the king of Egypt (2 Kings 24:7) and takes Judah with it. The border
of the promise and the reach of the empire that ends the kingdom are the same line, described in the
same words. See [[From the River of Egypt to the Euphrates]].

**And it keeps that double sense to the end of the canon.** Jeremiah is sent to bury a loincloth by
the Euphrates and dig it up ruined (Jeremiah 13:1–7); Psalm 137 sits and weeps *"by the rivers of
Babylon"*; Isaiah 8:7 makes Assyria *"the waters of the River, strong and many"* overflowing Judah;
and Revelation dries it up twice — at the sixth trumpet, releasing the angels bound at it (9:14),
and at the sixth bowl, *"that the way might be prepared for the kings that come from the sunrise"*
(16:12). It is the last frontier the Bible names.

## Appears in

- [[The Garden of Eden]] — Genesis 2:14; the fourth river out of Eden, named
  without a description
- [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]] — Genesis 15:18; the northern boundary of the land sworn to
  Abram, and the first border the promise is ever given
- [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]] — Genesis 31:21; ***"he rose up, passed over the River"*** — the only
  time in Genesis anyone is said to cross it, and **the definite noun with no qualifier is enough to
  name it.** It is Terah's journey reversed by a great-grandson going the other way, and **the
  geography does not work as a week**: the crossing and the arrival in [[Gilead]] are some four
  hundred miles apart, with a three-day head start and a seven-day pursuit. The wiki states the
  difficulty and does not resolve it

## Related places

- [[Eden]] — where Genesis 2:14 puts its source, along with the Tigris, Pishon, and Gihon
- [[Shinar]] — the plain on its lower course, and Babel's
- [[Ur of the Chaldees]] — near its mouth on the standard identification, and what Abram was brought
  out of
- [[Haran (city)]] — on the Balikh, a tributary of its middle course, where Terah stopped
- [[Canaan]] — the land whose promised northern limit it is, and which never reaches it
- [[Assyria]] — the empire on the Tigris that crosses it westward and takes the northern kingdom
- [[Damascus]] — the great city between it and Canaan, and Abram's furthest point of pursuit

## Events here

- Named as the fourth river out of Eden — Genesis 2:14, [[The Garden of Eden]]
- Made the northern boundary of the land promised to Abram's offspring — Genesis 15:18,
  [[The Covenant Between the Pieces]]
- Crossed westward by Jacob, fleeing Laban with his household and herds — Genesis 31:21,
  [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]]
- The boundary formula repeated to Israel — Exodus 23:31; Deuteronomy 1:7, 11:24; Joshua 1:4
- David strikes Hadadezer *"as he went to recover his dominion at the River"* — 2 Samuel 8:3
- Solomon's rule reaches from it to the border of Egypt — 1 Kings 4:21, 24
- Jeremiah's ruined loincloth — Jeremiah 13:1–7
- Nebuchadnezzar takes everything from the brook of Egypt to it — 2 Kings 24:7
- Dried up for the kings from the sunrise — Revelation 16:12
