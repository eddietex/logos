# Zoar

## Overview

**The fifth city of the plain, and the only one left standing — spared because a frightened man did
not want to walk uphill.**

It appears first under another name. The coalition list of Genesis 14 ends with *"the king of Bela
(also called Zoar)"* (14:2, and the same parenthesis again at 14:8), where the narrator supplies the
later name in an aside because that is the name his readers know. It is the one city of the five
whose king Genesis never names — Bera, Birsha, Shinab, and Shemeber have names, and this king does
not.

### The naming

> **20** See now, this city is near to flee to, and it is a little one. Oh let me escape there
> (isn’t it a little one?), and my soul will live.”

> **22** Hurry, escape there, for I can’t do anything until you get there.” Therefore the name of
> the city was called Zoar.

***Ṣōʿar*, from *miṣʿār*, "a little one"** — the word Lot uses twice in his plea, turned into the
town's name in the verse that grants it. It is one of Genesis's naming etiologies, and an unusually
pointed one: the city is called *Littleness* forever because its smallness was the argument for
sparing it.

**The concession is the chapter's structural rhyme with Abraham's.** Abraham bargained for a city he
had no tie to, on the ground that the Judge of all the earth should do right, and did not get it.
Lot bargains for a bolt-hole, on the ground that the mountains are too far and he is afraid, and
does — *"I have granted your request concerning this thing also"* (19:21). Genesis reports the
asymmetry and says nothing about it. See [[The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah]].

**And he leaves it immediately.** *"Lot went up out of Zoar, and lived in the mountain, and his two
daughters with him; for he was afraid to live in Zoar"* (19:30) — the mountain he had just refused,
and the fear he had just used as an argument, reversed inside one verse. The city he saved is the
city he then will not live in.

### Where it was, and afterwards

Zoar is the one city of the plain that outlives the chapter, and the later books treat it as an
ordinary functioning town at the southeast end of the Dead Sea:

- **Deuteronomy 34:3** — Moses on Nebo is shown the land *"to Zoar"*, the southern limit of the view.
- **Isaiah 15:5** and **Jeremiah 48:34** — in the oracles against Moab, a place fugitives run to,
  named alongside Horonaim and Eglath Shelishiyah.

That continued existence is itself a datum, and the wiki records it plainly: **whatever happened to
the plain, Zoar was still there, and Scripture keeps saying so for centuries.** The Byzantine town
of Zoara/Segor at the Ghor es-Safi, on the southeastern shore, is the traditional identification and
is drawn with a church and the caption *Balak also Segor* on the sixth-century Madaba mosaic map.
Like every other identification in the plain, it is not settled — see [[Sodom]] for why the whole
region's geography is contested.
