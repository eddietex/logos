# Eden

## Overview

The first named place in the Bible. Strictly, **Eden is a region and the garden is inside it** —
*"Yahweh God planted a garden eastward, in Eden"* (2:8) — though "Eden" and "the garden of Eden"
are used interchangeably from Genesis 3 onward. The Hebrew *ʿēden* suggests luxury or delight;
the Septuagint renders "garden" as *paradeisos*, a Persian loanword for a walled royal park,
which is how "paradise" entered the language.

What the text says is in it: every tree *"pleasant to the sight, and good for food"*, and two
singled out in the middle — the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. A
river rises in Eden, waters the garden, and divides into four heads: **Pishon** (through Havilah,
where there is gold, bdellium, and onyx), **Gihon** (through Cush), **Hiddekel** — the Tigris,
flowing "in front of Assyria" — and the **Euphrates**.

The geography half-locates and half-refuses to locate it. Two rivers are the great known rivers
of Mesopotamia; two are unidentifiable, and no river system on earth has one source splitting into
four. Proposals have put Eden at the head of the Persian Gulf, in Armenia where the Tigris and
Euphrates rise, and in Africa (reading Cush as Ethiopia). The description works better as a place
upstream of the known world — the source from which its waters come — than as a point on a map.

Several details read as sanctuary architecture rather than landscape: the entrance is on the east,
cherubim are stationed to guard it (3:24) as they are embroidered on the tabernacle curtains, gold
and onyx are named among its materials as they are among the tabernacle's and the high priest's,
and the man's assignment — *ʿābad* and *šāmar*, "cultivate and keep" — is the same word pair used
for the Levites' service in the tent. On this reading the garden is the first holy place and the
whole later temple is a memory of it.

Eden does not disappear from Scripture after Genesis 3. The prophets use it as the standard of
fertility and of lost glory (Isaiah 51:3, Ezekiel 36:35, Joel 2:3), Ezekiel makes it the setting of
an oracle against a proud king (28:13–16) and against Pharaoh as a great tree (31:8–9), and
Revelation closes the canon with the tree of life growing again beside a river, in a city.

## Appears in

- [[The Garden of Eden]] — planted, described, and given to the man to work and guard
- Genesis 3 *(not yet ingested)* — the tempting, the judgment, and the expulsion east of the garden

## Related places

- The four rivers — Pishon, Gihon, Hiddekel (Tigris), Euphrates — and the lands they run through:
  Havilah, Cush, Assyria. Their pages come with Genesis 10 and the narratives that use them.
- The tabernacle and the temple, which reuse the garden's guarded-eastward-entrance layout and its
  materials.
- The New Jerusalem of Revelation 21–22 — see
  [[The Tree of Life - Eden and the New Jerusalem]].

## Events here

- God forms the man from dust and breathes life into him (2:7)
- God plants the garden and places the man in it to cultivate and keep it (2:8, 2:15)
- The command concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (2:16–17)
- The animals are formed and brought to the man to be named (2:19–20)
- The woman is built from the man's side and brought to him (2:21–22)
