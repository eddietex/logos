# En Hakkore

## Overview

### The spring opened for Samson — Judges 15:18–19

After the jawbone, [[Samson]] *was very thirsty, and called on Yahweh*: *You have given this great
deliverance by the hand of your servant; and now shall I die of thirst, and fall into the hands of the
uncircumcised?* (Judges 15:18). *But God split the hollow place that is in Lehi, and water came out of
it. When he had drunk, his spirit came again, and he revived. Therefore its name was called En Hakkore,
which is in Lehi, to this day* (19).

*ʿÊn* is *spring*. *Haqqôrēʾ* has the consonants of *qārāʾ*, *call*, the verb of *called on Yahweh* in
18 (WLC). The WLC tags the whole name as one proper noun (lemma 5875). It is usually rendered *the spring
of the one who calls*; reported, not checked. If that reading is right, the spring is named for the prayer
and not for the battle, and Lehi now has two names: one for what Samson did, Ramath Lehi (17), and one for
what he asked.

*To this day* places the spring in the narrator's own time. The site is not identified here.

It is the third water God gives in the stored books to people who fear death by thirst, after Hagar's
well (Genesis 21:19) and the rock at Massah (Exodus 17:3, 6). Like [[Beer Lahai Roi]], it is named for an
act of God at the place. See [[Shall I Die of Thirst - Massah, Hagar's Well, and En Hakkore]].

See [[Samson Against the Philistines at Lehi]]. Source: stored WEB Genesis 21:19; Exodus 17:3, 6; Judges
15:18–19; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb, `Judg.xml`), 2026-11-16.
