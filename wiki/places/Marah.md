# Marah

## Overview

**The first camp after the sea, three days into the wilderness, named for its water.**

> **23** When they came to Marah, they couldn't drink from the waters of Marah, for they were bitter.
> Therefore its name was called Marah. (Exodus 15:23)

***Mārâ*** is the adjective *mar*, *bitter*, in the feminine — **the name says what the water was** (WLC).
The verse uses it three times. **Who named it is not said**: *"Therefore its name was called"* is the
impersonal naming formula of Genesis (*ʿal-kēn qārāʾ šəmāh*, as at Babel, Genesis 11:9; WLC).

**The water was made sweet, and the name stayed.** Moses cried to Yahweh, was shown a tree, threw it into
the water, and *"the waters were made sweet"* (15:25). Numbers 33:8–9 keeps the place in the itinerary —
*"encamped in Marah. They traveled from Marah, and came to Elim"* (WEB; *not yet ingested*) — and does not
mention the sweetening. **The place is remembered for the problem, not the solution.**

**It is also where Israel's first statute after the sea was given and its first test set**: *"There he
made a statute and an ordinance for them, and there he tested them"* (15:25), followed by the promise
that ends ***"I am Yahweh who heals you"*** (15:26). **Marah is the first place in the wilderness where
God speaks law.** See [[Law and Obedience]], [[Testing]], and [[Healing]].

**Where it is is not known.** The text puts it three days from the crossing in *"the wilderness of Shur"*
(15:22), which Numbers 33:8 calls *"the wilderness of Etham"*. Identifications with brackish springs on the
eastern side of the Gulf of Suez have been proposed; they are reported, not checked in this pass, and none
is adopted.

**Naomi borrows the name for herself**: *"Don't call me Naomi. Call me Mara; for the Almighty has dealt very
bitterly with me"* (Ruth 1:20; WEB; *not yet ingested*). The WLC spells her *Mārāʾ*, with a final *ʾālep*,
and files it under a separate number from the place; the meaning is the same.
