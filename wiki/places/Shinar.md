# Shinar

## Overview

Southern Mesopotamia — the alluvial plain between the lower Tigris and Euphrates — and the place
where, in Genesis, human political power begins.

> **10** The beginning of his kingdom was Babel, Erech, Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar.

Four cities in one clause, and three of the four are among the oldest on earth: **Babel** is Babylon,
**Erech** is Uruk, **Accad** is Akkad, Sargon's capital and the name that the East Semitic language is
called by. **Calneh** has no agreed identification and may not be a name at all — the same consonants
can be read *kullānâ*, *all of them*, which would give *"Babel, Erech, and Accad, all of them in the
land of Shinar"*. See [[Nimrod]] and [[Kingship and Empire]].

**The name.** *Šinʿār* is the Hebrew term for Babylonia and is used only of it, eight times in the
Bible. It is probably a rendering of Sumer, or of the Kassite-era designation *Šanḫar*; it is not the
Akkadians' or Babylonians' own name for their country. Genesis uses it as a fixed archaic label,
which is part of why the region reads, in these chapters, as somewhere far away and long ago.

**Everything that happens in Shinar in Scripture is about ambition.** The first kingdom starts here
(10:10). The tower is built here — *"they found a plain in the land of Shinar, and they lived there"*
(11:2). Amraphel king of Shinar joins the four-king coalition Abram defeats (Genesis 14:1, 9). Achan
steals *"a beautiful Babylonian robe"* — literally *a mantle of Shinar* — and is destroyed for it
(Joshua 7:21). Nebuchadnezzar carries the temple vessels *"into the land of Shinar, to the house of
his god"* (Daniel 1:2). And Zechariah 5:5–11 has wickedness carried in a basket to *"the land of
Shinar"* and given a house there. The place is a proper noun that behaves like a theme.

## Appears in

- [[The Table of Nations]] — Genesis 10:10, the site of the first kingdom in Scripture
- [[The Tower of Babel]] — Genesis 11:2, the plain the builders settle on; the whole pericope happens
  here, and the *biqʿâ*, the broad valley, is the one landscape with no height in it
