# The Jabbok

## Overview

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

The river marks the border of the children of Ammon in the eastern grant (16). Reaching this
boundary is not a grant of Ammonite territory.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### Sihon’s defeat recalled — Deuteronomy 2:24–37

Its banks appear among the limits Israel does not approach under Yahweh’s prohibition (37). Numbers
21:24 describes conquest up to the Jabbok; the accounts distinguish conquered Amorite territory from
protected Ammonite territory without supplying a complete boundary map.

See [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:24–37.

### Sihon and Og — Numbers 21:21–35

Northern endpoint in the description of Israel’s acquisition from Sihon, from the Arnon to the
Jabbok and up to Ammon (24). The verse gives a territorial boundary, not a new episode at Jacob’s
ford.

See [[Sihon and Og]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 21:21–35.


The river that splits Gilead in two, running west from the Transjordanian highlands into the Jordan
about midway between the Sea of Galilee and the Dead Sea — the modern Zarqa. It is a border in every
later text that mentions it: Numbers 21:24 and Deuteronomy 2:37 make it the line between Ammon and the
Amorite kingdom Israel takes from Sihon, and Joshua 12:2 repeats the boundary. **In Genesis it is not
yet a political border, only a ford one man crosses alone at night**, after sending his entire
household and everything he owns over ahead of him (32:22–23). ***Yabbōq*** and ***yēʾābēq***, *he
wrestled* (32:24), sit one letter apart in Hebrew — a wordplay Genesis lets stand without commenting
on it, in the same way it lets *gilʿād* and *galʿēd* stand one chapter earlier.
