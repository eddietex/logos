# Jerusalem

## Overview

### Adoni-Bezek brought to die — Judges 1:1–7

The defeated king of Bezek is brought to Jerusalem and dies there (7). The subject of *They brought
him* is not named. If it is Judah, Judah brings a captive to a city it takes only in the next verse
(8); if it is his own men, Jerusalem was his home or refuge. The text does not decide. The link with
Jerusalem invites comparison with [[Adoni-Zedek]], *king of Jerusalem* in Joshua 10:1; see
[[Adoni-Bezek]] for why the wiki keeps the two apart. Judah's capture and burning of the city in 1:8
stands against Joshua 15:63, *the children of Judah couldn't drive them out*; that tension belongs to
the next pericope.

See [[Judah Goes Up First]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 1:7–8; comparisons as cited.

### Benjamin’s inheritance — Joshua 18:11–28

The south border runs along the Jebusite’s south side (16); the city list explicitly identifies the Jebusite as Jerusalem (28). Its assignment to Benjamin stands beside Judah’s inability to expel its inhabitants in Joshua 15:63; allocation is not a report of occupation.

See [[The Inheritance of Benjamin]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 18:11–28; comparisons as cited.

### Judah’s cities — Joshua 15:20–63

The list ends with Jerusalem's exception: *As for the Jebusites, the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the children of Judah couldn’t drive them out; but the Jebusites live with the children of Judah at Jerusalem to this day* (63). Jerusalem is not itself listed among Judah's towns; Benjamin's list includes *the Jebusite (also called Jerusalem)* (18:28). The tension with the king of Jerusalem's death (10:26) is a difference between ruler and city, discussed in [[Kings Struck and Cities Not Driven Out - Joshua 12, 15-17, and Judges 1]].

See [[The Cities of Judah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:20–63.

### Judah’s borders — Joshua 15:1–12

Verse 8 identifies the Jebusite location with Jerusalem and traces the line on its south side. The border description does not report the city captured.

See [[The Borders of Judah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:1–12.

### The five kings at Makkedah — Joshua 10:16–27

Its king, [[Adoni-Zedek]], is brought from the cave and killed at Makkedah (23–27). The city is named to identify its ruler; the king’s death is not itself a report that the city has been captured.

See [[The Five Kings in the Cave at Makkedah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:16–27.


### At Gibeon — Joshua 10:1–15

Adoni-Zedek rules here and organizes the five-king attack on Gibeon (1–5). The unit reports his
army’s campaign, not the capture of Jerusalem.

See [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:1–15.


The city named in the heading of [[Ecclesiastes]] as the location of the Preacher's kingship.
In [[Vanity of Vanities]], the city gives the speaker a royal setting; no journey, building, temple
ceremony, or event in the city's political history is narrated. In [[The Burden of Wisdom]] the
first-person Preacher is king over Israel in Jerusalem (1:12) and measures his wisdom against all
who were before him there (1:16), which implies predecessors in the city without naming them. In [[The Trial of Pleasure]] the city is
again the measure: herds and flocks above all who were before him in Jerusalem (2:7), and increase
more than all of them (2:9).

[[Salem]] retains its page for the Genesis 14 name and its identification history. The new
page records the explicit Jerusalem name in Ecclesiastes 1:1. Ecclesiastes does not itself
identify Salem with Jerusalem, so the two textual occurrences are not collapsed.

In [[Dark and Lovely]] the city's name first appears in the Song of Solomon, in the address
*"you daughters of Jerusalem"* (Song of Solomon 1:5). Its women are the Song's audience
([[The Daughters of Jerusalem]]). The unit does not set any scene in the city itself.
The address recurs in the refrain of 2:7, and again word for word in [[Seeking Him by Night]] (3:5).
That unit's search runs through *"the city"* (3:2–3), which is not named; the wiki does not identify
it with Jerusalem on the strength of the address.
In [[Solomon's Wedding Procession]] the daughters of Jerusalem are named in 3:10, and then the
*"daughters of Zion"* are summoned to see the king (3:11). [[Zion]] there stands for Jerusalem, as in
later poetry. The procession comes up from the wilderness, but the unit does not say it arrives in
Jerusalem, and the wiki does not supply it.
In [[I Opened, but He Had Gone]] the daughters of Jerusalem are adjured again (5:8). The city of that
night search, with its watchmen and *"keepers of the walls"* (5:7), is again unnamed, and the wiki
again does not identify it with Jerusalem.

Source: stored WEB Ecclesiastes 1:1–2:11, Song of Solomon 1:5; 2:7; 3:1–11; 5:7–8, and Genesis 14:18; the earlier identification discussion
and its sources remain on [[Salem]].

In [[Altogether Lovely]] the woman again names the daughters of Jerusalem as her audience
(5:16), concluding her praise of the beloved. The questioners' offer to help seek him
(6:1) does not locate the garden or the exchange in Jerusalem. Source: stored WEB Song
of Solomon 5:9–6:3.

In [[My Dove Is Unique]] Jerusalem itself becomes a measure of the woman's loveliness, alongside
[[Tirzah (city)]] (6:4). This is a direct comparison with the city rather than its name in
an audience's title. It still does not locate the lovers or identify the unnamed city
of the night searches. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 6:4–10.

In [[Come into the Fields, My Beloved]], Jerusalem is again named only in the address to its daughters
(8:4). The proposed fields, villages, vineyards, and mother’s house are not geographically
identified. Source: stored WEB Song 7:10–8:4.

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** *The king of Jerusalem, one* (12:10) — Adoni-Zedek, killed at Makkedah. Yet Judah *couldn't drive out* the Jebusites (15:63), and Judges 1:21 (WEB checked) says the same of Benjamin. See [[Kings Struck and Cities Not Driven Out - Joshua 12, 15-17, and Judges 1]] and [[The Kings Struck Down]].

## Events here

- The heading locates [[The Preacher (Ecclesiastes)]]'s kingship here (Ecclesiastes 1:1).
  The opening poem supplies observations about all human life, not a scene set in a named street.
- The Preacher reigns here over Israel and surpasses in wisdom all who were before him in the city
  (Ecclesiastes 1:12, 16).
- The Preacher increases in possessions and greatness above all before him in the city
  (Ecclesiastes 2:7, 9).
- Its king counted (12:10).
- Adoni-Bezek, maimed by Judah, is brought here and dies (Judges 1:7).
