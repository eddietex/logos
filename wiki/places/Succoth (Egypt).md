# Succoth (Egypt)

## Overview

**The first camp of the exodus — the place the march from Rameses reaches on the first stage.**

> **37** The children of Israel traveled from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot who
> were men, besides children. (Exodus 12:37)

***Sukkōt*** is the plural of ***sukkâ***, *booth* or *temporary shelter*. **The name is the same as
Jacob's Succoth east of the Jordan** (Genesis 33:17), and the two are not the same place: that one is in
the Jordan valley, this one is in Egypt, on the route out of the eastern delta. See
[[Succoth (Transjordan)]].

**Where it is.** The text gives only its place in the itinerary: after Rameses, and before Etham, *"in the edge of
the wilderness"* (Exodus 13:20, not yet ingested). Numbers 33:5 repeats the stage: *"The
children of Israel traveled from Rameses, and encamped in Succoth"* (WEB). **It is commonly identified
with a site in the Wadi Tumilat, the valley that runs east from the delta toward the Bitter Lakes** — the
same region in which [[Pithom]] is usually sought — and with the Egyptian place-name *Tjeku*; the
identification is a scholarly reconstruction, not something the Bible states, and is not checked in this
pass.

**The name and the festival.** *Sukkōt* is also the name of the autumn festival — the WEB calls it
*"the feast of tents"* (Leviticus 23:34; *ḥag hassukkôt*, WLC) — and Leviticus 23:42–43 grounds that feast in the exodus:
*"You shall dwell in temporary shelters for seven days … that your generations may know that I made the
children of Israel to dwell in temporary shelters, when I brought them out of the land of Egypt"* (WEB).
**Whether the camp's name and the festival's shelters are connected is not stated in either book**; the
wiki records the shared word only. The Transjordan page's *"Feast of Booths"* is the same festival under
its traditional English name.
