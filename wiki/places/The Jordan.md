# The Jordan

## Overview

**The river that becomes the Bible's boundary between being outside the land and being in it — and
in Genesis it is never crossed.**

**Named four times in the book, in three places, and each time as a reference point for something
else**: a plain [[Lot]] chooses (13:10–11), a river [[Jacob]] says he once crossed with nothing
(32:10), and a bank a funeral stops on (50:10–11). **Nobody in Genesis fords it in the narrative
present.** **The crossing that matters happens in Joshua 3, and Genesis's last chapter puts a
cortege on the far side of it without explaining how it got there.**

**The name's derivation is disputed and probably from *yārad*, *to go down*** — which would make it
*the descender*, an accurate description: it falls from the slopes of Hermon to the [[The Salt Sea]]
at roughly four hundred metres below sea level, **the lowest river on earth.** **Genesis never
glosses it**, unlike almost every other name it cares about. See [[Names and Naming]].

## What it does in Genesis

| verses | what it is | passage |
|---|---|---|
| **13:10–11** | ***the plain of the Jordan*** — *"well-watered everywhere, like the garden of Yahweh, like the land of Egypt"* — and [[Lot]] picks it | [[Abram and Lot Separate]] |
| **32:10** | ***"with just my staff I crossed over this Jordan"*** — [[Jacob]]'s measure of what he left with and what he is returning with | [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]] |
| **50:10–11** | ***beyond the Jordan*** — twice, locating the threshing floor of Atad | [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] |

**In the first it is a landscape and a temptation.** **[[Lot]] lifts up his eyes and sees water**,
and the narrator undercuts the view in the same breath — *"before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and
Gomorrah"* — **and compares it to two places the book will spend the rest of its length getting
people out of: Eden, and Egypt.** See [[Lift Up Your Eyes - Two Ways of Seeing the Land]].

**In the second it is a ledger.** ***"With just my staff I crossed over this Jordan; and now I have
become two companies."*** **Jacob dates his own life from a river crossing**, and it is the one
sentence of his prayer at the Jabbok that counts what he has rather than what he fears. **The river
he names is not the one he is standing at** — [[The Jabbok]] is the tributary he is camped on —
**and Genesis lets him reach past it for the larger boundary.**

**In the third it is a problem.** **The threshing floor of Atad is placed *"beyond the Jordan"*
twice** (50:10, 11), **and the road from [[Egypt]] to [[Machpelah]] crosses the Jordan at no
point.** **The wiki reports the difficulty and the three available readings at [[Abel Mizraim]]**
rather than choosing one.

## Afterwards

**Everything the river means in the rest of the Bible is absent from Genesis and worth naming,
because Genesis's silence is the point.** **Israel crosses it dry-shod with the ark in front**
(Joshua 3:14–17); **twelve stones out of its bed become the memorial of entry** (Joshua 4:1–9);
**Elijah is taken up from beyond it and Elisha comes back across it** (2 Kings 2:8–14); **Naaman
washes in it seven times** (2 Kings 5:14); **John baptises in it** (Matthew 3:5–6), **and Jesus is
baptised in it** (Mark 1:9). **The river of arrival in every later book is, in Genesis, the river
the family looks at and does not cross.** See [[Land and Sojourning]].
