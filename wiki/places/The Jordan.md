# The Jordan

## Overview

### Reading to all Israel — Deuteronomy 31:9–13

The river remains the boundary Israel will cross to possess the land (13). The scheduled reading concerns life after entry; this passage narrates no crossing.

See [[Read the Law to All Israel]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:9–13.

### Courage and succession — Deuteronomy 31:1–8

Moses may not cross this river (2), while Joshua will lead Israel onward (3). The passage announces the transition; it does not narrate the crossing.

See [[Be Strong as Joshua Takes the Lead]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 31:1–8; Deuteronomy 31:16–18 and 34:7 where compared.

### Life on the far side — Deuteronomy 30:11–20

Not prolonging days *in the land where you pass over the Jordan* is the penalty of apostasy (18), echoing 4:26. The verb *ʿābar* (WLC) is the one verse 13 uses for going *over the sea*; the crossing is still ahead.

See [[The Word Is Near and the Choice Is Life]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 30:11–20.

### The responsive curses — Deuteronomy 27:11–26

The tribal ceremony is commanded for after Israel crosses the river (12). The crossing itself is not narrated in this unit.

See [[The People Answer Amen to the Curses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:11–26.


### Stones and an altar — Deuteronomy 27:1–10

The crossing is the temporal threshold for setting up the stones and altar (2–4).
Israel is still being instructed about what to do after crossing; no crossing is narrated.

See [[Stones and an Altar on Mount Ebal]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 27:1–10.

### The crossing that starts the rule — Deuteronomy 12:1–28

Once Israel goes over the Jordan and has rest from its enemies, all offerings go to the one chosen place (10–11). The crossing marks when the rule of the place takes full effect.

See [[The Place Yahweh Will Choose]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 12:1–28.

### Beyond the Jordan, westward — Deuteronomy 11:18–32

Gerizim and Ebal are *"beyond the Jordan"* (30), meaning the west bank, while Deuteronomy 1:1 used the same phrase for Moses' place on the east. Israel is *"to pass over the Jordan"* to possess the land (31).

See [[Set These Words Before You]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 11:18–32.

### Possession without merit — Deuteronomy 9:1–6

The river is the boundary Israel is about to cross (1). The speech announces entry; these six verses do not narrate a completed crossing.

See [[Not Because of Your Righteousness]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 9:1–6.


### Setting of the instruction — Deuteronomy 4:44–49

The repeated bearings beyond the river, toward sunrise, and eastward locate the instruction and conquered territory on the eastern side (46–49). No crossing is narrated.

See [[The Setting of the Covenant Instruction]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:44–49.

### Eastern refuge cities — Deuteronomy 4:41–43

The three designated refuge cities lie east of the river, explicitly toward sunrise
(41). This notice does not narrate a crossing or designate the western refuges.

See [[Three Cities Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:41–43.

### No form and no image — Deuteronomy 4:15–31

Yahweh swore that Moses would not go over the Jordan; he must die on the east side while Israel goes over (21–22). The land *which you go over the Jordan to possess* is also the land an idolatrous Israel will perish from (26).

See [[No Form Seen and No Image Made]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:15–31.

### Moses’ plea — Deuteronomy 3:23–29

The river marks the crossing Moses requests and is refused (25–27). Joshua will lead the people
over (28); no crossing happens within this unit.

See [[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:23–29.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

The river marks the eastern holdings and the crossing required of their warriors (17–20). In verse
20 the land beyond the Jordan is the western destination, showing that the expression depends on
viewpoint.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### Og’s defeat recalled — Deuteronomy 3:1–11

The two defeated kings are located beyond this river (8). This account concerns the eastern
conquests and reports no crossing of the Jordan.

See [[The Defeat of Og Recounted]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:1–11.

### Sihon’s defeat recalled — Deuteronomy 2:24–37

Moses asks for transit until Israel crosses this river into the land Yahweh gives (29). The crossing
remains ahead; the victory over Sihon occurs before it.

See [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:24–37.

### Moses’ address in Moab — Deuteronomy 1:1–5

The address is beyond the Jordan, in Moab (1, 5), hence east of the river in this setting. The river
has not yet been crossed.

See [[The Words of Moses Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:1–5.

### Marriage and tribal inheritance — Numbers 36:1–13

The river locates the plains where Moses conveys the commandments (13). No crossing is reported in
this final chapter.

See [[The Marriage of the Daughters of Zelophehad]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 36; comparisons as cited.

### Levitical cities and refuge — Numbers 35:1–34

The river marks the anticipated crossing (10) and the division of the six refuges: three beyond it and three in Canaan (14). Protection extends to both sides of Israel’s settlement.

See [[The Levitical Cities and the Cities of Refuge]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 35; comparisons as cited.

### The borders of the land — Numbers 34:1–29

The eastern line descends along the Jordan to the Salt Sea (12). Verses 14–15 simultaneously recognize Israelite inheritance east of it, distinguishing Canaan’s boundary from all Israel’s holdings.

See [[The Borders of the Land]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 34.

### Drive out the inhabitants — Numbers 33:50–56

The river marks the crossing still ahead (50–51). Directions for life beyond it are given
before Israel crosses; no crossing occurs within this unit.

See [[Drive Out the Inhabitants]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 33:50–56.

### The stages of the journey — Numbers 33:1–49

Israel camps beside the river at Jericho, from Beth Jeshimoth to Abel Shittim (48–49); no crossing is narrated.

See [[The Stages of the Journey]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 33:1–49.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

*Don't bring us over the Jordan* (5). The two tribes ask to stop on the east bank, and the condition is
that every armed man *will pass over the Jordan before Yahweh* (21). Verse 19 uses *ʿēber hayyardēn*,
*across the Jordan*, for both banks in one sentence, the direction set by *eastward* (Miqra according to
the Masorah). From here on the river is a border within Israel as well as the border of Canaan.

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

The river locates the census camp by Jericho (3, 63); no crossing is narrated.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


### Balak’s embassies — Numbers 22:1–21

The river locates Israel’s camp beyond it at Jericho (1). No crossing is narrated; Jericho is a
reference point across the river.

See [[Balak Sends for Balaam]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:1–21.

**The river that becomes the Bible's boundary between being outside the land and being in it — and
in Genesis it is never crossed.**

**Named four times in the book, in three places, and each time as a reference point for something
else**: a plain [[Lot]] chooses (13:10–11), a river [[Jacob]] says he once crossed with nothing
(32:10), and a bank a funeral stops on (50:10–11). **Nobody in Genesis fords it in the narrative
present.** **The crossing that matters happens in Joshua 3, and Genesis's last chapter puts a
cortege on the far side of it without explaining how it got there.**

**The name's derivation is disputed and probably from *yārad*, *to go down*** — which would make it
*the descender*, an accurate description: it falls from the slopes of Hermon to the [[The Salt Sea]]
at roughly four hundred metres below sea level, **the lowest river on earth.** **Genesis never
glosses it**, unlike almost every other name it cares about. See [[Names and Naming]].

## What it does in Genesis

| verses | what it is | passage |
|---|---|---|
| **13:10–11** | ***the plain of the Jordan*** — *"well-watered everywhere, like the garden of Yahweh, like the land of Egypt"* — and [[Lot]] picks it | [[Abram and Lot Separate]] |
| **32:10** | ***"with just my staff I crossed over this Jordan"*** — [[Jacob]]'s measure of what he left with and what he is returning with | [[Wrestling at the Jabbok]] |
| **50:10–11** | ***beyond the Jordan*** — twice, locating the threshing floor of Atad | [[The Deaths of Jacob and Joseph]] |

**In the first it is a landscape and a temptation.** **[[Lot]] lifts up his eyes and sees water**,
and the narrator undercuts the view in the same breath — *"before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and
Gomorrah"* — **and compares it to two places the book will spend the rest of its length getting
people out of: Eden, and Egypt.** See [[Lift Up Your Eyes - Two Ways of Seeing the Land]].

**In the second it is a ledger.** ***"With just my staff I crossed over this Jordan; and now I have
become two companies."*** **Jacob dates his own life from a river crossing**, and it is the one
sentence of his prayer at the Jabbok that counts what he has rather than what he fears. **The river
he names is not the one he is standing at** — [[The Jabbok]] is the tributary he is camped on —
**and Genesis lets him reach past it for the larger boundary.**

**In the third it is a problem.** **The threshing floor of Atad is placed *"beyond the Jordan"*
twice** (50:10, 11), **and the road from [[Egypt]] to [[Machpelah]] crosses the Jordan at no
point.** **The wiki reports the difficulty and the three available readings at [[Abel Mizraim]]**
rather than choosing one.

## Afterwards

**Everything the river means in the rest of the Bible is absent from Genesis and worth naming,
because Genesis's silence is the point.** **Israel crosses it dry-shod with the ark in front**
(Joshua 3:14–17); **twelve stones out of its bed become the memorial of entry** (Joshua 4:1–9);
**Elijah is taken up from beyond it and Elisha comes back across it** (2 Kings 2:8–14); **Naaman
washes in it seven times** (2 Kings 5:14); **John baptises in it** (Matthew 3:5–6), **and Jesus is
baptised in it** (Mark 1:9). **The river of arrival in every later book is, in Genesis, the river
the family looks at and does not cross.** See [[Land and Sojourning]].
