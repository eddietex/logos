# Nineveh

## Overview

Assyria's great city, on the east bank of the Tigris opposite modern Mosul. It enters Scripture as
something someone built.

> **11** Out of that land he went into Assyria, and built Nineveh, Rehoboth Ir, Calah,
> **12** and Resen between Nineveh and the great city Calah.

Four Assyrian foundations in two verses, in the one narrative interruption in
[[The Table of Nations]]. On the WEB's reading of 10:11 the builder is [[Nimrod]], expanding north out
of [[Shinar]]; on the alternative reading it is Asshur, and the verse is a migration note. See
[[Assyria]].

Of the four, two are identifiable. **Nineveh** is the city Sennacherib made his capital and whose
fall in 612 BC ended the Assyrian empire. **Calah** is Nimrud — the modern Arabic name preserves
Nimrod's — Ashurnasirpal II's capital, twenty miles downriver. **Rehoboth Ir** means *broad places of
a city* and may be a description of Nineveh's open squares rather than a separate settlement;
**Resen** has no agreed identification.

**"The great city."** Genesis 10:12 attaches the phrase to Calah, and Hebrew word order permits
attaching it to the four foundations taken together. It is worth noticing because *"that great city"*
is exactly what Jonah's commission calls Nineveh, four times (Jonah 1:2; 3:2, 3; 4:11), and because
Jonah 3:3 adds the strange gloss *"an exceedingly great city, three days' journey across"*, which is
larger than the archaeological Nineveh and may take in the whole administrative district that Genesis
10:11–12 happens to list.

**The two books about it disagree, and Scripture keeps both.** Jonah is sent to it and it repents, to
the prophet's disgust, and God spares *"more than one hundred twenty thousand persons who can't
discern between their right hand and their left hand, and also much livestock"* (Jonah 4:11). Nahum,
a century later, watches it fall and does not grieve (Nahum 3:7); Zephaniah 2:13–15 says the same.
The city that Genesis founds in a genealogy is the one place in the Bible that receives both a mission
of mercy and an oracle of total destruction, and the canon does not reconcile them.
