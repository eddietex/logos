# Gilead

## Overview

### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

Named in the overall eastern territory (11), Gad’s list (25), and the half-Gilead share of Machir’s descendants (31). The regional descriptions must be read with the division, not as exclusive grants of all Gilead to each group.

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### The death of Moses — Deuteronomy 34:1–12

The view from Nebo begins with *all the land of Gilead, to Dan* (1).

See [[The Death of Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 34:1–12.

### Eastern refuge cities — Deuteronomy 4:41–43

Ramoth in Gilead is set apart for the Gadites as a refuge for unwitting killing
without previous hatred (42–43). The region now supplies a named protective city.

See [[Three Cities Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:41–43.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

Half the hill country goes to Reuben and Gad, the rest to half of Manasseh (12–13); the grant to
Machir is stated separately (15). This explains how Numbers 32 can speak of Gilead in connection
with both groups without requiring identical holdings.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### Og’s defeat recalled — Deuteronomy 3:1–11

All Gilead appears in the summary of territory taken from the two kings (8–10). The division among
Israel’s tribes belongs to the following unit, not this conquest summary.

See [[The Defeat of Og Recounted]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:1–11.

### Sihon’s defeat recalled — Deuteronomy 2:24–37

The conquest summary extends from Aroer and the Arnon valley to Gilead (36). It reports no city too
high for Israel, attributing success to Yahweh; it is not yet the tribal allotment account.

See [[The Defeat of Sihon Recounted]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:24–37.

### Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan — Numbers 32:1–42

*The land of Gilead … was a place for livestock* (1). Reuben and Gad ask for it and receive it on condition
that their armed men cross the Jordan (29). Their families stay *there in the cities of Gilead* (26). The
children of Machir take Gilead from the Amorites and Moses gives it to Machir (39–40), and Jair takes
villages in it (41). The chapter gives Gilead both to the two tribes and to Machir; Deuteronomy 3:12–16
divides it, half to Reuben and Gad and *the rest of Gilead* to Manasseh (stored WEB, now ingested).

See [[Reuben, Gad, and the Land Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 32.

**The highland east of the Jordan**, running roughly from the Yarmuk down to the north end of the
Dead Sea and split in two by the Jabbok — **the country Israel holds outside the land it was
promised**, and the first Israelite territory taken in the conquest narratives.

**It enters Scripture as a direction.** *"He rose up, passed over the River, and set his face toward
the mountain of Gilead"* (31:21) — a fugitive aiming at a range of hills, with no city named, no
inhabitants mentioned, and nobody living there in the chapter. **Everything that happens on it in
Genesis happens to two camped households and a heap of stones.**

**Its name is given an etiology in a pun and no more.** Laban and Jacob pile up rocks and call them
***galʿēd***, *heap of witness* (31:47–48); ***gilʿād*** is the same three consonants with a
different vowel. **Genesis supplies the wordplay and does not claim the region was named from the
heap** — it has already used *Gilead* twice, at 31:21 and 31:23, before the heap exists. See
[[Galeed]] and [[Names and Naming]].

### What it becomes

Gilead is the Bible's standing image of **good pasture and marginal loyalty**. It is *"a place for
livestock"* (Numbers 32:1), which is why Reuben and Gad ask for it and are granted it **on the
condition that they cross over and fight for the land they are not settling** — the arrangement that
produces the altar of witness at Joshua 22, **a second heap east of the Jordan raised to prove that
its builders belong to the people on the other side.**

It is the country of **Jephthah**, whose own kin drive him out of it and then call him back to lead
them (Judges 11:1–11), and where the *shibboleth* test is administered at the fords (Judges 12:5–6) —
**a dialect boundary within Israel, three hundred miles and several centuries from the language
boundary Genesis 31 records on the same hills.**

It is **Elijah's country** — *"Elijah the Tishbite, of the inhabitants of Gilead"* (1 Kings 17:1) —
and it is where David goes when Absalom takes Jerusalem (2 Samuel 17:22–24). **Jeremiah asks *"is
there no balm in Gilead?"*** (Jeremiah 8:22), from the resin the caravans in Genesis 37:25 are
already carrying down to Egypt; **Amos opens his oracles against Damascus with a charge about
Gilead** (Amos 1:3); and **it is the first Israelite territory Assyria annexes** (2 Kings 15:29).

In [[My Dove Is Unique]] the goats along Gilead's side again picture the woman's hair (Song 6:5),
resuming the comparison of 4:1. The region supplies a pastoral image, not a location for
the lovers' exchange. Source: stored WEB Song of Solomon 4:1; 6:4–10.

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** Divided between the two kings: Sihon ruled *half Gilead* to the Jabbok (12:2), and Og ruled *half Gilead* to Sihon's border (5). See [[The Kings Struck Down]].

  and balm and myrrh, going to carry it down to Egypt"* — ***nəkōʾt ûṣərî wālōṭ***, and ***ṣŏrî*** **is the
  balm of Jeremiah's question**, *"Is there no balm in Gilead?"* (8:22). **The same three commodities are
  what Jacob sends back down this road as a gift when he finally has to deal with Egypt** (43:11). The
  region contributes the freight and the traders and appears in one verse
- [[You Are All Beautiful]] — Song of Solomon 4:1; ***har gilʿāḏ***, *"Mount Gilead"* (Sefaria), now a
  slope in a love poem. The woman's hair is *"a flock of goats, that descend from Mount Gilead"* — the
  pasture country of Numbers 32:1 turned into a picture of dark hair moving in waves. The line is
  repeated at 6:5, *"that lie along the side of Gilead"* (now ingested in [[My Dove Is Unique]]).
- [[My Dove Is Unique]] — Song of Solomon 6:5; the goats in the hair comparison return.

- The search of the tents, and Jacob's lawsuit — Genesis 31:33–42
- The heap, the pillar, the boundary oath, and the sacrifice and meal — Genesis 31:43–54

  37:25
