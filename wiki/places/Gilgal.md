# Gilgal

## Overview

### The stone idols by Gilgal — Judges 3:19, 26

Ehud *turned back from the stone idols that were by Gilgal* to bring Eglon his *secret message* (19),
and after the killing he *passed beyond the stone idols* to safety (26). *Happəsîlîm* (WLC) is
elsewhere the word for the nations' *engraved images* (Deuteronomy 7:5, 25; 12:3). The WEB has *stone
idols*. Some identify them with the twelve stones Joshua set up at Gilgal (Joshua 4:20), and some take
the word as *quarries*; both are reported, not checked. In the story they mark a line: the edge of
Eglon's reach, or of his watch.

See [[Ehud and Eglon]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 3:12–30; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb),
2026-11-13.

### The angel comes up from Gilgal — Judges 2:1

*Yahweh's angel came up from Gilgal to Bochim* (2:1). Gilgal had been Israel's camp through Joshua
4–14. The angel's route leads from the place where the conquest began to the place where Israel hears
it will stay unfinished. The text gives no reason for the route.

See [[The Angel at Bochim]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 2:1–5.

### Judah’s borders — Joshua 15:1–12

The border looks toward Gilgal opposite the ascent of Adummim (7). The shared name alone does not establish that this landmark is Joshua’s camp; this occurrence is recorded here with that identification left open.

See [[The Borders of Judah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:1–12.

### Caleb’s inheritance — Joshua 14:6–15

The children of Judah approach Joshua here, and Caleb presents his claim (6). This is the meeting’s location, distinct from Hebron, the inheritance requested and granted.

See [[Caleb Claims Hebron]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 14:6–15; comparisons as cited.


### The southern campaign — Joshua 10:28–43

Joshua and all Israel return to the camp here after the southern campaign (43). This repeats 10:15’s return notice; reading that earlier notice as anticipatory remains an interpretation.

See [[The Southern Cities Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:28–43; comparisons as cited.

### At Gibeon — Joshua 10:1–15

Joshua receives the plea at the camp and marches from it all night (6–9). Verse 15 reports a return
here, while 10:16–27 resumes action at Makkedah and 10:43 repeats the return. A summary anticipating
the campaign’s end is a possible reading of verse 15, not an explicit explanation supplied by the
text.

See [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:1–15.


### The Gibeonite deception — Joshua 9:1–27

Joshua receives the disguised embassy at the camp at Gilgal (6). This is the setting of
negotiations, before Israel travels to discover the four neighboring cities (17).

See [[The Gibeonite Deception]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 9:1–27; comparisons as cited.


### Passover and the end of manna — Joshua 5:10–12

The camp hosts Passover on the fourteenth day at evening (10). After circumcision and
healing, Gilgal becomes the setting for the feast and the transition from manna to land produce.

See [[Passover at Gilgal and the End of the Manna]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 5:10–12; comparisons as cited.

### Circumcision at Gilgal — Joshua 5:1–9

The camp becomes the site of circumcision and recovery (2–9). Verse 3 calls the location the hill of the foreskins; it is treated here as a descriptive site within this episode, without assigning modern coordinates. Verse 9 connects the name Gilgal with Yahweh’s removal of Egypt’s reproach. The precise reproach remains unexplained.

See [[The Second Circumcision at Gilgal]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 5:1–9; comparisons as cited.

### The twelve stones — Joshua 4:1–24

Israel camps here on Jericho’s east border on the tenth day of the first month (19). Joshua erects
the stones carried from the Jordan (20). This does not resolve the identity of Gilgal in Deuteronomy
11:30.
See [[The Twelve Stones from the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 4:1–24.

First named in Deuteronomy 11:30, where Moses locates [[Mount Gerizim]] and [[Mount Ebal]] *"in the
land of the Canaanites who dwell in the Arabah, near Gilgal, beside the oaks of Moreh"*.

**Which Gilgal is unresolved.** Joshua 4:19 has Israel camp after crossing the Jordan *"in Gilgal,
on the east border of Jericho"*, and Joshua 5:9 explains the name: *"Today I have rolled away the
reproach of Egypt from you"* (both WEB, verified from bible-api.com; Joshua 4 and 5:1–9 now ingested). That
Gilgal lies in the Jordan valley near [[Jericho]], a long way south of the two mountains by
[[Shechem (city)]]. Deuteronomy 11:30 may be orienting the reader loosely by the valley and its road,
or it may mean another place of the same name nearer Shechem. The text does not decide, and neither
does the wiki.

This page holds every Gilgal for now. If later books make a second Gilgal clear, it will be split and
both pages qualified.
