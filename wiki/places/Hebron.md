# Hebron

## Overview

### The whole land taken — Joshua 11:16–23

Joshua cuts off the Anakim from Hebron (21). Joshua 15:13–14 later has Caleb drive out the three sons of Anak there; the two notices are kept together. See [[Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1]].

See [[The Whole Land Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:16–23.

### The southern campaign — Joshua 10:28–43

Joshua captures Hebron, its dependent cities, and its king (36–37). This creates a narrative tension with Hoham’s death at Makkedah (10:23–26). A successor is possible but not stated. Joshua 15:13–17 later reports Caleb’s action at Hebron and Debir; the relation between these accounts is not explained.

See [[The Southern Cities Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:28–43; comparisons as cited.

### The five kings at Makkedah — Joshua 10:16–27

Its king, [[Hoham]], is brought from the cave and killed at Makkedah (23–27). The city is named to identify its ruler; the king’s death is not itself a report that the city has been captured.

See [[The Five Kings in the Cave at Makkedah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:16–27.


### At Gibeon — Joshua 10:1–15

Its king Hoham joins the coalition against Gibeon (3–5). The city itself is not captured in this
unit; its attack is narrated later at Joshua 10:36–37.

See [[The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:1–15.


The hill town Abram moves to at the end of Genesis 13 and never really leaves — the patriarchs'
address, their graveyard, and later David's first capital.

**He arrives by tent.** *"Abram moved his tent, and came and lived by the oaks of Mamre, which are in
Hebron, and built an altar there to Yahweh"* (13:18). Third altar, after [[Shechem (city)]] (12:7)
and the ridge between [[Bethel]] and [[Ai]] (12:8), and the one he settles at. Everything the rest
of the Abraham cycle does in a fixed location happens here: the three visitors and the promise of a
son (18:1), the intercession for Sodom (18:16–33), and the purchase of the cave of Machpelah
*"before Mamre"* (23:17–20). See [[Abram and Lot Separate]].

**Where it is.** About nineteen miles south-southwest of Jerusalem, high in the Judean hill country
at roughly 3,000 feet — one of the highest towns in the land, on the ridge road, and dry. Genesis
puts Abram there in the same chapter in which his nephew takes the irrigated valley floor, and does
not comment.

**Sarah dies here, and it is where the family becomes landowners.** *"Sarah died in Kiriath Arba
(also called Hebron), in the land of Canaan"* (23:2) — while Abraham was twenty-five miles south at
[[Beersheba]], so that the verse has him *come* to mourn her. The eighteen verses that follow are a
property conveyance: he calls himself *"a stranger and a foreigner"*, the children of Heth call him
*"a prince of God"* and offer him their tombs for nothing, and he refuses and pays four hundred
shekels for the field of [[Ephron the Hittite]] instead. **The town where Abram pitched a tent in
chapter 13 is the town where he buys ground in chapter 23**, and it is the only ground he ever buys.
See [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]] and [[Machpelah]].

**Its older name is Kiriath Arba**, *the city of Arba* — *"Arba was the greatest man among the
Anakim"* (Joshua 14:15; and Genesis 23:2, 35:27). The spies sent out from Kadesh reach it and find
*"Ahiman, Sheshai, and Talmai, the children of Anak"* there (Numbers 13:22, which also dates its
founding *"seven years before Zoan in Egypt"*), and it is Caleb who eventually takes it and is given
it (Joshua 14:13–14; 15:13–14). The verse's verb *came* is singular (WLC), which has been read of Caleb alone. See
[[Caleb and Hebron - Numbers 13, Joshua 14-15, and Judges 1]]. Its modern Arabic name, *el-Khalil*, **the friend**, is for Abraham —
*"Abraham my friend"* (Isaiah 41:8; 2 Chronicles 20:7; James 2:23).

**It is the patriarchal grave.** The cave of Machpelah is the only ground in the promised land
Abraham ever owns, bought from Ephron the Hittite for four hundred shekels of silver so that he could
bury Sarah (23:1–20). Genesis records six burials in it: Sarah (23:19), Abraham (25:9–10), Isaac and
Rebekah, Leah, and Jacob, carried up from Egypt to be laid there (49:29–32; 50:13).

**And it is David's first capital.** He is anointed king over Judah at Hebron and reigns there seven
and a half years before taking Jerusalem (2 Samuel 2:1–4; 5:1–5); Abner is killed there (3:27), and
Absalom launches his revolt from it (15:7–10). It is also named as one of the cities of refuge
(Joshua 20:7) and a Levitical city (21:11–13).

  [[Isaac]]** (35:27–29), where the journey to [[Dothan]] begins — sixty-five miles north — and where the
  bloodied coat is brought back to him. **The chapter's grief happens here and its violence happens at the
  other end of the road**
