# The Negev

## Overview

### The land of the South — Judges 1:9, 15–16

Judah goes down against the Canaanites *in the South* (9). Achsah's request names its dryness: *you
have set me in the land of the South, give me also springs of water* (15). The Kenites settle *in the
south of Arad* (16; WLC *bəneḡeḇ ʿărāḏ*, *in the Negev of Arad*).

See [[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 1:8–21; comparisons as cited.

### Judah’s cities — Joshua 15:20–63

The first district of Judah's cities is introduced as *the farthest cities ... toward the border of Edom in the South* (21). Thirty-six names follow, though verse 32 totals them as twenty-nine; several reappear in Simeon's list (19:1–9).

See [[The Cities of Judah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:20–63.

### Achsah’s springs — Joshua 15:13–19

Achsah describes her grant as land of the South and asks for springs; Caleb gives both upper and lower springs (19). Linked here under the existing South alias, without locating the field or identifying the springs at a modern site.

See [[Achsah Asks for Springs]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:13–19.

### The whole land taken — Joshua 11:16–23

*All the South* is among the regions Joshua takes in the summary (16). It is linked here on the alias *the South*; the verse gives no borders.

See [[The Whole Land Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:16–23.

### The death of Moses — Deuteronomy 34:1–12

The view includes *the south* (3); the Hebrew has *haNegev* (MAM via Sefaria).

See [[The Death of Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 34:1–12.

### The stages of the journey — Numbers 33:1–49

The South locates the king of Arad within Canaan (40).

See [[The Stages of the Journey]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 33:1–49.

### Arad and Hormah — Numbers 21:1–3

The king of Arad lives in the South and attacks Israel approaching by Atharim (1).
The southern border setting now holds victory as well as the defeat of Numbers 14.

See [[Arad and Hormah]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 21:1–3.

The dry country at the bottom of the land, and — because it is where the road to Egypt begins — the
place the patriarchs go when the land they were promised stops feeding them.

*"Abram traveled, still going on toward the South"* (Genesis 12:9). The WEB's **the South** renders
*hannegbâ*, toward the Negev. The Hebrew *negeb* means the parched or dry country and is used both
as a proper name for that region and as the ordinary compass word for *south*, because from Judah
the dry land is what lies southward. Translations split on which sense to take, verse by verse; the
WEB generally takes the compass sense and capitalises it.

**Where it is.** The wedge of semi-arid land below the Judean hills, running down from around
Beersheba toward the Wilderness of Zin and the head of the Gulf of Aqaba. Rainfall of a few inches a
year, enough for grazing and for wells and dry-farming in the north, not enough for anything
dependable. It is pastoral country, not agricultural country, and the difference is what drives the
famine narratives.

**Why it matters in Genesis.** It is the last stage of Abram's traverse of the land (12:9), the
direction he returns from out of Egypt (13:1), and where he settles *"between Kadesh and Shur"*
after Sodom (20:1). Beersheba, in its northern edge, is where Abraham plants a tamarisk and calls on
the everlasting God (21:33), where Hagar and Ishmael nearly die (21:14), where Isaac digs his wells
and receives his own version of the promise (26:23–25), and where Jacob offers sacrifices on his way
down to Egypt (46:1–5). Three generations of patriarchs live at the southern margin of the land they
have been promised, at the point where it runs out.

**And it is the corridor to Egypt.** The road south through the Negev and along the coast is how
Abram goes down in the famine of 12:10, how Jacob's sons go down for grain, and how Israel comes
back up. The pericope that ends *"still going on toward the South"* is followed immediately by
*"There was a famine in the land. Abram went down into Egypt"* — the promise is four verses old and
the promised land is already not enough to live on.

Later it is Judah's southern district (Joshua 15:21–32), David's raiding ground and refuge while he
is with the Philistines (1 Samuel 27:10; 30:1), and, in the prophets, the emblematic dry place that
water is promised to: *"Turn again our captivity, Yahweh, like the streams in the Negev"* (Psalm
126:4).

**Joshua 12:1–24 — the list of kings.** *The South* is one of the six western regions Joshua gives the tribes (12:8). See [[The Kings Struck Down]].
