# The Stone Ezel

## Overview

### David's hiding place in the field — 1 Samuel 20:19

When [[Jonathan (son of Saul)]] arranges the three-day test of [[Saul]]'s intentions at the new-moon feast, he names a
specific landmark in *the field* where [[David]] is to wait: *When you have stayed three days, go down quickly, and
come to the place where you hid yourself when this started, and remain by the stone Ezel* (1 Samuel 20:19). Jonathan
will shoot *three arrows on its side, as though I shot at a mark*, and send his boy after them; his words to the boy
will tell David whether there is peace or whether Yahweh has sent him away (20:20–22). See
[[Jonathan's Covenant and the Arrows]].

**Name and location.** In the stored WEB, *the stone Ezel* appears only in 1 Samuel 20:19. Its setting is *the field*
outside Saul's unnamed town (presumably [[Gibeah (Benjamin)]], 10:26; 15:34, though chapter 20 never names the town).
The phrase *where you hid yourself when this started* (20:19) links it to David's earlier hiding in *the field* when
Jonathan first spoke to Saul for him (19:2–3). No geographical site is identified. At 20:41, where the WEB has David
arise *out of the south*, the Septuagint is commonly reported to read a stone or mound corresponding to this landmark
(recalled, not checked; no Septuagint or Hebrew text was consulted).

Source: stored WEB 1 Samuel 10:26; 15:34; 19:2–3; 20:19–22, 35–42.
