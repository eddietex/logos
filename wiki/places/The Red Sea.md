# The Red Sea

## Overview

### The covenant at Shechem — Joshua 24:1–28

*The Egyptians pursued your fathers with chariots and with horsemen to the Red Sea. When they cried out to Yahweh, he put darkness between you and the Egyptians, and brought the sea on them* (6–7). The darkness matches the cloud of Exodus 14:20, where *there was the cloud and the darkness* between the camps (stored WEB).

See [[The Covenant at Shechem]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 24:1–28; comparisons as cited.

### The twelve stones — Joshua 4:1–24

Joshua explicitly compares Yahweh’s drying of the Jordan with his drying of the Red Sea (23).
Earlier deliverance explains the new entry and becomes part of the answer to future children.
See [[The Twelve Stones from the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 4:1–24.

### Rahab and the spies — Joshua 2:1–24

Rahab reports that Jericho has heard how Yahweh dried up the sea when Israel left Egypt (10). Israel’s remembered deliverance has become news that frightens another people.

See [[Rahab and the Spies]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 2:1–24.

### Remembered deeds and the rain-fed land — Deuteronomy 11:1–17

Moses recalls Yahweh making the Red Sea overflow Egypt's army, horses, and chariots as they pursued Israel (4). The sea is a memory used to argue for obedience.

See [[Remember His Deeds and Guard Your Hearts]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 11:1–17.

### Protected neighbors and the wilderness generation — Deuteronomy 2:1–23

The journey turns toward the wilderness by the Red Sea road before the long circuit of Seir (1).
This recalls a direction of travel, not another sea crossing.

See [[Around Seir and Past Moab and Ammon]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:1–23.

### The sentence and the ascent — Deuteronomy 1:34–46

The condemned people are told to turn toward the wilderness by the way to the Red Sea (40). This is
a route designation, not a new crossing of the sea.

See [[The Sentence and the Presumptuous Ascent]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 1:34–46; Numbers 14 and 20 for comparisons.

### The stages of the journey — Numbers 33:1–49

The itinerary places this stage after [[Elim]] and before [[The Wilderness of Sin]].

See [[The Stages of the Journey]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 33:1–49.

### The brass serpent — Numbers 21:4–9

The way to the Red Sea supplies the direction of travel from Mount Hor around Edom (4). The verse narrates no second sea crossing or exact route.

See [[The Brass Serpent]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 21:4–9.

**The sea Israel crosses out of Egypt — in Hebrew *yam sûp*, which most readers take as *the Sea of
Reeds*.** *Sûp* is the word for the *"reeds"* by the Nile's bank where Moses' basket was laid (Exodus
2:3, 5), so the name of the sea and the place of the infant's rescue share a noun. The WEB, following
the Greek and Latin tradition, translates *yam sûp* as *"the Red Sea"*; the page uses the WEB's name.
See [[The Birth of Moses]].

**It is first named at the locusts, not at the crossing.** *"Yahweh turned an exceeding strong west
wind, which took up the locusts, and drove them into the Red Sea"* — *yāmmâ sûp* (Exodus 10:19). In the
Torah *sûp* occurs at Exodus 2:3, 5; 10:19; 13:18; 15:4, 22; 23:31; Numbers 14:25; 21:4; 33:10–11;
Deuteronomy 1:40; 2:1; 11:4 (WLC lemma search). **The plague goes where Pharaoh's army will go**: the
locusts are thrown in and *"There remained not one"* (10:19); the chariots are covered and *"There
remained not so much as one of them"* (14:28). See [[Locusts and Darkness]].

**Which body of water is meant is disputed, and the wiki adopts no location.** The references in
Numbers 33:10–11 and Deuteronomy 1:40; 2:1, and 1 Kings 9:26 — Solomon's fleet at *"Ezion Geber, which is beside Eloth,
on the shore of the Red Sea, in the land of Edom"* (WEB) — have been taken to show that *yam sûp* can name the gulfs of the Red Sea proper; *sûp* as *reeds* has been taken
to point to marshy lakes nearer the Nile Delta. Some take *sûp* as *end*, a related word, rather than
*reeds*. **Those identifications are reported, not checked here, and none is adopted.**

**The crossing (Exodus 13:18–14:31).** God leads the people *"by the way of the wilderness by the Red Sea"*
(13:18), turns them back to camp *"by the sea"* (14:2), and divides it: *"Yahweh caused the sea to go back by
a strong east wind all night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided"* (14:21). **The waters
are *"a wall to them on their right hand, and on their left"*** (14:22, 29) — *ḥômâ*, the first *wall* in
Scripture (WLC lemma search, Torah) — and return *"to its strength"* over the army. **Exodus 14 itself calls
it only *hayyām*, *the sea*; the name *yam sûp* frames it at 13:18 and returns in the Song (15:4)** (WLC).
*Dry land*, *yabbāšâ*, is the word of the third day of creation (Genesis 1:9–10). See
[[The Crossing of the Sea]] and [[Dry Ground in the Midst of the Sea - Creation, Flood, and Exodus 14]].
**The Song names it once, and gives the sea's own depths new words.** *"His chosen captains are sunk in the
Red Sea"* (*bəyam-sûp*, 15:4); *"The deeps (təhōmōt) cover them. They went down into the depths (məṣôlōt)
like a stone"* (15:5); *"The deeps were congealed in the heart of the sea"* (15:8). ***Təhōmōt*** **is the
plural of the *təhôm* of Genesis 1:2**; *məṣôlōt* occurs only here in the Torah (WLC lemma search). See
[[The Song of the Sea]].

**At 23:31 the sea becomes a border.** *"I will set your border from the Red Sea (yam-sûp) even to
the sea of the Philistines"* (MAM). The sea Israel crossed in chapter 14 is now the first line of the
land promised beyond it. See [[The Angel Sent Ahead]].

## Events here

- The locusts thrown in by the west wind — Exodus 10:19
- The way of the wilderness by the sea — Exodus 13:18
- Israel camps by it, is overtaken, and crosses on dry ground; Pharaoh's army is covered — Exodus 14:2–31
- Sung by Moses and Israel, and by Miriam — Exodus 15:1–21
- Israel sets out from it into the wilderness of Shur — Exodus 15:22, [[Bitter Water at Marah]]
- Itinerary: Numbers 33:10 has Israel camp *"by the Red Sea"* again after Elim
- Named as the first limit of Israel's border — Exodus 23:31, [[The Angel Sent Ahead]]
- Israel is told to turn back toward it — Numbers 14:25, [[The People Refuse the Land]]
