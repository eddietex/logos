# The Tower of Eder

## Overview

***Migdal-ʿĒder***, **tower of the flock** — one clause in Genesis, and a prophet's vocative six
hundred years later.

> **21** Israel traveled, and spread his tent beyond the tower of Eder. (35:21)

**That is the whole of it.** Jacob has just buried [[Rachel]] on the road to Ephrath; he moves on,
pitches past a tower, and the next sentence is [[Reuben]] and [[Bilhah]]. **The verse exists to
locate a scandal**, and Genesis says nothing about the tower itself.

***Migdāl*** is a tower and ***ʿēder*** is a flock or herd, so the name is descriptive: **a
shepherds' watchtower**, the kind of stone lookout built over grazing ground to spot predators and
thieves. 2 Chronicles 26:10 has Uzziah *"built towers in the wilderness and dug out many cisterns, for
he had much livestock"* — the same structure, unnamed. **Jacob is a herdsman camping by a herdsman's
landmark**, and the clause is the sort of detail a stock-keeper's itinerary would carry.

**Where it is, is unknown.** The only fix Genesis gives is the sequence — Bethel, then Ephrath, then
here, then Hebron — which puts it somewhere on the ridge south of [[Bethlehem]]. **No later text
locates it**, and the traditional siting a mile or two outside Bethlehem rests on Jerome and on
later Jewish sources rather than on anything in Scripture.

### Micah 4:8, which addresses it as a person

> *"You, tower of the flock, the hill of the daughter of Zion, to you it will come, yes, the former
> dominion will come, the kingdom of the daughter of Jerusalem."* (Micah 4:8)

**Micah puts *migdal-ʿēder* in apposition with *the hill of the daughter of Zion*** and promises it
the return of *"the former dominion"* — the Davidic kingship. **Whether he means the Genesis landmark
or is using the phrase as an epithet for Jerusalem is genuinely unsettled**, and the two readings pull
in different directions:

- **An epithet.** The parallelism is the argument: *tower of the flock* and *hill of the daughter of
  Zion* name the same thing, and the image is of Jerusalem as the watchtower over the flock that is
  Israel. This is how most commentators read it, and it needs no geography at all.
- **The actual site.** Micah 5:2, four verses later, names **Bethlehem Ephrathah** as where the ruler
  comes from — the same stretch of road as Genesis 35:19–21. **On this reading the prophet walks the
  reader down Jacob's own itinerary**, from the tower to the town, and the *former dominion* is
  promised at the place David's line begins.

**The wiki records both and adopts neither.** What is checkable is that Micah 4:8 and Micah 5:2 name
two places that Genesis 35 names four verses apart, in the same order.

**A later tradition attaches the shepherds of Luke 2:8** — *"keeping watch by night over their
flock"* near Bethlehem — to this tower, sometimes with the further claim that the flocks pastured
there were temple flocks. **Neither Luke nor any earlier text says so**, and the wiki notes the
tradition without adopting it.
