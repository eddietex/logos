# Shechem

## Overview

The first place in the promised land the Bible stops at, and the place Israel keeps coming back to
whenever it has to decide what it is.

*"Abram passed through the land to the place of Shechem, to the oak of Moreh. At that time,
Canaanites were in the land"* (Genesis 12:6). It is the terminus of a four-hundred-mile journey and
the site of Abram's first altar, and Genesis introduces it in the same breath as a reminder that it
belongs to somebody else.

**Where it is.** In the pass between Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal, in the central highlands of what
is now the northern West Bank, at the junction of the north–south ridge route and the road running
east to the Jordan. Identified with Tell Balata beside modern Nablus — *Nablus* being Arabic for the
Roman *Neapolis*. It is one of the oldest continuously occupied sites in the region, named in
Egyptian execration texts of the nineteenth century BC and in the Amarna letters, where its ruler
Labaya is a persistent nuisance to Egypt.

**The oak of Moreh.** *ʾĒlôn môreh* — the oak, or terebinth, *of the teacher*: *môreh* comes from
*yārâ*, to instruct or to hand down an oracle, the root behind *tôrâ*. A single named sacred tree
outside a Canaanite town is a divination site, and Abram's first altar in the land goes up beside
one. Genesis registers no unease about that at all. The tree, or its successor, stays attached to
the place: Jacob buries the household's foreign gods *"under the oak which was by Shechem"* (35:4),
Joshua sets a witness-stone *"under the oak that was by the sanctuary of Yahweh"* there (24:26), and
Deuteronomy 11:30 fixes Gerizim and Ebal *"beside the oaks of Moreh"*.

### What happens here

Shechem's list is unusually violent for a place the Bible treats as sacred.

- **Abram builds his first altar** to the God who has just appeared to him (Genesis 12:6–7).
- **Jacob buys a field** from the sons of Hamor, pitches there, and erects an altar he names *El
  Elohe Israel* — *God, the God of Israel* (33:18–20). It is the second piece of ground a patriarch
  ever purchases, after Machpelah.
- **Dinah is raped and the city is massacred** by Simeon and Levi (34), the episode Jacob is still
  condemning on his deathbed (49:5–7).
- **Joseph is sent to his brothers there** and is sold on from nearby Dothan (37:12–14); his bones
  are eventually buried in Jacob's field (Joshua 24:32).
- **The blessings and the curses** are read out from Gerizim and Ebal on either side of the town
  (Deuteronomy 11:29; 27:11–13; Joshua 8:30–35).
- **Joshua renews the covenant here** in the last chapter of his book, beginning with Terah beyond
  the River and ending with *"choose today whom you will serve"* (Joshua 24:1–27).
- **Abimelech is made king here and destroys the place** (Judges 9), the Bible's first experiment
  with monarchy and its shortest.
- **The kingdom splits here.** Rehoboam goes to Shechem to be made king, refuses the elders' advice,
  and loses ten tribes (1 Kings 12:1–19); Jeroboam then fortifies it as his first capital (12:25).
- **Jesus meets the Samaritan woman at Jacob's well** outside *Sychar* (John 4:5–6), on the ground
  Jacob bought, in a conversation about which mountain to worship on — Gerizim is directly overhead.

The pattern is hard to miss: Shechem is where covenants are made and where Israel comes apart. Abram
builds the first altar in the land there, and the ten northern tribes walk out of the kingdom on the
same spot.
