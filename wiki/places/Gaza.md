# Gaza

## Overview

### Samson brought down blind — Judges 16:21

*They brought him down to Gaza, and bound him with fetters of brass; and he ground at the mill in the
prison* (16:21). The city whose gate Samson carried off (16:3) receives him back blind, as a prisoner
grinding in *the house of the bound* (WLC). See [[Samson and Delilah]]. Source: stored WEB Judges
16:3, 21; Hebrew WLC (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-16.

### The gate carried off — Judges 16:1–3

Samson goes to Gaza for a prostitute (16:1). The Gazites lie in wait for him all night *in the gate of the
city*, and he rises at midnight and takes *the doors of the gate of the city, and the two posts … bar and
all* up to the hill before Hebron (2–3). The city Judah *took … with its border* (1:18) is a Philistine city
here, and the ambush is laid by *the Gazites*, with no ruler named. It is
where Samson will be brought back blind to grind in the prison (16:21), and where the house of Dagon falls
(16:23–30). See [[Samson at Gaza]] and
[[The Prostitute and the City Gate - Rahab at Jericho and Samson at Gaza]].

Source: stored WEB Judges 1:18; 16:1–3, 21, 23–30.

### "Until you come to Gaza" — Judges 6:4

The Midianite raiders *destroyed the increase of the earth, until you come to Gaza* (6:4). Gaza, which
Judah took in 1:18, here marks the far western edge of the raids: the land is stripped from the eastern
desert to the Philistine coast. The idiom, *ʿad-bôʾăkā ʿazzâ*, addresses the reader, as Genesis 10:19
does when it draws the Canaanite border toward Gerar and Gaza (WLC).

See [[Midian's Oppression and the Prophet]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 6:1–10; Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

### Taken by Judah — Judges 1:18

*Also Judah took Gaza with its border, and Ashkelon with its border, and Ekron with its border* (18).
Joshua 13:2–3 had listed the city with Ashkelon and Ekron among *the land that still remains*. The next verse
says Judah could not dislodge the people of the valley, and later Judges has two of them in Philistine
hands (Ashkelon at 14:19, Gaza at 16:1–2). It is often said that the Septuagint reads verse 18 as a
negative; that claim has not been checked here. The WLC reads *wayyilkōd*, *and he took*.

See [[Judah's Conquests and Achsah's Springs]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 1:8–21; comparisons as cited.

### Judah’s cities — Joshua 15:20–63

Allotted to Judah with its towns and villages, *to the brook of Egypt* (47). 10:41 reaches Gaza, 11:22 leaves Anakim there, and 13:3 counts it as remaining; the allotment does not resolve those notices.

See [[The Cities of Judah]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 15:20–63.

### The land that remains — Joshua 13:1–7

The Gazites belong to the five Philistine lordships in land still remaining (3). This explicitly qualifies the campaign reaching Gaza in 10:41 and accords with the remnant there in 11:22.

See [[The Land That Remains]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:1–7.

### The whole land taken — Joshua 11:16–23

Gaza is one of three cities where some of the Anakim remain (22), with [[Gath]] and [[Ashdod]]. Joshua 13:3 lists the Gazites among the land remaining.

See [[The Whole Land Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 11:16–23.

### The southern campaign — Joshua 10:28–43

Gaza marks the reach from Kadesh Barnea in verse 41, without a separate capture narrative. Joshua 13:1–3 still lists Philistine territory, including the Gazites, as remaining; comprehensive language here does not settle permanent possession.

See [[The Southern Cities Taken]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 10:28–43; comparisons as cited.

Named as the extent of the Avvim’s villages in the account of their displacement by the Caphtorim
(Deuteronomy 2:23). Israel does not conquer Gaza in this pericope.

Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 2:1–23.
