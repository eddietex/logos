# Pisgah

## Overview

### The death of Moses — Deuteronomy 34:1–12

Moses ascends *to the top of Pisgah*, joined with Mount Nebo (1), and is shown the land, fulfilling 3:27. The relation of Pisgah to Nebo is not stated.

See [[The Death of Moses]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 34:1–12.

### Setting of the instruction — Deuteronomy 4:44–49

Its slopes locate the sea of the Arabah within the eastern territorial summary (49). No ascent or vision from its summit is narrated here.

See [[The Setting of the Covenant Instruction]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 4:44–49.

### Moses’ plea — Deuteronomy 3:23–29

Moses is commanded to ascend its top and look west, north, south, and east, while remaining
forbidden to cross the Jordan (27). The command is not an account of an ascent already performed.

See [[Moses Pleads to Cross the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:23–29.

### Eastern inheritance — Deuteronomy 3:12–22

Its eastern slopes supply a geographical bearing for the grant (17). Moses’ request to cross and
instruction to ascend its summit belong to the following unit.

See [[The Eastern Inheritance and the Charge to Joshua]]. Source: stored WEB Deuteronomy 3:12–22.

### The first two oracles — Numbers 22:36–23:26

The field of Zophim on Pisgah’s summit becomes the second sacrificial station (23:14). Another set
of seven altars produces another blessing, not the reversal Balak seeks.

See [[Balaam's First Two Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:36–23:26.


The top of Pisgah overlooks the desert at the end of this itinerary (20), in the description of the
valley in the field of Moab. The verse does not narrate Moses’ later death or a conquest here.

Source: stored WEB Numbers 21:10–20.
