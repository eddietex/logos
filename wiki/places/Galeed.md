# Galeed

## Overview

**A pile of stones on a hill in [[Gilead]], and the only place in the Bible that is given three names
in six verses.**

It is not a settlement. **It is a border marker, a witness, and a table** — the stones Jacob's
household gathered at Laban's proposal, beside a standing pillar Jacob had already raised, with a
meal eaten on top of it and an oath sworn over it that neither man would cross it to harm the other
(31:45–54).

### Three names

| name | language | meaning | who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jegar Sahadutha** | Aramaic — ***yəgar śāhădûtāʾ*** | heap of witness | Laban |
| **Galeed** | Hebrew — ***galʿēd*** | heap of witness | Jacob |
| **Mizpah** | Hebrew — ***miṣpâ*** | watchpost | given in 31:49 |

**The first two are the same phrase in two languages**, and Genesis prints both without translating
either — **the only Aramaic in the Torah**, and the moment the book lets the reader hear that the
branch that stayed in Mesopotamia no longer speaks the branch that left. See
[[Jegar Sahadutha - The Only Aramaic in the Torah]] and [[Language and Speech]].

**The third is a different word doing different work.** *Mizpah* is built on ***ṣāpâ***, *to keep
watch*, and its etiology is the clause that follows it: *"Yahweh watch between me and you, when we
are absent one from another"* (31:49). **The sentence is engraved on jewellery and read at partings
as a blessing; in Genesis it is a surveillance clause**, and the two verses after it say what is being
watched for. See [[The Mizpah Benediction - Genesis 31.49 and How It Is Read]].

***Galʿēd*** is also one vowel from ***gilʿād***, the region it stands in. **Genesis gives the pun
and does not press it into a claim** — it has already used the region's name twice before the heap
exists (31:21, 23). See [[Gilead]].

### What it is for

***ʿĒd***, *witness*, occurs **five times in eight verses** (31:44, 48, 50, 52 twice). **There is no
king, no court, and no priest on the mountain**, so the heap is appointed the judiciary: *"May this
heap be a witness, and the pillar be a witness, that I will not pass over this heap to you, and that
you will not pass over this heap and this pillar to me, for harm"* (31:52).

**It is the second treaty in Genesis between this family and an outsider** — the first was Abraham
and Abimelech at [[Beersheba]] (21:27–32), also over property, also sealed at a place that then took
its name from the oath. **The difference is that this one is between relatives**, and the border it
draws is the one that finally makes the Arameans foreigners.

**Later Mizpahs are other places.** *Miṣpâ* is a common Hebrew place-name — the Mizpah of Benjamin
where Israel assembles and Samuel judges (Judges 20:1; 1 Samuel 7:5–6), the Mizpah of Gilead
associated with Jephthah (Judges 11:11, 29, 34), and the Mizpah where Gedaliah governs after the fall
of Jerusalem (2 Kings 25:23). **Whether any of them is this heap is not recoverable**, and the wiki
does not identify them.

## Appears in

- [[Jacob's Flight from Laban]] — Genesis 31:45–54; **raised, named three times, sworn over, and eaten
  on.** Jacob sets up a pillar, the household gathers stones into a heap, Laban names it in Aramaic
  and Jacob in Hebrew, the *Mizpah* clause is added, the daughters are protected by treaty, the
  boundary is fixed in both directions, and a sacrifice and a meal close it. **Laban claims to have
  set up the pillar Jacob set up, and nobody corrects him.**
