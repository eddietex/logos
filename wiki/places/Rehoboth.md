# Rehoboth

## Overview

**The third well, the one nobody argues over, and the only place in the chapter Isaac names for
something God did rather than for something that was done to him.**

> **22** He left that place, and dug another well. They didn’t argue over that one. He called it
> Rehoboth. He said, “For now Yahweh has made room for us, and we will be fruitful in the land.”

***Rəḥōbôt*** is a plural — **broad places**, **open spaces**, **room** — from *rāḥab*, to be wide,
and Genesis supplies the etymology in Isaac's own sentence: *kî ʿattâ hirḥîb YHWH lānû*, **for now
Yahweh has made room for us.** The same root gives the *rəḥōb*, the broad open place inside a city
gate where Lot met the two messengers (19:2) and where public business was done.

**It is the only thing Isaac says in the whole well sequence.** He does not argue at [[Esek]], does
not argue at [[Sitnah]], does not appeal to his father's treaty or to the promise he had been given
nineteen verses earlier that *all these lands* were his — and when the digging finally goes
unchallenged, **what he says is not that he won but that God made space.** See
[[Strife and Peacemaking]].

**The sentence does not stop at the naming.** *"…and we will be fruitful in the land"* —
***ûpārînû bāʾāreṣ***, the verb of the creation blessing
(1:22, 28; 9:1, 7) and of the promise to Abraham (17:6) and to Ishmael (17:20), spoken by a man who
has just been evicted from a town and pushed out of two water sources. **It is the only time Isaac
quotes the promise language back**, and he does it about a well in a wadi.

**Where it is.** Unlocated, like the other two. The proposal most often repeated identifies it with
**Wadi Ruheibeh**, south-west of [[Beersheba]], on the strength of the name alone; the site there is
Nabataean and Byzantine, centuries too late to bear on Genesis, and the identification rests on
nothing else. **The wiki records it as a suggestion, not a location.**

**The name recurs twice in the Bible and neither is this place**: *Rehoboth-Ir*, one of the cities of
Nimrod's Assyrian foundation (10:11), and *Rehoboth by the River*, the home town of the Edomite king
Shaul (36:37; 1 Chronicles 1:48). **A common word makes a common place name.** See [[Assyria]] and
[[Edom]].
