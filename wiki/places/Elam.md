# Elam

## Overview

The highland kingdom east of Babylonia, and the seat of the overlord whose reprisal starts the
Bible's first war.

It enters Scripture as a person: *"The sons of Shem were: Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, and
Aram"* (Genesis 10:22), the first name in Shem's list. Genesis 14 then uses it as a country —
*"Chedorlaomer, king of Elam"* (14:1, 9) — which makes the chapter's chief aggressor a Semite by the
Table's own reckoning, whatever the Elamite language actually was. See [[The Table of Nations]] and
[[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

**Historically it is not Semitic at all.** Elam occupied the plain of Khuzestan and the Zagros
foothills of what is now south-western Iran, with Susa as its principal city; its language is an
isolate, related to nothing else known. The Table of Nations' placement of it under Shem is one of
the clearest cases of that chapter working by geography, politics, and trade orientation rather than
by anything a linguist would recognize as descent — the same chapter files Canaan under Ham. Elam
was a real and long-lived power, at times overrunning Mesopotamia; a raid west into Transjordan of
the kind Genesis 14 describes is not implausible for the second millennium, and no such campaign is
attested.

**Chedorlaomer's name fits the place.** The Elamite element *Kudur-* stands behind *Chedorlaomer*,
and *-laomer* is usually connected with the Elamite deity Lagamar. The name is linguistically at
home; the man is unidentified. See [[Chedorlaomer]].

**It stays in the Bible as a distant threat and a distant hope.** Isaiah 21:2 and 22:6 have Elam in
the armies that come against Babylon and Judah; Jeremiah 49:34–39 prophesies against it and then
promises *"I will reverse the captivity of Elam"*; Ezekiel 32:24 puts it among the nations gone down
to the pit; Ezra 4:9 lists Elamites among the deportees resettled in Samaria; and Acts 2:9 has
Elamites in the crowd at Pentecost, hearing the wonders of God in their own language — which puts
the chapter's aggressor nation in the list that answers Babel. See
[[The Table of Nations at Pentecost]].
