# Damascus

## Overview

The great oasis city of the Syrian plateau, and in Genesis a landmark for somewhere else.

*"He divided himself against them by night, he and his servants, and struck them, and pursued them
to Hobah, which is on the left hand of Damascus"* (Genesis 14:15). The city itself is not entered
and nothing happens in it; it is named because it is the one place in the region a reader could be
counted on to locate, and Hobah is not. *The left hand* is north in the Hebrew orientation, facing
east — the same idiom Abram used offering Lot the land at 13:9 — so the chase runs past Damascus and
out of Canaan altogether, something on the order of a hundred and fifty miles from [[Mamre (place)]].
See [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

**It returns immediately, in the next thing Abram says.** *"Lord Yahweh, what will you give me,
since I go childless, and he who will inherit my estate is Eliezer of Damascus?"* (Genesis 15:2).
Abram's steward and presumptive heir is from the city he has just chased an army past. The two
mentions are the whole of Damascus in Genesis, and they sit four verses of narrative apart.

**Historically it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth.** It sits in the
Ghouta, an oasis watered by the Barada running off the Anti-Lebanon range, on the road between
Mesopotamia and Egypt; it is named in Egyptian topographical lists from the fifteenth century BC.
From the tenth century it heads Aram-Damascus, Israel's most persistent northern rival: David
garrisons it (2 Samuel 8:5–6), Ben-Hadad and Hazael fight the northern kingdom from it repeatedly
(1 Kings 20; 2 Kings 6–8; 13), Naaman its commander is healed and asks for two mule-loads of
Israelite earth to worship on (2 Kings 5), Ahaz copies its altar for the temple in Jerusalem
(2 Kings 16:10–16), and Amos 1:3–5 and Isaiah 17:1–3 pronounce its ruin.

**And it is where the New Testament turns.** Saul is on the road to Damascus, carrying letters to
its synagogues, when the light stops him (Acts 9:1–19; 22:6–16; 26:12–18); he is baptized in the
city, preaches in it, and is let down over its wall in a basket (Acts 9:23–25; 2 Corinthians
11:32–33). Nothing in Genesis 14:15 points forward to that; the city simply keeps recurring at the
edges of Israel's story from the first chase to the last conversion.
