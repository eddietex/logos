# Midian

## Overview

**A people and a territory rather than a city — the caravan nomads of north-western Arabia and the
eastern Sinai — and one of the very few nations the Bible treats as both Israel's in-laws and Israel's
enemies.**

**It enters Genesis as a name in [[Keturah]]'s list**: *"She bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian,
Ishbak, and Shuah"* (25:2), making Midian a son of [[Abraham]] and the Midianites a collateral branch
of the covenant family — like Edom, and for the same structural reason. See
[[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]].

**Its first appearance as a nation is in Genesis 36**, on the losing end of a sentence:

> **35** Husham died, and Hadad, the son of Bedad, who struck Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in
> his place. The name of his city was Avith.

**The only military event in the whole of [[The Descendants of Esau]]**, and a war nothing else in the
Bible mentions. Edom beating Midian on [[Moab (place)]]'s ground is a note from a regional history
Scripture never tells. See [[Hadad (son of Bedad)]].

## Where

**No fixed borders, which is what one expects of a people the Bible mostly describes travelling.** The
core territory is usually placed along the eastern shore of the Gulf of Aqaba, in the north-west of
modern Saudi Arabia — the region classical geographers called *Madiama* — with the Midianites ranging
far north and west of it. Exodus 3:1 puts Moses' father-in-law's pasturage within reach of Horeb;
Numbers 22:4 and 25:6 have Midianites operating with Moab on the plains east of the Jordan; Judges 6
has them raiding as far as the Jezreel valley. **They behave in the text like a confederation of
caravan and pastoral clans rather than a kingdom**, and the Bible names five Midianite kings at once
(Numbers 31:8) rather than one at a time as it does for [[Edom]].

## The two faces

**Midian shelters Moses and then fights Israel, and the Bible never reconciles the two.**

- **Genesis 37:28, 36** — **the next pericope in this book.** Midianite (and Ishmaelite) traders pull
  [[Joseph]] out of the pit and sell him into Egypt; the passage uses both names for the same
  caravan, which is one of the best-known seams in Genesis.
- **Exodus 2:15–22; 3:1; 18:1–27** — Moses flees to Midian, marries Zipporah, keeps the flocks of the
  priest of Midian, meets God at the bush while doing it, and later takes that priest's advice on how
  to govern. **The single most consequential piece of counsel in the Torah's administration of Israel
  comes from a Midianite.** He is called **Reuel** at Exodus 2:18 and **Jethro** from 3:1 — and
  *Reuel* is also the name of Esau's second son, on which see [[Reuel (son of Esau)]].
- **Numbers 22:4–7; 25:16–18; 31** — Midian joins Moab in hiring Balaam, is implicated in the affair
  at Peor, and is destroyed.
- **Judges 6–8** — the Midianite camel raids, and Gideon.
- **Isaiah 60:6** — *"the dromedaries of Midian and Ephah"* bringing gold and frankincense to Zion;
  **the last biblical word on Midian is a caravan arriving with gifts.** See
  [[Abraham's Other Sons in Isaiah 60]].

## Appears in

- [[The Descendants of Esau]] — Genesis 36:35; struck by an Edomite king in the field of Moab, in the
  only clause of the chapter that describes a battle
- [[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]] — Genesis 25:2; a son of Abraham by [[Keturah]],
  and one of the six who are given gifts and sent east

## Related places

- [[Edom]] — its neighbour, and its opponent at Genesis 36:35
- [[Moab (place)]] — the field the battle is fought in, and Midian's ally in Numbers 22
- [[Egypt]] — where the Midianite traders of Genesis 37 take Joseph
- [[Paran]] and [[Shur]] — the desert country Midian's range abuts

## Events here

- A son of Abraham by Keturah, sent east with gifts — Genesis 25:1–6
- Struck by Hadad son of Bedad in the field of Moab — Genesis 36:35
- Moses' forty years of exile, his marriage, and the burning bush — Exodus 2:15–3:12
- Jethro's counsel on delegated judges — Exodus 18
- Destroyed after Peor; five kings killed, and Balaam with them — Numbers 31
- The raids Gideon ends — Judges 6–8
