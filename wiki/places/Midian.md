# Midian

## Overview

### The camp that turned on itself — Judges 7:16–25

At the middle watch three hundred horns sound around the camp, and *all the army ran* (7:21). *Yahweh
set every man's sword against his fellow, and against all the army* (7:22). The flight runs past Beth
Shittah, Zererah, and Abel Meholah toward the Jordan, where Ephraim holds the waters and kills *the two
princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb* (7:24–25). Later Scripture calls it *the day of Midian* (Isaiah 9:4,
WEB checked). See [[The Sword of Yahweh and of Gideon]] and
[[The Day of Midian - Oreb and Zeeb in Psalm 83 and Isaiah 9-10]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 7:16–25;
Isaiah 9:4 checked at bible-api.com (WEB), 2026-11-15.

### The tent that fell — Judges 7:9–15

Midian's army *lay along in the valley like locusts for multitude*, and its camels were *as the sand which
is on the seashore for multitude* (7:12), the image Joshua 11:4 used for the Canaanite kings at Merom.
Inside the camp a soldier dreams of a barley loaf that knocks *the tent* flat, and his fellow says it is
*the sword of Gideon … God has delivered Midian into his hand, with all the army* (7:13–14). It is the
first dream told in Scripture since Pharaoh's, and Midian interprets it against itself. A common
reading takes the loaf as the farmer and the tent as the nomad who came *with their tents* (6:5). That
is reported here, not adopted. See [[The Dream of the Barley Loaf]] and
[[Spies Who Hear the Enemy's Fear - Joshua 2 and Judges 7]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 6:5; 7:12–15;
Joshua 11:4.

### Beneath him in the valley — Judges 7:1–8

*Midian's camp was on the north side of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley* (7:1), and at the
end of the scene *the camp of Midian was beneath him in the valley* (7:8). Midian does nothing in the
pericope. Everything happens on Israel's side: the army is cut from thirty-two thousand to three
hundred, and Yahweh promises *deliver the Midianites into your hand* (7:7). See
[[Gideon's Three Hundred]], [[The Hill of Moreh]], and [[The Spring of Harod]]. Source: stored WEB
Judges 7:1–8.

### Encamped in the valley — Judges 6:33

*All the Midianites and the Amalekites and the children of the east assembled themselves together;
and they passed over, and encamped in the valley of Jezreel* (6:33). The raids of 6:3–5 become a
single army in a single camp, west of the Jordan in the north. Verse 33 says only *passed over*; the
river is not named. Against it Gideon musters Abiezer, Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali (6:34–35).
See [[The Fleece]] and [[The Valley of Jezreel]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 6:33–40.

### Hidden from — Judges 6:11–24

Gideon threshes wheat in a winepress *to hide it from the Midianites* (6:11), and is sent to *save
Israel from the hand of Midian* with the promise *you shall strike the Midianites as one man* (6:14,
16). He complains that Yahweh *has … delivered us into the hand of Midian* (6:13), the narrator's
phrase from 6:1.

Source: stored WEB Judges 6:11–24; Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

### Seven years over Israel — Judges 6:1–6

*Yahweh delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years* (6:1). The Midianites come every year at
harvest with the Amalekites and [[The Children of the East]], *with their livestock and their tents*,
camp on the fields, and strip the land *until you come to Gaza* (6:3–5). Israel hides in *the dens which
are in the mountains, and the caves, and the strongholds* (6:2). The narrator compares them to locusts,
and their camels are *without number* (6:5); see
[[Like Locusts for Multitude - Exodus 10, Deuteronomy 28, and the Midianite Raids]]. Midian, destroyed
in Numbers 31 by the account there, is here strong enough to rule Israel; the wiki records both, as
above, and does not harmonize them.

See [[Midian's Oppression and the Prophet]]. Source: stored WEB Judges 6:1–10; Hebrew from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OpenScriptures morphhb), 2026-11-15.

### Eastern allotments — Joshua 13:8–33

The five chiefs are recalled as princes of Sihon (21); Numbers 31:8 calls them kings of Midian. Their deaths are remembered within the land register, without a new battle narrative.

See [[The Inheritance Beyond the Jordan]]. Source: stored WEB Joshua 13:8–33; comparisons as cited.

### The war against Midian — Numbers 31:1–54

Twelve thousand Israelites kill every male, the five kings, and Balaam, take the women, children,
livestock, and goods, and burn the cities and encampments (7–11). Moses has the boys and the women who
had lain with a man killed, because of Peor (15–17). The chapter reads as Midian's end, yet Judges 6:1
has Yahweh deliver Israel *into the hand of Midian seven years* (WEB via bible-api.com); the wiki records
both without harmonizing them.

See [[The War Against Midian]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 31.

### Baal Peor — Numbers 25:1–18

Cozbi is the daughter of a Midianite leader. Yahweh commands hostility against the Midianites
because of their deception in the Peor affair (15–18). This differs from the hospitality of
Jethro’s household; the text does not identify that household with these actors.

See [[Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 25:1–18.


### Balak’s embassies — Numbers 22:1–21

Midian’s elders join Moab’s elders in carrying the rewards of divination to Balaam (4–7). This
collaboration contrasts with Midian’s earlier hospitality to Moses; a collective action here does
not identify these elders with Jethro or his household.

See [[Balak Sends for Balaam]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:1–21.

**A people and a territory rather than a city — the caravan nomads of north-western Arabia and the
eastern Sinai — and one of the very few nations the Bible treats as both Israel's in-laws and Israel's
enemies.**

**It enters Genesis as a name in [[Keturah]]'s list**: *"She bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian,
Ishbak, and Shuah"* (25:2), making Midian a son of [[Abraham]] and the Midianites a collateral branch
of the covenant family — like Edom, and for the same structural reason. See
[[The Death of Abraham and the Line of Ishmael]].

**Its first appearance as a nation is in Genesis 36**, on the losing end of a sentence:

> **35** Husham died, and Hadad, the son of Bedad, who struck Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in
> his place. The name of his city was Avith.

**The only military event in the whole of [[The Descendants of Esau]]**, and a war nothing else in the
Bible mentions. Edom beating Midian on [[Moab (place)]]'s ground is a note from a regional history
Scripture never tells. See [[Hadad (son of Bedad)]].

## Where

**No fixed borders, which is what one expects of a people the Bible mostly describes travelling.** The
core territory is usually placed along the eastern shore of the Gulf of Aqaba, in the north-west of
modern Saudi Arabia — the region classical geographers called *Madiama* — with the Midianites ranging
far north and west of it. Exodus 3:1 puts Moses' father-in-law's pasturage within reach of Horeb;
Numbers 22:4 and 25:6 have Midianites operating with Moab on the plains east of the Jordan; Judges 6
has them raiding as far as the Jezreel valley. **They behave in the text like a confederation of
caravan and pastoral clans rather than a kingdom**, and the Bible names five Midianite kings at once
(Numbers 31:8) rather than one at a time as it does for [[Edom]].

## The two faces

**Midian shelters Moses and then fights Israel, and the Bible never reconciles the two.**

- **Genesis 37:28, 36** — **the next pericope in this book.** Midianite (and Ishmaelite) traders pull
  [[Joseph]] out of the pit and sell him into Egypt; the passage uses both names for the same
  caravan, which is one of the best-known seams in Genesis.
- **Exodus 2:15–22; 3:1; 18:1–27** — Moses flees to Midian, marries [[Zipporah]], keeps the flocks of
  the priest of Midian, meets God at the bush while doing it, and later takes that priest's advice on
  how to govern. **The single most consequential piece of counsel in the Torah's administration of
  Israel comes from a Midianite.** He is called **Reuel** at Exodus 2:18 and **Jethro** from 3:1, and
  the wiki files him as [[Jethro]]; *Reuel* is also the name of Esau's second son, on which see
  [[Reuel (son of Esau)]]. See [[Moses Flees to Midian]].
- **Numbers 22:4–7; 25:16–18; 31** — Midian joins Moab in hiring Balaam, is implicated in the affair
  at Peor, and is destroyed.
- **Judges 6–8** — the Midianite camel raids, and Gideon.
- **Isaiah 60:6** — *"the dromedaries of Midian and Ephah"* bringing gold and frankincense to Zion;
  **the last biblical word on Midian is a caravan arriving with gifts.** See
  [[Abraham's Other Sons in Isaiah 60]].

  is Midian's first appearance as a people doing anything**
- [[Moses Flees to Midian]] — Exodus 2:15–22; **the fugitive's refuge.** Moses flees here from
  Pharaoh, sits down by *the* well, saves the priest of Midian's seven daughters from the shepherds,
  and marries into the house. **The descendants of the people whose caravan carried Joseph into Egypt
  (Genesis 37:28, 36) shelter the man who will bring Joseph's bones out of it** (Exodus 13:19). Moses
  names his son for being a *gēr* here — *"a foreigner in a foreign land"* (2:22)
- [[The Burning Bush]] — Exodus 3:1; **the pasture behind which lies the mountain.** Moses leads
  Jethro's flock *"to the back of the wilderness"* and comes to [[Horeb]]; Midian's country is within
  a shepherd's reach of God's mountain
- [[Moses Returns to Egypt]] — Exodus 4:18–20; **left in peace.** Moses asks Jethro's leave and is
  given it — *"Go in peace"* — and God's word comes to him *"in Midian"*: *"all the men who sought your
  life are dead"*. The refuge is left with a blessing, unlike Jacob's flight from Laban's Haran
- [[Jethro's Counsel]] — Exodus 18:1, 27; **the priest's home, and his return.** *"Jethro, the priest of Midian"*
  comes to the camp, blesses Yahweh, gives the counsel, and *"went his way into his own land"*. **Midian in Exodus is
  the friend; in Numbers it will be the enemy** (Numbers 25; 31)
- [[The March from Sinai]] — Numbers 10:29–30; Hobab the son of Reuel *the Midianite*, who says he will go to his own land
