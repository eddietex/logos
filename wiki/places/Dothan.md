# Dothan

## Overview

**A pasture and a road junction about fifteen miles north of Shechem, and the place Joseph is sold.**

> **17** The man said, “They have left here, for I heard them say, ‘Let us go to Dothan.’” Joseph went
> after his brothers, and found them in Dothan. (37:17)

**It enters Scripture as an overheard remark.** Joseph is wandering in a field at Shechem looking for
brothers who have moved on, and **an unnamed man repeats something he heard them say.** Without that
scrap the chapter ends with a boy walking home. **Dothan is in the Bible because somebody was listening.**

**It sits in a broad, well-watered plain at the north end of the Samarian hills**, where the route up the
central highlands meets the road running west to the coastal plain and south-west to Egypt — **which is
why a caravan out of [[Gilead]] carrying resin *"to carry it down to Egypt"* passes a family's flocks
there** (37:25). The site is generally identified with **Tell Dothan**, a large mound occupied more or
less continuously from the Chalcolithic period onward; **excavation found cisterns cut into the rock**,
which is the kind of installation 37:24's *"pit"* with *"no water in it"* describes.

**Genesis names it twice, in one verse and the next, and never again.**

### The other Dothan story

**The only other narrative set there is 2 Kings 6:8–23**, and it is built on the same two elements as
this one — **a trap laid at Dothan, and men who cannot see what is actually in front of them.**

The king of Aram sends *"horses, chariots, and a great army"* by night to surround the town because
Elisha is in it; Elisha's servant sees the encirclement and panics; **Elisha prays that his eyes be
opened and the young man sees *"the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire"***. Then Elisha
prays the opposite way, **the Arameans are struck blind**, and he leads the entire army to Samaria and
feeds them lunch.

**The wiki records the pairing as a coincidence of setting and of motif, and claims nothing more.**
Neither text refers to the other; there is no shared vocabulary; and Dothan is not a symbolic place in
Scripture the way Bethel or Shechem are. **What can be said is that both stories happen at Dothan, both
turn on what a man can and cannot see from a hillside, and in one of them heaven is invisible and in the
other it is merely unnoticed.** Genesis 37 is the chapter of the Joseph cycle in which God does not
appear at all.

**Judith 3:9; 4:6; 7:3, 18 also set an army at Dothan**, on the road to Jerusalem, which is consistent
with the strategic position but adds nothing to the biblical narratives.

## Appears in

- [[Joseph Sold into Slavery]] — Genesis 37:17; **the whole of the pericope's action happens here.** The
  brothers are found here; the conspiracy is formed here as Joseph is still *"afar off"*; the coat is
  stripped and the cistern used here; the caravan passes here; and **the sale takes place here for twenty
  pieces of silver.** The narration then follows the caravan and leaves the place behind

## Related places

- [[Shechem (city)]] — fifteen miles south, the errand's stated destination, and where the brothers were
  no longer to be found. **The town they had emptied three chapters earlier**
- [[Hebron]] — *"the valley of Hebron"* (37:14), some sixty-five miles south, where Joseph set out from
  and where the coat was brought back
- [[Gilead]] — across the Jordan, where the caravan and its spices came from
- [[Egypt]] — where the road past Dothan goes, and where Joseph is taken on it

## Events here

- Joseph finds his brothers, having been redirected by a stranger at Shechem — Genesis 37:17
- The brothers see him *"afar off"* and conspire to kill him — Genesis 37:18–20
- Reuben amends the plan to a cistern, intending a rescue — Genesis 37:21–22
- Joseph is stripped of the coat and thrown into a waterless pit — Genesis 37:23–24
- The brothers eat a meal beside the pit; a caravan of traders appears — Genesis 37:25
- Judah proposes the sale; Joseph is drawn out and sold for twenty pieces of silver — Genesis 37:26–28
- Reuben returns to the empty pit and tears his clothes — Genesis 37:29
- Elisha is besieged here by an Aramean army, which is blinded and led away to Samaria —
  2 Kings 6:8–23 *(not yet ingested)*
