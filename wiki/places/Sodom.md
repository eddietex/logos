# Sodom

## Overview

A city named four times before anything happens in it, and by then the reader already knows how it
ends.

**It enters Scripture as a survey marker.** *"The border of the Canaanites was from Sidon—as you go
toward Gerar—to Gaza—as you go toward Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboiim—to Lasha"* (Genesis 10:19).
Four of the five cities of the plain, used in [[The Table of Nations]] to fix the southeastern corner
of Canaan's territory, with no comment attached. It is a place on a map.

**It acquires a character in Genesis 13, and the order of the verses is the point.** Lot lifts his
eyes and sees the plain *"before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah"* (13:10) — the narrator telling
the reader the end of the story inside the verse in which the choice is made. Two verses later Lot
*"moved his tent as far as Sodom"* (13:12), and then: *"Now the men of Sodom were exceedingly wicked
and sinners against Yahweh"* (13:13). *Rāʿîm wəḥaṭṭāʾîm lə-Yhwh*, **wicked and sinners against
Yahweh** — Genesis's flattest verdict on a population since 6:5, delivered one verse after the man
who has just moved next to them made his choice. See [[Abram and Lot Separate]].

### What the Bible says its sin was

The question is genuinely disputed, and Scripture itself gives more than one answer.

- **Genesis 13:13** charges *wickedness and sin against Yahweh* with no content at all.
- **Genesis 18:20–21** speaks of a *cry* — *zaʿăqat Sodom*, the same noun used of Israel's groaning
  under Egypt (Exodus 2:23) and of Abel's blood (Genesis 4:10). It is the word for the protest of the
  wronged, which implies victims inside the city.
- **Genesis 19:4–11** supplies the scene the city's name became an English word for: every man of the
  town, young and old, demanding the two visitors, and Lot offering his daughters instead.
- **Ezekiel 16:49–50** is the Old Testament's one explicit definition: *"this was the iniquity of
  your sister Sodom: pride, fullness of bread, and prosperous ease was in her and in her daughters.
  She also didn't strengthen the hand of the poor and needy. They were arrogant and committed
  abomination before me."*
- **Isaiah 1:10–17** addresses Jerusalem as *"you rulers of Sodom"* and then specifies: bloody hands,
  and *"seek justice, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow."*
- **Jeremiah 23:14** compares Jerusalem's prophets to Sodom for adultery and lying.
- **Luke 10:10–12** makes it the measure of a town that refuses hospitality to messengers.
- **Jude 7** reads it as sexual: *"having in the same way as these given themselves over to sexual
  immorality and gone after strange flesh."*

The wiki records the range rather than choosing. The two poles — injustice and inhospitality on one
side, sexual violence on the other — are both textually grounded, and Genesis 19 contains a scene in
which they are not separable: a mob's demand to rape the guests is a violation of hospitality *and*
of the guests, and the *cry* of 18:20 was already rising before the visitors arrived.

### The city in the rest of Scripture

Sodom stops being a place and becomes a byword. It is the standard of judgment (Deuteronomy 29:23;
Isaiah 13:19; Jeremiah 49:18; 50:40; Amos 4:11; Zephaniah 2:9), the standard of corruption applied to
Israel and Judah themselves (Isaiah 1:9–10; Jeremiah 23:14; Lamentations 4:6; Ezekiel 16:46–56), and
in the New Testament the fixed comparison for a place that had its chance (Matthew 10:15; 11:23–24;
Luke 10:12; 17:28–32; Romans 9:29 quoting Isaiah 1:9; 2 Peter 2:6; Jude 7). Revelation 11:8 calls the
city where the Lord was crucified *"spiritually Sodom and Egypt"* — the two places Genesis has
already used as a pair of similes at 13:10.

### Where it was

Unknown, and contested. The cities of the plain sit in the Jordan Rift, and the two live proposals
are a **southern** one placing them under or beside the shallow water south of the Lisan peninsula at
the Dead Sea's foot, and a **northern** one identifying Tall el-Hammam on the eastern plain north of
the Dead Sea, where excavators have argued for a violent Middle Bronze destruction. The northern
identification's proposed mechanism — a cosmic airburst, published in 2021 — has drawn substantial
methodological criticism. Neither identification is settled, and the wiki endorses none. What the
geology does supply, uncontroversially, is a region on an active transform fault with bitumen seeps
(*"tar pits"*, Genesis 14:10) and salt domes: a landscape where the ground really can burn, subside,
and salt over. See [[The Plain of the Jordan]].
