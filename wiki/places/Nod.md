# Nod

## Overview

The land Cain settles in after his sentence: *"Cain left Yahweh's presence, and lived in the land
of Nod, east of Eden"* (4:16). It appears in this one verse and nowhere else in the Bible, and no
proposal has ever located it, because it is almost certainly not that kind of name.

*Nôd* is built on the same root as *nād*, the "wanderer" of Cain's own sentence two verses
earlier — *"you will be a fugitive and a wanderer in the earth"* (4:12), which Cain repeats back
in 4:14. So the verse reads, more or less, *he settled in the land of Wandering*: the place-name
is the sentence, made into a geography. Whether Genesis intends a real region whose name happened
to fit or a name coined to say what became of him, the effect on the page is the same.

Its only stated coordinate is relative and directional — **east of Eden**, one step further along
the line the story has been travelling since 3:24. In Genesis, east is where people go when they
are going away.

Two things sit oddly beside each other here, and Genesis leaves them that way. Nod is where the
man sentenced to wander stops; and the very next verse has him build a city (4:17), the first in
Scripture. Whether that is defiance of the sentence, evidence that it could be lived with, or the
ordinary way a punishment becomes a life, the text does not say.
