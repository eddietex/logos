# Machpelah

## Overview

**The only real property any patriarch owns in Canaan** — a field with a cave at the end of it,
opposite [[Mamre (place)]] at [[Hebron]], bought by Abraham from [[Ephron the Hittite]] for four
hundred shekels of silver so that he could bury [[Sarah]].

> **17** So the field of Ephron, which was in Machpelah, which was before Mamre, the field, the cave
> which was in it, and all the trees that were in the field, that were in all of its borders, were
> deeded
> **18** to Abraham for a possession in the presence of the children of Heth, before all who went in
> at the gate of his city.

**Note what Machpelah actually is in that sentence.** It is not the cave's name — it is the *locality*
the field is in: *"the field of Ephron, which was in Machpelah"* Genesis then uses it loosely as the
name of the field and the cave both (23:19; 25:9; 49:30; 50:13), which is how it has been used ever
since. The word is usually derived from *kāpal*, **to double** — hence *the double cave*, which is
what the Septuagint's *τὸ σπήλαιον τὸ διπλοῦν* makes of it and what the later tradition of a
two-chambered tomb builds on. That is a reading of the root, not a description Genesis supplies.

**It is bought, and the chapter will not let that go.** Twice the text says the field *was deeded*
(23:17–18, 23:20), and the second time is the chapter's last verse — **after** the burial, so that
the unit ends on the title rather than on the grave. Abraham had been offered the use of the
Hittites' tombs for nothing and refused twice. A gift is held at the giver's pleasure; a purchase
witnessed at the gate is not. See [[Nahor's Line and the Burial of Sarah]].

**Its coordinates are given by a tree.** *"which was before Mamre"* — and Genesis repeats the phrase
at 23:19, 25:9, 49:30, and 50:13, as though the grove were the surveyor's benchmark for the whole
family plot.

## Six graves

Genesis records the burials, and Jacob's instructions at 49:29–32 recite them as a list before the
last one happens:

| buried | reference |
|---|---|
| **Sarah** | Genesis 23:19 |
| **Abraham** | Genesis 25:9–10 — by Isaac and Ishmael together |
| **Isaac** | Genesis 49:31 |
| **Rebekah** | Genesis 49:31 |
| **Leah** | Genesis 49:31 |
| **Jacob** | Genesis 50:13 — carried up from Egypt |

*"There they buried Abraham and Sarah, his wife. There they buried Isaac and Rebekah, his wife,
and there I buried Leah"* (49:31). **Rachel is not among them** — she is buried on the road to Ephrath
with a pillar over her grave (35:19–20) — and neither is Joseph, whose bones go to Shechem (Joshua
24:32). See [[Buried at Machpelah - The Six Graves of Genesis]].

**The purchase is what makes the burials mean something.** Jacob, dying in Egypt, does not ask to be
taken back to a place his family is fond of; he cites the deed — *"in the cave that is in the field
of Machpelah … which Abraham bought with the field from Ephron the Hittite as a burial place"*
(49:30). **The one clause of the land promise that has actually been executed is the one his sons
are asked to honour**, and honouring it takes a state funeral, a chariot escort, and a seventy-day
mourning in Egypt (50:1–14).

## Where it is, and what stands there

Hebron sits about nineteen miles south-southwest of Jerusalem in the Judean hills. The site
traditionally identified as Machpelah is enclosed by a massive rectangular ashlar wall, without
mortar and with pilastered courses, that is generally dated to the reign of **Herod the Great** and
compared with his Temple Mount platform — which makes the enclosure one of the oldest intact
buildings in continuous use anywhere. What is inside it, below the floor, has not been excavated. A
Byzantine church, then a mosque, then a Crusader church, then a mosque again have stood within the
walls; it is today the *Ibrahimi Mosque* / *Cave of the Patriarchs*, holy to Jews and Muslims alike
and bitterly contested. **The continuity of the tradition is very old and the identification is not
archaeologically demonstrated**, which is the ordinary state of affairs for biblical sites and is
recorded here as such.

## Related places

- [[Hebron]] — the town it belongs to, called *Kiriath Arba* in the same chapter
- [[Mamre (place)]] — the grove it lies *before*, and the landmark that fixes it in four later verses
- [[Canaan]] — the land it sits in, named twice in the chapter as if to place the deed inside the
  promise
- [[Beersheba]] — where Abraham was living when his wife died at Hebron
- [[Egypt]] — where Jacob dies, and from which his body is carried back to this field
