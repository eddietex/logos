# Cush

## Overview

**The land south of Egypt on the upper Nile — Nubia, in modern Sudan — and the Old Testament's
horizon in that direction.** The WEB usually translates the Hebrew *Kûš* as *Ethiopia*, following the
Greek *Aithiopia*, but the biblical Cush is not the modern state of Ethiopia; it is the Nile kingdom
centred at Kerma, then Napata and Meroë, which for a period in the eighth and seventh centuries BC
ruled Egypt itself as its Twenty-fifth Dynasty.

Cush enters Scripture twice in Genesis. The river Gihon of Eden *"flows through the whole land of
Cush"* (Genesis 2:13), and Cush is the firstborn son of [[Ham]] (10:6), father of Seba, [[Havilah]],
Sabtah, Raamah and Sabteca, and of [[Nimrod]] (10:7–8). The Eden reference is the hardest to place,
because the other rivers point to Mesopotamia; some readers take this Cush to be the Kassite land
east of the Tigris (Akkadian *Kaššû*) rather than Nubia. See [[Eden]].

From then on the name is consistently African. Moses' wife is called *"a Cushite woman"* (Numbers
12:1); *"Tirhakah king of Ethiopia"* — Taharqa, one of the Nubian pharaohs — marches against
Sennacherib (2 Kings 19:9); the Persian empire runs *"from India even to Ethiopia"* (Esther 1:1); and
the prophets use Cush as the far edge of the world — *"beyond the rivers of Ethiopia"* (Isaiah 18:1;
Zephaniah 3:10) — and as a people on equal standing before God: *"Are you not like the children of
the Ethiopians to me, children of Israel?"* (Amos 9:7). In Acts 8:27 a treasury official of the
Ethiopian queen, returning from worship in Jerusalem, is baptized on the road home.

**In Job it is a source of precious stone**: *"The topaz of Ethiopia shall not equal it"* (Job 28:19),
in the catalogue of goods that cannot buy wisdom. The stone (*piṭdâ*) is uncertain — topaz, peridot
and chrysolite have all been proposed — but the point of naming Cush is distance: wisdom is worth more
than the most precious thing from the furthest place the poet can name.

**Naming.** The page carries the bare name because, like [[Ophir]] and [[Havilah]], the Genesis 10
eponym has no person page of his own. If a later pass makes one for Ham's son, the person/place rule
in `AGENTS.md` applies and both pages take a qualifier.

## Appears in

- [[The Garden of Eden]] — Genesis 2:13, the Gihon flowing *"through the whole land of Cush"*.
- [[The Table of Nations]] — Genesis 10:6–8, Cush son of Ham, father of Nimrod.
- [[Where Shall Wisdom Be Found]] — Job 28:19, *"the topaz of Ethiopia"*, as the measure of a
  far-fetched treasure that is still not enough.

## Related places

- [[Egypt]] — its northern neighbour, Mizraim, Cush's brother in Genesis 10:6; for a time ruled by
  Cushite kings.
- [[Havilah]] — listed as a son of Cush (10:7), and another of the Bible's sources of precious
  materials.
- [[Ophir]] — the gold beside which Cush's topaz is named in Job 28.
- [[The Nile]] — the river on whose upper course Cush lay.
- [[Eden]] — whose second river runs through a land called Cush.
