# The Land of the Philistines

## Overview

The coastal strip southwest of Canaan, between the hill country and the sea, running from the Yarkon
down toward the desert road to [[Egypt]] — and in the Hebrew Bible, the single most persistent
foreign neighbour Israel has.

**In Genesis it means something smaller and stranger than that.** The book uses the name three times
of the country around [[Gerar]] — *"they returned into the land of the Philistines"* (21:32),
*"Abraham lived as a foreigner in the land of the Philistines many days"* (21:34), and *"Isaac went
to Abimelech king of the Philistines, to Gerar"* (26:1) — and the world it describes has none of the
features the Philistines are known for. There is no pentapolis, no Gaza or Ashdod or Ekron, no
*seren* (the Philistine title for a ruler), no iron monopoly, no Dagon, and no army. There is a
king with a Semitic name, an army officer, a court friend, herdsmen, and a quarrel about wells.
**Genesis's Philistines are pastoralists with a king, and they behave better than the patriarchs
do.**

**The anachronism, stated plainly.** The Philistines as an archaeologically visible people — one of
the Sea Peoples, Aegean in material culture, with distinctive bichrome pottery — settle the southern
coast around **1200 BC**, in the wake of the collapse that ended the Late Bronze Age. That is
centuries after any date proposed for Abraham. The standard reading is that Genesis names the region
by the people who held it when the text was written or transmitted, which is the same habit that
calls a town [[Dan (city)]] at 14:14 several hundred years before Judges 18 gives it that name,
and that puts camels and *Ur of the Chaldees* into the patriarchal world. **The wiki records it as an
anachronism of that kind and does not treat it as a problem to be argued away.** The harmonizing
alternative — an earlier Aegean trading presence on the coast, of which there is some Middle Bronze
evidence, retrospectively called Philistine — is available and is not what the archaeology of the
Iron Age Philistine cities shows.

**The name.** *Pəlištîm* is the source, through Greek *Palaistinē*, of **Palestine**; the Hebrew is
almost certainly a loanword rather than a Semitic formation, and the Egyptian records of Ramesses III
call the same people *prst*.

**Where it goes afterwards.** Everything Genesis does not have arrives later. The Philistines hold
the coast through Judges and Samuel as Israel's defining enemy — Samson, the capture of the ark,
Goliath, David among them at Ziklag, Saul's death on Gilboa and the display of his body at Beth
Shan. The prophets address the pentapolis as a unit (Amos 1:6–8; Jeremiah 47; Zephaniah 2:4–7;
Zechariah 9:5–6). And Amos makes the one theological use of them that matters: *"Haven't I brought up
Israel out of the land of Egypt, and the Philistines from Caphtor?"* (9:7) — the same Caphtor
Genesis 10:14 already names in the Table of Nations.

## Appears in

- [[The Treaty at Beersheba]] — Genesis 21:32, 34; where Abimelech and Phicol go home to, and where
  Abraham then sojourns *many days*
- [[The Table of Nations]] — Genesis 10:14, the name's only prior occurrence, in the line of Egypt:
  *"Casluhim (which the Philistines descended from), and Caphtorim"*
- [[Isaac in Gerar]] — Genesis 26:1, 8, 14–18; *"Abimelech king of the Philistines"*, whose people
  envy Isaac's harvest — the first *qānāʾ* in the Bible — and stop his father's wells with earth;
  the only place in Scripture where Philistines are shown as herdsmen quarrelling over water

## Related places

- [[Gerar]] — the only town in it Genesis ever names, and the whole of its geography in the book
- [[Beersheba]] — on its inland edge, where both treaties with its king are sworn
- [[The Negev]] — the dry country between it and the desert
- [[Egypt]] — its parent in the Table of Nations, and the road it sits astride
- [[Canaan]] — the land it borders, and whose southwestern frontier runs *"as you go toward Gerar"*
  (10:19)
- [[Dan (city)]] — the wiki's other standing case of a later name used for an earlier time

## Events here

- Abimelech and Phicol return here after the covenant at Beersheba — Genesis 21:32,
  [[The Treaty at Beersheba]]
- Abraham lives here as a foreigner *many days* — Genesis 21:34
- Isaac comes to Abimelech king of the Philistines at Gerar in a famine; its people envy him, its
  herdsmen stop up his father's wells and contest two of his own, and its king expels him and then
  travels inland to swear a treaty with him — Genesis 26:1–33, [[Isaac in Gerar]]
- Israel is led out of Egypt away from *"the way of the land of the Philistines, although that was
  near"* — Exodus 13:17
- Named in the boundary lists of the unconquered land — Joshua 13:2–3; Judges 3:1–3
- The long war of Judges and Samuel — Judges 13–16; 1 Samuel 4–7; 17; 31
- Denounced by the prophets — Amos 1:6–8; Jeremiah 47; Zephaniah 2:4–7; Zechariah 9:5–6
- Brought up from Caphtor by the same God who brought Israel from Egypt — Amos 9:7
