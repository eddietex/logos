# Canaan

## Overview

The land the rest of the Torah is aimed at, and the only place in
[[The Table of Nations]] whose borders are drawn.

> **19** The border of the Canaanites was from Sidon—as you go toward Gerar—to Gaza—as you go toward
> Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboiim—to Lasha.

Every other branch of Genesis 10 gets names. Canaan gets a surveyed frontier: north to south along the
coast from Sidon to Gaza, then inland and south-east toward the cities of the plain. The chapter is
mapping the whole world, and it stops to trace one outline — the one Abram will be told to walk
through two chapters later, and the one Joshua will be told to take.

**The name is older than the Bible.** *Kənaʿan* appears in Egyptian and Akkadian sources — the Amarna
letters, the Idrimi inscription, Egyptian topographical lists — from the second millennium onward, as
the standard designation for the territory between the Jordan and the Mediterranean. Genesis is using
an existing geopolitical term, not coining one. A frequently repeated derivation from a word for
*purple* (the Phoenician murex dye) is a Greek-era guess and is not established.

**The land and the man are the same word, and the wiki keeps them on separate pages.** In Genesis 10
Canaan is Ham's fourth son with eleven descendants; here he is that territory. The identity is the
whole force of Noah's curse at 9:25 — a sentence pronounced on a person that reads, to every later
reader, as a sentence pronounced on a country. The person keeps [[Canaan (son of Ham)]]; this page is
the place. See [[The Curse of Canaan and the Conquest]].

**Its peoples are listed before they are met.** Genesis 10:15–18 derives from Canaan: Sidon (his
firstborn), Heth, and the Jebusites, Amorites, Girgashites, Hivites, Arkites, Sinites, Arvadites,
Zemarites, and Hamathites. Six of these recur in the seven-nation formula of Deuteronomy 7:1 and
Joshua 3:10 and in the boundary promise of Genesis 15:18–21. The Jebusites hold Jerusalem until David
takes it; Heth's descendants are the Hittites Abraham buys a grave from in Genesis 23; Sidon is the
Phoenician city that supplies Jezebel. The conquest lists are already here, in a genealogy, before
anyone has been promised anything.

**And the Table says nothing about the curse.** Twenty verses after Noah consigns Canaan to servitude
three times over, Genesis 10 gives him a firstborn, eleven sons, and a border, on exactly the same
footing as Japheth and Shem. The moral partition of 9:25–27 and the geographical partition of chapter
10 stand side by side, and Genesis does not make the second depend on the first.

**What the rest of Scripture does with it.** The land is promised (Genesis 12:7, 15:18–21, 17:8),
surveyed (Numbers 13), entered (Joshua 1–12), lost (2 Kings 17, 25), and promised again. Its
inhabitants are dispossessed on grounds the Torah states plainly and which are never Noah's oracle:
what they did (Leviticus 18:24–30; Deuteronomy 9:4–5), and not who their ancestor was.
