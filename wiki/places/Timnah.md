# Timnah

## Overview

**The town Judah goes up to for the sheep-shearing, and the reason he is on the road at all.**

> **12** After many days, Shua’s daughter, the wife of Judah, died. Judah was comforted, and went up to
> his sheep shearers to Timnah, he and his friend Hirah, the Adullamite.
> **13** Tamar was told, “Behold, your father-in-law is going up to Timnah to shear his sheep.”
> (38:12–13)

**Shearing is a festival as much as a job.** It is what Nabal is feasting at when David's men arrive
(1 Samuel 25:4–8), and what Absalom uses to get Amnon drunk and kill him (2 Samuel 13:23–29). **Three
of the Hebrew Bible's blunter stories are set at one**, and in each the shearing supplies the wine, the
crowd, and the distance from home. See [[Judah and Tamar]].

**It is also a piece of intelligence.** Tamar is *told* Judah is going up to Timnah — **she knows the
road and the date**, which is what makes the gate of [[Enaim]] a plan rather than an accident.

## Identification

**Most likely the Timnah of the Sorek valley**, on the border between Judah and Philistine country
(Joshua 15:10; and in Dan's list at Joshua 19:43) — **the Timnah Samson goes down to and marries
into** at Judges 14:1–5, in another story about an Israelite, a woman outside the line, and a young
goat (Judges 15:1). It is generally identified with **Tel Batash**, about four miles north-west of Beth
Shemesh. 2 Chronicles 28:18 records the Philistines taking it back under Ahaz.

**A second Timnah is listed in the Judean hill country** at Joshua 15:57, and some read Genesis 38's as
that one, since the text says Judah went ***up***. **The wiki records both identifications and adopts
neither**, noting that the direction of travel is the only evidence either way and that *up* and *down*
in this chapter are doing narrative as well as topographical work.

## Not to be confused with

**[[Timna]]**, the Horite concubine of Eliphaz and mother of Amalek at Genesis 36:12, 22 — **a person,
not a place, and one letter shorter in English.** The two names are distinct in Hebrew as well
(*Timnaʿ* / *Timnāh*), and the wiki keeps the pages apart by spelling; **if either ever needs
requalifying the other is renamed in the same pass**, per `AGENTS.md`.
