# Moab (place)

## Overview

### Daily, Sabbath, and New Moon Offerings — Numbers 28:1–15

Moab is not named in this unit; the plains of Moab remain the setting of Numbers 22–36, where the
offering calendar is given to the counted generation.

See [[Daily, Sabbath, and New Moon Offerings]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 28:1–15.

### Joshua commissioned — Numbers 27:12–23

Moab is not named in this unit; the setting of Numbers 22–36 is the plains of Moab, and
Deuteronomy 32:49 later places *this mountain of Abarim* at Nebo *in the land of Moab* (WEB,
verified; not yet ingested). See [[Abarim]].

See [[Joshua Commissioned]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:12–23.


### The daughters’ inheritance — Numbers 27:1–11

The hearing continues the plains-of-Moab setting supplied by Numbers 26:3, 63. Numbers 27:2
specifies the Tent of Meeting's entrance but does not repeat the regional name; Moab is therefore
contextual, not an explicit place-name in this unit.

See [[The Petition of Zelophehad's Daughters]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 27:1–11.


### The second census — Numbers 26:1–65

The plains of Moab are the setting of the second census (3, 63), after the plague and before the
land division.

See [[The Second Census]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 26.


### Baal Peor — Numbers 25:1–18

The opening names the daughters of Moab as inviting Israel to sacrifices (1–2), while the
following incident identifies Cozbi as Midianite (6, 15). The narrative involves both peoples;
they must not be silently equated.

See [[Baal Peor and the Zeal of Phinehas]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 25:1–18.


### Balaam’s last oracles — Numbers 23:27–24:25

The final main oracle predicts a ruler from Israel striking Moab (24:17). This is future speech, not
a report of a conquest during Balak’s encounter. The unidentified city in 24:19 is not assigned a
name.

See [[Balaam's Last Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 23:27–24:25.


### The first two oracles — Numbers 22:36–23:26

The reception and two oracles unfold in Moab’s setting. The king and princes listen while Israel is
viewed from heights; the nation is the object of attempted cursing, not a participant in the
sacrificial consultations.

See [[Balaam's First Two Oracles]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:36–23:26.


### Balak’s embassies — Numbers 22:1–21

Israel camps in Moab’s plains beyond the Jordan at Jericho (1). Fear of Israel’s numbers and
victories prompts Moab’s appeal to Midian and Balak’s embassies (2–7). No attack on Moab is
narrated in this unit.

See [[Balak Sends for Balaam]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 22:1–21.

### Sihon and Og — Numbers 21:21–35

Sihon had previously taken Heshbon and land down to the Arnon from Moab’s former king (26). Israel
now takes that land from Sihon, not from the Moabite king. The poem laments Moab’s defeat and
captivity (28–29), keeping historical possession distinct from present control.

See [[Sihon and Og]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 21:21–35.


### The journey to Moab — Numbers 21:10–20

Israel camps east of Moab and near the Arnon, identified here as the boundary with the Amorites
(11–15), before reaching the field of Moab and Pisgah (20). The previous alias Ar is too broad:
verse 15 names a dwelling, now treated separately as [[Ar]], not as a synonym for the entire
country. The earlier overview’s boundary with Ammon describes a different claim; this passage
explicitly says Amorites, and that distinction must be preserved.

See [[The Journey to Moab]]. Source: stored WEB Numbers 21:10–20.


**The tableland east of the Dead Sea, and one of only two countries in the Bible whose territory God
is said to have granted to someone other than Israel, Edom, or the nations of Canaan.**

It enters Scripture as a man's name in a cave (Genesis 19:37) and is a country by the time
Deuteronomy opens. The land itself is a high plateau — the *mîšôr* — cut by the deep gorge of the
Arnon, which is its northern border with Ammon and Israel's Transjordan tribes; good grain and
sheep country, and the reason 2 Kings 3:4 can describe its king paying tribute in a hundred thousand
lambs and the wool of a hundred thousand rams.

### The land grant

> Don't bother Moab, neither contend with them in battle; for I will not give you his land for a
> possession, because I have given Ar to the children of Lot for a possession.
> *(Deuteronomy 2:9)*

**In the book whose subject is the conquest of Canaan, Israel is told to leave Moab alone, and the
reason given is a divine grant.** Deuteronomy 2 supplies the same conquest theology it gives Israel:
the **Emim** lived there before, *"a people great and many, and tall as the Anakim"*, reckoned among
the Rephaim, and God dispossessed them for Lot's descendants. It is the clearest statement in
Scripture that the arrangement Israel receives is not unique to Israel. See
[[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]] and
[[Moab and Ammon - The Two Nations Born in the Cave]].

### What happens there

**Almost everything at the end of the Torah.** Israel camps on *"the plains of Moab by the Jordan at
Jericho"* for the whole of Numbers 22–36; Balak son of Zippor hires Balaam from there (Numbers
22–24); the apostasy at Baal Peor happens there with Moabite women (Numbers 25:1–3); the entire book
of Deuteronomy is delivered there; and Moses climbs Nebo, in Moab, is shown the land as far as
[[Zoar]], dies, and is buried *"in the valley in the land of Moab"* (Deuteronomy 34:1–6). **The
country founded in Genesis 19 is where Israel receives the law and loses Moses.**

Afterwards it is an enemy and a refuge by turns. Eglon king of Moab rules Israel eighteen years
(Judges 3:12–30). Elimelech takes his family there to escape a famine in Bethlehem and his sons
marry Moabite women, one of whom is **Ruth** (Ruth 1:1–4). David, hunted by Saul, leaves his father
and mother with the king of Moab and the text gives no reason (1 Samuel 22:3–4); his
great-grandmother was from there. Mesha king of Moab rebels after Ahab's death (2 Kings 3), and
records it himself: the **Mesha Stele**, found at Dhiban in 1868 and dated to the ninth century BC,
is a Moabite king's own account of throwing off Israel, naming Chemosh, Omri, and — on the reading
of line 31 that most epigraphers now accept — the *house of David*. It is the closest thing this
part of the wiki has to an external witness to anything.

The prophets then write at length against it: Isaiah 15–16, Jeremiah 48 at forty-seven verses, Amos
2:1–3, and Zephaniah 2:9's *"Moab will be as Sodom"* — the country's origin thrown back at it.
**The Song names it among the trembling nations.** *"Trembling takes hold of the mighty men of Moab"*
(15:15) — *ʾêlê môʾāb*, literally *the rams of Moab* (WLC), the WEB taking it as *leaders*. **It is the
first time Moab appears in Exodus.** See [[The Song of the Sea]].
