# Ammon

## Overview

**The country north of Moab across the Arnon, around the headwaters of the Jabbok — and, with Moab,
one of the two nations Genesis explains by a night in a cave.**

Scripture almost never calls it *Ammon* on its own. The standard form is *bənê ʿAmmôn*, **the
children of Ammon**, which keeps the eponym in view every time the nation is mentioned: they are a
people named as somebody's descendants, in the same idiom Deuteronomy uses when it calls them
*"the children of Lot"*. The name goes back to [[Ben Ammi]], *son of my kinsman*, born to the
younger of [[Lot's Daughters]] in Genesis 19:38.

Its capital is **Rabbah** — Rabbath Ammon, and the modern Amman, which keeps the name after three
thousand years. The country is drier than Moab and further from the Jordan, edging into the desert
on the east, which is roughly the shape of the Bible's border disputes about it: Ammon presses west
and north into Gilead whenever Israel is weak.

### The land grant, and the giants before it

> When you come near the border of the children of Ammon, don't bother them, nor contend with them;
> for I will not give you any of the land of the children of Ammon for a possession, because I have
> given it to the children of Lot for a possession. *(Deuteronomy 2:19)*

**The same protection given Moab ten verses earlier, on the same ground.** Deuteronomy 2:20–21 adds
the ethnography: the land was Rephaim country, held by a people *"the Ammonites call them
Zamzummim"* — great, many, and tall as the Anakim — whom God destroyed before the Ammonites so that
they could dispossess them and live in their place. Genesis 14:5 has already had Chedorlaomer strike
*"the Zuzim in Ham"* on the same march that hit the Emim of Moab and the Horites of Seir, and the
Zuzim are generally identified with these Zamzummim. **So the two nations of the cave hold their
land on precisely the terms Israel holds Canaan, and Genesis has quietly named the peoples they
displaced five chapters before either of them is born.** See
[[The Nephilim and the Giants of Canaan]] and [[The Rescue of Lot and Melchizedek]].

### What happens there

- **1 Samuel 11** — Nahash the Ammonite besieges Jabesh Gilead and offers terms: the right eye of
  every man in the town, *"and lay it as a reproach on all Israel"*. Saul's response is what makes
  him king in fact rather than only by anointing.
- **2 Samuel 10; 11:1; 12:26–31** — Hanun shaves David's envoys' beards and cuts their garments off
  at the buttocks; the war that follows is the one David stays home from, and Uriah dies at the wall
  of Rabbah.
- **Judges 11** — Jephthah's campaign, prefaced by a long diplomatic argument over exactly the
  Deuteronomy 2 territory question, and ending in the vow that costs his daughter.
- **1 Kings 11:5, 7, 33; 2 Kings 23:13** — Solomon builds a high place for **Milcom** *"the
  abomination of the children of Ammon"* on the hill east of Jerusalem, and it stands until Josiah
  breaks it down three centuries later. **Naamah the Ammonitess** is the mother of Rehoboam
  (1 Kings 14:21, 31) — which puts an Ammonite woman in the ancestry of every king of Judah after
  Solomon.
- **Amos 1:13–15; Jeremiah 49:1–6; Ezekiel 25:1–7; Zephaniah 2:8–11** — the oracles. Amos's charge
  is the ripping open of pregnant women in Gilead *"that they may enlarge their border"*; Zephaniah's
  sentence is that Ammon will become *"as Gomorrah"*.
- **Nehemiah 2:10, 19; 4:3, 7; 13:1–3** — Tobiah the Ammonite opposing the wall, and Deuteronomy
  23:3 read aloud and enforced against Ammonites and Moabites in the same chapter.

## Events here

- Ben Ammi is born in a cave in the hills above Zoar — Genesis 19:30–38, [[Lot and His Daughters]]
- Its territory is granted to *"the children of Lot"* and closed to Israel — Deuteronomy 2:19–21
- Jephthah's war over the Gilead border — Judges 11:4–33
- Nahash besieges Jabesh Gilead; Saul relieves it — 1 Samuel 11:1–11
- David's envoys humiliated at Rabbah; Uriah dies at its wall; the crown of Milcom taken —
  2 Samuel 10; 11:14–17; 12:26–31
- Solomon's high place for Milcom on the hill east of Jerusalem, torn down by Josiah —
  1 Kings 11:7; 2 Kings 23:13
- Sentenced to the fate of Gomorrah — Zephaniah 2:9
